## Executive Summary

Practitioners agree that state, local, school and cooperative buying is softer ground than the federal portal. It brings less competition, easier registration, local preference, and co-op awards that replace a year-long RFP with a priced quote. On federal set-asides, they agree the certification narrows the field but never delivers the work. Outreach does: sources-sought responses, contracting-officer calls, and team campaigns. The numbers they use are concrete. Texas SmartBuy drops from 20,000 pages to 27 once filtered. New Jersey quote thresholds run $17,500 to $53,000. An SDVOSB search narrows from 61 to 14 results with a Sources Sought filter. Twenty to 30 sources-sought responses yield 5 to 10 RFP responses. Janitorial work is priced per service point. They split on timing: one camp files certifications early, while others wait for prime wins, and one veteran operator says veteran status alone moves nothing.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every SLED-focused operator starts from the same premise: state, county, city and school-district buying is softer ground than the federal portal. SamSearch argues local work carries less competition than sam.gov, relaxed registration, a proximity edge, and the chance to stack in-state past performance [OmEqzgzoKlc @ 00:00:31]. It makes the same case for cities, which buy everything from cleaning to jiu-jitsu classes and lean toward local businesses over past performance [RY7zan9X3oY @ 00:01:40]. The same channel names the friction plainly: every city runs its own site and its own process, so the edge goes to whoever maps the portals first. Win the Bid frames the entry ramp around thresholds. Below the formal-bid line the goal is getting called for quotes, while bigger purchases go out as formal public bids [roNNP4jeQpc @ 00:00:55]. The Government Contracting Strategist & Host adds the discipline side: "don't chase the map, choose a lane." That advice came in a week that surfaced 12,441 new opportunities nationwide, and the host flagged a $13,000 records-scanning job as the kind of small contract that opens doors [-S6wZyOulKA @ 00:02:54]. The consensus playbook is to pick a geography and a trade, register on the free local portals, and work the small, recurring buys before chasing marquee awards.

On cooperative contracts, suppliers, a dealer and Sourcewell itself tell one story: the solicitation work is already done. Wheelen bid and won a Sourcewell award directly, then authorized US and Canadian distributors. Agencies simply pick a distributor and "cut out the bid process" [511aPYJ9WjI @ 00:01:00]. Foley Equipment runs the same mechanic from the dealer seat. Caterpillar won the national solicitation, cities buy straight from the Cat dealer, and a priced quote goes to council with no RFP or RFQ cycle [Hlnggncm3KQ @ 00:01:06]. Foley puts cooperative purchasing at roughly 20-30% of US purchases and says salespeople and city officials often don't know it exists, so Foley educates commissions and boards. Crosby Grindle of Provident has about 20 years in co-op buying. Grindle says co-op contracts satisfy the highest procurement tier and replace a conventional RFP that takes "a year or more." Buyers also keep their local supplier while getting national pricing [V3qc9e4w-4M @ 00:14:30]. Sourcewell's own fire-apparatus pitch matches: a traditional bid can add up to a year, while co-op means "most of that work's done for you" [vTX0ljZ_Nro @ 00:00:42]. The operator takeaway every one of them repeats is that once the award exists, the sale becomes a quote, not a bid, and the person who explains the contract vehicle to the board controls the deal.

On federal set-asides, the agreement is blunter: the badge narrows the field but never delivers the work. Rick Howard of GovClose is an ex-acquisitions officer who reports managing $82B in DoD contracts. Howard warns that the certification won't win contracts; the product or service will, and nothing will "land in your lap" [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:03:05]. GovKidMethod reaches the same verdict on 8(a): "The SBA is not going to be bringing you contracts," so the advice is to wait until one or two prime contracts are in hand [xXM1cyLQ_jY @ 00:07:09]. Where speakers align is the math that makes set-asides worth targeting anyway. US Federal Contractor Registration lays out goals of 23% small business, 5% for minority, women and 8(a), 3% SDVOSB, and 3% HUBZone. In that breakdown, 69,000 WOSBs and 62,000 minority firms split their 5%, while 16,000 SDVOSBs share an extra 3% [yFsBD592FN8 @ 00:06:51]. Neil McDonnell's FY2023 count shows how thin each pool is: 11,152 SDVOSB, 2,845 WOSB, 1,534 HUBZone, and 872 8(a) set-aside awards out of 5,413,622 new contracts [4poj2n7iLrs @ 00:00:58]. The shared baseline is SAM registration, a matching size standard, and then direct outreach that turns a certification into an actual set-aside tag on a real requirement.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The SLED search grind is concrete. On Texas SmartBuy, SamSearch shows an unfiltered view of "20,000 pages," then cuts it down by searching one keyword and setting status to "posted." Even with no keyword, the posted filter drops the list to 27 pages [OmEqzgzoKlc @ 00:02:43]. Win the Bid works New Jersey by the LFN 2025-08 thresholds, effective July 1, 2025. The limits are $17,500 for towns and counties without a qualified purchasing agent, $39,000 for school districts without one, and up to $53,000 with one. Legal notices must run at least 10 days before bids are due [roNNP4jeQpc @ 00:00:33]. New York's State Contract Reporter carries $50K-plus work, registration is free, and alerts do the watching. Pennsylvania runs through COSTARS, and the weekly routine includes 15 minutes of checking and calling the purchasing office. D'S Construction runs five Louisiana sites every morning: SAM.gov, LaPAC for state agencies, Central Bidding for cities, parishes and school boards, Centerline Bid Connect for LSU construction, and MyBidMatch through the APEX Accelerator. Damian works South Louisiana jobs under $50,000 and set-asides first [yxjYYFbCa5E @ 00:02:40]. Central Bidding documents and plan-holder lists cost about $110/month, and the free workaround is a paper copy from the clerk or school board [yxjYYFbCa5E @ 00:01:39].

For federal set-asides, the mechanics start in the search filter. GovKidMethod uses the set-aside filter in Contract Opportunities. SDVOSB plus a NAICS code returned 61 results, and adding "Sources Sought" narrowed it to 14. That list becomes market research before paying for any certification [nRXCetmIWUs @ 00:02:09]. Neil McDonnell turns those notices into set-asides with a five-step campaign [4poj2n7iLrs @ 00:22:58]:
1. Pick a low-friction opportunity that was full-and-open or total small business, not an 8(a) incumbent.
2. Call the contracting officer for 15-20 minutes.
3. Contact the agency's small-business professional.
4. Build a team of 10-20 firms.
5. Have each firm respond to the sources-sought notice requesting one tag only.
On the rule of two, McDonnell says "forget about it": 20-30 sources-sought responses yield 5-10 RFP responses. McDonnell's proof points: the 2018 set-aside calls helped move an extra $3B to HUBZone firms, and about 100 firms answering the State Department in one day produced a $200M set-aside. Rick Howard runs the same front end. Howard targets sources-sought notices and RFIs, pushes set-asides plus requirements such as PMP or CISSP, and backs every claim with metrics, never fabrications [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:54:12]. One RFI Howard walked through capped responses at 15 pages, so the pitch has to be tight.

Pricing and ordering mechanics close the loop. The Government Contracting Strategist & Host prices janitorial work by service point, not square foot: take the last job's price and divide it by visits, restrooms, or stops [iTD46ZpHbdI @ 00:16:25]. The same host reads bid tabs and award letters. One example is an NCDOT US-70 rest area last awarded at $111,648 to the lowest responsible bid, with the rule "price against history and not just guess at it" [iTD46ZpHbdI @ 00:10:32]. On simplified acquisitions, US Federal Contractor Registration reports that one quote is allowed when the price is fair against FPDS history. Agencies may prepay a deposit, half, or the full amount. An FPDS demo turned up a $10,000 award paid in full upfront with 4 offers, and a $45,000 SAP award with "no competition" [yFsBD592FN8 @ 00:05:43]. For co-op orders, Provident's Ryan Donovan says Sourcewell registration carries no cost or obligation and lasts for life. Group benefits still need a quote, and the PO must cite the agency account number and the contract number [V3qc9e4w-4M @ 00:30:12]. Donovan puts Sourcewell at about $1B in annual sales across 700+ suppliers and 50,000+ registered agencies. Grindle adds that sales through co-op contracts overall are almost $60B.

## 3. Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split on this circuit is when to chase set-aside paper. GovKidMethod openly rejects the industry dogma of stacking certifications the moment SAM registration clears [xXM1cyLQ_jY @ 00:00:15]. Their sequencing is surgical: grab WOSB or EDWOSB soon after SAM because it costs little time, but hold off on 8(a), a nine-year program that normally demands two years in business and tax returns, until the firm has one or two prime contracts under its belt [xXM1cyLQ_jY @ 00:06:36]. SamSearch frames the same program as worth going through, pitching 8(a) sole-source awards up to $4.5M, or $7M in manufacturing, against a 51% disadvantaged-ownership and two-year bar [PPOCeCEkaBk @ 00:04:57]. The two camps are not arguing about whether 8(a) pays. They are arguing about whether a firm with no federal track record should start its nine-year clock before it has anything to sell into it. Rick Howard of GovClose, an ex-acquisitions officer who says he managed $82B in DoD contracts, cuts across both camps: the certification will not win contracts, the product or service will [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:03:05]. The operator takeaway is to treat each certificate as a multiplier on an existing offer, sequenced by approval speed, not as an entry ticket.

On the veteran and HUBZone lanes, the practitioners read the same federal data and reach different temperatures. Rick Howard drills USASpending to show the VA sending over 90% of its awards in architecture, engineering and construction codes to SDVOSB or veteran set-asides, and tells uncertified firms to avoid those agencies outright [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:13:41]. He also argues that plain veteran-owned status, without the service-disabled certification, does not move the needle at all [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:24:43]. GovKidMethod sits at 50/50 on SDVOSB timing, citing a three-to-six-month VA certification timeline while waving off consultants who charge $2-3K for little added value. HUBZone produces the sharpest contrast. GovKidMethod warns there are few HUBZone set-aside opportunities, up to six months to certify, and an employee-residency ratio a growing firm can outgrow, so do not pursue it early [xXM1cyLQ_jY @ 00:14:21]. Neil McDonnell flips the problem from supply to demand: the 2018 set-aside calls helped move an extra $3B to HUBZone firms, and at the State Department about 100 firms responded in a single day, which led to a $200M set-aside [4poj2n7iLrs @ 00:11:57]. One camp waits for set-asides to appear; the other organizes firms to create them.

Cooperative purchasing splits the room on framing. Foley Equipment sells Sourcewell to commissions and boards as a shortcut: Caterpillar already won the national solicitation, so a priced quote goes straight to council with no RFP or RFQ process [Hlnggncm3KQ @ 00:01:06]. FDIC International pitches the Wheelen contract the same way, saying agencies choose their distributor and cut out the bid process [511aPYJ9WjI @ 00:01:00]. Provident's webinar draws a hard line against that language: never pitch a co-op contract as a way to get around procurement. Instead, position it as satisfying the highest procurement tier because a lead agency already ran the competition, and tell buyers to read their own policy for best-value versus lowest-price authority [V3qc9e4w-4M @ 00:19:43]. The SLED hunters split on breadth. The Government Contracting Strategist & Host tells newcomers not to chase the map but to choose a lane, while SamSearch sells AI coverage of about 300 procurement sites. D'S Construction checks five bid sites every morning, working South Louisiana jobs under $50,000 and set-asides first [yxjYYFbCa5E @ 00:02:40]. Win the Bid splits the difference with a five-step routine that budgets 15 minutes a week to checking boards and calling the purchasing office.

## 4. Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive trap the federal practitioners report is waiting for a certificate to deliver revenue. Rick Howard is blunt that contracts will not land in your lap after certification, even though the SDVOSB approval took about two weeks while 8(a) and WOSB can run far longer [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:03:39]. GovKidMethod lands on the same point from the SBA side: the SBA is not going to be bringing you contracts, which is why 8(a) should wait until real prime past performance exists [xXM1cyLQ_jY @ 00:07:09]. Certification can also slip out from under a firm. HUBZone eligibility hangs on an employee-residency ratio, with 35% of employees required to live in a HUBZone per US Federal Contractor Registration, so a firm that hires outside the zone can outgrow its own status [xXM1cyLQ_jY @ 00:13:48]. A quieter trap is assuming a state badge travels: GovKidMethod warns that state WOSB certification is not the same as federal [nRXCetmIWUs @ 00:02:42], and that federal WOSB is no longer self-certified. Neil McDonnell's numbers show the gap: about 95K self-certified WOSBs exist against 11-12K SBA-certified, and WOSBs won $27B while less than $1B was actually set aside for them.

On the state and local side, the failures are procedural and they are final. D'S Construction flags the mandatory pre-bid meeting: miss it and you can't submit [yxjYYFbCa5E @ 00:02:56]. The same episode exposes the paywall trap on Central Bidding, where cities, parishes and school boards post for free browsing but documents and plan-holder lists sit behind a subscription of about $110 a month. The workaround is a paper copy from the clerk or school board [yxjYYFbCa5E @ 00:01:07]. For subcontractors, that plan-holder list is the gold because it shows which GCs will receive your number. SamSearch names fragmentation as the core bottleneck of city work: every city has its own site and its own process, which is the pain its aggregation and AI summaries are built to absorb [RY7zan9X3oY @ 00:02:30]. Then there is scope that bleeds margin after award. Breaking down Miami-Dade's nightly bus cleaning, the Government Contracting Strategist & Host tells bidders to own the night shift with depot access and a 2 a.m. supervisor, but to price the on-call daytime piece carefully because that is where contractors get burned [iTD46ZpHbdI @ 00:07:46].

The economics checks come down to history and time. The Government Contracting Strategist & Host hammers one rule: price against history and not just guess at it, reading bid tabs and award letters. The last NCDOT US-70 rest-area award went to the lowest responsible bid at $111,648 [iTD46ZpHbdI @ 00:10:32]. For janitorial, the same host prices by service point rather than square foot, dividing the last job's price by visits, restrooms or stops, and splits daily work from event surge so the contracting officer isn't guessing. Time is the second check. Rick Howard tells operators to plan six to eighteen months to a first win [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:43:57], and warns that reported award data lags, so a peer firm's trajectory of $2.5M to $16.4M to $16.1M deserves skepticism [Qij-5QQmwc8 @ 00:37:47]. On the co-op side, Provident puts a conventional RFP process at a year or more, and Sourcewell Gov says a traditional apparatus bid can add up to a year, the cycle cooperative purchasing is sold to skip [V3qc9e4w-4M @ 00:18:57]. US Federal Contractor Registration adds the counterweight on small buys: a $45,000 simplified acquisition drew no competition, and a $10,000 award was paid in full upfront.
