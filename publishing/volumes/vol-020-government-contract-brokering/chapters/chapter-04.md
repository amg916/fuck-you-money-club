## 1. Protect the reserve before funding the first bid desk  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with the cash calendar, not the software catalog. Government Contract Brokering has a six-to-eighteen-month first-deal horizon, so allocate 60% of each startup package to working capital before signing annual contracts. At $25,000, that means $15,000 reserved and $10,000 available for setup; at $75,000, reserve $45,000; at $150,000, reserve $90,000. Keep the reserve in a business deposit account separate from operating disbursements, with weekly reconciliation and an explicit minimum balance. At $5,000 monthly cash burn, those reserves cover three, nine, and eighteen months; at $25,000, they cover only 0.6, 1.8, and 3.6 months. These calculations exclude receipts and assume the setup allocation has already been spent. The smallest package therefore cannot independently finance the stated sales cycle. Its viable entry is a founder-led desk with prepaid private-client research assignments, staged setup purchases, or additional committed funding. Do not solve that mismatch by describing an unsigned award as cash. Publish a thirteen-week forecast showing beginning cash, collectible invoices, contractor commitments, insurance installments, and ending reserve. Extend the forecast monthly through eighteen months, including the collection lag after the first signed engagement.

Treat the $5,000–$25,000 monthly operating range as a controlled overhead envelope, not proof that every scorecard case fits inside it. A lean $5,000 planning envelope can allocate $300 to software and communications, $800 to insurance and professional-support accruals, $2,200 to limited paid delivery capacity, $700 to targeted prospecting, and $1,000 to administration and contingency. Contractor hours must fit the actual rate card; $2,200 buys forty hours at $55, not a full proposal department. Larger assignments require separately priced delivery budgets and collection terms. The canonical annual expense residuals are $102,000 Conservative, $434,000 Base, and $700,000 Scaled, obtained by subtracting owner profit from recognized service revenue. Their monthly equivalents are $8,500, about $36,167, and about $58,333. Therefore, Base and Scaled require delivery and other costs outside the recurring overhead envelope; calling that envelope total business expense would break the model. The specified teams remain 2 FT + 3 C, 2 FT + 3 C, and 3 FT + 4 C. Those labels do not establish affordable market salaries. Validate paid hours, benefits, founder compensation treatment, and contractor utilization before using any scenario as a hiring authorization.

Release setup money in evidence-backed increments. Entity formation, contract review, banking, insurance placement, and secure document handling precede paid intelligence subscriptions. A federal prospect does not justify leasing an office, hiring a salaried capture executive, or buying warehouse stock. The allocation matrix contains spending ceilings rather than instructions to exhaust each category. Unused amounts return to the reserve, and no contingency dollar supports a speculative supplier deposit. For direct private-client services, negotiate a defined onboarding deliverable and an initial payment where commercially acceptable; federal contract payment terms are controlled by the actual award and applicable clauses. Never import private-client deposit assumptions into an agency purchase order. Establish separate accounts receivable aging for commercial retainers, prime-contractor subcontract invoices, and direct government invoices because each has different acceptance evidence and dispute routes. A $12,000 service invoice delayed sixty days ties up $12,000 of liquidity even when accounting profit looks healthy. Before increasing fixed overhead, require signed scope, measurable delivery capacity, and cash coverage through a delayed-payment scenario. If the reserve falls below the next eight weeks of committed disbursements, stop optional subscriptions and decline bids requiring unfunded deposits or irreversible third-party commitments.

## 2. Configure twelve production layers without buying twelve subscriptions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The twelve-layer matrix is a procurement worksheet, not a live vendor quotation. Dollar figures are explicit planning assumptions for the named tiers, in United States dollars before tax; paid annual-billing tiers show monthly equivalents and must be checked at checkout. Product packaging, seat minimums, and included capabilities can change. Start with the free layers and purchase paid seats only for people who produce or control deliverables. The illustrated minimum paid stack is two Google Workspace Business Starter seats at $7 each, two Bitwarden Teams seats at $4 each, one QuickBooks Simple Start subscription budgeted at $38, and one DocuSign Personal subscription budgeted at $10: $70 monthly equivalent. That is a software subtotal, not the firm's total infrastructure cost. It excludes hardware, domains, telephone service, tax, insurance, paid backup, and any annual prepayment. DocuSign's planned five-envelope monthly allowance must fit actual signature volume; replace it with an acceptable client signature workflow before paying for excess capacity. Free Airtable, Trello, and HubSpot layers can overlap, so activate only the interfaces that reduce handoffs. A spreadsheet with one accountable owner beats three unmaintained databases with conflicting opportunity deadlines.

For the solicitation-intelligence play, combine SAM.gov saved searches with municipal Bonfire and OpenGov Procurement notices. Record agency, notice identifier, amendment sequence, due time with time zone, NAICS code, set-aside status, and source link. An alert is a lead, not an authoritative deadline; the current solicitation and amendments control. Price an illustrative private-client monitoring package at $500 monthly for eight curated briefs. Two analyst hours at $60 plus $20 of allocated tools leave $360 before selling, management, and other expenses. If fewer than eight relevant opportunities exist, report that fact rather than padding the feed. For bid-calendar management, a $750 monthly service with three hours at $60 and $30 allocated tooling leaves $540 before other costs. Its fallback is a versioned spreadsheet and dual calendar reminders, not blind reliance on an API. For proposal production, a $4,000 fixed-scope package using thirty hours at $70 and $200 of outside review leaves $1,700 before overhead. Scope excludes technical claims the client cannot substantiate. Amendment-driven rewrites receive a written change order; a missing technical owner triggers a no-bid recommendation rather than fabricated qualifications.

Design security around the data actually authorized for the desk. Enforce multifactor authentication, individual accounts, encrypted disks, device updates, and role-limited client folders before importing the first proposal. Do not place credentials, banking instructions, personnel records, or restricted technical material in public Trello boards or shared chatbot workspaces. The starter stack is not an assertion that it meets Controlled Unclassified Information requirements. If a contract introduces FAR 52.204-21, DFARS 252.204-7012, or other security obligations, identify the covered information and required environment before accepting it. Decline receipt until the applicable controls and contractual responsibilities are implemented. Maintain a register for systems of record: HubSpot owns client contacts, the opportunity ledger owns deadlines, Drive owns approved working documents, and the accounting system owns invoices. Export those records weekly to access-controlled storage and test restoration quarterly; an export is useful only if relationships and attachments survive. Use SAM.gov APIs within published access conditions and rate limits, retaining source timestamps. Scripts may suggest classifications or checklist entries, but a named reviewer verifies eligibility, mandatory forms, pricing units, and submission acknowledgments. Automation should remove rekeying, never transfer responsibility for a missed amendment to software.

## 3. Establish lawful service boundaries, banking controls, and seven play economics  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Form the operating entity in the jurisdiction where it actually conducts business, obtain its EIN, document ownership, and open a business account under the same legal identity used on contracts and invoices. Confirm current federal and state reporting duties rather than copying an old formation checklist. SAM.gov registration and a Unique Entity ID are free; pursue the registration needed for the firm's actual federal role without charging clients a fictitious government registration fee. A service provider advising bidders is not automatically the prime contractor. Written engagements must identify who submits, who signs certifications, who performs, and who gets paid. Review FAR Subpart 3.4 and FAR 52.203-5 before any compensation tied to winning a federal contract; a broker label does not establish a bona fide agency exception. Protect nonpublic procurement information under 41 U.S.C. chapter 21, and prohibit improper payments under 41 U.S.C. chapter 87. Maintain general liability, professional liability, cyber coverage, and required workers' compensation according to actual exposures and contract terms. Verify exclusions, deductibles, defense-cost treatment, and subcontractor coverage with the carrier. A certificate evidences coverage information; it does not rewrite the policy or confer protection that an exclusion removes.

The next three operating plays monetize documented work rather than access to an award decision. Prime/sub teaming coordination can carry a $2,500 fixed diligence fee: twenty hours at $75 plus $250 of document review leaves $750 before overhead. If the contemplated arrangement depends on unperformed work, undisclosed commissions, or prohibited pass-through economics, stop structuring it and return only the contracted diligence findings. For covered set-asides, examine 13 CFR 125.6 and the applicable FAR 52.219-14 clause; the calculation depends on contract category and treatment of similarly situated entities. Supplier verification can charge $900 for a defined evidence packet: eight hours at $65 plus $80 of verification expense leaves $300 before overhead. Verify capacity, relevant registrations, exclusion status, quoted lead times, and references; otherwise narrow the finding rather than issue an unsupported endorsement. Invoice administration can charge $600 monthly for twenty documented invoices: four hours at $60 plus $20 allocated tools leaves $340 before overhead. Require acceptance records and submission receipts; disputed invoices escalate to the contracting parties. These examples, together with the three preceding plays, are chapter-level operating applications, not additional supplied catalog identifiers. Their contribution calculations are illustrative and must not be added to canonical revenue.

The seventh mapped play is transferable_tax_credit_brokerage_syndication, a separate specialist workflow rather than a government-award commission. Originate leads from public DOE and EPA project datasets, grid-operator interconnection queues, state film offices, and historic-preservation registries using permitted access methods. DOE and EPA records are not universal interconnection queues, and a project listing proves neither credit ownership nor transferability. For $1,000,000 of credit face value, the supplied $0.82–$0.88 seller range implies $820,000–$880,000, while $0.92–$0.96 buyer pricing implies $920,000–$960,000. The apparent $40,000–$140,000 spread is not automatically broker revenue. IRC 6418 generally bars a transferee from transferring the purchased credit again; structure an eligible producer-to-taxpayer transfer, not an intermediary resale. An illustrative separately agreed 1.5% fee on $940,000 consideration produces $14,100 service revenue. Buyer cash funds simultaneous escrow disbursements, with no broker inventory or bridge advance. Bind non-cancelable tax-credit insurance acceptable to the parties and verify exclusions and cancellation rights; if unavailable, decline the transaction under this play's required fallback. Georgia, Louisiana, historic, and brownfield programs require separate transfer analysis. Screen individual buyers for passive-activity limits, and obtain specialist review of eligibility, registration, recapture allocation, and transfer documentation.

## 4. Rehearse the entire workflow before accepting live commitments  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a production rehearsal using a public solicitation that matches the desk's intended niche. Download its attachments, establish the current amendment set, identify mandatory experience and certifications, and assign a primary owner plus a backup reviewer. Reconstruct the deadline from the source document, including time zone and submission channel. Prepare a compliance matrix linking each requirement to its proposed response location and responsible client subject-matter owner. The desk must distinguish an absent requirement from evidence it has not yet received. Review pricing units for each line item; a monthly service priced as an annual quantity can destroy otherwise sound economics. Have someone outside the drafting workflow verify the final attachment names, signature authority, required representations, and acknowledgment of amendments. Rehearse uploads only in a permitted practice environment or local simulation; do not submit a live bid as a test. Save the checklist, timing log, and defects in a reusable job folder. Launch only after the team can reproduce the workflow without relying on an individual's inbox or memory. Where the agency offers no practice portal, document submission instructions and plan an early real upload with the client's authorization.

Test money movement separately from proposal production. The person changing vendor bank details should not be the sole approver of the first payment to that account. Confirm changes through a previously established telephone number, not contact information supplied in the change email. Use bank dual authorization where available; a solo operator should document independent client or bookkeeper verification for material changes. Never route client purchase funds or tax-credit principal through the ordinary operating account merely to simplify reporting. A qualified escrow provider should define release conditions, account ownership, dispute handling, and callback procedures in signed instructions. Reconcile each service invoice to the engagement, delivery acceptance, and ledger entry. For federal payments, inspect the actual payment clause and invoice submission system; 31 U.S.C. 3902 does not turn an incomplete or disputed invoice into immediately available cash. The forecast should survive a sixty-day collection delay and the loss of the largest unsigned opportunity. Simulate a supplier missing a delivery milestone, then identify contractual substitution rights and a qualified backup. If replacement costs consume the entire service fee, reject or reprice the scope before promising a delivery date the supplier cannot substantiate.

Measure readiness with evidence, then expand only the constraint that actually limits earned fees. The launch packet should contain the reserve reconciliation, signed scope template, current policy documents, access register, tested recovery record, rehearsal results, and an example accepted invoice. Weekly reporting should show qualified opportunities, paid engagements, delivery hours, amendment defects, invoice aging, and cash coverage. Do not reward the team for gross bid volume when most opportunities fail eligibility or capacity checks. A useful capacity threshold is booked delivery hours divided by available paid hours for the next four weeks; investigate before that ratio exceeds 80%, leaving room for amendments and rework. At twenty additional hours required, a $65 hourly specialist costs $1,300; approve that expense only against priced scope and sufficient collection coverage. Preserve the canonical cases when reporting progress: $120,000 at 15% yields $18,000; $560,000 at 22.5% yields $126,000; $1,000,000 at 30% yields $300,000. Those are annual scenario outputs, not monthly run rates or promised outcomes. Maintain separate dashboards for client award value, credit principal, recognized service revenue, contribution after direct work, and owner profit. That separation keeps impressive transaction volume from concealing an underfunded service business.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Fund and segregate the reserve
   - Move 60% of startup capital into the designated business reserve account.
   - Record the remaining setup ceilings, build the thirteen-week forecast, and identify funding shortfalls across the six-to-eighteen-month sales horizon.
2. Provision the minimum authorized stack
   - Activate official source accounts, named identities, secure folders, and the opportunity ledger.
   - Verify current vendor prices and terms before purchase; enable paid seats only where the delivery owner documents their necessity.
3. Rehearse one representative engagement
   - Run a public solicitation through intake, eligibility review, drafting, review, and simulated submission.
   - Capture amendments, response mappings, time-zone checks, pricing units, restoration results, and payment-control test evidence without submitting an unauthorized bid.
4. Release production against documented evidence
   - Close critical defects and accept only funded, signed, executable client scopes.
   - Review reserve coverage and delivery utilization weekly; stop new commitments when unresolved legal structure, supplier capacity, or cash exposure prevents reliable performance.
