## Executive Summary

Practitioners converge on one rule: never price a federal bid blind. Kizzy Parks, Fox Wade, FedAccess and Neil McDonnell pull incumbent award values out of FPDS, USAspending, GSA eLibrary and direct POC emails, then work backwards from term length and evaluation criteria. ## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every pricing practitioner in this lineup starts from the same premise: the last price the government paid is public, and bidding without it is how owners burn money. Fox Wade admits their early pricing was off by $4-5M and once by $15M, and their answer is to pull what the government already paid out of free public data before touching a bid number [Z2ZeI5rayvk @ 00:00:37]. FedAccess opens with an owner who spent heavily chasing a contract that turned out to be worth only a couple hundred thousand dollars, not millions, then walks the fix through SAM.gov and FPDS step by step [GrYP5KyxBho @ 00:01:29]. Kizzy Parks makes it a standing habit: on every sources sought, RFI, RFQ or solicitation, email the POC and ask whether there is an incumbent, its name, its contract number and its contract value [L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:00:24]. Neil McDonnell frames FPDS as roughly 80% of where a contractor's activity should live, covering what they estimate is ninety-plus percent of awards and excluding only classified buys and purchases at or under $10,000 [17foq325L5w @ 00:04:57]. The shared logic is that pricing intelligence and pipeline intelligence come out of the same records, so the operator who mines award history wins twice: once on target selection and again on the number written into the bid, before a single labor rate is built.

The second point of agreement is that the price paid is only a reference until the evaluation criteria tell you how to use it. Kizzy Parks puts it bluntly: knowing the price is great, but the evaluation criteria always rule; on a lowest-cost award with a $500K prior, come in around that or lower, while technical-led awards turn the old price into a rough guide [L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:02:44]. Carrie, the Colorado government contracts lawyer with 25 years in the field, draws the same line in FAR Part 15 terms: LPTA makes technical pass/fail with no trade-off, while best value must still evaluate price and generally past performance [Tj7x3UDuOSE @ 00:12:51]. On the cost side, Solvability's Jenny says price must be as low as reasonable or you are just going to lose, and even a best-value buyer needs a reason to pay more [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:02:09]. Ellen Beebe, after 33 years in estimating and pricing, splits the market in two: commercial items under Part 12 are priced to what the market will bear against competitors' similar items, while Part 15 sole-source proposals over $2M demand certified cost or pricing data backed by a bill of materials consolidated across every line item [SpRIyXgvkg0 @ 00:01:06]. Every speaker treats the final number as a function of the evaluation method and the buying vehicle, never of what the contractor would like to charge.

The baseline prerequisite the cost-side speakers share is a books-and-records system that can defend the number after award. Solvability's Johnny Clark, a fractional GovCon CFO, tells contractors to book the way they bill, pointing to DCAA's SF 1408 checklist, and to run GAAP accrual books with a monthly close by the 15th to 20th, direct and indirect costs separated, and interest expense booked as unallowable [EBQwejU6UUQ @ 00:05:37]. GovSuccess reduces FAR 31.201-2 to five conditions that all have to pass: reasonableness, allocability, accounting standards, contract terms and cost-principle limits, warning that clearing one does not settle the rest and that an indirect-cost pool never makes an unallowable expense disappear [i7_rrrRpotk @ 00:01:10]. That lines up with Jenny's unallowable list: interest, alcohol, entertainment, first-class airfare, lobbying, gifts, contributions and advertising [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:09:52]. Carrie adds that certified cost or pricing data must be facts, must include all sub quotes, and must disclose profit or fee plus overhead and G&A, including the parent company's, with no exemption for small businesses [Tj7x3UDuOSE @ 00:17:35]. The consensus is that intelligence gets you to a competitive number, but only clean timekeeping, a segregated chart of accounts and documented allocations let you keep the money once an auditor or contracting officer starts asking questions.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Fox Wade's extraction routine is the cleanest on record: copy the incumbent contract number into FPDS, sort by date signed, ignore the latest modification (theirs showed 348k), and scroll down to modification 0 for the base award, which revealed an August 2020 award running Sep 28 to Sep 27 2023, not a set-aside, not a BPA, totaling $1,686,566.88 over three years [Z2ZeI5rayvk @ 00:02:50]. FedAccess runs the same date-signed sort on a Seeley Lake janitorial recompete with a base year plus four options and lands on $47,352.42 for 3/10/2020 to 9/30/2024, plus the incumbent's contact details and the number of bids received [GrYP5KyxBho @ 00:03:56]. Neil McDonnell insists on field-specific search rather than bare keywords, which USAspending cannot match, then targets 100 to 200 results with a ceiling of 500, sorts by action obligation or date signed, and exports CSV against a roughly 5,000-row limit, never PDF because there is no value in it [0cGdQvymmvY @ 00:25:43]. In the WOSB walkthrough they read a $146K action as probably around $1M total and a $2M action as possibly $10M, and they ignore anything under $1M when hunting [YR_aPfXedtA @ 00:15:26]. The repeatable mechanic is always the same: find the base action, rebuild the term and total, and only then decide where your number lands against the incumbent.

For labor rates, Kizzy Parks calls GSA eLibrary their number one intel tip: it shows which schedules an incumbent or competitor holds, such as a JV on STARS III and two firms on MAS, along with posted rates like 63.52 for specialists and labor category descriptions you can borrow to craft your own [L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:13:57]. On buy.gsa.gov a contract-number search failed, but a Project Manager II lookup averaged 143 with a 103 to 183 range, and the floor of 60 tied to a bachelor's degree and six years of experience, so a candidate with ten years justifies a higher rate [L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:11:49]. SamSearch works USAspending the same way, keyword-searching award descriptions such as website development to surface historical values, then filtering by the incumbent's ID to see everything that firm has been paid [NiWdxgnr3xo @ 00:12:51]. Eric Coffie's bulk version filters FY2019 and FY2018 plus a state, downloads the file, and builds an Excel pivot table to sort the fields into a usable rate and award picture [Hr_U9z1xK2g @ 00:00:59]. Stacked together, these four pulls give an operator a defensible labor-rate band, a list of who is charging what, and the exact category language buyers already accept, all before a single proposal page gets drafted or a wrap rate is applied.

Solvability's Jenny builds the burden in FOG order, fringe then overhead then G&A, with fringe equal to payroll costs over productive labor, overhead equal to overhead costs over direct labor, and G&A equal to corporate costs over total cost input [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:31:40]. Fringe runs about 30%: payroll taxes near 10% (bid 5% and you lose money), leave of 200 out of 2,080 hours, and traps like 88 holiday hours instead of 80 or 56 hours of SCA/EO sick leave. The old 2.0 multiplier, $50 to $100, dates to the Reagan years; at a client site, 30% fringe, 3% overhead, 8% G&A and 7% fee lands near 1.5, so $50 becomes about $75, and competitors may target 1.45 [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:40:51]. Run it backwards and a $75 bill rate caps pay at about $48. Johnny Clark adds that a strong growth forecast can drop G&A, so you don't need to bid 40% G&A [EBQwejU6UUQ @ 00:00:35]. On the product side, Bobby Butler prices landed cost: a $10 flashlight in 2025 can cost $13 in 2026 once tariffs, logistics, packaging and shipping are added, so $10 plus 15% is the wrong math [6Fz712e3oWc @ 00:24:31]. Fee stays modest across the board, with Jenny calling 5-10% good and noting you won't see 12-15%.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Extract incumbent identity and value
   - Email the POC on every sources sought, RFI, RFQ or solicitation asking for incumbent name, contract number and contract value.
   - Skip the email only when an attachment already names the incumbent and value, then work backwards to the term behind the number (1, 2.5, 3 or 5 years) [Kizzy Parks — L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:00:24]
2. Rebuild the base award in FPDS
   - Paste the incumbent contract number into FPDS, sort by date signed, and open modification 0.
   - Read award date, start and end dates, set-aside and BPA status, and total value; Fox Wade's base award showed $1,686,566.88 over three years while the latest action showed only 348k [Fox Wade — Z2ZeI5rayvk @ 00:02:50]
3. Benchmark labor rates
   - Search the labor category on buy.gsa.gov and the incumbent's schedules on GSA eLibrary.
   - Record the average, range and floor (Project Manager II averaged 143 across 103-183, floor of 60 at six years' experience) and adjust for your candidates' experience [Kizzy Parks — L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:11:49]
4. Wrap and back-solve the rate
   - Apply fringe, overhead, G&A and fee in FOG order in an Excel wrap calculator, then run it backwards from the target bill rate.
   - At a client site expect roughly a 1.5 wrap; a $75 bill rate means you can't pay more than about $48, so set pay from the competitive bill rate rather than the reverse [Solvability — lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:42:07]

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is over how hard to cut price. Solvability's Jenny compares indirect rates to taxes, using a downtown Atlanta dealer analogy, and says price must be as low as reasonable "or else you're just going to lose," while conceding that best value still needs a reason to pay more [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:02:09]. Kizzy Parks refuses to let the number drive the bid by itself: "the evaluation criteria always rules." A $500K lowest-cost prior award means coming in at or under that figure, while a technically weighted buy only uses price as a rough guide [L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:02:44]. On an Air Force exercise physiologist LPTA, they priced so low that contracting asked whether they had made an error, and they still won because they knew the profit they could keep. Carrie, a 25-year government contracts lawyer, pushes back on the race to zero: "Profit is good." Fee caps only bite on cost-plus-fixed-fee deals, at 15% for R&D and 10% otherwise, and sole-source awards simply get negotiated harder [Tj7x3UDuOSE @ 00:35:22]. Bobby Butler of myContractScout runs a third line on DLA buys. Break even or take a thin margin on the first one to twelve awards purely to build CPARS history, then price for profit [6Fz712e3oWc @ 00:17:49].

Practitioners also split on which rates belong in the bid. Johnny Clark, a fractional GovCon CFO at Solvability, tells operators to price on forecast: if growth will dilute corporate cost, you "don't need to bid 40% G&A" today [EBQwejU6UUQ @ 00:00:35]. Jenny sets rates per calendar year and acknowledges they fall as the company scales, yet assumes flat rates across three-to-five-year forecasts [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:29:37]. Jenny also retires the old rule of thumb. A 2.0 multiplier that turns $50 of pay into a $100 bill rate dates to "the Reagan years." A client-site staffing shop should run about 1.5, built from 30% fringe, 3% overhead, 8% G&A and 7% fee, and competitors may target 1.45 [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:40:51]. The pricing method itself forks by FAR part. Ellen Beebe, with 33 years in estimating, says Part 15 sole-source proposals over $2M demand certified cost or pricing data, including a bill of materials consolidated across every line item. Part 12 commercial items are priced to what the market will bear, off competitors' similar items [SpRIyXgvkg0 @ 00:01:06]. Carrie adds that certification is prohibited below the $250,000 SAT and required above $2M and on modifications above $750K. Adequate competition and commercial items are exempt [Tj7x3UDuOSE @ 00:20:51].

The intel stack is contested too. Neil McDonnell calls FPDS "80% of your activity, or it should be," and builds two-year recompete pipelines almost entirely from it, sizing $25-100M in opportunities to land $5M in revenue [17foq325L5w @ 00:11:22]. SamSearch counters that USAspending is free, easier than SAM, and covers $6.8T a year, making it the place to pull historical award values by keyword and incumbent ID [NiWdxgnr3xo @ 00:12:51]. Kizzy Parks prefers FPDS but leaned on paid GovWin for the Air Force pricing, distrusts SAM archives, and cross-checks GovTribe trials. They call GSA eLibrary rate sheets their "number one tip" for intel [L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:13:57]. Kizzy also works the small end of the market. GovWin showed Catholic youth coordinator awards running from about $17K to $40-50K, and a 20-50% profit on those still pays well. Bobby Butler rejects paid lead feeds outright. A $3,000-a-month list resold to 25 to 100 companies triggers a race to the bottom, and switching to the free DLA No Bid List saves $36,000 a year [6Fz712e3oWc @ 00:04:15]. Everyone here mines federal award data, but they split on which database leads, which dollar floor matters, and whether a paid layer earns its fee.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The costliest misses start with misread award data. Fox Wade admits their early pricing was off by $4-5M, and once by $15M. The fix was to pull the incumbent's FPDS record, sort by date signed, and scroll to modification 0 for the base award [Z2ZeI5rayvk @ 00:00:37]. That step matters because the latest action showed only $348K while the base award totaled $1,686,566.88 over three years [Z2ZeI5rayvk @ 00:02:50]. FedAccess opens with an owner who spent heavily chasing a contract worth only "a couple hundred thousand." They also warn that scope can jump from 2 people to 10, so the old solicitation must be pulled before trusting the $47,352.42 price [GrYP5KyxBho @ 00:05:33]. Kizzy Parks got burned when the Army withheld the value: they assumed at least $250K, and the award came in around $35K [L0SM3XAM3IE @ 00:05:28]. Neil McDonnell flags the opposite trap. Completion dates mislead because of option years, and a $146K obligation is probably about $1M total, while $2M could be $10M [YR_aPfXedtA @ 00:15:26]. Kizzy's counter is to work backwards on term every time, because a $2M value means something very different over one, three, or five years.

On the buildup side, Jenny warns that payroll taxes alone run about 10%. Bidding 5% fringe means "you're going to lose money cuz you haven't covered everything," and a 60% G&A puts a firm out of the game against an 8-15% norm [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:13:16]. Their own worked example uses $10M revenue with 30% fringe, 38.35% overhead, 10% G&A and 8% profit, and they flag those rates as too high to win [lOumu-Dk0Jw @ 00:35:09]. Johnny Clark's DCAA fail test is a single $300,000 wages line. Labor distribution must tie to gross payroll, timesheet changes need an audit trail, and a bill for 170 PM hours must trace back to daily time recording [EBQwejU6UUQ @ 00:17:14]. GovSuccess shows the allocation trap with a $2,000 shared test-equipment invoice across four projects. Dumping it on one job is wrong, equal splits are not automatic, and one-quarter usage points to a $500 allocation to investigate [i7_rrrRpotk @ 00:02:29]. They add that "an indirect-cost pool does not make an unallowable expense disappear." Bobby Butler's landed-cost lesson closes the loop. A $10 flashlight in 2025 can cost $13 in 2026 after tariffs, logistics, packaging and shipping, so "$10 + 15%" is the wrong math [6Fz712e3oWc @ 00:24:31].

Execution traps hit after the number is set. Bobby Butler watched an awardee fail to meet the delivery date and names the repeat mistakes: not verifying the item is still open, ignoring delivery dates, bidding without a supplier, omitting freight and tariffs, and skipping FOB and inspection clauses [6Fz712e3oWc @ 00:25:46]. The No Bid List is a 10-20K item snapshot with some lines already awarded or cancelled, and quoting 10 cents instead of $10 still obligates you if the award is made [6Fz712e3oWc @ 00:30:01]. Carrie lays out the Part 15 tripwires: "late is late," an accepted late proposal stays protestable, and a withdrawn proposal cannot be resubmitted [Tj7x3UDuOSE @ 00:22:09]. Defective pricing brings price adjustments, fees, interest, penalties, CPARS damage, and possible criminal or civil exposure [Tj7x3UDuOSE @ 00:50:04]. Neil McDonnell adds a research bottleneck. FPDS holds summary data only, with no PWS or scope documents, so the work statement has to come from SAM, the CO, or FOIA [0cGdQvymmvY @ 00:10:10]. Johnny Clark warns that CPAs are "general practitioners," so the books behind a cost buildup need a GovCon specialist rather than a generalist [EBQwejU6UUQ @ 00:31:06].
