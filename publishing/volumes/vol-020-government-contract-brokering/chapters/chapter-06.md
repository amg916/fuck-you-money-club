## 1. Find Purchasable Demand Before Chasing Published Bids  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one service family, two adjacent jurisdictions, and a buyer profile you can actually support. A useful opening wedge is custodial or grounds work for facilities with recurring requirements, measurable staffing, and public purchasing histories. Search SAM.gov Contract Opportunities by NAICS, place of performance, notice type, and response date; use relevant Product Service Codes to catch inconsistent labels. Pull historical award transactions from USAspending.gov, then verify the actual solicitation, amendments, and contract information against the issuing agency's records. An apparent expiration date is a research lead, not proof that a recompete will occur: options, extensions, consolidations, and cancellations change the calendar. For municipal work, inspect adopted budgets, purchasing forecasts, council agendas, bid tabs, and the city's designated vendor portal. Record the source URL, retrieval date, buying office, incumbent, estimated timing, and specific service requirement in a shared register. Separate government demand records from prospective paying clients: the agency buys performance, while a qualified prime or supplier may buy your research and coordination. That distinction prevents a large public award from masquerading as brokerage revenue and keeps outreach focused on an identifiable commercial customer with a solvable operating problem.

Use seven plays, but treat them as routes to paid work rather than seven simultaneous businesses. The supplied canonical TRACK_1 play is gsa_municipal_set_aside_facility_ghost_brokering; its permissible implementation is disclosed facility teaming, never an invisible middleman or fabricated eligibility claim. The other six chapter-level plays are recompete intelligence, municipal budget mining, sources-sought preparation, bid-desk retainers, supplier-readiness sprints, and post-award mobilization. Each must produce something a client can inspect: a verified pursuit calendar, procurement-route memo, capability response, submission package, capacity dossier, or mobilization tracker. Price defined work in advance and document acceptance; a fixed fee does not by itself resolve contingent-fee, kickback, conflict, or procurement integrity issues. Under FAR Subpart 3.4 and applicable contract clauses, review the actual arrangement, solicitation, and any claimed bona fide employee or agency exception before accepting award-linked compensation. Never sell access to an official or imply influence over source selection. For each play, the matrix supplies a small fee unit and a fallback when the underlying procurement disappears. Those units illustrate individual engagement design, not additional revenue stacked on top of the canonical annual cases. Count an engagement once even when it uses several sourcing methods.

Build a weekly signal queue rather than an indiscriminate notice feed. On Monday, review expiring awards and municipal purchasing calendars; on Tuesday, examine new sources-sought notices and draft requirements; on Wednesday, reconcile amendments and bid tabs; reserve Thursday for supplier verification and Friday for client pursuit decisions. Public-record requests should target existing records, such as executed contracts or releasable evaluation summaries, not demand that staff create custom analyses. Federal FOIA, 5 U.S.C. 552, has exemptions and timing constraints; use each municipality's applicable records process for local documents. Do not schedule a proposal around an assumed disclosure date. Strong triggers include a funded replacement requirement, a published outreach event, repeated publicly documented bid failures, or a client lacking coverage for an identified location. Weak triggers include a large ceiling value, a vendor directory entry, or an old award without current demand evidence. Preserve original files and annotate in separate notes so an amendment can be traced to its source. A simple Airtable register or Excel workbook is adequate initially; automate alerts only after manually checking a sample of matched and missed opportunities against the same written search rules.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Read the Procurement Trail, Not Just the Notice**
> 
> Experienced bid desks earn their fees by finding verifiable operating friction before a submission deadline makes every correction expensive.
> - Compare the last two public bid tabs with current specifications; investigate scope changes before treating a price increase as available margin.

## 2. Qualify the Client, Procurement Route, and Delivery Structure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Score the paying client separately from the government opportunity. For the client, inspect current service capability, management attention, cash available for mobilization, relevant past performance, and willingness to pay for defined work regardless of award outcome. For the opportunity, score demand evidence at twenty points, procurement-route access at twenty, delivery capacity at twenty, client economics at fifteen, timing at fifteen, and evidence quality at ten. Pursue seventy-five or more, investigate sixty through seventy-four, and archive lower scores with a reason. These are internal operating thresholds, not measured industry predictors. Hard failures override the total: false eligibility, prohibited compensation, an inaccessible vehicle, missing mandatory capacity, or insufficient submission time cannot be averaged away. Verify the legal entity and UEI when relevant, review SAM.gov registration and exclusions, and confirm applicable size and program status using authoritative records and the solicitation's assigned NAICS. Municipal registration and certification rules are separate; federal status does not automatically satisfy them. Require a dated evidence link for every score. A relationship owner's optimism is not evidence, and an attractive contract ceiling cannot cure a client that cannot staff or fund the first month of performance.

For facility teaming, map every work element to the entity actually performing it, the supervisor controlling it, and the invoice that will support payment. FAR 52.219-14 and 13 CFR 125.6 can restrict subcontracting on covered set-asides; for covered service contracts, the general rule limits payments to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities to fifty percent of the amount paid by the government for contract performance, subject to applicable exceptions and measurement rules. Do not substitute a generic gross-margin calculation for that analysis. Determine which costs and entities count, and document the governing clause and program requirements. Review the ostensible subcontractor rule in 13 CFR 121.103 when a proposed subcontractor performs primary and vital requirements or creates unusual reliance. Where applicable, FAR 52.215-23 addresses excessive pass-through charges; merely forwarding invoices does not demonstrate useful coordination. A $200,000 client award and a $6,000 coordination engagement therefore remain two different economic records: only the earned $6,000 belongs in the operator's service revenue. If the structure fails, withdraw the noncompliant bid and offer disclosed subcontract performance on unrestricted work, with real responsibilities and independently supportable pricing.

Turn supplier diligence into a short evidence interview followed by document checks. Ask who supervises the site, where replacement workers come from, which locations the supplier already covers, how long its price remains valid, and which assumptions would change the quote. Verify insurance through appropriate channels, obtain client permission for reference checks, and reconcile staffing hours against the statement of work. Where federal service labor requirements apply, price the applicable wage determination and fringe obligations under the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and implementing rules before quoting labor-dependent work. Check separately for local wage, licensing, bonding, and background screening requirements. Never treat a low quote as evidence of an executable margin until these inputs agree. Record contractor availability as a dated commitment with conditions, not a permanent capability. Identify one replacement route for each critical role and test whether substitution requires buyer permission. Finally, screen the compensation chain for kickbacks under 41 U.S.C. 8702 and protect procurement information under 41 U.S.C. 2102. If someone offers confidential competitor pricing or internal evaluation material, stop the exchange, preserve the incident record securely, and escalate without using that material to shape a bid.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Accepting a Pursuit Mandate**
> 
> A high score never overrides an ineligible entity, improper payment arrangement, unsupported representation, or unworkable delivery plan.
> - Verify the paying entity, authorized signer, procurement route, and relevant eligibility using dated authoritative evidence.
> - Review compensation against contingent-fee restrictions, procurement integrity, anti-kickback requirements, and applicable local purchasing rules.
> - Reconcile prime control, subcontract workshare, supplier commitments, labor pricing, and any excessive pass-through restrictions.
> - Approve a written scope with acceptance criteria, payment milestones, confidentiality boundaries, and a stop-work procedure for changed requirements.

## 3. Run Evidence-Led Outreach That Converts into Paid Work  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Construct a named-account list from demonstrable fit, not purchased email volume. Begin with firms already delivering the chosen service family, companies appearing in relevant public bid tabs, and suppliers whose published capabilities match an identified geographic gap. Resolve each legal entity and record one appropriate business contact, the source of that contact, the demand trigger, and a specific deliverable you could sell. Lead with an evidence-based observation: 'The city published a facilities requirement covering three locations; your service area overlaps two. We can assess coverage, teaming, and submission requirements in a paid diagnostic.' Avoid suggesting that you represent the city. Use commercial email in accordance with 15 U.S.C. 7704, including accurate headers, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out process; honor opt-outs promptly and within the statutory period. Keep phone and platform outreach within applicable rules and the recipient's preferences. Government communications belong through the solicitation's designated channel, especially during restricted communication periods. The person buying your service is normally the supplier's owner, business-development lead, or operations manager, not an official being asked to steer an award toward your client or introduce privileged information into the pursuit.

Use a five-touch, fifteen-business-day sequence with a reason for every contact. Day one sends the public trigger and a two-sentence fit hypothesis. Day three uses a permitted business call to confirm who owns pursuit decisions. Day six sends one useful finding, such as a mandatory site visit or documented coverage gap. Day ten offers a twenty-minute qualification discussion with a sample deliverable. Day fifteen closes the sequence and offers an explicit way to decline further contact. Stop immediately after an opt-out or clear refusal; suppress duplicate campaigns across the team. Support inbound demand with a narrow landing page showing a redacted pursuit brief, a clear service scope, and one booking action. Publish source-linked procurement notes without client confidentiality breaches, copied proprietary specifications, or implied government endorsement. An APEX Accelerator can help a client understand readiness requirements; do not claim that publicly supported counseling is your proprietary service. Track inbound and outbound separately because referral leads behave differently from cold contacts. Attribute a mandate to its original source and record assisted touches without counting the same paying client twice. The purpose is qualified commercial conversations, not a flattering dashboard of impressions and opens.

For planning, model a mature ninety-day cohort of two hundred researched accounts, one hundred forty verified reachable contacts, twenty-eight substantive conversations, fourteen qualified opportunities, seven scoped proposals, and three signed paid mandates. The successive conversion assumptions are seventy percent, twenty percent, fifty percent, fifty percent, and approximately forty-three percent; the end-to-end mandate rate is one and a half percent. These are testable operating hypotheses, not observed market benchmarks. Early cohorts may produce no mandates, and the first three to ten wins remain a six-to-eighteen-month objective as references and procurement timing develop. Define a win as a signed service engagement with a funded initial milestone, then track government awards separately. A $4,000 diagnostic should specify the records reviewed, suitability assessment, supplier checks, delivery date, and acceptance process; later work requires a separate scope. When a prospect says it can search SAM.gov itself, agree and show the missing verification labor. When it demands payment only after award, explain the actual deliverable and review any alternative compensation structure before accepting it. When it asks for a guaranteed outcome, offer a bounded research product instead. Declining an unsuitable engagement preserves capacity for customers willing to purchase work you can control.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build a verified weekly account cohort
   - Research twenty paying-client candidates around one service family and two jurisdictions.
   - Attach the demand source, legal entity, contact source, eligibility route, and fit hypothesis before outreach.
2. Run the bounded contact sequence
   - Use the fifteen-business-day cadence and maintain one shared suppression list.
   - Record substantive conversations and refusal reasons; never treat email opens as purchase intent.
3. Score and scope the diagnostic
   - Apply the weighted scorecard and hard-stop checks before sending a proposal.
   - Specify deliverables, acceptance, fees, payment timing, and exclusions without implying government endorsement.
4. Deliver evidence and earn the next scope
   - Complete the initial milestone and request a documented acceptance decision.
   - Offer follow-on coordination only when the findings support a feasible pursuit and the client funds the next work package.

## 4. Turn Early Mandates into a Reconciled, Repeatable Bid Desk  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Reconcile every sourcing decision to the canonical fee business. Conservative revenue is $120,000, with $102,000 total costs and $18,000 owner net profit at fifteen percent, using two full-time employees and three contractors. Base revenue is $560,000, with $434,000 total costs and $126,000 owner net profit at twenty-two and a half percent, using the same team count. Scaled revenue is $1,000,000, with $700,000 total costs and $300,000 owner net profit at thirty percent, using three full-time employees and four contractors. Illustrative annual fee recipes are six clients at $20,000, fourteen at $40,000, and twenty at $50,000 respectively, with all amounts earned through delivered service periods and accepted work. They are alternative cases, not additive targets or a claim that every first-year client reaches a full annual fee. Individual play contributions exclude shared overhead and therefore cannot be substituted for these net margins. The Conservative cost envelope is particularly tight for its stated staffing: obtain actual compensation, contractor, insurance, and software quotes before deciding whether it is executable. If local costs exceed the envelope, report the shortfall rather than silently treating unpaid founder effort as free labor or changing the canonical profit figure.

Run delivery from a single pursuit record with controlled access and a named owner for each deadline. Store original notices, amendments, client representations, supplier quotes, scope versions, and acceptance records in an organized SharePoint or Google Drive workspace appropriate to the data involved. These ordinary tools are not automatic substitutes for contract-specific security requirements; assess any restricted information before receiving it. Use calendar reminders for the questions cutoff, required visits, pricing expiry, review date, and submission receipt. Automate notification and checklist creation, but require a person to resolve changed requirements and confirm the final package. Maintain an amendment acknowledgment log and a submission rehearsal that checks file limits, portal credentials, and authorized signers without transmitting a premature bid. If a supplier fails, compare the replacement against the original qualification evidence and applicable substitution rules. If a buyer cancels, pause unearned tasks and apply the engagement's cancellation terms. If a client delays payment, follow written collection and stop-work provisions rather than assuming the agency's payment timetable governs your separate service agreement. Award uncertainty, payment delays, and supplier failure deserve distinct incident codes because each requires a different change to sourcing, scope, or delivery.

Use the first six months to validate a niche and a repeatable paid diagnostic, not to hire ahead of assumed awards. Months one and two establish the signal register, supplier evidence, service scope, and first researched account cohorts. Months three through six test conversations and proposals while building references from completed work. Months seven through twelve concentrate on the strongest client segment and renew only engagements with actual recurring deliverables. Months thirteen through eighteen can extend the first three wins toward ten, subject to observed demand and delivery capacity. Review cohort conversion, acquisition effort, recognized fees, cash receipts, revision hours, and referral sources every month. Calculate acquisition cost from attributable outreach labor and spending divided by new paying clients; compare it with collected contribution, not a government contract's face value. The stated startup capital range is $25,000–$150,000 and monthly operating expense range is $5,000–$25,000; neither substitutes for a dated cash forecast. Retain enough runway for delayed buying cycles and model milestone collections explicitly. Expand only when repeatable service delivery, measured demand, and cash support the next capacity step. A smaller verified pipeline is more valuable than a large list of awards the business cannot lawfully support.
