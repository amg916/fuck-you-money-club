## 1. Define the Paid Work Before Calling Yourself a Broker  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Government contract brokering is a commercial shorthand, not a federal procurement license or a transferable right to an agency award. In this chapter, it means operating a specialist service business that helps qualified suppliers find solicitations, evaluate fit, assemble responses, coordinate legitimate teaming, and administer documented obligations. The customer usually is the supplier or prime contractor; the agency remains that customer's buyer. The service firm earns agreed fees for work performed rather than claiming the face value of contracts it touches. A $2 million municipal equipment award supported by a $12,000 proposal engagement produces at most $12,000 of service consideration for this operator, subject to performance and the applicable accounting treatment. It does not create $2 million of operator sales. If the firm instead signs as prime, controls promised goods or services, and carries delivery responsibility, it has entered a different contracting model requiring a separate principal-versus-agent assessment. Do not combine that model with this volume's service-fee scorecard. Begin each engagement by naming the contracting parties, deliverables, payment obligations, submission authority, and responsibility for every factual representation. Those boundaries determine what can be sold repeatedly without transferring unpriced delivery risk into a small bid desk.

The market supports five useful operator archetypes: intelligence desk, proposal studio, supplier qualification desk, teaming coordinator, and post-award administrator. Choose one initial buyer and one procurement category rather than advertising access to every government purchaser. A janitorial contractor needs wage-determination analysis, site-visit tracking, staffing evidence, and realistic mobilization assumptions; a distributor needs exact product equivalence, authorized sourcing, lead-time verification, and shipping terms. Those are different production systems. SAM.gov registration is generally the prospective federal contractor's responsibility under applicable requirements, and registration itself is free. Charge for documented preparation and maintenance work only when the customer understands that distinction. For municipal opportunities, verify the issuing jurisdiction's vendor registration, lobbying, disclosure, and procurement rules rather than copying federal clauses into every checklist. A durable engagement specifies monthly research coverage, a maximum number of evaluated opportunities, proposal complexity bands, response times, and exclusions. It also names who approves pricing and who signs certifications. A broker who uploads a proposal using somebody else's credentials has not created efficiency; they have obscured accountability. Use authorized portal roles where available and retain written submission instructions with the final package. Keep the customer's own designated signer responsible for representations and commitments.

Lawful fee design starts before lead generation. FAR Subpart 3.4 and FAR 52.203-5 address contingent fees for obtaining federal contracts, with defined treatment for bona fide employees and established commercial or selling agencies. The exceptions are fact-dependent, not a label that any consultant can adopt. Prefer scoped retainers and fixed deliverable fees, while reviewing the actual arrangement and applicable solicitation terms; changing an invoice description does not cure improper influence. Procurement integrity restrictions under 41 U.S.C. 2102 and FAR 3.104 prohibit specified conduct involving contractor bid or proposal information and source-selection information before award. The Anti-Kickback Act separately addresses improper payments connected with favorable treatment under federal prime contracts and subcontracts. A referral payment to a prime's employee is therefore not equivalent to paying an independent researcher for documented work. Set-aside eligibility, limitations on subcontracting, organizational conflicts, and applicable excessive pass-through charge clauses can defeat otherwise attractive structures. Map each rule to the actual transaction instead of describing all teaming as prohibited. Your product is a reliable operating process, not agency influence. Never imply that a personal relationship can override competition, responsibility findings, evaluation criteria, or the contracting officer's authority.

## 2. Map Seven Plays Into a Narrow, Defensible Bid Desk  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Seven plays fit this business. The supplied mapped identifier is public_record_api_intelligence; the other six labels here describe chapter-specific operating workstreams rather than additional registry identifiers. Public-record intelligence converts lawful public signals into qualified supplier conversations and procurement forecasts. County dockets, probate registries, unclaimed property ledgers, and public APIs can reveal ownership transitions, unresolved administrative work, or business events, but none automatically proves a procurement need or an uncontested claim. Connect each signal to an identifiable service requirement, purchasing authority, and legitimate customer. For government sales, budget hearings, capital plans, expiring awards, and agency procurement forecasts usually provide more direct evidence. An illustrative batch of 400 records yielding 40 relevant events, eight supplier conversations, and one $2,000 monthly engagement creates $24,000 of annual fees only if twelve months of service are delivered. It creates no revenue merely because a lead exists. Record source URLs, retrieval dates, permitted use, and confidence levels. If APIs fail or access terms change, switch to scheduled bulk FOIA or state public-record exports, official data feeds, and low-frequency cached queues. FOIA processing time and exemptions make it a research channel, not a dependable last-minute bid feed.

Solicitation triage, bid-calendar operations, and proposal production turn signals into bounded work. For triage, screen 120 notices monthly against geography, NAICS alignment, capacity, incumbent evidence, and mandatory qualifications; twelve credible matches and four customer-authorized pursuits are a better output than 120 forwarded emails. At an illustrative $2,000 monthly service price, the unit is qualified coverage, not a promised award. Bid-calendar operations track questions, amendments, site visits, bonding, quote validity, and submission timestamps. Twenty live pursuits requiring fifteen minutes of weekly maintenance consume five hours before exception handling. Price that workload inside the retainer and reduce coverage when amendment volume exceeds capacity. Proposal production can sell an $8,000 package with forty planned production hours, equivalent to $200 per planned hour before overhead and rework. Separate technical writing, cost inputs, attachments, and final assembly so customer delays remain visible. The fallback for a missing mandatory credential is a documented no-bid decision, not creative wording. The fallback for an overloaded production queue is a narrower pursuit count or a vetted specialist under a bounded assignment. These plays strengthen one another only when they share a single opportunity record and explicit customer decisions about what deserves expensive production time.

Supplier qualification, prime/sub teaming coordination, and post-award administration complete the seven-play map. A $4,000 qualification package covering eight suppliers budgets $500 per supplier before shared overhead; verify capability, required representations, capacity, source authenticity, exclusions, and references rather than collecting attractive brochures. If no supplier meets the requirements, deliver the documented finding and stop the pursuit. A $5,000 teaming coordination milestone requiring twenty-five hours yields $200 per planned hour before expenses; define scope allocation, pricing dependencies, confidentiality, and negotiation ownership without promising award. If the proposed prime lacks required eligibility or cannot perform its required share, restructure lawfully or abandon the arrangement. Post-award administration at $3,000 monthly for fifteen planned hours likewise starts at $200 per planned hour, but disputed acceptance can erase that capacity quickly. Bound invoice support, deliverable tracking, and modification logs; leave changes to authorized contract personnel. Use named portals as workflow infrastructure, not as substitutes for category knowledge. The comparison matrix shows sixteen distinct entry points. Its prices are illustrative service packages, not published platform rates or incremental revenue to add automatically to the scorecard. Select a lane where repeated specifications and recurring buyers let evidence compound across engagements without copying confidential client material into another customer's proposal.

## 3. Reconcile Earned Fees, Cash Timing, and the Three Profit Cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use an earned-fee ledger that separates contracted consideration, cash collected, services performed, recognized revenue, and remaining obligations. Signing a twelve-month $24,000 engagement does not automatically create $24,000 of current revenue. A monthly stand-ready service may be recognized over its service period when the applicable accounting criteria are met; a discrete proposal package follows the actual performance obligation and transfer of control. Customer acceptance provisions matter when substantive, but an invoice date alone does not establish performance. An advance for future work generally remains a liability until earned. Track service credits, refund rights, disputes, and variable consideration rather than assuming every invoice becomes permanent revenue. Keep client award values in a separate operational field so dashboards cannot quietly convert procurement volume into service sales. A $10 million indefinite-delivery contract ceiling also differs from obligated orders and from a supplier's realized revenue. None is this operator's recognized revenue merely because the bid desk helped prepare the submission. Establish these definitions in the chart of accounts and reporting specification before building automation. That discipline prevents a visually impressive pipeline from concealing weak collections, unfinished engagements, and fees that remain subject to valid customer credits or contractual refunds.

The canonical Conservative case is $120,000 annual gross service revenue, a 15% net margin, and $18,000 annual owner net profit with two full-time people and three contractors. One illustrative earned-fee bridge is four clients at $2,000 monthly for twelve months, producing $96,000, plus six completed $4,000 packages, producing $24,000. Total modeled costs must therefore equal $102,000. The Base case is $560,000 revenue, a 22.5% margin, and $126,000 profit with the same roster: eight clients at $4,000 monthly produce $384,000, and twenty-two $8,000 packages produce $176,000. Costs equal $434,000. The Scaled case uses three full-time people and four contractors: ten clients at $6,000 monthly produce $720,000, while twenty-eight $10,000 packages produce $280,000. Revenue reaches $1,000,000, costs equal $700,000, and the 30% margin yields $300,000 owner profit. These bridges assume full-year delivery and distinct package work outside retainer scope; they are not acquisition forecasts. Contractor counts describe available people rather than full-time-equivalent utilization. The Conservative cost envelope is particularly tight for two full-time roles. Validate actual compensation and overhead before treating that roster as affordable, and preserve the canonical scenario separately from any local staffing revision.

Startup capital of $20,000 to $90,000 and a six-to-eighteen-month time to first deal demand deliberate runway planning. The stated $5,000 to $25,000 monthly operating-expense range must not be confused with an all-inclusive cap on annual scenario costs. Base and Scaled total costs average approximately $36,167 and $58,333 monthly respectively, including whatever delivery costs the model requires. If the stated operating range is interpreted as fully inclusive, that interpretation conflicts with the canonical totals; keep the distinction visible instead of forcing a false reconciliation. At $5,000 monthly burn, $25,000 provides five months before setup costs, shorter than the earliest stated deal timing. At $25,000 monthly burn, even $150,000 supplies only six months before setup costs. Build a weekly cash schedule and sell paid readiness work where useful, rather than funding all effort until an award. Federal prompt-payment provisions do not automatically make the service firm's supplier customer pay on the agency's timetable. Specify deposits, earned milestones, invoice disputes, and suspension rights. Under stress, pause unfunded work, reduce pursuit counts, and revalidate collections before hiring. Award uncertainty, payment delays, and supplier failure are separate exposures and require separate reserves, contract terms, and operating responses.

## 4. Build a Small-Team Moat With Evidence, Permissions, and Controlled Workload  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A durable small-team moat is the accumulated ability to reject bad pursuits quickly and deliver good ones without reconstructing every fact. Start with a customer-owned evidence library containing approved capability statements, past-performance references, licenses, resumes, insurance evidence, and sourcing documentation. Tag each artifact with its owner, expiration, permitted uses, and verification date. Store public research separately from confidential client materials. Microsoft SharePoint can manage controlled documents, while a carefully permissioned Airtable base or PostgreSQL database can track opportunities and deadlines. Tool selection matters less than access separation, backups, and a reliable amendment process. Use SAM.gov alerts and official feeds for discovery, calendar automation for dates, and checklists for recurring requirements; do not automate factual certifications or assume generated text accurately describes customer capabilities. Require source evidence for every material claim. An analyst can prepare a draft, a specialist can review technical fit, and the customer's authorized signer can approve commitments. For controlled information, use only environments and handling procedures permitted by the relevant requirements. A generic shared workspace does not satisfy every contract's security obligations. When requirements exceed the desk's supported environment, narrow the engagement to permissible material or decline that portion.

Operational discipline begins with an explicit bid/no-bid record. Capture mandatory eligibility, delivery capacity, credible pricing inputs, submission feasibility, and customer funding before assigning writing hours. A scored pursuit sheet should support judgment, not manufacture precision from weak assumptions. Track qualified opportunities, authorized pursuits, submitted responses, awards, earned fees, overdue receivables, rework hours, and customer concentration as separate measures. An award rate can improve while owner profit deteriorates if the desk accepts increasingly complex packages at unchanged fees. Likewise, a low award rate can coexist with useful, well-delivered research services, but persistent customer losses usually damage retention. Review both outcomes and contracted service quality. Protect procurement integrity by prohibiting solicitation of protected competitor information and recording the source of unusual market intelligence. Check organizational conflicts under FAR Subpart 9.5 when the desk's roles could create biased ground rules, impaired objectivity, or unequal access to information. Do not advise competing clients on the same live procurement without addressing confidentiality, contractual restrictions, and actual conflicts. If a customer requests an unsupported certification, stop that submission path and resolve the evidence gap. False statements and claims can create exposure far beyond the value of a proposal fee.

Launch with a ninety-day evidence-building cycle rather than a promise to close an award within ninety days. During the first month, select one category, interview qualified suppliers, review recent solicitations, and test a paid readiness package. During the second month, run the full workflow on a small number of authorized pursuits and measure actual production hours, customer input delays, amendment volume, and correction rates. During the third month, adjust scope and pricing from those observations, then decide whether recurring coverage has enough demand to justify another hire. Preserve the stated six-to-eighteen-month first-deal horizon when planning contract-award-dependent activity. Require a supplier contingency plan before submission: alternate sources must satisfy the solicitation, preserve quote validity, and remain consistent with the offered technical solution. After award, route changes through the authorized contractual process instead of letting informal buyer requests expand the client's obligations. Close each engagement with evidence of delivered services, billing reconciliation, lessons learned, and the next renewal decision. The strongest desk can explain exactly why a pursuit was selected, what it produced, what the customer approved, and how the resulting fee was earned. That audit trail is commercially valuable even when the agency selects another supplier.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Qualify the customer and transaction
   - Record customer authority, procurement category, scope, fee basis, and applicable restrictions.
   - Confirm whether the desk provides services or assumes prime obligations; document conflicts, eligibility concerns, and customer funding before production begins.
2. Create the opportunity evidence record
   - Archive the solicitation, amendments, portal instructions, deadlines, and qualification decision.
   - Assign an owner to every mandatory requirement and identify which customer facts still require verification before a pursuit can proceed.
3. Produce and authorize the response
   - Assemble verified content, reconcile supplier inputs, and obtain authorized customer decisions.
   - Check attachment integrity, quote validity, and final amendment coverage; retain submission evidence without assuming a receipt proves substantive responsiveness.
4. Close delivery and reconcile earned fees
   - Document completed obligations, resolve credits, update receivables, and capture operating lessons.
   - Separate award outcomes from service performance, exclude client contract values from revenue, and revise capacity assumptions using recorded production hours.
