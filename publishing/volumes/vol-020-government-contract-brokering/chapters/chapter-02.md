## The Practitioner Consensus: The Registration Stack Every Broker Must Clear

Every practitioner who covers registration describes the same identity stack, and the order matters. Neil McDonnell breaks it down: the TIN is the umbrella term, an SSN identifies the person, an EIN identifies the company, and he tells operators never to post the EIN on a website because it is effectively a social security number for the business. SAM.gov then issues the Unique Entity ID, which McDonnell says exists mainly to mask the EIN while making the firm visible in every government tool [QIEmDXcWhSE @ 00:03:05]. He adds that a company holds exactly one UEI but can carry multiple CAGE codes, which is what he did when he opened offices in Duncan, Oklahoma and in Virginia and simply requested another CAGE for the second location [QIEmDXcWhSE @ 00:04:04]. FedOpp pins the UEI at 12 characters, assigned the moment the legal name and address validate, with a Federal Service Desk ticket available from that same page if validation fails [2Bp78kNCVGI @ 00:02:10]. The CAGE code is the last layer: Colleen Spiegel of SEMSCIO calls it a five-character alphanumeric identifier issued by the Defense Logistics Agency to U.S. entities, with foreign firms receiving an NCAGE code instead, and says a valid CAGE or NCAGE is needed to finish SAM.gov registration and bid on contracts [rRWCLLWqrV0 @ 00:00:33].

The practitioners also agree on what not to chase. Eric Coffie is blunt that nobody needs a CAGE code before registering: once the entity information is submitted for processing, the government assigns the code and adds it to the SAM record, and the registrant does 'not a thing' beyond finishing the SAM flow [wcz-GpTaAp8 @ 00:01:36]. The lever the operator does control is the registration purpose. FedOpp says to choose Register Entity rather than a UEI-only record and to select All Awards, because an ID alone will not let a firm bid as a prime [2Bp78kNCVGI @ 00:01:48]. Kizzy Parks makes the identical call on screen, telling viewers they are interested in all awards because a CAGE code is required to obtain a prime government contract, and that nearly everyone watching should answer no to the government-entity question [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:01:59]. Her pre-flight kit is short and deliberately forgiving: business banking details, or plain personal checking if no business account exists yet; a copy of the IRS-issued EIN document; a physical address affiliated with the business, whether it is a for-profit or a nonprofit; and at least one NAICS code, because every one of these items can be changed after the CAGE lands [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:00:40]. The shared message is to stop polishing and push the record through to submission.

Beyond the base registration, the certification practitioners share one hard rule: SAM.gov comes first. Nick Waters states that no firm can be federally certified without a SAM.gov registration plus a UEI and CAGE code, and that being a small business is the next gate, since all four federal programs run directly through the SBA [sqfiBzNcTPI @ 00:01:40]. Derek James of GovKidMethod gives the same order, telling viewers to have the business entity registered in SAM before applying for any set-aside through the SBA certification site, where self-certification is usually easier and faster in his experience [LnPiGkUTs2E @ 00:05:54]. On the money behind those tags, the speakers converge on a 23% governmentwide small business goal. Neil McDonnell frames it against a small-business-eligible pool of roughly $500 to $550 billion a year rather than the multi-trillion-dollar federal budget, with subcategories carved out of that 23% for women-owned firms and 8(a) firms at 5% each [9sogImn1jJQ @ 00:04:16]. Squared Compass cites the same 23% overall target for all small businesses [oSSAuniLuDI @ 00:01:05]. McDonnell's practical read is that these are tags, not strategy: they shrink the competition pool when something is set aside, but tightly focused core competency and past performance are what actually win.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers: Pushing Through to the CAGE Code

The fast path starts with paperwork, then Login.gov. FedOpp lays out the front end: pull the SAM.gov Entity Registration Checklist and gather the legal business name and physical address exactly as the IRS has them, with no post office box, plus incorporation date and state, Taxpayer Identification Number, bank details, NAICS codes and points of contact, before building a Login.gov account with a reachable email, a password of 12 or more characters, and multi-factor authentication [2Bp78kNCVGI @ 00:00:28]. Kizzy Parks says any email works, Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook or Hotmail, as long as the operator can access it, because two-factor texts and one-time passcodes gate almost every save [FWfV2m3K4jY @ 00:00:39]. Once inside, she selects Get Started, creates a new entity, confirms a physical address, answers no to an existing CAGE, and enters the year and state of incorporation rather than the state where the owner currently lives. In her 2024 walkthrough the UEI came back roughly three minutes in, at which point the operator can pause or keep pushing straight toward the CAGE [FWfV2m3K4jY @ 00:05:56]. Her other warning is operational: SAM typically sends zero reminder emails for a half-finished registration, so once the process starts, log in at least weekly and use Update Entity to correct misspellings or add an approved DBA [eIFlVzfusWo @ 00:02:23].

Core Data and Assertions are where Parks's speed-over-perfection doctrine shows. If there is no business bank account, she enters personal checking and swaps it out once the CAGE is assigned [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:11:52]. She tells everyone to answer yes to accepting credit cards, whether through a merchant account, Stripe or Square, because it is an easy button for the government and often how micro-purchases get paid [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:12:15]. For NAICS, one code is enough to clear the gate. She entered 541611 as a common default and recounts winning an order for Oculus headsets she bought on Amazon and flipped to the Air Force, listed under a computer assembly code she never would have guessed, which is why codes get added after the fact [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:15:33]. With nothing to report on size metrics, she types one dollar in receipts and one employee. Neil McDonnell runs the same playbook: he answers yes to credit cards and yes to Disaster Response everywhere, including Guam, because saying yes keeps the operator on government outreach while the decision to decline is made situation by situation, and he enters zero receipts and one employee rather than pretending to be bigger [QIEmDXcWhSE @ 00:15:08].

The back half of the form is reps and certs, and the practitioners answer them tactically. Parks selects yes on bidding for DoD-issued or DoD-funded contracts unless the operator truly wants no Army, Navy or Air Force work [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:22:42]. On foreign end products she picks 'vendor will provide' because Government Publishing Office work often runs on Alibaba-sourced goods from China and buyers will ask for images and origin details [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:20:13]. She also answers yes on GAAP-compliant financial statements even without QuickBooks or Xero, reasoning that the question asks whether you will follow the principles, not whether you have an account. FedOpp requires three mandatory points of contact, Accounts Receivable, Electronic Business and Government Business, and warns that validation emails go to the Government Business contact, so that inbox must be watched [2Bp78kNCVGI @ 00:03:30]. After submission, the CAGE side verifies the physical address. Parks says an email asking for proof of address or company details may arrive within days, must be answered within three business days, and usually carries a phone number worth calling [FWfV2m3K4jY @ 00:23:47]. FedOpp tells operators to allow at least ten business days, check spam, and renew every 365 days.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Stage the documents before logging in
   - Gather the IRS EIN letter, proof of physical address, bank routing and account numbers, and one NAICS code.
   - Keep the IRS-issued legal business document and a lease or similar address evidence on hand at all times, because the entity data is matched against IRS records [Kizzy Parks — eIFlVzfusWo @ 00:03:40].
2. Create Login.gov and validate the entity
   - Set up Login.gov with MFA, choose Register Entity and All Awards, and validate the legal name and address.
   - Once the name and address validate, SAM.gov assigns the 12-character Unique Entity ID; if validation fails, open a Federal Service Desk ticket from that page [FedOpp — 2Bp78kNCVGI @ 00:02:10].
3. Push through Core Data, Assertions and reps
   - Answer no to an existing CAGE, yes to credit cards, DoD and disaster response, enter one NAICS and minimal size metrics.
   - All of this information can be revised after the fact, so the goal is reaching the CAGE code, not perfection [Kizzy Parks — wNMt73DArFc @ 00:09:39].
4. Submit, answer DLA, verify and renew
   - Respond to the address-verification email within three business days, confirm the CAGE, and calendar renewal.
   - Answer the proof-of-address email and call the listed number if needed [Kizzy Parks — FWfV2m3K4jY @ 00:23:47], then check status by UEI or CAGE and renew every 365 days [FedOpp — 2Bp78kNCVGI @ 00:04:47].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split is over the capability statement. Kizzy Parks tells beginners flatly that they do not need one to win any work. She calls it a flyer for outreach, partnering, and marketing, and says to build it after the first win unless you are deliberately chasing the subcontract route [GA5_o6kPhnw @ 00:08:14]. Yet she credits a capability statement for one of her own wins. She emailed it to the point of contact on a USDA forecast, got flown to Maryland for a meeting, and walked away about a month and a half later with a $4 million sole source award [GA5_o6kPhnw @ 00:09:51]. Nick Waters GovCon reverses the order for certified firms. He says to build a very nice capability statement first and use it to pitch large primes that must carry a subcontracting plan, presenting yourself as the certified firm that helps them meet their obligations [sqfiBzNcTPI @ 00:17:09]. Kizzy's one-pager breakdown lands between the two. She treats the document as a resume that gets you in the door for one-on-one capability briefs with small business reps, contracting officers, and program managers. You email it as a PDF before the call, but it is never the thing that wins the job [K_zNUytFOQo @ 00:02:22]. The operator read: if your first lane is open-market bidding, skip it for now. If your first lane is teaming or capability briefs, you need it to get in.

The second fault line is how fast to push through SAM.gov. Kizzy Parks runs a speed-first playbook to reach the CAGE code. She chooses 'vendor will provide' on foreign end products, enters one dollar of receipts, and selects yes on GAAP-compliant financials even with no QuickBooks account. Her reasoning is that every field can be revised later, and 'it's not about being perfect it's taking action' [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:10:02]. Neil McDonnell calls clicking yes on everything 'incredibly irresponsible' and insists on taking two seconds to read the FAR provision behind each representation. He also argues that an owner should be a salesperson who hires FAR experts later, not a FAR expert [QIEmDXcWhSE @ 00:13:56]. The same two operators split on the physical address. Kizzy says it has to be a place where someone can physically meet you in an office setting, whether that is a house, apartment, condo, or virtual working space with an office. If it is not, expect an email asking for more information or a flat rejection [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:27:14]. Neil is deliberately testing a virtual physical address on his brand-new company. He concedes it may fail for HUBZone, but says that for general registration 'physical address really who cares it's a virtual world' [QIEmDXcWhSE @ 00:09:29]. Choose your approach knowing which of these risks you are taking on.

The numbers collide on whether certifications are worth it. Squared Compass pulls 2024 data showing government-wide HUBZone spend at 0.44% against a 3% goal, with DoD at 0.38% and HUD at 0.24%. Its conclusion is that for many companies the juice is not worth the squeeze, unless HUBZone is the only certification they can get [oSSAuniLuDI @ 00:02:50]. Diamond Hunter Enterprises, which runs a woman-owned firm with more than 420 employees, says the opposite. She calls 8(a) and HUBZone 'where the money resides' and says 8(a) sole source runs up to 4.5 million in services and 7 million in manufacturing. She adds that the power of these certifications has not diminished, despite what others say on YouTube [-0giDdnv0RA @ 00:04:00]. GovKidMethod says he cannot recommend 8(a) enough. He also highly recommends HUBZone for its 10% price evaluation preference in full and open competition [LnPiGkUTs2E @ 00:17:31]. Neil McDonnell lands in the middle. HUBZone is his favorite program because it targets a community rather than an individual owner. Still, he tells small firms the designations are 'just tags' and not to dwell on them, and to compete on core competency instead [9sogImn1jJQ @ 00:08:42]. Even the 8(a) sole source ceiling differs, with GovKidMethod quoting up to 4 million per contract against Diamond's 4.5 million for services.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The first losses happen inside the registration itself, and most are self-inflicted typos. The taxpayer name has to match the IRS record exactly, and Kizzy Parks had her one-time passcode verification fail because she typed a period after her middle initial. She also had a date rejected for not using double digits [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:07:15]. On another registration she hit the same wall by adding a period after 'Dr' [FWfV2m3K4jY @ 00:09:42]. The bigger trap is silence. Kizzy reports that you will typically get zero emails telling you it is time to continue entity validation, so she logs in at least weekly once the process starts [eIFlVzfusWo @ 00:02:23]. After you submit, the CAGE desk checks your physical address and may ask questions about your company. She warns that you must answer that email within three business days and should call the number at the bottom rather than freeze [FWfV2m3K4jY @ 00:23:28]. Speed varies a lot by step. Kizzy pulled a UEI in about three minutes, but the CAGE code is only issued after that address verification clears. The economics are simple: a stalled CAGE code means you cannot bid as a prime. Put a weekly login on the calendar, and keep the IRS letter and the lease or proof of residence on hand before you start.

Security failures cost real money. Kizzy Parks explains that SAM.gov holds the bank account every prime payment goes to. People have created dummy accounts to get in and change the banking details, and then 'poof your money is diverted to them.' That is why she is cautious about letting a third party register for you [wNMt73DArFc @ 00:03:30]. Neil McDonnell flags a leak beginners create themselves: posting the EIN on a website because no one told them not to. He treats the EIN like a Social Security number for the company and notes the UEI exists precisely to mask it [QIEmDXcWhSE @ 00:02:33]. Maintenance is the slow failure. FedOpp says to renew every 365 days and update the record whenever your address, bank, or contacts change. It also says never to request a second UEI, and warns that an expired registration can block awards [2Bp78kNCVGI @ 00:05:15]. When a CAGE code will not show up in a lookup, Federal Processing Registry lists the usual causes: an expired registration, incorrect data on file, or a submission that was never completed. The fix is to update the SAM.gov profile or contact DLA support [lcWf2_8nWcc @ 00:01:15]. Treat the SAM profile like a bank login, because that is effectively what it is.

Certification traps hit after approval as often as before. GovKidMethod warns that going over the three-year average size standard on your primary NAICS code costs you your small business status. In 8(a), it can get you kicked out of the program for growing too quickly, so he tells winners to spread contracts across different NAICS codes [LnPiGkUTs2E @ 00:22:47]. HUBZone status is fragile too. Firms must notify the SBA of material changes, such as a new office or falling below the 35% employee residency minimum, and the SBA may visit unannounced to check [LnPiGkUTs2E @ 00:20:45]. Nick Waters GovCon describes male owners who list their wife as majority owner while she works a 9-to-5, and says the SBA is getting very good at catching it. The woman must own at least 51%, run the day-to-day operations, typically be the highest paid, and show 40 hours a week on paper [sqfiBzNcTPI @ 00:08:56]. Kizzy Parks adds a marketing failure from her own career. A CDC small business rep praised her patriotic, freshly branded capability statement as pretty, then tore it apart because it had no NAICS codes. Pride turned into embarrassment in the middle of an in-person meeting [K_zNUytFOQo @ 00:08:54]. Content beats design every time.
