## 1. Sell to the Budget Owner Who Absorbs the Failure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start by identifying the legal customer, the budget owner, and the person who can accept your work; they are often three different parties. A small prime's president may want more bids, their controller may control vendor payments, and their proposal manager may certify delivery. The government contracting officer normally pays the awarded contractor, not an outside adviser hired by that contractor. A contracting officer's representative may inspect performance without possessing authority to change price or obligate additional funds. Under FAR 1.602-1, contracting officers bind the government only within delegated authority. Therefore, a friendly agency introduction, purchase forecast, or program manager email cannot substitute for an authorized contract. For a private contractor engagement, obtain the customer's executed service order, purchasing authorization, billing address, and accounts-payable contact before starting. For a direct municipal engagement, inspect the actual charter, purchasing ordinance, appropriation, and delegation documents rather than transplanting federal rules. Separate signature authority from banking authority: the person requesting a supplier setup form may have neither. Your first sales deliverable is a one-page authority map connecting the buyer's operational problem to an enforceable payment obligation and a named acceptance owner.

The strongest buyer is paying to remove a measurable bottleneck, not to purchase influence over an award. A proposal director sees an approaching deadline and insufficient reviewer capacity; a CFO sees delivered work trapped behind rejected invoices; a supplier manager sees a subcontractor whose missing records threaten the prime's performance. Translate each problem into controllable service units. A $5,000 bid package might include a solicitation crosswalk, amendment log, pricing workbook checks, and submission evidence, while leaving technical certifications with the customer. If it replaces 40 internal hours budgeted at $150 each, the comparison is $6,000 of internal capacity against a $5,000 external fee, not a promised contract win. Similarly, a $2,500 acceptance-evidence package can be evaluated against the customer's documented rework hours and collection delay. Do not claim that assembling documents creates statutory eligibility or compels an agency to pay. Ask buyers which rejected document, missed amendment, or unresolved discrepancy caused their last loss. Their answer reveals the acceptance test they will actually fund. Procurement anxiety supports a sale only when your scope converts that anxiety into specific work, bounded responsibility, and inspectable evidence that another employee can verify.

Use seven chapter-level commercial mappings: capture retainers, bid packages, supplier onboarding, acceptance evidence, municipal readiness, teaming administration, and sponsored directory placements. These are service offers, not seven permissions to intermediate public money. A $4,000 monthly capture retainer buys monitored opportunities and a maintained bid calendar; a $3,000 teaming assignment buys documented workshare and responsibility coordination, not an award-dependent introduction. The directory offer is the specified ego_bait_directory_badge_arbitrage play, monetized through advertising sold separately from rankings. Each offer needs its own payer and deliverable because the advertising buyer may have no procurement relationship with any listed agency. For federal opportunities, screen contingent compensation against FAR Subpart 3.4 and applicable FAR 52.203-5 terms; bona fide employee and established commercial or selling agency exceptions require actual factual support. Renaming a success fee as consulting does not change its substance. Fixed fees also do not sanitize improper influence, kickbacks, or misuse of protected information. When the customer insists on paying only after an award, offer a smaller prepaid diagnostic or decline that compensation structure. Preserve a commercially useful service relationship without making the public procurement decision your payment trigger.

## 2. Separate the Service Invoice from the Government Disbursement  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Draw two money paths before selecting a payment method. The agency-to-prime path follows the public contract, acceptance requirements, proper-invoice rules, and designated disbursement system. The prime-to-adviser path follows your separate service agreement unless you are directly contracted by the agency. A $2 million government award remains the contractor's contract value; a $5,000 bid-desk invoice is your service revenue when earned under the applicable accounting policy. Never route the full award through your account merely to extract a fee. Federal electronic payments generally operate under 31 CFR Part 208, while the applicable contract may use FAR 52.232-33 and banking information maintained through SAM.gov. Invoice submission still depends on the agency and contract: Treasury's Invoice Processing Platform and DoD's Procurement Integrated Enterprise Environment are examples, not interchangeable destinations. Municipal systems have their own vendor registration and remittance instructions. Map the purchase order, invoice identifier, receiving record, and remittance reference so the controller can reconcile each payment without guessing. A remittance notice is not bank settlement, and bank settlement does not establish that your performance obligations are complete. Track invoiced, accepted, paid, and earned amounts as separate fields.

The eight commonly proposed rails combine actual transfer methods with commercial arrangements, so compare them on custody, finality, and release authority rather than calling them equivalent. A wire can move a deposit quickly but provides no automatic dispute adjudication. ACH is usually efficient for recurring business payments, but return exposure depends on transaction type, authorization, timing, and applicable rules. Escrow introduces an independent holder and a written release mechanism; it does not manufacture an agency appropriation or override contractual assignment restrictions. Retainers and milestone holds define when money is due, while an underlying wire, ACH transfer, or card transaction moves it. For example, split a $5,000 bid package into a $2,500 deposit and $2,500 payable upon documented delivery. If the customer needs a holdback, cap it at an agreed amount tied to an explicit checklist instead of waiting indefinitely for an award. A platform checkout through Stripe or Square can simplify a $500 directory subscription, but card disputes remain possible. Disclose renewal terms, retain placement evidence, and provide a clear cancellation process. The checkout receipt should identify advertising services, not imply that the buyer purchased public certification or preferred access to government decision-makers.

Public payment timing requires contract-specific analysis. The Prompt Payment Act, including 31 U.S.C. 3903, and 5 CFR Part 1315 establish federal payment requirements and interest mechanisms; they do not make every private service invoice payable on a universal schedule. FAR 52.232-25, when applicable, links payment administration to proper invoices and acceptance conditions. FAR 52.232-40 addresses accelerated payments to small business subcontractors without creating a new Prompt Payment Act entitlement or automatically rewriting the subcontract's payment terms. If a prime offers factoring as your collection solution, identify whose receivable is being sold. An assignment of federal contract proceeds may implicate 31 U.S.C. 3727, 41 U.S.C. 6305, and FAR Subpart 32.8; ordinary broker instructions cannot replace those requirements. Financing also introduces fees, reserves, recourse, and dilution risk. On an illustrative $50,000 eligible receivable, an 85% advance supplies $42,500, leaving $7,500 before reserves and charges. That advance is liquidity against the receivable, not new service revenue. When eligibility or assignment is disputed, use a smaller prepaid service scope rather than assuming a financier will cure weak documentation or an unenforceable payment claim.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Map both obligations
   - Separate the agency's contractor obligation from the contractor's service-provider obligation.
   - Record the legal payers, contract identifiers, acceptance owners, invoice destinations, and applicable payment terms for each path.
2. Verify the account instructions
   - Confirm beneficiary details through an independently established customer contact.
   - Require separate review for bank changes, preserve callback evidence, and never treat a forwarded email as sufficient verification.
3. Attach delivery evidence
   - Submit the invoice with the exact evidence required by the service order.
   - Include the purchase-order reference, delivered version, acceptance checklist, agreed milestone, and any documented customer exceptions.
4. Reconcile cleared funds
   - Match bank settlement to the invoice and update earned revenue separately.
   - Track deposits, holds, processor fees, returns, credits, and remaining receivables without counting client contract proceeds as operator revenue.

## 3. Protect Fees with Evidence, Credit Limits, and Lawful Workshare  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Fee protection begins with scope architecture, not an intimidating noncircumvention paragraph. Define the customer entities covered, named deliverables, customer dependencies, permitted use, price, acceptance criteria, change procedure, and collection terms. A bid-package engagement can require written identification of missing checklist items within five business days, followed by a specified correction process; the enforceability and effect of silence must be checked under the governing agreement and applicable law. Preserve version history in SharePoint or a controlled document repository, plus delivery receipts and the customer's requested changes. Tie the final invoice to delivery of the contracted service, not the government's award decision. Use a narrowly defined confidentiality and work-product license arrangement to protect research, templates, and authorized introductions without restricting lawful public competition. Any noncircumvention provision needs a defined relationship, duration, and legitimate scope; it cannot confer ownership of a publicly posted solicitation. Avoid percentage tails on government awards disguised as protection against bypass. If the prime brings the work in-house, collect for completed milestones and the expressly purchased capacity, subject to the agreement, rather than claiming an unearned slice of future public spending. This turns a potentially emotional bypass dispute into a narrower evidentiary collection problem.

Credit-screen the actual payer before extending terms. Confirm legal existence through the relevant secretary of state, match the contracting entity to its tax and payment records, and inspect exclusions where relevant through SAM.gov. Active registration is not evidence of solvency. USAspending.gov can help test claims about federal award history, but obligations and reported awards are not available cash. Request recent receivables aging, customer concentration, references from comparable service vendors, and evidence of the authorized purchasing process. Review public liens, litigation, and bankruptcy records through appropriate official systems and PACER where useful; interpret findings with the entity and jurisdiction correctly matched. Establish exposure limits in dollars. For a new $5,000 package, a $2,500 deposit limits the unpaid contract balance to $2,500 before approved changes. A $4,000 retainer billed after service can become $8,000 of receivables when a second month begins unpaid, with additional unbilled work increasing exposure. Stop new discretionary work at the contractual threshold and notify the customer before suspension. For essential existing obligations, follow the agreed termination and transition provisions rather than abruptly withholding files the customer already owns. Credit policy should protect cash without creating a fresh performance breach.

Lawful teaming requires an actual performance role. Under FAR 9.6, contractor team arrangements can organize complementary capabilities, but the label does not excuse a shell prime that contributes no meaningful value. FAR 52.215-22 and 52.215-23 address excessive pass-through charges when applicable; check their inclusion and the transaction facts rather than asserting a universal prohibition on subcontracting. Small-business set-asides add distinct requirements under 13 CFR 125.6 and, where incorporated, FAR 52.219-14. For covered services, the general limitation concerns amounts paid to firms that are not similarly situated, with contract-category rules and specified exclusions requiring careful calculation. Review affiliation and ostensible subcontractor issues under 13 CFR 121.103 as well. Procurement integrity restrictions in 41 U.S.C. 2102 protect contractor bid or proposal information and source-selection information before award. Anti-kickback restrictions in 41 U.S.C. 8702 reach improper benefits used to obtain or reward favorable treatment in covered contracting. Never pay a prime employee personally to route subcontract work, and never buy competitor proposal contents. The fallback is a documented service assignment with permitted information, real deliverables, and traceable payment to the contracting business. If the economics require hidden influence or fictitious workshare, abandon that structure before preparing the invoice.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Verifications Before Committing Delivery Capacity**
> 
> Complete these checks before accepting exposure that depends on a customer's future award, disputed invoice, or supplier performance.
> - Verify the actual contracting entity, authorized signer, purchasing reference, acceptance owner, and accounts-payable destination.
> - Review the proposed fee structure against applicable contingent-fee, procurement-integrity, anti-kickback, pass-through, and set-aside requirements.
> - Confirm the deposit or approved credit limit, independently verified bank details, milestone evidence, and written collection escalation terms.
> - Document genuine workshare and lawful information sources; reject unsupported supplier certifications, hidden personal payments, and award-dependent influence fees.

## 4. Reconcile Seven Service Plays to the Three Canonical Profit Cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The table supplies one illustrative revenue mix that reconciles exactly to each case; it does not claim these prices or sales volumes are observed market results. Contractor counts do not specify hours, and the Conservative cost envelope must be tested against actual compensation before hiring. In particular, $102,000 cannot casually be treated as funding five full-time market-rate positions. Track delivery hours and contribution by offer so heavy proposal revisions do not consume the retainer margin. A $5,000 package requiring 40 hours yields $125 per delivery hour before overhead; at 80 hours that falls to $62.50. Use scope boundaries and priced change orders, not optimistic award probabilities, to protect those economics.

For ego_bait_directory_badge_arbitrage, publish data-scored state and city lists such as 'Top 15 Verified Specialists in Austin' using documented verification criteria. State precisely what verified means: for example, checked business identity and documented specialty evidence, not government endorsement or award eligibility. Issue free embeddable 'Top Rated' winner badges only to firms meeting the published method. Freeze each published edition's objective scores against commercial editing; retain the input snapshot and publish factual corrections transparently. Sell $299–$799 monthly Featured #1 Lead-Capture placements in a separate 'Sponsored / Featured' advertising area, never by rewriting the underlying ranking. At $500 monthly, three annual subscriptions produce $18,000, matching the Base bridge. Five specialists in each of 20 cities embedding a badge would produce 100 referring sites, but neither adoption nor domain-rating scores are assured. Earn hundreds of links through genuine distribution, not purchased ranking treatment. Use clean branded anchor text, distinguish sponsored links appropriately, and never promise search position. FTC Act Section 5 and applicable endorsement principles require truthful presentation of the commercial relationship. If badge adoption or lead quality disappoints, retain the free objective directory, stop unsupported performance claims, and let advertisers cancel rather than manipulating scores to rescue subscription revenue.

The other six plays need equally explicit fallbacks. Capture retainers sell a $4,000 monthly monitoring scope; reduce coverage or pause renewal when no useful opportunities fit. Bid packages sell $5,000 deliverables; collect for accepted work when a customer abandons a bid. Supplier onboarding sells $2,000 files; record unresolved evidence instead of inventing eligibility. Acceptance packages sell $2,500 documentation assignments; escalate disputes without representing that the agency must release cash. Municipal readiness sells $7,500 jurisdiction-specific projects; narrow scope when local requirements expand. Teaming administration sells $3,000 assignments; stop when proposed workshare or compensation cannot survive the applicable rules. These controls address award uncertainty, payment delays, and supplier failure without merging the operator's receivable with the client's public contract. The stated $25,000–$150,000 startup range and $5,000–$25,000 monthly opex range do not establish sufficient runway for a 6–18 month first-deal cycle. At $25,000 monthly, $150,000 covers six months before setup costs; eighteen months would consume $450,000. Treat the opex range as a planning input, not a replacement for the scenario's complete cost reconciliation. Launch with paid diagnostics, committed deposits, and flexible contractor hours, and forecast cash weekly. Automate solicitation alerts, bid calendars, and checklist reminders while retaining human review of authority, certifications, and payment exceptions.
