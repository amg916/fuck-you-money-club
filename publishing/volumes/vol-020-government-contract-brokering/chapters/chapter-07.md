## 1. Convert authorized demand into seven bounded service plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with the customer relationship, not the procurement notice. The bid desk sells defined research, coordination, documentation, and delivery services to an identified client under a signed scope. It does not sell access to contracting officers or promise an award. Record the client's legal entity, authorized sponsor, billing contact, permitted systems, purchasing objective, and responsibility for final representations. Pull federal notices from SAM.gov through authorized interfaces; preserve the solicitation identifier, amendment number, attachment digest, retrieval time, and submission deadline with its stated time zone. Municipal opportunities require their own portal instructions and local purchasing rules rather than an assumption that the FAR applies everywhere. Score fit only after checking eligibility, delivery capacity, mandatory registrations, and actual response time. A high-value notice with an impossible site visit is a rejected opportunity, not a pipeline asset. For federally funded local purchases, determine whether 2 CFR 200.318 and related procurement standards apply. Separate public-source research from restricted information: 41 U.S.C. 2102 prohibits specified disclosures and receipt of protected procurement information before award. Quarantine unsolicited source-selection material and escalate through the client's designated reviewer without circulating it through the production team.

Use seven explicitly bounded plays rather than a vague brokerage mandate. Federal triage creates an evidence-backed shortlist; the municipal desk maintains jurisdiction-specific bid instructions; teaming support documents genuine prime and subcontractor responsibilities; response production assembles compliant artifacts; supplier verification tests capacity; invoice reconciliation traces accepted work to payment; allocation documentation supports eligible Section 179D projects. These are chapter-level operating mappings, not seven separate promises of incremental revenue. Price an accepted service unit at $2,000 in the worked model, with a $1,550 average fully allocated delivery budget and $450 operating contribution after that allocation. Seventy units produce $140,000 of quarterly fees, $108,500 of costs, and $31,500 of profit. One unit might be a bounded solicitation review or a separately accepted supplier dossier; the statement of work defines the actual deliverable and revision allowance. Never charge twice because the same artifact supports two plays. Review FAR Subpart 3.4 and applicable contingent-fee clauses before accepting compensation arrangements. Fixed pricing does not cure an otherwise prohibited activity, and a percentage of an award is not automatically permissible merely because the invoice calls it consulting. Suspend unsupported structures and retain only independently lawful, documented services.

The mapped play section_179d_municipal_deduction_allocation_arbitrage requires especially disciplined language and evidence. Under TRACK_1, coordinate eligible government or specified tax-exempt building owners, qualifying designers, and independent energy certifiers; the deliverable is an allocation documentation file, not ownership of a freely transferable deduction. Before pricing, establish building ownership, construction timing, qualifying designer activity, technical certification requirements, and the applicable version of 26 U.S.C. 179D. The statutory termination for property whose construction begins after June 30, 2026 makes construction-start evidence an essential intake field. Also resolve applicable prevailing-wage and apprenticeship conditions before anyone models enhanced deduction amounts. A $2,000 documentation engagement recognizes only $2,000 of service revenue; even an independently supported $100,000 deduction estimate contributes zero additional operator revenue. The designer's tax position remains separate from the desk's ledger. Obtain owner authorization, certifier independence evidence, allocation signatures, and a version-controlled supporting file before marking the documentation milestone accepted. If eligibility, allocation authority, or tax treatment is unresolved, pause allocation, obtain independent tax review, and offer fixed-fee documentation support only for eligible projects. ## 2. Install a six-module ledger with serialized, replay-safe workflow  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The reference table contains six executable modules that share one Python namespace; concatenate their code cells in numbered order and run them with Python 3.9 or later. They use only the standard library and a local SQLite database. The final module executes assertions against isolated test databases; it does not contact SAM.gov, move money, or access customer systems. Provision the production database once, then give each worker its own connection. All business mutations acquire BEGIN IMMEDIATE before checking existing records, which serializes competing writers and closes the usual check-then-insert race. Unique constraints independently protect source identities, milestones, and posting keys. A repeated request with identical content returns its prior result; reuse with different content raises an error. Busy-lock failures are safe failures rather than permission to bypass the transaction. Retry the same request identifier after bounded backoff. Keep the database on a supported local filesystem, not a shared network drive. This design addresses concurrent mutations inside one database; external portals and bank systems remain outside its transaction boundary. No local schema can make an unrelated remote submission exactly once without the remote system's own deduplication contract.

Treat verification as an evidence-bearing state transition, not a visual status label. Intake records begin staged; eligible records become verified, unresolved records become held, and client-accepted service milestones may enter accepted. Every transition supplies the expected prior state, a stable event key, an actor, and evidence. A stale worker cannot overwrite a newer decision because the state comparison occurs while holding the write transaction. The evidence requirements establish workflow completeness, not the truth of client claims; human reviewers still inspect the referenced source files. Enforce authentication and reviewer permissions in the application before exposing these functions. The table deliberately treats escrow as a noncustodial boundary: held means work is paused, not that the desk possesses customer funds. There is no escrow release or payment initiation function. If a separate authorized arrangement uses a regulated custodian, record its confirmed status as external evidence and keep its funds outside this service ledger. For prime/sub work, review FAR 52.215-23 when included, 13 CFR 125.6 where applicable, and relevant affiliation and set-aside requirements before accepting the structure. A clean database cannot rescue an impermissible arrangement.

The ledger records balanced account pairs rather than unbalanced free-form lines. Accepted invoices debit accounts receivable and credit service revenue; matched collections debit cash and credit receivables; paid operating costs debit expense and credit cash. Amounts are positive integer cents, and every receipt has a bank-scoped transaction identifier. Database triggers prevent invoice mutation, journal edits, duplicate revenue recognition, and collections exceeding the invoice balance. The reference intentionally supports one invoice allocation per bank transaction; split remittances, credits, refunds, prepayments, and disputed corrections require a separately designed extension rather than manual database edits. Preserve rejected imports outside the posted ledger until reviewed. The reconciliation view reports earned fees, cash receipts, paid expenses, operating profit, and operating cash flow separately. Opening capital affects cash but never service revenue. Protect the database with service-account file permissions, encrypted backups, and tested restoration; append-only triggers are application controls, not protection against a privileged database administrator. Add an external audit-log destination before multi-user deployment. For production cash reporting, reconcile the imported receipt set to bank statements and preserve statement identifiers alongside the event keys used by the posting functions.

It is an operating quarter, not a claim that a new desk can win seven government contracts in ninety days. Each customer buys an average of ten accepted $2,000 service units across the quarter, producing $20,000 per customer and $140,000 in aggregate earned fees. The work schedule contains twenty accepted units in Month 1, twenty-five in Month 2, and twenty-five in Month 3. Their recognition totals are therefore $40,000, $50,000, and $50,000. Neither solicitation face value nor prospective award size appears in those figures. New prospects are tracked separately: Month 1 starts eighteen leads, Month 2 starts twenty, and Month 3 starts twenty-two. Eligibility scrubs, commercial losses, parked opportunities, and qualified follow-ups exhaust each monthly lead cohort without double counting. Eleven qualified follow-ups remain potential future business; none is treated as a signed customer or recognized revenue during the quarter. This separation makes the model useful for an established practice while preserving the startup's actual sales-cycle constraint.

Assume zero opening receivables and $60,000 of opening owner-funded cash. In Month 1, customers pay $30,000 against $40,000 of accepted invoices, leaving $10,000 receivable. Month 2 collects that $10,000 plus $30,000 against its own $50,000 invoices, leaving $20,000 receivable. Month 3 collects the prior $20,000 plus $30,000 against its own $50,000, again leaving $20,000 outstanding. Paid operating costs are $36,000, $36,000, and $36,500. Monthly operating cash movement is consequently negative $6,000, positive $4,000, and positive $13,500; ending cash is $54,000, $58,000, and $71,500. Quarterly profit is $31,500, while operating cash generated is only $11,500 because $20,000 remains in receivables. The opening contribution is funding, not profit. The illustration assumes all modeled operating costs are both incurred and paid in the quarter, with no opening liabilities, advance billings, distributions, or capital expenditures. Adding any of those items requires an explicit bridge rather than a silent adjustment to the owner-profit number. Track disputed invoices separately from ordinary processing delays before deciding that aging is harmless.

Annualize the quarter's earnings, not its collection shortfall: four times $140,000 equals the Base revenue of $560,000, and four times $31,500 equals $126,000 of annual owner net profit. The implied annual expense budget is $434,000 and the margin is exactly 22.5%. Repeating the same quarterly receivable increase four times would accumulate $80,000 of additional working-capital demand; it is not a collection plan. Require Month 4 follow-up on the remaining $20,000 and build a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast from actual customer payment behavior. The two full-time staff and three contractors must fit within the cost envelope. Here, quarterly loaded employee cost is $42,000, tools are $6,000, and insurance plus accounting is $6,000, yielding $18,000 monthly fixed overhead. The three-contractor pool supplies $54,500 of variable delivery and specialist review across the quarter. Thus total monthly expense can exceed the metadata's $5,000–$25,000 monthly operating-overhead range without hiding the additional direct delivery cost. Contractor allocations reflect purchased capacity, not three additional full-time salaries. Any staffing plan that cannot deliver seventy accepted units within this budget must change scope, price, or throughput.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze each cohort's source population
   - Assign immutable monthly lead identifiers and distinguish prospects from established customer accounts.
   - Reconcile starting leads to scrubs, losses, parked records, and qualified follow-ups; never let a reopened lead create a second acquisition count.
2. Accept and recognize bounded services
   - Attach customer acceptance to each uniquely identified milestone before issuing its earned-fee invoice.
   - For this cohort, twenty, twenty-five, and twenty-five accepted units at $2,000 produce the three monthly revenue totals.
3. Allocate cleared receipts without duplication
   - Match each bank-scoped transaction identifier to its invoice and post the collection once.
   - Route split payments and unmatched deposits outside this reference workflow until a reviewed allocation extension supports them.
4. Close earnings and cash independently
   - Reconcile monthly fees, paid costs, receivable movements, opening funding, and ending cash.
   - Require $31,500 quarterly profit, $20,000 ending receivables, $11,500 operating cash generated, and $71,500 ending cash under the stated assumptions.

## 4. Close the evidence loop and scale only collectible production  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Close each week with an exception review organized by economic consequence. Award uncertainty belongs in pipeline probability, not in a receivable. Customer acceptance disputes belong in the delivery queue with the contractual revision allowance visible. Bank transactions lacking a valid invoice match remain unposted until resolved. Supplier failure requires a replacement plan, revised delivery dates, and an assessment of the client's actual contractual obligations. Payment delays require named follow-up responsibility and a cash forecast update, not optimistic comments in the customer record. FAR Subpart 32.9 and the Prompt Payment Act inform federal payment administration where applicable, but the desk's private service invoice follows its own customer contract; federal payment protections do not automatically pass through to every consultant. Preserve government acceptance and payment evidence when the customer is a prime contractor, while recognizing that the desk's debtor remains its contracting customer. Escalate repeated delays before purchasing more contractor capacity. A customer with an impressive award notice but weak collection behavior can consume more working capital than a smaller, punctual account with narrowly scoped service requirements.

Run the production board on accepted units, rework hours, and aged receivables rather than bid count. At the Base case, 280 annual $2,000 units average roughly 5.4 accepted units per week across fifty-two weeks; actual staffing must allow for holidays and deadline bunching. The fully allocated target cost remains $1,550 per unit, but individual scopes need their own labor estimate and specialist review allowance. Solicitation alerts automate discovery, bid calendars automate reminders, and checklist generation automates requirement tracking. None should autonomously certify eligibility, sign representations, or invent supplier capacity. Require human review for final source interpretations and client-facing commitments. Track the percentage of delivered artifacts returned for material correction; rising rework consumes the $450 unit margin before annual financial reports reveal the problem. For each repeated defect, update the requirement mapping or intake question that should have prevented it. Keep solicitation-specific evidence separate from reusable templates so a past certification cannot silently migrate into a new bid. Limit work in progress when review capacity, rather than drafting capacity, becomes the binding constraint.

Maintain the three canonical scenarios as separate operating configurations. Conservative revenue of $120,000 at 15% margin leaves $18,000 owner net profit and $102,000 of costs with two full-time staff and three contractors; this thin cost envelope requires explicitly validated compensation and capacity assumptions. Base revenue of $560,000 at 22.5% margin leaves $126,000 after $434,000 of costs with the same headcount. Scaled revenue of $1,000,000 at 30% margin leaves $300,000 after $700,000 of costs with three full-time staff and four contractors. Do not obtain the Scaled margin by silently removing owner labor, specialist review, insurance, or collection work from costs. Before expanding, validate contractor availability, review throughput, customer concentration, and the minimum cash balance under delayed-payment stress. The $20,000–$90,000 startup capital range must support the actual six-to-eighteen-month first-deal horizon; the illustrated $60,000 opening balance belongs to an already operating cohort. If runway is inadequate, reduce committed capacity or secure funding before accepting more scope. Growth is an increase in accepted, collectible service production, not a larger spreadsheet of government opportunities.
