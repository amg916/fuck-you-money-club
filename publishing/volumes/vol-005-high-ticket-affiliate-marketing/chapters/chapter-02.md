## Executive Summary

The merchant funds a commission because a new customer can yield more cash than the full cost to win and serve that account. The publisher gets paid only for the event the contract defines, not for a click or a sales team's hope. This chapter turns that gap into a buying discipline: price the customer, test the event, inspect the payer, and reconcile the cash. Volume 005, Candidate #24, ranks #02 at 92/100 across 35 mapped operator plays. Its economics work only when earned commissions survive reversals and collection costs.

## 1. Price the customer's cash, not the headline CPA  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A B2B software vendor buys a paid account, not a page view. A fintech issuer may buy an approved and funded account; a commercial insurer may buy a bound policy; a specialty service firm may buy a completed job. Those events have different costs and different rules. A customer with lifetime value of $3,000-$50,000+ can support a $300-$3,000+ fee when enough contribution remains after service, sales, and retention costs. Treat those bands as a model to test, not a rate card. The title's $500-$5,000 CPAs describe possible contracts, not normal earnings. Ask which event earns the fee, which party owes it, and which later event can reverse it. Without those three answers, a large payout is just an unpaid claim on someone else's sales process, with no known date for cash.

Build the merchant case with contribution rather than lifetime revenue. Suppose an account pays $12,000 a year, stays three years, and leaves a 75% gross margin before acquisition costs. That yields $27,000 of gross contribution before discounting cash flows or allowing for early churn. A $2,000 affiliate fee plus $4,000 of sales and onboarding costs consumes $6,000, leaving $21,000 before overhead. The fee alone is not the merchant's full CAC. On these assumptions, $750 of monthly gross contribution covers that combined cost in eight months. Now cut tenure to one year: the cushion falls to $3,000. This is why merchants demand cohort quality, not just lead counts. Get the merchant to show its own retention and refund data before treating a generous CPA as durable enough to fund a year of content production.

Performance pricing shifts part of variable acquisition risk to the publisher. You pay for traffic and content before you know which accounts will close, while the merchant pays for the agreed result. It does not shift every risk: the merchant still carries service cost, sales capacity, fraud losses, and its own cash needs. Split your ledger into referred merchant gross merchandise value, pending fees, earned affiliate commissions net of reversals, and cash received. None of those balances equals owner profit. A $100,000 sale with a $2,000 fee gives the publisher $2,000 before costs, not $100,000 of revenue. Track merchant GMV only as context for the contract. Base bids on expected collectible fees per visit, then subtract traffic, content, tools, labor, and a buffer for delayed payment. Scale only the cohorts that pay those bills.

## 2. Underwrite the payer and the reversal clock  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Read the contract as a cash schedule. Name the legal debtor, the currency, the minimum payment, the invoice date, the payout date, and the evidence needed to dispute a rejection. A platform can track a fee without owing it. Ask whether the network pays only after the merchant pays, and whether the merchant can change rates on traffic already sent. Define the treatment of old leads, duplicate accounts, existing customers, sales assisted deals, and canceled orders. Record the version of the terms that governed each event. A screenshot of a dashboard total is weaker than an event record tied to a signed schedule. Keep a small test budget until a complete cohort moves from click to cash. One payment proves the path worked once; it does not prove the payer can support your planned scale.

Separate three clocks: the attribution window, the validation period, and the payment term. A thirty day click window does not mean cash arrives in thirty days. A sale on day twenty nine could enter a sixty day review and then wait another thirty days for payment. Ask when each clock starts and whether a refund restarts any part of it. Holdbacks should state an amount, a reason, and a release date or objective release rule. Track earned but unpaid fees by age, with a separate field for disputed amounts. Do not call all unpaid cash a loss, and not call all tracked fees earned. Compare rejection rates by source and vintage after the same time has elapsed. Young cohorts look better simply because their refunds and duplicate checks have not yet arrived in full.

Set a loss limit before buying more traffic. If one merchant owes $20,000 and your business has $25,000 of cash, a higher CPA does not fix the concentration problem. Set exposure using unpaid earned fees plus committed spend that cannot be stopped this week. Require a named contact who can explain rejected events with stable reason codes, not a vague quality label. Match those codes to the contract and challenge exceptions with dated records. Keep a second offer ready, but never send the same prospect to several buyers unless the agreed scope permits it. Reduce spend when collection slows beyond the stated term, even if reported conversion rises. The durable operator protects working cash first, then negotiates rate increases with evidence of account quality, retention, and paid cohort contribution rather than raw lead volume.

## 3. Match the attribution rail to the payable event  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Choose a rail after defining the event, not before. The matrix is a buying checklist, not a promise that every vendor includes every feature or accepts every traffic source. PartnerStack can suit partner led software sales; an enterprise program on Impact.com can require different contracts and integration work. Direct Everflow S2S needs your team and the merchant to agree on event IDs, deduplication, and status changes. Server to server tracking reduces dependence on browser storage, but it does not prove a sale was incremental or erase privacy duties. Test a synthetic click through the exact landing page and sales flow. Confirm that a rejected event cannot return as a second payable conversion on retry. Keep click time, event time, currency, rate version, and settlement reference in a ledger you control outside the platform's summary charts.

Sub-networks add reach and also add distance from the actual publisher. Require stable source IDs, permitted placement types, and a way to suspend one source without losing the rest. CJ and Awin are separate networks; the combined row groups a procurement category rather than asserting shared rules. Newsletter co-registration needs explicit subscriber intent and clear terms about who receives the address. Do not count a raw email record as a customer acquisition. Define a qualified subscriber or downstream sale as the payable event and measure duplicate, inactive, and disputed records separately. For every rail, put a plain disclosure of the paid relationship near the recommendation or referral link. Under 16 CFR Part 255, a material connection can matter to the weight readers give an endorsement; a buried footer is not a sound operating substitute for clear disclosure.

For pay per call, more than ninety seconds is an illustrative buyer threshold, not a universal Ringba or Phonexa rule. Duration alone can reward hold music, wrong departments, and callers outside the service area. Write the buyer's geography, operating hours, intent, duplicate window, and qualifying conversation requirements into the routing rules. Route only to buyers with capacity, and stop routing when a daily cap is reached. Define whether the platform bills connected time, talk time, or another measure. Retain only the call evidence needed for settlement, with lawful recording practices and restricted access. Ping-post bidding likewise needs explicit buyer eligibility and data sharing permissions before selling a lead. Reconcile the winning bid, accepted delivery, final qualification, and paid amount. A switch that finds a buyer quickly still fails economically when the buyer rejects most of its deliveries.

## 4. Separate regulated services from prohibited referral compensation  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play out_of_network_dme_telehealth_billing_arbitrage is not permission to sell patients. When Medicare, Medicaid, or another federal healthcare program is involved, 42 U.S.C. section 1320a-7b(b) prohibits specified knowing and willful remuneration for referrals or purchasing arrangements involving federally payable items or services. A per-patient fee for steering federally reimbursed DME or telehealth business can be an illegal kickback. Separate a genuine flat-fee marketing or MSO technology service from any referral sale: define actual work, price it at fair market value, document delivery, and avoid linking compensation to referral volume or value. A flat fee or an MSO label does not by itself make an arrangement lawful. Have qualified healthcare counsel evaluate the complete facts and any applicable exception or safe harbor before accepting the engagement. State rules and payer contracts may also reach arrangements outside federal programs.

The play high_risk_payfac_iso_mid_cascading_arbitrage belongs in a different ledger: B2B ISO or payment-orchestration merchant referral residuals on settled volume. The merchant's processed sales are not affiliate revenue. Suppose an approved merchant settles $1,000,000 of eligible volume and your contract grants five basis points of that volume: the initial residual is $500 before contractual deductions. If the contract instead grants a share of net processing revenue, volume alone cannot price the residual. Obtain the statement waterfall for interchange, network fees, processor charges, reserves, losses, and your split. Never use cascading to disguise the seller, transaction type, or activity from an acquirer. Any routing must stay within approved merchant accounts and underwriting terms. Establish who can terminate the residual, whether it survives contract termination, and how clawbacks work. Check sector-specific licensing requirements before describing a merchant referral as an unrestricted affiliate offer.

The play mvno_lifeline_acp_telecom_subsidy_aggregation must not blend an old subsidy with a current commission. ACP ended in June 2024; do not forecast new ACP reimbursements as an operating revenue stream. Lifeline is a separate program governed by 47 CFR Part 54, including eligible telecommunications carrier duties, household eligibility, duplicate prevention, and enrollment procedures. Section 54.406(b) prohibits commissions for enrollment representatives based on how many consumers apply for or enroll in Lifeline. Relabeling that payment as an affiliate CPA does not solve the restriction. Treat those enrollment incentives as non-commissionable, not as a low-margin version of a software referral. Evaluate fixed service compensation against the actual role and rules; a change in invoice wording cannot change the work performed. Keep subsidy receipts, ordinary retail sales, and any lawful service fees in separate accounts, with evidence supporting every enrollment and service claim.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Classify the payment before assigning a CPA**
> 
> The same tracking software can record a lawful merchant referral or a prohibited incentive. Payment basis and actual conduct determine the distinction, not the rail's name.
> - Healthcare: exclude patient-referral commissions involving federal program business; assess genuine services and applicable protections on their facts.
> - Payments: use approved merchant referrals and a documented settled-volume or net-revenue residual formula.
> - Lifeline: exclude enrollment-representative commissions tied to applications or enrollments; do not model ACP as active funding.
> - Commercial insurance and fintech: confirm licensing and program-specific referral limits before quoting any acquisition fee.

## 5. Reconcile annual earnings to cash you can actually draw  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Candidate #24's conservative case earns $50,000 of gross commissions net of reversals, spends $40,000, and leaves $10,000 of owner net profit at a 20% margin. The base case earns $325,000, spends $211,250, and leaves $113,750 at 35%. The scaled case earns $600,000, spends $300,000, and leaves $300,000 at 50%. These are annual planning scenarios, not observed results. Here gross means before publisher operating costs, while earned commissions already exclude reversals and clawbacks. Merchant GMV is excluded entirely. The staffing labels remain 1 FT + 2 C, 1 FT + 2 C, and 2 FT + 3 C respectively, where FT means full-time and C means contractor. Assume the first full-time role is the owner and contractors have scoped assignments, not full-time workloads. The conservative budget cannot fund three market-rate full-time salaries, and should never be presented as doing so.

Owner net profit here means business profit after the listed operating costs, before personal income tax and without a separate wage for the owner's labor. Labor includes contractor fees and, in the scaled case, the second full-time employee's loaded cost. The table makes those assumptions visible instead of hiding free labor inside the margin. Traffic, content, tooling, and other operating costs each have their own budget; content costs exclude labor already counted in the labor row. For a base unit model, assume 250 matured acquisitions yield an average $1,300 commission after reversals. That produces $325,000 of commission revenue. Average operating cost is $845 per acquisition and owner profit is $455. The $60,000 traffic budget allows $240 per matured acquisition, not per lead. If only one in ten qualified leads matures, the matching traffic allowance is $24 per qualified lead at that same conversion rate.

Profit still does not equal spendable cash. The reconciliation assumes no opening receivables, no opening cash buffer, no unpaid operating bills, and no other working capital changes. Ten percent of annual earned commissions remains collectible but unpaid at year end; this is a timing assumption, not another reversal expense. Cash collected therefore equals ninety percent of earned commissions. After paying operating costs, retain a separate cash reserve of $3,000, $20,000, or $30,000 before drawing funds. Reserve funding reduces available cash but does not reduce accounting profit. Bad debt, if it occurs, would require an additional expense and a lower profit forecast. Rebuild the model monthly using actual collection ages and matured cohorts. Raise spend only when cash covers committed costs and the selected reserve; do not borrow against a dashboard balance that still includes pending or disputed fees.
