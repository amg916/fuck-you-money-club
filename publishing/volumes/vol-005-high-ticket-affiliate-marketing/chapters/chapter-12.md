Your first month buys evidence, not an income claim. ## Days 1-7: Underwrite before you write  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Day 1, choose one vertical where a buyer can compare three credible high ticket offers against the same job. Write the job in plain language, name the buyer, and list the cost of staying put. On Day 2, obtain current rate cards and apply to at least three competing recurring or CPA programs. A directory listing is not live access. Record the partner ID, approved domain, allowed channels, payout basis, and contact who can resolve disputed sales. On Day 3, test each live link and ask how long approval normally takes. If only one merchant accepts you, keep research moving but do not buy traffic. Three names on a page do not create choice when two links cannot earn money or serve the same reader need at the point of purchase.

On Day 4, read the actual agreement rather than a summary from another affiliate. Put brand bidding, direct linking, trademark use, cookie window, last click rules, payout delay, refund window, clawbacks, and termination rights in one sheet. Ask whether renewals remain payable after the merchant ends the relationship. For recurring offers, record the rate and eligible duration without treating lifetime revenue as cash earned today. On Day 5, score the merchants on buyer fit, proof quality, support, tracking access, and reversal exposure. Choose a lead offer and two real substitutes. Keep the comparison order tied to disclosed buyer criteria, not the highest rate. A higher payout cannot repair poor fit if a merchant rejects leads or buyers leave before the commission becomes payable under the signed terms you will actually operate against.

On Day 6, map twenty five bottom of funnel keyword and video intent blueprints. Use [Incumbent] pricing, [Incumbent] alternative, and [Incumbent] vs [Challenger] as research patterns, then assign a buyer question, proof source, calculator input, and next action to each. Search demand is a hypothesis until measured; do not invent volume from autocomplete. Keep organic brand comparisons separate from the paid list, which will use non trademark terms only. On Day 7, rank the blueprints by purchase proximity and your ability to show first hand evidence. Select ten pages and five screen recorded tutorials for production. Give each a named owner and a dated source check. Save rejected ideas too, with a reason, so a thin topic does not return next week disguised as a fresh growth plan for the same audience.

## Days 8-14: Build a measurable switching path  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Day 8, ship a small serverless edge comparison hub with a fast page for each buyer problem, a shared offer record, and a clear route to the merchant. Cache public content, never private lead records. On Day 9, add an interactive ROI or fee switching calculator that shows its inputs and arithmetic. Include setup fees, migration hours, contract overlap, and the period being compared. Let readers change the assumptions; a savings claim built on hidden defaults is weak evidence. Test zero usage, large usage, and a case where switching costs more. Display that losing case honestly. Record the price source and date beside the output. Do not describe savings as your commission or treat a calculated benefit as a promise that the buyer will achieve the same result in their own business.

On Day 10, place an above the fold disclosure bar explaining that you may earn a commission from linked purchases, with clear disclosure near recommendations as needed. Apply the endorsement principles in 16 CFR Part 255 to the actual reader journey, including video and email. On Day 11, offer a useful switching worksheet before the outbound link. Keep capture optional so the buyer can continue directly. For co-reg email capture, identify each receiving partner and the use of the address before collection; do not quietly sell a worksheet request as permission for unrelated lists. Apply the commercial email requirements of 15 U.S.C. section 7704, including accurate headers, a valid postal address, and a working opt out honored within ten business days. Separate service delivery from later promotional sends in your records.

On Day 12, mint an opaque click_id at outbound redirect and write it to the click_ledger with merchant, asset, channel, timestamp, and test status. Pass it through the merchant's supported sub ID field. Keep email addresses out of URLs and use a separate restricted lead store. On Day 13, receive authenticated S2S postbacks, deduplicate by merchant event ID, and support pending, approved, reversed, and paid state changes without counting each update as another sale. Validate currency and amount as well as identity. On Day 14, run a merchant supported synthetic test conversion from landing page to postback. Replay it, reverse it, and inspect the ledger. Exclude test events from all earnings reports. If the merchant lacks a test path, document that limit and verify the join with an authorized sandbox rather than inventing a sale.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — First-30-Day Speed & Validation Shortcuts**
> 
> Experienced operators compress six months of trial-and-error into thirty days by sequencing four tactical moves:
> - Include One Honest 'Stay With Your Current Vendor If...' Case in Your Calculator: Buyers immediately trust a switching calculator that tells <5-seat teams NOT to switch yet—lifting conversion rate on qualified 25-to-100-seat buyers who see they do save money.
> - Launch Bing / Microsoft Advertising Exact-Match Alongside Google Search in Week 3: B2B desktop controllers and IT buyers on corporate Windows machines frequently convert at 30%-50% lower CPCs on Microsoft Search for `[Vendor] pricing` queries.
> - Record All 5 Launch YouTube Tutorials From the Exact Same Screen-Test Session: Capturing real workflows across Offer A, Offer B, and Offer C in one afternoon gives you authentic UI footage for 5 `vs` / `pricing` videos plus screenshots for all 10 guides.
> - Send Your Partner Manager Your Day-21 Sub-ID Quality Readout Before Asking for a Bump: Showing exact buyer seat counts and zero trademark-bidding violations makes approving your Day-25 +25% private CPA bump an easy internal win for the AM.

## Days 15-21: Publish proof and purchase a bounded sample  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Days 15 and 16, publish the first six comparison assets, then finish the remaining four on Day 17. Each asset needs dated prices, a specific buyer fit, a meaningful drawback, and a traceable basis for every performance claim. State when you have not tested a feature instead of converting vendor copy into personal experience. The prohibition on deceptive practices in 15 U.S.C. section 45 is relevant to fabricated savings, false scarcity, and unsupported comparative claims. Preserve the evidence behind each recommendation. On Days 18 and 19, publish five screen recorded YouTube tutorials that solve a problem or compare a workflow. Show the actual steps, costs, and limits rather than narrating a sales page. Include the affiliate relationship in the video and near linked recommendations, not only behind an expanded description that viewers may never open.

Start a capped exact match search test on Day 15 so seven days of acquisition data exist by Day 21. Spend $300-$500 total, not per asset, and use non trademark comparison terms allowed by the agreements. Set an account budget control where available and a daily manual stop check; platform pacing can exceed a nominal daily cap. Review search terms each day because exact match can still admit variants. Exclude irrelevant and trademark queries promptly. Route paid clicks to the best evidenced page, not automatically to the highest paying merchant. Record paid landing sessions, valid leads, outbound clicks, and spend separately. Click to lead means valid leads divided by paid landing clicks in this test. Net EPC means commissions earned after reversals divided by eligible outbound affiliate clicks from the same acquisition cohort, not all site visits.

On Day 20, compare the paid cohort with organic and video traffic without pooling them into a flattering blended average. Paid visitors can behave differently from readers who watched a full tutorial. On Day 21, report observed click to lead, outbound rate, and net EPC with their denominators and maturity dates. A seven day test can reveal a broken form or expensive intent; it may not reveal final earnings when trials and refunds take weeks. Label pending payouts separately and show an observed settled figure even when it is zero. Retain a provisional projection only as a clearly named hypothesis with its assumed approval rate. Do not call a sample of two sales stable. The milestone matrix sequences the work, while the later decision rules prevent a calendar deadline from forcing an unsupported decision to scale spend.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze definitions
   - Name the cohort and channel.
   - Use acquisition date and a fixed maturity window.
2. Trace
   - Join merchant events to click_id.
   - Deduplicate events and exclude synthetic traffic.
3. Reconcile
   - Apply reversals to the original cohort.
   - Keep paid cash separate from earned commissions.
4. Decide
   - Apply the numeric rules.
   - Log evidence, owner, and next review date.

## Days 22-30: Reconcile before negotiating or expanding  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Day 22, export merchant transactions and reconcile them against the internal click_ledger by merchant and click_id. On Day 23, inspect duplicates, missing identifiers, currency mismatches, and differences between event time and reporting time. Define unmatched share as eligible merchant conversion records lacking a valid internal click divided by all eligible merchant conversion records. Investigate any missing event; above two percent, stop paid expansion until the join is repaired. A small denominator makes that threshold coarse, so list the count alongside the rate. On Day 24, classify each scrub or reversal as duplicate, ineligible geography, existing customer, fraud review, cancellation, refund, or unexplained rejection. Ask the partner manager for documented reasons rather than assuming theft or accepting an unexplained haircut. Update the original cohort so yesterday's apparent winner cannot keep yesterday's inflated earnings after today's reversal.

On Day 25, request a private payout bump using approved volume, buyer quality, and retained customer evidence, not traffic screenshots. Ask for the effective date, eligible events, exclusions, and payment schedule in writing. Apply the new rate only where the revised agreement allows; never retroactively improve the model by assumption. On Day 26, fix the largest evidenced loss point. A low lead rate suggests a mismatch between promise and worksheet; a healthy lead rate with poor merchant acceptance suggests qualification or offer fit. On Day 27, retest the changed path with a fresh labeled cohort. On Day 28, compare it with the original while holding the denominator and maturity period constant. Do not attribute a lift to your rewrite if a merchant changed price, payout, or eligibility at the same time without recording that confounding change.

On Day 29, close the test ledger and accrue content, contractor, tooling, and traffic costs even if their invoices arrive next month. On Day 30, use the decision matrix in order: repair measurement first, check maturity second, then evaluate economics. The numeric thresholds here are operating assumptions, not industry averages. Cost per outbound click equals attributable paid spend divided by valid paid outbound clicks. Coverage equals cohort net EPC divided by that acquisition cost. Require at least one hundred eligible paid outbound clicks and ten finalized conversions before an economic scale decision, plus a completed merchant review window. Even that sample remains noisy. If the month ends before maturity, choose Fix with spend held rather than annualizing pending sales. Preserve enough cash for the next payout delay and a reversal reserve; a profitable spreadsheet does not pay contractors while receivables are frozen.

**Day-30 Kill / Fix / Scale Decision Matrix**

| Decision | Numeric condition | Operator action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fix measurement | Unmatched events >2% or failed replay | Pause paid traffic; repair and rerun synthetic lifecycle |
| Fix evidence | Review window open, <100 outbound clicks, or <10 finalized conversions | Hold budget; await maturity or design a separately capped follow-up |
| Kill paid segment | Mature coverage <1.0x after sample minimum | Stop this paid segment; retain useful organic assets |
| Fix economics | Mature coverage 1.0x to <1.5x, or fully loaded loss | Change one evidenced constraint; no budget increase |
| Scale cautiously | Mature coverage >=1.5x and positive fully loaded cohort profit | Increase daily cap 20%; recheck mature cohorts before another increase |

> **OPERATOR GATE — Evidence outranks the calendar**
> 
> Day 30 is a decision date, not a demand to scale. Use the worst applicable condition; measurement defects take priority over attractive EPC.
> - Hold expansion if unmatched events exceed 2%.
> - No scale decision before the review window closes.
> - Require 100 paid outbound clicks and 10 finalized conversions.
> - Never substitute pending payouts for earned commissions.

## Translate verified EPC into an annual trajectory  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Consider a worked paid cohort with $400 of traffic spend, two hundred landing clicks, forty valid leads, and one hundred outbound affiliate clicks. Click to lead is twenty percent and the outbound rate is fifty percent. Suppose twelve transactions generate $1,200 of recorded commissions, then two reversals remove $200 after the review window closes. Ten finalized conversions leave $1,000 earned after reversals, a $10 net EPC, and a $4 acquisition cost per outbound click. Coverage is 2.5 times. Allocate another $350 for content and contractor work and $50 for tooling to this cohort, making total cost $800 and owner profit $200 before personal income tax. These allocations must come from actual records rather than convenient estimates. Merchant GMV could be much larger, but it is not affiliate revenue and does not enter this profit calculation. This example satisfies the sample floor, not statistical certainty.

Candidate #24 uses gross earned commissions to mean affiliate commissions after reversals but before operating expenses. Preserve that definition despite the awkward word gross. The Conservative trajectory is $50,000 of annual gross earned commissions at a twenty percent margin, producing $10,000 owner net profit with 1 FT + 2 C. Base is $325,000 at thirty five percent, producing $113,750 with 1 FT + 2 C. Scaled is $600,000 at fifty percent, producing $300,000 with 2 FT + 3 C. FT denotes full time roles and C denotes contractors; headcount labels do not establish affordable wages or hours. Charge actual employee and contractor compensation to costs. Where the owner fills an FT role without salary, the remainder compensates their labor as well as capital. Do not present that amount as passive profit or claim all three staffing patterns fit every labor market.

For planning only, hold the worked $10 net EPC constant and divide each annual commission target by it to estimate required outbound clicks. The table converts those totals into monthly pacing, not a claim that one short test predicts a year. Recurring commissions require a separate renewal and churn schedule; count only installments earned in the modeled period and never multiply a first payout by an assumed lifetime. The Conservative cost ceiling is just $40,000 annually, which may not support the stated roles unless the owner supplies unpaid labor and contractors work narrow scopes. Base and Scaled also require explicit compensation budgets. Keep the assumed paid share visible because buying every click at $4 consumes forty percent of commissions before other expenses. Scale only when retained cohort evidence, capacity, and cash support the next increment; a target margin is an outcome to demonstrate, not a setting to select.
