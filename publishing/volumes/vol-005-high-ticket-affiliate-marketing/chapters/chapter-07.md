## Executive Summary

Pay-per-call pays for a qualified conversation, not simply a ringing phone. WordsAtScale, John Casto, and ExclusiveLiveCall put buyer demand, qualification, and answer capacity at the center; Ringba and Melissa Lai emphasize tracking and tested routing. Execution ranges from expired-domain funnels and local SEO to Facebook groups, Marketplace, and paid creative testing. Vanel Sylvestre reports a $22 flight-call offer; Bens Review Videos describes JayKay's $165 gross walk-in-tub funnel with about $34 in costs. The disagreements are operational: network numbers versus independent tracking, marketplace coverage versus direct relationships, and waiting for organic trigger events versus funding continuous paid tests. Andy's elevated ROI claim includes unpaid advertising invoices, while Matthew Zivkovic's former $1,000-a-day operation lost its attraction as labor and account problems mounted.

## The Practitioner Consensus

The product is a qualified phone conversation. Rob Reece calls pay-per-call affiliate marketing without the buy button: a plumber needs work, a lead-generation company supplies opportunities, and the affiliate sends calls without answering them. Reece identifies home services, travel, industrial services, chiropractors, cosmetic dental, and legal services as examples, while explicitly describing their RHIMS 10 assessment as a research review rather than a campaign they ran [5QOU-NXPWw4 @ 00:00:40]. Asghar Speaks separates the roles: advertisers are buyers, publishers generate calls, and an intermediary takes money from advertisers, retains a share, and pays publishers. The same structure applies across locksmith, plumbing, HVAC, and cleaning campaigns. Ringba is the tracking layer in that structure, not a business that starts paying merely because someone purchases an account [r74nmdjWZ88 @ 00:02:03]. Jordan Soto at Ringba makes the payment condition explicit: the call must connect to a buyer and satisfy predetermined criteria. Those criteria can include minimum duration, geography, operating hours, age, or income. A flat payout is common, with some arrangements adding volume or quality bonuses. Generating a call and earning a payout are therefore different events in the buyer's campaign, even when the affiliate never handles the conversation [a4Aa0kexuh8 @ 00:01:12].

Find demand before treating a niche as inventory you can sell. WordsAtScale starts with the rank-and-rent problem: a site ranks, its phone rings, but there is nobody to receive the call. A marketplace supplies a tracking number, routes the caller to a bidding business, and pays when the call qualifies. For hoarding cleanup, WordsAtScale searches the broader categories buyers already purchase: junk removal, cleanouts, and biohazard cleanup. The search sequence is the niche plus pay-per-call, then a search for buyers of that niche's calls, followed by OfferVault's pay-per-call filter [DJFaYJ3O7aU @ 00:00:16]. ExclusiveLiveCall approaches the same problem through buyer conversations: ask which customers the business wants, which areas it covers, how much volume it can handle, and what a qualified opportunity is worth. Direct buyers and networks serve different coverage needs; the latter can fill markets a single contractor does not serve [XKj8PVuNBOs @ 00:03:09]. John Casto adds that finding a listed offer is only the beginning. Affiliates may work through many buyers before identifying consistent answering, workable payouts, and manageable relationships. Casto credits meetings at industry events and relationships with networks and direct buyers as a major difference-maker, not merely a supplement to searching behind a computer [lXG5t2aVZE8 @ 00:02:26].

Tracking makes the buyer's behavior visible, and that visibility is central to the business. John Casto contrasts calls with submitted leads: with calls, the affiliate can hear the conversation and see what happens in real time. Buyer performance includes answering, using the right script, and closing; when those functions fail, the affiliate's campaign can become expensive. Casto uses routing priority to manage who receives calls according to performance [lXG5t2aVZE8 @ 00:04:50]. Melissa Lai, speaking with OfferVault, describes beginners launching advertisements before testing numbers or setting up their tracking properly. Their checks include the IO, caps, filters, and campaign restrictions before traffic arrives. Without tracking, an affiliate can spend heavily without knowing the return or managing partners effectively [rsmZ9FBIPks @ 00:17:49]. Abhishek Sood shows the practical reporting view inside LeadSmart's Ringba access: campaign, publisher, caller ID, dialed number, and caller ZIP code, with recordings available to play or download. Sood uses those conversations for research into neighborhoods, micro-services, and what the caller actually needed. The dashboard is consequently more than an earnings display in Sood's operation; it supplies details for the next round of local research and website work, grounded in actual caller conversations rather than only the original keyword choice [g7SAWXINXDo @ 00:02:28].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The expired-domain route starts with a former business and the marketing it left behind. Bens Review Videos describes JayKay's RHIMS 10 sequence: filter ExpiredDomains.net for available English .com domains dropped between the last twelve hours and seven days, using a service or city keyword. Check each candidate in the Wayback Machine for a real business rather than a parking page, and check OfferVault for a matching service offer. JayKay supplies the archived homepage and assets to ChatGPT or Claude, rebuilds the old appearance without the old logo, adds a mobile sticky call banner and independent-operation disclosure, and hosts on GitHub Pages [eqw-Rqw7PNY @ 00:02:32]. The NumberBarn stack uses a local number costing about $10 once and a $6.99 monthly press-one plan. An ElevenLabs greeting identifies an independent referral service; validation sends callers to voicemail, and five press-one callers become application evidence before forwarding to the affiliate number [eqw-Rqw7PNY @ 00:03:49]. Reece describes the same course as an organic alternative to starting with paid advertisements, highlighting expired domains and a claimed fifteen minutes of monthly maintenance per domain [5QOU-NXPWw4 @ 00:02:24]. WordsAtScale adds the buyer-side preparation: identify the paid category and inspect the city's rate card before building [DJFaYJ3O7aU @ 00:05:27].

LeadSmart's website-led onboarding follows a different order: build the site before submitting the application. Abhishek Sood lists name, email address, phone number, Microsoft Teams ID, website link, verticals, and the method used to generate calls. The website field is mandatory in Sood's walkthrough, and SEO is the traffic method entered. After acceptance, LeadSmart supplies Ringba access and campaigns such as plumbing, roofing, dumpsters, and water damage [g7SAWXINXDo @ 00:00:15]. For the single-city plumbing site, Sood opens the campaign, selects Add Number, chooses Local rather than toll-free, and enters Covina's 626 area code. The resulting number goes on the website and into the working sheet. Reporting then associates incoming calls with the campaign and the dialed website number [g7SAWXINXDo @ 00:01:15]. Asghar Speaks describes this publisher arrangement separately from buying Ringba to operate a company: an accepted publisher receives the company's dashboard and numbers, then generates calls through websites or social traffic [r74nmdjWZ88 @ 00:07:36]. Decoding Ads describes the independent alternative for programmatic SEO: use a number controlled by the operator and route through Ringba. That setup exposes reporting, campaigns, publishers, ring trees, call flows, number purchasing, and integrations rather than putting the network's number directly across the website [OFst3KoUCj0 @ 00:00:38].

Traffic execution ranges from a single offer page to a stream of tested creatives. Vanel Sylvestre builds separate Hostinger landing pages for MarketCall offers and places the supplied tracking number on each page. For flight calls, Sylvestre searches Facebook for airline-complaint groups, joins them, and posts the landing page; the stated approach uses no paid advertising [a32vgNX3Qkw @ 00:02:44]. Matthew Zivkovic's Marketplace method starts with items that require professional installation, including furnaces, water heaters, gutters, and electrical panels. Replies are directed toward a tracking number, with project questions sometimes used to involve the prospect before asking for a call. When asked technical questions, Zivkovic says they post advertisements for contractors and directs the person to the contractor's phone conversation [7lpBAuhtQBY @ 00:22:07]. John Casto's paid sequence comes after selecting the offer, buyers, landing page, and call tracking: create five to ten concepts, then multiply them through variations such as image-background colors. Casto says a color change will not rescue a heavily losing campaign, but can reduce traffic costs enough to matter to margin. Facebook and YouTube are Casto's suggested starting platforms, with ongoing creative and landing-page improvement accompanying buyer testing rather than ending when advertisements first go live [lXG5t2aVZE8 @ 00:06:06].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Define the buyer's performance event
   - Choose one vertical and market, then establish what the buyer purchases.
   - Ask about service, ZIP codes or states, hours, daily opportunities, simultaneous calls, qualifications, accepted traffic, price, and billing. Those answers become campaign configuration. [ExclusiveLiveCall — XKj8PVuNBOs @ 00:08:38]
2. Build the funnel and establish the number
   - Use the onboarding sequence associated with the selected route.
   - For Sood's LeadSmart route, submit the website-led application, receive Ringba access, open the campaign, and add a local number for the city. [Automate & Chill By Abhishek Sood — g7SAWXINXDo @ 00:00:15] [Automate & Chill By Abhishek Sood — g7SAWXINXDo @ 00:01:15]
3. Check configuration before buying traffic
   - Test the number and review tracking, commercial terms, caps, and filters.
   - Melissa Lai describes number typos, untested setups, and unfinished IOs among beginner mistakes; take the additional minutes to check caps, filters, and restrictions before launching. [OfferVault — rsmZ9FBIPks @ 00:18:33]
4. Prove the buyer relationship before scaling
   - Run a controlled test and review answering, fit, closing, payment, and disputes.
   - ExclusiveLiveCall suggests ten or twenty opportunities, or a defined test budget, depending on the campaign. Scale the relationship after the test demonstrates that its economics work. [ExclusiveLiveCall — XKj8PVuNBOs @ 00:09:14]

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The clearest split concerns who controls the number and the call path. Sood's LeadSmart workflow puts a number obtained through the network's Ringba dashboard on the site; Asghar likewise presents the publisher's starting point as receiving company access and generating calls, rather than buying a tracking subscription first [g7SAWXINXDo @ 00:01:15] [r74nmdjWZ88 @ 00:03:23]. Decoding Ads takes the opposite position for programmatic SEO, advising against direct network numbers and favoring an operator-controlled number routed through Ringba. Their operation uses a full RTB system, and the tools described include publishers, ring trees, call flows, and integrations [OFst3KoUCj0 @ 00:00:38]. Jordan Soto gives the economic argument for independent tracking: shared concurrency caps can drop calls, buyers can end a conversation before the payout duration and call the prospect back, and affiliates without recordings cannot readily catch that behavior. Soto also recommends at least three buyers in almost all circumstances, providing alternatives when capacity or technical problems interrupt a campaign. Soto acknowledges that independent call tracking costs more than click tracking and that some affiliates operate without it, but reports significant lost calls and wasted advertising spend among the untracked operators Ringba has encountered [a4Aa0kexuh8 @ 00:04:37].

Organic funnels and paid acquisition also demand different working rhythms. Ben describes the expired-domain model as waiting for trigger events: old marketing sends someone back to a former business's domain when a need arises. JayKay's walk-in-tub funnel produced four calls in its first fourteen days, with two press-one callers and one voicemail. From May through July it recorded 165 visitors, nine calls, and three paid calls at $55 each; the first paid call came after five forwarded calls. Ben's account calls this modest and says to give funnels weeks while building the next one [eqw-Rqw7PNY @ 00:05:05]. Casto's paid campaigns require regular attention, sometimes hourly or minute by minute depending on spending and scaling. Finding and testing buyers continues alongside creative and landing-page optimization [lXG5t2aVZE8 @ 00:08:37]. Zishan, in conversation with Casto, describes a much larger content engine: test hundreds of pieces, identify winning angles, change their bodies, and double down. Zishan separates chasing a hot vertical for immediate cash from building a harder business that may take three to six months to break even. That is a different commitment from the low-cost funnel portfolio Ben describes, even though both approaches sell opportunities to buyers [WwyCibTrAlY @ 00:22:42].

Revenue stories also describe different products and workloads. Vanel Sylvestre reports roughly $14,000 over six months from MarketCall and says the traffic came without paid advertising, using landing pages promoted through Facebook groups. The flight offer shown pays $22 per target call [a32vgNX3Qkw @ 00:02:26]. Andy's later MarketCall update describes results as stable, but the check at around eleven in the morning shows no calls yet and thirteen hours remaining. Andy also reduces mentorship intake from ten to five people monthly because supporting members has become demanding [HhGv4y6TrXs @ 00:00:00]. Mark at NTAS Affiliate Marketing reports about $11,000 for November through PayKickstart, including recurring and high-ticket commissions; that is a software-sales campaign, not a payment for qualified calls. Mark's route sends Facebook-group and YouTube visitors to a blog documenting product testing, then to a name-and-email capture page and a sales page [u9io9T1yRU8 @ 00:00:43]. During the software promotion, Mark says commissions fell from $1,000 to $300 as the product price fell from $2,000 to $1,000. Mark pursued the promotion anyway and used the existing article and funnel rather than rebuilding them. The reported commission spike belongs to that sales sequence [u9io9T1yRU8 @ 00:03:37].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

Matthew Zivkovic's Marketplace history includes a substantial retreat. The in-house operation peaked around $1,000 daily revenue, but Zivkovic says it was closer to $400 daily when shut down and no longer justified the workers' expense. Acquiring additional real Facebook accounts became difficult, and purchased accounts could lose Marketplace access. Zivkovic also expresses concern that some purchased accounts might have been hacked [7lpBAuhtQBY @ 00:24:46]. The earlier revenue came from collecting prospect information and posting leads, not solely from inbound calls. Zivkovic reports that lawsuits ended that approach for the operation: people now needed to call, or complete the supplied online form themselves. Their reported response rate fell from roughly thirty-five to forty percent sharing information to twenty to twenty-five percent calling [7lpBAuhtQBY @ 00:04:15]. WordsAtScale describes another operational reversal: eLocal's junk-removal top bid had reached $85 in July, but later fell to less than half without a change on the publisher's side. Buyers changed. The network also owns the buyer relationship and can remove an offer, leaving calls without a destination. WordsAtScale therefore treats the rate card as a top bid rather than the affiliate's assured price and recommends retaining a marketplace as overflow when building direct relationships [DJFaYJ3O7aU @ 00:02:20].

Andy's ROI story has an explicit billing qualification. The July update reports $80 spent and $210 made, described by Andy as roughly 250% ROI. Andy then attributes a higher claimed 400–450% figure to leaving a final Google Ads invoice unpaid and acknowledges suspension when payment cannot be collected. The higher figure is therefore presented by Andy as an unpaid-invoice outcome, rather than a separate improvement in keyword performance or buyer payout [lHKdHRzs75g @ 00:02:47] [lHKdHRzs75g @ 00:03:55]. Ben's RHIMS review supplies a contrasting economics account: the walk-in-tub case includes both $165 gross and about $34 in costs, with only three paid calls from nine calls during the stated period. The initial fourteen days produced just four calls, and the first paid call arrived after five forwards. Ben identifies research as the real work and says the method costs about $20 per funnel to test [eqw-Rqw7PNY @ 00:05:05]. Rob Reece independently flags large income figures on the RHIMS sales page as best-case rather than typical. Reece also says they did not personally run a pay-per-call campaign for the review. That distinction belongs beside the course's low-cost pitch and the campaign figures Ben reports from JayKay's own funnel [5QOU-NXPWw4 @ 00:06:04].

The recurring breakdowns sit between traffic arrival and a paid conversation. Melissa Lai has seen beginners launch with number typos, untested setups, and unfinished IOs, then stumble when traffic starts arriving. Their remedy is specific: check the tracking, caps, filters, restrictions, and commercial paperwork before trying to accelerate [rsmZ9FBIPks @ 00:18:33]. Ringba's Jordan Soto reports affiliates losing calls and wasting media spend without independent tracking. Shared buyer capacity can leave calls unanswered, overflow can be abandoned, and callback fraud can interrupt a conversation before its payable duration. Recordings and independent statistics are Soto's tools for identifying those problems and selling overflow elsewhere [a4Aa0kexuh8 @ 00:02:29]. Zishan describes a different interruption: after a strong ACA run, a change in the calling process disrupted the vertical. Zishan's response centers on retained cash, the ability to cover payroll during slow months, and time spent developing the next opportunity. Zishan says having enough cash to remain comfortable for another year removes the pressure to force an immediate replacement campaign. The same discussion recommends putting at least forty percent of working time into the next big thing rather than assuming today's strong vertical will continue unchanged [WwyCibTrAlY @ 00:17:19].
