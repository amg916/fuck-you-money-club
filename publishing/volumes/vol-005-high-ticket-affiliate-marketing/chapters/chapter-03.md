## 1. Price the click, not the merchant's sale  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A merchant may book a large sale while the publisher earns only a fixed fee. Keep referred merchant gross merchandise value, or GMV, outside the publisher revenue ledger. Record the sale value as a merchant metric, the fee as pending commission, and the fee less expected reversals as earned affiliate revenue when the earning conditions are met. Cash received is a fourth field, not a synonym for revenue. This separation stops a dashboard from making a weak business look large. Give each click a source, offer, and cohort month. Join the merchant's transaction record to that cohort without counting repeat status updates as new sales. Where identifiers are missing, keep the balance unresolved rather than spreading it across good campaigns. A missing join is a measurement defect, not permission to claim revenue.

Use Net EPC = (Gross Conversions x Payout x (1 - Scrub_And_Clawback_Rate)) / Outbound Clicks. The denominator is tracked clicks sent to the merchant, not page views or all visits to the site. For mixed fees, sum reversal-adjusted commission dollars first and divide once by the same cohort's outbound clicks. Do not average the EPC of a tiny high value offer with a large weak offer. A hundred conversions at $500, with ten percent removed, produce $45,000 on 10,000 outbound clicks: $4.50 net EPC. That is revenue per click, not profit per click. A $3 media cost per outbound click leaves $1.50 before content, staff, tools, and financing. Name the measurement window beside every value so a young cohort is never compared directly with a fully settled one in the weekly report.

Set bids from the mature cohort rather than the best day in the ad account. A reserve should reflect refunds, invalid leads, duplicate accounts, and later contract clawbacks separately where the merchant supplies reasons. Track the fraction still pending at each age and the share of approved fees that later disappear. A new offer can have a healthy early conversion rate and a poor final yield. Keep its spend small until enough transactions pass the actual return and locking windows to support the estimate. Use a downside case with fewer sales and more scrubs before raising spend. No single universal sample size makes a small cohort reliable. Show counts alongside rates, keep a range for uncertain losses, and price the bid against the conservative end until actual settlements narrow that range.

## 2. Compare upfront cash with retained subscriptions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Compare a $500 upfront CPA with a thirty percent share of a $200 monthly SaaS bill. The recurring fee is $60 for each paid active month. Assume the first bill succeeds, monthly retention stays constant, and both deals lose ten percent of fees to scrubs distinct from churn. At ninety percent monthly retention, expected active months sum to 7.18 over twelve months and 9.20 over twenty-four months. Net recurring fees are therefore about $387 and $497 per acquired customer, against $450 for the upfront deal. These are cohort expectations, not promises about an individual buyer. They exclude changes in price, expansion revenue, discounting, and contract termination. State those limits next to the comparison. A recurring offer that wins at month twenty-four can still lose badly on the cash needed to buy traffic today.

At ninety-five percent monthly retention, the same sum rises to 9.19 paid months over twelve months and 14.16 over twenty-four months. Multiplying by the $54 net monthly fee gives roughly $496 and $765. Use the geometric sum (1 - r**n) / (1 - r), with retention r and horizon n; at full retention use n directly. These figures assume churn begins after the first paid month and fees remain payable throughout the horizon. Check whether the merchant caps months, excludes renewals, changes attribution, or ends trailing payments when the publisher leaves. Treat unearned future renewal fees as a forecast, not current revenue or collateral. Discount future cash at the business's chosen funding hurdle when comparing present values, and stress a contract change as well as a retention decline before selecting the winner.

A hybrid deal can trade some immediate CPA for a smaller recurring share, but its headline parts do not automatically add to more value. Suppose it pays $250 upfront plus fifteen percent of the same monthly bill. With ten percent fee loss and ninety percent retention, net expected receipts are $225 plus $27 times retained paid months: about $419 over twelve months and $473 over twenty-four months. Ask whether the upfront fee is an advance against later revenue share; if so, offset it rather than counting it twice. Compare cash dates, refund exposure, and caps across all three contracts. Keep observed retention by acquisition source because cheap traffic may buy customers who leave sooner. The best blend is the one that pays its own acquisition and service costs under a downside retention curve.

## 3. Fund the interval between earning and collecting  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the cash forecast from actual contract dates. A thirty to ninety day locking window may run before the payment clock starts, or the stated payment term may already include it. Never add both by habit or omit either without reading the settlement terms. The worked cohort below assumes $45,000 of earned fees, $34,500 of cash operating costs, and collection sixty days after the cohort closes, with no extra lock. Two full monthly cost cycles require $69,000 before the first receipt under this simplified monthly convention. Net-30 requires one cycle, or $34,500. A separate ninety day lock followed by Net-60 could require five cycles, or $172,500. Actual daily timing, month-end billing, holidays, and payment disputes can extend the gap. Forecast them by week and hold a liquidity buffer rather than treating the round monthly estimate as the maximum exposure.

The ten percent scrub reserve reduces the $50,000 pending commission balance to $45,000 expected earned revenue. It is not a second operating expense and does not itself provide cash. Retain the reserve until settlement evidence supports releasing it; late clawbacks still need a cash buffer after the original invoice is paid. The table assumes $1,000 of content and tooling, $3,000 of people cost, and $500 of financing and other overhead alongside $30,000 media. The resulting $10,500 is illustrative monthly owner profit before owner income taxes, not the base annual scenario below. Treat it as a different cohort used to expose the cash mechanics. A positive accrual profit does not prevent a missed card payment when all collections sit in receivables. Keep both the profit bridge and the dated cash ledger visible during every spend decision.

A credit line is a bridge only when its repayment source and timing are explicit. Measure committed availability after existing draws, borrowing restrictions, and covenant limits; an advertised credit limit is not spendable cash. Add interest and fees to the forecast and test a delayed merchant payment before drawing. Principal repayment is a cash outflow, not a profit expense. Stop buying incremental traffic when the downside cash balance would fall below the required buffer, even if the mature EPC remains attractive. Growing monthly spend changes the bridge: sum each actual future week's disbursements rather than multiplying last month's costs by two. Diversify settlement exposure where practical, but do not count another merchant's pending balance as immediate liquidity. A funded plan needs cash or committed borrowing that remains available through the stressed collection date, not merely expected lifetime commissions.

## 4. Keep adjacent plays inside real economic boundaries  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play sportsbook_promo_ev_matched_betting_syndicates belongs in a contract review, not in an assumption that promotional betting produces affiliate profit. State requirements differ: confirm the relevant gaming regulator's affiliate registration or licensing rules, permitted compensation structure, and operator authorization before serving that jurisdiction. A merchant's own license does not establish the publisher's status. CPA pays for a defined qualified customer; gaming revenue share depends on contract-defined net gaming revenue, deductions, and possibly negative carryover. A losing revenue-share month can offset later positive earnings where the contract allows it. Track that balance separately from customer refunds. Self-referrals, duplicate identities, coordinated bonus exploitation, prohibited incentivized traffic, and other promo-abuse indicators can trigger scrubs or termination. Do not build a forecast around defeating those controls. Compare lawful acquisition cohorts using settled fees, and price jurisdictional restrictions into the reachable traffic pool.

The mapped play telecom_voice_traffic_pumping_8yy_sms_arbitrage requires a hard distinction between genuine customer utility and artificial traffic designed to extract intercarrier payments. FCC access-stimulation restrictions and tariff obligations, including relevant provisions of 47 CFR Part 61, constrain covered arrangements; Part 61 is not a complete account of every 8YY or messaging rule. Do not budget profit from fabricated calls, induced routing loops, or inflated billable minutes. A legitimate model can charge for useful inbound call handling, appointment routing, or opt-in SMS service with clear customer value and permitted carrier use. Measure completed service interactions, customer retention, carrier charges, and complaint rates rather than raw traffic volume. Opt-in alone does not resolve every messaging requirement. Verify the applicable consent, identification, opt-out, carrier, and numbering rules for the actual service before treating a telecom partner's payout schedule as durable revenue.

The mapped play agency_ad_spend_card_multiplier_rebate_cycling is a cost adjustment, not a second profit engine. Model eligible realized card points or rebates as a two to four percent media-cost reduction only when issuer commercial-card terms, reward caps, redemption values, and payment discipline support it. At $100,000 eligible spend, that is $2,000 to $4,000 before interest, fees, or lost discounts; debt costs can erase the benefit. Exclude manufactured spend, circular transactions, and client funds whose reward ownership is unresolved. Record a qualifying purchase rebate against the related cost in this management model, with the accountant determining tax treatment from the actual arrangement. Section 162 supports ordinary and necessary business expense deductions but does not classify every reward. Separately, Part 255 calls for clear disclosure of material affiliate relationships; truthful offer comparisons protect conversion quality as well as the reader.

## 5. Reconcile the annual model before buying growth  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Candidate #24's annual scenarios start with gross earned commissions net of reversals: $50,000 conservative, $325,000 base, and $600,000 scaled. Here gross means before operating expenses, not before scrubs. Their exact owner profit targets are $10,000 at twenty percent margin, $113,750 at thirty-five percent, and $300,000 at fifty percent. Staffing is 1 FT + 2 C in both conservative and base, then 2 FT + 3 C at scale; FT denotes full-time capacity and C denotes contractors. In this model the owner supplies one FT role and receives the residual profit rather than a salary charged again as an expense. People costs cover nonowner labor and limited contractor hours. This is a workload assumption, not a claim that the conservative budget buys three market-rate salaries. Price a replacement owner salary separately when assessing whether the operation is an attractive investment rather than merely a job.

The annual table is a complete illustrative operating profit bridge, not an extrapolation of the monthly paid cohort. Its different margins require different traffic mixes, operating costs, and mature recurring stacks. Media is shown net of any realized qualifying rebates; never subtract those rewards again below profit. Content includes production work outside the people line, tools include software and tracking, and other costs include finance charges and remaining overhead. Actual employer taxes and contractor fees belong in people costs where incurred. Owner profit is before owner income taxes and distributions. No merchant GMV is included and no reversal reserve is deducted twice. A business with equipment purchases, debt principal, or rising receivables also needs a cash-flow bridge because those items change cash without matching this profit statement. Replace each assumption with booked actuals monthly and explain the variance before adopting a higher revenue target.

Use milestones that test the mechanism instead of treating an annual target as a spend instruction. During the first week, capture the current offer terms and rebuild the click-to-settlement ledger for existing cohorts. During the second, calculate reversal-adjusted EPC and retained subscription value using only observed data, with explicit ranges where evidence is thin. During the third, map weekly cash needs through the longest applicable lock and settlement schedule and price committed financing. During the fourth, authorize only the incremental spend that the downside model can fund while preserving its buffer. These weeks establish measurement, not proof that a ninety day cohort has matured. Keep tracking beyond the first month until cash arrives and late reversals are understood. Move toward the scaled case only when repeatable acquisition, retained commissions, and staffing capacity explain the improvement in margin; a larger credit limit alone explains none of it.
