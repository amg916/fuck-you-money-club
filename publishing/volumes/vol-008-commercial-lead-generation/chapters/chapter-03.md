## Executive Summary

Practitioners agree the big databases are crowded and incomplete: Apollo and Sales Navigator miss hotel, farm, and wholesale buyers, so operators stack LinkedIn pulls with Google Maps scrapes run city by city or zip code by zip code. Precision beats volume, with Alex Vacca favoring 50 matched prospects over 500 random names and Evaboot finding at least 20% of Sales Navigator results off-criteria. The mechanics are cheap: Apify's Google Maps Scraper at about $4 per thousand places, cookie-based Apify scrapers replacing PhantomBuster, and Evaboot at $9 per 100 credits. Email coverage is the choke point, from a 2% Philippines match rate to Prospeo's test showing 73% accuracy on verified Apollo leads. Disagreements center on change-job filters, Apollo's built-in intent data, and whether AI personalization still earns replies.

## 1. The Practitioner Consensus

The operators who build lists for a living agree on one uncomfortable truth: the big databases are crowded and incomplete. Lead Gen PH warns that Apollo can work, but so many other senders pull from it that more creative sourcing produces fresher contacts with fewer competitors hitting the same inbox [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:00:29]. Will Butler goes further, calling Apollo, ZoomInfo and Sales Navigator outdated tools built for software buyers, and showing that a commercial solar installer's real targets are not on Sales Navigator or Apollo at all [FJ2CA-mGueY @ 00:00:32]. Eric of Growth Engine X hits the same wall with wholesale clients: the hotel managers who buy vacuums rarely keep LinkedIn profiles, which makes list building the hardest part of every onboarding [BRZJjgou7ic @ 00:01:25]. The shared fix is a two-source stack. Teams grab whatever LinkedIn coverage exists first, then scrape Google Maps to fill the gaps, with Growth Engine X running hotel, motel, spa, resort and casino keywords across every zip code in the United States to build a repository that LinkedIn-only competitors are not working from [BRZJjgou7ic @ 00:02:05]. The shared operating logic is that fresher, less-contacted prospects produce better results than the same database export everyone else is already mailing.

Every practitioner also agrees that precision beats volume. Alex Vacca puts a number on it: 50 well-matched prospects get better results than 500 random names, so start with small, tightly targeted pulls and only then scale [Ouq5ayjDmmI @ 00:09:03]. Marcus Chan builds deliberately niched saved searches that hold fewer leads each, then disqualifies hard, reporting that over half the prospects the team reviews miss the size or trigger bar and never enter a sequence [HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:21:51]. Jed Marley of Sell Better starts with a saved persona covering function, seniority set to director and VP, and geography, so every later filter only returns ideal buyers inside the territory [3Y7sW7ob3hY @ 00:01:29]. The reason precision must be enforced rather than trusted comes from JB at Evaboot: at least 20% of Sales Navigator leads do not actually match the search criteria, with an intern showing up inside a head-of-sales search [rryPKFY2BRI @ 00:05:44]. Chan sees the same decay at the contact level and re-verifies that each prospect still works at the listed company, because the record is sometimes simply wrong. The consensus routine is filter tight, eyeball the actual profiles, cut anyone outside the ICP, and adjust settings until a sample looks right.

The third point of agreement: a name is not an email, and coverage is where lists die. Lead Gen PH explains that LinkedIn never hands over the address; tools guess it from first name, last name and company, and a healthy match rate is 20 to 25%, sometimes 50%, while their Metro Manila test of 343 rows returned only 7% [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:13:51]. Evaboot's JB sets expectations at 40 to 90% email discovery depending on industry, with startup employees easy to find and construction workers hard because they share less information online [rryPKFY2BRI @ 00:03:10]. Even verified data leaks: Prospeo ran 1,000 verified Apollo leads and measured 73% email accuracy against Apollo's claimed 91%, meaning over 270 addresses bounced [QnSW0B2oztg @ 00:09:46]. Will Butler treats every Google Maps pull the same way, saying the output is not 100% clean and needs enrichment, which the team runs through Clay to find emails and phone numbers before sending [FJ2CA-mGueY @ 00:10:13]. So every serious operator budgets a verification or enrichment pass between scrape and send, and nobody trusts a raw export to land in an inbox regardless of which source produced it.

## 2. Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

On LinkedIn, the mechanics start with Sales Navigator, which Luke Weiler prices at one month free then $100 a month and Lead Gen PH says community partners can get down to roughly $30. Build the search, aim for an audience near 2,000, and respect the hard ceiling: only 2,500 come out per extraction, so split big searches by geography, scraping Metro Manila first and excluding it on the next pass [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:03:13]. Weiler skips PhantomBuster entirely: sign up for Apify, open the LinkedIn Sales Navigator Scraper actor, export LinkedIn homepage cookies as JSON with the free Cookie Editor extension, paste them in with the search URL, and set minimum and maximum wait durations to avoid tripping spam flags [zR0U-wv3Bh8 @ 00:02:16]. Weiler reports pulling 1,400 results in under ten minutes, the same data PhantomBuster would deliver for $100 [zR0U-wv3Bh8 @ 00:05:31]. The paid alternative is Evaboot, which adds an export button inside Sales Navigator and charges $9 per 100 credits, one credit per lead plus one per email found, so 100 credits buys 50 leads with emails [rryPKFY2BRI @ 00:08:16]. Its file also strips emojis and all-caps names and flags off-target rows before outreach.

Google Maps is scraped city by city, never state by state. Georgey Tishin explains that searching 'HVAC in New Jersey' versus walking specific towns like Edison is the difference between roughly 500 and 5,000 results, because Google will not display every company for a broad query [p1rfyeLoS2s @ 00:00:53]. The free route is the Instant Data Scraper extension with a three-to-five-second delay on the next button, but it bugs out and Edison alone returned only 92 rows, so Tishin's paid route feeds a city-list file into a Discord export command that pulled 816 companies in about three minutes [p1rfyeLoS2s @ 00:05:29]. Lead Gen PH prefers Apify's Google Maps Scraper at about $4 per thousand places and, after testing the add-ons, turns on only company contact enrichment, which visits each listed website and harvests the emails Google Maps does not show [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:10:23]. Alex Vacca then filters the raw dataset: has a website or no website depending on the offer, 50-plus reviews as a sign of an established business that can afford you, four stars or above, and category cleanup to remove irrelevant results [Ouq5ayjDmmI @ 00:06:22]. Maps rows carry company names but usually no first name, so copy has to be written for the business.

Apollo gets filtered harder than most users bother. Prospeo's walkthrough notes that niche verticals like fintech return nothing in the industry filter but surface under company keyword search, pairs that with 51-to-200 headcount over the unreliable revenue and funding fields, and sticks to verified emails and mobile numbers only [QnSW0B2oztg @ 00:02:39]. For volume without the bill, Kirke Männik says Apollo's API change killed external scrapers, so they run an Apify Apollo lead scraper with the search URL and Cookie Editor JSON, waits of 5 to 15 seconds, and a fresh account whenever the 48-hour trial expires [2OJzjgoV9mo @ 00:00:33]. Marcus Chan layers triggers on top of any list: under one year in the current company, posted on LinkedIn, and a Growth Insights check where 35% sales job-opening growth plus a ChatGPT prompt pulling quota attainment, 23.6% at one account, becomes the opening point of view [HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:11:07]. At scale, Growth Engine X skips paid model endpoints and tells Claude Code or Codex to spawn sub-agents on cheaper models, Sonnet or 5.4 mini or nano, to ICP-score the entire scraped database inside existing usage [BRZJjgou7ic @ 00:03:15].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the persona and saved searches
   - Save function, seniority, and geography as a persona, then build narrow saved searches with trigger filters.
   - Set director and VP seniority plus territory in a persona [Sell Better — 3Y7sW7ob3hY @ 00:01:29], then build niched saved searches using under-one-year tenure and posted-on-LinkedIn filters [Marcus Chan — HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:05:33].
2. Pull LinkedIn-covered contacts and find emails
   - Export each search under the 2,500 cap and run email finding on every row.
   - Split searches by geography to stay under the 2,500-per-pull limit [Lead Gen PH — i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:03:13], and budget Evaboot credits at one per lead plus one per email found [JB - Evaboot — rryPKFY2BRI @ 00:08:16].
3. Fill the gaps with Google Maps
   - Scrape every keyword across every zip code or city, with website contact enrichment switched on.
   - Run category keywords across every US zip code and exclude companies already found [Smartlead — BRZJjgou7ic @ 00:02:05], enabling only the company contact enrichment add-on in Apify [Lead Gen PH — i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:10:23].
4. Filter, score, and load weekly
   - Filter raw rows, ICP-score with cheap sub-agents, and add qualified prospects to sequences on a fixed weekly block.
   - Filter for website, 50-plus reviews, and four stars [Alex Vacca — Ouq5ayjDmmI @ 00:06:22], ICP-score with Sonnet or mini sub-agents [Smartlead — BRZJjgou7ic @ 00:03:15], and refresh saved searches every Monday [Marcus Chan — HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:26:03].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first fault line is whether to rent a shared database or mine fresher ground. Cleverly runs the database play straight: sign up for Apollo, filter for something like project managers at companies with 200 to 1,000 employees, click to reveal emails, select all and export, and because Apollo doubles as a sending tool, that is the recommendation [VXChXeJuEhI @ 00:00:56]. Lead Gen PH concedes Apollo can work but warns that plenty of other senders are pulling the same records, so the more creative the source, the fresher the leads, the fewer people contacting them and the better the replies [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:00:43]. Will Butler goes harder, calling Apollo, ZoomInfo and Sales Navigator outdated and incomplete outside the software and SaaS space they were built for; commercial solar buyers, from vineyards to cold storage facilities, simply are not in those tools, so Butler scrapes Google Maps through Apify instead and points to one live workspace that generated 347 new leads over 120 days [FJ2CA-mGueY @ 00:00:16]. Eric on the Smartlead show splits the difference: everyone else is grabbing LinkedIn data, so the team searches every US zip code for keywords like hotel, motel, spa, resort and casino, building a second repository that fills the gaps LinkedIn leaves [BRZJjgou7ic @ 00:02:22].

Signal selection splits the LinkedIn camp. Jed Marley of Sell Better builds a persona, then layers changed jobs in the last 90 days, arguing new leaders want immediate impact and should be hit as early as their first month while they are still in information-gathering mode [3Y7sW7ob3hY @ 00:03:23]. Marcus Chan finds that same changed-jobs filter hit or miss, sometimes flat wrong, and swaps in years in current company set to less than one year, preferring someone around 11 months in who has seen the lay of the land over someone two months in who is still overwhelmed [HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:04:56]. Intent data draws the same split. Marley uses the Sales Navigator intent feature to cut a thousand-lead list down to the warmest leads [3Y7sW7ob3hY @ 00:13:57], while the Prospeo presenter says they do not rely much on Apollo's buying intent filter and instead source intent by checking LinkedIn company pages for roles left unfilled for over two months [QnSW0B2oztg @ 00:04:10]. Chan builds intent from growth insights, flagging a 35% rise in sales job openings, then runs a custom ChatGPT prompt that pulls quota attainment and strategic priorities into trigger notes before anyone enters the sequence [HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:11:07].

The cost debate runs from zero-dollar hacks to paid convenience, and every practitioner prices their own time differently. Mac Creator Workflow sells the free route: Instant Data Scraper on a Google Maps search pulls names, ratings and websites in under three seconds, then ChatGPT cleans the mess and writes a free-video-audit cold email [kRxM5oUoNno @ 00:00:58]. Georgey Tishin uses the same extension but calls it the inconvenient way, since it bugs out and Edison, New Jersey returned only 92 results, meaning hours of city-by-city manual scraping; the paid Lead Academy scraper at $99 a month returned 816 companies in one run and 887 unique leads across five New Jersey cities [p1rfyeLoS2s @ 00:03:50]. On Sales Navigator, Luke Weiler replaces a $100 PhantomBuster habit with a free Apify actor fed by Cookie Editor, while Evaboot charges $9 per 100 credits and argues the payoff is cleaned data and match flags [zR0U-wv3Bh8 @ 00:06:12]. Copy philosophy splits too: Eric at Smartlead says the AI personalization edge has faded and the test is blunt, if another customer could use the same AI prompt, skip it, leaning on segmentation because the list is the message [BRZJjgou7ic @ 00:04:31].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive trap is assuming a big audience equals a big list. Lead Gen PH built a 2,000-person Sales Navigator audience of event managers for a Philippine fireworks client, ran it through ICPS, and got a 2% email match rate; a later 343-row test hit only 7% against the 20 to 25%, sometimes 50%, they want to see [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:07:09]. The cause was structural: most of those buyers use Gmail rather than a firstname-at-company domain, so email-guessing tools stall, and the fix was Apify's Google Maps scraper with only the company contact enrichment add-on switched on, at roughly $4 per thousand places [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:09:29]. Evaboot's JB sets expectations at 40 to 90% discovery depending on industry, with startup employees easy to find and construction firms hard because they share less online [rryPKFY2BRI @ 00:03:28]. Will Butler hits the mirror problem on Maps: a solar run cost 13 cents, but one contact came back with a phone number and no email, forcing a Clay enrichment pass, and the data is not 100% clean [FJ2CA-mGueY @ 00:09:53]. Lead Gen PH also flags that Maps exports lack first names, so copy has to be written without them.

Verified rarely means verified. The Prospeo presenter ran 1,000 Apollo leads marked verified and measured 73% email accuracy, over 270 bounces, against Apollo's claimed 275 million contacts and 91% accuracy, then reran the identical ICP, filters and messaging on Prospect.io data and reports 98% accuracy with almost zero bounces, a head-to-head run on the Prospeo channel's own product [QnSW0B2oztg @ 00:09:46]. Evaboot's JB says maybe at least 20% of Sales Navigator search results do not match the search criteria, showing a head-of-sales search returning an intern, which is why the export carries match-filter and no-match-reason columns [rryPKFY2BRI @ 00:05:44]. Marcus Chan verifies every profile is still at the listed company because records are sometimes out of date, and reports that over half the time prospects fail the parameters, such as having fewer than 25 AEs, and get skipped rather than sequenced [HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:21:51]. Lead Gen PH adds a hard ceiling: Sales Navigator sources at most 2,500 leads per pull, so larger audiences must be sliced by geography, for example Metro Manila first and then excluded on the next pass [i-oQ5e48OF8 @ 00:03:13]. Skipping these checks, JB warns, burns time, energy and focus on leads outside the target.

Tooling fragility is the third trap. Kirke Männik opens by noting Apollo changed its API and put external scrapers out of business, then routes around it with an Apify Apollo lead scraper fed by Cookie Editor cookies, five-to-15-second waits, and fresh accounts every time the 48-hour trial lapses [2OJzjgoV9mo @ 00:00:33]. Alex Vacca warns that platforms block aggressive scraping, that only public profiles can be pulled, and that actor quality varies, so check reviews, usage and pricing for two minutes and start with small runs before scaling [Ouq5ayjDmmI @ 00:11:55]. Luke Weiler treats the minimum and maximum wait settings as protection against mass-scraping spam flags, and still watched a 50-lead test crawl for minutes after a prior 1,400-lead pull finished in under ten minutes [zR0U-wv3Bh8 @ 00:03:26]. Process decay kills lists too: Marcus Chan says the common mistake is treating Sales Navigator as a one-time event or a phone book, and the team instead reviews saved searches every Monday, with Chan blocking a minimum of eight hours a week for cold outbound as an AE [HL8AeTPufLU @ 00:25:48]. Searching names and hoping for the best, in Chan's words, is not prospecting but busy work.
