## 1. Assign accountable seats before adding inboxes or clients  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start Candidate #18 with one full-time owner and three contractors, not four interchangeable outreach generalists. The owner holds sales, pricing, customer acceptance definitions, collections, and final account accountability. Contractor one owns account research, contact provenance, deduplication, and suppression matching. Contractor two owns authenticated sending infrastructure, sequence deployment, mailbox monitoring, and technical incident recovery. Contractor three owns reply triage, qualification evidence, appointment confirmation, and CRM disposition. Each responsibility needs a primary owner, a documented backup, and an observable completion artifact. In the four-person pod, backups provide limited continuity rather than another full production shift. Maintain Candidate #18's 54 mapped plays in one responsibility register, preserving their existing identifiers and assigning exactly one accountable seat per play. Add fields for required inputs, completion evidence, service deadline, exception owner, and recovery instructions. Do not invent fifty-four new initiatives or run every play simultaneously. Activate only the subset needed for the current offer and channel, while retaining ownership for dormant plays. A HubSpot pipeline, a Google Workspace evidence folder, and a password manager can support this structure without hiring a separate operations manager. Separate client workspaces and access permissions from the start.

Contractor statements of work should purchase defined outcomes and service availability rather than disguise employment through invoices. An illustrative Base monthly schedule pays research $1,800, delivery infrastructure $2,200, and qualification $1,500, totaling $5,500 monthly or $66,000 annually before separately budgeted incentives. These are planning assumptions, not required market rates or the entire expense base. Specify accepted account batches, monitored mailbox capacity, qualified reply coverage, and rework responsibility against each fee. Require research corrections within two business days, urgent suppression propagation within one business day, and reply routing within four covered business hours. Define coverage hours explicitly; a small pod cannot silently promise continuous service. Pay agreed base invoices on time and attach any variable reward to audited accepted outcomes, not sends or raw appointments. Investigate whether federal common-law employment factors and applicable state tests permit each arrangement; California Labor Code section 2775 can impose an ABC test subject to statutory exceptions. A contractor label does not settle classification. Preserve genuine control over work methods where appropriate, and use employment when the actual relationship requires it. The fee schedule must also identify which tools the business purchases directly and which contractor costs are included.

At Scaled, add one full-time client operations lead and one contractor dedicated to independent QA and routing audits. The owner retains commercial strategy and major renewals; the new employee owns routine customer reviews, capacity planning, collections follow-through, and incident coordination. Research, infrastructure, and qualification remain distinct contractor seats, while the fourth contractor samples their outputs without approving their own production. This produces exactly two full-time people plus four contractors, not a hidden seventh account manager. Hire against sustained workload and projected contribution, not a single unusually large order. For four consecutive weeks, record minutes spent on accepted outcomes, rework, customer meetings, and exceptions; reserve twenty percent of scheduled delivery capacity for variability. As an illustrative capacity test, eighteen clients requiring three weekly account-service hours consume fifty-four hours before prospecting production. That load cannot fit inside a single forty-hour seat and requires narrower service scope, redistributed work, or fewer clients. Maintain tested playbooks for domain loss, calendar failure, absent contractors, and disputed qualification. Each active mapped play should survive a planned five-business-day absence of its primary operator before further client volume is added. Measure continuity with completed artifacts, not assurances.

## 2. Pay for accepted demand and run one weekly evidence review  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Define the commercial acceptance event before calculating any KPI. For a qualified appointment, require the agreed company segment, geography, role or purchasing influence, relevant business need, and an explicit meeting commitment. State whether the purchased unit is a booked meeting, a held meeting, or a qualified connected call; these are different products and cannot share an ambiguous fee trigger. A call-routing agreement should specify minimum connection conditions, duplicate lookback, excluded callers, and evidence retained. Store timestamps and qualification fields in HubSpot or Salesforce, linking each event to campaign, customer, contractor, and source cohort. Record an appointment identifier that persists through rescheduling so one prospect does not become three billed outcomes. Give customers a defined evidence-based review period, such as five business days, with escalation for substantive disputes. Silence should count as acceptance only when the signed agreement expressly provides that mechanism. Keep rejected, credited, and disputed events visible rather than deleting them to improve conversion reports. For every rejection, distinguish targeting failure, false qualification, duplicate delivery, customer scheduling failure, and prospect withdrawal. Those causes drive different remedies and prevent contractors from absorbing losses they did not cause.

Run a thirty-minute weekly review using a frozen reporting window and a small set of reproducible ratios. Acceptance rate equals accepted submissions divided by submissions whose review period has closed; hold rate equals held meetings divided by meetings whose scheduled time has passed. Report unresolved submissions separately rather than counting them as successes. Add credited fees divided by originally billed outcome fees, median routing latency, reply coverage attainment, and customer-level contribution after direct labor and attributable tools. Inspect positive replies and qualified outcomes by domain and source cohort, while treating open rates as diagnostic noise rather than a payment basis. Initial internal targets might be at least eighty-five percent acceptance, seventy-five percent held meetings, no more than five percent credited outcome fees, and ninety-five percent routing within the contracted service window. These are explicit starting assumptions to recalibrate against segment economics, not universal industry benchmarks. Any suppression failure receives immediate investigation even when aggregate KPIs look healthy. Close the meeting with one named owner and one deadline for each exception. Avoid twenty competing dashboard tiles that leave nobody accountable for the customer experience.

Design incentives so good qualification pays more than calendar stuffing. An illustrative contractor bonus pays $15 for each incremental accepted held meeting above the agreed baseline, provided the sampled qualification audit meets its threshold and the contractor-controlled routing SLA is satisfied. Forty eligible meetings produce a $600 bonus; include that cost in the campaign's contribution calculation before offering the arrangement. Do not retroactively change earned compensation because a customer pays late; define any collection-dependent component prospectively in the agreement. Audit every customer dispute plus ten randomly selected submissions per active contractor weekly, or all submissions when volume is smaller. The owner performs this check in the starting pod; the independent QA contractor performs it at Scaled. Preserve the sample selection method to deter cherry-picking. Multi-inbox infrastructure must respect provider terms, accurate sender identity, applicable opt-out duties, and recipient preferences; extra domains are not permission to evade blocks. CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. section 7704, applies to commercial email including B2B messages. Phone workflows require separate assessment under 47 U.S.C. section 227 and 47 CFR section 64.1200; a business label alone does not resolve technology, number-type, or consent requirements.

**Weekly Acceptance, Contractor Quality, and Routing Scorecard**

| Metric with explicit measurement population | Calculation and evidence required | Initial operating target or treatment | Named response when performance breaks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acceptance rate for submissions with completed customer review windows | Divide accepted submissions by all submissions with closed review periods | Start at eighty-five percent, then recalibrate against segment economics | Qualification owner samples rejection reasons before changing targeting or scripts |
| Held meeting rate for appointments whose scheduled time has passed | Divide completed meetings by scheduled meetings within the matured cohort | Start at seventy-five percent with reschedules tracked under persistent identifiers | Qualification owner tests reminders and isolates customer calendar failures |
| Credit rate for outcome fees originally billed during each cohort | Divide credited outcome fees by original billed outcome fee dollars | Investigate levels above five percent without deleting disputed event history | Owner separates qualification defects from customer-caused exceptions before issuing remedies |
| Routing service attainment during explicitly contracted business coverage hours | Divide timely qualified-reply assignments by all assignments requiring covered-hour routing | Target ninety-five percent within the agreed four-business-hour response window | Infrastructure owner repairs routing while qualification owner clears stranded replies |
| Direct contribution per customer after attributable delivery resource costs | Subtract allocated delivery labor, incentives, and attributable tools from revenue | Require positive contribution and a documented path toward scenario margins | Owner reprices scope or reduces costly exceptions before renewing service |
| Suppression integrity across every active sending and routing workflow | Compare opt-out timestamps against subsequent sends and routing eligibility | Treat every confirmed post-suppression error as an incident requiring investigation | Infrastructure owner pauses affected workflows and records verified corrective evidence |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly evidence window
   - Export matured submissions, scheduled meetings, credits, routing events, and contractor coverage records.
   - Use one timezone, persistent event identifiers, and a dated export so later edits cannot silently change the reviewed denominator.
2. Audit before rewarding production
   - Review every dispute and the documented random sample against the signed qualification rubric.
   - Record defect category, evidence link, responsible seat, and whether the event qualifies for the pre-agreed incentive.
3. Resolve one accountable action per exception
   - Run the thirty-minute review and assign corrections with specific deadlines.
   - Stop affected sending immediately for suppression failures; do not wait for the next reporting cycle to contain an active incident.
4. Close compensation and update the play register
   - Approve earned fees, record rework obligations, and revise the affected mapped play's runbook.
   - Retain the original scorecard, payment calculation, corrective evidence, and revised runbook version in the customer operating record.

## 3. Reconcile accrual earnings and cap transferable-book concentration  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Reconcile every operating plan to the canonical scenarios before discussing growth. Conservative revenue of $100,000 less $80,000 of expenses produces $20,000 owner net profit at twenty percent. Base revenue of $425,000 less $297,500 produces $127,500 at thirty percent. Scaled revenue of $750,000 less $450,000 produces $300,000 at forty percent. Those totals describe retainers and accepted-lead fees, excluding client-funded advertising pass-through; they do not describe customer pipeline value, merchant sales, or prospective deal size. An illustrative Base bridge uses five customers paying $5,000 monthly retainers, producing $300,000, plus 500 accepted outcomes billed at $250, producing $125,000. This assumes full-year retention and separate contractual deliverables for the retainer and outcome fee. Allocate one hundred accepted outcomes to each customer and annual revenue becomes $85,000 per customer, or twenty percent each. Monthly recognized revenue averages $35,416.67 but cash receipts can differ materially. The $66,000 illustrative contractor base fees belong inside the $297,500 Base expense envelope, leaving $231,500 for all remaining costs and adjustments. Do not treat that remainder as another profit pool. Document actual payroll, tools, acquisition, insurance, refunds, and overhead against it.

Close the books monthly on a consistent accrual basis, with customer contracts defining service periods and acceptance evidence supporting earned fees. Advance retainer cash becomes deferred revenue until the contracted service is delivered; an annual prepayment is not automatically current-month earnings. Accrue contractor work already performed even when an invoice arrives next month, and estimate credits using documented disputes and cohort experience. Use QuickBooks Online or Xero customer dimensions, supported by a CRM event ledger, to reconcile invoices, recognized revenue, credits, cash, and receivables. Track advertising funds in separate clearing records consistent with the contractual principal-agent arrangement; never inflate the canonical operator revenue with customer media spend. Build monthly acquisition cohorts showing first service date, opening recurring fees, expansion, contraction, churn, accepted outcomes, and direct delivery cost. Net revenue retention measures retained recurring fees plus expansion minus contraction and churn against the same opening customer cohort; disclose whether variable outcome fees are included. Report fixed-retainer retention separately when variable volumes obscure stability. Reconcile aged receivables to customer disputes, since apparently profitable delivery can consume cash when acceptance remains unresolved. A buyer should trace any reported cohort total back to individual events and signed scopes.

Set a maximum twenty-five percent exposure to any customer group, referral counterparty, or material delivery counterparty, measured against the relevant denominator. Combine affiliates under common control; five invoices to one corporate parent do not create diversification. For customers, measure trailing-twelve-month recognized revenue and forward ninety-day contracted revenue separately. For referral partners, measure the revenue of accounts they control or can withdraw. For nonexclusive delivery vendors, measure critical production capacity and document substitute availability. During startup, a single first customer necessarily breaches the customer cap; label that exposure openly and avoid claiming the transferable-book standard has been met. Stop expanding that account until diversification restores the limit, unless consciously accepting a documented temporary exception. Prefer twenty percent as the operating target so a neighboring account's churn does not immediately create a breach. Where a core CRM or mailbox provider cannot practically meet the counterparty limit, report the exception and test export, restoration, and alternate-provider migration. Splitting mailboxes into multiple tenants at the same provider does not diversify that dependency. Concentration is a transferable earnings issue: losing one account must not destroy the pod's ability to cover its fixed obligations.

> 
> These four checks establish whether the pod's reported earnings can survive operational and buyer scrutiny.
> - Recalculate all three canonical revenue-minus-expense bridges and confirm that customer-funded advertising never enters the operator revenue numerator.
> - Verify that every active mapped play has an accountable seat, an authorized backup, measurable acceptance evidence, and a tested recovery instruction.
> - Recompute customer-group and referral exposure against the twenty-five percent cap, then disclose startup breaches and unavoidable platform dependencies with dated remediation plans.
> - Trace a sample of accepted outcomes through signed criteria, CRM evidence, accrual entries, credits, contractor compensation, and actual customer receipts.

## 4. Transfer enforceable assets and value the earnings a buyer can keep  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the transfer package while operating, not after receiving an offer. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a writing signed by the owner or their authorized agent. Obtain signed assignments for custom scripts, copy, documentation, and other transferable work; identify excluded pre-existing materials and the licenses needed to use them. Do not assume contractor payment creates ownership or that every commissioned asset qualifies as work made for hire. Separately inventory domains, telephone numbers, trademarks without decorative symbols, CRM configurations, customer contracts, and licensed data. A copyright assignment does not override data-provider restrictions, privacy duties, or a customer's contractual limits. Review assignment and change-of-control clauses before promising contract transferability. Use business-controlled administrative accounts with named individual access, recovery procedures, and audit logs; never make a buyer dependent on a departing contractor's personal mailbox. For customer and prospect records, document permitted uses, retention schedules, suppression obligations, and any transaction-related transfer conditions. For covered California businesses, evaluate sale, sharing, and service provider obligations under the California Consumer Privacy Act, including Civil Code sections 1798.100 and 1798.120. B2B contact information is not categorically outside that framework.

Price the business using a clearly named earnings measure rather than multiplying revenue because the service uses automation. For illustration only, evaluate a small owner-operated book at two to three times normalized seller discretionary earnings, and a genuinely manager-run operation at three to four-and-a-half times normalized EBITDA. These are underwriting sensitivities, not observed transaction comparables or promised bids. Smaller, concentrated, project-heavy books can warrant less; verified retention, clean rights, and low founder dependence may support stronger pricing. Canonical owner net profit is not automatically either valuation denominator. If a reconciliation establishes that Base's $127,500 equals normalized seller discretionary earnings, the illustrative enterprise value range is $255,000 to $382,500. If that same profit excludes compensation for necessary owner work, an assumed $90,000 replacement cost leaves $37,500 before other normalization adjustments. Likewise, Scaled's $300,000 less an assumed $110,000 replacement cost leaves $190,000; if this reconciles to normalized EBITDA, the illustrative range becomes $570,000 to $855,000. Deduct replacement cost only once and only when it is absent from recorded expenses. Neither exercise changes the canonical scenario profits or establishes a market clearing price.

Separate headline enterprise value from cash delivered to the seller. In an illustrative $700,000 transaction, twenty percent contingent consideration leaves $560,000 payable before closing adjustments; debt repayment, working-capital adjustments, fees, taxes, and any escrow further change immediate proceeds. Specify whether an earnout measures retained collected revenue, gross contribution, or another auditable outcome, and define customer attribution and buyer operating obligations. A vague earnout can transfer pricing and staffing risk back to the seller after control has changed. Assemble twenty-four months of monthly statements when available, with younger books showing their complete operating history rather than fabricated longevity. Include cohort exports, customer concentration, rights assignments, contractor agreements, incident history, and a reconciliation from reported profit to the proposed earnings denominator. Test a two-week founder absence at Scaled, measuring service attainment, accepted outcomes, collections, and renewal progress. If the operation fails that test, improve delegation before claiming manager-run economics. For a durable cash cow, use the same controls but distribute only cash remaining after taxes, reserves, and working-capital needs. Candidate #18's 54 mapped plays create value through repeatable ownership and evidence, not through the size of the documentation folder.
