## 1. Contract the acceptance unit before connecting acquisition channels  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a signed service schedule defining territory, buyer role, commercial need, exclusions, duplicate window, evidence standard, acceptance deadline, and dispute owner. A qualified appointment is not interchangeable with an answered call: specify whether attendance, decision authority, or a documented buying project creates the fee. For this chapter, five clients each purchase a $5,000 monthly service retainer plus $250 per finally accepted lead. The retainer covers campaign operations, reporting, and routing maintenance; the variable fee covers a separately evidenced delivery unit. Client-funded media stays outside recognized operator revenue and preferably remains in client-owned advertising accounts. Record authorization to use each domain, inbox, telephone number, CRM workspace, and destination calendar before production access. In HubSpot or Salesforce, require a client identifier, source event identifier, service period, qualification fields, and evidence pointer. Store sensitive source material in a restricted evidence repository rather than free-text CRM notes. Assign one full-time owner to acceptance disputes and three contractors to research, delivery operations, and quality review. This allocation matches the Base staffing envelope without assuming that contractor availability equals three additional full-time employees. Establish named backups before the first campaign creates a customer response.

Build the acquisition lane around actual authorization, not around mailbox quantity. Configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for clearly identified sending domains using Google Workspace or Microsoft 365; separate client credentials and publish working reply addresses. Multiple inboxes provide operational segregation and capacity management, never a method for bypassing provider limits or recipient suppression. Under 15 U.S.C. § 7704, commercial email requires accurate transmission information, nondeceptive subjects, applicable advertising identification, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out mechanism. Process opt-outs immediately in operations even though the federal deadline generally permits ten business days. Preserve suppression across every campaign acting for the same sender. Telephone and text permissions require a separate analysis under 47 U.S.C. § 227 and 47 CFR § 64.1200; a business job title does not establish that a number is exempt from restrictions affecting wireless or residential recipients. Check state calling and recording requirements before enabling dialing or call recording. Use CallRail or Twilio routing logs to prove delivery without assuming a recording is always available. When permission evidence is incomplete, stop that channel and offer a verified inbound form or explicitly requested callback instead.

Run intake through a deterministic funnel: normalize the source, deduplicate within the contracted window, score explicit facts, route eligible work, and hold the proposed fee until verification finishes. Normalize telephone numbers with country context and preserve original values in the restricted evidence store. For email identity, avoid aggressive transformations that could merge distinct recipients. Create a client-scoped HMAC fingerprint so the ledger can detect repeats without storing contact details. A high score only prioritizes review; it does not establish permission, truthfulness, legal eligibility, or client acceptance. Route calls against staffed schedules, fail over to a named backup, and return unattended calls to a follow-up queue rather than marking them qualified. Require a reason code for wrong territory, duplicate contact, absent buying need, unreachable prospect, and client-side missed appointment. Weekly reviews should separate acquisition defects from recipient staffing failures because the remedies differ. Suspend a source when its accepted cost exceeds the contracted contribution allowance, even if raw lead volume looks attractive. Deliver the client a compact evidence packet containing timestamps, qualification answers, routing outcome, and the acceptance decision. Only then create the variable receivable and its durable billing event.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Bind the commercial unit
   - Sign the service schedule and register an immutable client fee configuration.
   - Document duplicate windows, evidence requirements, territory, channel permissions, dispute ownership, and separate client-funded advertising.
2. Qualify and route authorized demand
   - Normalize identities, check suppression, score explicit facts, and route only within staffed capacity.
   - Use stable source identifiers and retain restricted evidence outside the ledger; a score alone never authorizes contact.
3. Resolve the verification hold
   - Record acceptance or rejection through the transactional state transition.
   - Accepted leads receive one immutable obligation; rejected leads receive no fee and retain a scrub reason in the evidence packet.
4. Close invoices and reconcile receipts
   - Dispatch durable billing events and allocate bank-confirmed receipts with unique identifiers.
   - Compare earned revenue, delivery costs, cash collections, and open receivables before approving the next month's capacity.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before the first production delivery**
> 
> Complete these checks for each client and channel; a successful test for one client does not authorize another client's campaign.
> - Replay one opt-out through every sender workspace and confirm the contact cannot re-enter a scheduled sequence.
> - Place a permitted test call during staffed and unstaffed hours; verify destination, overflow, timestamp, and recording configuration.
> - Have the client classify ten sample records against the signed acceptance definition before activating variable fees.
> - Submit the same intake and payment identifiers concurrently; confirm one delivery obligation and one allocated receipt survive.

## 2. Implement six atomic ledger modules with explicit external-system boundaries  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The reference below uses integer cents, SQLite foreign keys, business-key uniqueness, and BEGIN IMMEDIATE transactions. Save the SQL blocks as schema.sql and views.sql, concatenate modules two through four in order into ledger.py, and save module six as qa.py in the same directory. Run python qa.py against a local filesystem with SQLite supporting window functions, version 3.25 or later. These are reference artifacts supplied as text; their tests have not been executed here. Deploy one authoritative database per operating ledger and prohibit direct application writes outside these functions. Transactions serialize the read-check-write sequences that otherwise allow duplicate acceptance, conflicting retries, or concurrent overcollection. A busy timeout fails the request rather than weakening a constraint; retry the identical operation identifier after backoff. Do not place the database on a shared network drive or create independently writable replicas. Use managed Postgres with equivalent unique constraints and row locking when multiple application hosts need write access. Back up the database through SQLite's backup interface and test restoration alongside the separately protected evidence repository. A ledger backup without its evidence index cannot substantiate the billable delivery unit.

Module two records four explicit qualification facts and an evidence digest; the caller must already have completed channel authorization and suppression checks. Module three implements held, accepted, and rejected states. Here, verification escrow means a noncash operational hold on a proposed fee, not custody of client money or a regulated escrow account. Only a perfect qualification score can proceed to acceptance, and a human reviewer must still authorize that transition. Acceptance writes the lead state, receivable, and outbox event in one transaction. A retry returns the same obligation, while an attempted terminal state reversal raises an exception. Retainers are recognized when the month's contracted service is completed, not merely because an invoice was sent. The worked cohort has no deposits, post-acceptance credits, refunds, tax collections, or foreign currency transactions. Route those transaction classes to an accounting system with explicit liability and adjustment entries rather than changing historical obligations here. Keep pre-acceptance scrubs separate from credit notes: the former never become revenue. This distinction prevents rejected prospects from inflating both reported sales and apparent collection losses when the operator explains performance to the client.

Module four deliberately performs remote delivery outside the database transaction. Its sender callback must target an API that atomically deduplicates the supplied stable key and returns the same durable receipt for every replay. The key must remain valid throughout the retry horizon; a provider's short retention window is insufficient for indefinite unattended retries. Two workers may transmit the same outbox item, but that provider contract produces one external invoice and the local acknowledgement records one receipt. If the destination lacks this property, stop automatic replay after an ambiguous timeout and retrieve the external result before proceeding. No local SQL transaction can independently promise exactly-once behavior at an unrelated service. Receipt allocation uses a separate bank-origin identifier and rejects conflicting reuse or amounts exceeding the remaining receivable. The reconciliation view reports earned revenue, recorded costs, collections, operating cash movement, and receivables by service month. Module six builds the entire cohort, exercises concurrent acceptance and receipt retries, and checks the expected totals. Add integration tests for the chosen provider, restricted evidence store, access controls, and restore process before using these functions with live client records.

## 3. Walk three delivery cohorts from accepted units to collected cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Begin the 90-day walk when five contracted clients enter a full service month, not on the day the business is incorporated. The scorecard's one-to-four-month first-deal interval precedes this mature cohort assumption. Month 1 submits 50 distinct candidates, rejects ten, and accepts 40. Five $5,000 retainers produce $25,000 of earned service revenue; 40 accepted units at $250 produce another $10,000. Month 2 submits 54, rejects 12, and accepts 42, producing $35,500. Month 3 submits 56, rejects 13, and accepts 43, producing $35,750. The quarter therefore contains 160 submissions, 35 prebilling scrubs, and 125 accepted leads. Its 78.125% acceptance rate is a planning assumption, not a claim about any acquisition channel. Each month's retainer is earned and paid by that month's close; accepted-lead invoices are collected in the following month. There is no opening receivable, cancellation, refund, or uncollectible invoice in this worked example. Those exclusions make the timing bridge inspectable while leaving rejection losses visible in the production denominator. Never count the client's downstream contract value, sales pipeline, or advertising budget as additional operator revenue.

Recognize $35,000 in Month 1 but collect only $25,000 because the first $10,000 of lead fees remains outstanding. Operating costs of $24,500 leave $500 of operating cash movement and $10,500 of earned owner profit.
