## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator teaching distribution builds around the same engine: get the lead or the call in front of the buyer who pays the most, and do it in seconds. boberdoo.com describes ping post as a two-stage auction where the ping goes out without personal info to all eligible buyers simultaneously, bids get compared, and the full lead posts to the winner or winners 'in a matter of seconds' [o5v8ME_4M_M @ 00:00:38]. Lead Prosper drills the same rule into every buyer setup: the ping carries only partial, case-specific data such as zip code or city and never contains the lead's personal information [gX0fjRlg1lg @ 00:00:16]. Local SEO Made Easy runs identical logic for phone traffic inside Callvane, sketching a chain where the customer (A) calls through the host (B) and gets forwarded to the business owner (C) [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:05:28]. The host then layers on a real-time bidding example where bids of 50, 40, 60, 52 and 62 come in and buyer five takes the call inside a 10-to-20-second window [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 01:12:03]. The shared baseline is simple: strip the ping to the minimum, put a hard clock on the decision, and hand the lead or caller to the top bidder when that clock runs out.

The second point of agreement is that follow-up speed decides who gets paid. Opsynx AI builds its entire local-business pitch on the claim that most consumers pick the first business that responds, framing getting found as only the beginning of the job [-uqaX9Doo6s @ 00:00:37]. Jason Grossman operationalizes that inside GoHighLevel: the moment someone signs up, a voice AI agent named Alex calls them within 60 seconds, and a VA follows with a manual call later in the sequence [Ot93upK_YX4 @ 00:52:38]. Grossman is blunt about channel preference, saying 'I'm a huge fan of phone calls and SMS,' and builds the rest of the follow-up around text and voice rather than inbox-only nurture [Ot93upK_YX4 @ 00:40:13]. On the distribution side, the same impatience shows up as hard server timeouts. boberdoo.com sets example windows of 15 seconds for the ping and 30 seconds for the post, and any buyer whose server lags past that window is simply excluded from the auction instead of holding up the sale [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:15:43]. Whether the slow party is a buyer's API or a prospect deciding which business to call back, the operators agree the money goes to whoever answers first, so they automate the first touch and staff humans behind it.

Before any of that routing pays, the practitioners insist on three prerequisites: volume, filters and compliance data. Jason Grossman states flatly that you cannot have quality without quantity, which is why their system chases lead flow first and refinement second [Ot93upK_YX4 @ 00:03:35]. MoreMozi's advisor hammers the same point from the agency side. A B2B lead gen shop wants higher-seniority, higher-salary leads and is eyeing new platforms, and the advisor's first warning is to keep Meta running and 'don't stop the flow' while anything new gets tested [94mDeuLH24c @ 00:00:55]. Once volume exists, boberdoo.com calls filter sets the most important part of the entire distribution system, with daily, weekly and monthly caps plus delivery schedules configured on each buyer so traffic lands only where and when a partner has agreed to take it [Dktb23hf-d8 @ 00:00:42]. Compliance is treated as plumbing rather than an afterthought: Lead Prosper's LeadsPedia buyer setup requires either a jornaya_lead_id or a trusted_form field in the payload [_1N3fncM-Ss @ 00:00:41]. Skip volume and the auction has nothing to sell. Skip filter sets and buyers get flooded past their caps. Skip the compliance token and the buyer's spec is never satisfied, so the integration cannot go live at all.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

boberdoo.com teaches the money side of ping post as margin carving. Two numbers decide whether an auction is worth running: what buyers bid and what the operator keeps. With Return Best Price switched on, the system pings the lead out, gathers the bid, carves out a profit margin and passes the balance back to the seller as the represented bid [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:16:42]. Static margins are straight subtraction: a $10 buyer bid minus a $2 margin becomes an $8 bid back to the seller, and a $3 margin turns it into $7 [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:28:02]. Dynamic buyers get a matrix instead, with 10% margin on bids from $0 to $10, 15% from $10 to $25 and 25% above that, plus optional bid floors [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:34:06]. A max-best-price ceiling catches fat-finger bids, because a $1,000 typo at a 10% margin would otherwise pass a $900 bid back to the seller [yCAVYNpSdZI @ 00:17:35]. Every one of these settings sits on the source or buyer record, which means the margin is locked in before the first ping fires rather than negotiated lead by lead. The operator's job is choosing the margin structure that fits each buyer, static for flat-priced buyers and the matrix for dynamic bidders.

Lead Prosper's buyer-setup walkthroughs show the field-level grind behind every one of those bids. For a LeadsPedia buyer, lp_campaign_id and lp_campaign_key ride on both the ping and the post, and lp_test=1 goes into the payload during testing and comes out before launch [_1N3fncM-Ss @ 00:00:21]. A boberdoo buyer uses the same URL for ping and post, distinguished only by the Mode field, with Key and API_action required, Return_Best_Price=1 set for dynamic pricing and TYPE=34 in the example payload [Ma1kH51odZ0 @ 00:00:55]. Phonexa buyers need a promise token echoed from the ping response into the post with {{lp_ping_response:promise}} [Pzlk50aGkUg @ 00:01:23]. Inside Lead Prosper's own builder, the ping response is set to status=ACCEPTED with the real-time price toggle on, and the bid path points at /bids/payout so the system extracts the buyer's price from the response [gX0fjRlg1lg @ 00:02:53]. None of these field names are interchangeable; each buyer platform has its own vocabulary for the same job. The pattern across platforms is the same: echo an ID or token from the ping into the post, parse the price from a known response path, and keep test flags in place until the buyer confirms the round trip works.

Local SEO Made Easy puts hard numbers on the call side of distribution. The host runs two models: pay-per-call at $100 per call and rank-and-rent tenants paying $500 or $1,000 a month [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:02:33]. When pricing the plumbing, they break down Ringba at 5 cents per minute incoming, 3 cents outgoing and 1 cent for recording, so a single 3-minute call costs 27 cents before any platform fees are counted [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:15:53]. That per-minute math is the benchmark the host holds every routing tool against. Inside Callvane, every buyer gets a ring strategy: ring in order, split traffic 70/30, take turns between buyers, or ring every buyer at once [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:35:21]. Network mode turns the operator into a broker. Hartley & Sons pays $100 for a call that runs past 120 seconds, a second buyer pays $95, and the publisher who generated the call is paid $70, so the operator keeps the spread on every qualified connection [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:59:31]. The working model is identical to ping post: price each buyer separately, set a duration threshold that defines a billable call, choose how traffic is divided among buyers, and live on the difference between what buyers pay and what sources cost.

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Routing splits operators into two camps: race-to-answer and race-to-bid. The Callvane demo on Local SEO Made Easy pushes calls through a ring tree with four modes: in order, a 70/30 split, take turns, and all at once [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:35:21]. When a viewer pushed back that all-at-once ringing is bad because of callbacks, the host shut it down flat: there is no call-back system, so the mode stays on the menu [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:45:10]. boberdoo.com runs the opposite logic on leads. The ping goes out stripped of personal information to every eligible buyer simultaneously, the bids are compared, and the post lands with the winner or winners in a matter of seconds [o5v8ME_4M_M @ 00:00:38]. Callvane bolts the same auction idea onto calls: in its real-time bidding example, buyers bid 50, 40, 60, 52 and 62, and buyer B5 takes the call at 62 inside a 10 to 20 second window [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 01:12:03]. The split is not about software brand; it is about what buyers compete on. Ring trees reward whoever picks up, splits and rotations spread volume across tenants, and auctions reward the highest bid that clears filters. Match the mode to how each buyer actually pays you before wiring a single number or posting a single lead.

Pricing is where the sharpest disagreements live. boberdoo.com teaches static margin as plain subtraction: a buyer bidding $10 with a $2 margin shows the seller an $8 bid, and a $3 margin drops the represented bid to $7 [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:28:02]. For dynamic buyers the lead price sits at $0 and a margin matrix takes over, 10% on bids from $0 to $10, 15% from $10 to $25, and 25% above that, with bid floors available on top [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:34:06]. The same team draws a hard line on revenue share: if you are going to run rev-sharing models, they recommend not running them in a ping post fashion [yCAVYNpSdZI @ 00:25:13]. The Callvane camp sells the opposite flexibility to tenants: fixed rent, per qualified call, rent plus call, or per booked job, with a sample rent of $300 [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:23:26]. Under rent plus call, the tenant pays the base and still owes on every qualified call, so the operator earns on both the listing and the call volume. The ping post seller only ever sees a carved bid in real time; the rank-and-rent tenant sees a fixed bill plus metered calls. Choose based on whether you want margin on every auction or predictable rent with call fees stacked on top of it.

Follow-up splits between automation-first stacks and people with AI bolted on. Opsynx AI sells the speed argument: getting found is only the beginning, because most consumers pick the first business that responds, so its system funnels website chat, SMS, email, Google Business chat and Facebook and Instagram DMs into one unified inbox [-uqaX9Doo6s @ 00:00:37]. Jason Grossman refuses to let software carry the load alone. A voice AI agent named Alex calls a new sign-up within 60 seconds, a VA named Chona makes a manual call two days after the event, and the team then pushes 200 to 400 manual SMS a day with Closebot switched off [Ot93upK_YX4 @ 00:52:38]. Grossman's rule is blunt: you add AI to the people, staffed as three setters, one each for SMS, phone and DM [Ot93upK_YX4 @ 01:12:54]. The quality debate splits the same way. Grossman argues you cannot have quality without quantity. MoreMozi faces a B2B client buried in low-salary Meta leads, propped up by an R1 R2 sales process that qualifies for ten minutes, and tells them not to jump platforms yet: you might just be fine and only need to tweak the LTV, then consider LinkedIn or cold outreach [94mDeuLH24c @ 00:02:29].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

Proof is the first leak. In the Callvane demo, the host walks through a buyer showing 50 calls against only 40 recordings, the exact mismatch that turns a pay-per-call invoice into an argument [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:11:08]. Duration rules decide what bills: in the network example Hartley & Sons pays $100 for calls that reach 120 seconds, and a 47-second call is flagged as not billable [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 01:03:07]. Callvane supports disputes, but the cleaner play is setting the qualifier per buyer up front, such as 90 seconds at $40. On the lead side, boberdoo.com returns a response on every single post so sellers know exactly what happened to each lead, which they say reduces discrepancies [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:05:27]. Refunds are the next drain. boberdoo says its roughly 150 clients handle refunds about a hundred different ways, and the fix they built is a cap, for example refunds capped at 15% over the past 30 days, with CSV bulk approval for the rest [yCAVYNpSdZI @ 00:22:50]. Lead QC closes the front door: junk like 555-1212 gets held with an error returned, so you are not responsible to buy the lead. Every one of these is a billing fight you either automate away or eat by hand.

Integration breakage kills revenue silently. Lead Prosper warns that switching the payload type after editing resets the builder, so set form or JSON before touching a single field [gX0fjRlg1lg @ 00:01:33]. On LeadsPedia buyers, lp_test=1 goes in for testing and must come out before go-live, and the same rule applies to lp_action test on Lead Prosper's own buyer setup [_1N3fncM-Ss @ 00:00:21]. Phonexa posts carry their own trap: most fields are optional on the post, so you confirm with the buyer exactly which fields they need before sending live traffic [Pzlk50aGkUg @ 00:01:37]. Field names are case-sensitive, so copy and paste them instead of retyping. On the auction side, boberdoo.com built a max best price ceiling for one reason: a buyer who fat-fingers a $1,000 bid at a 10% margin would pass $900 back to the seller as the represented bid [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:17:44]. Slow buyers get cut by timeouts, such as 15 seconds on the ping and 30 on the post, so lagging buyers are excluded from the auction. boberdoo adds that a buyer not returning a bid typically has something wrong with its server, and dynamic buyers that return nothing get a $0 fallback price.

Unit economics punish anyone who skips the math. The Callvane host prices Ringba at a $150 monthly minimum plus per-number fees, then meters 5 cents a minute incoming, 3 cents outgoing and 1 cent recording, so a single three-minute call costs 27 cents [Bu_9XhXpDSs @ 00:15:53]. LeadSmart now demands a website before issuing numbers, unused numbers expire the next month, and Callvane's receive-only SMS numbers at $0.10 a month are not 10DLC-registered. boberdoo.com is equally blunt that its DNC list is internal only and not tied to state or federal lists [DSbDVWpyXUI @ 00:47:23]. Jason Grossman says 80% of GoHighLevel users are not getting results because it is not set up properly; it is a beast that needs one tech person to build it and a VA team to manage it, on top of the 97 USD monthly fee [Ot93upK_YX4 @ 00:35:44]. Scaling spend has its own trap. MoreMozi's client hit diminishing returns pushing more budget through Meta, so the advisor ran the math the other way: lift customer value from 3K to 10K or 12K and 3:1 becomes 12:1, and even if returns slide from 3:1 to 2:1, the 12:1 still lands at 9:1 [94mDeuLH24c @ 00:02:11]. The advisor's other warning is direct: don't turn off Meta, don't stop the flow.
