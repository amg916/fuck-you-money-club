## 1. Classify Every Channel, Recipient, State, and Regulated Sector  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the rule map around the actual message and destination, not the label B2B. CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. § 7704, reaches commercial email sent to business recipients: use accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, required advertising identification, a valid physical postal address, and a working unsubscribe mechanism. Honor qualifying opt-outs within ten business days and keep the mechanism available for at least thirty days after transmission. Operationally, suppress immediately across every connected sender. Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, HubSpot, and sequencing vendors must consume the same suppression ledger; changing an inbox never restores permission. Publish SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, but treat authentication as delivery infrastructure rather than permission to contact someone. The sender and promoted company can both face exposure depending on their roles, so outsourcing execution does not erase statutory duties. Separate transactional confirmations from promotional follow-ups and classify mixed messages by their primary purpose. Preserve the rendered email, subject, sender identity, address block, audience source, and unsubscribe test result for each campaign version. A purchased business address is evidence of acquisition, not evidence that every later channel or purpose is permitted.

For calls and texts, evaluate 47 U.S.C. § 227 and 47 CFR § 64.1200 against the equipment, message, telephone category, and recipient. A business contact using a cellular number does not automatically lose TCPA protections. Artificial or prerecorded voice outreach and covered automated dialing require different analysis from a human placing an individual live call. Marketing texts require their own permission and suppression controls; permission for a newsletter does not authorize a sales text. Check applicable national and company-specific do-not-call requirements, calling hours, revocation procedures, and stricter state rules before assigning a route. The Telemarketing Sales Rule generally exempts many B2B calls from many provisions, but its current framework retains prohibitions on specified B2B misrepresentations. Never translate that exemption into unrestricted dialing. Florida Statutes § 501.059 has its own covered-call definitions and restrictions; determine whether the specific offer and recipient fall inside them. Store recipient timezone and state with provenance, and quarantine records whose location cannot be resolved. For cross-state recording, use an affirmative agreement workflow designed for the stricter applicable standard instead of assuming federal one-party permission resolves every state issue.

Privacy analysis starts with a data-flow diagram showing collection, enrichment, qualification, auction, routing, retention, and deletion. California Civil Code § 1798.100 and following provisions can cover business-contact personal information when the relevant business meets statutory conditions; the former broad B2B exemption has expired. Determine whether a recipient is a service provider, contractor, or independent third party, and whether a transfer constitutes sale or sharing. Other state privacy laws differ in thresholds, exclusions, consumer definitions, and sensitive-data requirements, so maintain state-specific rules rather than copying California settings everywhere. Separately assess data-broker registration and deletion duties when collecting and selling personal information without a direct relationship. Licensing depends on conduct: finding commercial prospects differs from negotiating insurance, brokering loans, arranging securities transactions, or receiving regulated settlement-service referrals. RESPA, 12 U.S.C. § 2607, restricts referral payments connected to covered federally related mortgage settlement services. Exchange Act § 15(a) creates a separate broker-registration boundary when securities activity crosses into brokerage. Keep staff within documented qualification questions, route regulated advice to licensed professionals, and have counsel classify proposed compensation before activating a sector-specific buyer.

## 2. Contract for Accepted Outcomes, Evidence, and Controllable Liability  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a master services agreement, campaign order, data-processing schedule, and acceptance specification that agree on the same definitions. Scope must identify the commercial offer, target industries, employee or revenue bands, territories, channels, buyer capacity, exclusions, and reporting cadence. For appointments, define whether payment requires booking, attendance, a named decision-maker, verified need, or a minimum conversation duration. These are different products with different refund rates. A useful starting term makes a lead billable only when the buyer accepts a unique record meeting the written criteria; receipt alone does not prove acceptance. Require rejection within two business days using enumerated reason codes and supporting evidence. Define duplicate lookback periods, rescheduling treatment, no-show responsibility, attribution windows, and whether exclusivity applies to a person, company, opportunity, or territory. Include buyer-response obligations because an unanswered transfer is not equivalent to an unqualified prospect. Record routing timestamps and call dispositions in HubSpot or Salesforce, while keeping sensitive evidence in restricted storage. Tie invoice lines to immutable lead identifiers and accepted events so finance can reproduce the bill without relying on an account manager's memory.

Reconcile contract economics to the canonical annual scorecard before promising volume. Conservative revenue of $100,000 at a 20% net margin produces $20,000 owner profit and implies $80,000 total costs with 1 FT plus 3 C. Base revenue of $425,000 at 30.0% produces $127,500 owner profit and $297,500 costs with the same team. Scaled revenue of $750,000 at 40% produces $300,000 owner profit and $450,000 costs with 2 FT plus 4 C. One illustrative Base revenue bridge is five clients paying $5,000 monthly, producing $300,000, plus 1,000 accepted leads at $125, producing $125,000. Define what the retainers buy so accepted-lead fees do not duplicate an included allowance. Client-funded advertising pass-through is excluded from all three revenue figures; client sales, merchant volume, and loan principal are also excluded. Keep campaign experiments separate from annual forecasts. The $2,000–$12,000 monthly operating range is an orientation, not a substitute for each scenario's full cost bridge. Model acquisition, contractors, verification, credits, infrastructure, insurance, and administrative costs explicitly, with enough reserve for buyer disputes and delayed collections.

Make anti-circumvention narrow enough to explain and administer: protect documented introductions for a defined period, exclude provable preexisting relationships, and avoid claiming ownership of an entire market. Specify who owns creative work, qualification logic, CRM configuration, prospect records, and evidence artifacts. Personal information remains subject to statutory rights regardless of commercial ownership language. Buyer access to a lead is a limited permitted use, not unlimited resale authority. Set retention by purpose and applicable obligations, with deletion propagation and a documented litigation-hold exception. Negotiate a liability cap using a stated fee period, identify any carve-outs precisely, and align indemnities with the party controlling collection, claims, outreach, security, or resale. Insurance coverage and exclusions must match the actual channels. Contract allocation cannot eliminate government enforcement or third-party rights. Define escalation contacts, evidence exchange, negotiation deadlines, forum, governing law, arbitration if selected, and treatment of undisputed invoices. Termination provisions should stop sending, revoke credentials, settle accepted events, return eligible data, and preserve necessary suppression records. Require written change orders for new states, channels, offers, or subprocessors rather than quietly expanding the original scope.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Translate the buyer promise into testable acceptance criteria
   - Draft the campaign order using territory, role, company fit, need, timing, duplication, and attendance rules.
   - Run ten synthetic records through buyer review and resolve every disputed definition before live delivery.
2. Bind evidence to every billable event
   - Assign a lead identifier and attach collection disclosures, routing events, acceptance status, and invoice references.
   - Restrict access to sensitive artifacts while exposing enough evidence for the buyer to verify each charge.
3. Reconcile quality decisions before invoicing
   - Review reason-coded rejections, duplicates, missed transfers, and unresolved acceptance events with the buyer.
   - Issue documented credits where warranted and keep pending disputes separate from accepted revenue.
4. Close the campaign without leaving active data routes
   - Disable sending, revoke buyer credentials, reconcile earned fees, and execute the agreed return or deletion process.
   - Preserve necessary suppression and dispute evidence under a defined retention schedule rather than keeping every record indefinitely.

## 4. Use Honest Scripts and Keep Privacy-Claim Work Counsel-Led  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Scripts should expose the commercial purpose and make refusal easy. The examples below assume the named operator, service, filing event, and promised deliverable are all real; replace identifying facts with verified facts before use. A live caller asks whether the prospect wants a short explanation without asserting an existing relationship. An email references a public event without claiming private knowledge of budgets, staffing, or distress. Include the actual sender's valid physical postal address in the production footer; the example body does not supply that factual address. Do not use invented reply chains, fake meeting confirmations, fabricated referrals, or a misleading service notice to increase opens. Discovery should establish company fit, decision responsibility, need, timing, and permission for the specific next step. Record a declined meeting as declined rather than recycling it through a different inbox. For routing, identify the receiving seller and proposed contact method before collecting the corresponding choice. Call-transfer announcements should name the destination and explain what information will be shared. Permission to discuss one vendor does not become permission for unrelated buyers, texts, recordings, or continuing newsletter enrollment.

For cipa_vppa_wiretap_pixel_settlement_arbitrage, restrict the service to counsel-led evaluation of genuine tracking-related privacy claims and authorized remediation. California Penal Code § 631 addresses specified interception and related conduct; § 632 concerns confidential communications and recording. Application to tracking technologies depends on facts and developing decisions, not merely detecting a network request. The Video Privacy Protection Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2710, has distinct requirements involving covered providers, consumers, personally identifiable information, disclosures, and available exceptions. Do not advertise an automatic payout or convert statutory damages figures into expected operating revenue. With authorization, preserve browser network logs, tag configurations, disclosure versions, permission events, timestamps, and artifact hashes while minimizing unrelated personal information. Counsel determines applicability, standing, claims, and recoverable damages. An alternative six-hour remediation audit at $150 per hour earns $900; six hours of delivery labor at $60 cost $360, leaving $540 before other costs. Keep that technical-service fee separate from claimant recoveries. Do not manufacture browsing events, pressure targets with unsupported allegations, or charge compensation that violates applicable attorney fee-sharing or referral rules. Close unsupported matters and offer authorized privacy-remediation audits instead.

Operate the scripts through a controlled release process rather than an editable shared document with no history. Store each version with its channel, audience, offer, territory, factual substantiation, and required disclosures. Have a second person replay the journey from ad to form, qualification, buyer routing, outreach, opt-out, and deletion using synthetic records. Test failed handoffs: the buyer endpoint times out, a person revokes permission after an auction, a recording starts before agreement, or a contractor exports a suppressed contact. The correct response is a documented stop, repair, and evidence trail, not another sending domain. Review rejection reasons and complaints weekly with the buyer, then compare results against collected fees and actual acquisition costs. A falling acceptance rate may indicate mismatched creative or a changed buyer definition rather than poor sales execution. Maintain least-privilege vendor access, rotate credentials when contractors leave, and reconcile deletion acknowledgments across processors and independent recipients where duties apply. Before entering another state or sector, refresh the authoritative rule map and written contract scope. Scale only repeatable evidence-backed delivery; the canonical Scaled team has six people, leaving little capacity for avoidable disputes or manual incident recovery.
