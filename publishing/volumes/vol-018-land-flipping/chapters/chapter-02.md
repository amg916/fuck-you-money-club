## 1. Sell the buyer a usable right and a credible closing date  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The check writer changes with the parcel's next economic use. A cash household buyer purchases recreation, privacy, or a future homesite; a production builder purchases a deliverable construction input; an adjoining owner purchases control over access, drainage, or expansion. Ask each buyer what event releases their budget and what unresolved fact would stop their acquisition committee. For a builder, an expiring subcontractor price or a depleted lot pipeline can justify speed, but neither cures an unbuildable parcel. Underwrite their residual: an illustrative $420,000 finished home less $280,000 construction and soft costs, $45,000 selling and financing costs, and $55,000 required profit leaves $40,000 for land. A $48,000 asking price needs better evidence, not stronger sales language. Use county GIS and QGIS for screening, then obtain recorded documents, utility responses, and jurisdictional confirmation for the purchase decision. Parcel boundaries on a map do not establish legal access. Price the buyer's avoided delay only after separating legal entitlement, physical feasibility, and willingness to pay. The resulting offer should name the specific right delivered, the evidence supporting it, and the remaining contingencies rather than promising an unrestricted future use.

Map six distinct applications without pretending their proceeds share one accounting treatment. Vacant-parcel cash resale might buy at $24,000, spend $4,000 on diligence, carrying, and closing, and sell at $36,000, leaving $8,000 contribution before central overhead. Infill-builder disposition might produce $16,000 from a $70,000 sale less $48,000 acquisition and $6,000 direct costs. Minor-subdivision lot release could sell three approved lots at $50,000 each against $90,000 acquisition and $35,000 survey, engineering, infrastructure, and selling costs, leaving $25,000 before overhead. Access-cure resale might yield $12,000 on a $55,000 sale after $30,000 acquisition, $8,000 documented easement work, and $5,000 other direct costs. Seller-financed disposition requires a separate receivable and collection model. The sixth application is mineral-interest acquisition around pooling deadlines, examined below. These are illustrative transaction economics, not replacements for the volume scorecard. If subdivision approval fails, retain only a purchase basis supported by the unsplit parcel. If an easement cannot be recorded in acceptable form, abandon the cure thesis. If builder liquidity fails screening, seek a funded alternate buyer rather than financing an involuntary speculative development partnership. Document those fallback prices before acquisition, when declining the deal remains inexpensive.

Budget authority is not settlement authority. A builder's land acquisition manager may negotiate while a treasury employee initiates the wire and an authorized officer signs the deed acceptance or borrowing documents. Obtain the purchasing entity's exact legal name, current formation status, signer authority, and any lender conditions before accepting a compressed closing schedule. For an individual buyer, distinguish liquid funds available for this purchase from an account balance already pledged elsewhere. Ask for a recent, appropriately redacted bank statement or bank-issued funds confirmation, and verify material representations through independently sourced contact details. Do not collect online banking credentials. A statutory deadline creates urgency only when it actually applies: a recorded tax obligation can affect a seller's proceeds, while a pooling election affects specified mineral interests under the governing order. Neither establishes a surface buyer's duty to buy. Capture pain as an operational fact, such as monthly carrying cost, an option expiration, or a documented construction start, then test the proposed settlement date against it. Maintain a buyer register showing decision maker, signer, funding source, title contact, and dependency dates. A buyer with clear authority and modest funds often closes more reliably than a higher bidder with conditional capital.

## 2. Match the settlement mechanism to the obligation being discharged  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat payment rails and release arrangements as different layers. A wire moves money; escrow governs who may release it; a milestone hold reserves an agreed portion after specified performance. For a conventional cash land closing, the buyer normally sends verified funds to the authorized settlement agent, which satisfies documented payoffs, records or submits the deed under local practice, and disburses according to signed instructions and applicable good-funds requirements. Do not describe every settlement agent as a fiduciary under one national rule; agency duties, trust accounting, and disbursement requirements vary by jurisdiction and engagement. Obtain the title commitment early enough to resolve vesting, taxes, liens, easements, and exceptions before funds become urgent. On a $70,000 sale with a $5,000 deposit, the buyer owes $65,000 of price at closing, plus their allocated costs and adjustments. The deposit is part of the price, not extra revenue. The seller's wire will be smaller after seller charges, payoffs, and prorations. Reconcile the settlement statement to the contract and transaction ledger rather than recording only the bank deposit as revenue. Retain both the final statement and disbursement confirmation so every dollar has a documented economic classification.

Use eight mechanisms deliberately, not interchangeably. Domestic wire is usually suited to time-sensitive closing funds, but its speed makes beneficiary substitution dangerous. ACH fits serviced installments and some deposits when the agent accepts it; network settlement does not eliminate return or fraud exposure. Escrow adds conditional custody but requires a competent, authorized holder and precise instructions. A retainer ordinarily funds professional work or another defined engagement, not an undisclosed land seller's commission. Statutory disbursement follows the applicable court order or legal distribution process and cannot be accelerated by privately relabeling funds. Platform checkout can collect permitted reservations or earnest money if provider terms and the contract support that use; processor limits, chargebacks, and payout holds make it unsuitable as an assumed substitute for closing escrow. Factoring or a note sale converts an eligible receivable into discounted cash but introduces assignment, recourse, and debtor-defense questions. Milestone holds address incomplete work through a defined reserve and release test. Request the settlement agent's written acceptance rules before naming any method in the purchase contract. A payment instruction that conflicts with those rules creates a failed closing even when both parties intended to perform on time.

Defend the final funding window with a callback protocol established before money moves. Exchange the settlement office's verified telephone number through an independent source, confirm the beneficiary name and bank instructions verbally, and treat any late change as a reason to stop and reverify. A familiar email thread is not proof because compromised mailboxes preserve convincing conversation history. Where the bank offers it, use beneficiary controls, dual authorization, and transaction limits; these controls supplement rather than replace verification. Never route sale funds through an employee's personal account or accept a refund request to an unrelated third party without settlement-agent investigation and appropriate documentation. For a $10,000 improvement reserve, specify the work, inspection evidence, deadline, dispute procedure, and recipient of any remaining balance. Define whether the reserve is funded from seller proceeds or additional buyer funds. Neither side should unilaterally certify ambiguous completion. Obtain written confirmation of funds receipt from the closing office, not a buyer's screenshot. Release possession, original instruments, or other leverage only through the agreed closing sequence. If fraud is suspected, immediately contact the sending bank's fraud team and the settlement office through known numbers; recovery depends heavily on speed and is never assured by an emailed recall request.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open the settlement file
   - Deliver the signed contract, entity records, legal description, and deposit instructions to the selected settlement agent.
   - Confirm the agent's authority, accepted funding methods, good-funds timing, title requirements, and responsibility for recording.
2. Resolve documentary conditions
   - Clear title requirements, confirm signer authority, and reconcile the proposed settlement statement with the contract.
   - Identify every payoff, commission, credit, deposit, and reserve; assign an owner and deadline to each unresolved item.
3. Verify and transmit funds
   - Confirm bank instructions using a previously established independent telephone contact before initiating the authorized transfer.
   - Record the callback, authorized beneficiary, amount, and bank reference without storing account credentials in a shared sales system.
4. Confirm release and reconcile
   - Obtain recording or closing confirmation, the final settlement statement, and documented disbursement results.
   - Reconcile gross price, basis, costs, receivables, and cash; investigate differences before marking the transaction financially complete.

## 3. Protect earned economics without mistaking contracts for cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A principal land flipper normally earns a trading spread, not a service fee. Protect that spread with enforceable purchase rights, accurate disclosures, and a controlled closing, rather than presenting yourself as an unpaid broker who can collect later. When representing another owner or arranging transactions for compensation, evaluate the applicable state broker-licensing rules and any narrow exemption before marketing. If the business uses assignments, disclose the actual contractual interest being sold, verify assignability and required consents, and address state-specific wholesaling requirements. Do not record a memorandum merely to pressure an owner; recording rights and document content require transaction-specific review. For a lawful $6,000 assignment payment, put the obligation, payer, earning event, and settlement treatment in signed documents accepted by the closing agent. If the original agreement prohibits assignment, obtain consent or use another permitted structure with genuinely available funding. A non-circumvention clause alone does not create a lien, repair missing authority, or compel an escrow holder to release disputed money. Reduce circumvention by controlling a valid property or contract right and delivering a documented transaction package. Keep brokerage compensation, consulting retainers, assignment consideration, and principal sale proceeds in separate ledger categories.

Counterparty credit screening begins before granting exclusivity. For a builder entity, confirm existence, signer authority, available acquisition funds, financing conditions, and recent comparable closings. Search relevant bankruptcy, judgment, and public litigation records with proper entity matching; a shared name is not proof of financial distress. Ask whether capital is equity, a committed facility, or a loan still awaiting appraisal. Match diligence intensity to exposure: a fully funded cash buyer presents different risks from a thinly capitalized purchaser asking for ninety days and a refundable deposit. For an illustrative $100,000 contract, a $5,000 earnest deposit leaves $95,000 of purchase price unfunded until closing. It does not establish capacity to fund the balance. Define when the deposit becomes nonrefundable, what contingencies survive, and how disputes are resolved, subject to applicable law. A failed buyer should trigger documented notice and contract remedies, not unilateral seizure of escrow. Offer extensions only through signed amendments that price additional carrying exposure and name a final deadline. If a personal guaranty is commercially justified, verify its execution and practical collectability rather than treating the signature as cash. Preserve a backup marketing path consistent with the existing buyer's contractual rights.

Seller financing exchanges immediate liquidity for credit exposure and servicing work. A $60,000 sale with $15,000 down and $45,000 financed at an illustrative 9% annual rate over 120 monthly payments produces a payment of approximately $570.04, excluding taxes, insurance, and servicing charges. First-month interest is $337.50, so roughly $232.54 reduces principal. Principal collection is not new sale revenue each month, and interest is tracked separately from the parcel sale. Recognize the receivable and gain under the accounting method actually used; installment tax treatment under 26 U.S.C. § 453 is a separate analysis. Determine federal and state credit, licensing, disclosure, servicing, and security-instrument requirements from the buyer's purpose, collateral, transaction frequency, and structure. Raw land is not automatically treated like a dwelling-secured mortgage, and neither category supports a blanket seller-financing exemption. Use a qualified servicer, a recorded security instrument where appropriate, tax monitoring, and a documented default process. If immediate liquidity becomes necessary, a hypothetical $45,000 unpaid principal balance sold for $36,000 creates a $9,000 principal discount before other adjustments. Underwrite that downside before offering terms; otherwise a high headline price can conceal a weak cash outcome.

> 
> Each check needs retained evidence and a named reviewer. An optimistic email is not a substitute for a closing condition.
> - Verify vesting, legal description, recorded access, and title requirements against the actual interest being conveyed.
> - Verify buyer funds, entity signer authority, and every lender condition that could prevent the scheduled closing.

> - Verify that subdivision, credit, brokerage, or mineral-election requirements applicable to the specific transaction have been resolved before commitment.

## 4. Isolate mineral-election risk and reconcile proceeds to owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The supplied TRACK_1 play, forced_pooling_mineral_lease_curve_jumping, uses public pooling applications and drilling schedules to identify unleased mineral interests before election deadlines. In Oklahoma, 52 O.S. § 87.1 provides the forced-pooling framework, but the actual order, notice, ownership, formation, and election terms govern a particular opportunity. An application is not a final order, and surface ownership does not establish mineral ownership. Build a docket register containing the cause number, hearing history, order date, affected formation, election deadline, payment obligations, and service evidence. Obtain specialist title and legal review before acquiring an interest or choosing an election. Assume ten verified net mineral acres cost $800 each, or $8,000, with $2,000 additional title and legal work. A hypothetical available $600-per-acre bonus would generate $6,000, leaving $4,000 of acquisition and diligence cost unrecovered before royalties. That is not a completed profitable flip. Model royalty upside separately from bonus cash and price defects, suspended payments, and uncertain production explicitly. Stop acquisitions pending counsel review and choose only timely, fully understood permitted elections. Never assume a late election can be repaired by sending funds after the order's deadline.

Royalty arithmetic must start with the correct ownership and unit assumptions. If the order and title support ten net mineral acres participating uniformly in an illustrative 640-acre unit at a one-fifth royalty, the simplified decimal is 10 divided by 640 times 0.20, or 0.003125. Applied to $2,000,000 of assumed attributable sales value, that equals $6,250 before relevant taxes, deductions, adjustments, and timing differences. Actual allocation may differ for multiunit wells, formation-specific ownership, burdens, and governing documents. The $2,000,000 is underlying production value, not the land operator's revenue. A participating working interest has different economics: a simplified ten-sixty-fourths of one tenth, equivalent to 10/640, of an $8,000,000 well budget is $125,000 before overruns. Non-consent treatment, recoverable costs, and royalty alternatives depend on the governing law and order; do not import another state's penalty assumptions. Use the actual permitted election language and document timely delivery and required payment. Title defects or an incorrect division-order decimal can suspend receipts long after drilling starts. Keep this mineral strategy in a separate capital and receipts ledger so speculative production cannot mask a shortage of cash for surface-land closings.

Close the annual model on the canonical revenue basis: gross parcel sale proceeds, with acquisition costs included in net margin. Conservative revenue of $200,000 at 12% yields $24,000 owner net profit and $176,000 total implied costs. Base revenue of $850,000 at 18.5% yields $157,250 profit and $692,750 costs. Scaled revenue of $1,500,000 at 25% yields $375,000 profit and $1,125,000 costs. Teams remain 1 FT + 3 C for Conservative and Base, then 2 FT + 4 C for Scaled. These are annual scenarios, not transaction counts or promised collection schedules. An illustrative Base bridge of ten $85,000 sales produces exactly $850,000; it does not establish the cost mix needed to achieve the stated profit. Allocate acquisition basis, diligence, improvements, carrying costs, selling costs, and overhead once, avoiding duplicate deductions. Distinguish cost of sold parcels from cash still invested in unsold inventory. Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast because $50,000–$400,000 startup capital, $3,000–$15,000 monthly operating expense, and a 3–12 month first-deal timeline can create funding pressure before annual profit appears. Parcel screening and comparable-sale tracking improve throughput; neither eliminates illiquidity, environmental constraints, or missing legal access.
