## 1. Identify the legal capacity and statutory exposure of every parcel  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start each opportunity with a capacity statement: the acquisition entity buys for its own account, controls its own diligence, bears carrying costs, and expects to resell its own property. That statement describes actual conduct, not a substitute for licensing analysis. California Business and Professions Code sections 10130 and 10131 distinguish licensed activity involving another person's property for compensation; Texas Occupations Code section 1101.0045 addresses certain sales of options or contract interests and requires written disclosure. Do not assume an ownership exemption covers negotiating for a neighbor, collecting referral compensation, or advertising a parcel before acquiring the interest being advertised. For this chapter, the six mapped plays are vacant-parcel principal purchases, infill repositioning, GIS parcel screening, comparable-sale tracking, minor subdivisions, and title-insured dispositions. GIS research and comparable tracking support the other plays; they do not independently authorize brokerage. The full-time principal owns capacity decisions. The research contractor assembles evidence, the appropriately licensed market contractor handles assigned brokerage work, and the transaction coordinator tracks documents without independently negotiating restricted terms. Obtain jurisdiction-specific review before changing from owned inventory to assignments, seller representation, pooled investment, or installment-sale products.

Build the land-use file from the recorded legal description outward. County GIS polygons and assessor acreage identify research targets, but neither establishes surveyed boundaries, legal access, or permission to create lots. Request the zoning district, dimensional standards, frontage requirement, overlay restrictions, utility service conditions, and the written subdivision determination from the responsible jurisdiction. Texas Local Government Code section 232.001 addresses platting in unincorporated areas, subject to statutory exceptions; municipal authority and additional county rules require separate review. California Government Code section 66424 supplies the subdivision definition, while Business and Professions Code section 11010 establishes a separate public-report filing framework for covered subdivided-land offerings. A local lot-split permission therefore does not settle state marketing duties. At the federal level, the Interstate Land Sales Full Disclosure Act, 15 U.S.C. sections 1702 and 1703, can govern interstate offerings within a common promotional plan. The fewer-than-25-lot exemption and the fewer-than-100-lot registration exemption have different effects; the latter does not erase all antifraud obligations. Aggregate related development activity before selecting an exemption. Record who supplied each determination, its effective date, and whether a changed access route or lot count requires a fresh decision.

Treat environmental and disclosure questions as acquisition economics, not closing decorations. CERCLA, 42 U.S.C. sections 9601(40) and 9607(r), provides a potential bona fide prospective purchaser defense only when the buyer satisfies the applicable acquisition and continuing-obligation conditions. All Appropriate Inquiries under 40 CFR Part 312 is a defined investigation process, not a county-map screenshot. Its timing rules include a one-year outside period and specific components requiring updates within 180 days before acquisition. An environmental professional should determine the appropriate scope when historical dumping, tanks, industrial uses, or fill appear. Separately, filling jurisdictional waters can require authorization under Clean Water Act section 404, 33 U.S.C. section 1344; a wetlands inventory layer is a screening lead, not a jurisdictional determination. For residential land marketing, the Fair Housing Act reaches vacant land offered for residential construction through 42 U.S.C. section 3602(b). Describe physical characteristics and permitted uses rather than preferred occupants. State seller disclosures, common-law misrepresentation duties, recorded restrictions, floodplain rules, and septic suitability remain separate checks. Preserve adverse findings alongside favorable reports so the resale packet cannot silently contradict the acquisition file.

## 2. Draft the purchase, contractor, and resale documents around actual failure modes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a state-adapted purchase agreement that identifies the exact buying entity, seller capacity, legal description, purchase price, earnest-money holder, and closing mechanism. Attach the legal description instead of relying solely on an assessor number, which can change after a split. Specify when the inspection period begins, which missing documents delay commencement, how termination notice must be delivered, and who receives it. A deadline that depends on receipt should identify the delivery channel and controlling time zone. Require access for surveys and environmental work, with reasonable restoration and insurance provisions, but obtain separate permission before invasive sampling. Define satisfactory legal access through recorded rights benefiting the parcel; a visible road, permissive neighbor arrangement, or emergency-services route is insufficient evidence. Give the buyer explicit review rights over the title commitment, exception documents, survey, taxes, special assessments, mineral reservations, and restrictive covenants. State how objections are delivered, what the seller must cure, and what happens if cure fails. Keep closing extensions, deposit refunds, and dispute handling mechanically consistent so the escrow holder can act without interpreting conflicting provisions.

For minor subdivisions and infill repositioning, distinguish feasibility from promised entitlement. A purchase condition can require an acceptable written planning determination, recorded plat, utility commitment, or specific permit, but the document must identify which event actually controls closing. If the buyer will perform entitlement work before ownership, address application authority, reimbursement, abandonment, document ownership, and restoration. Prohibit representations that staff comments equal final approval. Allocate rollback taxes, transfer charges, survey costs, association assessments, and special district obligations expressly. Include casualty and condemnation procedures appropriate to vacant land, including partial takings affecting frontage. Assignment language must reflect the intended transaction: prohibit assignment, allow identified affiliates, or permit broader transfers with required disclosures and any seller consent. Do not promise a double closing until the settlement provider confirms funding, timing, and documentary requirements. On resale, deliver known adverse facts and required state forms through a tracked channel; an as-is clause does not authorize concealment. Match advertising statements about acreage, buildability, utilities, and access to the evidence attached to the file. Preserve the buyer's receipt and the exact document version supplied.

Keep contractor terms separate from purchase-price economics. Scope should name outputs: a GIS exception report, comparable-sale adjustment worksheet, listing engagement, or deadline register. Fee triggers must match lawful activity and measurable delivery; do not pay an unlicensed researcher a closing-contingent referral fee without resolving the state's compensation rules. A licensed broker's commission belongs in the applicable brokerage agreement with agency and payment terms. Draft any anti-circumvention provision narrowly around identified introductions, permitted activities, a stated duration, and preexisting relationships. It cannot convert unlicensed brokerage into a valid service. Liability caps require deliberate allocation: distinguish ordinary service errors from fraud, intentional misconduct, confidentiality breaches, and obligations that applicable law does not permit the parties to limit. Specify governing law, venue, notice, mediation timing, and whether arbitration applies, while preserving urgent relief where appropriate. Assign ownership of commissioned reports and workbooks, but preserve vendor license restrictions on underlying imagery and databases. Require exportable files, deletion or return procedures, access revocation, and confidentiality. The principal signs commercial changes; the coordinator may circulate documents and chase signatures, not rewrite negotiated risk allocations independently.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Preserve options before paying to solve the parcel**
> 
> Experienced land operators buy time and evidence before they buy survey revisions, engineering work, or speculative improvements.
> - Request every title exception instrument immediately; an exception number alone cannot reveal a maintenance burden or access restriction.
> - Calendar a decision checkpoint several business days before contractual notice expires, including the delivery method and backup recipient.
> - Ask the surveyor to reconcile the access easement's legal description with the purchased tract instead of merely depicting the visible road.
> - Make preclosing entitlement reports exportable and transferable where permitted, so a terminated purchase does not strand essential work inside a contractor account.

## 3. Fit legal review, title evidence, and capital exposure inside the four-person pod  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run the Base operation with one full-time principal and three contractors, not an invisible department behind the scorecard. Assign parcel research and GIS screening to one contractor, licensed market execution and comparable-sale interpretation to another, and transaction coordination to the third. The principal owns offers, capital exposure, final underwriting, and exception decisions. Lawyers, surveyors, environmental professionals, engineers, and title or escrow providers are transaction-specific outside services, not extra recurring pod seats; their charges still belong in deal costs. If workload requires dedicated additional staff, revise the staffing economics rather than hiding that labor. Use QGIS for screening, the county recorder for instruments, and a shared deadline register for contract events. Preserve source dates and parcel identifiers in every export. Comparable-sale tracking should distinguish recorded consideration, listing assertions, concessions, and verified closing evidence. Infill research needs utility availability and connection-cost evidence, not just a nearby line on a map. Minor subdivisions need a dependency schedule covering plat review, access construction, recording, and sale eligibility. Each handoff should name the unresolved issue, responsible person, required evidence, and next contractual deadline.

Reconcile every decision to gross parcel sale proceeds, the canonical revenue basis for Candidate #6. Conservative revenue of $200,000 at a 12% net margin produces $24,000 annual owner net profit and implies $176,000 of combined acquisition and operating costs. Base revenue of $850,000 at 18.5% produces $157,250 and implies $692,750 of costs. Scaled revenue of $1,500,000 at 25% produces $375,000 and implies $1,125,000 of costs. These are annual scenario identities, not budgets for any single parcel. Conservative and Base retain 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C, six people total. Purchase principal is an acquisition outflow, not revenue, and a refundable buyer deposit is not an additional parcel sale. Do not count gross sale proceeds and the same transaction's spread as separate revenue streams. The $15,000–$75,000 startup range and $3,000–$15,000 monthly operating range constrain liquidity; they are not additions to the stated profit cases. Reserve funding for diligence failures, property taxes, insurance, title cures, and extended holding periods. Track available cash separately from reported profit because unsold inventory can consume cash without producing a current disposition.

Use a title-insured disposition as a documented endpoint, not a promise that every parcel risk disappears. Obtain the proposed policy form, commitment requirements, exceptions, and available endorsements from the issuing provider. Ask which access, survey, subdivision, and zoning matters remain outside coverage; policy availability and endorsements vary by jurisdiction and underwriting facts. Review Schedule B instruments rather than relying on the summary label. Require payoff information and release mechanics for liens, and establish how delinquent taxes and special assessments will be handled. For entity sellers, confirm signing authority and settlement-provider identity requirements early. Verify wire instructions using an independently established telephone number, with a second confirmation for any change; email alone is insufficient. After closing, reconcile the settlement statement, recorded deed, disbursement evidence, and issued policy against the approved transaction. Retain delivery records for disclosures and buyer questions. A contractor cannot mark an access problem resolved merely because escrow closed. Close the exception register only when the promised document, cure, or explicitly accepted residual risk is recorded. Feed actual cure costs and holding days back into GIS filters and comparable-sale adjustments.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify capacity and jurisdiction
   - Open a parcel record naming the owner, buying entity, location, and intended transaction structure.
   - Principal identifies ownership versus brokerage activity; coordinator records applicable state licensing and subdivision review assignments.
2. Collect evidence before pricing certainty
   - Assemble title instruments, access evidence, zoning requirements, utilities, and environmental screening.
   - Research contractor labels unresolved findings; licensed market contractor adjusts comparable sales without converting assumptions into advertised facts.
3. Execute and monitor the negotiated protections
   - Use reviewed documents and calendar every inspection, objection, extension, and termination event.
   - Coordinator prepares notices for authorized execution; principal decides whether evidence supports closing, repricing, extension, or termination.
4. Reconcile closing and recycle operating evidence
   - Match sale proceeds, acquisition costs, closing charges, recorded documents, and delivered disclosures.
   - Principal updates deal results and cash availability; contractor reports feed actual access, entitlement, and holding-cost outcomes into future screening.

## 4. Use exact outreach scripts that disclose capacity and preserve contact choices  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Select the contact channel before writing the pitch. The Telephone Consumer Protection Act, 47 U.S.C. section 227, and FCC rules at 47 CFR section 64.1200 impose requirements that depend on technology, destination, purpose, and consent. Artificial or prerecorded voices, automated dialing features, and text campaigns need specific review; a purchased owner list does not establish consent. Federal treatment of a pure offer to buy property can differ from a solicitation selling services, while state telephone-solicitation laws can reach additional conduct. Do not classify every land-acquisition call as automatically exempt. Before any campaign, document the applicable calling rules, suppression process, local-time windows, caller identification, and any registration requirements. For commercial email, CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. section 7704, requires accurate headers, a nondeceptive subject, applicable advertising identification, a valid physical postal address, and an effective opt-out process; honor covered opt-outs within ten business days. Classify the message by its actual primary purpose. Start the four-person pod with truthful direct mail and individually reviewed responses rather than an unreviewed automated campaign. Keep contact-source provenance and a shared suppression record, even when a contractor supplies the list.

Use this direct-mail opening only after inserting the real entity, parcel identifier, return address, and contact information: 'I represent the company named above. We buy land for our own account and may resell it for a profit. County records identify you as an owner of the parcel described above. Would you consider discussing a sale? This letter is an inquiry, not an appraisal or a statement that you must sell. If you prefer no further contact, reply using the contact details above and we will record that preference.' Do not mimic a tax notice, imply government affiliation, or fabricate a competing offer. For a permitted live call, say: 'My name is the name shown in my introduction, and I am calling for the purchasing company I identified. We are interested in buying your parcel for our own account. Is now a suitable time for two questions?' Then ask: 'Are you authorized to discuss a sale, and are there other owners who must participate?' If the person declines further contact, stop the pitch and enter the request immediately. Never make refusal conditional on completing a survey or hearing the offer.

Discovery should produce evidence, not pressure. Ask: 'What do you know about recorded access, boundary disagreements, utility commitments, dumping, prior industrial activity, and notices from the county? Which documents can you share so we can verify the details?' Follow with: 'What timing matters to you, and would you like your own adviser involved before reviewing terms?' Do not describe an elderly owner as confused, a distressed owner as an easy target, or a tax deadline as proof that immediate acceptance is necessary. For resale inquiries, say: 'We own the parcel described in the listing. The document packet identifies the recorded access evidence, current zoning information, and known restrictions. Please identify your intended use so we can direct you to the relevant records without promising a permit.' If the entity holds only a contractual interest, replace the ownership claim with the reviewed, jurisdiction-specific interest disclosure before marketing. Log the exact script version, response, authority question, promised follow-up, and suppression status. Avoid recording calls until the applicable state recording-consent requirements are resolved. The principal reviews complaints and exceptions weekly; contractors may not improvise investment-return claims or conceal the operator's role.
