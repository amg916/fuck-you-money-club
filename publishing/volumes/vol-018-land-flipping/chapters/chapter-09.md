# Chapter 09: Approvals, Restrictions, and Encumbrances

*Minor partitions, subdivision approval steps and costs, road frontage requirements, backyard subdivision mistakes, raw land traps on access and utility costs, wetland reserve easements, coastal storm flowage zones, deed restrictions and how to remove them, HOA CC&Rs, and mineral, timber, and grazing rights*

## Executive Summary

Land flippers who survive approvals treat the recorded file and the county rulebook as the real product. Restriction specialists agree that deed restrictions and CC&Rs run with the land, that 'no HOA' still leaves recorded covenants able to bar livestock, RVs, or garages, and that only the original grantor or their heirs can lift a deed restriction. Subdivision operators agree every parcel needs real road frontage and a recorded plat before lots sell. The street numbers are hard: perc tests near $1,300, septic $5,000 to $30,000, utilities $9,000 to $34,500 or $40,000 to $80,000 with site work, Nashville plats at $1,375, and county roads demanding 20 feet of surface and 40 to 50 feet of right-of-way. Disagreement centers on scale: Cody Bjugan says partition paydays are not comparable to his 250-lot projects yet still backs them for beginners.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Across the restriction-focused operators, the baseline is blunt: whatever is recorded against the dirt travels with the dirt, and the buyer inherits it whether or not anyone mentioned it at the showing. Just Call Maggie frames deed restrictions as private controls written into the deed by the seller, enforced privately by the previous owner, a developer, or an HOA rather than by government, and binding every future owner; her example is a 'No commercial businesses' clause that blocks a cafe or shop permanently unless it is legally lifted [sazxc_VdXOw @ 00:01:05]. The Real Estate Lawyer lands in the same spot, describing deed restrictions such as 'no livestock' as legal limits that run with the land [gP3fDaUu5Iw @ 00:00:15]. Danielle Dojcak attacks a common assumption in land deals: agents and buyers hear 'no HOA' and conclude there are no rules, yet recorded covenants can still bar chickens, livestock, horses, detached garages, or boat and RV parking [ieOUjD1jQXA @ 00:00:24]. Tyler Bobo sees the same pattern in the Alaska valley, where buyers move to escape rules but most subdivisions still carry CC&Rs that travel with the property and stay enforceable even when vague and ignored [zzxrwQSJnC4 @ 00:00:49].

On the approval side, the subdivision operators agree that the county, not the seller, decides what a parcel can become. Tim Howell treats any new parcel, or even moving a line between two parcels, as a subdivision, and says each resulting parcel needs frontage on an actual road rather than an easement, though some counties accept a joint permanent easement [rtRAjfGZ_nY @ 00:00:14]. Liberty Acres runs the same front-end screen: check zoning, minimum lot size, frontage, and setbacks, then ask planning staff whether the split is minor or major and get the checklist; a minor split runs along an existing road, while a major one needs a new street, utilities, or engineering [qSoWEXftiCQ @ 00:00:20]. Cody Bjugan draws the line by count, saying a minor partition is about three parcels and that in a lot of jurisdictions four or more lots counts as a subdivision, and he concedes partitions are easier and quicker to push through [1B8q3q_Xt-s @ 00:00:00]. Liberty Acres also flags the legal hook that kills shortcut flips: most states bar lot sales until the plat is recorded, and selling the whole parcel instead shifts the risk to the buyer [qSoWEXftiCQ @ 00:01:22].

The third point of agreement is timing: dig up every restriction and access instrument before money goes hard, not after the buyer has fallen in love. US Realty Training warns that buyers fall for a house without knowing its restrictions and tells agents to research before the buyer plans substantial changes, naming two lookups, a title search and the county clerk's deed abstract that lists all restrictions [B_XnC97BYF8 @ 00:03:38]. Dana Sparks of Real Estate Made Crystal Clear pins the window precisely: pull the CC&Rs during due diligence so the buyer can still terminate and protect earnest money [okIP6dYiTD4 @ 00:04:03]. Barndo And Tiny Homes Vision applies the same logic to raw land, where sellers usually are not required to disclose problems, and insists that physical access is not legal access; verbal access means nothing because lenders and title companies need a recorded easement [VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:04:05]. Danielle Dojcak turns it into a field rule for anyone representing land: when a buyer says 'I want land,' ask why, then hunt for recorded deed restrictions that conflict with that use, alongside zoning, easements, permits, and health-department requirements, and stop, verify, and ask before making representations [ieOUjD1jQXA @ 00:00:36].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Liberty Acres lays out the approval run in five moves: check zoning, minimum lot size, frontage, and setbacks; get the minor-or-major call and checklist from planning staff; have a licensed surveyor draw the plat; do the septic soil work before the vote; then collect utility sign-offs, file, attend the hearing, and record the plat [qSoWEXftiCQ @ 00:00:00]. The thresholds and fees swing by jurisdiction: Washington allows four or fewer lots as a short subdivision, Toledo allows up to five as a lot split, Huntsville charges $50 plus $1 per lot, Toledo charges $150 per split, and Nashville charges $1,375 for a final plat and $3,000 for a major concept plan, with Toledo clearing in several weeks against roughly 120 days in Huntsville [qSoWEXftiCQ @ 00:01:00]. The channel's point is that the government application fee is often the smallest line in a stack that also carries surveyor, septic, engineering, road, bonds, and recording. Tim Howell shows where that stack explodes: upgrading a driveway to a county approved road means about 20 feet of surface, six to eight inches of base rock, a 40 to 50 foot right-of-way, and a grade no steeper than 15 percent, more likely 12, which he calls very costly [rtRAjfGZ_nY @ 00:01:20].

Barndo And Tiny Homes Vision prices the hidden approval stack on raw land, opening with the warning that an $8,000 acre can turn into $60,000 more [VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:00:00]. A perc test averages about $1,300, typically $750 to $1,900 and anywhere from $250 to over $3,500, with survey at $370 to $770 and design and permitting at $500 to $1,200; septic then runs $5,000 to $25,000 for a standard system, $15,000 to $30,000 for a mound, $12,000 to $25,000 aerobic, and $15,000 to $25,000 for a sand filter [VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:02:13]. The channel tells buyers to make the offer contingent on the perc test, use a licensed local tester, and remember results stay valid two to five years. Utilities total $9,000 to $34,500, or $40,000 to $80,000 and up with full site work: power at $5 to $25 per foot with some utilities charging $50 to $100, poles at $1,200 to $5,600 each, and wells at $25 to $65 per foot complete, typically 100 to 300 feet deep, with contractor bids varying 30 to 60 percent [VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:06:13]. Peter Kelly Property adds the backyard-split version: about four metres of clear access width, not three, plus compliant parking for both homes [KmCwZ5I6BYU @ 00:03:49].

On recorded restrictions and mineral claims, the mechanics are records work. Dana Sparks hunts CC&Rs through the seller, the Georgia HOA directory, county deed books, the gsccca.org real estate index using 'view image' with printing costing extra, and the Secretary of State corporations search to pin down the HOA's legal name [okIP6dYiTD4 @ 00:07:16]. The Real Estate Lawyer warns that removing a deed restriction means tracing the original grantor's heirs or successor entities; in one case two children fanned out to ten grandchildren, and a single holdout makes removal challenging if not impossible [gP3fDaUu5Iw @ 00:01:53]. When an HOA claims minerals, HOA LAND WARS runs the chain parcel by parcel: a county parcel map showed the lease acreage exceeded HOA-owned parcels, five document requests went out for the lease, board resolution, legal description, recorded authority instrument, and the payee-determining company, and the recorder search turned up a reservation, assignment, and partial release, none naming the HOA [CnlqGcAGqOo @ 00:06:26]. In the HOA Insanity case, the owner spent two weeks tracing title back to the 1890s, found no severance in the chain, and defended a $50,000 signing bonus and 18 percent royalty offer against the HOA's common-assets claim [KuFkYy1kf7s @ 00:02:28].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Screen the split at the planning counter
   - Check zoning, minimum lot size, frontage, and setbacks, then get the minor-or-major call and checklist.
   - Ask planning staff whether the split runs along an existing road as a minor split or needs a new street, utilities, or engineering as a major one, and get the surveyor drawing the plat [Liberty Acres — qSoWEXftiCQ @ 00:00:20].
2. Lock legal access and road frontage
   - Confirm a recorded easement and frontage on an actual road built to county standards.
   - Physical access is not legal access, so demand the recorded easement and watch for multi-owner roads, seasonal roads, and maintenance agreements; each split parcel needs county-road frontage [Barndo And Tiny Homes Vision — VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:04:05] [Tim Howell — rtRAjfGZ_nY @ 00:00:43].
3. Price the dirt-to-buildable stack
   - Make the offer contingent on the perc test and load septic, power, well, and driveway costs.
   - Add a 10 to 20 percent buffer to the utility and site budget and keep every contingency in place until each check is done [Barndo And Tiny Homes Vision — VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:10:09].
4. Pull every recorded restriction and mineral link
   - Run the title search and deed abstract, and chain any mineral claim parcel by parcel.
   - Use the title search and the county clerk's deed abstract to list all restrictions, and when an HOA claims minerals, chart deeds, reservations, assignments, and releases until a conveyance to the HOA is proven or missing [US Realty Training — B_XnC97BYF8 @ 00:03:38] [HOA LAND WARS — CnlqGcAGqOo @ 00:22:46].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split among land operators is how big to cut. Cody Bjugan judges every deal on 'return on effort,' runs projects of 100 to 500 lots averaging around 250, and calls it 'highly unlikely' he would ever do another minor partition because 'paydays aren't even comparable'; matching one 240-lot project would take 80 partitions [1B8q3q_Xt-s @ 00:01:06]. Yet he refuses to trash the small play: 'I don't want to poo poo on minor partitions,' he says, ranking them above a lot of real estate strategies and pushing beginners to start there, then level up [1B8q3q_Xt-s @ 00:01:56]. Liberty Acres draws the line on infrastructure instead of lot count: a minor split runs along an existing road, while a major one needs a new street, utilities or engineering, and the thresholds move by jurisdiction, with Washington capping short subdivisions at 4 lots and Toledo allowing up to 5 as a lot split [qSoWEXftiCQ @ 00:00:20]. Peter Kelly Property works the opposite end of the scale, a single backyard split, and argues the hurdle rate depends on motive: a pure investor needs margin after costs, interest, tax and a risk buffer, while an owner-occupier living in one home can accept a slimmer return [KmCwZ5I6BYU @ 00:01:24].

Practitioners split on how hard a recorded restriction actually bites. Tyler Bobo, working Alaska valley deals, says most subdivisions still carry CC&Rs that are often vague and unenforced yet still enforceable, so his playbook is to drive the neighborhood and compare real compliance against the paper: if nobody keeps horses, don't get horses, but if farms are everywhere, enforcement is less likely [zzxrwQSJnC4 @ 00:01:33]. Just Call Maggie takes the hard line, teaching that deed restrictions run with the land, bind every future owner, and that a 'No commercial businesses' clause blocks a cafe or shop permanently unless it is legally lifted [sazxc_VdXOw @ 00:02:11]. The Real Estate Lawyer says lifting one is far narrower than buyers assume: only the original grantor can remove a deed restriction, not a later seller, and if the grantor is gone you trace heirs or successor entities, as in one case where 2 children branched into 10 grandchildren and a single holdout makes removal 'challenging if not impossible' [gP3fDaUu5Iw @ 00:01:53]. Danielle Dojcak attacks the comfort zone from the other side, warning that 'no HOA' does not mean no rules because recorded covenants can still ban chickens, horses, detached garages or boat and RV parking [ieOUjD1jQXA @ 00:00:24].

On mineral fights, two owner war stories show different playbooks against an HOA claiming the rights. In the HOA Insanity case, a $50,000 signing bonus and 18% royalty offer triggered a two-week courthouse title trace back to the 1890s that found no severance; the owner then ignored $50 to $100 fines to build a harassment record, pursued fees, and leaned on the principle that a covenant 'cannot supersede a recorded deed' [KuFkYy1kf7s @ 00:11:46]. The HOA LAND WARS narrator went quieter and more surgical: five document requests (lease, board resolution, legal description, recorded authority instrument, and payee-determining company), then a parcel-by-parcel chain chart sent with an attorney letter to the energy company's land department, which got private lots stripped out of the lease without a courtroom win [CnlqGcAGqOo @ 00:18:31]. The consolidation pitch also splits the room: the HOA Insanity board pitched a '$20M' pool with a 10% management fee the owner tagged as a '$2 million' payday, countering that owners can negotiate collectively through their own transparent trust with minimal fees [KuFkYy1kf7s @ 00:18:22]. James Tanner frames the separate-estate logic: owners can lease mineral rights without leasing the land, and with wind turbines owners collect rent while still grazing cattle [potSOIxnw0E @ 00:10:54].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The biggest economic trap is buying cheap dirt and then paying for everything underneath and around it. Barndo And Tiny Homes Vision warns an $8,000 acre can turn into $60,000 more, and that sellers usually are not required to disclose problems on land; the perc test averages about $1,300, while septic runs $5,000 to $25,000 standard and $15,000 to $30,000 for a mound [VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:03:09]. The same channel prices utilities at $9,000 to $34,500, or $40,000 to $80,000 and up with full site work, notes bids vary by 30 to 60 percent, and insists on a 10 to 20 percent buffer [VIdzLB23cAw @ 00:06:13]. Tim Howell shows how frontage kills splits: upgrading a driveway to a county approved road means about 20 feet of surface, 6 to 8 inches of base rock, a 40 to 50 foot right-of-way and a grade no steeper than 15 percent, more likely 12, which he calls 'very costly,' and counties won't let you create a non-conforming lot [rtRAjfGZ_nY @ 00:01:20]. Liberty Acres adds that 'the government application fee is often the smallest line' once surveyor, septic, engineering, road, bonds and recording stack up [qSoWEXftiCQ @ 00:01:22].

Site geometry and mapping errors are the quiet killers. Peter Kelly Property says the position of the existing house matters more than total land size even at 600 to 700 square meters, and his access rule is blunt: 'If you cannot get cars to the back safely and comfortably, you do not have a project,' with about 4 meters of clear width needed, not 3, and the front house often losing compliant parking [KmCwZ5I6BYU @ 00:03:49]. He also flags that services bring easements, protected trees and existing pits, that building behind an existing house means hand work and slower progress, and that feasibility must be stress-tested for delays and a softer market because 'a quick permit is not the goal' [KmCwZ5I6BYU @ 00:10:58]. On coastal parcels, MassDEP's presenters warn about the 'invisible A zone': a transition narrower than 0.2 inches on the map, roughly 80 feet on the ground, is simply not mapped, and because detailed analysis happens only at transects that get interpolated between, operators should check the date of the coastal study rather than the map date [lRc-2cEUn7U @ 00:08:36]. Claiming a less restrictive zone needs a Letter of Map Change, while photo evidence of higher flooding can make an area more restrictive without one [lRc-2cEUn7U @ 00:16:14].

Encumbrance traps hit after the contract is signed if the paperwork pull comes late. Dana Sparks of Real Estate Made Crystal Clear says to get the CC&Rs during due diligence so the buyer can terminate and protect earnest money, warning that assessments 'tend to increase' and covenants can be amended; she pulls them from the seller, county deed books, the gsccca.org index or a Secretary of State search for the HOA's name [okIP6dYiTD4 @ 00:04:03]. The amendment risk is real: in the HOA Insanity case, the mineral clause was slipped in 7 years earlier through a 30-day 'silence equals acceptance' notice with only 12 of 30 owners attending, the TRO was denied, and $3,000-plus in fines became a lien while the HOA's cease-and-desist delayed drilling for months [KuFkYy1kf7s @ 00:19:15]. The Real Estate Lawyer warns the seller side too: adding restrictions without disclosing them before contract risks breach under the North Carolina standard offer to purchase [gP3fDaUu5Iw @ 00:03:30]. Conservation encumbrances cap income as well: on the MissStateExtension Panther Tract, a Wetland Reserve Easement means crops can't be pulled, the trees are too young for pulpwood, and hunting leases are the 'number one income generator' [UkiDG5GpG_Y @ 00:02:46].
