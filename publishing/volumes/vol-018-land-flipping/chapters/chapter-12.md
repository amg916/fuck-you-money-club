## 1. Days 1–7: Select One County and Establish the Capital Boundary  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one county, one parcel-size band, and one buyer use case rather than a statewide mailing list. Candidate #6 rewards disciplined screening, not maximum acreage under contract. For this sprint, map six operator plays explicitly: P1 GIS discount sourcing; P2 access-verified rural parcels; P3 utility-ready infill; P4 adjacent-owner assemblage; P5 minor subdivisions; and P6 title-insured resale. These are this chapter's operating labels, not six separate businesses to launch simultaneously. P1 feeds every opportunity, P6 closes the loop, and the owner selects either P2 or P3 as the initial product. Keep P4 and P5 as documented exceptions until buyer demand and county procedures support their additional complexity. On Day 1, write the intended purchaser, maximum parcel basis, allowed zoning categories, minimum usable area, and excluded conditions into a one-page buy box. On Day 2, examine county recorder documents, assessor exports, local planning rules, FEMA mapping, and actual recorded sales. QGIS organizes overlays; it does not establish boundaries, ownership, or legal access. Reject counties where available evidence cannot support at least ten relevant closed sales from the preceding twelve months without mixing incompatible parcel uses or infrastructure conditions. Record the search boundary so later expansion cannot disguise weak evidence.

Assign the four-person pod before buying data. The full-time owner controls offers, capital allocation, seller conversations, and final underwriting. Contractor one maintains parcel records, QGIS layers, and comparable-sale tracking. Contractor two coordinates title, surveying, access documents, and planning inquiries with qualified local providers. Contractor three handles authorized outreach administration, listing production, and buyer follow-up, using a licensed broker where the activity requires one. Surveyors, laboratories, closing firms, and counsel remain separately purchased services; their invoices must still enter parcel economics and workload reporting. Cap the owner's planned week at forty hours: twelve for sourcing and seller calls, twelve for underwriting, eight for transaction coordination, and eight for buyer development and management. Give each contractor a ten-hour weekly planning allowance during the pilot, subject to actual rates and the spending ceiling. Hours are capacity limits, not evidence that every specialist can be retained at the implied price. Maintain one Airtable or spreadsheet record per assessor parcel number, with a separate county identifier to prevent collisions. Store source dates, document links, responsible person, next action, and cash exposure. Never let an attractive asking price substitute for a complete parcel record or a confirmed person responsible for resolving its outstanding questions.

Set the first month's non-acquisition cash ceiling at $6,000 before outreach starts. Allocate $1,500 to data and outreach, $1,200 to parcel analysis, $1,000 to title and access investigation, $800 to field and planning checks, $900 to buyer development, and $600 to contingency. These are proposed allocations within the volume's $3,000–$15,000 monthly operating range, not vendor quotations. Include contractor invoices inside these buckets rather than adding labor afterward. At the $50,000 startup-capital floor, ring-fence another $18,000 for three future months at the same ceiling. That leaves no more than $26,000 for purchase price, escrow deposits, and parcel-specific closing obligations after the first month's planned spend. Larger acquisitions require more capital or a different buy box; do not consume the operating reserve to force a deal. Treat deposits as committed cash even when refundable, and record their refund deadlines. Review incurred costs plus signed obligations twice weekly. If specialist quotes exceed the budget, reduce parcel count or stop a play before ordering work. The three-to-twelve-month time to first deal makes liquidity preservation more important than producing a ceremonial Day-30 purchase. No debt proceeds or anticipated resale receipts belong in available cash until the funds are actually accessible.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before the First Acquisition Commitment**
> 
> An accepted price does not authorize a purchase. The owner records these four checks before releasing acquisition funds or allowing protections to expire.
> - Verify the seller's signing authority and match the legal description across the deed, commitment, contract, and survey evidence.
> - Obtain documented legal access and inspect the recorded easement scope; a visible road or assessor frontage line is insufficient.
> - Confirm intended use, utility feasibility, environmental investigation scope, and any required plat action with the responsible local authority or specialist.
> - Recalculate purchase cash, closing charges, downside proceeds, and remaining operating reserve using written quotes and dated comparable evidence.

## 2. Days 8–14: Run One Measured Outreach Cohort and Underwrite Responses  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a 200-parcel research cohort during Week 2, then remove duplicates, parcels outside the buy box, unresolved ownership conflicts, and obvious disqualifiers. The target is 150 screened records and 100 distinct owners reached through documented delivery, not 200 indiscriminate messages. Prefer individually addressed postal letters for the initial test, with accurate purchaser identity and no suggestion of government affiliation. Calling and texting require a separate review of 47 U.S.C. § 227, applicable state requirements, and suppression procedures; a purchased telephone number does not itself establish permission. Record sent, returned, delivered or reasonably delivery-supported, responded, qualified, and offered as separate events. Use a consistent maturity window: evaluate response rates only after fourteen calendar days from supported delivery. A Day-14 mailing therefore contributes mature observations by Day 28, whereas a replacement mailed on Day 25 does not. Count owners rather than parcels for response rates because one owner may control several lots. Keep undelivered records outside the response denominator and inside the address-quality denominator. Do not mix a rural letter cohort with infill broker referrals when calculating channel performance. Their acquisition costs and conversion paths differ materially, and blending them would conceal which repeatable process deserves the next dollar.

Define a qualified seller conversation before the first response arrives: the person has plausible authority, identifies the parcel, expresses willingness to discuss a sale, and provides a price expectation or agrees to review an offer. A callback requesting removal is not qualified demand. Target five qualified conversations from the first 100 mature delivered contacts, but treat five percent as a pilot decision threshold rather than a market benchmark. For each promising parcel, retrieve at least three genuinely comparable closed transactions and explain differences in access, usable acreage, utility distance, entitlement, and sale timing. Use a conservative resale estimate based on supportable adjustments, not the highest neighboring listing. Calculate maximum purchase price as expected gross proceeds minus closing and selling costs, diligence, carrying costs, allocated overhead, and required profit. As an illustrative Base-margin screen, a $100,000 resale supports $81,500 of total costs and $18,500 of profit. One possible cost stack is $55,000 acquisition, $4,000 diligence, $7,500 settlement and marketing, $5,000 carrying, and $10,000 allocated overhead. That example exceeds the smallest launch's acquisition capacity; it illustrates underwriting, not permission to spend beyond available cash. Quote each material input before converting the screen into a binding commitment.

Separate desktop rejection from paid investigation. P2 requires a recorded access path benefiting the actual parcel, practical entry conditions, and evidence that maintenance obligations are affordable. P3 requires utility-provider information about service availability, connection charges, capacity, and timing; a line on the next street does not establish a usable connection. P4 requires an identified adjacent buyer and a premium supported by their use case, not a speculative assemblage multiplier. P5 requires written procedural guidance about subdivision, frontage, drainage, minimum lot dimensions, and infrastructure obligations. In applicable unincorporated Texas areas, Local Government Code § 232.001 supplies a county plat framework, with exceptions and additional rules requiring local review. Never assume that drawing two polygons creates two saleable lots. P6 requires a closing path that addresses commitment requirements and material exceptions before promising clean delivery. Ask the title company what coverage is actually available; insurance does not automatically establish buildability or remove exclusions. By Day 14, the owner should have three documented offer candidates or a specific diagnosis explaining the shortfall. If every candidate depends on an unpriced easement, rezoning, or utility extension, the county may be workable but the selected product and capital envelope are not aligned.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Land-Specific Ways to Avoid Paying for False Positives**
> 
> Buy inexpensive evidence first, but never confuse inexpensive evidence with a final professional determination.
> - Ask the recorder for the easement instrument and amendments before commissioning field work; the grant may exclude the intended use or benefit another parcel.
> - Call the utility's service-design desk with the parcel identifier and intended load; a general customer-service statement rarely prices extension work.
> - Interview adjacent owners about actual purchase interest before modeling assemblage value, and keep their statements separate from a binding purchase commitment.
> - Maintain rejected comparable sales alongside accepted ones; a documented exclusion log prevents the next analyst from reintroducing inflated subdivision or improved-property prices.

## 3. Days 15–21: Resolve Fatal Defects Before Advancing Contract Exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Week 3 turns seller interest into a controlled investigation, not an obligation to rescue every attractive parcel. Issue offers only after the owner signs the underwriting record and checks cash availability. Use locally appropriate contract forms and transaction counsel when drafting or modifying protections. Specify inspection periods, title review rights, deposit handling, permitted assignment if relevant, closing conditions, and the procedure for timely termination. Do not assume a contingency exists because a spreadsheet contains an unresolved issue. Open a deadline register immediately after execution, with reminders five, three, and one business day before each critical notice deadline. Route funds through the selected closing provider and independently verify wiring instructions using a previously established telephone number. A changed email attachment is not sufficient verification. Cap the pilot at two simultaneous paid diligence files; this limits both cash leakage and the coordinator's task load. Each file receives a defect ledger listing the issue, source document, responsible resolver, estimated cost, completion date, and effect on the offer. If a seller will not provide enough investigation time to answer a material question, decline or renegotiate rather than substituting confidence for evidence. The owner's signature records a decision, not merely attendance at a review.

Investigate environmental constraints in proportion to the parcel's history and intended use, with qualified environmental professionals determining appropriate scope. County GIS, EPA mapping, aerial history, and FEMA layers identify questions; they cannot establish absence of contamination, wetlands, or developable area. CERCLA's bona fide prospective purchaser framework includes requirements in 42 U.S.C. § 9601(40) and § 9607(r), while 40 CFR Part 312 governs all appropriate inquiries. Some inquiry components require updating within 180 days before acquisition, and the overall timing framework includes a one-year limit. Have the professional confirm applicable components and continuing obligations rather than treating a report date as a universal shield. Where fill or dredged material may affect jurisdictional waters, examine Clean Water Act § 404, 33 U.S.C. § 1344, before modeling earthwork as routine preparation. For access, distinguish recorded legal rights, physical usability, and lender or title-insurer treatment. For zoning, obtain the permitted-use basis and dimensional constraints from the responsible authority. A parcel can have an insurable ownership interest while remaining commercially unsuitable for the intended purchaser. Record that distinction in the investment memo so a clean title discussion cannot silently replace the separate buildability investigation.

Price the exit while diligence is still reversible. Contractor three develops a buyer evidence sheet with two independent price opinions, three comparable closings, likely buyer financing, estimated marketing time, and disposition expenses. A broker opinion is useful evidence but not a completed sale. When representing others for compensation, check the governing licensing rules; California Business and Professions Code §§ 10130–10131 illustrate why buying for one's own account and performing brokerage for another are distinct activities. Do not assume an ownership structure or assignment label resolves every state's requirements. Build a downside case with resale proceeds ten percent below the supported estimate and an additional six months of quoted carrying costs. The pilot purchase rule requires nonnegative owner profit under that downside and at least an 18.5 percent modeled net margin under the supported case, after allocated overhead. These are selection thresholds, not a restatement of the portfolio's realized outcomes. For example, an extra $3,000 carry on the $100,000 illustration leaves $5,500 profit after a ten percent price reduction. An unpaid easement cure can erase that cushion immediately. Reprice or terminate when verified costs break either threshold; do not preserve a contract merely because diligence money has already been spent.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the evidence package
   - Save dated title, access, comparable, utility, planning, and cost documents.
   - Link each underwriting input to its source and distinguish professional findings from preliminary desktop observations.
2. Reconcile the entire cash obligation
   - Add purchase cash, deposits, fees, diligence, carrying, and selling obligations.
   - Avoid double-counting credited deposits while retaining their full effect on cash availability before closing.
3. Apply supported and downside screens
   - Require the supported margin threshold, nonnegative downside profit, and protected operating reserve.
   - Use verified cure costs and six extra months of carrying charges rather than a generic contingency percentage.
4. Execute or exit before the deadline
   - Record the owner's decision and deliver any required notices through the contract's specified method.
   - Retain confirmation of delivery, deposit disposition, and the closed defect ledger in the parcel file.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Reviews at 60/90: Earn Expansion with Cohort Evidence  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the final nine days to finish evidence, inspect workload, and reconcile cash rather than manufacture a closing milestone. Candidate #6 normally needs three to twelve months to reach a first deal, so Day 30 measures process validity and investable pipeline. Reconcile every invoice, refundable deposit, signed purchase obligation, and remaining reserve on Day 24. On Day 25, ask each contractor to report actual hours and unresolved tasks. If the owner exceeds forty planned hours or a contractor exceeds ten for two consecutive weeks, stop expanding file count until work is removed or repriced. On Day 26, audit ten randomly selected parcel records against original sources. Require at least nine complete files, with zero misidentified owners or unsupported legal-access conclusions. On Days 27–29, mature the outreach cohort, refresh the pipeline, and classify defects as fatal, curable within ten business days, or awaiting an external milestone. Day 30 produces a written Kill, Fix, or Scale decision for each channel and play. An immature cohort receives Fix, not a fabricated response rate. A healthy research pipeline can earn more screening capacity while still earning no authority to purchase a parcel.

Apply the decision matrix in order: capital and fatal-defect stops first, qualified evidence second, expansion last. Kill means stop new spending on the affected channel, play, or parcel and preserve contractual rights; it does not mean abandon existing obligations. Fix means hold spending flat, name one bottleneck, and give its owner ten business days to produce a specific correction. Scale means increase screened or contacted volume by no more than twenty-five percent per review while preserving the same purchase rules and reserve. At Day 30, require 100 mature delivered contacts, five qualified seller conversations, three costed offers, and one diligence-ready candidate before expanding sourcing. Diligence-ready means plausible seller agreement on price, complete preliminary underwriting, an identified investigation path, and affordable cash exposure; it is not a title clearance. At Day 60, require 200 mature contacts, ten qualified conversations, six costed offers, and one executed investigation-protected contract. At Day 90, require 300, fifteen, and nine respectively, plus one fully underwritten acquisition-ready file and two evidenced buyer prospects. These cumulative targets test repeatability, not a predicted sales calendar. Failure to close by Day 90 alone does not trigger termination when the milestones, liquidity, and priced investigation path remain intact.

Keep cash-control reporting separate from annual earnings reconciliation. The launch ceiling is $6,000 by Day 30, $12,000 cumulatively by Day 60, and $18,000 by Day 90 for non-acquisition pilot outlays. Purchase principal and closing commitments sit in a separate exposure schedule, never outside total cash control. Categorize direct parcel costs, inventory, and period overhead consistently with the accountant's treatment; deposits and unsold land are not completed-sale proceeds. The canonical Conservative case is $200,000 gross parcel sale proceeds less $176,000 total costs, leaving $24,000 owner profit at twelve percent. Base is $850,000 less $692,750, leaving $157,250 at 18.5 percent. Scaled is $1,500,000 less $1,125,000, leaving $375,000 at twenty-five percent. Conservative and Base retain one full-time owner plus three contractors; Scaled requires two full-time people plus four contractors. Do not multiply a single successful flip into an annual forecast without accounting for capital recycling, inventory duration, overhead, and failed investigations. Add the sixth-person structure only after completed-sale evidence and workload support it, not because a Day-90 sourcing threshold was reached. Track realized margin separately from modeled margin and explain every variance. The sprint succeeds when the next capital decision is better supported, even when that decision is to stop buying.
