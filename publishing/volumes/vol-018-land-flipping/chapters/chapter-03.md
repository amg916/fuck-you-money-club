# Chapter 03: Cold Calling, Texting, and Multichannel Deal Acquisition

*Live cold calling sessions on vacant land, tax-delinquent, and code-violation lists, texting systems that produce contracts, DNC and TCPA legal rules and lawsuit risk, getting landowners to say yes, the 4-channel marketing and 4-part acquisition systems, and why old direct-response tactics are failing*

## Executive Summary

Land operators agree that outbound volume plus fast offers drives contracts: Michael Bull says calling, texting and mail all profit and reach different sellers on one list, while Dylan Applegate's students hit 6 contracts in 7 days by pushing 20 offers a day. The street math varies widely. The Apke Brothers clock one deal per 5,000 texts for a skilled texter versus one per 20,000 for beginners; Bull says 10,000 to 40,000 texts per contract; Clay Hepler logs 30,000 to 250,000. Ajay Sharma prices callers at $4 to $8 an hour, and Maximilian Dier says stacked government lists convert roughly 10x better. The split is sharp: Land Academy and Bull favor mail, Applegate and the Apke Brothers start with SMS, and TCPA demand letters, honey-pot sellers and million-dollar settlements haunt every texting playbook.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every serious operator here treats deal acquisition as a volume game built on outbound reach, and none of them claims a single channel is the only one that pays. Michael Bull, who once ran three separate channel teams across 300-plus transactions, says cold calling, texting and direct mail are all profitable and hit mutually exclusive seller types, so the same 10,000-record list can be worked three ways [PMSn1EaZ42Y @ 00:00:53]. Maximilian Dier frames wholesaling as a pyramid: mass outreach at the base, follow-up in the middle, the close at the top, with paid upgrades like callers, pay-per-lead, Google ads and mailers layered on once cash exists [uQ2jDRUrtoY @ 00:00:36]. Jeff and DeAndre of the Land Flipping Empire run text-first because sellers respond faster to SMS than to cold calls, pushing about 1,000 texts a day through the Flip Force CRM [4KWC7a6vLcY @ 00:40:39]. Clay Hepler, with 200-plus deals across 25-plus states at a $37K average profit, adds the discipline layer: pick one channel, one data type, and commit to it for a year before adding another, and only stack new channels after crossing $1M a year [c8ekY72VGy0 @ 00:06:04]. The shared baseline is relentless, repeatable outreach rather than clever one-off hustle.

The second point of agreement is that leads are worthless until an offer goes out, and that qualification, not raw lead count, is what scales. Dylan Applegate's diagnosis of struggling students is blunt: everyone has the same tools, but the people who fall behind let leads stall in the qualify stage instead of pushing every single one to offer given, with phone offers preferred and text as the fallback [MaWsUKnc8CI @ 00:06:33]. Clay Hepler compresses the same idea into two lines: scale qualified leads, not gross leads, because qualified leads equal offers [c8ekY72VGy0 @ 00:11:05]. The Apke Brothers list the skills that separate a texter who closes from one who burns two to three months with nothing: qualifying and disqualifying both the person and the land, copywriting, A/B testing, follow-up, and a tight definition of what counts as a lead [Ls2lelB6ImM @ 00:10:28]. Ajay Sharma builds that filter into payroll, putting a roughly $6-an-hour lead manager in place whose entire job is to confirm the owner would sell, extract an asking price, and book the appointment [Jb9R7WTh63s @ 00:14:01]. Across channels, the operators converge on speed and volume of offers as the real needle mover.

Baseline prerequisites are cheap in hardware and expensive in discipline. DeAndre says a phone, a laptop and Wi-Fi are the only three things needed to start, and the assignability clause lets the pair sell contracts with no license and no capital of their own [4KWC7a6vLcY @ 00:21:51]. Where the operators get demanding is the front end: data and market selection. Ajay Sharma's four-part acquisition system starts with data and market selection before lead generation, lead management and closing [Jb9R7WTh63s @ 00:03:15]. Dylan Applegate screens counties for a 100% one-year sell-through rate, at least 15 actives and 15 solds in six months, a pending sell-through of 20% or more, a rising trend across every window, and a median pending price of at least $125K, then pulls rural 2-plus-acre parcels owned 36-plus months with government, church, school, landlocked, heavy-wetland and heavy-flood parcels scrubbed out, and adds that only about 13 to 14 counties fit everything at once [Hyk7GilIJ8g @ 00:04:17]. Clay Hepler runs a parallel screen, targeting 10 to 15% monthly sell-through and 60 to 75% over six months, and reads a weak six-month number as a slowing market [c8ekY72VGy0 @ 00:01:54].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

On the texting side, the Apke Brothers lay out a full monthly stack: Launch Control at $500, Land Portal Unlimited Pro at $500 for 20,000 data points, and roughly $400 of skip tracing at 4 cents a record, landing at about $1,400 a month for 10,000 new contacts, or $1,400 to $1,800 for 20,000 [Ls2lelB6ImM @ 00:12:06]. They flag REI Reply at $50 to $90 a month as cheaper but less legally compliant, and their student Roy texts from 9 to 11 every day, closing about one deal per 5,000 texts against roughly one per 20,000 for beginners, at an average margin near $12K. Maximilian Dier runs a far cheaper send cost through GoHighLevel via XLeads at a penny a text, so 20,000 texts cost $200, and calls SMS the highest return on ad spend at 10 to 30 times [xPy40xTlt64 @ 00:23:27]. Ajay Sharma's staffing benchmark is one VA handling about 2,000 texts a day, roughly 50,000 a month, using SmarterContact or Launch Control, and insists owners should not be sending texts themselves [Jb9R7WTh63s @ 00:06:51]. Michael Bull's team model for a text shop adds a sender, a valuations manager, an acquisition manager and a transaction coordinator [PMSn1EaZ42Y @ 00:05:47].

Cold-calling mechanics hinge on dialer throughput and honest yield math. Maximilian Dier runs the Enzo dialer across up to 14 lines at once, scaling from 1 to 14 based on how many people answer, and logged 680 calls in 1 hour 7 minutes against roughly 100 calls in 30 minutes on a regular dialer [hIbsCLgyqbg @ 00:54:33]. Dier's KPI is one hot lead per 1,000 contacts on average, and $300 buys XLeads plus Enzo with about 25,000 skip-traced numbers a month. Nick, the cold-calling agency guest of the Apke Brothers, publishes the package math: 5,000 records skip trace to about 4,000 mobile numbers at an 80% match, worked in 8,000 dials across two passes with no double-dialing, yielding one lead per 150 to 350 dials [lWYFLEd7PA0 @ 00:35:06]. Layer SMS on top at one lead per 80 to 150 texts and the package delivers 40 to 100 leads for $3,600 with an American caller, at roughly one deal per 20 leads, or 5 to 10 contracts and 2 to 5 closings [lWYFLEd7PA0 @ 00:38:10]. Ajay Sharma prices ReadyMode callers at $4 to $8 an hour and expects about 40 leads a month from one full-time caller, roughly 1.7 to 1.8 a day [Jb9R7WTh63s @ 00:09:24].

The scripts are built to disarm. The Apke Brothers' session with cold-calling agency owner Nick lays out an opener that is a deliberately flustered 'sorry to bother,' asks whether the owner has ever maybe considered selling, counts a maybe-plus as a lead, and bans the word investor because investor already means lowball offer [lWYFLEd7PA0 @ 00:24:44]. The same session's rules: never ask the seller's price, since an early offer is like kissing at the start of a first date, start low and go up, and run 11 questions ordered easy to hard. Maximilian Dier's live call on a 2-acre Port Charlotte lot shows the lowball in action: two comps at $9,000 and $9,500, then a hard line that Dier couldn't go a dollar over $7,000 while covering closing costs, a roughly $500 survey and no agent fees, framed as a way to lowball without offending people [hIbsCLgyqbg @ 00:22:12]. When renegotiating after contract, Dier brings ammunition such as a repair estimate jumping from $40K to $60K, blames a $15,000 gap with the lender, asks what do we do, then stays silent, landing about a 50% success rate [uQ2jDRUrtoY @ 01:08:14]. Dylan Applegate's rule closes the loop: if the seller cannot be reached by phone within 24 to 48 hours, text the offer anyway [Hyk7GilIJ8g @ 00:13:10].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Pull a Filtered List in a Proven Market
   - Screen the county on sell-through, then pull a scrubbed rural list.
   - Pull rural 2-plus-acre parcels owned 36-plus months, off the MLS, with no more than 25% wetlands or flood zone, and scrub government, county, church, school and landlocked owners; pull broad rather than only tax-delinquent owners [Dylan Applegate — Hyk7GilIJ8g @ 00:06:48].
2. Skip Trace and Launch Outreach Volume
   - Skip trace the list and start at a fixed monthly send volume.
   - Start with 10,000 texts a month, scaling toward the 4,000 to 6,000 monthly texts the hosts tie to a $100K year once copy and qualification are dialed in [Apke Brothers — Ls2lelB6ImM @ 00:09:48].
3. Qualify Every Positive Response
   - Run each responder through the qualification questions fast.
   - Ask about utilities, other owners, how long they have owned it and the reason for selling; try for the asking price first, but send an offer even if they will not give one [Dylan Applegate — Hyk7GilIJ8g @ 00:11:08].
4. Push Offers Out Daily
   - Set a hard daily offer quota and let the CRM handle follow-up.
   - Jacob set a goal of 20 offers a day, did not wait for perfect comps or phone contact, let the CRM automate follow-up and re-engagement, and landed 6 contracts in 7 days [Dylan Applegate — MaWsUKnc8CI @ 00:02:04].

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Street-Tested Edges From the Dialer and SMS Floor**
> 
> Small moves the operators use to squeeze more contracts out of the same list and the same spend.
> - Stack lists before you touch them: code violations combined with pre-foreclosure or tax delinquency get the sniper treatment of hand-dials, texts and a 20-second video, while single-list records get the power dialer and text blast [Maximilian Dier — xPy40xTlt64 @ 00:04:41].
> - When a seller claims a higher bid, ask to see the contract or offer to match legitimate recent offers; about 50% of the hosts' deals were not the highest offer, as with 117K on 90 days versus 85K now [Apke Brothers — lWYFLEd7PA0 @ 00:19:10].
> - Watch deliverability: under 13% signals carrier flags, so verify numbers with Number Verifier and triple-dial for 48 hours before dropping to double-dial in two-week blocks [Ajay Sharma — Jb9R7WTh63s @ 00:16:29].
> - Text the sellers but call the buyers, qualifying each buyer by zip code, city, county and subdivision inside the Flip Force CRM [DGB Media — 4KWC7a6vLcY @ 00:39:07].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split is which channel to lead with. Michael Bull ran three separate channel teams before settling on one: “Now, we just do direct mail because I love the simplicity of it” [PMSn1EaZ42Y @ 00:08:28]. Bull prices 1,000 letters at about $650 and pitches mail as a 10-hour-a-week, one-operator lifestyle business running mid-six to low-seven figures. The Apke Brothers reverse the order, “if I were to start over I'd start with texting first,” because texting pulls 10%+ response against 1–1.5% on mail and works as a clear on-off button [Ls2lelB6ImM @ 00:16:11]. They still concede blind-offer mail is easier for beginners, while texting is the higher-skill, transferable channel. Jeff and DeAndre of Land Flipping Empire run fully text-first, pushing about 1,000 texts a day through the Flip Force CRM because “a lot of people respond faster to text,” and they closed a Georgia acre contracted at 20K and sold at 40K in 30 days by text alone [4KWC7a6vLcY @ 00:40:39]. Land Academy rejects both calling and texting, dismissing texting as a “worse version of emailing” that recipients report as spam, and keeps only plain-envelope mail with the exact dollar offer in the first sentence at roughly 1% response [NqKIWNpO1Ic @ 00:12:48].

Texting volume thresholds don't reconcile. Michael Bull says a text operation needs at least 50–80K messages a month, ideally 100K, to carry the fixed cost of a sender, valuations manager, acquisition manager and transaction coordinator, and at about 10K texts per contract for a skilled team, “if you're going to send a 100,000 texts a month, it's only three contracts a month” [PMSn1EaZ42Y @ 00:06:15]. The Apke Brothers say 4–6K texts a month gets a $100K year, and their student Roy closes about one deal per 5,000 texts against roughly one per 20,000 for beginners [Ls2lelB6ImM @ 00:14:06]. Clay Hepler's benchmark is wider still at 30K–250K texts per deal, set beside one deal per 5–8K mail pieces and one deal every six weeks per cold caller [c8ekY72VGy0 @ 00:08:17]. Ajay Sharma describes top texters firing 100–300K messages a month off whole-state pulls with minimal filtering, with one VA handling about 50K a month [Jb9R7WTh63s @ 00:06:51]. Deal size splits the same way: the Apke Brothers say they are “very very Pro bigger deals and less volume,” point to a $400K subdivide closed by brother Mike, and call Roy's roughly $12K average a small margin that could reach $15–20K.

Legal exposure is the sharpest fault line. Zach of Flip With Rick reads Jantz v. Home Run Offer as a green light for unlicensed wholesalers, “Neither definition encompasses a scenario in which the caller offers to buy something from the recipient of the call,” and adds, “I've never been attacked once” [U4t9j_wTBLk @ 00:08:03]. Josh Stevens of McMurray & Shuster, interviewed by REIPrintMail's Jeff Charlton, counters with Anderson v. Catalina Structured Funding, Eagle v. GVG Capital and Pepper v. VGG Capital, where courts treated as-is buying as a service paid for through the purchase-price discount, motions to dismiss were denied, and the cases settled [L4oZCP8R6RQ @ 00:07:26]. Josh says there are “really good arguments” those rulings are wrong, but fighting one is very expensive. Nick, the cold-calling agency guest of the Apke Brothers, takes a third line: “Laws and enforcement are two different things,” with callers using only the client's first name from the agency's own number, producing one one-star review and zero lawsuits in nine years, while the hosts book a $5,000 litigator demand as an operating expense [lWYFLEd7PA0 @ 00:06:06]. Ryan Dossey says about half a dozen of roughly 240 community members got tagged and points to B2B prospecting of LLC owners through registered agents as the clean lane [hzDGK6PCjPg @ 00:06:37].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The litigation trap is real and documented. Ryan Dossey lays out the honey pot: a fake seller feigns motivation, may even allow photos or an inspection, and “what they're waiting for is for you to send them the purchase and sale agreement,” the document that identifies your company [hzDGK6PCjPg @ 00:02:41]. From there the pipeline runs through a ChatGPT demand letter, an OpenCorporates lookup, certified mail and small claims, where four texts at $1,500 each makes $6,000 under an $8,000 ceiling, and ignoring the summons ends in a default judgment against company assets, rentals or the owner personally. REIPrintMail's Jeff Charlton reports a customer who settled for $1M plus hundreds of thousands in legal fees, and Josh Stevens prices defense at $300–1,000 an hour, calling $500K in fees ruinous for a shop clearing $200–300K a year [L4oZCP8R6RQ @ 00:08:18]. Michael Bull says courts treated paying closing costs or a quick close as services, that “99.9%” of operators skip DNC scrubbing, litigator scrubbing and safe harbor, and concludes, “to me, that risk wasn't worth it” [PMSn1EaZ42Y @ 00:07:54]. Land Academy adds that class actions over these channels are spreading, and its hosts treat mail as the one channel that works [NqKIWNpO1Ic @ 00:14:04].

The economics checks are blunt. Maximilian Dier says, “If you have thousand bucks a month, you shouldn't be cold calling,” adds that a VA at about $800 a month produces only one to two good leads a day, puts 20 good leads at one close worth roughly $5K, and walked away from a Conway seller demanding $250K per lot on a back-end split [uQ2jDRUrtoY @ 00:33:47]. Land Academy pegs an eight-hour calling day at maybe one warm lead every two weeks, or roughly two deals a year, and one host quit cold calling doctors' offices to sell gloves in under two weeks [NqKIWNpO1Ic @ 00:07:15]. The Apke Brothers warn that without qualifying and disqualifying people and land, copy, A/B testing, follow-up and a clear lead definition, a new texter should expect two to three months with no deals while the roughly $1,400 monthly stack keeps billing [Ls2lelB6ImM @ 00:14:49]. Ajay Sharma's dashboards catch the slow leaks: deliverability under 13% means carriers are flagging numbers, burnt lists and an 8-pushed, 5-connected, 0-booked gap mark where the pipeline stalls, and a $100K take-home needs $200–250K gross profit, which means underwriting $30–40K of deals a month [Jb9R7WTh63s @ 00:16:29].

Time and trust are the other bottlenecks. One of the Land Flipping Empire founders admits, “it took me 5 months to get my first deal which it took too long,” and Jeff spent four to five months on houses with no results before moving to land [4KWC7a6vLcY @ 00:47:32]. Dylan Applegate cold called government lists for about two months with zero success before an SMS system on a housing list produced an $11,000 first deal [Hyk7GilIJ8g @ 00:01:48]. Dylan's diagnosis of students who fall behind is that everyone has the same tools, but leads stall in the qualify stage instead of reaching “offer given”: “This is not a game of perfection” [MaWsUKnc8CI @ 00:06:33]. Anthony Weiler names the trust crisis that sinks lowball-only blasting: sellers get the same envelopes several times a month, promised one-to-three-week closes stretch to 30 days or 90 on a double close, and offers below 40–50% of perceived value stop working once sellers see a lot “bought for 30,000, listed for 90,000” [4vZN9zHC26c @ 00:02:29]. Weiler's fix is solving easement, clearing or minor-subdivision problems so the offer can go higher, then farming sellers with direct-mail drips, QR-code interest checks and retargeting.
