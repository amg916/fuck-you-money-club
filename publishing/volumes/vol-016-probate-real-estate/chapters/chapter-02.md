## 1. Identify the estate's signer, economic payer, and urgent cash problem  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate the person requesting help, the person authorized to contract, and the party providing settlement money. A beneficiary may introduce the property without possessing authority to list it. The personal representative ordinarily engages the brokerage for the estate, subject to the appointment, governing law, and any court restrictions. The buyer supplies purchase funds, sometimes through a mortgage lender, but those funds do not become brokerage revenue merely because they pass through escrow. Obtain current letters, the appointment order, the recorded vesting deed, and identification for the actual signer. Ask estate counsel to resolve mismatches between the decedent's name, trust ownership, joint ownership, and the probate inventory before promising a closing date. California Probate Code section 10501 distinguishes powers requiring court supervision, including real-property transactions under limited authority. Full authority also requires checking applicable notice procedures and objections. Build an authority record showing who signs the listing, purchase contract, escrow amendments, and disbursement instructions. Family consensus helps execution, but it does not replace documented signing authority. A good intake question is: which document empowers this specific person to sell this specific parcel today? Keep the answer attached to the transaction record rather than buried in an agent's messages.

The executor purchases reduced uncertainty, preserved estate value, and fewer hours spent coordinating unfamiliar work. Their acute pain is often carrying cost rather than a statutory obligation to hire any particular broker. A vacant house with a $2,100 mortgage payment, $650 monthly tax accrual, $240 insurance expense, and $310 utilities and yard expense consumes $3,300 monthly before repairs. A four-month delay therefore absorbs $13,200 of estate cash; distinguish tax accrual from cash actually due when presenting the liquidity schedule. A distant representative may value a documented cleanout and vacancy-insurance workflow more than an optimistic asking price. A beneficiary who lives in the house may instead fear displacement, while another beneficiary wants immediate distribution. Interview these constituencies separately without treating each as a client with independent instruction rights. Present two net-proceeds paths using documented repair bids, realistic marketing periods, and identified court dependencies. Do not convert family urgency into invented legal deadlines. Calendar actual notices, hearings, insurance conditions, loan demands, and contract dates. The brokerage earns trust by naming the constraint it can control and the constraint it cannot. Record the representative's selected tradeoff between speed, price, cash expenditure, and execution risk in the listing file.

Use the following eleven-play operating map as a chapter workflow, not as eleven independent revenue streams added to the scorecard. Probate listing, attorney relationships, executor concierge, title cleanup coordination, vacancy stabilization, court-sale preparation, occupied-property coordination, valuation packages, acquisition alternatives, settlement recovery, and public_filing_trigger_mail_arbitrage each answer a different payer problem. The named trigger-mail play requires particular discipline: Secretary of State new-LLC filings and recorder deed or mortgage events are prospecting signals, not proof of a death, probate appointment, or willingness to sell. Deduplicate daily records, validate the relevant business or parcel, and send plainly commercial Lob or PostGrid mail rather than government-lookalike invoices. An illustrative batch of 1,000 pieces at $0.95 costs $950; a 1.5% response produces fifteen inquiries, five qualified conversations, and potentially one signed engagement. At an assumed 50% eventual closing rate and $17,000 retained commission, that engagement represents $8,500 expected commission before labor, overhead, and timing risk. These are test assumptions, not observed conversion data. If relevance or conversion fails, stop the batch and offer genuine property-assessment review or business services through properly qualified providers, with clear private branding and separately measured economics.

## 2. Select the payment rail without confusing escrow custody with collection rights  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat settlement as a controlled waterfall rather than a buyer paying the agent directly. The purchase agreement determines buyer obligations; the listing and other compensation agreements establish brokerage rights; title requirements, court orders, and applicable law constrain the final disbursement. The closing agent prepares a settlement statement showing consideration, prorations, payoffs, permitted expenses, brokerage compensation, and the estate's remaining proceeds. Request a preliminary version early enough to correct the brokerage payee and commission calculation. The estate representative authorizes instructions only within their powers, and escrow must independently satisfy its release conditions. A signed listing alone does not give the broker authority to redirect estate money or skip a required confirmation hearing. For financed residential sales, coordinate the lender's disclosure timetable with the probate calendar rather than assuming either controls the other. Separate court-approved representative compensation from brokerage compensation; statutory administration fees are not a shortcut for paying the agent. If sale proceeds cannot cover required payoffs and agreed expenses, identify the deficit before accepting an unconditional closing promise. Obtain written treatment of any negotiated shortfall from the affected parties. Never treat a hoped-for beneficiary contribution as funds already available for settlement.

Wire and ACH describe transfer mechanisms; escrow describes custody and conditional release. Retainers, statutory disbursements, platform checkout, factoring, and milestone holds describe different commercial arrangements, so compare them without pretending they are interchangeable bank products. A verified wire into a reputable settlement account is common for purchase funds, but an emailed routing change can still divert the entire amount. Call a previously authenticated number, read back account details under the settlement provider's procedure, and use dual review for changes. ACH may fit ordinary vendor invoices or an authorized brokerage payout, subject to bank limits, processing windows, and return exposure. Card checkout can simplify a separately contracted concierge service, but chargebacks and processor terms make it unsuitable as an assumed home-purchase settlement rail. A retainer belongs in the account and ledger treatment required by local law and the agreement; calling it nonrefundable does not settle whether it is earned. In California, apply the relevant broker trust-fund handling rules, including Business and Professions Code section 10145, when a broker receives funds belonging to others. Prefer routing sale proceeds through the properly appointed settlement provider rather than introducing unnecessary broker custody and reconciliation obligations.

Price payment friction in dollars before selecting a workaround. On a $17,000 retained commission, a hypothetical $25 outgoing wire charge consumes about 0.15%; a 2.9% plus $0.30 card charge consumes $493.30 before any dispute reserve. These are comparison assumptions, not quoted provider prices or permission to charge commissions by card. A commission advance that costs $1,000 to receive $16,000 sixty days early consumes 5.88% of the $17,000 receivable and can leave recourse if the sale fails. Read assignment restrictions, required broker consent, cancellation terms, and collection rights before using factoring. Milestone holds work for specified vendor deliverables or contractually supported reserves, but an informal hold on brokerage compensation creates avoidable payment disputes. Identify the release event, evidence, reviewer, deadline, and disagreement procedure in writing. A court-directed payment requires the applicable order or statutory authority, not a salesperson's assertion that probate automatically protects every invoice. If a hearing delays closing ninety days, a $6,000 monthly brokerage burn requires $18,000 additional operating liquidity. That cash belongs in the operator's runway plan, never in a unilateral withdrawal from estate escrow or an unsupported advance against the representative's expected inheritance.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four facts before committing labor or moving money**
> 
> A promising property is not a collectible engagement until authority, compensation, settlement readiness, and payment identity have been verified.
> - Confirm current representative authority, parcel ownership, and the applicable notice or court-confirmation path with supporting documents.
> - Obtain signed negotiated compensation terms, brokerage supervision, permitted expenses, and any separately priced concierge scope.
> - Review preliminary title, payoff estimates, estate liquidity, and lender or buyer funding evidence for the proposed timetable.
> - Authenticate the settlement provider and beneficiary account independently; route instruction changes through documented dual review.

## 3. Protect earned fees and screen counterparties before extending credit  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Fee protection begins with a valid engagement, not an aggressive invoice after the family changes its mind. Specify the licensed brokerage, authorized estate signer, property, listing term, negotiated compensation method, and conditions under which compensation is earned and payable. Address cancellation, permitted marketing expenses, buyer exclusions, and any protection period using state-appropriate forms and brokerage review. Document introduced buyers and deliver any required protection list on time; a vague claim that the broker found the opportunity is weak collection evidence. Distinguish an earned contractual claim from permission to disburse that claim through escrow. Neither a protection clause nor an unpaid concierge invoice automatically creates a lien on the home. California Probate Code section 10313 provides a court role in fixing brokerage compensation for sales within its scope, so build the applicable court process into the expectation rather than presenting every listing percentage as immune from review. Request written commission instructions through the supervising brokerage and settlement provider. If a family attempts to replace the broker midstream, preserve the signed agreement and performance record, then use its dispute procedure without blocking access to estate records or threatening unsupported title encumbrances.

Screen the estate, buyer, settlement provider, and vendors as different credit risks. The estate may own an expensive property while lacking cash for a $2,500 cleanout; equity and available operating cash are separate facts. Ask the representative for an authorized expense budget and confirm who pays each vendor if the listing expires without a sale. Review the preliminary title report and obtain dated payoff figures rather than estimating debt from the original mortgage recording. For a cash buyer, request current proof of funds through the agreed verification process and identify whether the purchaser is an individual, entity, or contract assignee. For a financed buyer, review preapproval, material contingencies, and lender readiness without collecting unnecessary sensitive records. Verify the escrow or closing provider through the relevant regulator, known professional channels, and independently sourced contact information. Collect vendor insurance evidence and written scopes where the work warrants them. A contractor proposing payment only at closing is extending credit to someone; identify that debtor explicitly. Do not let a broker's casual scheduling message become an unintended personal payment promise. If verification remains incomplete, reduce the commitment instead of increasing pressure for a faster signature.

Attorney relationships should compound service quality, not create hidden settlement payments. For transactions involving federally related mortgage loans, 12 U.S.C. section 2607 restricts kickbacks for settlement-service referrals and fee splitting for unperformed services. State licensing restrictions also matter; California Business and Professions Code section 10137 restricts compensation to unlicensed persons for acts requiring a real-estate license. Pay for genuine, documented services only when the arrangement is permissible; a marketing label does not change the underlying conduct. Apply the same separation to acquisitions. If the broker or an affiliate wants to purchase the estate property, disclose the principal role and conflict, obtain required consents, and support the representative's comparison with independent market evidence. An acquisition price or later resale spread does not belong in this chapter's retained-commission scorecard. For trigger-mail offers, 39 U.S.C. section 3001 addresses deceptive mailed solicitations, including invoice-like and government-affiliation presentations within its provisions. Use private commercial branding and check applicable state solicitation rules. Genuine compliance-update subscriptions, qualified tax-assessment appeals, or B2B lead services need their own customer agreements, delivery evidence, and accounting. Never sell an attorney introduction as an exclusive mortgage-settlement lead with a disguised referral payment.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open the authority and credit file
   - Collect appointment documents, vesting evidence, preliminary title, and the expense budget.
   - Record unresolved restrictions and assign each issue to the representative, counsel, title provider, or supervising broker.
2. Execute the compensation and service documents
   - Obtain authorized signatures on negotiated brokerage terms and any separate vendor or concierge scopes.
   - State who owes each amount, when it becomes payable, and what happens if the property does not close.
3. Validate settlement readiness and payment identity
   - Review the draft statement, required court evidence, payoffs, buyer funding, and verified disbursement instructions.
   - Resolve mismatched payees and routing changes through independent callbacks before money is transmitted.
4. Reconcile collection and retain the evidence
   - Match final compensation, brokerage split, bank receipt, and transaction-level accounting.
   - Investigate short payments promptly and retain authority, compensation, settlement, and authentication records under the applicable retention schedule.

## 4. Reconcile retained commissions, operating costs, and delayed cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Reconcile the canonical scenarios on retained brokerage commissions after splits, not on home values or gross commissions before the brokerage allocation. An illustrative Conservative construction is eight closings at $750,000 each, a separately negotiated 2.5% commission payable to the relevant brokerage side, and an 80% operator retention after the brokerage split. Each closing contributes $15,000 retained revenue, producing exactly $120,000 annually. The Base construction uses thirty $800,000 closings at an illustrative negotiated 2.5% and 85% retention: $20,000 before the split becomes $17,000 retained, totaling $510,000. Scaled uses forty-five $1,000,000 closings at an illustrative negotiated 2.5% and 80% retention: $25,000 becomes $20,000 retained, totaling $900,000. These assumptions illustrate the supplied totals; they establish neither prevailing compensation nor a required rate. Underlying property consideration is $6,000,000, $24,000,000, and $45,000,000 respectively, none of which is operator revenue. Calculate any buyer-broker payment or referral allocation under the actual agreement before applying the operator's retained-revenue definition. Do not subtract the same brokerage split again as an operating expense after reporting revenue net of that split. Keep alternative commission structures in separate sensitivity models rather than silently changing the canonical cases.

The corresponding annual operating cost envelopes are $96,000 Conservative, $369,750 Base, and $585,000 Scaled. Subtracting those amounts from retained revenue produces $24,000, $140,250, and $315,000 owner net profit, or 20%, 27.5%, and 35%. Preserve the specified teams of 2 FT plus 3 C, 2 FT plus 3 C, and 3 FT plus 4 C; Scaled therefore involves seven people. Contractor headcount does not establish full-time contractor utilization, and the scorecard does not supply a wage schedule or treatment of owner salary. Build those details explicitly before hiring against these totals. The stated $3,000 to $18,000 monthly operating range should not be treated as an all-in ceiling that overrides the annual scenario expenses: Base implies $30,812.50 monthly all-in cost and Scaled $48,750. Separate recurring infrastructure from payroll, transaction delivery, marketing, insurance, and other modeled costs. The $15,000 to $100,000 startup range likewise does not automatically fund a four-to-eighteen-month path to the first deal. At $6,000 monthly cash burn, eighteen months consumes $108,000 before setup spending. Resolve that shortfall through a smaller launch, additional capital, or existing income rather than assuming earlier probate distributions.

Run a weekly collection forecast by transaction rather than recognizing a probability-weighted pipeline as earned revenue. Record expected retained commission, authority status, title status, financing status, next court event, expected settlement date, and cash actually received. A $17,000 Base closing delayed from December into February shifts cash across years even if the listing remains valuable; accounting recognition must follow the actual earning terms and chosen accounting method. Use docket alerts to surface hearing changes, then have a person verify the controlling entry before changing commitments. Use transaction checklists to prevent missing notices, stale payoff statements, and unsigned amendments, not to manufacture legal conclusions from incomplete documents. Reconcile the final statement to the supervising brokerage's commission detail and the bank receipt, with a separate entry for genuine transfer charges. Review exceptions for underpaid compensation, duplicate vendor deductions, incorrect split percentages, and misdirected deposits. Track time from appointment verification to listing, contract to confirmation where applicable, and closing to collected commission. These intervals reveal whether the actual bottleneck is lead quality, family conflict, court capacity, lender readiness, or settlement administration. Spend automation money on the measured bottleneck and preserve human review where authority or money movement changes.
