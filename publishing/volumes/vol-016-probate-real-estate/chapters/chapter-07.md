## Executive Summary

Probate Mastery founder Chad Corbett, LA agent Bill Gross, and North Carolina operator Bruce Hill converge on a two-prong probate business: direct outreach to families plus a referral network of attorneys and senior-care professionals, built on a value-first, multi-option offer instead of a one-sided lowball. Street numbers are specific: Chad held a 6% close rate on cold probate lists, Bruce pays $32 per lead and runs ISAs, two mailhouses and a VA scrub, and Chad's campaign calculator prices a county at $813 a month recurring. Execution runs through a vendor bench, one reply-all email to heirs, and tiered or concierge listing fees. Disagreements center on expected conversion (6% personal versus 2-3% coached), when probate earns full-time focus, and whether the numbers-game mindset belongs anywhere in this niche.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The core architecture every speaker repeats is a two-prong business. Chad Corbett teaches marketing directly to families in probate alongside a referral network built as a "long-term sustainable source of business" [_T085ecA9bI @ 00:00:18], and gives investors the same split: inbound marketing to families plus referrals from estate planning attorneys, RIAs and CPAs [cbWbIqZ3KnU @ 00:19:57]. The second consensus point is the offer. Chad's turn came from asking how "nine out of every 10 motivated sellers" could all be junk leads, then resolving to "monetize every real estate conversation" with lease options, subject-to, wraps, owner financing, contract for deed, land contracts and short sales [3VpuIk5nId0 @ 00:09:21]. The label matters too. Chad avoids "realtor" and "investor" in favor of "real estate professional," because blurring that line is what lets an operator monetize every deal, and any seller told "I can't help," "you need to wait" or "you should rent it" means the operator "came up short on skill set" [d3g2CkHYVp4 @ 00:06:18]. In practice that means every conversation ends with a path forward for the family, whether that path is cash, a listing, creative terms or a referral out, and the operator who can present all of them never has to walk away from a motivated estate empty-handed.

Speakers agree probate is a slow-compounding channel, not a switch. Bruce Hill's line is that probate becomes the main focus only once the business runs 5 to 6 probate deals a month; even at $30,000 per deal, 1 to 2 deals a month is "too risky," and leaning on a single source can mean a "dry year" [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:02:34]. Bruce learned that the hard way after launching Zillow, homes.com, realtor.com, radio, a billboard and farming all at once in North Carolina, going nearly broke inside six months, and crediting probate with saving the business; the lesson is to "build slowly" and "perfect one thing before you add another" [avr5LcVkuhw @ 00:05:57]. Chad Corbett maps the expected curve: months 1 through 3 are slow, month 3 brings overwhelm with personal property as the hardest piece, and at 6 to 8 months inbound business equals outbound, off roughly 2% response per quarter and 6% cumulative over three months [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:06:45]. Consistency is the fuel through that curve. Corey, a Probate Mastery student with five listings going active in one week worth about $4.2 million at 4% commission, credits calling multiple times, voicemails included, and says "hands down calling is the best way to get in" because few competitors will pick up the phone [0oMatPnporI @ 00:03:29].

The third consensus pillar is a bench of partners instead of payroll. Chad Corbett's strategy video lists the roster: a savvy attorney or title company to clear messy title and "write scary letters," cash investors found by asking title companies who buys 10 homes a year and by working trustee sales and courthouse-steps auctions, an estate sale company, a $400 flat-fee cleanout crew, a Class A licensed contractor, plus stagers and photographers, all tracked in a simple Excel sheet with name, email, phone and notes [jgstz7zruMg @ 00:02:14]. The same logic covers data: Chad says "if you're a six figure earner you have no damn business being a court researcher," and recommends education and a clear offer before buying leads at all [sy23pOWuMEU @ 00:24:55]. Reputation is the payoff. Chad describes a student who listed one house, got referred across the street to a neighbor in an old probate that was never on the list, took 14 listings that day and made about half a million dollars as "the problem solver" [WAsEf6usAg0 @ 00:02:00]. Bill Gross confirms the spillover from the other side: half of the referrals that reach Bill are not probate at all; people simply know Bill as the expert in solving real estate problems [WAsEf6usAg0 @ 00:00:36].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Bruce Hill runs the most fully costed lead machine. Bruce spends $32 for every lead, against a market where "most people are between $10 and $20 a lead," and in Fayetteville, with about 90 filings a month, that works out to roughly $2,800 a month at a 4 to 5% conversion [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:47:04]. Scrubbing cuts 100 raw leads to 65 to 70 that actually hold real estate; a VA strips apartments and properties the decedent did not own and builds a 20-person call list each day, with PropStream at $200 [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:57:02]. Bruce pays for consistency instead of relying on willpower: list builders, ISAs, two mailhouses and follow-up agents, with ISAs earning $3,000 against a $1,000 local norm because "I bonus them high," marketing packages at $40 to $50 each, cold mail to 25 to 30 of every 100 leads four to five times starting at month 3, and an all-in cost near $1,000 per deal [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:49:10]. Chad Corbett leans on the same data tool even harder: Chad calls PropStream the best data, with up to 10,000 exports a month, and uses its ARV as the top-line underwriting tool for private mortgages in Virginia, West Virginia and Florida, while disclosing that a friend founded it [cbWbIqZ3KnU @ 00:22:15].

Chad Corbett's campaign calculator gives the cleanest per-county budget: 89 leads a county, $362 for leads, three letters over three months at $401 in mail, and a $50 website, so $813 a month recurring plus $700 one-time; over three months that buys 267 leads for about $3,000 total [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:51:47]. At a 1% investor conversion that is 2.7 deals and $36,000 net, about 1,200% ROI; at a 2% agent conversion it is about 8 deals and $83,000, over 2,000% ROI, with Chad noting California commissions are capped at 2.5%. Chad's own run: $1,100 a month on an All The Leads list, mail and ringless voicemail, against a $117,000 median price, produced a $21,000 average gross commission at roughly 6% conversion, and Seth turned the same $1,100 into $120,000 a month [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:48:09]. Scaled across a brokerage, that probate-only revenue stream took Chad from 52 closings in year one to 72 in year two and about 105 in year three with one admin, while marketing spend fell from $8,000 a month to under $1,100 [sy23pOWuMEU @ 00:12:43]. Bill Gross structures the time side: business development from 8 a.m. to noon daily, with any lost morning hour replaced in the afternoon [6dyFJnKpQDI @ 00:13:21].

The call itself is scripted around questions, not pitches. Bruce Hill opens with "probate and estate administration support," asks whether the family wants an easy sale or to maximize value, then pins timing to "next couple of weeks" or "a month or two," and re-calls monthly after that [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:40:19]. In the coaching call, Bruce asks "do you have everything handled," sends resources without ever asking to follow up, transitions with "can I ask one more question," frames every question as X-or-Y, treats "too early to tell" as the mark of a likely seller, and bans "oh by the way I almost forgot" [avr5LcVkuhw @ 00:57:25]. Chad Corbett's rule is that "if you don't get it papered on the first phone call you're not getting the paper," which is why Session 3 spends about six hours on the call and appointment [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:35:22]. Once papered, one email goes to every heir, cc'ing the attorney and each vendor, from the Tuesday 2 to 3 p.m. cleanout to Molly Maid, the stager, the photographer and twilight video, with everyone told to "reply all"; five minutes of email drives three months of execution [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:18:36]. Firing non-performers is the backstop, though Chad says it has never been necessary.

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is what a cold probate list should actually return. Chad Corbett held about a six percent close rate on a cold probate list, describing 100 leads turning into six deals per quarter by running a consultation while competitors pitched 'we buy houses' [cbWbIqZ3KnU @ 00:04:48]. When Chad coaches, though, the expectation drops to a 2-3% run rate per 100 cold leads by month three, with cash usually landing inside 90 days, and Chad calls it the shortest cash conversion cycle of any list Chad has marketed [sy23pOWuMEU @ 00:57:26]. Bruce Hill models Fayetteville's roughly 90 monthly filings at 4-5% conversion and openly spends $32 for every lead, while noting most people sit between $10 and $20 a lead [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:47:46]. Chad's own calculator defaults land near $813 a month recurring for leads, three letters over three months, mail and a $50 website, and Chad personally ran the campaign on $1,100 a month [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:51:47]. The gap is not data quality alone: Bruce's premium funds heavily bonused ISAs earning $3,000 against a $1,000 local norm, list builders, two mailhouses and follow-up agents. Plan the business on the coaching number, then let Bruce's premium model or Chad's lean model prove itself against your own conversion data.

The second fault line is how many hats an operator wears and how hard the offer leans. Chad Corbett avoids the words realtor and investor, preferring 'real estate professional' because blurring that line means every deal can be monetized, and says anyone who has told a seller 'I can't help' came up short on skill set [d3g2CkHYVp4 @ 00:06:18]. Chad also states that every investor should hold a license or leave hundreds of thousands, if not millions, on the table [NH2VGynt0Wk @ 00:04:30]. Bruce Hill agrees on licensing but allows a partner-with-a-lister route, and will pay $1,000 for a solid appointment from an unlicensed source; on offers, Bruce calls a $300k bid on a $500k house presented as the only option 'unethical,' insisting on a cash offer, a listing or a rental laid side by side [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:10:07]. Where Bruce parts with the do-everything mindset is branding: taking $500 rental placements, coaching and property management confused an audience that saw every message, so Bruce now tells operators to build slowly and perfect one thing before adding another [avr5LcVkuhw @ 00:05:57]. Bruce still runs a full vendor roster behind the brand, and argues that presenting a probate and estate specialty never costs other business.

Referrals and pricing produce the third set of contrasts. Chad Corbett admits that out of thousands of certified CPEs only a few dozen have built referral networks producing as much or more business than their marketing, which is why a separate attorney-referral course now exists [_T085ecA9bI @ 00:00:18]. Bill Gross sits at the other end, crediting about $1.3 million to a single attorney referral, and Chad's rule is to walk in the door with value rather than asking for referrals, for example offering to pay for an hour of trust planning for a contact brought to an attorney [sy23pOWuMEU @ 00:44:46]. Bruce Hill finds repeated 'send me referrals' asks annoying and instead brings coffee or donuts 'in lieu of a referral' while slipping partner brochures into the 200 monthly probate letters [avr5LcVkuhw @ 00:41:38]. On fees, the camps diverge sharply: Bruce has priced a concierge listing at 4.5% instead of 2.5% while other agents add a flat $1,500, Bruce's wife offers one option plus a 1% concierge upcharge, and others charge $1,000, $2,000 or $5,000; Corey put $4.2 million of estates live, every one at four percent commission [0oMatPnporI @ 00:01:14]. Chad reports never having to negotiate commission at all.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure reported is building too much, too fast. After moving to North Carolina, Bruce Hill launched Zillow, homes.com, realtor.com, radio, a billboard and farming to tens of thousands at once, was nearly broke within about six months, and says probate 'saved my skin' [avr5LcVkuhw @ 00:05:37]. Bruce also warns that relying on any single source invites a dry year, and that even at $30,000 a deal, one or two probate deals a month is too risky a base for going all-in [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:03:26]. David Pannell's path shows the cost of the wrong mindset: eight months at zero closings while carrying a six-person ISA team, a $10,000-a-month lease, roughly fifteen staff and seven-day weeks, before a value-first shift took the business to seven figures on two or three days a week [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:04:40]. Chad Corbett's own timeline for new operators is blunt: months one through three are slow, month three brings overwhelm with personal property as the hardest piece, and inbound only matches outbound around months six to eight; even Chad's record week of seven probate listings followed a two-month dry spell [0oMatPnporI @ 00:02:11]. Cash runway has to cover that curve before the referral flywheel turns.

Execution failures cluster around confidence and the first conversation. Chad Corbett says coached agents who jumped in haphazardly usually failed, showing up with 'marble mouth' because they lacked the knowledge, the team or the why [jgstz7zruMg @ 00:05:32]. A get-rich-quick mindset makes an operator sound rushed and turns every call into an uphill battle, and Chad is explicit that if the deal is not papered on the first phone call, the paper is not coming; Session 3 spends about six hours on that call and appointment [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:02:15]. Data purchases fail without that groundwork: at All the Leads roughly 3 of every 100 buyers got results and churn was high, while customers who took the course first stayed four times longer, and Chad insists a six-figure earner has no business doing courthouse research personally [sy23pOWuMEU @ 00:20:11]. On the phone, Matthew Price reports that robocall-style openers such as 'Fred from Keller Williams' always get a no, while Bruce Hill bans 'oh by the way I almost forgot' pivots and never asks permission to follow up, sending resources instead [avr5LcVkuhw @ 00:50:52]. The fix every one of them lands on is the same: lead with solutions, not a pitch.

Deal-level traps surface late and kill margin. In California, the court's inventory-and-appraisal rule bars a sale below 90% of appraised value; Bill Gross got a judge to approve roughly 85% only after black mold surfaced, and warns that filing for probate does not stop a foreclosure because lenders run the clock [6dyFJnKpQDI @ 00:41:22]. Chad Corbett's Roanoke listings averaged eight days on market by pricing 15% under and holding offers until day eight, except a smoke-damaged condo that sat 81 days after Servpro quoted $18,000, finally solved with a seller holdback in escrow [4hp5f-_boUM @ 00:27:23]. Financing breaks deals too: Chad's chosen Navy-veteran couple failed conventional financing over an unpaid $80 utility bill, and VA appraisals fail on a wood stove with no secondary heat or a well casing within ten feet of the line, which pushes the fix to a community-bank portfolio loan kept off the secondary market [3VpuIk5nId0 @ 01:00:56]. Bruce Hill's economics check is discipline over tinkering: the current system is the 19th iteration, five or six versions failed, and Bruce waits about six months of data before adjusting, budgeting roughly $1,000 per deal at an 8-12% ROI [7FoxQuVXm2k @ 00:55:15].
