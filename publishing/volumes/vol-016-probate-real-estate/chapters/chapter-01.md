## 1. Define the Estate Assignment Before Selling the Property  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Probate real estate is property whose transfer or disposition intersects with administration of a deceased owner's estate. The commercial opportunity is the work required to establish who can act, prepare a marketable property, find a buyer, and complete a valid transfer. A death does not automatically place every associated parcel inside a supervised probate sale. Trust ownership, survivorship arrangements, transfer instruments, and local succession procedures can produce different routes. Start with the vesting deed, death record where available, court file, appointment documents, and title company's requirements; do not classify a lead from an obituary alone. The client is ordinarily the properly authorized representative acting for the estate, rather than whichever relative answers first. Counsel determines disputed authority and legal strategy; the broker manages pricing, marketing, negotiation, and transaction execution within the permitted scope. The representative may owe duties to multiple beneficiaries and creditors whose preferences conflict. That makes documented alternatives, expense controls, and neutral reporting commercially valuable. A small team wins by reducing uncertainty for the representative and estate counsel, not by promising privileged court access or assuming that bereavement creates an automatic discount. Treat every property as an authority-and-title problem before treating it as saleable inventory.

The scorecard business is a licensed brokerage service, with revenue measured as retained commissions after brokerage splits. A $750,000 sale at a negotiated 2.5% side commission produces $18,750 before an illustrative 20% brokerage split and $15,000 retained afterward. The $750,000 belongs in underlying transaction volume, not operator revenue. Rates are negotiable, and neither that rate nor that split is an industry requirement. Record revenue when the contracted brokerage service is earned and collection is supportable under the applicable accounting policy, commonly at a completed closing; a signed listing and an accepted offer remain pipeline. Reconcile the settlement statement to the commission disbursement authorization and brokerage remittance. Keep earnest money, estate repair funds, sale proceeds, and vendor reimbursements outside the retained-fee ledger unless a separately analyzed arrangement establishes an actual revenue component. An executor concierge may organize access, cleanout bids, utilities, and status reports, but must identify its contracting party, spending authority, and compensation. Direct acquisitions are a different capital business: purchase principal is an investment, sale proceeds are not automatically profit, and holding costs matter. Never blend acquisition gains or concierge invoices into these commission-only scenarios to make a weak brokerage funnel appear productive.

Authority varies materially by state and by the powers actually granted in the case. California illustrates the distinction: Probate Code section 10538 permits certain real property sales under full independent-administration authority without ordinary court supervision, while sections 10580 and following establish notice-of-proposed-action requirements and exceptions. A supervised sale follows a different path, including applicable confirmation procedures under sections 10300 and following. Do not translate one county's checklist into a nationwide rule. Ask estate counsel to identify the authority route, required notices, objection periods, bond issues, and whether particular conflicts require additional court involvement. Give the title officer the same packet before accepting an aggressive closing schedule. Real estate licensing remains a separate requirement; California Business and Professions Code section 10130 generally requires a license to act as a broker or salesperson. The five archetypes below describe different service boundaries, not interchangeable permissions. One firm can coordinate several functions, but an investor buying an estate asset has interests different from a broker maximizing the estate's result. Disclose roles, separate contracts and ledgers, and arrange independent representation where appropriate rather than hiding an acquisition spread inside an executor-service relationship.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify Four Conditions Before Promising a Sale Timeline**
> 
> A commercially attractive parcel is not an executable assignment until authority, title, compensation, and dependencies agree.
> - Match the proposed signer's identity to current appointment documents, limitations, and counsel's written authority assessment.
> - Obtain a preliminary title report and reconcile vesting, liens, deceased owners, co-owners, and required conveyance instruments.
> - Document the listing party, negotiated compensation, brokerage split, and commission recipient before representing expected retained revenue.
> - Put notices, objection periods, court dates, access restrictions, and title conditions into a dated closing dependency list.

## 2. Map Eleven Plays Without Confusing Intelligence With Control  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The eleven chapter-mapped plays comprise two specified cross-volume plays and nine descriptive operating applications shown in the matrix. Start with public_record_api_intelligence: combine permitted county docket access, probate registries, assessor records, and available public APIs into a case-to-parcel research queue. Unclaimed property ledgers can provide research context but do not establish property ownership, heirship, or authority to recover assets. An illustrative monthly batch of 200 records might yield 40 parcel matches, 12 verified representative contacts, three substantive conversations, one listing, and 0.5 eventual closings. At $10,000 retained per closing, that mature cohort implies $5,000 expected retained fees before research and selling costs, not immediately recognized monthly revenue. Spend $600 on data access and ten research hours at $30 each, and direct research cost is $900. Measure conversion by cohort because court delays separate research spending from cash collection. Respect access terms, redactions, sealed records, and contact restrictions. When an endpoint changes or automated access is unavailable, switch to scheduled bulk FOIA/public-record exports where applicable, official feeds, and low-frequency cached queues. State and local disclosure laws usually govern county records; federal FOIA does not compel a county probate clerk to provide an API.

The second specified play, fractional_heir_share_partition_forcing, requires unusually careful separation from the brokerage proposition. Its legitimate operating path is to acquire a documented transferable fractional interest, then pursue consensual buyouts or counsel-supervised partition where legally available. A one-quarter interest in a debt-free property appraised at $400,000 has $100,000 of simple proportional value, but that arithmetic ignores illiquidity, possession, expenses, sale costs, and co-owner rights. Suppose acquisition costs $55,000, counsel and title work cost $8,000, and holding and settlement expenses total $7,000. A voluntary $85,000 buyout leaves $15,000 before taxes and other allocated costs; a $65,000 exit instead loses $5,000. Neither outcome belongs in the commission scorecard. An expectancy before distribution may not convey the same rights as a vested recorded interest. Counsel must verify what the seller owns and what an assignment actually transfers. California's heirs-property partition provisions, including Code of Civil Procedure sections 874.311 and 874.317, illustrate valuation and co-owner buyout protections. Never market litigation pressure as a guaranteed extraction mechanism. The fallback is a voluntary exit renegotiation or holding the interest within a funded reserve, without threatening remedies that the facts or jurisdiction do not permit.

The remaining nine plays monetize execution rather than confidential access: representative verification, attorney relationship development, executor concierge coordination, title triage, vacant-property readiness, occupied-property coordination, court-sale execution, remote-heir communication, and transaction checklist automation. Their unit examples appear in the matrix as workload or cost math; they are not additional scorecard revenue streams. Platforms such as CourtListener, PropertyRadar, ATTOM, Clio, DocuSign, and Qualia solve different problems, and subscriptions never establish coverage, authority, or a right to redistribute records. Test county coverage with a sample before buying annual access. A docket index may omit useful probate detail, while an assessor record can lag a recent transfer. Attorney relationships should grow through accurate comparative market analyses, concise status reporting, and dependable execution, rather than payments for steering estate clients. Where a transaction involves a federally related mortgage loan, 12 U.S.C. section 2607 and its implementing rules restrict settlement-service referral kickbacks and unearned fee splitting. Genuine services need independently supportable scope and compensation; calling a referral payment marketing does not change its substance. For each application, define the work unit, the responsible person, the evidence required, and the fallback when the dependency fails. These controls make eleven plays one coherent brokerage system.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Practices That Experienced Estate Brokers Protect**
> 
> The strongest advantages are quiet controls that prevent expensive surprises for representatives and counsel.
> - Ask the title officer for the conveyance and authority requirements before publishing a closing date; a court appointment alone may not satisfy every exception.

## 3. Reconcile Every Scenario to Retained Commissions and Real Costs  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical Conservative case recognizes $120,000 annual retained brokerage revenue at a 20% net margin, producing $24,000 annual owner net profit with two full-time people and three contractors. An illustrative reconciliation is 12 completed sides at $500,000 each, a negotiated 2.5% side commission, and 80% retention after the brokerage split. That creates $6,000,000 of underlying property volume, $150,000 of pre-split commissions, a $30,000 brokerage split, and $120,000 retained revenue. Implied annual operating costs are $96,000. The Base case uses 34 sides at $750,000, the same illustrative rate and retention, and therefore $25,500,000 property volume, $637,500 pre-split commissions, $127,500 brokerage splits, and $510,000 retained revenue. Its 27.5% margin produces $140,250 owner profit and $369,750 implied operating costs, with the same two full-time people and three contractors. These deal counts explain the supplied totals; they are not forecasts of local demand or published commission norms. Use completed sides rather than loosely defined deals, and avoid counting both buyer and seller compensation unless both were actually earned and permitted. A pending $750,000 property adds no recognized retained fees merely because the representative accepted an offer.

The Scaled case recognizes $900,000 retained revenue at a 35% margin, producing $315,000 annual owner net profit with three full-time people and four contractors, seven people total. Forty-five completed sides at $1,000,000, a negotiated 2.5% side commission, and 80% retention reconcile exactly: $45,000,000 property volume, $1,125,000 pre-split commissions, $225,000 brokerage splits, and $900,000 retained revenue. Implied annual operating costs are $585,000. The supplied startup range is $15,000 to $100,000, the monthly operating-expense screening range is $3,000 to $18,000, and the first-deal window is four to eighteen months. Those screening figures do not reconcile to every scenario as complete cost budgets: implied monthly total costs are $8,000 Conservative, $30,812.50 Base, and $48,750 Scaled. Keep the canonical scenarios intact and explicitly flag that scope mismatch when building a local plan. In particular, $96,000 annually may be insufficient for two full-time people plus contractor support at local compensation levels. Validate whether the owner occupies a full-time seat, which compensation is included, and how contractor costs behave before committing capital. Do not silently remove labor or call property proceeds revenue to preserve an attractive margin. Personal taxes and owner distributions require separate cash planning.

Cash conversion is the critical stress test because a small number of delayed closings can consume a substantial share of annual profit. In the Base illustration, one closing contributes $15,000 retained revenue. If three scheduled closings move into the following year, annual recognized revenue falls by $45,000 unless other closings replace them. Holding the modeled $369,750 cost base constant solely for this sensitivity leaves $95,250 profit on $465,000 revenue; this downside test does not replace the canonical Base case. Separately, if $4,000 of variable cost can actually be avoided for each delayed closing, the same timing event reduces profit by $33,000 instead. Document which costs truly move with completed transactions. Maintain a case-level ledger with appointment status, listing date, offer status, notice deadline, hearing date where relevant, anticipated retained fee, collection evidence, and paid acquisition cost. Measure acquisition cost against collected retained fees, not gross property value. After closing, reconcile rebates, corrected disbursements, contractual refunds, and genuine fee disputes; do not assume every later complaint creates an automatic clawback. Before closing, a canceled transaction is usually lost pipeline rather than reversed earned revenue. Keep those categories separate so marketing and operations learn from the correct failure.

## 4. Build a Small-Team Moat From Verified Exceptions and Repeat Referrals  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A durable moat is a repeatable reduction in the representative's coordination burden that competing brokers cannot reproduce by buying the same lead list. Build it around county-specific authority checklists, title relationships, vendor reliability, and concise reporting to estate counsel. Airtable can hold case identifiers, parcel numbers, authority evidence, and next actions; DocuSign can manage permitted signatures; a brokerage-approved transaction system can preserve the executed file. None of these tools determines legal authority. Assign one human owner to every exception and preserve the source document supporting its resolution. In the two-full-time-person configuration, one seat can own licensed client representation and negotiation while the other owns permitted coordination and records work. Contractor seats might support field access, transaction overflow, and research, subject to local licensing limits. Do not assign negotiation or regulated advice to an unlicensed researcher because a checklist makes the task look administrative. In the seven-person Scaled case, add capacity only after measuring active files, unresolved exceptions, response times, and coordinator hours per closing. Calendar density alone does not prove productive workload. A court continuation may generate five extra touches without adding any retained revenue, so staffing should follow measured service demand rather than headline property volume.

Protect the referral flywheel by making each matter easier for professionals who will handle another estate next month. Offer counsel a supported price range, a preparation budget with authorized alternatives, and a dated dependency report. Ask representatives for communication preferences and distinguish authorized case updates from marketing outreach. Public availability of a phone number does not eliminate calling restrictions or create permission for automated campaigns. Screen outreach against applicable federal and state rules, including relevant requirements under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, 47 U.S.C. section 227, and the Telemarketing Sales Rule where applicable. Use a suppression list and record opt-outs promptly. Never imply that the brokerage is the court, the estate's existing lawyer, or a government recovery office. Store only the personal information needed for the assignment, restrict contractor access, and avoid broadcasting heir disputes through shared vendor folders. Track referral quality by completed assignments and collected fees, not by how many contacts attended a lunch. Eight professional conversations producing two qualified matters and one eventual $15,000 retained closing can outperform hundreds of poorly targeted messages. If the channel stops converting, interview referral partners about service failures before increasing outreach volume or offering compensation for introductions.

The principal failure modes are court delays, family disputes, and housing-market cycles, but each becomes manageable only when translated into an observable condition and a fallback. A delayed hearing requires a revised calendar and cash forecast. Conflicting beneficiary instructions require referral back to the authorized representative and counsel, not an informal vote run by the broker. A stale price requires fresh comparable sales and documented marketing feedback. A buyer's failed loan condition requires requalification or replacement demand, not counting the original offer as completed volume. Review the exception queue weekly and the economics monthly. Compare retained fees per closing, research cost per qualified matter, days between listing and cash collection, and actual contractor hours against assumptions. Keep court-caused waiting separate from preventable operator delay so the team fixes what it controls. Use the four-step procedure below on the next authorized assignment before buying more leads. The objective is not to maximize the number of names in a database; it is to create a defensible chain from public signal to authorized service to collected compensation. Scale only after that chain works repeatedly without concealing labor, relying on pressure against heirs, or confusing the estate's asset value with the operator's earnings.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Convert a public signal into a verified research record
   - Match the case, parcel, and apparent representative using permitted official sources.
   - Record source URLs, retrieval dates, access restrictions, and unresolved identity conflicts; do not infer authority from a matching surname.
2. Establish the authorized assignment and actual sale route
   - Collect appointment evidence, obtain title requirements, and ask estate counsel to identify applicable procedures.
   - Execute the appropriate brokerage agreement with the authorized party and document compensation, role conflicts, notices, and court dependencies.
3. Run preparation and marketing through an exception-owned checklist
   - Assign access, bids, property disclosures, marketing, offers, and required procedural milestones to named owners.
   - Use authorized spending limits and dated evidence; escalate disputed instructions instead of letting a task automation decide substantive rights.
4. Close the property file and reconcile the retained-fee ledger
   - Match the completed transaction to the settlement statement, brokerage split, and collected remittance.
   - Separate property volume from revenue, resolve actual disbursement discrepancies, measure labor and acquisition costs, and send the representative a concise completion report.
