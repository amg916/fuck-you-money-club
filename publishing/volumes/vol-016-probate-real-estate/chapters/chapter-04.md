## 1. Protect the Cash Runway Before Buying a Pipeline  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Allocate startup capital around the time between spending money and receiving a cleared commission, not around an attractive annual revenue target. The three budgets below reserve $9,000 of $15,000, $32,000 of $50,000, and $65,000 of $100,000: respectively 60%, 64%, and 65%. These are unrestricted business reserves, separate from earnest money, estate proceeds, and acquisition deposits. Release the remaining capital against invoices for licensing, insurance, equipment, and measured launch work. A representative can sign a listing before title defects, creditor claims, occupancy problems, or family disputes become visible. That signature creates workload rather than spendable cash. At illustrative monthly fixed burns of $3,000, $9,000, and $18,000, the respective reserves cover only 3.00, 3.56, and 3.61 months. None fully covers even a four-month first-deal interval. Four months requires $12,000, $36,000, or $72,000 before closing-specific costs, leaving shortfalls of $3,000, $4,000, and $7,000. At eighteen months, the corresponding requirements reach $54,000, $162,000, and $324,000. Close those gaps with documented outside income, additional committed capital, or lower actual burn. Do not treat a hoped-for commission, unsigned credit line, or estate reimbursement request as an available reserve.

Reconcile the operating plan to the scorecard before choosing headcount. Conservative revenue of $120,000 less $96,000 of total modeled expenses produces $24,000 owner profit. Base revenue of $510,000 less $369,750 produces $140,250; Scaled revenue of $900,000 less $585,000 produces $315,000. These are retained commissions after brokerage splits, not gross property consideration. One illustrative bridge is fifteen closings at $400,000, a negotiated 2.5% brokerage commission on the represented side, and an 80% retention rate: $8,000 retained per closing and $120,000 annually. Sixty closings at $425,000 under the same assumptions yield $8,500 each and $510,000 annually. Ninety closings at $500,000 yield $10,000 each and $900,000 annually. Commission rates and splits are negotiated assumptions, never prescribed market rates. Annual fixed overhead of $36,000, $108,000, or $216,000 leaves $60,000, $261,750, or $369,000 for all remaining modeled expenses. Assign every expense once, including payroll, contractors, acquisition marketing, travel, and transaction support. The canonical teams remain 2 FT + 3 C, 2 FT + 3 C, and 3 FT + 4 C. Those staffing counts describe operating capacity; they do not establish that local wages fit the budget.

Keep owner distributions subordinate to reserve coverage and signed obligations. If the owner fills one full-time role, identify whether their cash compensation is an expense or the residual profit; never silently count the same dollars twice. Obtain actual wage, payroll-tax, benefit, and contractor quotes before committing to the canonical staffing footprint. The Conservative case can become uneconomic when two full-time market salaries are required from launch. Stage hiring against files that genuinely need coordination, rather than buying capacity because a spreadsheet labels the business high leverage. Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash schedule with opening unrestricted cash, contractual payments, probability-weighted collections, and a separate zero-closing stress case. A transaction coordinator can quote per-file work, but define payment triggers when a sale falls through. Estate cleanouts, locksmiths, utilities, and emergency repairs should normally be contracted and paid by the authorized estate representative. If the brokerage advances any amount, record written authority, a reimbursement ceiling, and the collection risk before disbursement. Principal acquisition requires its own capital model and account structure; a purchase deposit cannot also serve as brokerage payroll runway. Reforecast weekly when a hearing moves, an heir contests authority, or a buyer loses funding.

## 2. Assemble Twelve Infrastructure Layers Without Twelve Paid Contracts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the stack around a case identifier, a responsible operator, and one authoritative transaction record. A probate matter should carry the court case number, county, property parcel, representative contact, authority status, next required action, and source-document link. Keep sensitive attachments inside the broker-approved repository instead of replicating them through every marketing application. The matrix uses named public tiers as procurement planning references, not verified current offers. Confirm each checkout price, renewal term, seat minimum, usage cap, tax, and broker requirement before purchase. For two operators, the illustrated paid configuration is Google Workspace Business Starter at $14 monthly equivalent, Bitwarden Teams at $8, Backblaze Computer Backup at $18, and one Docusign Personal seat at $10. That totals $50 monthly equivalent, or $600 annually, excluding domain registration, hosting, court charges, postal spending, and transaction-system fees. Workspace, Bitwarden, and Docusign prices here assume annual billing; their combined illustrated annual commitment is $384. Cheap software does not make a $3,000 operating budget unrealistic or unnecessary: labor, licensing, insurance, transportation, and marketing dominate the cash requirement. Replace the signature tier when its envelope limit becomes restrictive, rather than quietly relying on unpaid workarounds.

CourtListener is a useful free research layer, but probate is predominantly a state-court workflow and its coverage cannot be assumed. Use the county clerk's official docket and alert service as the authoritative source wherever available. Test coverage with twenty known matters across the target counties, record missed events, and assign a human review cadence where automated alerts are incomplete. An Airtable checklist can schedule follow-up, but it cannot establish that letters remain effective or interpret the legal consequence of a contested petition. Likewise, HubSpot can record relationship history without becoming the storage location for identification documents, death certificates, estate account statements, or unredacted court attachments. Separate public property facts from restricted client documents and grant contractors access only to assigned work. Establish an export routine before importing the first live contact. A free tier that cannot produce usable exports, assign appropriate access, or meet the broker's retention requirements is expensive operational debt. Test one closed-file export containing its signed agreement, communications, authority evidence, deadline history, and commission reconciliation. Searchability and recoverability matter more than the number of automations advertised on a pricing page.

Procure software in stages rather than annually prepaying an imagined team. Start with a shared mailbox convention, a single intake form, manual docket verification, and a weekly exception report. Add automated notifications only after the manual workflow has a named owner and a written response deadline. Protect Workspace and the password vault with strong multifactor authentication; use separate administrator accounts and recovery methods stored outside the primary laptop. Backblaze protects supported endpoints, not every cloud application's records, so schedule separate exports and test restoration of the transaction repository. Prohibit payment-instruction changes solely through email. Call a previously verified number before acting on new escrow wiring details, even when the message appears inside a real transaction thread. Reconcile subscription charges monthly against an owner, a purpose, and an observable usage measure. Remove departed contractors promptly, transfer file ownership, and revoke active sessions where supported. Before purchasing an integration, calculate its actual value: saving two hours monthly at $40 per hour creates $80 of capacity, not permission to buy a $150 connector. Keep the manual procedure available for clerk outages, vendor failures, and expired payment cards. Reliability comes from tested recovery, not a longer list of applications.

## 3. Separate Brokerage Authority, Estate Money, and Acquisition Economics  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Create the entity and bank structure around the licensed activity actually being performed. An LLC registration and an EIN do not authorize real estate brokerage; confirm the individual license, supervising broker relationship, business-name permissions, and any required entity license in the operating jurisdiction. California Business and Professions Code section 10130, for example, prohibits acting as a real estate broker or salesperson without the required license. Maintain a business operating account and a separately identified reserve account. Client money belongs in the permitted escrow or trust arrangement, never in the working-capital pool. California section 10145 and title 10, section 2831 of the California Code of Regulations provide concrete handling and recordkeeping rules for broker trust funds. Assign bookkeeping responsibility, reconciliation frequency, and escalation ownership before receiving any deposit. If the brokerage never holds trust money, document the escrow routing and prohibit staff from accepting checks outside that process. Obtain errors-and-omissions, general liability, cyber, and employment-related coverage appropriate to actual exposures. Review exclusions for principal-owned properties, property management, referral arrangements, and wire fraud. A certificate of insurance establishes evidence of coverage; it does not resolve exclusions or replace policy review.

Verify the representative's present authority instead of treating an obituary, will, or family introduction as permission to sell. Obtain current letters, relevant orders, ownership records, and the listing signer's identity through approved channels. In California, Probate Code section 10501 distinguishes transactions requiring court supervision under limited authority, including real-property sales. Section 10580 addresses notice of proposed action, subject to statutory exceptions. When court confirmation applies, section 10309 addresses the relationship between sale price and appraised value, while section 10311 supplies the initial increased-bid rule. These are jurisdiction-specific workflow inputs, not rules to copy into every state's checklist. Ask the estate's attorney to identify the controlling procedure and deadlines for the actual matter. Record whether occupants, co-owners, surviving spouses, liens, or litigation create additional constraints. Attorney relationships should develop through useful case execution rather than unreviewed payments for referrals. Where a transaction involves a federally related mortgage loan, 12 U.S.C. 2607 restricts settlement-service referral fees and unearned fee splits. A marketing invoice does not automatically convert a referral payment into a permissible service expense. Require a defined service, evidence of performance, and review of both federal and applicable state restrictions.

Underwrite executor concierge work as a distinct service with explicit scope, pricing, and authorization. Coordinating a locksmith, arranging photographs, or obtaining cleanout estimates does not authorize staff to dispose of personal property, negotiate estate debts, or decide which beneficiary receives an asset. Use written work orders identifying who selects the vendor, who contracts, who pays, and who can approve changes. A $600 coordination fee against six hours of work at $45 per hour and $80 of direct costs leaves $250 before overhead. That example explains unit economics; it does not add concierge revenue to the canonical retained-commission scorecard. Treat property acquisitions similarly. A $300,000 purchase and $40,000 rehabilitation budget represent principal deployment and project costs, not brokerage revenue. If the operator becomes a buyer, disclose the capacity change and follow applicable fiduciary, brokerage, and court requirements. Keep acquisition underwriting, deposits, and carrying costs outside the brokerage reserve ledger. Do not use an estate relationship to obtain undisclosed advantages over represented parties. When authority or incentives become disputed, suspend the affected action, preserve the chronology, and route the issue to the supervising broker and estate counsel before spending more cash.

## 4. Test Acquisition Claims and Rehearse the Entire Closing Path  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat identity_graph_pixel_arbitrage as a procurement claim requiring rejection or redesign, not as a ready-made probate acquisition engine. The proposed 35–55% anonymous-visitor identification rate is unverified; neither a first-party CNAME nor a hashed identifier establishes permission to identify people or reuse their information. Do not implement covert matching of anonymous visitors to names, personal emails, profiles, or residential addresses. Probate-related browsing can expose highly sensitive family circumstances. Substitute explicit inquiry forms, permissioned relationship records, contextual advertising, and properly reviewed public-record mailing workflows. For a purely illustrative 1,000-visit cohort, a vendor's claimed match rate would produce 350–550 claimed matches, not 350–550 qualified sellers. A permission-based funnel with a 4% inquiry rate creates forty inquiries; a 25% qualification rate produces ten appointments; a 20% listing conversion produces two listings; and a 50% eventual closing rate produces one closing. At the Base illustration's $8,500 retained commission, that is $8,500 before acquisition spending and delivery costs, potentially many months after the initial visit. Require observed cohort evidence before substituting any of these assumptions into a hiring decision or reserve forecast.

Evaluate the fallback channels with the same discipline. Do not cold-email unconsented consumer Gmail addresses from the primary business domain, and do not evade that restriction through disposable domains. Physical mail is outside CAN-SPAM's commercial-email framework, but that does not exempt it from deceptive-advertising rules or applicable state privacy requirements. A SHA-256 audience identifier also remains linkable information; upload audiences only when the collection, permission, platform terms, and applicable rules support that use. United States edge filtering can reduce irrelevant traffic but does not establish residence, erase state obligations, or supply permission. Test a reviewed mailing cohort independently: 500 letters at an assumed $1.25 fully loaded cost require $625. At a 2% response rate, ten responses cost $62.50 each; two appointments cost $312.50 each. If one listing ultimately closes, the postage-and-production cost per closing is $625, excluding research, labor, follow-up, and failed cohorts. Validate representative addresses rather than automatically mailing the decedent's vacant property. Suppress objections and duplicate households, identify the brokerage plainly, and never imitate a court notice. If no closing occurs, record the full cohort loss instead of assigning value to speculative pipeline.

Before launch, run one synthetic matter from first inquiry through commission reconciliation, using invented test records rather than a real family's documents. Confirm intake permissions, case assignment, authority review, listing preparation, court milestones, escrow routing, and archive export. Then inject four failures: an expired representative appointment, a hearing continued sixty days, changed wire instructions, and a contractor account that must be removed immediately. Every failure needs a named decision owner, an observable stop condition, and a documented recovery path. Recompute cash after the sixty-day delay; at $9,000 monthly fixed burn, the added requirement is $18,000 before transaction-specific spending. That single event consumes more than half the Base launch's $32,000 reserve. Keep launch spending bounded until the team demonstrates that records survive an outage, client money never enters operating accounts, and a listing cannot advance without documented authority. Review actual versus planned spending weekly for the first ninety days. Expand a county, mailing cohort, or contractor assignment only when the existing workflow has measured capacity and funded downside. The practical production stack is the combination of people, records, cash, and verified handoffs; subscription count is merely a purchasing detail.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the capital envelope
   - Transfer the protected reserve and document permitted launch expenditures.
   - Assign each invoice to the seven-row budget, forecast zero closings through the first-deal stress horizon, and identify committed gap funding.
2. Configure the minimum production record
   - Create one case identifier, restricted repository, and named task owner.
   - Record the clerk source, authority evidence, next deadline, document location, contact permissions, and export procedure before adding integrations.
3. Run bounded acquisition experiments
   - Launch a small permission-based inquiry test or reviewed mailing cohort.
   - Capture actual spending and conversion dates; measure retained commission receipts separately from responses, appointments, listings, and property values.
4. Rehearse exceptions and authorize capacity
   - Complete the synthetic transaction and resolve every failed control.
   - Reforecast a sixty-day delay, verify payment instructions independently, restore the archive, remove test contractor access, and release only funded capacity.
