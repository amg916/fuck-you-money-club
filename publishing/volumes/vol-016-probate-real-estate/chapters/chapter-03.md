# Chapter 03: The Inherited House: Executor and Heir Decisions Before the Sale

*First steps after inheriting, executor pre-sale checklists, as-is sales, sibling buyouts, step-up in basis and capital gains, probate value vs. market value, setting the asking price, carrying costs of sitting on the market, and managing California probate sales*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Probate-side agents, tax explainers and cash buyers agree on the order of operations for an inherited house: court letters before any contract, a professional date-of-death appraisal to lock the step-up basis, and immediate protection of a vacant asset that burns $3,000 to $6,000 in six months of heir indecision. The step-up math is the headline: Tricia Garcia took a client's projected $180,000 capital gains bill on an $800,000 home to zero, while Cortes Law Firm warns lifetime gifts and irrevocable trusts forfeit it. Street mechanics include the 90% court-confirmation floor, half-mile model-match comps, $500 to $700 neutral appraisals for sibling buyouts, IAEA full authority with a Notice of Proposed Action, and as-is cash formulas. Practitioners split on exit: Ramsey says sell instantly, Michelle Bell maps financed buyouts, and Duc Minh Lam runs 72-hour bidding windows.

## The Practitioner Consensus

First consensus point: the will is not a key. Maureen at Alix is blunt that even a will reading 'sell the house and split the money' does not let the executor sign a listing agreement or a purchase contract; authority comes only when the probate court issues Letters Testamentary, or Letters of Administration where there is no will [McUc7Xu_MHo @ 00:01:32]. Maureen warns that putting the house under contract before those letters land feels like a time saver but triggers delays once everyone realizes the paperwork was never in place [McUc7Xu_MHo @ 00:02:09]. Kim Ward of Horizon makes confirming authority item one on a ten-point pre-sale checklist, before any decision on the home is made [lo2f0qbkjaQ @ 00:00:55]. The California Probate Real Estate Made Simple agent lists the order as probate attorney first, probate-experienced agent second, court appointment and certified letters third, and only then listing paperwork [32VmI6oEsPQ @ 00:01:53]. Stephanie Burt adds the field observation: families burn weeks, sometimes months, planning around a property before they understand who can actually decide, and the real mistake is not skipping the probate attorney but waiting until a problem forces the call [6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:02:43].

The second consensus point is the step-up in basis, and four operators run the same arithmetic. Alix uses parents who paid about $80,000 in the 1980s for a house worth $500,000 at death: basis resets to $500,000, a $500,000 sale shows $0 gain, and the $420,000 run-up evaporates for tax purposes [McUc7Xu_MHo @ 00:02:43]. The Legacy Desk walks a $100,000 purchase forward to a $400,000 date-of-death value, notes the reset works both ways as a step-up or a step-down, and points out inherited property is treated as held more than a year even if sold within twelve months [qlBYQPngO0o @ 00:03:05]. Tricia Garcia's Ventura County client was told by a CPA to expect roughly $180,000 in capital gains on an $800,000 home; with the step-up the bill went to zero [qNeCeQehzRg @ 00:00:00]. Cortes Law Firm frames the flip side: a parent who hands over a $10,000 house worth $200,000 during life triggers a $190,000 gain, and the 'sold it to me for a dollar' fix fails because the IRS looks at appraised value on the transfer date [oqbFju_2Muc @ 00:01:08]. The shared lesson: inherit at death, then sell.

Third, the house starts bleeding the day it goes vacant, so protection and heir alignment come before any sale decision. Stephanie Burt says keep utilities on with heat between 55 and 60 degrees and a walk-through at least weekly, or fully winterize by draining the lines, because one cold snap can cost a family the equivalent of a full first-floor replacement [6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:00:46]. Burt also flags that most standard homeowner's policies quietly stop covering a property after 30 to 60 days vacant. Kim Ward at Horizon independently warns not to assume coverage exists, noting vacant home insurance can cost up to three times normal insurance but fully protects the estate [lo2f0qbkjaQ @ 00:02:24]. On the people side, Michelle Bell tells an heir who wants to keep the house to tell siblings and the executor as soon as possible, then ask what each sibling actually needs, such as a sister counting on her share for a mortgage renewal [F1mwNdsm-HA @ 00:01:00]. The Ramsey team pushes alignment even earlier: a caller set to inherit a $300,000 Arizona rental was told to have her uncle tell her father now, so she does not 'inherit drama' after the death [eC2q1zU3G2A @ 00:04:14].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Pricing starts with comps, not the court number. Kim Ward's ProbateBiz routine is to pull bedrooms, baths and lot size for the decedent's property, then search a half-mile radius for model matches; on one appointment Ward found three and brought paperwork to back every figure [cDuy_EpieeM @ 00:00:44]. Ward also reports losing a listing to an agent who quoted too high; four months later that house was still on the market, cut down close to Ward's original number [cDuy_EpieeM @ 00:02:49]. Matt McGilvray of RE/MAX New Dimension layers recent comparable sales, condition, the probate appraisal, and live competition and days-on-market, and warns executors not to list at the referee's value because California referees typically never visit the property and work from public records and online tools [fi8ZbbtnVgM @ 00:01:39]. The floor is the 90% rule on court-confirmed sales: an $800,000 referee value means roughly $720,000 minimum, and Ward's $600,000 example floors at $540,000 [fi8ZbbtnVgM @ 00:02:33]. McGilvray's trap: the first few weeks draw the most attention, so an overpriced launch gets ignored and then stigmatized as buyers wonder what is wrong with it. Duc Minh Lam puts the stale threshold at 30 days, after which buyers assume something is fundamentally wrong [dQOLpmObWhY @ 00:01:38].

For the heir who wants to keep the house, the buyout math is simple once value is agreed. Michelle Bell runs a $900,000, mortgage-free house split three ways: each share is $300,000, so the keeper needs $600,000 plus closing costs, not a repurchase of their own third [F1mwNdsm-HA @ 00:03:59]. Add a $150,000 mortgage and equity drops to $750,000, $250,000 each, but financing the $500,000 payout plus retiring the mortgage could mean $650,000 before closing [F1mwNdsm-HA @ 00:04:37]. Bell's sequencing: get the lender's read on how the inherited share is treated before spending weeks negotiating, set a three-week check-in deadline, and put price, closing date, financing conditions and who pays utilities in writing. The Prepared Heir channel prices the independent appraisal at $500 to $700, tells heirs to skip Zillow, and says hire an appraiser with no relationship to anyone, ordering a second neutral appraisal if one heir balks [dtCGp46sZEg @ 00:02:29]. Funding can be cash, a refinance, or an estate loan; then siblings sign a quitclaim deed through a title company or attorney, with notarized documents recorded at the county recorder [dtCGp46sZEg @ 00:04:43]. Deadlock ends in a partition action, which the channel calls very expensive and lengthy.

Three exit routes get taught with real mechanics. In California, the agent behind California Probate Real Estate Made Simple says that once an offer is accepted, the agent asks the probate attorney to prepare a Notice of Proposed Action; if no heir objects within the timeframe, the sale moves without waiting on court approval [32VmI6oEsPQ @ 00:03:48]. The insider play is requesting full authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act, showing the court it serves the estate, versus limited authority that brings confirmation hearings and sometimes a public auction [32VmI6oEsPQ @ 00:07:01]. Calvora Property Group sells the as-is cash route: a two-minute form, a call, an in-person or video walk-through, then a written offer built as fixed-up value minus repairs minus project cost and risk, using a $150,000 house needing roughly $40,000 in work [mT_cRaJJe8A @ 00:03:06]. Duc Minh Lam pitches a third path for contested estates: an upfront inspection audit, a multi-channel blast, proof-of-funds vetting, a 72-hour bidding window against a confidential reserve, and a 14 to 21-day close [1UT8pIVtejc @ 00:04:33]. Lam's cited Magnolia Luxury Estate closed in 14 days at 4.8% over reserve, and the Tran family sold in 16 days with eight verified buyers, $95,000 above reserve [dQOLpmObWhY @ 00:05:34].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock legal authority
   - Hire a probate attorney, get appointed by the court, and hold certified letters before signing any listing or purchase contract.
   - Attorney first, probate-experienced agent second, then appointment and Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration [California Probate Real Estate Made Simple — 32VmI6oEsPQ @ 00:01:53]; contracting before the letters arrive only causes delays later [Alix — McUc7Xu_MHo @ 00:02:09].
2. Protect the vacant asset
   - Keep utilities on or fully winterize, switch to the right vacant policy, rekey every lock and walk the house weekly.
   - Heat at 55 to 60 degrees with weekly walk-throughs, or drain all lines [Sell That Wreck! With Stephanie Burt — 6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:01:16]; vacant home insurance can run up to three times normal cost but fully protects the estate [Horizon Real Estate & Probate and Trust Help — lo2f0qbkjaQ @ 00:02:40].
3. Nail down basis and debts
   - Order the date-of-death appraisal and a title search to surface liens, unpaid taxes, judgments and HOA special assessments.
   - The title search, ordered through the probate attorney, reveals what the house actually owes before heirs plan around inflated numbers [Sell That Wreck! With Stephanie Burt — 6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:03:32]; the appraisal sets the step-up tax benchmark [Alix — McUc7Xu_MHo @ 00:03:23].
4. Price and pick the exit
   - Price from half-mile comps and condition, confirm full or limited authority, then choose listing, buyout or as-is cash.
   - Never list at the referee's value because referees typically do not visit the property, and the 90% floor applies only to court-confirmed sales [Matt McGilvray — fi8ZbbtnVgM @ 00:02:54]; with full IAEA authority, use a Notice of Proposed Action instead of waiting on a judge [California Probate Real Estate Made Simple — 32VmI6oEsPQ @ 00:03:48].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether the house should be kept at all. Dave Ramsey's desk on Real Estate The Ramsey Way tells a caller who will inherit a $300,000 Arizona rental to sell it 'instantly,' arguing that nobody holding $300,000 in cash would go buy a rental in a state they have never visited, and branding the keep-it reflex a 'landlord by default' setup that ends with someone changing their Harley oil in the living room [eC2q1zU3G2A @ 00:05:46]. Tricia Garcia runs the opposite branch for Ventura and LA County families: keep the home when rental income comfortably covers expenses or the place carries real family meaning, and stack the step-up with the $250,000 to $500,000 primary residence exclusion by living there at least two years [qNeCeQehzRg @ 00:08:34]. Michelle Bell treats keeping as a financed buyout, not a feeling: on a $900,000 house split three ways, the keeper needs $600,000 for the siblings, not their own third again, and the inherited share may count as equity depending on the lender [F1mwNdsm-HA @ 00:04:19]. The Prepared Heir lists cash, a refinance, or an estate loan as the funding routes, with the rate driven by whether the house becomes a primary residence, second home, or rental, so the keep decision lives or dies on the loan file [dtCGp46sZEg @ 00:03:54].

Where to anchor the price splits the probate specialists. Matt McGilvray of RE/MAX New Dimension tells executors not to simply list at the probate referee's number, because California referees typically never visit the property and lean on public records, comparable sales, and online valuation tools, so they cannot tell a beautiful remodel from a house with serious condition issues [fi8ZbbtnVgM @ 00:01:39]. Kim Ward of ProbateBiz frames the same referee as the estate's protector: an appraiser trained for the role who values the house as of the date of death and sets the floor, where a $600,000 referee value means no sale under $540,000, though nothing stops the house from closing higher [cDuy_EpieeM @ 00:03:42]. On family buyouts the anchor shifts again. The Prepared Heir wants a licensed appraiser with no relationship to anyone in the deal, at roughly $500 to $700, and a second neutral appraiser if an heir rejects the first report [dtCGp46sZEg @ 00:02:48]. Michelle Bell refuses the split-the-difference shortcut when two opinions land far apart: find out whether they were done at different times, used different comparables, or skipped the actual condition, and value the house as it stands rather than at the fully renovated price a brother has in mind [F1mwNdsm-HA @ 00:03:15].

The exit channel is the third fault line. Calvora Property Group pitches the as-is cash sale for heirs living states away: a two-minute form, a walk-through by video, and a written offer built as fixed-up value minus repairs minus project cost and risk, illustrated with a $150,000 after-repair house needing roughly $40,000 of work [mT_cRaJJe8A @ 00:03:06]. Kim Ward of Horizon Real Estate pushes the other way: get an experienced agent to evaluate the home before any repair, because big improvements sometimes make sense but much of the time they do not, then pick strategic fixes, a basic cleanup, or as-is [lo2f0qbkjaQ @ 00:04:21]. Duc Minh Lam treats the plain MLS listing as a trap and runs a five-phase controlled sale with upfront inspections, proof-of-funds vetting, and a 72-hour bidding window that pushes qualified buyers past a confidential reserve, claiming a Magnolia estate closed in 14 days at 4.8% over reserve [1UT8pIVtejc @ 00:04:33]. Authority splits the playbook too: California Probate Real Estate Made Simple says full authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act lets the sale run almost like a normal one through a Notice of Proposed Action, while limited authority means court confirmation, longer timelines, and sometimes a public auction [32VmI6oEsPQ @ 00:05:25].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failures happen while the family is still deciding. Stephanie Burt of Sell That Wreck has watched heirs shut off utilities to save money, then eat the equivalent of a full first-floor replacement when one cold snap burst the pipes; Burt's fix is a full winterization that drains every line, not dripping faucets, or heat held between 55 and 60 degrees with someone walking the house at least weekly [6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:00:46]. Burt also warns that most standard homeowner's policies quietly stop covering a property after 30 to 60 days vacant, so the call to the carrier has to happen before the loss, not after [6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:01:35]. Kim Ward's Horizon checklist confirms the short coverage window after a death and prices the cure: switching to vacant home insurance can cost up to three times a normal policy, but it completely protects the estate [lo2f0qbkjaQ @ 00:02:40]. Burt's carrying math is the economics check most heir groups skip: six months of a vacant house in family debate burns $3,000 to $6,000 in property taxes, utilities, insurance, and basic maintenance, and then the real damage starts as gutters fill, roof leaks develop, and the HVAC stops being serviced, with every preventable repair coming straight out of what each heir walks away with [6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:04:23].

Paperwork traps are mostly self-inflicted. Maureen at Alix warns executors not to put the house under contract before Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration arrive, even when the will says plainly to sell and split the money, because the shortcut causes delays later when everyone realizes the paperwork was never in place [McUc7Xu_MHo @ 00:02:09]. Burt sees families spend weeks or months planning around a property before knowing who has authority, and says the real mistake is not skipping the probate attorney but waiting until there is a problem; the title search routinely surfaces liens, unpaid taxes, judgments, and the rare but significant HOA special assessment [6xUa7KjeSSY @ 00:03:32]. The Ramsey call shows the same trap upstream: an uncle promised his $8,000 mortgage would be paid from his bank account, but the will sent that checking account to a nephew, leaving the executor-heir holding the debt until the will was reworked, plus a father likely to come asking 'where's my cut' unless the uncle tells him now [eC2q1zU3G2A @ 00:01:48]. California Probate Real Estate Made Simple adds the operator mistakes that make probate homes sit: starting too late, hiring a related agent who does not truly know probate, marketing to the wrong buyers, and trying to DIY the whole process [32VmI6oEsPQ @ 00:06:14].

Pricing and tax errors are where the biggest numbers move. Kim Ward of ProbateBiz lost a listing to an agent who quoted high; four months later that house was still on the market, its price slowly cut back toward Ward's original number, and Ward ties the pattern to agents who overestimate value just to win the listing [cDuy_EpieeM @ 00:02:49]. Matt McGilvray explains the damage: the first few weeks bring the most buyer attention, and a home that sits makes buyers wonder what is wrong with it [fi8ZbbtnVgM @ 00:03:49]. Duc Minh Lam puts dates on the spiral: past 30 days buyers assume a defect, and contingency roulette hits around day 21 when financing falls through, restarting the sale with a scarred market history [dQOLpmObWhY @ 00:02:08]. On tax, Tricia Garcia's Ventura County client was told by a CPA to expect roughly $180,000 owed on a $600,000 gain; with a date-of-death appraisal setting an $800,000 basis, the gain on an $800,000 sale was zero. Two traps survive the step-up: Prop 19 can reassess the parents' low property tax base to market value when no heir moves in, and appreciation after death, such as an $800,000 home sold two years later for $900,000, can be taxed on that extra $100,000 [qNeCeQehzRg @ 00:06:42].

> **OPERATOR GATE — Clear These Four Before Listing, Contracting, or Promising a Sibling Anything**
