## Executive Summary

Probate specialists working the listing side agree on one core truth: estates in probate produce listing inventory that does not care about rates, and the agent who learns the process wins it. Carolynn Castillo reports counties throwing 20 to 40 leads a week and about 70% of probate leads selling; The Aaron Novello Podcast claims 20% of 2025 California sales were probate. The street mechanics are blunt: control the lead at the courthouse or buy it, skip trace the personal representative, call before you mail, and turn put-offs into permission to send resources. Probate Mastery's backward math needs 480 pipeline people for 24 closings, budgets $61 per resistant PR over 6 to 8 months, and warns three touches fail. Practitioners split on courthouse versus vendor data, mail-heavy versus call-forward models, and signing listing agreements before letters issue.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator in this circle sells the same thesis: probate is a listings business driven by a life event, not by the rate cycle. Carolynn Castillo calls the niche listings only and points to one probate listing that pulled over 30 offers in 24 to 48 hours after the house was prepped and put in front of buyers [RR9l10XmnC4 @ 00:06:42]. Castillo also reports being the only probate realtor in the room, a lane far less saturated than FSBOs and expireds where every agent in the office is already fighting [RR9l10XmnC4 @ 00:09:20]. The Aaron Novello Podcast pushes the same point from the macro side: rates moving from 3% to 7% for three years thinned out the four horsemen of expireds, cancels, FSBOs and just listed/just sold, while probate is claimed to be 20% of all 2025 California sales and untied to rates, market conditions or administrations [44RbpWBPcbI @ 00:01:15]. The proof offered is Connor, who took 5 probate listings in one month for $50K in commissions and now regularly takes 3 to 5 [44RbpWBPcbI @ 00:01:41]. The shared baseline is simple: the filings keep arriving every week regardless of the market, and the agent standing closest to the personal representative when the property finally has to move is the one who takes the listing.

The second point of agreement is hard and procedural: nothing moves until the court hands someone authority. Castillo walks the sequence: the attorney files a petition naming the PR, and the family cannot sell yet; only Letters of Administration, called Letters Testamentary in some states, let the PR sign a listing agreement and close a sale [ewbkVPWFtKM @ 00:07:40]. Castillo's first warning is to confirm Letters before listing or eat title-company problems at the closing table [ewbkVPWFtKM @ 00:17:34]. Justin Stivers, a Miami probate attorney working about 17 Florida counties, is blunter: being named in a will is not good enough, the PR must be court-appointed, and no MLS entry, yard sign or door hanger goes out before that appointment, because the realtor risks trouble or a different PR gets appointed and hires someone else [Owbx6t3ocjM @ 00:10:10]. Bruce at Probate Mastery reduces it to one line, 'You have to get your authority from the court,' and stresses that heirs have input but no legal authority [7xCfSyqYEys @ 01:26:47]. The operating consequence is that the person to win is the personal representative, not the loudest heir, and the first job on any lead is confirming who actually holds signing power and whether the letters carry bond requirements or restrictions.

The third consensus is about time. Probate Mastery opens its business-building talk by warning that people quit because they 'didn't get a closing fast enough,' and that one month of work will not produce predictable deals, so expectations have to be reset before the first dial [dzqHqDnAl_E @ 00:00:00]. The same channel calls out the industry norm directly: the average agent or investor in the niche touches a lead three times, and 'That doesn't do it,' so its own marketing and follow-up runs six to eight months on every pipeline lead, defined narrowly as someone already spoken with who is believed likely to sell [dzqHqDnAl_E @ 00:06:02]. The All The Leads mastermind backs the timing with the decision curve: many families do not decide until month five, six or seven, and dropping out on month two with mail gives them months to forget who you are [9tTK_fAGgNE @ 00:20:59]. Probate Mastery's five-model breakdown closes the loop with a blunt filter: if you need a deal next month, probate may not suit you, so plan a 6-month runway before judging results [Xt7XgZo03Nw @ 00:37:36]. Every serious operator here builds the business as a long pipeline of nurtured personal representatives, never as a one-call close on a grieving family.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Lead acquisition splits into two routes, and the operators price both. The Aaron Novello Podcast's courthouse rule is 'whoever controls the lead controls how much it costs': pulling filings yourself fixes your cost, gets fresh data instead of the monthly vendor drop that investors 'beat it up with mailings,' and includes beneficiaries, which won listings through siblings when the executor was unreachable [H-CW9uu5s-I @ 00:00:43]. At 60 transactions a year that operation had three people at three courthouses, documented the pull as a Loom-video SOP, paid a helper $2 per lead, ran single contact lookups through forewarn and loaded every record into Follow Up Boss [H-CW9uu5s-I @ 00:04:02]. Castillo buys instead: a prior vendor sent about 100 leads, many with no real estate, yielding only 1 to 2 listings a month, while Foreclosures Daily sends about 25 leads a week per county, all with real estate, a feed Castillo has run for 2.5 years [XN-9ankgVNk @ 00:13:05]. Probate Mastery prices purchased data at $400 to $500 for about 150 records and warns that a single mailer to that list wastes money unless it is hyper-aggressive and real-estate focused [Xt7XgZo03Nw @ 00:11:13]. The vendor trade-off named on the courthouse side is no price control, often monthly delivery and frequently no beneficiaries.

Calling is the spine of the system. Castillo runs CallTools as the dialer, ClearSkip to skip trace PRs and relatives, and KV Core through eXp for follow-up across four counties, and calls the PR rather than the attorney [XN-9ankgVNk @ 00:33:09]. The opener is direct, 'calling regarding your pending probate case... considering selling the property,' and the goal is a booked consultation rather than a first-call close, with both a cash offer and a listing option put on the table [XN-9ankgVNk @ 00:55:51]. Probate Mastery softens the ask: when a PR puts the agent off, the script converts it into permission to market with 'let me shoot you some resources,' then asks whether, once probate is over, the real estate is something they are thinking about selling or it is just too early to say [dzqHqDnAl_E @ 00:03:24]. On cadence, the Probate Mastery host calls each lead 10 to 12 times a month, expects 1 to 3 deals per 100 leads monthly at roughly 40 hours a month, and in the combo model reports a 40 to 60% answer rate across 12 calls, mailing only the roughly 50 non-answerers [Xt7XgZo03Nw @ 00:15:54]. The phone builds the relationship; the mail only covers the people the phone could not reach.

The nurture budget is explicit. Bruce at Probate Mastery spends $61 per resistant PR over 6 to 8 months on about 8 pieces, a few of them 'bombshell,' emails only people already spoken with, and deliberately gives the PR the satisfaction of holding the agent off on the first conversation [kvSTArlGoMU @ 00:54:45]. The All The Leads mastermind sequences the letters: letter one sells concierge services, letter two adds a real-estate PS, letters three and four lead with real estate and carry a services PS, with Chuck preferring a trifold as the first touch [9tTK_fAGgNE @ 00:44:54]. Scaling runs through ISAs: the Probate Mastery host hired about 10 to find one good one, pays a low base near $300 plus roughly $500 per deal so ISAs earn $2,500 to $3,000 against a Philippine norm near $1,000, keeps them calling about 4 hours a day, and hands off only a PR who answers and accepts resources [Xt7XgZo03Nw @ 00:29:00]. In the automated model, engaged leads get about $50 of high-value mail and non-engaged leads about $7, and the host's marketing spend fell from $30-50K a month to $3-4K a month for a couple of deals [Xt7XgZo03Nw @ 00:18:27]. Heavy users of the free concierge vendor network get listed at 4% instead of 3%.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the weekly data feed
   - Pull probate filings weekly from the courthouse with a paid helper, or subscribe to a county list that includes real estate.
   - Pay a helper $2 per lead under a Loom-video SOP, run forewarn lookups and load records into Follow Up Boss; at high volume run three people at three courthouses [The Aaron Novello Podcast — H-CW9uu5s-I @ 00:04:02]
2. Skip trace and dial the PR
   - Skip trace the personal representative and relatives, then call the PR directly rather than the attorney.
   - Run CallTools with ClearSkip and KV Core, open with the pending probate case, and book a consultation offering both a cash offer and a listing option [Carolynn Castillo — XN-9ankgVNk @ 00:53:12]
3. Turn the put-off into permission
   - When the PR says it is too early, ask permission to send resources and confirm sale intent after probate closes.
   - Use 'let me shoot you some resources' and the once-probate-is-over question to move the contact into the pipeline as a likely seller [Probate Mastery — dzqHqDnAl_E @ 00:03:10]
4. Run the 6 to 8 month nurture
   - Fund a multi-piece mail and email sequence for every resistant PR instead of stopping at three touches.
   - Budget about $61 per resistant PR across roughly 8 pieces over 6 to 8 months, emailing only people already reached by phone [Probate Mastery — kvSTArlGoMU @ 00:54:45]

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Phone versus mail is the loudest split. Carolynn Castillo calls cold calling the quickest, fastest way into the niche, with counties producing 20 to 40 leads a week that take one to two hours to dial [RR9l10XmnC4 @ 00:15:14]. Probate Mastery's host splits the field into five models and calls an exclusively mail-driven business very inconsistent, swinging from eight dead months to a burst of three or four calls; the call-forward version dials each lead 10 to 12 times a month and expects one to three deals per 100 leads, which can eat roughly 40 hours a month [Xt7XgZo03Nw @ 00:15:54]. The same host's verdict on half-measures is blunt: the middle ground is the kiss of death, so a single mailer must be hyper-aggressive and real-estate focused, while a multi-piece run of five to seven pieces leads with concierge instead. The data fight runs parallel. The Aaron Novello Podcast insists whoever controls the lead controls its cost, pulls courthouse records weekly, and warns that vendor files like all the leads and US probate leads arrive monthly, often without beneficiaries [H-CW9uu5s-I @ 00:00:43]. Bruce at Probate Mastery takes the opposite trade and buys lists from alltheleads.com just for the sake of time [kvSTArlGoMU @ 00:49:14].

Timing the signature splits the same panel. Justin Stivers, a Miami probate attorney, draws a hard line: no MLS entry, yard sign or door hanger until the court appoints the personal representative, because a different PR can get appointed and hire someone else [Owbx6t3ocjM @ 00:10:10]. Castillo backs that line from the listing chair, telling agents to confirm Letters before listing or eat title-company problems at closing [ewbkVPWFtKM @ 00:17:34]. Bruce plays it differently. Bruce signs pre-letters agreements knowing they are not binding, purely to lock in emotional commitment, and openly admits there is no way to hold the family to anything; David Cordero only does it with one uncontested heir and the listing marked contingent upon the probate court hearing, accepting that marketing spend is lost if the client walks [d-rZkDYaJhQ @ 00:50:21]. On the creditor window, Bruce's North Carolina playbook says you can list and sign contracts inside it, but closing usually waits three to four months after the first ad, and most of the time you are just not allowed to close during it [7xCfSyqYEys @ 01:29:23]. Bruce also rejects an attorney who told a client to wait until probate closes before selling, calling that position not correct, which puts listing agents squarely at odds with slow-walking counsel.

Who to chase is contested too. Castillo calls the personal representative, not the attorney [XN-9ankgVNk @ 00:53:12], while David at Probate Mastery spent about a year and a half reaching seven attorneys and aims for 20, the point where chasing PRs stops being necessary [d-rZkDYaJhQ @ 00:08:36]. On heirs, mastermind regular Chuck argues the best file is simply a smooth one and the economics are not tied to heir count; Bruce counters that a single decision-maker is easiest, yet multiple-heir files produced 13 to 14 transactions and one cash-hungry heir helps pricing [9tTK_fAGgNE @ 00:16:16]. Licensed auctioneer John Rogers offers an outright alternative to the listing: a no-commission, as-is auction using the exact title payoff as the reserve, 45 days of marketing and a 15-day close once the reserve is met, while paying referring agents a full three percent. Asked how common auctions are versus agent listings, Rogers put it at 'might be 80 20,' with the ratio shifting as the economy turns [9eNkvtVoeao @ 00:04:43]. Even conversion benchmarks diverge: David converts about half of a 46-person pipeline while the host calls 20 percent realistic, and David Cordero reports roughly one in 30 warm contacts ready to list.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The costliest losses in this niche are paperwork losses. Justin Stivers watched a realtor waste four months marketing a tenants-in-common property before title flagged that probate was required, and now tells agents to order a title report first [Owbx6t3ocjM @ 00:17:02]. Michelle's amended concession, raised from $8,000 to $10,000, never reached the court papers; the buyer's lender caught it and the deal ate a two-week re-hearing, so the lesson was to treat attorneys as transaction coordinators and get seller permission to contact the lender [kvSTArlGoMU @ 00:25:30]. Bill Gross reports that in Los Angeles about one in four court confirmations gets confirmed while three of four get continued because attorneys don't do the paperwork properly, and adds that 95 percent of probate cases are handled by an attorney who has done one probate or less in the last year [Gyb24k08sGk @ 00:07:59]. Bruce's North Carolina files add three more traps: heirs aged 8 and 11 with a non-heir representative got a closing blocked by the court, an attorney who titled a house to the estate froze sale proceeds at closing, and a family that inventoried a $400k tax value then sold at $600k created a $200k taxable gain [7xCfSyqYEys @ 00:49:08].

People problems kill deals as reliably as paperwork. Carolynn Castillo names family dynamics the biggest challenge, citing a file with eight or nine beneficiaries, two of them minors [XN-9ankgVNk @ 00:24:29]. David Cordero's Hawaii escrow stalled when the court set the formal-probate date for the wrong year; after David got the judge's chambers to reset it, the solo attorney ghosted everyone over an $800 balance the client owed. Bruce's fix was to offer to be the attorney's gopher or paralegal, a move that fed Bruce referrals for years [kvSTArlGoMU @ 00:10:55]. The All The Leads mastermind logs harsher reversals: a ten-day overbid on Bruce's double-ended, full-price deal ended with their own buyer outbid, and a short-sale seller was jailed for 30 days, twice, for contempt [9tTK_fAGgNE @ 00:28:26]. The front desk is a trap too. Probate Mastery reports that agents who tell clerks they want to list inherited properties meet resistance, and clerks will look at you like you have two heads; in most states they cannot deny access, but that does not mean they won't if you approach it the wrong way [Mash-3wq9zY @ 00:05:09]. Even Richie, who got a 20-to-30-minute heir-lookup tutorial from a Northern Virginia clerk, had reached no one yet off those names.

Run the math before the money. Probate Mastery's backward plan says 24 closings at a 20 percent or better conversion means finding 480 pipeline people a year, and warns that people quit because they didn't get a closing fast enough [dzqHqDnAl_E @ 00:01:53]. The growth-model host puts the runway at six months, telling anyone who needs a deal next month that probate may not suit them, and admits marketing spend once ran $30K to $50K a month before dropping to $3K to $4K for a couple of deals; leads cost $400 to $500 for about 150 records [Xt7XgZo03Nw @ 00:37:36]. The urgent slice is crowded: only 10 to 15 percent of filings must sell for financial reasons, and in 100-lead markets about 10 other investors or agents mail and call those same people, while a 1,000-note-a-month handwritten pre-probate campaign converts to about one deal [QU_nb_NyIhU @ 00:04:28]. Bruce's own branding experiment, a '29 days or free' radio ad, burned $25,000 over five months before the first client arrived, and the lesson stuck: never go a year without contacting a referral source [d-rZkDYaJhQ @ 00:33:35]. Castillo's previous vendor sent about 100 leads, many with no real estate, yielding only one to two listings a month.
