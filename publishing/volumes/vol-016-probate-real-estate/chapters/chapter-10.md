## Executive Summary

Probate callers across Probate Mastery, All The Leads, and the wholesale camp converge on one playbook: dial first, lead with the family's hardest problem, and sell the relationship rather than the house. Kevin Ward says one-shot sell calls miss 95 to 99 percent of opportunities, while Bruce Hill's call-first math produces about 50 conversations per 100 leads for roughly $400 of ISA cost versus $900 for 600 mailers. The street mechanics are specific: an offshore VA scrubbing 25 prospects a day, three dials on day one, a resource package as the ask, follow-up inside 15 minutes, and no more than 50 calls per number per day before carriers flag it. The splits are sharp: concierge versus direct-to-sell openers, mail-first versus call-first, and Rick's 'half a mile from you' cover line versus full investor disclosure from Ward.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The loudest point of agreement is that the one-shot 'do you want to sell' call leaves most of the money on the table. Kevin Ward frames probate as a relationship business, arguing that a single sell-or-not call misses roughly 95 to 99 percent of the opportunity, because 'the story changes' as the estate moves forward [URZzrpQSKmU @ 00:05:36]. Bruce Hill backs that with personal numbers: the old direct script produced five to six deals a year, and repositioning as an estate 'concierge' who solves the family's entire new job is what changed the business [sVEhoM1KXUo @ 00:08:38]. Chad Corbett treats the calls as a duty rather than an intrusion, claiming about 98 percent of these owners are likely to sell and that 77 percent of estate listings in the local MLS expired, wasting six to twelve months for families [-ZhpzThVK5o @ 00:01:07]. Even the wholesaler camp agrees on what not to say. Rick warns that opening with 'I saw this person pass away, I want to buy their house' gets the caller cursed out, so the script assumes ownership, never names the deceased, and lets motivated heirs volunteer the death themselves, which builds trust [utX5VR1nHk4 @ 00:01:41]. Ward's baseline prerequisite is patience: the relationship, not the property, is the asset being worked.

The second consensus is that the phone is the fastest and most predictable lead channel, and mail is a supporting act. Hill ranks four channels for an operator who needs income quickly and puts cold calling first, noting that marketing 'is just less predictable' and that a fresh 700-letter drop went out in hope of about six calls [LNsr_JuZvn8 @ 00:43:14]. The call-first math per 100 leads is blunt: roughly 50 percent answer in month one, giving about 50 conversations for around $400 of ISA cost, against a six-month campaign of 600 mailers at $1.50 that runs $900 and pulls inbound calls from about 5 percent [y1TInRM-Flc @ 00:00:43]. When an All The Leads subscriber asked to mail first so the call could open with 'did you receive my letter,' the answer was that mailing first is 'semi valid' but mostly an excuse to postpone dialing; if the opener cannot stand without a mailer reference, the fix is practice and better scripts [omZ157LG-W8 @ 00:03:57]. Rick reaches the same conclusion from the wholesale side, calling the phone 'the best one' and the cheapest option, while still crediting 45 to 50 cent mailers as technically effective and pointing to a mailer that drew a call on a $7M Dallas house [utX5VR1nHk4 @ 00:00:28].

Every coach agrees the first minutes belong to the seller's emotion, not the property. Corbett pairs a probate-specific value proposition with 'what's been the hardest thing for you so far,' deliberately keeping the conversation emotional rather than logical and leading sellers out of 'probate quicksand' instead of pushing them [URZzrpQSKmU @ 00:07:40]. Jim on the All The Leads mastermind calls 'sorry for your loss' false sympathy and swaps in 'I'm sure this is a difficult time... what's been the hardest part?', while Elaine adds a pattern interrupt, the word 'families,' and a hard ban on saying 'I understand' [P5kUaYnRKbo @ 00:25:08]. In the inbound role play, Corbett's debrief ranks the order of operations: rapport, family dynamics, and motivation first, property details after, and no listing appointment without every decision-maker on the phone or in the room [z0GTjTG76ls @ 00:06:24]. Surviving spouses are not off-limits either. Corbett cites 88 percent of Americans over 65 planning to stay in their home for life against one of the fastest-growing nursing-home industries, and recounts a widow who swore she would never sell; two months later a stroke brought the daughter's call, and the house sold for $25,000 more than the price it had expired at with a Remax agent [1d9vQy5dUpU @ 00:01:41].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The conversion engine Bruce Hill runs on Probate Mastery starts with an offshore virtual assistant deliberately hired without phone-sales experience to avoid 'bad habits,' working roughly 300 probate leads a month [uTFKO8zRNsI @ 00:09:40]. Each day the VA spends 60 to 90 minutes building a 25-prospect list: drop records where the decedent did not own the residence, and park personal representatives living at the decedent's address as likely surviving spouses for a callback in three to four weeks. Non-answers get three calls on day one with a voicemail on the third, then two calls the next day, and personal representatives at a different address get dialed on day one [uTFKO8zRNsI @ 00:34:53]. The VA script never mentions real estate, the target is 80 percent agreeing to 'send me the package,' and hand-offs run about three leads a day, or 27 to 29 in two weeks. The stack is a lead-list login, county GIS and tax records, free homes.com ownership checks, and JustCall.io [uTFKO8zRNsI @ 00:40:37]. Speed matters after the yes: Hill wants the agent's follow-up call inside 15 minutes and never later than a day, otherwise wait until the package lands [3UwWnM4igSY @ 00:09:27]. The pipeline rules are fixed: never ask permission to call back, and never end a call without labeling the next one.

Hill's soft opening confirms the personal representative, makes an empathy statement, then asks permission to continue, and it gets a yes 90 to 95 percent of the time; Hill counts 95 percent polite engagement as a success even when the prospect is not excited [6EHNu72T83s @ 00:09:39]. The selling question carries a built-in exit, 'or is it just way too early to tell?', so prospects never have to lie, followed by an offer to bring numbers, an estimate slipped into the packet, and a roughly ten-minute drive-by property preview [6EHNu72T83s @ 00:43:36]. Rick's wholesale script asks only 'are you the owner of this property and are you looking to sell it,' and when a heir asks how the information was obtained, trained VAs answer that a cash-buyer partner bought 'half a mile from you,' never framing it as probate [utX5VR1nHk4 @ 00:02:54]. For 'I have a realtor,' Hill guesses the connection out loud, 'probably a family member,' because people give more information correcting you than answering a question, then asks whether to bring the offer to them or to the realtor; 'bring it to me' means nothing is signed, and at open houses the variant is 'how does this one compare?' [uTFKO8zRNsI @ 00:53:32].

Jim's capacity math on the All The Leads mastermind: 500 leads at three to four touches is about 2,500 dials; at roughly 30 dials an hour that is about 40 hours a month, and a power dialer cuts it to about 20, roughly an hour a day, worked in windows of 5 to 7pm, 8 to 11am, occasional 1 to 3pm slots, and Tim's Wednesday nights and Saturdays around 11am [KirkNi05zPU @ 00:32:42]. The same session sets the carrier ceiling: more than 50 outbound calls a day from one number gets flagged as spam, so rotate Line2, JustCall, or Aircall numbers, register 10DLC and STIR/SHAKEN, and treat asterisked DNC numbers as a business-risk decision, with Tim citing TCPA fines up to $2,500 per incident [KirkNi05zPU @ 00:37:37]. Jim learned the flag problem expensively, burning hours on about 100 calls before discovering a Verizon flag, and a 954-400 RingCentral prefix went dead because it reads as the 'telemarketing capital'; the self-check is calling a non-contact and asking what displayed [P5kUaYnRKbo @ 00:07:30]. On yield, Hill's funnel turns 300 to 400 raw leads into about 100 pipeline leads a month, 20 to 25 percent of which reach an appointment and roughly 15 to 20 percent of which close, with a deliberately low barrier to entry for the pipeline [3UwWnM4igSY @ 00:22:09].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Scrub and stage the daily call list
   - Have the VA build 25 prospects a day, drop non-owners, and park spouse-occupied homes for a three-to-four week callback.
   - Run roughly 300 monthly leads through county GIS, tax records, and homes.com ownership checks, then dial different-address PRs on day one [Probate Mastery — uTFKO8zRNsI @ 00:09:40]
2. Open soft and ask for the resource package
   - Confirm the PR, make an empathy statement, ask permission, and offer the resource package without mentioning real estate.
   - Bruce Hill reports a 90 to 95 percent yes rate on the soft opening and targets 80 percent of VA conversations ending in 'send me the package' [Probate Mastery — 6EHNu72T83s @ 00:08:29]
3. Agent follow-up and next-call labeling
   - Call the handed-off lead inside 15 minutes, never later than a day, and log a dated next call plus next marketing piece.
   - If the window is missed, wait until the package lands; sort leads into Discovery, Hot, Warm, and Nurture without ever asking permission to call back [Probate Mastery — 3UwWnM4igSY @ 00:09:27]
4. Mail non-answerers and fund real conversations
   - In month two, mail only the people not reached and spend the heaviest budget on conversations that read as sellers.
   - Treat 'we don't even know what we're going to do yet' as a seller signal; about 25 of every 50 conversations become a marketable nurture pipeline [Probate Mastery — y1TInRM-Flc @ 00:01:59]

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Bruce Hill and colleague David run opposite sequences on the same probate list. David mails first. Bruce calls first and does the math out loud. On 100 leads, roughly 50% answer in month one, which buys 50 conversations for about $400 of ISA cost. A six-month campaign of 600 mailers at $1.50 apiece costs $900 and pulls inbound calls from about 5% [y1TInRM-Flc @ 00:00:43]. Bruce concedes that mail responders arrive hot while the phone conversations do not, but turns about 25 of the 50 into marketable pipeline: 'I do it my way because I get a bigger pipeline.' An All The Leads subscriber wanted to mail first so the opener could be 'did you receive my letter.' Bruce called that semi-valid but mostly an excuse to put off calling, and told the asker to practice and build better scripts [omZ157LG-W8 @ 00:03:57]. Rick of More Flip with Rick splits the difference. Calling is the cheapest channel, yet mailers at 45 to 50 cents are 'technically the best.' One mailer surfaced a $7M Dallas house near Mark Cuban's, even though the seller went with a higher bidder [utX5VR1nHk4 @ 00:00:28]. Hill's 2025 playbook sequences both: call only for 30 to 45 days, which takes 100 leads down to 40, then send a five-piece blitz spaced 7 to 10 days apart [6EHNu72T83s @ 01:03:06].

The script itself is contested. Kevin Ward argues that 'nothing is impossible to script' and likens a script to a golf club, which is useless until you learn to swing it. In the same session, Chad Corbett admits to resisting scripts in this niche because the conversation tree blows out within a few turns [URZzrpQSKmU @ 00:10:38]. Bruce Hill frames the core fork as speed versus pipeline. Lead with real estate and you get to deals faster, but people lie to you. Lead as a concierge and you move slower, but you fill the CRM [LNsr_JuZvn8 @ 00:43:14]. Wholesaler Rick runs the hard real-estate version for investors. Ask 'are you the owner of this property and are you looking to sell it,' assume ownership, never bring up the deceased, and give the person an exit if they are uncomfortable. Rick adds that motivated heirs volunteer the death on their own, and that builds trust. When a prospect asks how Rick got their information, the trained VA answer is that a cash-buyer partner bought a house half a mile away, and the call is never framed as probate [utX5VR1nHk4 @ 00:02:54]. Ward sits at the opposite pole on disclosure. An investor-agent names the buyer entity up front with no bait and switch, because hiding it signals a conflict while trust sits at a lifetime low [URZzrpQSKmU @ 00:18:12].

Coaches also split on how much to unpack on the first call. In the All The Leads role play, Bruce Hill let the estate-sale objection slide and pivoted straight to a 15-minute walkthrough. Corbett argued the team should be presented as a 'golden safety net' because that spurs action. Corbett would then use personal-property equity, plus the brother's need for a mortgage to buy out the other heir, to trigger the prospect's own epiphany [et_msiK8rvo @ 00:11:01]. Family logistics cut both ways. Corbett's inbound rule is to skip any listing appointment until every decision-maker is on the phone or in person, and to email a PDF of the listing package ahead. Otherwise, siblings expecting 500 against a CMA of 350 will feel 'you just stole 150 grand' [z0GTjTG76ls @ 00:07:33]. Yet when Ashley booked a Panera meeting with estranged sisters joining on Zoom, Corbett warned it risked inflaming the conflict. The prescribed alternative was to hand the seller the solution: replace the slow attorney, pull the address and tax card, have an insurance agent call within the hour, then get the seller in front of an attorney to explain state succession [et_msiK8rvo @ 00:42:21]. On pricing, a questioner's cash tier of 70% of ARV minus repairs drew pushback. Most people cannot estimate repairs, and lumber jumped from $200 to $950 per board foot in 2020 [5IPg3A5CXFU @ 00:01:50].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The dialer is the first place campaigns die quietly. Jim's carrier rule is blunt: dial more than 50 calls a day from one number and it gets flagged as spam. The fix is to work Line2, JustCall or Aircall numbers and finish 10DLC and STIR/SHAKEN registration ahead of the August 1 deadline [KirkNi05zPU @ 00:37:37]. Jim learned the cost firsthand. A RingCentral 954-400 number went unanswered because that prefix is the telemarketing capital, so Jim switched to a Rocky Mount, North Carolina number. Verizon still flags Jim about every other month, and getting a number unflagged means roughly 20 minutes on the phone with the carrier [P5kUaYnRKbo @ 00:07:30]. The worst version is not knowing. Jim burned hours on about 100 calls before discovering a flag. The routine now is to call a non-contact and ask exactly what displayed on their screen before a session [P5kUaYnRKbo @ 00:08:24]. Local area codes help on the receiving end: San Francisco and Marin dialers use 415, and the North Bay uses 707. Dave Pinel's baseline keeps expectations honest even with a clean line: 'You're going to get a hold of 10% of the calls you make' [URZzrpQSKmU @ 00:55:44]. Pinel cited Hoss Pratt, who needed four months to land a first expired before stacking 61 expireds and FSBOs.

Conversation failures cluster around value and grief. Ashley's call reluctance traced back to a fear of being exposed as ignorant, and the coaching verdict on a referral who went silent was brutal: 'He's not calling you back because you didn't provide any real value.' The prescribed recovery was to promise a 4:00 PM callback with the answer and lean on the 10,000 to 11,000-member community for it [et_msiK8rvo @ 00:26:05]. Rick warns that opening with 'I saw this person pass away, I want to buy their house' gets you cursed out [utX5VR1nHk4 @ 00:01:41]. Corbett tells callers to expect to offend someone eventually, refuse to be pushy or unethical, and move on [1d9vQy5dUpU @ 00:03:11]. On the investor side, Randy in Arkansas reported that about 60% of heirs lied, four deals in a row. Title uncovered the hidden heirs almost every time through obituaries, Find a Grave and headstones. The fallout was delays of one to two weeks, and a title company refused a second affidavit from the same people. Bruce Hill's fix is a single question that makes heirs feel they are tattling to an ally: 'no other family members that are going to try to nose into this, are they?' Hill says it brings the truth out most of the time [sVEhoM1KXUo @ 00:49:04].

Economics checks separate busy from profitable. Corbett's earlier model ran about $8,000 a month in overhead and $140,000 gross, yet produced only $2,900 in adjusted gross income. Internet leads converted at about 1 in 100, which is why Corbett called a lead-buying recommendation 'bad advice' [URZzrpQSKmU @ 00:31:14]. Bruce Hill's county filter is equally cold: skip counties that consistently run under 25 records. By Hill's math, 100 records yield about 60 to 70 with property and roughly 2 deals at a bare minimum, and 30 files yield 15-plus sales, of which capturing 20 to 30% means 1.5 to 3 deals [uTFKO8zRNsI @ 01:05:36]. Hill's funnel numbers from a separate session land in the same range: 100 raw leads, 70 of them carrying real estate, produce about 1.5 deals [3UwWnM4igSY @ 00:22:52]. Mail has its own floor. About 20% of competitors send a single letter, prospects forget within 30 days, and financially urgent sellers make up only 10 to 20% of the market. Hill's verdict: if you are going to mail only one or two letters, save your money [6EHNu72T83s @ 01:04:04]. Hill backs that with a heavy-envelope, five-piece sequence rather than a one-off drop, because a single letter is gone from memory before the estate is ready to move.
