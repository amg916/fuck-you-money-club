## 1. Assign eleven plays to five seats before buying more capacity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Translate Candidate #9's eleven-play scope into an explicit operating map rather than eleven departments. For this chapter, the work packages are docket discovery, representative-authority verification, attorney referral development, executor intake, title and occupancy triage, valuation and acquisition screening, listing preparation, property readiness coordination, court-sale execution, closing and commission collection, and executor aftercare. These labels organize the eleven plays; they do not create additional revenue categories. FT1 is the producing licensed broker or appropriately supervised licensee, owning relationships, pricing, negotiations, and brokerage decisions. FT2 is the transaction and executor-service lead, owning intake, calendars, documents, vendor coordination, and collection reconciliation. C1 provides docket research and administrative enrichment, C2 supplies property-condition documentation and readiness coordination, and C3 provides specialist transaction administration. Count each contractor as a seat, not an unlimited labor pool. The representative retains estate decisions; counsel supplies legal interpretation; the broker retains licensed supervision. Write an accountable owner, backup, required evidence, and next action into every work package. This prevents the familiar failure where everyone assumes someone else checked whether the person signing actually holds current authority. Review the map whenever a jurisdiction, service, or contractor changes.

The seven-person version adds FT3, a licensed associate dedicated to listing execution and relationship coverage, plus C4, a contract bookkeeper handling reconciliations and cohort reporting. FT1 then spends protected time developing attorney relationships and coaching exception handling rather than becoming the universal document chaser. FT2 remains the operating owner; adding a bookkeeper does not transfer responsibility for transaction completeness. Introduce these seats only after a rolling thirteen-week workload forecast shows persistent service failures or economically attractive unmet demand. Record available hours, accepted files, court-dependent files, and rework hours separately. An illustrative capacity model might budget eight administrative hours per new listing and three hours per active file monthly, then replace those assumptions with six weeks of measured activity. Court delays consume monitoring capacity without generating a new commission. Contractor businesses may serve other clients, so contracted availability must be documented instead of assumed. Outside counsel, escrow, title officers, and estate-paid trades are counterparties, not invisible extra pod members. If coordination needs exceed the five-seat or seven-seat budget, reduce simultaneous intake or explicitly price additional service capacity. Do not preserve an attractive staffing ratio by moving real operating work outside the headcount narrative.

Pay contractors for accepted deliverables with stated scope, not for unlicensed brokerage outcomes. Illustrative purchasing budgets are $35 per verified docket packet for C1, $225 per documented property-readiness inspection for C2, and $450 per accepted administrative transaction file for C3. These are planning assumptions, not market quotes, and require workload validation. For C4, test an $800 monthly bookkeeping retainer covering named reconciliations and a fixed close deadline. Define revision allowances, exception charges, access restrictions, invoicing evidence, and termination assistance in each agreement. Reject pay-per-attorney-referral arrangements and percentage commissions to unlicensed administrators; California Business and Professions Code sections 10130 and 10137 illustrate licensing and compensation boundaries that require state-specific implementation. Contractor classification follows actual working conditions and applicable tests, including relevant exceptions, rather than the contract heading. Output standards do not justify treating a controlled full-time employee as an independent business. Require contractor cure of documented defects under the agreement without imposing improper wage deductions. Before launch, compare the complete labor plan with the canonical expense envelope: the Conservative case is a stress case, and its two full-time seats may be uneconomic at local lawful wages and required owner compensation.

## 2. Run a weekly evidence review with contractor acceptance and concentration limits  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run the weekly operator review from a frozen Friday snapshot, not from whatever the CRM displays during Monday's meeting. Use Airtable or a comparable database for estate identifiers and relationships, a brokerage transaction system such as Lone Wolf Transactions for controlled documents, and the accounting ledger for earned commissions. Docket alerts initiate review; they do not prove authority or replace the court record. Each of the eleven plays needs an entry condition, completion evidence, accountable seat, and timestamp. Measure verified packets, qualified executor conversations, documented authority, signed listings, readiness completion, accepted offers, completed court milestones, closings, and collected retained commissions. Use denominators that match the event: accepted packets divided by submitted packets measures contractor quality, while signed listings divided by qualified consultations measures conversion. An illustrative target is at least 98% first-pass acceptance for administrative packets, with every authority defect treated as critical regardless of the aggregate percentage. Review unanswered executor requests older than one business day and internal tasks overdue more than two business days. Publish counts beside rates so a two-file week cannot masquerade as reliable statistical improvement. Keep closed cohorts immutable except for logged corrections with named reviewers.

Score contractor performance on accepted quality, responsiveness, and controllable elapsed time. Start the service clock only when the required inputs and authorized access are available; record the missing input rather than silently pausing work. A C1 packet must match the property, case, representative, and source record. A C2 inspection must include dated images, access authorization, observed hazards, and explicit limits on what was inspected. C3 must reconcile names, signatures, document versions, and calendar events without supplying legal conclusions. FT2 samples five completed contractor outputs weekly, or every output when volume is lower, and reviews every authority, money, or confidentiality exception. A routine defect gets a one-business-day correction request; a critical defect suspends use of the affected output immediately and alerts FT1. Track rework hours and repeat-defect categories alongside nominal unit price. A cheaper packet that consumes broker time is not cheaper delivered capacity. Pay accepted invoices on the contractual schedule, resolve disputed line items with evidence, and avoid using payment delays as informal discipline. The weekly meeting ends with named corrective actions and due dates, not a color-coded dashboard that leaves the same defects unresolved.

Set a concentration policy of no more than 25% from any single estate customer, referring attorney firm, or material dependency counterparty. Calculate customer and referral shares using trailing-twelve-month retained commissions, with a separate forward view of contracted pipeline. Aggregate related estates or firms where common control creates the same dependency. Measure contractor dependence using spend and the share of critical workflow capacity, documenting approved backup capacity without pretending additional vendors are staffed pod seats. A new brokerage may breach the revenue cap after its first closing; report that breach honestly and place it on a diversification plan rather than altering the denominator. Use 20% as an early-warning threshold, reduce discretionary relationship-development concentration, and build coverage through other probate firms and direct executor education. Never reject fiduciary obligations on existing engagements merely to repair a metric. Track the dependency separately when the supervising brokerage itself dominates access or revenue rights; a principal brokerage relationship cannot be made diversified by renaming it. Buyers should see both actual concentration and the remediation history. The target is a resilient book, not statistical cosmetics or an incentive to pay for referrals.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly evidence set
   - Export Friday pipeline, contractor acceptance, calendar, and ledger snapshots.
   - Preserve estate identifiers and report timestamps; retain corrected versions with an explanation rather than overwriting the evidence.
2. Resolve critical exceptions first
   - Review authority, funds, access, confidentiality, and court-deadline exceptions before production metrics.
   - FT1 owns brokerage decisions; FT2 assigns administrative cures; counsel resolves legal interpretation within the engagement.
3. Rebalance the existing pod
   - Assign workload using measured hours and contracted availability across the five or seven seats.
   - Do not add hidden assistants or count title, escrow, and estate-paid trades as free internal labor.
4. Publish commitments and verify closure
   - Issue a one-page scorecard with action owners, deadlines, concentration breaches, and cash exceptions.
   - Start the next review with unresolved commitments and record evidence of correction before marking them complete.

## 3. Reconcile retained commissions, cohort economics, and staffing feasibility  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep the canonical scenarios intact: Conservative revenue is $120,000 with a 20% margin and $24,000 owner net profit; Base revenue is $510,000 with a 27.5% margin and $140,250 profit; Scaled revenue is $900,000 with a 35% margin and $315,000 profit. Revenue means retained brokerage commissions after brokerage splits, never the property's sale price, estate assets, escrow balances, or acquisition principal. The corresponding total expense envelopes are $96,000, $369,750, and $585,000. At an illustrative $7,500 retained commission per closing, the cases require 16, 68, and 120 annual closings; this is a throughput illustration, not a prescribed commission rate. Prices remain negotiated under the actual brokerage engagement. Base requires roughly 5.7 closings monthly, while Scaled requires ten. The additional $390,000 of revenue supports $215,250 of incremental expenses and $174,750 of incremental owner profit. That bridge must fund FT3, C4, and all other growth costs rather than treating their work as free. Separately model the Conservative staffing feasibility problem: two full-time seats plus three contractors may exceed its expense envelope, making that case a runway warning rather than evidence of a sustainable local wage structure.

Maintain one estate-level subledger with distinct dates for intake, authority verification, engagement, listing, accepted offer, court milestone, closing, earned commission, and cash receipt. Use QuickBooks Online or an equivalent ledger with an estate identifier carried through invoices, vendor bills, and supporting settlement statements. Recognize revenue when earned under the brokerage contract and applicable accounting policy, ordinarily around completed closing rather than at a speculative offer. Accrue accepted contractor work in the service period, even when the invoice arrives later. Record brokerage splits consistently so the chapter's management revenue remains the retained amount; reconcile any different statutory presentation through an explicit bridge. Estate-funded repairs and acquisition funds need separate treatment based on the actual arrangement, not automatic inclusion in commission revenue. Client trust money never becomes operating cash merely because the brokerage can see the balance. Reconcile the bank, commission receivable, vendor accruals, and relevant trust records monthly. Preserve a cash collection report beside the accrual income statement so delayed court sales cannot hide a payroll problem. The bookkeeper prepares evidence, while the accountable broker reviews unresolved differences and signs the monthly close checklist.

Analyze performance by both engagement-start cohort and closing-month cohort. The first shows conversion, elapsed time, abandonment, and acquisition cost; the second explains recognized revenue and delivered margin. Charge research costs from unsuccessful estates to the relevant origination cohort rather than assigning costs only to winners. Allocate shared pod costs through a documented driver such as measured service hours, and show contribution before allocation separately from owner profit after all expenses. An acquisition screening play can route a property to an investment entity, but principal, resale proceeds, financing, and investment gains belong in a separate schedule. Disclose conflicts and obtain the required transaction permissions; do not improve the brokerage scorecard by mixing investment economics into retained commissions. The stated $3,000–$18,000 monthly operating range cannot represent every expense at every scenario: even its upper annualized value is below Base total expenses. Define which overhead categories that range covers and reconcile labor and other delivery costs separately. With $15,000–$100,000 startup capital and four to eighteen months to the first deal, test runway through a thirteen-week cash forecast and longer monthly stress case. Never finance payroll from estate funds or assumed future closings.

## 4. Build an assignable operating book and price only transferable earnings  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A transferable probate book is a repeatable service system, not ownership of grieving families or an unrestricted right to attorney referrals. Estate engagements end; referral relationships depend on confidence and continued performance. Build the asset around documented workflows, clean records, trained backup coverage, permitted relationship introductions, and contracts whose transfer terms are understood. Review brokerage affiliation, listing agreements, vendor arrangements, software subscriptions, domains, and telephone numbers for assignment, termination, and change-of-control provisions. Licenses and court authority do not automatically transfer with a business sale. California Probate Code section 10538 illustrates notice requirements under independent administration; section 10308 addresses court confirmation in the supervised sale framework. Document the applicable route for each estate instead of copying one state's workflow nationwide. Maintain signed contractor copyright assignments for original templates, training materials, photographs, and software where ownership is intended; 17 U.S.C. section 204(a) requires a signed writing for a copyright ownership transfer. A paid invoice alone does not establish that transfer. Separate preexisting contractor materials and third-party licenses from assigned work. Keep sensitive estate documents out of unrestricted sale materials and disclose identifiable records only through an appropriately authorized, access-controlled diligence process.

Value the operation using a buyer-tested earnings definition before discussing a multiple. For a small owner-operated brokerage, an illustrative negotiation range of two to three times normalized seller's discretionary earnings is a useful sensitivity, not a sourced market comparable. A three-to-three-and-a-half-times stretch case requires unusually strong evidence of transferability, diversification, clean records, and limited founder dependence. Canonical owner net profit is not automatically seller's discretionary earnings or EBITDA. Bridge recorded owner compensation, supported one-time items, continuing expenses, and replacement labor to the selected buyer metric without double counting. If diligence supports $140,250 as normalized Base owner-operator earnings, two to three times implies $280,500–$420,750 of illustrative operating-business value. Using $315,000 on the same conditional basis implies $630,000–$945,000. A passive buyer must instead deduct any incremental replacement cost for unpaid owner functions before pricing managed cash flow. Debt, excess cash, working-capital adjustments, taxes, and transaction costs affect proceeds separately. Do not apply an earnings multiple to estate property values, gross commissions before splits, or unsigned pipeline. Require buyer feedback to support any claimed premium rather than presenting it as an entitlement earned by documentation alone.

Test transferability through a thirty-day founder-absence exercise while preserving required licensed supervision. FT3 covers licensed execution in the seven-person pod; a five-person pod cannot assume C3 may replace its only licensed producer. Where backup licensed coverage is unavailable, report that dependency and its replacement cost. Track missed deadlines, executor escalations, attorney relationship requests, and commissions dependent on personal founder intervention. Build a diligence room containing three years of available monthly accounts, a complete operating history when younger, cohort schedules, contractor agreements, IP assignments, concentration reports, litigation summaries, and documented supervisory procedures. Redact unnecessary personal information and log access. For seller financing or earnouts, define retained commission revenue, allowed deductions, collection timing, audit rights, and dispute procedures precisely. Do not structure attorney introductions as disguised settlement-service referral payments; 12 U.S.C. section 2607 requires transaction-specific analysis where covered settlement services are involved. If sale offers undervalue durable cash flow, retain the business and distribute only cash exceeding payroll, operating reserves, and documented obligations. The same controls support either outcome: an honest earnings bridge, transferable work products, diversified relationships, and a pod that completes its eleven plays without unrecorded founder rescue.

> 
> Require documentary evidence for each condition; an attractive pipeline does not substitute for authority, cash, ownership, or coverage.
> - Confirm every active estate's representative authority, applicable sale procedure, listing status, and supervising license coverage before increasing intake.
> - Reconcile retained commissions, vendor accruals, bank balances, and the canonical scenario bridge; test new seats against the actual expense envelope.
> - Verify signed IP assignments, software transfer terms, contract assignment requirements, and authorized handling of estate records before opening buyer access.
> - Review every customer, referral-firm, and material counterparty exposure against the 25% policy; disclose breaches and demonstrate tested backup coverage.
