## 1. Days 1–7: Establish the Licensed Pod, County Scope, and Eleven-Play Operating Map  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Launch in one county where the supervising broker already understands probate sales and can obtain current court procedures. California supplies the worked legal example here; substitute the actual state's licensing, fiduciary, notice, and confirmation requirements before operating elsewhere. Assign FT1 as the licensed broker or properly supervised licensed agent responsible for representation, pricing, negotiation, and attorney relationships. Assign FT2 as operations lead responsible for docket review, evidence capture, scheduling, and transaction checklists; restrict any unlicensed activity to permitted administrative work. The three contractors are C1, a brokerage-supervised transaction coordinator; C2, a properly licensed property-services coordinator where required; and C3, an independent probate attorney engaged for defined business questions. Estate counsel remains separately engaged by the estate. These are five people, not five unlimited service departments. Start with a weekly capacity ledger of 40 hours per full-time seat, eight hours for C1, eight for C2, and four for C3. Those hours describe proposed capacity, not prepaid labor. Obtain written rates, licensing boundaries, insurance evidence, and escalation rules before assigning work. If quoted labor breaks the cash ceiling, reduce purchased hours and active files rather than silently assuming free professional services or adding another person.

Translate the volume's 11 mapped plays into an explicit sprint work map: P1 county docket intelligence; P2 decedent-to-property matching; P3 representative-authority verification; P4 permitted executor outreach; P5 probate-attorney relationships; P6 executor concierge coordination; P7 property access and condition triage; P8 title, occupancy, and lien triage; P9 licensed listing and disposition; P10 notice, court, and closing coordination; and P11 separately controlled acquisition screening. These are this chapter's operational labels, not eleven separately forecast revenue streams. FT2 owns P1–P3, FT1 owns P4–P5 and P9, C2 supports P6–P7, C1 supports P8 and P10, and FT1 reviews P11 with independent counsel when a genuine conflict question arises. Each play receives one checklist, one accountable owner, one completion artifact, and one defect code in Airtable or an equivalent access-controlled CRM. Store letters of administration, orders, and sensitive correspondence in a restricted document repository, not a broadly shared prospect spreadsheet. Record court source, retrieval date, case number, property identifier, and review status. Docket alerts may identify a changed filing, but they cannot establish sale authority automatically. A public probate filing also does not establish permission to enter property, contact every relative, or market an estate asset.

Commit $60,000 of available launch capital for this illustrative pilot, within the canonical $15,000–$100,000 startup range. Limit Days 1–30 cash outflow to $9,000: $1,200 for licensing, insurance, and onboarding; $900 for docket, CRM, and communications tools; $1,500 for scoped counsel; $1,800 for administrative and transaction support; $600 for permitted outreach; $600 for travel and property triage; $1,800 for founder draw or other operator cash compensation; and $600 contingency. Replace these envelopes with binding quotes before spending. They are cash limits, not assertions that two market-rate salaries fit inside $9,000. Track unpaid founder labor separately when evaluating sustainable economics. Authorize no property purchase, earnest-money deposit, estate repair advance, or speculative contractor mobilization from this budget. Keep estate-paid work on separate authorizations and invoices without counting reimbursements as commission revenue. Set Days 31–60 and 61–90 ceilings at $6,000 each, making cumulative burn limits $15,000 and $21,000. At Day 90, $39,000 remains if nothing closes, covering 6.5 additional months at $6,000 monthly burn. That does not cover the longest 18-month first-deal case; longer delays require committed funding, lower burn, or suspension.

## 2. Days 8–14: Build a Verified Docket Cohort and Permission-Based Referral Flywheel  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a cohort of 60 eligible estate-property opportunities by Day 30, beginning with 30 by Day 14. Eligibility requires a relevant county case, a defensible property match, an identifiable appointed representative or a clearly documented appointment-pending status, and an allowed contact route. Pending appointments remain research records rather than qualified representation opportunities. Deduplicate by estate case and parcel, because several heirs or multiple docket entries do not create several leads. Count a substantive conversation only when the representative or their authorized adviser discusses property status, timing, or a service need; voicemail, mail delivery, and an email open do not count. A qualified opportunity additionally requires verified representative authority, plausible sale intent within twelve months, a known decision process, and permission for a specific next step. FT2 logs both the cohort entry date and event dates so later conversion rates have stable denominators. Sample 20 records weekly against original court and assessor sources; require at least 90% accuracy during setup and 95% before expansion. A mismatch involving identity or authority is a critical defect regardless of the aggregate percentage. Correct the source rule and recheck all similarly generated records before resuming that workflow.

Use P4 and P5 as separate, attributable channels rather than treating every attorney name as an endorsement. For direct outreach, FT1 approves a restrained letter explaining the brokerage's county experience, property-preparation process, and optional next step. Respect known representation, address accuracy, suppression requests, and applicable solicitation restrictions. Do not launch autodialed or prerecorded campaigns merely because a number appears in a public filing; 47 CFR 64.1200 imposes channel-specific restrictions. For attorney relationships, identify twelve local probate practices, seek six substantive introductory discussions during the first month, and offer a useful artifact: an anonymized property-readiness checklist showing access, occupancy, title, repair, and court-path dependencies. Ask how the practice prefers brokerage updates and who may authorize property work. Do not pay attorneys for settlement-service referrals. Apply a no-paid-referral pilot policy and have counsel assess any proposed arrangement under 12 U.S.C. 2607 and applicable professional rules. The flywheel is reliable execution followed by permission to remain useful, not purchased influence. Record introductions, meetings, permissioned follow-ups, and actual referred opportunities separately. One friendly lunch is relationship activity; it becomes pipeline evidence only when a specific estate requests a defined service through an authorized decision-maker.

Give executor concierge work a narrow operating promise: organize the property decision, expose dependencies, and coordinate approved vendors without taking over the representative's fiduciary role. During intake, ask about occupancy, keys, insurance, utilities, personal property, family disagreement, existing counsel, mortgages, and anticipated timing. Record answers as reported facts until corroborated. C2 may obtain repair estimates after lawful access is arranged, but cannot authorize a cleanout simply because a beneficiary wants speed. C1 requests a preliminary title review through an appropriate provider and routes ownership, vesting, lien, and transfer questions to qualified professionals. FT1 then supplies a brokerage scope with separately stated commission terms and a documented pricing process. Keep P11 acquisition screening outside the brokerage pitch: disclose any ownership interest, separate the estate's representation decision, obtain independent review where appropriate, and exclude acquisition returns from the canonical commission forecast. The first sprint does not buy houses. Its deliverables are two completed property-readiness reviews and at least one qualified opportunity with a dated next action by Day 30. If families request services outside the pod's scope, make a transparent introduction without promising court outcomes, accelerating statutory timelines, or treating personal relationships as a substitute for representative authority.

## 3. Days 15–21: Rehearse Sale Execution and Reconcile Commission Economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Convert discovery into a controlled transaction rehearsal before accepting more files. For the California example, verify whether the representative has full or limited authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act and whether the contemplated act falls within that authority. Probate Code section 10501 identifies actions requiring court supervision; section 10580 addresses notice of proposed action, with related provisions governing timing, recipients, and objections. A docket label saying independent administration is insufficient without the operative documents and restrictions. Where court confirmation applies, C1 builds a hearing, notice, deposit, and bidder-instruction checklist from current local requirements and estate counsel's direction. Section 10311 supplies the statutory increased-bid framework: for a $500,000 original bid, the first qualifying increase under its basic formula is $25,500, producing $525,500, subject to the statute's conditions and court process. Do not advertise an ordinary escrow timeline for that file. FT1 explains the applicable sale path to prospective buyers without promising confirmation or possession. Test the checklist against one fictional full-authority sale and one fictional court-confirmed sale, mark every deadline owner, and require a second-person review. An objection, changed order, or authority conflict returns the file to counsel rather than an automated closing sequence.

Keep the scorecard's revenue basis intact when constructing the unit model. Conservative revenue $120,000 less $96,000 total modeled costs equals $24,000 owner profit at 20%; Base revenue $510,000 less $369,750 equals $140,250 at 27.5%; Scaled revenue $900,000 less $585,000 equals $315,000 at 35%. These are annual operating scenarios, not first-quarter forecasts. A simple illustrative volume bridge is ten closings retaining $12,000 each, 34 retaining $15,000 each, and 50 retaining $18,000 each, respectively. Those retained amounts require actual commission agreements and brokerage splits; they are not standard rates. For example, a negotiated $20,000 brokerage commission with $5,000 paid under the brokerage split leaves $15,000 of operator revenue, regardless of the home's sale price. Do not subtract that split a second time. Track contribution as retained commission minus incremental file-specific costs, then separately deduct overhead and all other modeled costs when measuring net margin. The canonical $3,000–$18,000 monthly operating-expense range is a planning input, not a substitute for those complete annual cost totals. Explicitly classify compensation, contractor work, acquisition-channel expense, insurance, software, and file delivery so the model cannot create attractive margins by omitting difficult cost categories.

Measure service capacity before using pipeline counts to justify growth. Reserve FT1's weekly 40 hours as twelve for relationships, twelve for consultations and pricing, eight for active negotiations, and eight for supervision and exceptions. Reserve FT2's 40 hours as twelve for research, ten for intake, ten for transaction administration, and eight for quality checks and reporting. Contractor hours remain limited to their purchased scopes. Begin with a ceiling of five active represented estate files across the pod and no more than two new property-readiness reviews weekly. An active file has a signed engagement and a currently assigned delivery obligation; dormant prospects do not consume the same capacity measure. Use a two-business-day target for acknowledging substantive estate requests and a 95% target for timely scheduled file milestones. Timeliness is measured against operator-controlled commitments, not the court's calendar. Track family disputes, court continuances, title defects, and housing-market repricing as distinct delay codes. If milestone performance falls below 95% for two consecutive weeks, pause new discretionary acquisition spending and fix workload allocation. The Scaled case permits three full-time people and four contractors, seven total; the initial five-person pod does not inherit that staffing capacity simply because a spreadsheet shows $900,000 of potential commissions.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open the evidence packet
   - Attach the current appointment documents, property match, and engagement status.
   - FT2 records source dates and unresolved restrictions; FT1 confirms that requested brokerage activity falls within the verified authority.
2. Select the actual sale pathway
   - Assign the applicable notice, objection, confirmation, and closing checklist.
   - C1 uses estate counsel's direction and current local procedures rather than copying a neighboring county's timeline.
3. Price the retained-commission contribution
   - Record negotiated commission, brokerage split, incremental delivery costs, and overhead treatment.
   - FT1 approves the unit model; FT2 prevents home value, purchase principal, and expense reimbursements from inflating commission revenue.
4. Release and monitor controlled work
   - Assign each milestone to one person with a dated evidence requirement.
   - C1 logs completion; authority changes, missed critical deadlines, and unresolved conflicts suspend the affected task pending review.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Day 30/60/90: Decide Kill, Fix, or Scale from Evidence  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Close the sprint with one auditable cohort report, not a presentation assembled from disconnected activity totals. Days 22–24 finish the second readiness review, reconcile suppressed contacts, and audit source accuracy. Days 25–27 complete attorney follow-ups, validate each qualified opportunity's next step, and review signed engagement terms. Days 28–30 reconcile cash, test capacity, and hold the decision meeting. Use cumulative targets of 60 eligible opportunities, eight substantive conversations, and one qualified opportunity at Day 30; 100, sixteen, and three at Day 60; and 140, twenty-four, and five at Day 90. The targets are pilot hypotheses, not industry conversion benchmarks. The implied conversation rates are 13.3%, 16%, and 17.1%; examine cohort age before attributing improvement to messaging. Require two signed brokerage engagements by Day 90 for expansion, but do not require a closed sale: the canonical first-deal window is four to eighteen months. A signed engagement is backlog evidence, not recognized revenue or spendable cash. Record court-dependent dates separately from operator commitments, and remove opportunities whose representative withdraws permission or whose authority cannot be verified. Preserve the original denominator so deleting unpromising records cannot manufacture an improved conversion rate.

Apply decision precedence consistently: critical authority, licensing, or material contact-rule defects stop the affected activity immediately; runway and cash limits come next; demand and efficiency determine the remaining choice. Kill means stop the tested acquisition channel and its discretionary spending, while preserving existing client duties and orderly handoffs. It does not mean abandon an estate. Day 30 Kill requires zero substantive conversations after 60 eligible opportunities have received the completed permitted contact sequence, or a cash-limit breach without replacement funding. Day 60 Kill requires zero qualified opportunities after at least sixteen substantive conversations and one documented repair cycle. Day 90 Kill applies when qualified opportunities remain below two after at least twenty-four substantive conversations and two repair cycles, or funded runway drops below six months. Insufficient sample completion is Fix, not evidence that demand is absent. Each Fix cycle lasts fourteen days, changes one bottleneck, names an owner, and caps incremental spending at $1,500 within the existing cumulative budget. Examples include correcting parcel matches, replacing a weak service artifact, or tightening intake questions. A failed contact route cannot be repaired by ignoring suppression requests, and an unresolved family dispute cannot be repaired by pressuring the representative into a sale.

Scale means a bounded increase in evidence-producing activity, not a jump to the Scaled annual profit case. At Day 30, meeting the full target with at least 95% source accuracy, no critical defects, burn no greater than $9,000, and six months of funded forward runway permits a 25% increase in weekly research and outreach volume. Day 60 additionally requires the sixteen-conversation and three-qualified-opportunity targets, at least one signed brokerage engagement, cumulative burn no greater than $15,000, and 95% controlled-milestone timeliness. Day 90 additionally requires twenty-four conversations, five qualified opportunities, two signed engagements, cumulative burn no greater than $21,000, and positive projected file contribution on each signed engagement. Keep projected contribution separate from earned results and exclude unsigned prospects from its calculation. At every review, require available cash divided by approved forward monthly burn to equal at least six; include proposed incremental spending in that denominator. No gate authorizes hiring beyond the five-person pod. Add the third full-time seat and fourth contractor only under a separately funded staffing plan supported by sustained delivery demand. If all Kill conditions are absent but any Scale condition fails, choose Fix, preserve the cash ceiling, and document the next fourteen-day test before leaving the meeting.
