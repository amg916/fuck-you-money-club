## 1. Establish sale authority before marketing, pricing, or promising a closing  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Open every estate file with an authority packet, not a listing presentation. Obtain the appointment order, current letters, relevant will provisions, recorded vesting, and any restrictions on the representative's powers. A death certificate establishes death; it does not appoint a seller. A beneficiary's enthusiasm does not substitute for authority, and a predeath power of attorney generally ends at death. Confirm whether the parcel belongs to the probate estate, a trust, surviving joint owners, or another entity before selecting a contract form. Order a preliminary title report and have estate counsel resolve inconsistent names, ancillary administration, spousal rights, and competing appointments. Record the court, case number, representative, authority expiration or limitations, and next required procedural event in the transaction checklist. Clio can hold counsel's matter reference; the brokerage CRM should hold only the documents necessary for its assignment. Docket alerts identify events but do not authenticate authority: verify material filings against the court record and retain the actual order. Never advertise a confirmed closing date merely because a petition has been filed. Build the schedule backward from transferable title, required notices, objection periods, and any hearing, with separate owners for each dependency.

California illustrates why the phrase 'probate sale' is too broad for an operating rule. Under the Independent Administration of Estates Act, Probate Code sections 10400 through 10592 distinguish authority and procedural requirements; limited authority generally does not permit an independent real-property sale. With full authority, determine whether notice of proposed action is required under sections 10580 through 10592, whether an exception applies, and whether an objection changes the route. Where section 10586 applies, calculate its notice timing rather than substituting a sales team's preferred deadline. A court-supervised sale can instead involve confirmation and overbid procedures under sections 10300 through 10316. Florida adds a different fork: section 733.613 addresses real-property sales and the effect of a power of sale, while protected homestead can require separate analysis under the state constitution. Texas independent administration under Estates Code Chapter 402 can reduce routine court involvement without eliminating fiduciary obligations or title review. For an out-of-state parcel, obtain a local authority analysis; the home-state appointment is not a universal conveyancing credential. Put the selected route, supporting documents, and remaining conditions in writing before the broker publishes terms that suggest an ordinary unrestricted sale.

Separate three commercial roles: licensed brokerage, administrative concierge work, and buying for the operator's own account. California Business and Professions Code sections 10130 and 10131 regulate compensated brokerage activity; another state's license does not automatically authorize local negotiations. Unlicensed assistants may perform permitted clerical tasks but must not price, solicit, negotiate, or explain transaction terms when those acts require a license. A concierge agreement can coordinate cleanouts, utilities, and vendor scheduling, but drafting probate pleadings or choosing heirs' legal positions belongs with counsel. Executor instructions also do not authorize self-dealing: California Probate Code section 9600 imposes a care standard, and other jurisdictions supply their own fiduciary duties. If the operator wants to buy a listed estate property, disclose the principal interest and route the conflict to the supervising broker and estate counsel before substantive negotiation. Do not assume a signed disclosure cures every conflict. Use an independent valuation and whatever additional process the jurisdiction requires. With 4–18 months to a first deal, $15,000–$100,000 startup capital, and $3,000–$18,000 monthly operating costs, an unauthorized shortcut can consume the entire runway through rescission, commission disputes, or a delayed closing.

## 2. Draft contracts around actual authority, earned fees, and controllable exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a coordinated document stack rather than forcing every service into the listing agreement. The brokerage agreement identifies the estate's authorized signer, property, agency relationship, negotiated compensation, term, termination rights, and the legally permitted fee trigger. The purchase agreement separately addresses title, inspections, deposits, possession, and any required court confirmation or notice process. A concierge scope describes specific deliverables: three cleanout quotes, utility transfer coordination, photographic inventory, or weekly vendor reporting. It excludes legal representation and regulated brokerage acts unless the contracting provider holds the necessary authority. State whether vendors contract directly with the estate and whether the operator may approve spending; use a written dollar ceiling and reimbursement evidence. Do not represent a representative's personal signature as an estate obligation without checking capacity language and applicable law. Tie deadlines to observable events such as delivery of letters or expiration of a required notice period. If confirmation fails or an objection pauses the sale, specify whether services pause, which documented expenses remain payable, and how unused advances return. A sale contingency cannot promise that the court, heirs, buyer's lender, or title insurer will act on the operator's preferred timetable.

Use fee language that prevents the brokerage and concierge ledgers from becoming indistinguishable. State whether a commission is earned on closing or another permitted contractual event, how the brokerage split applies, and whether any protection period survives termination. A narrowly drafted anti-circumvention provision can identify introduced prospects, a defined tail period, and written notice requirements; it should not purport to block the executor's statutory duties or create an unlawful fee. Liability limits should address the actual service and preserve exclusions required by governing law, including treatment of fraud, intentional misconduct, trust funds, and professional duties. Align insurance with the promised scope rather than treating a contract cap as coverage. Dispute provisions should specify governing law, venue, mediation timing, and any arbitration terms while preserving matters reserved to the probate court. Data terms should allocate the estate's documents, the broker's preexisting templates, vendor photographs, permitted processing, retention, and deletion. Give the estate an export of its transaction records, but do not promise destruction that conflicts with mandatory record retention or a litigation hold. Restrict subcontractor access to the assigned task and prohibit reuse of beneficiary contact details for unrelated campaigns.

Reconcile the contract economics to retained brokerage revenue before hiring. An illustrative $400,000 sale at a separately negotiated 2.5% commission for the operator's represented side produces $10,000 before a brokerage split; retaining 60% produces $6,000 recognized operator revenue. Neither percentage is prescribed or presented as a market standard. At that illustrative retention, 20 closings yield the Conservative $120,000, 85 yield the Base $510,000, and 150 yield the Scaled $900,000. The underlying property values are $8,000,000, $34,000,000, and $60,000,000 respectively, but none is operator revenue. Apply the canonical margins exactly: 20% produces $24,000 owner profit, 27.5% produces $140,250, and 35% produces $315,000. Implied annual operating costs are $96,000, $369,750, and $585,000 after the illustrated brokerage retention. Staffing remains 2 FT + 3 C in Conservative and Base, then 3 FT + 4 C in Scaled. These closing counts explain one possible fee mix, not a replacement forecast. Do not add concierge billings, referral receipts, or acquisition gains to these cases without a separately reconciled model. Stress-test delayed closings against cash reserves, because signed listings do not pay payroll and court calendars do not accelerate to match a monthly sales target.

## 3. Use permission-led outreach and uncompensated attorney introductions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A public probate docket supplies a research lead, not permission for every contact channel. Before telephone outreach, classify the number, call purpose, technology, recipient relationship, and applicable federal and state restrictions. The TCPA, 47 U.S.C. section 227, and 47 CFR section 64.1200 distinguish calling and messaging practices; prerecorded or artificial-voice marketing and automated campaigns require particular scrutiny. A manually dialed call is not a universal exemption from do-not-call rules. Where applicable, scrub the National Do Not Call Registry on the required cycle, no longer than 31 days, maintain an entity-specific suppression list, and honor direct stop requests. Observe the federal calling window and any stricter state limits using the recipient's local time. Do not infer a mobile number from a relative's identity and label it executor permission. Commercial email must satisfy CAN-SPAM requirements, including accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, required sender identification, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out honored within ten business days. Direct mail avoids some telephone-specific restrictions but still cannot imply court sponsorship, invent urgency, or misstate the broker's role. Save list provenance and suppression results with each campaign version.

Use a script that identifies the commercial purpose without exploiting the death. For a permitted live call, say: 'Hello, I am Morgan with Harbor Estate Realty, a licensed brokerage. I am calling to offer property-sale coordination services, not on behalf of the court. Is this a suitable time for a brief explanation? If you prefer no further calls, I will record that now.' Replace the example identity with the actual licensed business before deployment. If the recipient agrees, continue: 'We help authorized estate representatives coordinate valuation, cleanout vendors, and a sale process with their attorney. Are you the appointed representative, and has counsel identified any restriction on selling the property?' For discovery, ask: 'What documents establish your authority, who handles estate legal work, and what court or title milestone must occur before a sale can close?' For resistance, say: 'Understood. I will stop this outreach and record your preference. You do not need to explain your decision.' Do not ask for beneficiary account numbers, tax identifiers, or an entire estate inventory during prospecting. If recording calls, check all applicable state recording-consent requirements before enabling the recorder; a CRM default is not an adequate legal determination.

Build the probate-attorney referral flywheel around useful work, not paid access to clients. For transactions within RESPA's coverage, 12 U.S.C. section 2607 prohibits specified referral payments and unearned fee splits involving settlement services. Paying an attorney $250 for each referred seller is not transformed into a legitimate service by labeling the invoice marketing. Actual services require a separate coverage and compensation analysis; bona fide compensation must not disguise payment for referrals. State licensing rules and attorney professional rules can impose additional restrictions outside RESPA's reach. Use this introduction: 'We provide licensed probate brokerage and documented property coordination. We do not pay for client referrals. With your client's permission, we can send a sample authority checklist and a scoped service proposal. Would those materials help your estate team?' For email, use the subject 'Probate property coordination services' and include the actual firm's postal address and a simple unsubscribe method. Keep attorney relationship notes separate from confidential client materials, and request only authorized introductions. Housing advertising and buyer screening must also respect 42 U.S.C. section 3604: do not steer buyers or tailor access using protected characteristics. Measure meetings and retained commissions, not vulnerable-household contact volume.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect trust before optimizing response rates**
> 
> Probate relationships compound when the brokerage reduces uncertainty without treating a family event as an urgency tactic.
> - Send the attorney a one-page authority dependency sheet listing the missing order, notice, or title item instead of requesting a vague status update.
> - Exclude already-listed properties and recorded stop requests before ordering direct mail; preserve the exclusion logic with the campaign export.
> - Bring cleanout vendors a written inventory and disposal authorization process so family keepsakes never become an undocumented hauling decision.
> - Track days from authority verification to listing separately from days from appointment to authority verification; only the first interval measures brokerage execution cleanly.

## 4. Underwrite HOA priority and tax-sale redemption as separate statutory interests  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The mapped play hoa_super_lien_tax_deed_redemption_arbitrage belongs to TRACK_1 and requires a separate acquisition file, ledger, and authority review. Its full name is HOA Super-Lien & Sub-Threshold Tax-Deed Statutory Redemption Arbitrage. Neither a small balance nor the label super-lien establishes transferable title. Nevada's NRS 116.3116 creates an association-lien framework with priority rules, while foreclosure procedures and their version-specific requirements require additional review. Florida offers a different example: Chapter 197 distinguishes tax certificates, redemption, and tax deeds rather than treating them as one interchangeable asset. 'Sub-threshold' is an operator screening label, not a nationwide statutory exemption. For each proposed purchase, identify exactly what is acquired, who can redeem, when redemption ends, what amounts are recoverable, and which interests may survive. Counsel must examine notice recipients and service evidence, including due-process requirements reflected in Mennonite Board of Missions v. Adams. Check federal liens separately; 26 U.S.C. section 7425 can affect federal lien survival and redemption after certain nonjudicial sales. A county receipt is evidence of a payment, not proof that every notice defect disappeared. Reject any seller presentation that equates a low purchase price with automatic extinguishment of a senior mortgage.

Use a deliberately conditional unit model to prevent statutory assumptions from becoming projected profit. Suppose counsel confirms that a particular interest can be acquired for $8,000 and that a timely redemption would return that principal plus $1,200 of lawfully recoverable amounts. Add $900 for title work, counsel, recording, and servicing, producing $8,900 total cash deployed. A $9,200 redemption receipt then produces only $300 surplus, approximately 3.37% of deployed cash, before overhead and taxes. The $1,200 is an assumed verified recovery for this example, not a quoted statutory rate or a universal premium. An additional $1,500 notice dispute turns the same receipt into a $1,200 loss. If redemption never occurs, do not replace the missing receipt with the property's assessed value; model the actual enforcement route, remaining liens, possession costs, and available exit evidence. For an HOA example, a claimed $12,000 balance may contain only a counsel-confirmed $3,000 priority component. An $8,000 bid justified by calling the entire balance senior would therefore rest on a false premise. These amounts are illustrative acquisition cash flows and do not increase the canonical $120,000, $510,000, or $900,000 retained brokerage revenue.

Set the bid ceiling from enforceable recovery after costs, not from a headline property discount. Using the redemption example, a required $1,000 surplus and $900 costs permit a maximum $7,300 purchase price against a supportable $9,200 receipt; increase reserves or lower the ceiling for timing and litigation risk. A seller demanding $8,000 fails that hurdle even before uncertainty discounts. Preserve the jurisdiction, statutory version, title search date, notice evidence, and counsel's assumptions with the bid memorandum. Do not use estate client deposits or escrow balances to fund operator acquisitions. If the opportunity involves an estate the brokerage serves, resolve the principal-buyer conflict before pursuing it; distressed timing does not excuse fiduciary scrutiny. Tyler v. Hennepin County, 598 U.S. 631 (2023), also cautions against assuming government may retain value beyond a tax debt without a compensation issue. It does not establish a universal purchaser refund. When priority, notice, or redemption cannot be verified, suspend bidding, seek available statutory refunds only where the specific grounds support them, and limit acquisitions to counsel-cleared interests. Track court delays, family disputes, and housing-market cycles as distinct risk drivers rather than hiding all three inside a single optimistic exit price.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify the asset and the operator's role
   - Identify whether the engagement is brokerage, concierge service, or a principal acquisition.
   - Record ownership, representative authority, local license requirements, and any conflict before collecting signatures or authorizing spend.
2. Assemble the statutory evidence packet
   - Collect the controlling orders, title evidence, notices, and jurisdiction-specific procedure analysis.
   - For HOA and tax-sale interests, add the statutory version, redemption calculation, lien survival analysis, and service evidence supporting enforceability.
3. Reconcile contracts, contact permissions, and unit economics
   - Approve the scoped agreement, permitted outreach channel, fee trigger, and transaction cash model.
   - Keep brokerage retention distinct from property value and acquisition principal; apply the supported bid ceiling rather than a promised property discount.
4. Release work only against documented conditions
   - Verify each required condition before listing, contacting, closing, or bidding.
   - Assign an owner and review date to unresolved items; suspend unsupported acquisitions and pursue refunds only when the applicable statute supplies a valid remedy.
