## 1. Days 1–7: Charter the Pod, Ring-Fence Capital, and Select One County  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start Day 1 by separating a launch decision from an investment return promise. Candidate #3 carries a $100,000–$1,500,000 startup capital range and a six-to-thirty-six-month time to first deal; this thirty-day sprint establishes capability rather than assuming an auction award or redemption. Map the work into four plays: auction screening, certificate bid-down acquisition, redemption tracking, and redeemable-deed or foreclosure disposition. These are distinct workflows, not interchangeable statutory products. Assign the full-time owner capital allocation, county communication, bidding, and ledger signoff. Contractor one performs parcel, title, and physical-access research; contractor two is locally qualified counsel responsible for procedure review and recovery estimates. Use contractors for bounded work orders, not fictional full-time coverage. Reserve forty owner hours and eight hours per contractor each week, with any additional hours requiring a revised budget. Day 2 produces a signed capital charter, task ownership sheet, and separate account structure. Day 3 produces a county selection memo comparing auction format, redemption mechanics, notice duties, and available records. Begin with one certificate jurisdiction; keep redeemable deeds in a separate research lane until their distinct vesting and redemption rules are documented. No parcel advances because its advertised rate looks attractive.

For a concrete pilot, assume $100,000 of dedicated capital, not borrowed household liquidity. Ring-fence $25,000 as reserve and limit the first authorized batch to $20,000 of all-in committed exposure. All-in exposure includes certificate principal, nonrefundable fees, committed subsequent taxes, and asset-specific recovery costs. Cap each parcel at $5,000 and each county at $20,000 throughout the pilot. Preserve the remaining capital for later reviewed batches; auction deposits count as restricted cash, not expenses unless forfeited. Set cumulative operating-cash ceilings of $3,000 at Day 30, $6,000 at Day 60, and $9,000 at Day 90. Allocate the first month's ceiling to $1,200 counsel, $800 parcel and title research, $300 records and software, $200 travel, and $500 contingency. Obtain fixed-scope quotes on Day 4; if local pricing exceeds these allowances, reduce the parcel batch rather than remove legal review. The ceiling excludes invested principal but includes external services and overhead. Owner labor still consumes capacity even when no salary leaves the account. Keep the $25,000 reserve floor after subtracting all commitments and the next ninety days of planned operating spending. That prevents an apparently affordable bid from consuming the recovery runway.

Days 5–7 convert public records into a reproducible underwriting dataset. Record the parcel identifier, legal description, auction identifier, owner of record, tax years, assessed classification, mapped access, delinquent balance, and source retrieval date. Download the county's bidder packet and results history, then retain immutable copies with SHA-256 hashes generated by Python hashlib. Use county GIS for boundaries, the recorder for recorded instruments, and FEMA's Map Service Center for flood exposure; none substitutes for a title examination or site-specific access assessment. Assemble thirty unique parcels and advance at least twelve to complete review. Mark apparent duplicates by legal description as well as parcel number because reassessments and splits can change identifiers. Contractor one checks whether a tiny strip, drainage tract, contaminated use, or inaccessible lot explains the delinquency. Counsel distinguishes certificate ownership from possession and documents which later proceeding could create marketable title. A suspected bankruptcy, unresolved ownership mismatch, or missing redemption rule blocks bidding immediately. Day 7 ends with an evidence-backed shortlist, not a purchase target. If fewer than twelve parcels survive, keep screening within the same spending ceiling instead of lowering the evidence standard.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Conditions Before Any Auction Deposit**
> 
> A deposit can create restricted cash and binding obligations. Complete all four checks before funding registration.
> - Confirm bidder identity, entity eligibility, deposit deadline, settlement deadline, and default consequences directly against the current county packet.
> - Have local counsel document the specific instrument, redemption calculation, expiration rules, notice responsibilities, and permitted recovery route.
> - Reconcile unrestricted cash after deposits, committed taxes, recovery allowances, and ninety-day spending against the $25,000 reserve floor.
> - Require matched parcel identifiers, completed title review, verified bid ceilings, and zero unresolved bankruptcy or ownership issues for every proposed bid.

## 2. Days 8–14: Price the Instrument, Stress Redemption, and Rehearse Bidding  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Day 8 builds the auction calculator from the selected county's actual mechanism: interest-rate bid-down, premium bidding, or another specified allocation method. Never assume an advertised maximum is the rate earned. Day 9 translates redemption rules into dated cash flows, including rounding, minimum charges, noninterest-bearing premiums, reimbursable expenses, and payment timing where applicable. Florida Statutes sections 197.432 and 197.472 illustrate why sale mechanics and redemption computation must be read together; a generic annual-rate spreadsheet cannot replace those rules. For a deliberately simplified certificate example, invest $4,000 principal and incur $400 of upfront, nonrecoverable costs. Redemption after twenty-four months at twelve percent simple annual interest yields $960 of interest and $560 after those costs. Dividing $560 by $4,400 and two years gives a 6.36% simple annualized planning yield. At fourteen percent, interest becomes $1,120 and the same calculation gives 8.18%. These are screening calculations, not internal rates of return or promises about a particular jurisdiction. Under this pilot's eight-percent stressed hurdle, the first bid fails and the second barely passes. Any additional unbudgeted cost can reverse that decision, so contractor quotations belong inside the calculator before the auction starts.

Day 10 tests early redemption, twenty-four-month redemption, and a thirty-six-month unresolved position with no assumed interest receipt. The last case is a liquidity test, not an invented right to collect forever. Model statutory accrual limits and expiration dates separately. Day 11 runs a recovery waterfall using conservative property proceeds, sale costs, taxes, title work, notice expenses, and amounts legally payable to other parties. For coverage screening, require independently supported net property proceeds before satisfying this position to exceed three times projected all-in exposure. Coverage between two and three times requires correction or a smaller bid; below two times kills the parcel. Coverage is a loss-severity proxy, not a right to retain the entire property surplus. Tyler v. Hennepin County, 598 U.S. 631 (2023), makes excess-value assumptions especially dangerous in tax foreclosure analysis. Counsel must map the governing distribution procedure rather than credit the investor with every dollar of equity. Bankruptcy protection under 11 U.S.C. section 362 can interrupt collection and enforcement; a search hit triggers case-specific review rather than an automated notice or filing. Record the worst-case additional cash demand and whether the reserve can actually fund it without selling another position.

Days 12–14 convert the model into an executable bidding discipline. Run twenty dry-run bids spanning early redemption, reduced rates, premium erosion, parcel withdrawal, competing bids, settlement failure, and duplicate records. Require all twenty expected outputs to reconcile, with currency differences no greater than one cent against independently calculated fixtures. Keep an approved maximum principal and a minimum acceptable rate on each bid ticket; premium auctions need an explicit maximum premium instead. The owner enters bids while the research contractor checks the ticket and resulting exposure. Counsel does not become an auction clerk; their limited hours belong to exceptions and procedure. Use the county-designated portal, whether Realauction, Grant Street Group, or another provider, only under the county's published bidder rules. Do not scrape authenticated areas or automate submission without permission. A spreadsheet with protected formulas and a second-person readback can outperform fragile browser automation at this scale. Day 14 ends with twelve fully reviewed parcels, at least three economically qualified candidates, and a locked bid file. If market bidding pushes every candidate below the hurdle, record a valid no-bid result. Winning nothing is preferable to purchasing a rate that cannot cover the actual work.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Practitioner Tactics That Protect the Bid Before the Bid**
> 
> Protect the economics at the parcel level. County averages and advertised rates conceal the work that consumes returns.
> - Compare the auction legal description against the recorder, not just the assessor address; vacant strips and split parcels can share misleading location labels.
> - Keep the latest withdrawal list beside the bid ticket and recheck immediately before submission; a redeemed or withdrawn item should never survive on a stale shortlist.
> - Ask the county which redemption amounts are remitted to certificate holders and which fees remain with the county; model the actual holder receipt.
> - Record the losing rate or premium on qualified no-bids; repeated market-clearing failures reveal a county-selection problem before capital is trapped.

## 3. Days 15–21: Operate the Ledger and Rehearse the Recovery Handoff  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Day 15 registers the approved bidder and verifies funding instructions through an independently obtained county contact. An emailed account change never authorizes a transfer by itself. If the auction calendar falls outside the sprint, complete registration research and a sandbox rehearsal without manufacturing a purchase deadline. Day 16 reconciles deposits, restricted cash, available cash, and commitments. Day 17 conducts the permitted auction session or repeats the dry run against published historical results. The owner alone releases bids, and every award must match a previously reviewed ticket. Day 18 verifies the certificate or deed record, payment receipt, ownership registration, parcel identity, and applicable start dates. A bid acknowledgment is not sufficient evidence of the acquired interest. Keep original county receipts alongside a normalized CSV register maintained with Python csv and decimal; SQLite can hold the transaction history without introducing a paid platform. Record who entered and who reviewed each change. For the three-person pod, the research contractor performs the second check while counsel handles legal exceptions. If settlement instructions cannot be authenticated or an award differs from the approved parcel, freeze additional activity and contact the county promptly. Do not quietly substitute a nearby parcel to preserve the appearance of sprint completion.

Day 19 builds the redemption tracker around events rather than optimistic accruals. Store acquisition date, statutory redemption dates, certificate expiration, subsequent-tax choices, notice prerequisites, and recovery eligibility separately. Use thirty-day and seven-day internal reminders where the actual procedure permits, but calendar statutory service and filing deadlines exactly; internal reminders never extend them. Test five events: full redemption, partial county remittance, incorrect parcel allocation, upcoming expiration, and a bankruptcy flag. Every event must route to an owner and preserve source evidence. Day 20 reconciles a mock $4,600 receipt composed of $4,000 returned principal and $600 realized interest. The operating dashboard reports $600 revenue, not $4,600, while the principal ledger reduces the asset balance by $4,000. Penalties count only when legally payable to this holder and realized under the dashboard's stated basis. Disposition revenue is the realized gain after the relevant asset basis, not gross property sale proceeds; separately tracked expenses must not be deducted twice. Maintain unrealized accruals in a distinct forecast column. A county redemption notice without cleared funds updates status but does not create spendable cash or prove the annual scorecard economics.

Day 21 completes the fourth play's recovery handoff without initiating premature enforcement. A certificate position and a Texas redeemable deed require different instructions: Texas Tax Code section 34.21 describes redemption rights and redemption amounts, not a universal certificate coupon. Counsel produces separate decision trees identifying the eligible initiating party, necessary searches, parties requiring notice, waiting periods, service evidence, and distribution rules. Jones v. Flowers, 547 U.S. 220 (2006), illustrates why returned notice can require additional reasonable steps when practicable; a mailing log alone may not finish the task. Federal interests can add requirements under 26 U.S.C. section 7425, so flag federal liens for specific review rather than assuming every tax sale eliminates them. Obtain a capped estimate for the next procedural stage and a separate estimate for contested work. Do not initiate owner contact, possession activity, or foreclosure through an unreviewed automated sequence. For every held position, name the next lawful action, its earliest permissible date, its cost, and its evidence requirement. The sprint succeeds when the pod can maintain a dormant position accurately for months, not when it can generate aggressive letters in minutes. A clean handoff preserves both capital and procedural options.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture the originating county event
   - Save the official notice, remittance detail, or position update.
   - Attach parcel and instrument identifiers, retrieval date, original file, and the responsible owner before changing the register.
2. Classify cash and procedural consequences
   - Separate principal, realized holder income, reimbursed costs, and unresolved differences.
   - Update deadlines independently from cash recognition; a status notice does not establish cleared funds or authority to enforce.
3. Obtain the appropriate second review
   - Route arithmetic to the research contractor and legal exceptions to counsel.
   - Require one-cent cash reconciliation and documented resolution of bankruptcy, notice, ownership, and distribution questions.
4. Close the event with an auditable next action
   - Record the reviewer, supporting evidence, next deadline, and remaining commitment.
   - Release capital only after cleared receipts reconcile; preserve the prior register state and retain unresolved exceptions in the owner queue.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Reviews at 60/90: Kill Defects, Fix Friction, Scale Evidence  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Day 22 audits every reviewed parcel against its original sources and closes duplicate identifiers. Day 23 reruns the twenty calculator fixtures after any changes. Day 24 rechecks county withdrawals, remittance status, and bankruptcy exceptions. Day 25 obtains counsel's written confirmation that every live deadline and contemplated next action matches the selected instrument. Day 26 reconciles bank cash, restricted deposits, principal, realized receipts, and unpaid commitments. Day 27 measures throughput using completed files rather than downloaded records: target no more than ninety owner minutes and $150 of repeatable external screening cost per fully reviewed parcel. Allocate recurring research costs across all completed files, including rejected ones; identify initial procedure-design costs separately. Day 28 tests the thirty-six-month no-receipt case and revalidates the reserve floor. Day 29 assembles a decision packet containing the shortlist, no-bid log, cash bridge, exception register, and next sixty-day calendar. Day 30 holds a forty-five-minute review with both contractors. Record Kill, Fix, or Scale for the operating lane and separately for each parcel. No completed auction is required when the calendar prevents one. A complete rehearsal can justify maintaining readiness, but cannot establish actual recovery speed or realized investment yield.

Apply decisions in order: any Kill condition overrides all positive indicators; Scale requires every listed prerequisite; everything else becomes Fix with an owner and a fourteen-day correction deadline. Kill means stop new commitments in the affected lane, preserve records, and maintain existing positions lawfully. It does not mean abandon notices, surrender value, or force an immediate sale. Hard stops include an unresolvable legal right, coverage below two times, an unauthorized bid, or unrestricted reserves below $25,000 after commitments and planned spending. Operating-cash ceilings are $3,000, $6,000, and $9,000 at the three reviews; unauthorized overspending pauses new deployment until a corrected charter is expressly adopted. Day 30 Scale authorizes only the initial $20,000 aggregate exposure ceiling. Day 60 can raise it to $35,000, and Day 90 to $50,000, provided evidence and capacity tests pass. The $20,000 county concentration cap remains, so higher aggregate deployment requires a separately reviewed second or third county rather than silent concentration. All limits include existing holdings and commitments, not just the next auction's bids. If suitable auctions or qualified inventory are absent, retain the current cap and use Fix to continue bounded research without forcing a purchase.

Keep the scorecard separate from this pilot's milestone dashboard. Conservative annual revenue is $15,000 with $10,500 implied costs and $4,500 owner profit. Base revenue is $97,500 with $56,062.50 implied costs and $41,437.50 profit, displayed as $41,438 at a 42.5% margin. Scaled revenue is $180,000 with $81,000 implied costs and $99,000 profit at 55%. These are annual realized-interest, penalty, and disposition-gain cases; neither principal recovery nor auction spending increases revenue. The $3,000 monthly sprint ceiling is a spending authorization, not a forecast of perpetual costs. Spending that amount every month would exceed the Conservative case's entire annual cost allowance, so Day 90 must approve a reconciled twelve-month cost plan before continuation. Keep the launch pod at one full-time owner plus two contractors. The Scaled scorecard assumes two full-time people plus three contractors and cannot be claimed merely because the pilot passes Day 90. Add capacity only after a funded workload plan supports it. Treat no-redemption positions as unresolved evidence, not failures by default: the stated six-to-thirty-six-month deal horizon makes ninety-day profit extrapolation unreliable. Scale reviewed deployment capacity first; claim economic validation only when realized cohort results actually support the proposed expansion.
