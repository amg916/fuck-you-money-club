## 1. Price the enforceable cash claim before pricing the certificate  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with the revenue denominator, because tax receivable face value is not operator sales. The canonical Conservative, Base, and Scaled cases recognize $15,000, $97,500, and $180,000 of realized interest, penalties, and disposition gains. Returned certificate principal remains a balance-sheet recovery. For a transparent illustrative cohort, assume twenty $5,000 positions, one hundred $6,500 positions, and one hundred fifty $8,000 positions, respectively. Those cohorts deploy $100,000, $650,000, and $1,200,000 and require average realized revenue of $750, $975, and $1,200 per position to reproduce the scorecard. The resulting 15% revenue-to-deployed-principal ratio is a modeling bridge, not a statutory rate or a promised annualized return. Each jurisdiction, purchase date, redemption date, bid rate, premium, and recovery route must support its own dollars. A portfolio generating only 8% cannot retain the 15% revenue assumption by relabeling principal distributions. Keep four operating lenses distinct: auction bid-down discipline, redemption-ladder construction, redeemable-deed recovery underwriting, and the separately identified agency rewards play. The first three describe this chapter's asset workflows rather than additional catalog identifiers. The fourth concerns advertising payment economics and contributes nothing to the canonical lien revenue cases. This separation prevents an attractive side business from disguising weak underlying asset returns.

For auction bid-down discipline, calculate collectible dollars after the actual winning bid, not after the advertised maximum. Florida Statutes 197.172 and 197.432 establish interest and bidding mechanics; section 197.472 controls redemption amounts and the minimum-interest mechanism, including its zero-percent exception. A $5,000 certificate at a winning 6% simple rate held six months produces $150 of ordinary interest before the applicable statutory minimum and any collectible charges. A 12% bid would produce $300 over the same interval. That $150 difference is economically material against a Conservative target of $225 owner profit per position. Do not extrapolate Florida's minimum to Arizona certificates or Texas redeemable deeds. Arizona Revised Statutes 42-18114 sets its own bid-rate framework; Texas Tax Code 34.21 supplies redemption-premium rules with property classifications and deadlines that require separate modeling. Any auction premium, subsequent tax payment, filing advance, or service charge belongs in invested cash until its recoverability is established. Compute a dated cash-flow return using actual settlement and receipt dates in Excel or LibreOffice, alongside the simple dollar spread. If competitive bidding removes the spread, the fallback is uncommitted cash or another eligible auction, not a speculative assumption that foreclosure will rescue an overbid.

Redemption-ladder construction is the second asset play: stagger maturities and collection expectations instead of purchasing an entire year's exposure in one county sale. Ten $6,500 Base positions represent $65,000 of principal and $9,750 of modeled revenue; shifting their collections beyond year-end removes that revenue from the realized-year forecast while leaving the principal locked. The third asset play, redeemable-deed recovery underwriting, requires a different waterfall. A hypothetical $20,000 purchase with a legally applicable 25% first-period redemption premium would produce $5,000 of gross premium before eligible advances and expenses; it does not establish a repeatable annual yield or immediate possession right. Classify the property and redemption period under Texas Tax Code 34.21 before bidding. If redemption fails, price title work, possession proceedings, carrying taxes, insurance, remediation, and sale costs before forecasting disposition gain. Tyler v. Hennepin County rejects treating surplus equity as automatically confiscable by government; implement the applicable state's current sale and surplus-distribution procedure rather than assuming every dollar of property value belongs to the certificate holder. Where collectible recovery cannot be established, the fallback is a lower bid or exclusion. A lender's optimistic valuation cannot cure defective notice, impaired title, or a legally unavailable recovery theory.

## 2. Reconcile every acquisition, delivery, financing, and reserve deduction  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the gross-to-net bridge with six explicit expense buckets rather than one opaque management percentage. Acquisition and screening consume $1,500, $7,500, and $12,000 across Conservative, Base, and Scaled. These allocations explain the supplied scorecard; they are illustrative budget assignments, not measured results from an existing portfolio. For this managerial waterfall, reserves are explicit economic charges for expected leakage; statutory accounts and tax returns require their own recognition treatment. Keep returned principal outside both the revenue numerator and ordinary operating deductions. Adding recovered principal to sales would inflate margins and make the business appear larger without creating a dollar of additional earnings.

Acquisition CAC here means cost per funded asset, not consumer marketing cost per lead. The three budgets imply $75, $75, and $80 per funded position. A Base screening funnel of 1,000 parcels, 250 document reviews, and 100 purchases converts 10% of initial records into funded assets; its $7,500 budget therefore equals $7.50 per initial parcel but still $75 per purchase. Their work includes redemption tracking, document indexing, payment reconciliation, and exception follow-up; the separate $10,000 title and recovery bucket pays specialist work without duplicating those hours. The canonical teams remain 1 FT + 2 C, 1 FT + 2 C, and 2 FT + 3 C. Headcount alone supplies neither salaries nor full-time-equivalent workload. This bridge leaves employee payroll and owner replacement salary unpriced because the scorecard supplies no compensation schedule. Consequently, the Scaled $99,000 is not demonstrated profit after two market-rate salaries. Budgeting an additional $60,000 of wages and employer costs would reduce that figure to $39,000. Display that limitation directly rather than presenting five people as an automatically affordable staffing plan.

Scrub reserves cover purchased-record defects, mistaken eligibility, duplicate payments, and nonrecoverable diligence; reversal reserves cover corrected county allocations, voided certificates, and recovery shortfalls. The Base $10,062.50 charge could be allocated analytically as $2,000 record scrub, $3,000 interest or fee reversals, and $5,062.50 incremental principal impairment. Those categories are mutually exclusive. If a county refunds $6,500 of principal and reverses $975 of expected interest, the principal refund is not revenue and the uncollected interest is not both a revenue reduction and another full reserve charge. Maintain a reserve rollforward showing opening allowance, new provision, realized usage, releases, and closing allowance. Reconcile the managerial bridge to cash collections and formal accounts each month so that a gross realization convention does not silently change between periods. Illustrative fixed monthly overhead envelopes of $500, $2,000, and $4,000 sit within the supplied range and are included in the annual buckets, never added again. Variable contractor work, exceptional recovery costs, and reserve charges explain why total annual deductions can exceed twelve times fixed overhead. Set replenishment cash apart from the earnings reserve: an expense estimate measures loss, while a funded buffer determines whether the operation survives the wait.

## 3. Fund redemption delays without confusing profit with spendable cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat cash timing as two clocks, not one blended DSO statistic. The first clock runs from auction settlement to the legal event creating a payable redemption distribution; the second runs from that event to cleared funds in the investor's bank account. Certificates are purchased assets, not ordinary invoices, so invoice DSO is a poor label for the first interval. Track asset holding days and distribution-processing days separately. For planning, a 270-day holding period followed by a 30-day processing allowance creates a 300-day cash cycle; neither duration is a county promise. A Base $6,500 position then ties up its purchase cash throughout that interval even if the ledger shows an enforceable claim. Obtain the county's published disbursement process and reconcile actual electronic transfers or checks to it. A redemption notice is not cleared cash, a check issued is not a settled deposit, and an auction deposit refund is not investment income. Use an asset register in SQLite or a controlled spreadsheet with purchase dates, legal milestones, expected distributions, bank dates, and disputed balances. Flag deadline exceptions separately from routine payment delays so a collection forecast never substitutes for preserving a legal right.

Size incremental float from the purchase cadence the operator actually intends to maintain. Assume annual replacement purchases equal the illustrative $100,000, $650,000, and $1,200,000 deployment budgets, spread evenly across 365 days. An additional 90-day collection delay requires approximately $24,658, $160,274, and $295,890 of purchase float if buying continues unchanged. Excluding the noncash economic provisions, annual modeled cash deductions are $8,500, $46,000, and $70,000. Ninety days of those payments adds approximately $2,096, $11,342, and $17,260. Total incremental delay liquidity is therefore approximately $26,753, $171,616, and $313,151, using unrounded components before final rounding. These are bridge requirements, not additional annual expenses and not automatic additions when purchases can be paused. They also assume even settlement activity; seasonal auctions require a dated cash schedule rather than a daily average. At Scaled, $1,200,000 already deployed plus the full incremental bridge reaches roughly $1,513,151, exceeding the stated $1,500,000 startup ceiling before any separate initial liquidity buffer. The practical fallback is smaller commitments or slower replacement purchases, not silently expanding startup capital. A committed credit facility only solves the gap if its collateral eligibility, advance rate, and maturity survive the same delay.

Use a thirteen-week direct cash forecast for near-term control and a thirty-six-month asset schedule for the stated 6–36 month time to first deal. At the illustrative fixed overhead envelopes, six months without receipts consumes $3,000, $12,000, or $24,000; thirty-six months consumes $18,000, $72,000, or $144,000 before purchase funding and variable recovery work. Those figures are runway uses already represented by overhead when the corresponding period is modeled, not fresh deductions from the annual scorecard. Record auction deposits, settlement deadlines, subsequent taxes, filing advances, debt service, and contractor invoices by payment date. Separate restricted deposits from bank cash available for ordinary bills. For a deliberately simple payout-lock example, $100,000 borrowed at 12% simple annual interest for 30 extra days costs about $986 using a 365-day basis, before facility fees. Add that cost only to the extent it exceeds financing already budgeted. Bankruptcy can interrupt enforcement through 11 U.S.C. 362; route affected assets into a separately reviewed collection schedule rather than assuming ordinary foreclosure dates continue. Suspend owner distributions whenever available cash falls below the dated purchase commitments, near-term operating obligations, and documented recovery buffer. Accounting profit cannot pay a contractor while the underlying redemption remains unsettled.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the dated asset cash register
   - Record principal, premium, advances, rate, settlement date, and expected collection milestones for every certificate or deed.
   - Maintain unique county and parcel identifiers, link auction evidence, and separate enforceable income claims from purchase-basis recovery.
2. Reconcile collectible proceeds to bank cash
   - Match county redemption reports to cleared deposits and allocate each receipt between principal, interest, penalties, and recoverable advances.
   - Place unmatched amounts in an exception register; do not classify an entire redemption transfer as revenue.
3. Rebuild the delay bridge each week
   - Refresh thirteen-week payments and thirty-six-month asset collections, then run an additional ninety-day delay against unchanged purchase commitments.
   - Separate cash expenses from economic reserve charges and verify that restricted deposits cannot be used to pay ordinary bills.
4. Release purchases and distributions from verified liquidity
   - Fund dated obligations and recovery buffers before making new bids or distributing owner cash.
   - Reduce commitments if the stress bridge exceeds available capital; retain the revised bid schedule and the person responsible for each exception.

## 4. Stress the yield, isolate agency rewards, and enforce preflight checks  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run sensitivities against the canonical cases without changing several assumptions at once. A two-percentage-point decline in realized revenue relative to deployed principal removes $2,000, $13,000, and $24,000, leaving owner profit of $2,500, $28,437.50, and $75,000 when modeled deductions stay fixed. These are portfolio revenue shocks, not statements that a statutory coupon has fallen by two points. A separate 25% shift of current-year realized revenue into the following year removes $3,750, $24,375, and $45,000, leaving $750, $17,062.50, and $54,000. That timing case preserves lifetime revenue only if eventual collection occurs, and it ignores additional borrowing costs until they are explicitly added. A further 1% principal loss beyond existing reserves costs $1,000, $6,500, and $12,000, leaving $3,500, $34,937.50, and $87,000. Label that loss incremental to avoid charging the existing impairment provision twice. Finally, a 25% contractor-delivery overrun removes $750, $4,500, and $7,000. Compare these sensitivities to concentration by county, property type, and redemption vintage. A small portfolio can lose its entire annual profit on one defective position, even when its average modeled yield appears comfortably above cash operating costs.

The required adjacent play, agency_ad_spend_card_multiplier_rebate_cycling, belongs in a separate operating ledger and must never be presented as certificate yield. Route only genuine, client-authorized advertising spend through eligible arrangements whose issuer, network, agency, and client contracts permit the rewards and rebates. Consider $100,000 of authorized monthly spend earning an illustrative 2% cash-equivalent reward and 0.5% volume rebate: gross benefit is $2,500. Deduct $800 of processing, $600 of financing, and $700 owed to the client, leaving $400 before agency servicing labor and overhead. A further $300 of reconciliation labor leaves $100. If the $500 rebate disappears, the same month loses $400 after that labor. Reward rates and redemption values must come from actual eligible terms, not aspirational point valuations. The $100,000 client advertising outlay is neither lien revenue nor automatically agency revenue; account for the underlying service arrangement separately. At $100,000 monthly spend, a fifteen-day mismatch between platform payment and client reimbursement creates about $50,000 of float using a thirty-day month. Never fund that gap with cash reserved for certificate settlement. The fallback is client-direct billing or prefunded approved agency terms, with rewards-dependent spending stopped rather than expanded.

Third, tie the purchase schedule and contractor statements of work to the cash forecast, distinguishing the supplied headcount from a funded compensation plan. Fourth, reconcile the three scenario waterfalls and rerun delay, yield, loss, and labor sensitivities before authorizing the next batch. A Base batch of ten assets consumes $65,000 of purchase principal plus its associated diligence and operating cash; its modeled $9,750 revenue cannot finance settlement before collection. Record who owns each deadline and who independently reviews payment allocations. When the legal recovery route, cash bridge, or bid economics fails verification, reduce or reject the batch and preserve the rejected assumptions for later comparison. Keep any agency rewards experiment outside portfolio funding and performance reporting. The objective is a repeatable, auditable spread after real costs, not the largest auction invoice or the highest interest rate displayed in a sales presentation.
