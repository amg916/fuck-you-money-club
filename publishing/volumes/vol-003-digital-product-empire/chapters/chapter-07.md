## Package one repeatable outcome in a ten-day production sprint  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a domain SOP that already produces a specific artifact: a reconciled project budget, a qualified prospect brief, or a client handoff packet. Interview the operator while they execute it, then capture inputs, decisions, exceptions, and acceptance criteria. Remove client identifiers and confirm rights to every example, image, font, and recording before repackaging. Define the buyer's first win as an observable output, not watching a lesson. For a project-budget system, the win is entering three costs and receiving a usable margin calculation. Fix the scope on day one: one buyer, one recurring task, one output, and one supported software configuration. The ten-day constraint applies to this bounded system, not an entire professional curriculum. Assign one full-time product owner and three contractors covering domain editing, asset design, and checkout engineering, with explicit acceptance criteria for each handoff.

Create a source-of-truth manifest before designing attractive pages. Give each module an identifier, version, prerequisite, input schema, expected output, owner, and license scope. The Notion workspace supplies navigation and task state; Sheets performs calculations; Figma supplies editable presentation components; the PDF gives a portable reference; short videos demonstrate the same workflow. These are coordinated interfaces to one method, not five conflicting products. Keep sample data consistent across formats and distinguish fictional demonstrations from customer evidence. Protect formula ranges while leaving intended inputs editable, and test duplicate-template permissions from an account outside the production workspace. Publish a compatibility note covering required accounts and paid software features. Export a recovery copy of essential instructions so a vendor outage cannot erase the customer's entire purchase. Version the manifest and record which release every order receives.

Use days two through four for source cleanup and functional asset construction, days five and six for instructional recording and design, and days seven through nine for checkout, fulfillment, and novice testing. Day ten is release and reconciliation, not a rescue day for unfinished content. Ask three representative novices to perform the first task without narration from the creator. Record elapsed time, abandoned steps, formula errors, and questions; a beautiful template that needs twenty minutes of explanation has failed the fifteen-minute objective. Replace optional sophistication with working defaults and a populated example. Freeze a release candidate before testing so feedback refers to the same files. The product owner accepts each module against its declared output, while contractors fix their own defects. Delay an unsupported module rather than shipping a broken promise, and state the narrower included scope on the offer page.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Extract the method
   - Document one proven SOP on days 1-2.
   - Capture input, output, exceptions, asset rights, and the first-win acceptance test.
2. Build modular assets
   - Produce functional Notion, Sheets, and Figma modules on days 3-4.
   - Use shared sample data, stable module identifiers, and explicit editing permissions.
3. Explain the workflow
   - Finish the PDF and short videos on days 5-6.
   - Demonstrate the tested release rather than a designer's unfinished mockup.
4. Wire and test
   - Exercise checkout and delivery on days 7-9.
   - Test declines, duplicate events, delayed events, refunds, permissions, and novice activation.
5. Release and reconcile
   - Publish the bounded system on day 10.
   - Match payments to entitlements and assign every unresolved delivery incident.

## Wire explicit checkout choices and reconcile the actual contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use a two-step checkout to separate offer identification from payment without concealing the final amount. Step one captures the buyer's email and chosen license; step two displays the complete order, applicable tax, renewal terms when relevant, and payment controls. An optional order bump should solve the next immediate problem, such as a populated industry example, and remain unselected until the buyer chooses it. Following successful payment, offer a one-click post-purchase upsell only through a processor-supported saved-payment flow with explicit purchase authorization and any required authentication. Show its incremental price and whether it creates a separate subscription. Declining must preserve the original purchase and proceed directly to delivery. Give every base order, bump, and upsell separate line-item identifiers and entitlement mappings. Test a failed upsell independently: it must neither revoke the base purchase nor grant the unpaid extension.

Model a checkout cohort before increasing traffic. Suppose one hundred buyers purchase a $99 core product, twenty explicitly add a $29 bump, and eight buy a $149 upsell. Gross billings excluding pass-through sales tax are $11,672, or $116.72 per base buyer. An illustrative ten percent merchant-of-record and payment allowance consumes $1,167.20, a five percent refund and dispute allowance consumes $583.60, acquisition costs $2,500, and delivery plus variable support costs $700. That leaves $6,721.20 of order contribution before allocated production and team costs. These are planning assumptions, not quoted provider rates or observed conversion benchmarks. Reconcile fees on refunded transactions and any reserves separately from eventual expense; a withheld payout is not automatically lost revenue. Attribute spend to the original cohort and measure margin after the refund window instead of congratulating the team on checkout-day cash receipts.

For annual planning, hold the staffing label at 1 FT + 3 C while changing contractor hours, automation leverage, and acquisition efficiency explicitly. Conservative means $50,000 gross billings at twenty percent net contribution, or $10,000; Base means $425,000 at 37.5 percent, or $159,375; Scaled means $800,000 at fifty-five percent, or $440,000. The corresponding total modeled costs are $40,000, $265,625, and $360,000. Treat net contribution here as the residual after modeled operating costs, including a stated labor allowance, but before income taxes; it is not automatically distributable cash. Conservative cannot fund a normally paid full-time owner plus three substantial contractor commitments within that cost envelope. It requires disclosed founder subsidy, limited contractor hours, or different economics. Scaled likewise needs evidence that support and acquisition costs improve rather than assuming digital delivery makes labor disappear. Include deferred subscription obligations in the cash plan.

## Deliver through verified events, signed tokens, and usable evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the payment webhook as an authenticated event, not a command to trust every field it carries. Verify the provider signature against the exact raw body, enforce its timestamp tolerance, and accept only configured accounts and relevant event types. Persist the event identifier and work item durably before acknowledging receipt. A worker checks authoritative payment status, amount, currency, product mapping, and customer linkage, then grants entitlements with a unique order-line key. Duplicate deliveries must return the existing result; an older event must not resurrect access revoked by a refund. Apply versioned state transitions or recheck provider state when ordering is uncertain. Retry transient failures with bounded backoff, retain failed work for intervention, and reconcile paid orders against delivered entitlements daily. The browser return page displays pending delivery and polls status; it never constitutes proof that payment settled successfully.

Issue cryptographically signed download and license tokens only after the entitlement transaction commits. Include an opaque subject, entitlement identifier, asset version, audience, issuance time, expiry, and unique token identifier; avoid embedding email addresses or payment details. Use a standard maintained signing library, a fixed algorithm allowlist, protected keys, and rotation identifiers. Signing prevents tampering but does not hide the payload or prevent forwarding. Exchange a short-lived download token for a brief storage URL, and check current entitlement state before that exchange. A durable license record lives server-side rather than depending on an endlessly valid bearer link. Bind workspace access to an authenticated account and provide a controlled recovery path when the purchaser used another email. Record key rotation and token redemption events. Revocation can stop future access but cannot retrieve a file already downloaded, so license terms and product design still matter.

Build Visa CE 3.0 usage receipts as structured evidence records, not a badge claiming automatic dispute protection. Retain the order, terms version, fulfillment time, authenticated account identifier, asset version, relevant IP or device evidence, and successful usage events with access controls and a documented retention purpose. CE 3.0 generally concerns qualifying card-absent fraud disputes and requires two qualifying prior undisputed transactions on the same payment credential, typically within the 120-to-365-day lookback, plus prescribed matching data, including an IP address or device identifier. Eligibility, exclusions, and submission requirements belong to the current network and processor program; confirm them before relying on a packet. A new buyer's download alone cannot satisfy that history requirement. Keep raw card data out of the application and use processor references. Submit truthful delivery evidence for other disputes without relabeling it CE 3.0 evidence or claiming that usage eliminates legitimate refund rights.

## Run five first-win emails and measure refunds without hiding exits  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Make the five-email First-Win in 15 Minutes sequence a branching assistance system rather than five generic reminders. Email one arrives after verified fulfillment and links directly to the purchased workspace, sample dataset, and a three-step first task. Email two, two hours later, addresses the most common setup failure; buyers who already finished receive a useful shortcut instead. Email three, on day one, helps interpret the first output and flags the most frequent calculation mistake. Email four, on day three, shows how to repeat the workflow with the buyer's own inputs. Email five, on day seven, reviews the saved result, explains ongoing use, and offers direct support plus the applicable refund or cancellation route. Keep each email focused on one action. Suppress obsolete messages after completion, refunds, or cancellation, and do not turn product help into an undisclosed recurring sales sequence.

Treat cuts refund requests in half as a testable target, not copy earned by installing an email scheduler. Define the baseline as unique refund-requesting buyers divided by paid buyers within thirty days, with matched product, channel, price, and purchase period. An illustrative control cohort with forty requests among five hundred buyers has an eight percent request rate; twenty among five hundred treated buyers gives four percent, a fifty percent relative reduction and four percentage points absolute. Randomize when practical, wait for both cohorts to mature, and examine uncertainty before attributing causality. Track completed refunds, disputes, activation, and support minutes alongside requests so an inaccessible help address cannot masquerade as improvement. Record first-win completion as a meaningful saved output, not an email open or link click. Investigate whether low refund rates reflect satisfied customers, a changed acquisition mix, or buyers unable to find assistance.

Wire recurring offers to the applicable rules rather than assuming a merchant of record absorbs every seller obligation. For covered online negative-option transactions, 15 U.S.C. section 8403 requires clear material terms before obtaining billing information, express informed consent before charging, and a simple mechanism to stop recurring charges. Distinguish access and support messages from commercial email under 15 U.S.C. section 7704; commercial messages need accurate routing and subject information, applicable advertising identification, a postal address, and a functioning opt-out honored within ten business days. Do not classify promotional upsells as transactional merely because the recipient purchased before. Read 16 CFR Part 425 for its applicable negative-option scope; the expanded 2024 rule was vacated in 2025, so do not describe its click-to-cancel amendments as operative nationwide law. Verify current text and applicable state requirements before launch. Keep cancellation, refunds, entitlement changes, and email suppression synchronized without erasing necessary purchase records.
