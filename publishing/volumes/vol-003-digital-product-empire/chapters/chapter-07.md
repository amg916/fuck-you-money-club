# Chapter 07: Build a Self-Liquidating Offer Funnel

*Low-ticket entry offers, ad-cost recovery, paid email-list growth, HighLevel builds, affiliate monetization, and coaching or agency funnel examples*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

The practitioners converge on a buyer-acquisition engine: sell a small, relevant result, recover acquisition spending through the offer stack, then sell the next useful product or service. Pete McPherson packages tools, systems, and strategy; Jonathan Montoya compares average cart value against customer acquisition cost; Pam Obasa adds a private invitation into coaching. The implementation can use HighLevel, Funnel Freedom, or system.io, with checkout bumps, upsells, delivery, and email follow-up. The disagreements matter. Mike at Sell Your Service reserves SLO for a customer-to-affiliate model paying full front-end commissions. Daniel Fazio demands immediate cash recovery, while Solo Marketer Funnels allows several days. Refund policies, entry prices, advertising budgets, and scaling methods also diverge. Failed trials, stalled ascension, broken checkout connections, and ad-account shutdowns expose the operational limits.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Buy the customer without leaving the acquisition bill hanging over the business. Pete McPherson describes spending on audience growth but sending that reach toward a specific offer stack rather than only a free opt-in. Breaking even lets that spending build the audience; a little front-end profit is welcome, but the stack exists to liquidate the advertising cost [wYKYVjEiqLY @ 00:00:18]. Jonathan Montoya makes the operating comparison explicit: cost per acquisition belongs beside average cart value, not beside the price of the cheapest item. Their $5 challenge can lead into order bumps, upsells, and downsells, so the customer may spend substantially more across the funnel than the opening price suggests [lJzDn-Qma9o @ 00:04:50]. Daniel Fazio applies the same cash-flow objective to larger setup offers: their reported acquisition cost is approximately $2,000 to $2,500 against about $2,500 collected initially, with monthly subscriptions following afterward [By4MPc6yyFA @ 00:05:13]. Nick Tsai explains the simpler opt-in version with an illustrative purchase: after $100 in advertising, one $100 offer sale recovers that spending. The subscriber encounters the paid offer immediately instead of waiting through a long nurture period before seeing anything to buy [Mw5p6Dsd_y8 @ 00:01:12].

The entry product must point toward the customer you actually want, not merely toward somebody willing to buy something cheap. Mateo specifies three requirements: solve one specific problem, make the product quickly consumable, and attract the ideal customer for the high-ticket offer. The product is supposed to produce a result quickly enough that the buyer can move forward [Q4J9fiMh3C0 @ 00:00:35]. Max Auren describes the same connection through the advertising promise: the transformation shown in the ad must closely relate to what the business will sell later. Their landing page repeats that promise and shows the product again, rather than making the visitor discover a different offer after clicking [kmRwEjvxk-w @ 00:03:12]. Matt from Growbo calls this alignment. A lead magnet about decorated coffee mugs does not prepare somebody to buy computer blue-light glasses; the initial interest and eventual offer need to belong together [ZWpExzy5MUg @ 00:09:53]. Pete makes that connection concrete with content production: a tool helps create content faster, the bump explains the writing process, and the later training addresses what happens after publication. The products either tackle the same pain point or follow a logical sequence of problems [wYKYVjEiqLY @ 00:19:08].

The front end opens the relationship; the next purchase gives the system its larger commercial purpose. Pam Obasa separates ordinary leads from buyers because even a small purchase identifies somebody who has paid for help. Their funnel then moves beyond an introductory offer and checkout toward an upsell and a private invitation to discuss coaching [Kgj8rFDeRIs @ 00:05:37]. Jonathan Montoya gives the backend several forms: a challenge, webinar, or sales team can sell the next offer, and that offer can belong to an affiliate partner rather than the funnel owner. A beginner can therefore send prospects to an existing closing process instead of building that process personally [QdGtcnjJSno @ 00:05:15]. PDAC Media & Marketing describes consulting, funnel-building services, software, coaching, certification, and an online course as possible follow-on purchases after the low-ticket funnel has recovered advertising spending [oA-5XBJJ1VE @ 00:03:35]. Profitable Talents approaches the same bridge from an existing one-to-one coaching business: people discovering the business through social media may want a small paid experience before becoming clients. Their proposed funnel makes existing resources accessible at that earlier stage, allowing people to see the process before committing to working together directly [_I5YFl6Ym_k @ 00:01:56].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Start by assembling the actual paid result, then connect the adjacent purchases. Pete's working example is a $27 One Hour Blog Post offer, a $37 Lightning Writing bump, and a $97 Content Everywhere upsell. The opening bundle contains templates and supporting resources, while the bump supplies the writing system and the later course addresses content distribution. Pete first sold a discounted $17 version to ten beta customers, seeking feedback and testimonials before refining the stack [wYKYVjEiqLY @ 00:20:22]. PDAC's offer uses a different ladder: a $27 bundle, a $17 eleven-step conversion checklist, and a $47 seven-day funnel design challenge. The next offer answers what the buyer wants after acquiring the templates, rather than introducing an unrelated subject [oA-5XBJJ1VE @ 00:02:10]. Profitable Talents sketches a $9 sales-page framework with a complementary Kajabi template: the guide helps write the copy, and the template supplies somewhere to place it. People who already build pages can use the guide without the extra purchase [_I5YFl6Ym_k @ 00:21:36]. Max Auren creates their $7 product by preparing a deeper YouTube lesson, publishing a stripped-down free version, and packaging the paid version with extra downloads and existing bonus products [kmRwEjvxk-w @ 00:05:14].

Jonathan Montoya's Funnel Freedom build separates the learning content from the payment products. Import the SLO funnel and form, create the membership content, then create chargeable products under Payments. A membership lesson can contain only a link to a Google Document; the payment product supplies the name and price used in checkout. Their demonstration gives an upsell a $97 price, a $77 downsell, and a recurring option of three monthly payments of $27 [g9_sJ3XLyHs @ 00:20:23]. At checkout, attach the main product and designate the additional product as a bump. Then open the order-form editor, enable the order bump under Advanced, and select the attached bump product [g9_sJ3XLyHs @ 00:31:04]. Mike at Sell Your Service shows a HighLevel template beginning with an opt-in and a $27 video sales-letter offer; after purchase, their distinctive next page invites the customer to become an affiliate earning the entire front-end sale [_csKVgudVVY @ 00:07:21]. Solo Marketer Funnels demonstrates another implementation in system.io: the opt-in leads to a related offer, checkout can include a bump, and a short yes-or-no upsell page offers a $20 monthly community. Their demonstration also places product hosting and email campaigns inside the same platform [gba265EaF2g @ 00:12:34].

Connect the sale to access and connect the nonbuyer to follow-up. Montoya uses an Order Submitted trigger filtered to the relevant global product, adds a buyer tag, and grants the matching course offer. The course offer is created as free because payment already happens through the funnel; its job is to connect the purchase automation to the membership content [g9_sJ3XLyHs @ 00:40:13]. For people who submit the opt-in form without buying, their separate workflow starts with Form Submitted, adds an opt-in tag, and sends successive emails with waits between them. Solo Marketer Funnels describes a five-email example that introduces the seller, builds trust, and repeatedly presents the original offer from different angles rather than abandoning subscribers after the first refusal [gba265EaF2g @ 00:07:14]. On the buyer side, Montoya's affiliate-stacking sequence can introduce several products over time while also sending people toward YouTube videos and other brand channels [QdGtcnjJSno @ 00:09:40]. Pam's coaching version puts a video and calendar on the private invitation page. Buyers who schedule move toward a Zoom conversation; those who do not schedule reach the thank-you page. That invitation follows the earlier digital purchases rather than replacing their delivery [Kgj8rFDeRIs @ 00:06:36].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Map the connected product journey
   - Choose the quick-win tool, related system, and next strategy.
   - List the customer's steps from their starting problem to the desired result, identify existing resources, and connect the lead offer, bump, and upsell to those steps. [Pete McPherson DYEB — wYKYVjEiqLY @ 00:07:20]
2. Build content and chargeable products separately
   - Create the membership content, then create payment products and prices.
   - Use memberships for delivery and Payments for the products charged through the funnel; attach the appropriate price to each checkout or upsell step. [Jonathan Montoya — g9_sJ3XLyHs @ 00:18:26]
3. Wire purchase delivery
   - Trigger access from the purchase of the relevant product.
   - Use Order Submitted with a product filter, add a buyer tag, create the matching course offer, and grant that course offer through the workflow. [Jonathan Montoya — g9_sJ3XLyHs @ 00:38:32]
4. Install follow-up and compare acquisition economics
   - Follow up with nonbuyers and calculate the spending across the funnel.
   - Use automated emails to reintroduce the offer, then add sales, bumps, upsells, and downsells and divide by the number of initial buyers to calculate average cart value. [Solo Marketer Funnels — gba265EaF2g @ 00:07:14] [Jonathan Montoya — lJzDn-Qma9o @ 00:11:00]

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Even the label SLO carries different operating instructions. Mike at Sell Your Service insists that their version is not simply an ordinary funnel with a one-click upsell. After the first purchase, the customer receives an invitation to promote the opt-in and offer pages for 100% of the front-end revenue. The business keeps the resulting subscribers and buyers for later products, while customers become salespeople [_csKVgudVVY @ 00:03:27]. Online Business Made Easy explains the same full-commission logic through the One Funnel Away Challenge: charging the buyer qualifies the purchase, while the affiliate receives the front-end commission [swjcIFF0Ehs @ 00:03:52]. Daniel Fazio instead defines self-liquidation around immediate cash recovery and strongly rejects cold-traffic lead magnets in their acquisition playbook. Their preferred front end sells a specific, low-commitment result before a retainer or broader service relationship [By4MPc6yyFA @ 00:16:23]. Matt from Growbo explicitly teaches both direct-to-offer traffic and a lead-magnet-first route, calling them standalone and sneak attack. The latter introduces the paid offer after someone requests the free resource. These are different entry paths: Mike recruits promoters after purchase, Fazio wants cold traffic buying the setup, and Matt also uses the free opt-in before the paid invitation [ZWpExzy5MUg @ 00:05:43].

Neither the cheapest price nor the strongest refund promise wins by default across these operators. Pete declines refunds on immediately usable digital tools because customers can consume them within minutes, while allowing a refund policy on the larger training that takes longer to implement [wYKYVjEiqLY @ 00:12:11]. Solo Marketer Funnels takes the opposite position on digital-product reassurance: they describe a money-back guarantee as something that can encourage purchases even when some buyers later request refunds [gba265EaF2g @ 00:12:03]. Allie Bjerk reports that lowering their own front end from $27 to $17 tripled conversions. But another client sold a $55 front end successfully, and Bjerk's attempt to copy that price did not generate enough sales to remain profitable. They also caution that more opening purchases can leave daily revenue lower if those buyers stop taking the other offers [Ii70ybaGJ68 @ 00:08:19]. Fazio treats guarantees as an acquisition variable rather than a fixed doctrine: keep a guarantee when it lowers acquisition cost and improves closing without a high refund rate; do not remove it if the change destroys self-liquidation. Their instruction is to watch what the change does to CAC and LTV [By4MPc6yyFA @ 00:39:52].

Budget, payback timing, and sales-page length also divide the playbooks. Fazio calls $200 per day the minimum paid-ad budget for their approach and argues against advertising without enough money for testing. Their agency examples revolve around setup offers costing substantially more to acquire than a tiny digital-product buyer [By4MPc6yyFA @ 00:16:34]. Max Auren gives a much smaller starting example: for a $7 product, try $20 or $30 per day and wait at least a week to see performance. They favor broad Facebook targeting, with a qualification about accounts lacking pixel data or existing traffic [kmRwEjvxk-w @ 00:07:25]. Solo Marketer Funnels allows the automated sequence to recover advertising over several days, describing a week or less and other possible payback periods instead of insisting every dollar return immediately [gba265EaF2g @ 00:00:46]. Online Business Made Easy supplies a more persuasion-heavy front end for $47, $97, or $197 offers: the Star, Story, Solution approach starts with a pattern interrupt, desire questions, past failures, a big promise, and the seller's introduction. The speaker connects this additional persuasion to the higher ticket price and the larger questions a buyer brings to that purchase [swjcIFF0Ehs @ 00:06:32].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

A purchase does not mean the customer has consumed the product or wants the next one yet. Mateo describes a client with a thirty-day challenge whose buyers were difficult to move into the high-ticket offer. Their diagnosis centers on consumption: many low-ticket buyers never finish or even use what they bought, and asking for another purchase before a result can make little sense to them [Q4J9fiMh3C0 @ 00:01:52]. Profitable Talents rejects a more complex workshop as the opening offer because it could keep people occupied for months. They want a practical win within days, not content that buyers feel obliged to complete before considering coaching [_I5YFl6Ym_k @ 00:14:25]. Max Auren similarly makes consumption central to trust, while also reporting a specific downside to repackaging public content: approximately five refunds among thousands of sales came from people who had already watched the YouTube version [kmRwEjvxk-w @ 00:06:04]. Matt from Growbo reports that a trial for their done-for-you service did not work well. They attribute the difficulty to subscription hesitation and say those prospects would rather watch a demo before deciding. A subscription required a different level of commitment from the small introductory products that had worked previously [ZWpExzy5MUg @ 00:12:31].

Checkout and traffic failures appear in the builds themselves, not just in marketing theory. Solo Marketer Funnels clicks a demonstration order bump that does not respond, then identifies the missing payment-processor connection in the dummy account. The page layout was present, but the payment setup was not complete [gba265EaF2g @ 00:10:45]. Montoya hits a related configuration problem when an upsell product is missing from the page: the button cannot select a product that has not first been attached to that funnel step. They return to Products, add the correct product and price, and then finish linking the button [g9_sJ3XLyHs @ 00:36:28]. Pete's early feedback also changes the commercial wiring: they swap a bump with an existing bonus because the revised fit works better. Their launch sequence leaves room for that refinement rather than treating the initial bundle as permanent [wYKYVjEiqLY @ 00:20:55]. PDAC reports a more abrupt interruption: their earlier template-bundle ads had begun producing daily sales before the ad account was shut down. They subsequently returned to organic content and personal-brand development. The account shutdown changed the acquisition route even though the speaker says the paid funnel had started working [oA-5XBJJ1VE @ 00:00:19].

Scaling exposes numbers that a cheap opt-in or successful checkout can hide. In Mariana Henninger's interview, Allie Bjerk describes a client stuck around $400 daily spend: pushing the budget higher reduced performance, and the client repeatedly moved it back down. Bjerk's intervention was to preserve the working campaign and add separate campaigns rather than keep squeezing the same one. They also describe a separate low-budget sandbox for new creative [Ii70ybaGJ68 @ 00:29:09]. Fazio reports students moving from a $5,000 offer to $10,000 overnight and watching close rates fall. Their response is smaller price increases, tracking the close-rate effect, and restoring the earlier price if self-liquidation disappears [By4MPc6yyFA @ 00:39:20]. Matt from Growbo describes initial offers recovering only 25% or 50% of advertising as a possible starting point, rather than assuming immediate break-even from the first launch [ZWpExzy5MUg @ 00:04:14]. Solo Marketer Funnels adds the expense qualification to backend profit: subtract operating costs, people paid to run the system, and monthly tool fees. Their own explanation does not stop at the headline that later sales are profit; it explicitly retains those ongoing costs after advertising has been recovered [gba265EaF2g @ 00:15:56].
