# Chapter 11: Small-Team Scaling: Running an $800k/yr Portfolio With 4 People

*The 1 FT + 3 Contractor Operating Pod, Micro-Distribution Acquisitions (Extensions & Dormant RSS) & Weekly Portfolio Scorecards*

## Executive Summary

An $800,000 portfolio is a throughput problem before it is a hiring problem. Give one owner capital allocation and three recurring specialists bounded deliverables, then buy distribution only when transferable rights and retained buyers justify the price. This chapter models annual gross billings separately from operating contribution, including labor, traffic, refunds, and delivery costs. Neglected extensions, dormant podcast feeds, and utility television channels are acquisition experiments, not automatic audiences. The operating objective is a repeatable weekly close that exposes cash consumption early enough to stop spending before revenue growth disguises a broken offer.

## 1. Give four people four accountable lanes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The org chart has one full-time Owner/Architect above three recurring contractors, with no hidden management layer. The owner controls pricing, product architecture, cash allocation, acquisition diligence, and the weekly release decision. The Product/Design Specialist owns templates, curriculum packaging, accessibility checks, and versioned product assets. The Video/Funnel Editor owns tutorial edits, landing-page assemblies, checkout experiments, and attribution tagging. The Support & Affiliate Ops Coordinator owns response queues, refund triage, partner onboarding, commission reconciliation, and customer feedback labels. Each specialist reports directly to the owner; collaborators exchange finished inputs through a shared production board rather than creating supervisory relationships. Every task has one accountable person, a measurable acceptance test, and a due date. The owner stays responsible for commercial outcomes even when execution is delegated to someone with deeper technical expertise.

Buy capacity through clear deliverables rather than imaginary unlimited availability. An illustrative scaled arrangement pays the Product/Design Specialist $2,000 monthly for two bounded product releases, the Video/Funnel Editor $1,500 for eight edits and two funnel updates, and the Support & Affiliate Ops Coordinator $1,000 for a defined queue volume and monthly partner reconciliation. That totals $54,000 annually, with separately priced overages. Reserve $60,000 for the owner's labor so contribution does not depend on pretending the owner works free. These are planning inputs, not market wage claims; validate local rates and actual workload. At lower revenue, reduce deliverables and use smaller recurring scopes. Document independent business arrangements honestly: a contractor label does not cure employee-like control, mandatory schedules, or a relationship that fails the applicable jurisdiction's classification tests in practice.

Keep ownership paperwork as specific as the production brief. Section 3508 of the Internal Revenue Code covers qualifying real estate agents and direct sellers; it is not a blanket exemption for designers, editors, or support staff. Evaluate these roles under the applicable federal and state tests instead. Copyright also needs an actual chain of title: under 17 U.S.C. section 201(b), an employer or qualifying commissioning party owns a work made for hire unless otherwise agreed in writing, but commissioned work must first satisfy the statutory definition. Obtain signed assignments for deliverables that do not qualify, and identify retained tools, fonts, music, and licensed components. Store the agreement beside the source assets. Otherwise a profitable portfolio can become unsellable because nobody can demonstrate ownership of its core instructional materials.

## 2. Acquire extension utility, not an installation counter  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The expired_chrome_extension_acquisitions play means purchasing neglected browser extensions or plugins through an authorized transfer, not taking over abandoned credentials or expired infrastructure that still serves unsuspecting users. Screen listings with 10,000 to 250,000 reported users, then demand recent active usage, retention, store standing, source ownership, dependency health, and documented transfer eligibility. A headline user count is not a reachable mailing list and does not establish permission to reuse personal data. Underwrite one narrow bridge to the catalog: a spreadsheet helper can recommend a related template pack inside a clearly identified optional product surface. Preserve the tool's existing function. If monetization requires surprise permissions, injected advertisements, or a different purpose from the installed utility, the acquired distribution is less durable than the seller's screenshots suggest to an inexperienced buyer.

Treat a price of one to two times annual verified cashflow as a proposed sourcing discipline, not a market fact or an entitlement to a bargain. Define cashflow after hosting, store fees, support, refunds, and replacement maintenance labor. An extension generating $18,000 annual receipts but consuming $8,000 in those costs has $10,000 normalized cashflow; the illustrative offer range is $10,000 to $20,000, before transition costs. Match payment records to bank deposits and usage periods rather than accepting a monthly revenue screenshot multiplied by twelve. Model a downside case with half the active users retained and zero cross-sell lift. Use a limited migration schedule and retain a repair reserve. A cheap asset with unresolved dependencies can consume the entire specialist's monthly capacity before the first catalog sale reaches the bank.

Use the first thirty days to establish whether the purchased utility earns continued use. Record active users, uninstall rate, crash reports, support minutes, and product clickthrough before making commercial changes. Ship a maintenance release first, with a clear ownership update and a reversible deployment. Test a relevant catalog recommendation on a small eligible audience; compare incremental contribution against an unchanged cohort where feasible. Do not count all subsequent purchases as acquisition lift merely because the buyer once installed the extension. Attribute incremental refunds and support time to the same experiment. If a store cannot transfer the listing, a seller cannot establish source rights, or a technical migration would break existing accounts, value only the assets that can actually move. Walking away preserves the operating pod's most constrained resource: focused production time.

## 3. Buy podcast continuity and build useful television inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The expired_podcast_rss_redirect_arbitrage play starts with a dormant niche show whose owner can transfer the domain, feed administration, archive rights, and relevant hosting account permissions. An expired domain alone does not convey the show's identity, subscriber relationship, recordings, or advertising rights. Verify historic download logs and current listening by episode, platform, and geography; exclude bots and hosting migrations that inflate counts. DR 85+ is a third-party domain-rating screen, not a property of an RSS feed, a Google metric, or a promise of transferable authority. Inspect the actual linking pages and retained referral visits. Price the acquisition on collected revenue from reachable listeners with clear ownership continuity. Keep a speculative search benefit outside the base valuation until measured visits and purchases demonstrate that the benefit survived the authorized transition.

Dynamic back-catalog audio ad slots exist only where the hosting setup supports insertion and the acquired recording contracts permit it. Check host-read exclusions, guest releases, music licenses, old sponsor exclusivity, and which episode files actually remain distributed. A feed redirect should preserve episode identifiers and subscriptions while transparently explaining the new publisher; test major listening clients before switching the full archive. Start with a relevant catalog offer, a dedicated URL, and a measured redemption window. Track verified downloads, attributable orders, refunds, and contribution after hosting and editing. Do not imply the previous host endorses the new product without permission. Section 1125(a) addresses misleading affiliation and false advertising; buying a domain does not buy an unlimited right to borrow the former publisher's reputation or audience trust for unrelated commercial claims.

For ctv_mfa_programmatic_supply_arbitrage, replace the made-for-advertising temptation with a genuine utility channel: organized tutorials repackaged for Roku or FireTV viewing. The editor adapts existing lessons into readable television segments, checks music and footage rights for the new medium, and adds navigation that helps viewers finish a task. The owner verifies each platform's current publishing and monetization requirements before spending on production. Programmatic supply is valuable only when inventory is honestly described, viewers are real, and collected advertiser revenue exceeds distribution costs. Never manufacture viewing, disguise inventory, or buy traffic solely to recycle it into impressions. Measure valid viewing hours, completion, net collected advertising yield, hosting expense, and catalog sales separately. A channel without an accepted monetization arrangement is an owned-media experiment, not an advertising receivable on the scorecard.

## 4. Turn recurring work into a weekly exception system  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the SOP library around repeated outputs rather than a large handbook nobody opens. Maintain short procedures for product release, asset licensing, funnel tagging, extension updates, podcast migration, television publishing, refund handling, dispute evidence, affiliate reconciliation, and weekly financial close. Each procedure specifies its trigger, accountable role, input links, exact actions, acceptance test, and recovery path. Include one completed example and the last revision date. The Product/Design Specialist maintains product procedures, the Video/Funnel Editor maintains publishing procedures, and the Support & Affiliate Ops Coordinator maintains customer and partner procedures. The owner resolves conflicts and owns financial definitions. When an exception happens twice, amend the procedure instead of scheduling another recurring meeting. Source files, credentials, and recovery documentation stay in owner-controlled systems with individual access appropriate to each person's actual scope.

Use a weekly KPI dashboard as a decision ledger, not a decorative revenue display. Contribution Margin $ means gross billings less refunds, payment costs, advertising, affiliate payouts, delivery costs, contractor fees, and the modeled owner labor allocation; label this chapter's fully loaded convention because other businesses define contribution differently. MER / Blended ROAS equals gross billings divided by paid media spend here, with affiliate costs shown separately; it is not an incremental attribution claim. Day-1 Payback % equals first-day attributable contribution before acquisition spend divided by that cohort's acquisition spend. Refund % uses refunded order count over original cohort order count, with refunded dollars separately visible. Dispute % uses disputed transactions over original cohort transactions and targets below 0.35%; also retain processor reports because their periods, denominators, and thresholds can differ.

Close the scorecard every Monday using consistent cohorts and a separate cash view. Show seven-day results, trailing four-week results, and mature cohort outcomes so delayed refunds and disputes cannot disappear inside recent growth. The coordinator reconciles processor exports, settlements, affiliate liabilities, and refund reasons; the editor explains traffic and checkout changes; the product specialist explains defect trends. The owner assigns one action, one person, and one deadline to each material variance. If payback weakens while MER rises, inspect customer mix and repeat purchases before increasing spend. If disputes approach the internal ceiling, isolate the offer, correct delivery or billing confusion, and reduce exposure while evidence develops. Use support minutes per hundred orders as a capacity signal. A four-person model breaks when unpriced service obligations grow faster than contribution per order.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Reconcile
   - Close the previous week.
   - Match billings, refunds, fees, and settlements; record timing differences.
2. Diagnose
   - Review mature cohorts by offer.
   - Separate acquisition performance from repeat sales and delayed losses.
3. Allocate
   - Choose one constrained experiment.
   - Set its spend ceiling, accountable owner, comparison, and stop condition.
4. Ship
   - Release against the relevant SOP.
   - Save acceptance evidence and a recovery path alongside the assets.
5. Learn
   - Record contribution and capacity consumed.
   - Update the procedure and fund another iteration only from measured evidence.

## 5. Scale contribution before expanding the portfolio  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use three annual scenarios to make the staffing promise falsifiable. Conservative means $50,000 gross billings at 20% net operating contribution, or $10,000. Base means $425,000 at 37.5%, or $159,375. Scaled means $800,000 at 55%, or $440,000. Each uses the same one-owner, three-contractor architecture, but not the same purchased capacity or owner compensation. The conservative case budgets only $12,000 of owner labor and $6,000 of contractor work annually: it is a thinly funded owner-operated experiment, not a sustainable full-time wage proposition. The base case raises those allowances to $48,000 and $36,000. The scaled case funds $60,000 and $54,000. If actual local labor costs exceed those amounts, revise contribution downward immediately. Keeping the headcount label unchanged does not make the workload, compensation, or economics comparable across stages.

The table is an explicit cost envelope, not a forecast derived from market demand. Gross billings exclude sales taxes collected for remittance. Refunds and payment costs are deducted once, while advertising and affiliate payouts share a separate acquisition line. Delivery and overhead include hosting, software, maintenance, insurance, and other recurring operations. Net operating contribution is the residual after these modeled costs; it is not after-tax accounting profit or distributable cash. Asset purchase prices, acquisition financing, and purchase-accounting amortization are outside this operating view and require a separate investment schedule. For example, a $20,000 acquisition plus $5,000 migration paid from the base case leaves $134,375 before taxes, working-capital changes, and any debt service. Include that cash bridge before deciding how much the owner can safely withdraw from the business.

The scaled margin requires evidence of operating leverage rather than wishful multiplication. Its $140,000 acquisition budget is 17.5% of billings, compared with roughly 28.2% in the base case; that improvement needs a stronger retained audience, repeat buying, better conversion, or some combination demonstrated in mature cohorts. It cannot be assumed merely because an extension or podcast was purchased. Stress the scaled case by adding $80,000 of acquisition expense and $40,000 of labor and delivery cost: contribution falls to $320,000, or 40%, before any revenue decline. Fund only one distribution integration at a time until the pod can absorb another without delaying existing customers. Keep a cash reserve based on settlement delays and fixed commitments. The portfolio becomes an empire only in branding; operationally it remains a small business with finite attention and real bills.
