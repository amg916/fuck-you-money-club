## 1. Assign thirteen plays to four accountable seats before adding capacity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Organize the pod around deliverables, not a miniature law firm. For this chapter, the thirteen-play operating map is an explicit task decomposition: campaign procurement, advertising review coordination, inquiry intake, contact-permission evidence, identity and duplicate screening, law-firm retainer routing, record-request preparation, EMR retrieval, pharmacy retrieval, chronology extraction, deficiency resolution, MSO billing, and buyer-ready reporting. These labels allocate work; they do not establish thirteen separate revenue streams. The full-time operator owns pricing, client relationships, scope control, billing, reporting, and exception escalation. Contractor one owns campaign operations and permission artifacts; contractor two owns intake, identity checks, and retainer routing; contractor three owns record retrieval, chronology production, and administrative deficiency work. Each play receives one accountable seat, one backup, and a documented handoff. The client firm's lawyers retain advertising decisions, representation decisions, legal qualification, and advice. They are counterparties, not hidden employees in the four-person staffing claim. External accounting and security services remain purchased overhead with explicit expense lines. If those services become recurring production labor, revise the staffing disclosure rather than preserving an attractive headcount by relabeling workers. Record weekly hours by play so the claimed automation advantage can actually be measured.

Use written statements of work that price hours or defined administrative deliverables at supportable market rates. An illustrative starting schedule is $45 per hour for campaign operations, $30 for intake administration, and $40 for record operations, each with a separately negotiated monthly hours ceiling. These are budgeting assumptions, not additions to the canonical profit figures. A contractor invoice must identify work period, deliverable IDs, authorized hours, and accepted corrections without exposing medical details in the accounting ledger. Pay for completed administrative work, never a percentage of attorney fees or claimant recovery. Avoid bonuses per signed claimant or accepted legal case; those incentives encourage steering and can implicate referral restrictions even when labeled marketing. Quality incentives should instead reward auditable accuracy, timely handoffs, and reduced operator rework within a fixed services arrangement. Contractor status depends on actual working conditions under applicable tests, not the invoice heading. Define service windows and output standards without pretending that a tightly controlled employee becomes independent through contract language. Require confidentiality, appropriate data-processing terms, and signed IP assignments before access. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a signed writing; payment alone does not transfer every script or template.

Scale by splitting bottlenecks rather than duplicating the founder. The second full-time seat becomes delivery and quality lead, while the original operator retains commercial management and cash control. The fourth contractor becomes a dedicated records and pharmacy specialist, allowing the existing records contractor to concentrate on chronology QA and deficiency resolution. That is exactly two full-time seats plus four contractors, not six full-time equivalents. Schedule the expansion only after eight consecutive weeks show sustained utilization above the internally selected 80% planning threshold, acceptable rework, and sufficient contracted contribution to cover the added cost. Maintain a capacity sheet converting volume into minutes for intake, request preparation, follow-up, extraction review, and correction. Automation reduces a measured task duration; it does not erase exception handling. A non-clinical reviewer can check source citations and field completeness but must escalate medical interpretation to appropriately qualified reviewers retained within the disclosed budget. Every production play needs a tested substitute who can resume from the queue without contacting the founder. Test that promise with a five-day operator absence, read-only cash dashboards, and documented escalation authority. If customer service collapses during that exercise, the business still sells the owner's availability rather than transferable operating capacity.

## 2. Score the complete evidence waterfall without pricing legal outcomes  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The weekly scorecard must preserve the entire waterfall: raw inquiry to signed law-firm retainer to usable HITECH EMR or pharmacy proof to deficiency-free accepted case to collected MSO or vendor fee. Tag each event with acquisition cohort, customer, source vendor, matter identifier, timestamp, and responsible seat. A retainer is a law-firm event, not permission for the MSO to practice law; an accepted case means the firm's documented intake standard was satisfied, not that causation or recovery is established. Consider an illustrative monthly cohort of 1,000 inquiries. Exclude 120 duplicates or identity exceptions, route 220 of the remaining 880 into signed retainers, obtain usable proof for 154, and deliver 132 deficiency-free files. Report 25% clean-inquiry-to-retainer conversion, 70% retainer-to-proof yield, and 60% retainer-to-acceptance yield. Record duplicates by reason rather than silently deleting them. A LexisNexis product or another authorized identity service can assist matching, but product permissions, permissible uses, and manual false-positive review remain necessary. Never treat an SSN match or a vendor's exclusive-lead promise as proof that the claimant has no other retainer. Ask the firm to resolve representation conflicts through its own procedures.

Connect that cohort to a services ledger without turning the waterfall into a bounty schedule. The Base scenario averages $58,750 monthly recognized MSO revenue, derived from $705,000 divided by twelve. For a steady-state illustration, allocate that month's service revenue across 132 accepted files: $445.08 per file is an analytical cost-recovery ratio, not a contractual case price. If 90% of the corresponding invoiced amount has been collected by the measurement date, cash receipts are $52,875 and the remaining $5,875 stays receivable subject to collectibility assessment. Do not multiply $445.08 by future cases to rewrite a fixed-fee contract. At an illustrative $45 external retrieval outlay for each of 220 retainers, retrieval spend is $9,900 before internal labor. If non-qualifying evidence rises from 30% to 50%, usable proof falls from 154 to 110. Holding the earlier proof-to-acceptance ratio approximately constant produces about 94 accepted files, raising retrieval outlay per accepted file from $75 to approximately $105.32. The service fee may remain fixed while rework grows; capacity, credits, and renewal economics absorb the shock. Show this stress separately from the unchanged canonical annual scorecard. Repeat the test with duplicate exclusions at 20% and slower provider responses before increasing paid-media commitments.

Define clocks precisely. Intake acknowledgments can target one business day, complete request packets two business days after required documents arrive, and administrative defect correction five business days after a valid notice. These are negotiated MSO targets, not promises that a provider will release records on that schedule. Under 45 CFR section 164.524, covered entities generally have thirty days to act on an access request, with one permitted thirty-day extension subject to the regulation's conditions. Distinguish individual access requests from authorization-based disclosures; patient-rate restrictions and third-party delivery rules do not attach identically to every retrieval route. Track EMR and pharmacy requests separately, including request authority, provider receipt, missing identifiers, lawful charges, and follow-up dates. Use a negotiated thirty-day administrative replacement window measured from delivery, limited to specified vendor-caused defects and documented remedies. A firm's later legal rejection, changed MDL criteria, or Rule 702 causation decision should not automatically create an unlimited replacement obligation. Score first-pass accuracy alongside correction time, and sample high-risk files completely until defects stabilize. Keep medical information out of generic task boards, and use LLM chronology tools only within approved data arrangements, access controls, and human verification workflows.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly cohort
   - Export timestamped inquiry, retainer, request, acceptance, and invoice events.
   - Use a fixed weekly cutoff and retain later adjustments as dated entries rather than rewriting the original snapshot.
2. Reconcile exceptions before celebrating conversion
   - Review duplicate flags, permission gaps, missing records, and unsupported chronology assertions.
   - Assign each exception to a seat and distinguish administrative repairs from decisions reserved to the client firm's lawyers.
3. Match work to invoices and receipts
   - Tie service periods, approved outlays, customer credits, and bank receipts to the accounting ledger.
   - Keep cash collection separate from recognized revenue and keep per-file analytical allocations separate from contractual pricing.
4. Publish decisions with named owners
   - Approve the next week's capacity, correction priorities, and vendor allocation.
   - Record the chosen action, accountable seat, due date, and expected effect; verify completion at the following weekly review.

## 3. Protect the canonical margins with accrual books and concentration limits  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Keep the Formula v2.0 scorecard intact: Conservative revenue of $160,000 less $118,400 in total modeled costs produces $41,600 owner net profit at 26%; Base revenue of $705,000 less $437,100 produces $267,900 at 38.0%; Scaled revenue of $1,250,000 less $625,000 produces $625,000 at 50%. The first two cases use one full-time seat and three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time seats and four contractors. These are complete annual outcomes, not incremental margins to which an additional labor budget can casually be appended. The listed $2,500 to $14,000 monthly operating range is not a substitute for the complete scenario expense envelope; the case totals also require all modeled delivery and acquisition costs to reconcile. Revenue consists of contracted administrative, marketing, intake, and record-triage services. Client trust balances, settlement awards, claimant damages, and law-firm contingency fees never enter the MSO revenue line. Where the MSO arranges third-party services, assess principal-versus-agent presentation under ASC 606 from the actual control facts. If only a retained service share qualifies as revenue, record that share rather than gross pass-through billings. Do not invent a retained-share percentage or silently alter the canonical figures; identify the accounting difference and reconcile it explicitly for the actual business.

Build the ledger around customer, service period, acquisition cohort, vendor, and delivery activity. Accrue contractor work when performed, record retrieval charges when incurred, and recognize fixed monthly services over the period in which the contractual performance obligation is satisfied. Customer deposits may create contract liabilities rather than immediate revenue. Track credits and bounded replacement obligations using a documented accounting policy that reflects the contract and applicable standards. Maintain separate schedules for invoiced receivables, unbilled amounts, advance collections, disputed invoices, and aging. A cash receipt against last quarter's invoice improves liquidity without increasing this quarter's service revenue. Close monthly within ten business days using bank reconciliations, vendor accruals, cohort cost allocations, and a bridge from operating income to owner distributions. Allocate shared overhead consistently rather than moving unprofitable acquisition costs into an invisible corporate bucket. Reconcile the cohort waterfall to the ledger through service periods and customer identifiers; do not force each accepted file to generate a separate invoice. Preserve both invoice-level detail and an aggregated buyer view stripped of claimant identifiers. A quality-of-earnings review should reproduce monthly margin from source records without requiring unrestricted access to sensitive medical files or the founder's private spreadsheet formulas.

Set concentration limits before a dominant firm or vendor becomes indispensable. Cap each customer group at 25% of trailing-twelve-month recognized revenue and separately at 25% of receivables; aggregate affiliated firms under common economic control. Target five balanced customers so ordinary churn does not immediately breach the ceiling. A simple Base allocation is five customers paying $141,000 annually each, or $11,750 monthly, for an aggregate $705,000. Equivalent illustrative allocations are five annual contracts of $32,000 for Conservative and $250,000 for Scaled, with scope adjusted to the actual labor budget. These are capacity illustrations, not claims that five customers will buy identical packages. For critical acquisition and retrieval counterparties, measure both spend share and routed-volume share, with each economic group capped at 25%. Multiple vendor names do not diversify a common upstream lead supplier. Foundational software needs documented exports and recovery substitutes rather than a fictional four-way split of every application. During initial customer acquisition, disclose any unavoidable concentration breach and make diversification a dated priority; never present an immature book as already diversified. Separately monitor exposure to a single tort thesis because four firms can all lose demand after the same general-causation ruling under Federal Rule of Evidence 702.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before expanding scope or presenting a sale book**
> 
> Treat unresolved evidence as an operating task with an owner, not as a presentation problem.
> - Verify every MSO agreement uses supportable fixed or cost-plus administrative pricing, preserves lawyer independence, and contains no disguised recovery participation.
> - Verify the record-request route, disclosure authority, access permissions, and applicable business-associate arrangements before moving medical information into any new tool.
> - Verify customer revenue, receivables, and critical-vendor routing against the 25% concentration limits, documenting temporary launch-stage breaches and corrective dates.
> - Verify twelve monthly closes, signed contractor IP transfers, assignment restrictions, and a tested founder-replacement plan before circulating buyer materials.

## 4. Value transferable service earnings, not the settlement narrative  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A buyer acquires enforceable service economics, documented systems, and an organization capable of continuing without the seller. Owner net profit is therefore the starting point for normalization, not automatically EBITDA or seller's discretionary earnings. Reconcile taxes, interest, depreciation, amortization, owner compensation, recurring replacement labor, and defensible one-time adjustments from actual books. For illustration only, assume the Base $267,900 owner profit contains no owner replacement salary and otherwise requires no EBITDA adjustments. Deduct an $80,000 fully loaded replacement seat to obtain $187,900 normalized annual earnings. A negotiated two-to-three-and-a-half times multiple would imply enterprise value of $375,800 to $657,650. For Scaled, assume $100,000 replacement cost for the remaining owner role, leaving $525,000; a two-and-a-half-to-four times range implies $1,312,500 to $2,100,000. These are illustrative negotiation ranges, not observed transaction comparables or promised clearing prices. The Conservative $41,600 cannot support an $80,000 replacement role on those assumptions, making an owner-operated cash-flow purchase or asset transaction more plausible than a management-independent earnings multiple. If owner labor is already expensed, do not deduct it twice. Support any premium with evidence of renewal durability, stable gross contribution, low rework, and customer continuity through an actual operator transition.

Prepare a transfer package that separates ordinary business assets from restricted information and professional relationships. Include executed customer contracts, service descriptions, renewal history, assignment and change-of-control provisions, vendor agreements, employee documents, contractor statements of work, and signed copyright transfers covering scripts, parsers, templates, and documentation. Inventory third-party and open-source components so the buyer knows which rights can transfer and which require fresh licensing. A contractor's preexisting toolkit needs an express license broad enough for continued operation; an assignment cannot convey rights the contractor never owned. Provide de-identified operating samples initially and stage sensitive disclosures according to the transaction structure and applicable permissions. HIPAA protections and business-associate contract duties do not disappear when an acquisition agreement is signed. Customer contracts may require consent before assignment, and law-firm clients remain free to make representation decisions independently of the MSO sale. Deliver administrator access through controlled account transfers, rotate credentials after closing, and verify exports with a restoration test. The buyer should receive a working service organization, not the seller's personal logins or an unrestricted copy of every claimant record. Keep a transaction issue register with remediation owner, required counterparty action, and evidence of completion.

Negotiate enterprise value separately from equity proceeds. Debt, cash treatment, transaction costs, escrow, working-capital adjustments, and contingent consideration can materially change what the seller receives at closing. Base any earnout on clearly defined MSO service revenue or normalized service earnings, with rules for customer allocation, buyer-imposed overhead, and collection disputes; keep settlement percentages and attorney-fee participation out of the mechanism. ABA Model Rule 5.4 is a model rule whose binding implementation depends on jurisdiction, so contract review must follow the applicable state rules. Arizona's licensed alternative business structure framework is a specific authorization path, not a portable exception for ordinary MSOs elsewhere. Maintain a one-seller-specific contact-permission policy for campaign operations and separately assess the current TCPA requirements under 47 U.S.C. section 227 and 47 CFR section 64.1200; do not describe the vacated FCC one-to-one lead-generator rule as operative federal law. If offers underprice durable service earnings, retain the business with the same controls: delegated delivery, quarterly concentration testing, documented distributions, and funded correction reserves. A cash cow still requires replacement labor and disciplined renewal work. Sell only when risk-adjusted net proceeds compare favorably with realistic retained distributions and the owner's desired future involvement.
