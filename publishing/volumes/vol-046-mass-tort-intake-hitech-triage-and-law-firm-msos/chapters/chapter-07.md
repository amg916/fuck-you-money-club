## The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator in this chapter lands on the same diagnosis: the money leaks at the phone, not the ad. Saad Khan prices one mass tort lead at up to $3,000 against a $4,000 signed retainer. Khan says missing a 60-second callback drops conversion by over 70%, so the problem is speed to lead, not bad ads [ZkN7hMCBwmQ @ 00:00:00]. Alert Communications runs a 425-seat intake floor and sets the bar at an outbound call within 1 to 3 minutes. Alert says the loss turns significant after 5 minutes, and that injured callers show a fivefold drop inside that window [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:22:15]. K'Lee Terrazas backs the same point with a Google report showing 28% of Google Business Profile calls go unanswered on the first attempt [XcpDdsuGoEY @ 00:03:27]. Terrazas is blunt about what happens next: those callers are not going to leave a voicemail [j80F3HgdRAw @ 00:03:15]. All three treat round-the-clock coverage as the baseline prerequisite. Khan sells an always-on workflow that answers every channel, Alert staffs around the clock, and Terrazas pitches an agent that never burns out at 5 p.m. A firm that buys TV, radio, or paid search traffic without that coverage pays full price for leads it will never reach, and then blames the media vendor.

The second point of agreement is that intake is a sales function and should be judged like one. Tony Prito and Nick Worker of Answering Legal say marketers take the blame when the P&L shows no cases, even though intake is the real gap. They state flatly that the first consultation with a prospective client is a sales call, and that the motive is helping the caller, not chasing money [2az9KaozXKQ @ 00:16:30]. Alert Communications goes further. Alert says the intake manager is really a sales manager, and that firms with broken intake convert 20% to 40% of leads instead of the 70% to 80% a tuned operation delivers [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:16:47]. Andrew Picket's Melbourne and Titusville firm has recovered about $38 million at a 95% settlement rate. Picket tells operators to record every call, honoring two-party consent where it applies, and to audit intake before spending another dollar on marketing [lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:29:07]. Host K'Lee Terrazas underlines the cost of skipping that audit with the story of a $500 click that could have been a million-dollar case, lost at intake [lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:30:22]. The shared rule: the intake desk is the conversion engine for the whole acquisition budget. Staff it with closers, script it like a sales floor, and measure it before blaming the media buy.

The third consensus is that client communication does not stop at the signed retainer, because the long middle of a mass tort is where clients drift. Lawsuit Tracker explains that once the attorney submits the very detailed case information sheet, little happens on an individual MDL case until bellwethers, settlement, or remand. That is why the question asked most often is what is going on with my individual case [WmvLnQq2ZuY @ 00:00:29]. Chaffin Luhana answers it with a deliberate client journey. The firm assigns each client a team of lawyers, paralegals, and assistants, then walks the client through case development, record review, litigation, settlement, and an itemized disbursement of every penny, so there are no surprises [5cou1UWhrYw @ 00:00:02]. Maury Goldstein of Aylstock Witkin Kreis & Overholtz frames the stakes in money terms: staying connected through settlement is how clients get paid and how the firm earns a return on its acquisition spend [kjUaKK45Ahg @ 00:00:04]. Answering Legal adds that lack of communication is the number one bar complaint, and positions a 24/7 human answering service as the reassurance layer [2az9KaozXKQ @ 00:20:09]. Silence during the MDL is predictable, so operators agree the communication system has to be built before the silence starts.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Alert Communications runs a specific response ladder. An inbound call rings no more than 3 times, then plays a recorded message, holds for no longer than 15 seconds, and rolls to the overflow center. Alert built the ladder this way because abandonment climbs after 3 rings and after a 15-second hold. One firm had callers waiting on hold for 6 minutes [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:21:02]. Web leads get an instant text, then a call and an email. On callbacks, Alert reports that immediate dials reach 37% to 40% of leads, while repeating attempts at varied times of day lifts contact to 75% to 80% [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:35:45]. Saad Khan automates the first rung with event-driven injection, which fires an outbound call within milliseconds of a form fill. Khan claims a 391% boost from first-minute contact and runs ingestion, routing, qualification, data structuring, and CRM sync with no human in the loop [ZkN7hMCBwmQ @ 00:01:09]. Inside the CRM, Litify trainers show how to catch leads that slip through. Build a party list view filtered by last-called date so unreachable intakes surface for another attempt, then pin and clone that view for daily cleanup [5eCAkl3wUBg @ 00:19:48]. Every tool here applies the same rule: no lead sits untouched, and every missed contact goes back into a queue.

Screening scripts start short when a tort is new and get longer as it matures. Alert Communications says its 3M earplug intake started at 4 questions in early 2019 and grew to 50 by the late stage, and that intake should be re-customized monthly [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:30:58]. Saad Khan's AI agent applies MDL screens automatically: a jurisdictional baseline, a 30-day exposure minimum, and a 1953 to 1987 date range. Non-qualifiers are auto-rejected with no paralegal needed, and complex cases go to a human review queue [ZkN7hMCBwmQ @ 00:01:27]. Litify builds one questionnaire per case type, branching like a Choose Your Own Adventure book. CQC rules auto-turn-down cases that fail, answers auto-map to record fields, and output can be downloaded as a PDF or emailed [5eCAkl3wUBg @ 00:39:38]. K'Lee Terrazas's demo agent, Kim, walks a personal injury caller through eleven steps in a fixed order: name and phone, callback permission, self or someone else, accident date, treatment, other attorney, city and state, fault, description, injuries, and ER or clinic. Kim then pitches no fees upfront and offers a live transfer [XcpDdsuGoEY @ 00:00:47]. In every version, screening produces structured, reportable fields that drive auto-rejection, routing, and CRM sync, instead of free-text notes someone has to re-read later.

Closing and unit economics are where the numbers get hard. Alert Communications closes in a strict order. First, stay on the call and text the retainer for e-signature. Next, email it. Only then mail packets, which older hernia mesh claimants needed but 3M military claimants almost never did [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:37:21]. Alert's cost model puts cost per acquisition at $2,000, with $500 to $1,750 going to marketing and $250 to $500 to intake, and each lost lead costing up to $750 [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:17:17]. On answering cost, K'Lee Terrazas quotes AI agents at $1 per minute for 1,000 minutes or less, against $2 to $5 per minute for call centers. After 3 years in the industry, Terrazas calls that call-center service subpar [e67Bx9d5FBc @ 00:00:46]. Modern Legal Ops split its pipeline into open, working, under review, and retainer response. The team then tracked time-to-touch, open-to-offer, time under review, and open-to-close, segmented by investor claims, class action, mass tort, and assigned attorney. The slowdown turned out to be internal attorney workup, not the intake agent [jycz9TsdE9w @ 00:01:16]. The takeaway is to price every rung of the funnel and time every handoff, because the bottleneck often sits after intake has already done its job.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Answer within the ring limit
   - Configure inbound routing so no call rings more than 3 times or holds more than 15 seconds before overflow.
   - Run 3 rings, then a recorded message, then a hold of no more than 15 seconds, then the overflow center, because abandonment climbs after each threshold [Alert Communications — PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:21:02]
2. Text first, dial fast, redial at varied times
   - Fire an instant text on every web lead, call within 1 to 3 minutes, and keep redialing at different times of day.
   - Immediate calls reach 37-40% of leads; varied-time redials lift contact to 75-80%. Pull unreached leads from a last-called list view [Alert Communications — PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:35:45]
3. Screen with a tort-specific questionnaire
   - Run one branching questionnaire per case type, with auto-turn-down rules and answers mapped to record fields.
   - Use CQC rules to auto-turn-down cases that fail, and download or email the output as a PDF. Update the script monthly as the tort matures [Litify — 5eCAkl3wUBg @ 00:39:38]
4. Close on the call and hand off on a deadline
   - Text the retainer for e-signature while the caller is still on the line, then hand the signed client to pre-suit staff.
   - After signing, a pre-suit paralegal contacts the client within 24 hours and a pre-suit attorney within 48 hours, and phase changes trigger automated update texts [Legal Lead Sharks Podcast | K'Lee Terrazas — lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:22:47]

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is over how fast is fast enough. Saad Khan frames the whole mass tort leak as a speed-to-lead problem, not a bad-ads problem: a lead can run up to $3,000, a signed retainer $4,000, and missing a 60-second callback drops conversion by over 70% [ZkN7hMCBwmQ @ 00:00:00]. The Khan build fires an outbound call within milliseconds through event-driven injection and claims a 391% boost from first-minute contact [ZkN7hMCBwmQ @ 00:01:09]. Alert Communications runs a human floor and sets a looser but still brutal bar: outbound dial within one to three minutes, with significant loss after five [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:22:15]. Alert cites the InsideSales/MIT numbers, 12,700 reached in the first hour versus 1,190 later, and a 5x drop within five minutes for injured callers [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:23:00]. Where Alert breaks openly with the research is the Harvard finding that leads are 7x more likely to qualify within one hour; the speaker's verdict is that one hour is too long because the claimant signs elsewhere [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:23:57]. So the operator choice is between a sub-second machine dial and a one-to-three-minute human dial, and nobody on either side defends a sixty-minute window.

The second fight is machine versus human on the phones. K'Lee Terrazas pitches AI intake agents at $1 a minute for a thousand minutes or less, against the $2 to $5 a minute that call centers charge for what Terrazas calls subpar service, based on three years in the industry [e67Bx9d5FBc @ 00:00:46]. Terrazas argues traditional centers never learned the Google Ads cadence, while AI runs 24/7 with unlimited concurrent calls, no payroll, no training, no turnover, and never hits the 5:00 p.m. burnout that human intake does [e67Bx9d5FBc @ 00:01:16]. Answering Legal takes the opposite position: the top bar complaint is lack of communication, and a live 24/7 human answering service reassures callers in a way the hosts explicitly contrast with AI automation [2az9KaozXKQ @ 00:20:09]. Alert Communications stakes out the scaled-human version, with all agents US-based, 65% bilingual, and pricing by the minute in tenth increments, flat cost-per-lead, or flat rates [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:55:20]. On the retention side, Maury Goldstein of Aylstock Witkin Kreis & Overholtz says Kayse lets two to three people handle tens of thousands of clients weekly, where the alternative was an entire department or an entire offshore development [kjUaKK45Ahg @ 00:00:47]. The trade is per-minute cost and concurrency on the AI side against human reassurance and bilingual staff on the call-center side.

After the retainer is signed, the camps split again on how much the client should hear. Lawsuit Tracker tells MDL clients bluntly that once the attorney submits the very detailed case information sheet, little happens on an individual case, and the client should track the internet, YouTube channels, and monthly or bi-monthly attorney newsletters instead [WmvLnQq2ZuY @ 00:00:29]. Andrew Picket runs the opposite playbook: every phase change in Hona triggers an automated text carrying a Picket video explaining investigation, treatment, or negotiation and settlement [lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:23:39]. On top of that, the firm ships a welcome box around day 14 with a blanket and power bank for comfort and connection, mails birthday cards, and asks outright for referrals [lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:25:26]. Chaffin Luhana builds an intentional client journey so there are no surprises, assigning each client a team of lawyers, paralegals, and assistants who learn the story, the injuries, and the life impact [5cou1UWhrYw @ 00:00:21]. Kayse users treat silence as a money problem, not a courtesy problem: Goldstein ties lifecycle contact directly to clients getting paid and the firm earning a return on its acquisition investment [kjUaKK45Ahg @ 00:00:04]. The low-touch MDL model saves staff hours; the high-touch model protects the docket from client drift.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure is paying for leads that intake then wastes. Saad Khan cites one firm that burned $24,000 in payroll processing 800 rejected leads, the exact cost the auto-reject screening is built to kill [ZkN7hMCBwmQ @ 00:00:15]. Alert Communications puts numbers on the conversion gap: firms with broken intake convert 20% to 40% of leads instead of 70% to 80% [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:16:47]. Alert's worked example runs a $2,000 cost per acquisition built from $500 to $1,750 of marketing and $250 to $500 of intake, with a single lost lead costing up to $750 [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:17:17]. The trap that hides this is an undersized funnel: a firm reporting 70 out of 100 looks healthy while the leads it never captured stay invisible, against an Alert campaign running at 70% qualified that can reach 80% to 85% [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:14:18]. K'Lee Terrazas adds the paid-media version of the same loss: another attorney's $30,000 in Facebook ads was a 100% loss [lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:13:28]. Andrew Picket's fix is procedural, not budgetary: record every call, honoring two-party consent where it applies, and check intake before spending another dollar on marketing [lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:29:07].

The phone itself is where leads bleed out. Alert Communications measured abandonment rising after three rings and again after a 15-second hold, and reported one firm running six-minute holds [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:19:14]. The overflow sequence Alert prescribes is three rings, a recorded message, a 15-second hold, then roll to the overflow center [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:21:02]. Alert also found voicemail or no answer on 35% of calls, and flags paralegals juggling intake alongside other work as a weak point [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:28:06]. Repeat-attempt data shows why one dial is never enough: immediate calls reach 37% to 40%, and varying call times lifts contact to 75% to 80% [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:35:45]. K'Lee Terrazas cites a Google report that 28% of GBP calls were missed on the first attempt [XcpDdsuGoEY @ 00:03:27], and is blunt about what happens next: those callers are not going to leave a voicemail [j80F3HgdRAw @ 00:03:15]. Terrazas built Intake EQ after watching a $500 click that could have been a million-dollar case get lost [lW3uPdauQrA @ 00:30:22]. Even the AI fix shows seams: in the live demo, the agent Kim misnamed the caller Kaitlin versus Caitlyn while running the eleven-question PI screen.

Some failures sit downstream of the phone. Modern Legal Ops watched volume roughly double from 2024 to 2026 with a single intake agent, Lisa, while conversion lagged because three to four hidden steps sat between open and close [jycz9TsdE9w @ 00:00:00]. After splitting the pipeline into open, working, under review, and retainer response, and timing each stage, the culprit turned out to be internal attorney workup, not intake [jycz9TsdE9w @ 00:01:16]. Litify's trainer flags quieter CRM traps: a list-view search only covers that filtered list, so staff can miss intakes that exist elsewhere [5eCAkl3wUBg @ 00:11:34], and stacking too many banners on a record slows the page [5eCAkl3wUBg @ 00:23:03]. Litify's answer to the unreachable lead is a party list view filtered by last-called date [5eCAkl3wUBg @ 00:19:48]. The late-stage failure is client drift after signing. Goldstein describes thousands of Aylstock clients the firm had absolutely no hope of connecting with after exhausting every other channel, until Kayse reached 20% to 30% of them and brought them back to get paid through settlements [kjUaKK45Ahg @ 00:01:00]. Alert adds that screeners never sit still: the 3M intake grew from four questions in early 2019 to fifty late-stage, and intake gets customized monthly [PJEVefyQCxA @ 00:33:14].
