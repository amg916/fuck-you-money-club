## Executive Summary

Practitioners from Broughton Partners, King Law, Napoli Shkolnik and Tort Talk converge on one operating truth: toxic-exposure dockets pay only after defendants can count their liability, so filing deadlines, plaintiff fact sheets and deficiency rounds are the real gatekeepers. The AFFF MDL ballooned to 131,000 filings, was scrubbed toward 80,000 to 90,000, and resolved for about $7.35 billion covering 115,000 to 120,000 claims across six injuries, with Michael Stanisci forecasting first funds around the second half of 2028. Steve Smith pegs the Roundup class average near $70,000 and Depo-Provera at $150,000 to $200,000, and sells a $10,000-a-month Consortium that spreads capital across underwritten torts. Disagreements are sharp: Smith avoids MDLs for state-court premiums while Tort Talk works MDL enrollment mechanics, and Miller & Zois forecasts mid-six-figure AFFF payouts.

## The Practitioner Consensus: Countable Liability, Documented Claims, Trusted Jockeys

The baseline rule every toxic-exposure operator repeats is that defendants do not write checks until they can count the claimants on the docket. Steve Smith of Broughton Partners explains that the AFFF judge imposed a hard-cut filing deadline because 3M would not agree to a global number without knowing its liability, and the deadline showed exactly how many claimants it would have to pay [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:04:23]. Smith's second appearance puts numbers on the shock: the market expected roughly 60,000 to 70,000 AFFF cases, 131,000 got filed, the judge was disgusted, and the first trial was postponed while the docket was scrubbed for dual representation, medical qualification and exposure location down to roughly 80,000 to 90,000 [-H3O5pPP-XA @ 00:07:28]. Robert King of King Law reads the same tape from the filing side: the fall 2025 bellwether was postponed indefinitely, Case Management Order 35 allowed mass filing against a deadline, and that told King the judge believed the parties could reach an agreement [aOm5hBG6t9s @ 00:00:38]. Joe Fantini on Tort Talk confirms the endpoint: a roughly $7.35 billion AFFF settlement resolving 115,000 to 120,000 personal injury claims across the top six injuries [08ES8nUZRYk @ 00:00:39]. The operating lesson is that inventory size and deadline discipline drive resolution timing more than trial rhetoric.

The second point of agreement is that a claim is only as strong as the paper behind it. Andrew Kroner of Napoli Shkolnik warns that courts in the MDL already require very specific proof for a case to remain viable, and that believing you were exposed or generally knowing you suffer from a PFAS-linked condition is not enough; clients need documented diagnoses and medical histories [S1VC15_WonY @ 00:01:44]. Kroner singles out ulcerative colitis, where symptoms can stretch across years and multiple providers, as a claim that needs pathology reports, diagnostic imaging, treatment histories and specialist evaluations [S1VC15_WonY @ 00:02:15]. Robert King frames the standard as two proofs: proof of injury, pulled through medical records the firm orders from various providers sometimes covering many years, and proof of exposure, meaning firefighter work history or residency inside a known contaminated water supply system [aOm5hBG6t9s @ 00:03:52]. On the consumer side, Salenger, Sack, Kimmel & Bavaro applies a parallel three-part screen to hair relaxer: years of use rather than once or twice, a documented brand that is part of the case, and a uterine, ovarian or endometrial cancer diagnosis sitting in the medical charts [ch5IVo6DOos @ 00:05:33]. Kroner expects courts to keep narrowing who qualifies because PFAS exposure is ubiquitous, so organized records preservation is the operator's insurance.

Third, practitioners agree that who litigates the inventory, and where it sits, changes the payout. Steve Smith tells referral firms the jockey matters as much as the horse, noting that Bayer settled with a handful of firms outside the Roundup class deal at a premium while class-only cases take lesser amounts [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:02:55]. In NEC baby formula, Smith reports the MDL has had no trial success while state court cases have had great success, which is where the pressure comes in and why firm selection matters, because not all courts and venues are created equal [-H3O5pPP-XA @ 00:14:06]. Salenger, Sack, Kimmel & Bavaro describes bellwether trials as the mechanism that lets both sides see their strengths and weaknesses and promotes settlement across a docket of roughly 12,000 hair relaxer cases [ch5IVo6DOos @ 00:01:31]. Joe Fantini reads defense behavior the same way: after the October Uber bellwether settled in the MDL with about 4,600 cases still pending, Fantini concluded the days of Uber trials are done, and once defendants wave the flag on individual bellwethers, bigger settlements usually follow [ql3WYzhinZk @ 00:06:38]. Operators treat trial settlements, postponements and scheduling gaps as leading indicators for when to tighten inventory and prepare enrollment.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers: Intake Screens, Enrollment Drills and Underwritten Buys

Intake screens are spelled out to the number. Elizabeth of Lawsuit Tracker lists the six filable AFFF injuries, namely kidney, liver, testicular and thyroid cancer, thyroid disease and ulcerative colitis, and qualifies claimants who were stationed on, lived on as a dependent, or worked on a military base, or who live where the utility water report shows over four parts per trillion of PFAS chemicals [tVouqZmWW5I @ 00:03:16]. Lawsuit Tracker's firm runs a 40-year latency window from exposure to diagnosis and openly tells claimants that other firms may set different criteria [tVouqZmWW5I @ 00:03:33]. Robert King's shop maps known contaminated systems by PWSID, the public water ID number, or by ZIP code, so residency can be matched to a contaminated supply [aOm5hBG6t9s @ 00:04:07]. For social media addiction, Joe Fantini gives the LexHive Podcast acquisition screen: a minor who started before age 16, used a platform for hours a week, and developed any mental health issue, from depression, anxiety and body issues up through school problems, abuse problems and suicidal thoughts [DQ8EXxoteKo @ 00:07:27]. Fantini sizes that market at maybe 200,000 to 250,000 eventual plaintiffs against under 10,000 cases pending today, which is why acquisition urgency has increased significantly.

Once a deal lands, the work becomes a deadline drill. In the AFFF settlement, Joe Fantini told firms to rush the full ID order into the portal by Friday, November 13, listing every case they believe belongs in the deal, followed by de-duping, proof of specific injuries and verification that water claimants lived at specific addresses during the relevant time periods [08ES8nUZRYk @ 00:01:19]. In talc, Fantini describes an uncapped $5.5 billion settlement with a 95% participation threshold across about 70,000 cancer cases, forcing firms to track down clients who moved or died and use the settlement mechanism to name a potential representative [NHmq2W3F_do @ 00:05:45]. Depo-Provera runs on an administrative docket Judge Rodgers created for roughly 6,000 settling cases, with November 30 as the deadline to move non-qualifying or opt-out cases onto the traditional docket under the Lone Pine fact sheet and expert report requirements [NHmq2W3F_do @ 00:07:41]. In the Missouri Roundup class, Fantini flags a 90-day opt-out window that requires a wet signature, not DocuSign, plus a copy of ID, so firms must review records, place each client on a tier, and counsel big-damages clients with recurrences or heavy chemo on whether to opt out [3Qxw8dtCCns @ 00:07:40].

The unit economics are explicit. Steve Smith positions Broughton Partners as a consultant rather than a lead vendor, with the Goldwater Law Firm handling advertising, intake and paid analytics so a referring firm takes on no new staff [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:17:22]. The Consortium costs as little as $10,000 a month for one year, or $120,000, and pools each member with 24 other attorneys; every monthly one-hour Zoom spends 30 minutes on headlines and questions, then presents opportunities underwritten on cost per contract, attrition, expected value and timeline of settlement for a group vote, spreading the $120,000 across multiple torts and litigating firms with higher buying power [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:18:21]. On case value, Smith expects Depo-Provera qualifying cases to average $150,000 to $200,000, backed by a greater than 5x increased risk against the 2x rule of thumb, with signing effectively closed by mid last year because most claimants were already retained [-H3O5pPP-XA @ 00:16:14]. Camp Lejeune shows the spread operators must price: Fantini reports the first bench trial asked for a little under $11 million, while the same case would have been about a $500,000 case had it settled through the EO [ql3WYzhinZk @ 00:04:06]. That gap is why the parties are fighting over a Lone Pine order versus a census of the 25 injuries.

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Venue is the sharpest split on the street. Steve Smith of Broughton Partners says the shop does everything to avoid MDLs, routing cases to partners who create their own traction, retain their own experts, and file in state court, because those firms keep landing higher claim values and are not blinded by common benefit fees that might push a firm to settle for lesser amounts [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:15:28]. Smith uses NEC as the proof point: the MDL has had no trial success, while the cases held in state court have had great success, so not all courts and not all venues are created equal [-H3O5pPP-XA @ 00:14:06]. Smith still carves PFAS out as one of the rare dockets with no state court or individual firm play, calling it a global resolution [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:03:39]. Joe Fantini of Tort Talk treats the state lane as a file-by-file call rather than a doctrine. In the Missouri Roundup class, Fantini tells firms to isolate the big files, such as a cancer recurrence or heavy chemo, and counsel those clients on opting out with a wet signature and ID inside the 90-day window, while weaker files simply ride the proposed settlement tiers [3Qxw8dtCCns @ 00:09:14].

PFAS timing calls split badly, and the record shows who ran early. Smith pegged the start of Q2 as the quarter for PFAS clarity, reasoning that the hard filing deadline finally showed defendants like 3M exactly how many claimants they would have to pay [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:04:38]. Robert King of King Law, working up thousands of water contamination files, said in June that a July or August 2026 settlement would not be a surprise, tracking roughly a year of case management orders from Judge Gergel [aOm5hBG6t9s @ 00:05:03]. The deal landed in November instead, and Fantini called the roughly $7.35 billion settlement covering 115,000 to 120,000 personal injury claims pretty surprising [08ES8nUZRYk @ 00:00:39]. Michael Stanisci pushes cash expectations out further than any headline implies: no plaintiff payments in 2026, none in 2027, maybe some in the second half of 2028, then a rolling run through 2029 and 2030, because a defendant will not pay $7 billion upfront and instead pays tranche by tranche once releases and proof clear, exactly as Stanisci saw in Bard hernia mesh. Every case still has to clear the special master and the defendants. Stanisci's rule of thumb is at least a year and a half from settlement to actual funds [_qBek9AFPiI @ 00:00:35].

Intake criteria are where firm playbooks diverge most. Smith says the PFAS case management order capped the docket at six qualifying injuries, four backed by large epi studies plus two that the plaintiff side added without a Daubert ruling, and that a Daubert win on those plus two would hand plaintiffs leverage to demand more value [-H3O5pPP-XA @ 00:09:19]. Elizabeth of Lawsuit Tracker reports that some firms still sign tier 2 injuries, especially prostate cancer, on the view that it is the next injury the judge allows even with no official order, and says the Lawsuit Tracker firm screens on 40 years of latency from exposure to diagnosis while other firms run different criteria [tVouqZmWW5I @ 00:01:19]. Andrew Kroner of Napoli Shkolnik reads the next chapter as individual PFAS personal injury claims not tied directly to AFFF, with the MDL's proof requirements setting the roadmap and courts that may keep building barriers that narrow who qualifies [S1VC15_WonY @ 00:00:46]. On social media addiction, Fantini sizes the eventual docket at 200,000 to 250,000 plaintiffs against under 10,000 pending now, and holds both positions at once: there is plenty of time to get involved, yet urgency has increased significantly after the $375 million and $6 million verdicts [DQ8EXxoteKo @ 00:03:54].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The costliest self-inflicted wound in PFAS was inventory bloat. Smith says roughly 60,000 to 70,000 cases were expected, but 131,000 were filed by the deadline; the judge was completely disgusted, Smith calls the number obscene, and the first trial, the main pressure point for a resolution, got postponed, turning the year into cleanup of dual rep issues, medical record qualifications, and exposure proof [-H3O5pPP-XA @ 00:07:44]. In the earlier segment Smith adds that loss of consortium claimants padded the count and upset the judge [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:04:56]. King describes the grind that followed: Case Management Order 37 set minimum standards for plaintiff fact sheets submitted into a portal, four deficiency rounds have already run, and a fifth round attacking exposure is expected, with exposure proven through firefighter work history or residency in a contaminated system mapped by PWSID or ZIP code [aOm5hBG6t9s @ 00:04:28]. Kroner of Napoli Shkolnik warns the bar is concrete: believing you were exposed and generally knowing you have a PFAS-linked condition is not enough, and an ulcerative colitis client treated by multiple providers over years may lack the pathology reports, diagnostic imaging, treatment histories, and specialist evaluations courts want to keep a case viable [S1VC15_WonY @ 00:02:15].

Settlement mechanics punish firms that lose touch with clients. Fantini says the uncapped $5.5 billion talc deal carries a 95% participation threshold across about 70,000 cancer cases, and firms face a monumental chase because people have moved and many have passed away, so a potential rep must be submitted or the client can be excluded, an uphill spot since two of the main plaintiff experts withdrew their opinions before the deal was announced [NHmq2W3F_do @ 00:05:45]. Stanisci lists the paperwork that stalls a 100,000-claimant payout: multiple signed documents, deceased plaintiffs who need an estate and the proper party, prior bankruptcies, and approval by the special master and ultimately the defendants [_qBek9AFPiI @ 00:00:17]. Deadlines compound the drag. In Depo-Provera, Judge Rodgers created an administrative docket for settlement-only filings, while any case outside the criteria or opting out must hit the regular docket by November 30th and comply with a Lone Pine order demanding the fact sheet and an expert report [NHmq2W3F_do @ 00:07:58]. In the Missouri Roundup class, an opt-out needs an original wet signature, not DocuSign, plus a copy of ID and case-specific requirements inside 90 days, or the file is included in the settlement by default [3Qxw8dtCCns @ 00:07:40].

The economics checks run against the hype. Smith calls the Roundup class numbers bad: an average near $70,000, about $40,000 for residential use, a 10-tier grid from roughly $165,000 down to $10,000, with Bayer getting 5 years to pay and a 16-year latency tail that keeps it paying for 21 years. Smith notes Bayer separately paid a premium to a handful of firms outside the deal, which is why the litigating partner matters as much as the tort [IHvCfh-WJ6E @ 00:01:52]. Smith also flags saturation: Depo-Provera carried over a 5x increased risk against the 2x rule of thumb, yet by mid last year the market could not produce a good claimant because most were already signed [-H3O5pPP-XA @ 00:15:58]. On Camp Lejeune, Fantini puts the first bench trial demand at a little under $11 million against roughly a $500,000 value through the EO, a gap that has defense pushing a Lone Pine order while plaintiffs push a census of the 25 injuries [ql3WYzhinZk @ 00:04:06]. Trial risk cuts the other way too: in Bard PowerPort, the first infection trial deadlocked and the second came back a defense verdict, while fracture-migration cases stayed strong behind a $40 million verdict [08ES8nUZRYk @ 00:06:23].
