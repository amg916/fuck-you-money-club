## 1. Sell Budget Certainty to the Person Who Can Release Operating Cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The economic buyer is usually a managing partner, practice-group leader, or chief operating officer who has committed payroll and advertising cash before knowing which inquiries become supportable claims. The acute pain is stranded acquisition expense: duplicate retainers, missing exposure evidence, unreachable patients, and staff repeatedly opening the same incomplete file. Sell a measurable administrative service that removes those costs, rather than a promise of settlement value. The budget owner wants predictable monthly capacity; the intake director wants fewer rework loops; the controller wants an invoice that matches a purchase order. Ask each person what evidence permits payment, who can reject work, and whose signature changes scope. A partner's enthusiasm is insufficient when accounts payable requires vendor onboarding, tax documentation, and a separate banking review. Document the contracting entity, authorized signer, billing address, purchase-order reference, and operating-account payer before launch. For an illustrative $30,000 pilot, require a $15,000 advance credited against services and a $15,000 invoice after the agreed processing period. Record the advance as a contract liability until earned. The advance purchases capacity; it does not buy ownership of a claimant, a legal retainer, or settlement proceeds.

Budget authority and custody authority are different permissions. A firm's finance lead may approve vendor expenses without authority to release client trust money, while a settlement administrator may control distributions without purchasing your services. Make the ordinary payment source the firm's operating account. If a litigation funder supplies working capital, the borrower remains the invoice obligor unless a separate enforceable payment undertaking says otherwise. A funder's presentation, term sheet, or conditional commitment is not cash available for payroll. Inspect executed facility conditions, borrowing availability, concentration limits, and permitted uses with the firm's authorized finance team. Never seek claimant-level privileged material merely to perform credit screening; aggregate balances and redacted financial evidence normally answer the commercial question. ABA Model Rule 5.4 is a model rule, so identify the jurisdiction's adopted version and applicable exceptions instead of treating its text as a nationwide statute. Fair-market fixed or cost-plus pricing helps separate administrative compensation from legal fees, but pricing alone does not cure paid recommendations, prohibited solicitation, or interference with professional judgment. The supervising lawyer must retain decisions about representation, legal eligibility, case strategy, and client communications requiring legal judgment. Your contract should identify those reserved decisions explicitly.

Reconcile the commercial story to Formula v2.0 before discussing scale. Conservative annual revenue of $160,000 supports $118,400 of total modeled costs and $41,600 owner profit at 26%. Base revenue of $705,000 supports $437,100 of costs and $267,900 profit at 38.0%. Scaled revenue of $1,250,000 supports $625,000 of costs and $625,000 profit at 50%. Staffing remains 1 FT plus 3 contractors in the first two cases and 2 FT plus 4 contractors at scale. One illustrative Base billing mix is five firms purchasing $8,000 monthly capacity, producing $480,000 annually, plus fifteen separately scoped $15,000 projects producing $225,000. That mix explains revenue, not an automatic cost outcome; labor, software, insurance, retrieval, and collection expense must still fit the $437,100 envelope. Do not add merchant advertising volume, a funder's loan principal, gross settlements, or lawyer contingency fees to these numbers. Apply ASC 606 principal-agent analysis separately to pass-through purchases; no retained-share multiplier is supplied for this scorecard. Startup capital of $12,000–$50,000 and monthly operating expense of $2,500–$14,000 describe launch planning, not a substitute for complete annual scenario costs. Cash advances improve liquidity without increasing recognized revenue before performance.

## 2. Price the Entire Inquiry-to-Cash Waterfall, Including Failed Medical Proof  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Track the full conversion waterfall as separate events: raw inquiry, signed law-firm retainer, usable HITECH EMR or pharmacy proof, deficiency-free accepted case, and collected MSO or vendor fee. An illustrative cohort begins with 1,000 raw inquiries and removes 120 duplicate or suspect submissions, leaving 880 for processing. Of those, 220 sign retainers, a 25% conversion among screened inquiries. Medical proof supports 154, reflecting 30% nonqualifying or unusable records among signed matters. Sixteen additional files retain unresolved deficiencies, leaving 138 accepted cases. If collections reporting links 124 accepted files to paid service periods, fourteen remain associated with unpaid periods; this is an attribution metric, not a per-case fee entitlement. Separately, a $30,000 fixed pilot invoice with $27,000 collected and $3,000 contractually held has a 90% cash realization rate. Never multiply accepted cases by an invented contingency share. Client-direct media of $12,000 represents $12 per raw inquiry, $54.55 per signed retainer, and $86.96 per accepted case before other costs. These are illustrative administrative funnel economics, not the mandated annual scorecard and not the much higher all-in acquisition costs assumed by the litigation-finance play. Keep the denominators visible in every buyer report.

Do not advertise $6.50 as the universal price of a lawyer-directed medical-record request. Under 45 CFR 164.524, covered entities may use an optional flat fee of no more than $6.50 for certain electronic copies supplied through the individual's access right; it is one permitted fee method, not a universal cap on every request. The Ciox decision and subsequent HHS guidance distinguish third-party directives from requests delivering records to the individual. A HIPAA authorization under 45 CFR 164.508 is also not interchangeable with an access request. Record the actual request pathway, recipient, requested format, provider, and quoted fee before committing retrieval expense. For illustration, 220 qualifying requests at an elected $6.50 flat fee cost $1,430; a $25 average instead costs $5,500, a $4,070 increase. Additional providers and pharmacy custodians create additional requests, so count requests rather than claimants. Section 164.524 generally allows thirty days, with one additional thirty-day extension supported by timely written explanation. Shorter applicable state requirements require separate tracking. Do not promise seven-day evidence delivery when provider response timing remains outside your control. Escalate overdue requests, preserve delivery receipts, and let the contract distinguish completed administrative work from unresolved provider production.

Treat fraud screening and clinical triage as evidence assembly, not automated representation decisions. TrustedForm can preserve lead-source and interaction evidence; it cannot establish medical eligibility or prove every element of valid calling consent. LexisNexis identity tools may support permitted identity checks, but no vendor search establishes a universal duplicate-retainer registry. Combine permitted identifiers, claimant attestations, firm-conflict procedures, and documented human review. Minimize SSN collection and restrict access to any necessary identifiers. An LLM chronology parser should extract dates, ICD-10 references, medications, and exposure statements with page citations, then route contradictions to trained reviewers. A diagnosis code does not establish product exposure or legal causation. Where the relationship creates business-associate obligations, execute the required agreement and apply minimum-necessary access, encryption, and retention controls. Define a thirty-day illustrative replacement window for objectively defective administrative deliverables, excluding later changes in litigation strategy or general-causation rulings under Federal Rule of Evidence 702. Reserve service credits against genuine vendor defects, not every unsuccessful claim. For calling campaigns, preserve seller-specific permission as an operating standard while applying current TCPA requirements; the FCC's attempted federal one-to-one consent rule was vacated in 2025. Review state calling restrictions separately rather than presenting that vacated rule as binding nationwide.

## 3. Choose a Payment Rail That Matches the Obligor and the Funds  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A payment rail moves money; it does not establish entitlement to money. Select the rail only after identifying the obligor, earned amount, source account, release conditions, and reconciliation owner. Wire transfers suit larger advances when bank instructions have been verified through an independently sourced callback. Domestic commercial wires can provide rapid availability, but fraud recovery is difficult and finality depends on the applicable system and law. ACH credits suit repeat operating invoices; ACH debits require appropriate authorization and monitoring for returns. Do not treat an initiated ACH entry as irrevocably collected cash. Record invoice identifiers in remittance fields, reconcile deposits daily, and investigate payer-name mismatches before crediting balances. An illustrative $20,000 invoice carrying a $25 wire charge yields $19,975 cash if the vendor absorbs that charge; contractually allocate charges instead of discovering the shortfall after payroll. Dual verification of changed bank details is more valuable than an elaborate dashboard that accepts emailed substitutions. Separate the person editing vendor bank instructions from the person authorizing releases. Require a new callback for every changed destination, even when the email appears inside an authentic prior thread. Preserve bank confirmation and remittance evidence with the invoice.

Escrow, advances, statutory disbursement, and milestone holds are commercial or custody arrangements rather than interchangeable bank networks. A neutral commercial escrow may release an MSO project payment upon specified administrative evidence, but its terms must identify whose money is held, permitted investments, dispute procedures, and fees. Do not relabel lawyer trust funds as commercial escrow to bypass professional rules. A vendor capacity retainer is an advance against administrative services, distinct from a client's legal retainer; its refund and earning terms belong in the service agreement. Statutory or court-supervised disbursements follow their own priorities, including applicable Medicare recovery obligations under 42 U.S.C. 1395y(b)(2). They are not an ordinary mechanism for paying unrelated MSO invoices. Platform checkout can simplify small purchases, yet processor underwriting, reserves, chargebacks, and restricted-business terms can interrupt availability. Use a truthful business description and obtain processor acceptance for the actual services sold. A 10% hold on a $30,000 project ties up $3,000; specify release after ten business days following documented acceptance, rather than after an indefinite future settlement. Every hold needs a finite trigger and an identified person authorized to confirm it. Otherwise the apparent receivable is an unsecured option granted to the customer.

Protect fees with documentation that survives staff turnover and disagreement. The master services agreement should define administrative deliverables, fair-market pricing support, authorized change orders, acceptance timing, correction obligations, invoice disputes, suspension rights, and governing law. A narrow noncircumvention clause can protect confidential vendor introductions and administrative work product where enforceable; it cannot give a nonlawyer ownership of clients or restrict client choice. Invoice accepted work even when a firm later changes its litigation thesis. Permit prompt payment of undisputed amounts while identified defects are resolved. Factoring may convert an earned MSO receivable into earlier cash, but distinguish that receivable from legal fees, claimant recoveries, and contingent settlement rights. For a $100,000 eligible invoice, an illustrative 85% advance provides $85,000 initially. If the debtor pays in full and total agreed fees equal $3,000, a $12,000 reserve release produces $97,000 total proceeds before other charges. Check recourse, dilution, notification, assignment restrictions, and applicable enacted UCC Article 9 provisions. Never send patient charts to a factor merely to verify an invoice. Provide redacted acceptance evidence and obtain proper authorization for any necessary disclosures. Financing accelerates collection timing; it does not improve the underlying customer's obligation or erase disputes.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the payable obligation
   - Match the signed scope, purchase order, legal entity, and authorized payer.
   - Identify fixed fees, cost-plus components, earning dates, and any genuinely refundable advance before creating the invoice.
2. Attach minimum necessary acceptance evidence
   - Deliver a dated service report with checklist versions and specific exception codes.
   - Use project identifiers and redacted evidence; keep medical records out of ordinary accounts-payable email attachments.
3. Verify the destination and initiate payment
   - Confirm bank instructions independently and use the contractually selected payment arrangement.
   - Record remittance references, allocated fees, expected availability, applicable holds, and the person responsible for bank reconciliation.
4. Reconcile cash and control further exposure
   - Apply receipts, investigate shortages, release earned holds, and enforce credit limits.
   - Pause new discretionary purchases after the agreed trigger while preserving records and completing the contracted transition obligations.

## 4. Underwrite the Counterparty Before Scaling Financed Dockets or Advertising Spend  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The mapped play litigation_finance_mass_tort_docket_securitization requires separation between licensed legal economics and vendor economics. Its proposed 1,000–25,000 retained plaintiffs at $1,200–$2,500 acquisition cost imply $1.2 million to $62.5 million of acquisition capital before financing and ongoing case expense. At the low endpoint, a proposed 2.5x–4x valuation on $1.2 million means $3 million–$4.8 million, but neither a buyer nor a lawful transferable asset follows from that arithmetic. Claimants are not inventory that an MSO can sell. Any financing, portfolio interest transfer, or co-counsel arrangement requires an authorized legal structure, client-rights analysis, professional independence, and applicable lawyer fee-division requirements. A proposed 35–40% settlement contingency describes legal compensation under valid representation arrangements, not ordinary MSO revenue or an automatic co-counsel allocation. Arizona ABS licensing is entity-specific and does not export permission to every jurisdiction. Verify the Supreme Court's licensing record and actual scope before committing capital. The ordinary MSO earns its agreed administrative fees whether the supported docket concerns PFAS, AFFF, Paraquat, NEC, rideshare assault, or medical devices. Do not embed the play's speculative portfolio multiples in the $705,000 Base revenue case or imply every named docket remains open, viable, or fundable.

Apply the play's fallback as a funded risk-management plan, not a slogan. Six to ten genuinely differentiated tort or commercial-casualty exposures can reduce concentration, but shared causation theories, judges, experts, and funders can make apparently separate dockets highly correlated. A Federal Rule of Evidence 702 general-causation exclusion may impair many matters simultaneously. Model a complete freeze of the largest docket and thirty additional days of unpaid administrative invoices. Offloading 50% of a $1.2 million tranche cost basis means recovering $600,000 through an executed, permitted financing or transfer arrangement; assume zero recovery until conditions are satisfied and cash clears. The instruction to pay media vendors only on EMR-verified retainers also needs translation into lawful procurement. Verify service quality against medical evidence where authorized, but do not mechanically adopt per-retainer compensation if local rules prohibit paid recommendations or solicitation. Use fixed-capacity or cost-plus contracts, documented defect credits, and short purchase periods instead. Screen the firm through professional-license records, entity registries, trade references, authorized financial statements, and relevant public litigation records. Confirm unrestricted operating liquidity rather than counting client trust balances. Require prefunding when the firm cannot demonstrate an independent source of payment.

The second mapped play, agency_ad_spend_card_multiplier_rebate_cycling, monetizes genuine authorized advertising purchases only when issuer, platform, and client contracts permit the arrangement. For an illustrative $100,000 monthly media purchase, a 2% eligible cash-equivalent reward creates $2,000 before costs. Subtract $600 financing expense, $400 processing expense, and a $700 contractually owed client rebate: only $300 remains before operational overhead and taxes. A $2,500 reserve or unexpected interest charge overwhelms that spread. Count points at a supportable redemption value, observe caps and exclusions, and never describe manufactured transactions as client media. The $100,000 advertising volume is not automatically MSO revenue; document the principal-agent conclusion and reward accounting separately from the scorecard. Obtain written spending limits, authorized platform access, beneficiary ownership, and reimbursement timing. Prohibit rewards-driven spending beyond the approved campaign budget. A practical exposure limit includes unpaid invoices, committed contractor work, unreimbursed media, and retrieval obligations, less cleared advances. Review that total weekly against the customer's approved credit ceiling. If reimbursement slips or reward eligibility changes, move to client-direct billing or prefunded approved agency terms immediately. Pause new purchases without blocking the firm's access to its existing campaign records, claimant files, or required transition materials.
