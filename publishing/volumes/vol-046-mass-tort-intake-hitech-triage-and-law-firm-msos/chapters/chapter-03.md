## Executive Summary

Operators converge on one rule: timing beats raw case strength. Joe Fantini maps every tort from triggering event through a 6-9 month emerging phase, 1-2 years of discovery, Daubert and settlement, with entry cost rising like a stock price at each stage. Underwriting runs six gates: legal viability, science (2-3 studies, 100+ subjects), numerosity (25+ cases for the JPML, only 8-10% of viable claimants come forward), defendant, venue and stage. The Jon and Marc power rankings turn that into live windows: hair relaxer 6-9 months, Bard PowerPort almost gone. Buyers quote $5,000 leads against $50K-$250K typical payouts and 2x-10x returns, but Ron Lasorsa and Kate Hoffman report 20%-plus fake claims and zero-good-case dockets, so verification is priced in. The rankings also shift: Fantini projected GLP-1s past 10,000 cases, then doubted they would cross it.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Joe Fantini's core thesis is that timing outranks raw case strength. Every tort starts with a triggering event, whether that is a wave of contacts from injured people, a new study, an FDA recall or a label change [Ytmnnu5SPIg @ 00:00:16]. The emerging phase that follows is the highest-risk window and runs roughly 6 to 9 months, which is why Fantini usually sees only the most experienced mass tort firms get involved upfront, because the risk is huge [Ytmnnu5SPIg @ 00:00:50]. On the LexHive underwriting panel Fantini frames entry like buying a stock: the cost of getting in climbs at every stage, so the right entry point depends on how much risk the firm can stomach [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:30:37]. Moshe Amsel's Profit with Law session with Smart Reviews founder Kate Hoffman puts numbers on that curve. Early Depo-Provera leads cost $200 to $400, while getting them now for under $10K would be shocking, because once a tort moves along the availability of claimants falls and the price rises [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:22:13]. Settlement-stage torts carry minimal risk but lower opportunity, and the Jon and Marc rankings keep flagging the closing door: on Bard PowerPort, Fantini said the window for acquiring cases is almost gone [J0vh1lrcwJk @ 00:07:09].

The underwriting checklist is shared across plaintiff camps. Fantini's LexHive framework starts with legal viability: causes of action, injuries, statute of limitations, statute of repose, arbitration clauses and preemption, with the discovery rule available in 40-plus states [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:03:33]. Science is the second gate because mass torts are a battle of the experts. One study is usually not enough for Fantini to commit significant time and resources, so the rule is to wait for 2 to 3 studies and distrust anything under 100 subjects [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:10:34]. Numerosity, defendant profile, venue and stage round out the six factors. Coral Odiot-Rivera of Napoli Shkolnik builds the same map before launch, charting state-by-state causes of action, damages and defenses, including preemption, learned intermediary and statute of limitations [p519y7c1was @ 00:11:03]. Tobi Millrood would rather err on the side of cases that follow the science and are borne out by the medicine, and warns against signing uninjured pill takers [yCOD2nZqkok @ 01:05:50]. Kelsey Stokes of Fleming, Nolen & Jez adds that expert conversations should start before the MDL even forms, because internal documents keep reshaping the case like peeling an onion [YHzG4RIk3g8 @ 00:01:45].

The third point of agreement is that a docket is only worth what survives verification. Ron Lasorsa told Moore Injury Funding that over 20% of a 15,000-case transvaginal mesh docket was fake, sourced from Indian call centers, on a $50M loan, and the lender later went bankrupt at $250M [hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:10:18]. In the 3M earplug settlement, 996 of one firm's thousand claims were fake, and those claims dilute legitimate plaintiffs' recoveries [hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:08:21]. Kate Hoffman traced the current fraud wave to Camp Lejeune money: of 5,000 fact sheets only a couple hundred were workable, and some 1,000-case dockets had zero good cases [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:28:01]. Adam Slater runs the opposite of a volume shop, using experts who will say no and hunting for reasons to decline, on the logic that if no reason turns up, it is probably a really good case [HrjoNgJuhLU @ 00:16:07]. Slater's corollary is blunt: if you have to do a sales pitch, don't take the case [HrjoNgJuhLU @ 00:26:54]. Millrood's firm only accepts referred cases with verified screening, and the hormone therapy history shows why: about 3,000 calls in three weeks yielded roughly 25% injured callers [yCOD2nZqkok @ 01:15:40].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The clock Fantini teaches is specific enough to schedule capital against. After the 6 to 9 month emerging phase, litigation and discovery run about 1 to 2 years while case criteria tighten, then comes the Daubert hearing, where multiple experts present over days or even weeks [Ytmnnu5SPIg @ 00:02:03]. On LexHive the numbers sharpen: Daubert hearings typically run 2 to 3 days, the ruling lands 60 to 90 days later, the hearing falls about two-thirds to three-quarters of the way through the case, and the ruling applies MDL-wide [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:14:03]. The bellwether process is agreed 3 to 6 months in, with picks from plaintiffs, defendants and the judge at random, and the pool is cut from 50 to 30 to 10 trial-ready cases. The host, Michael Steni of Demand Lane, warned that weak filed cases can become defense bellwethers [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:18:12]. Judge Rodgers' Depo-Provera rocket docket set a Daubert hearing at the end of June and trial on December 7, with filings up more than 50% in two months to about 4,000 and a projection of 6,000 to 8,000 [7TfOtDaTez8 @ 00:13:12]. Hair relaxer's first bellwether is set for September 2027, and Fantini gives a 6 to 9 month window to get involved [LaXcjMiTQWI @ 00:12:05].

Numerosity is pure arithmetic. Fantini says the panel usually needs to see more than 25 cases before an MDL forms, but only 8 to 10% of people with viable claims ever come forward, so a 10,000-case tort needs about 100,000 injured people behind it [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:21:30]. That math drives the rankings. On GLP-1s, roughly 4,000 GI filings sit in one MDL and the vision-loss MDL is projected at a couple thousand, so the play is to run one ad, apply the gastro criteria, and then ask every contact about vision problems [7TfOtDaTez8 @ 00:09:15]. Spinal cord stimulators are expected to split into three MDLs, one per manufacturer, so firms pull records to identify the device maker the way they did with hernia mesh, and Fantini called it a great time to get involved [J0vh1lrcwJk @ 00:14:04]. For platform torts the acquisition move is internal: target minor children of existing clients for social media addiction, and sign up existing PI, family law and SSDI clients for video game addiction [YItGL_4J9i4 @ 00:09:26]. The hair relaxer instruction is just as direct: if you can get the cases, go and get them, because the window to sign runs roughly 6 to 8 months [J0vh1lrcwJk @ 00:21:46].

The unit economics come from the buying desk. On Profit with Law, leads were quoted at about $5,000 against cases that may resolve at $100K, $500K or $1M [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:11:58]. Typical payouts run $50K to $250K, a Tier 1 case is almost always worth over a million dollars in any tort, and one example docket averaged $108K with 10% Tier 1; settlements over the past five years returned 2x to 10x the purchase price [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:16:04]. Moshe Amsel layers a tax pitch on top: with a New York combined rate over 50%, a $1M marketing spend deployed within 12 months is deductible immediately, which Amsel frames as the government taking 50% of the risk [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:22:45]. Verification is priced in, not bolted on. Smart Reviews charges a $200 flat fee on roughly $1,000 leads for medical records and background checks, and the episode's closing checklist is know the tort, vet the marketer, and use the 14-day rejection window [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:39:39]. Lasorsa goes further with belly-to-belly checks by mobile notaries, expensive but less expensive than sanctions or bankruptcy [hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:16:51], then tracks every client red, yellow or green by contact at one, two and three weeks [hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:21:30].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Clock the tort
   - Place the target tort on the five-phase lifecycle and pick an entry stage by risk tolerance.
   - Triggering event, 6-9 month emerging phase, 1-2 years of litigation and discovery, Daubert, then settlement; entry cost rises with each stage like a stock price [LexHive Podcast (Formerly- The Legal Growth Lab) — Ytmnnu5SPIg @ 00:01:26] [LexHive Podcast (Formerly- The Legal Growth Lab) — jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:30:37]
2. Run the six-gate underwrite
   - Score legal viability, science, numerosity, defendant, venue and stage before committing spend.
   - Check causes of action, SOL, repose, arbitration and preemption; wait for 2-3 studies; confirm 25-plus cases are realistic given only 8-10% turnout [LexHive Podcast (Formerly- The Legal Growth Lab) — jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:03:33]
3. Price the docket
   - Set lead budgets against tiered case values rather than headline settlements.
   - Leads near $5,000 against $50K-$250K typical payouts, Tier 1 over $1M, and an example $108K docket average with 10% Tier 1 [Profit with Law (Moshe Amsel) — tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:15:45]
4. Verify before funding or filing
   - Confirm each claimant in person and keep them on a contact cadence after signing.
   - Belly-to-belly checks by mobile notaries, then red/yellow/green status by contact at one, two and three weeks [Moore Injury Funding — hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:16:51]

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split among these operators is entry timing, and the same analyst argues both sides depending on the tort. On the LexHive lifecycle breakdown, Joe Fantini calls the emerging phase the highest-risk stretch, running about 6 to 9 months, and says usually only the most experienced mass tort law firms get involved upfront because the risk is huge; the same segment tells firms to be wary of 'get involved now' emails pushing brand-new torts [Ytmnnu5SPIg @ 00:00:50]. On the Jon and Marc pre-MTMP rankings, Fantini flips to offense on spinal cord stimulators, projecting three MDLs, one per manufacturer, plus state-court activity, and declaring 'now's a great time to get involved' [J0vh1lrcwJk @ 00:14:04]. Kate Hoffman and Moshe Amsel put a price tag on waiting: early Depo-Provera leads ran $200 to $400, today anything under $10K would be 'shocking,' and the claimant pool shrinks over time, as seen in Roundup round 2 [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:22:13]. Fantini's underwriting framework reconciles the two positions by treating stage like a stock price, where entry cost rises with every phase and the right buy point is a function of each firm's risk tolerance rather than a universal rule [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:30:37].

Volume versus verification is the second fault line, and it runs straight through the power-rankings desk. On hair relaxer, the Jon and Marc crew tells listeners 'if you can get the cases, go and get them' inside a 6 to 8 month signing window [J0vh1lrcwJk @ 00:21:46], and later repeats 'if you guys have cases, get them on file' because time is running out on a docket projected at 13,000 to 15,000 total cases [7TfOtDaTez8 @ 00:15:15]. Kate Hoffman of Smart Reviews answers with the arithmetic of a bad marketer: a pitch of 100 Depo cases a week at $5K each yields 'zero,' so the shop charges a $200 flat fee on roughly $1,000 leads for medical records and background checks [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:35:45]. Adam Slater runs intake as a hunt for disqualifiers, using experts who will tell the firm no and actively looking for reasons to turn cases down: 'If I can't come up with a reason to turn it down, it's probably a really good case' [HrjoNgJuhLU @ 00:16:07]. The speed camp sells the closing window; the screening camp sells survival when that window shuts on a dirty file of unverifiable claimants.

Even the forecasts flip between episodes of the same show, and the swings are large enough to change a buy decision. On GLP-1 drugs, an earlier Jon and Marc edition logged about 3,000 GI cases, a vision MDL formed in December and a call for 10,000 cases within about 15 months [IUAtVEe81NI @ 00:03:58]; a later edition dropped the tort from #3 to #4, with 4,200 GI cases awaiting a Daubert ruling, about 300 NAION eye cases filed, and Fantini doubting the litigation ever crosses 10,000 filings [LaXcjMiTQWI @ 00:08:24]. Bard PowerPort draws a factual dispute over the scoreboard itself: the August rankings describe one defense verdict so far, a trial on August 18 and two more bellwethers in a short window [YItGL_4J9i4 @ 00:03:20], while the pre-MTMP edition insists the first trial was a mistrial rather than a defense verdict, the second produced a big verdict, fracture cases are strongest and infection cases unproven, and the acquisition window is 'almost gone' at the eighth or ninth inning [J0vh1lrcwJk @ 00:06:36]. The movement comes from inputs, not sentiment: case counts, Daubert dates and bellwether outcomes shift, and the rank shifts with them.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

Fraud is the failure mode every funder and vetter in this lineup has eaten firsthand. Ron Lasorsa walks through the 3M earplug settlement, 240,000 claimants and $6B, where 996 of one firm's thousand claims were fake Ugandan nationals, and stresses that those fake claims dilute legitimate plaintiffs' recoveries; defense counsel, by that account, is not motivated to root them out [hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:08:21]. Lasorsa's own docket took the hit directly: 15,000 transvaginal mesh cases, over 20% fake and sourced from Indian call centers, carried on a $50M loan, with the lender later going bankrupt at $250M [hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:10:18]. Kate Hoffman traces the current wave to Camp Lejeune money: out of 5,000 fact sheets only 'a couple hundred' were workable, some 1,000-case dockets had zero good cases, and the fraud now comes in three flavors, claimant coaching on Reddit and Facebook for $20 to $50, affiliate marketers backfilling budgets, and offshore rings recruiting from bankruptcy and arrest lists with $5 to 10K promises that double for five referrals [tjuCUb5CKVY @ 00:28:01]. Asbestos shows the long tail of the same problem, with almost 100 companies bankrupted over 40 years and Garlock surfacing duplicate claims for the same person at Illinois and Alabama sites [hBSeW8fizPA @ 00:00:39].

Winning big is its own trap. Adam Slater watched the $68M McGinnis Bergen verdict get reversed on an issue three circuits, the 4th, 7th and 11th, had already decided for plaintiffs, and draws the lesson bluntly: 'courts don't love big numbers' [HrjoNgJuhLU @ 00:09:28]. The $125M Verni verdict was also knocked out, and both reversed cases settled rather than being retried, so the headline number never became the payout [HrjoNgJuhLU @ 00:10:00]. Tobi Millrood's warning cuts against the science-first crowd: plaintiffs lost every PPA trial despite a strong Yale study, because jurors engaged in defensive attribution, and Millrood counters 'no cause, no case' openings with general plus specific causation and the Bradford Hill criteria rather than the bad-luck replication theory [yCOD2nZqkok @ 00:30:19]. Inside the MDL machine, the trap is self-inflicted: the bellwether process gets agreed 3 to 6 months in, picks come from plaintiffs, defendants and the judge at random, the pool is cut from 50 to 30 to 10 trial-ready cases, and the LexHive host warns that weak cases a firm filed can become the defense's bellwethers. Every filed case is a potential trial pick, not just a settlement unit [jn6XvG1gC9w @ 00:18:12].

The slow bleed is duration and attrition. Ron Lasorsa flags duration risk and 'loan to own' lenders, pointing to J&J talc at a decade, alongside event risk from clients taking their cases to another firm and the claim that e-signature has 'completely undermined' the attorney-client relationship [hZFw0Dvtfuk @ 00:02:50]. Coral Odiot-Rivera, who has worked the AFFF MDL since its inception, answers with contact discipline: reach out every quarter and get face time, or clients say 'I thought this was a scam' [p519y7c1was @ 00:19:48]. Carlton Fields defense lawyers Ryan Cobbs and Ashley Drumm hit the same calendar from the other side, telling teams to hold the budget discussion early and revisit it quarterly or annually because 'these things go on for a long time' [ieQJBTxA0z4 @ 00:09:46]. Product identification is the late-surfacing trap on Depo-Provera, the 'rocket docket' Judge Rodgers is running toward a December 7 trial: some cases go back 10 to 20 years, which makes proving which product the client took difficult even as filings jumped more than 50% in two months to about 4,000 [7TfOtDaTez8 @ 00:12:25]. Each of these costs lands after the lead has already been paid for.
