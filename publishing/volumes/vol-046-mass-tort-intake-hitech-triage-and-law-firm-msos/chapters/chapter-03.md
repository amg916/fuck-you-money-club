## 1. Price the administrative service, then reconcile every dollar  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with the revenue boundary, because a profitable spreadsheet can conceal an impermissible contract. The scorecard measures contracted flat or cost-plus MSO administrative, marketing, intake, and record-triage fees. It excludes settlement awards, client trust balances, loan principal, and the law firm's contingency receivable. Use a written statement of work defining staffing capacity, retrieval tasks, quality standards, invoice dates, and reasonable replacement obligations. Document fair-market pricing through comparable vendor quotations and time studies, rather than a percentage of legal fees or a payment for recommending counsel. ABA Model Rule 5.4 is a model framework; each applicable jurisdiction's adopted rules determine the enforceable restrictions. Marketing and solicitation restrictions also matter even when compensation is fixed. For this illustrative reconstruction, recognized annual service revenue equals gross service billings because there are no modeled pass-through collections or price concessions. Assess principal-versus-agent presentation under ASC 606 before adopting that treatment in actual accounts. If the operator merely arranges third-party retrieval, recognize its net contractual compensation where agent treatment applies, not the entire reimbursement. No additional retained_share haircut applies to these canonical service-revenue figures; they already exclude the underlying legal recovery.

The annual waterfall below assigns explicit costs to the three supplied outcomes without changing their revenue or profit. Conservative expenses total $118,400; Base expenses total $437,100; Scaled expenses total $625,000. Subtracting those amounts from $160,000, $705,000, and $1,250,000 produces $41,600, $267,900, and $625,000 respectively. These cost allocations are planning assumptions, not additional facts supplied by the scorecard. Delivery labor includes contractor execution, supervision, and applicable employee payroll burden. The first full-time position is the working owner, whose residual compensation is the displayed owner profit; the Scaled labor budget also accommodates the second full-time employee. Contractor counts describe three or four fractional specialists, not guaranteed full-time equivalents. Convert dollars into purchased hours before promising service levels: Base delivery labor of $132,000 buys 3,300 annual hours at a blended loaded $40 rate. That supports 275 hours monthly across intake, retrieval coordination, and chronology quality control. Technology covers licensed systems and secure processing, while overhead covers insurance, accounting, and operating administration. Owner profit is before the owner's personal taxes. The model includes no borrowing expense; actual financing costs reduce the residual dollar for dollar.

Treat the four-percent reserve as a budget for expected replacement delivery work, not a device for protecting inflated revenue. Conservative reserves are $6,400, Base reserves $28,200, and Scaled reserves $50,000. Within the Base monthly cohort, $2,350 funds incremental recontact, corrective retrieval, and deficiency repair beyond the routine labor and retrieval budgets. At $100 per repair, that supports 23.5 repairs monthly. Track the incremental tasks separately so the same contractor hour never lands in both ordinary delivery expense and reserve utilization. A planning reserve does not automatically qualify as an accounting provision: the controller determines recognition and classification from the actual obligation. If the customer receives a price credit instead, evaluate variable consideration and reduce recognized revenue when appropriate; do not label that credit a replacement expense merely to preserve the headline. Uncollectible balances require a separate credit-loss assessment. Record disputes by cause, vendor, originating cohort, and deadline. A late duplicate discovered during a replacement window belongs to its original cohort's economic history, even when cash leaves next quarter. This preserves an honest comparison between acquisition sources and prevents new invoices from masking old delivery failures.

## 2. Follow the claimant funnel without turning it into a referral tariff  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the complete conversion waterfall: raw inquiry to signed law-firm retainer to usable HITECH EMR/pharmacy proof to deficiency-free accepted case to collected MSO/vendor fee. Twenty duplicate or identity-deficient records are removed, leaving 180 retrieval attempts. Usable evidence emerges for 140 matters, and 120 survive the law firm's document and factual deficiency check. A retainer is a lawyer-client engagement, not evidence that exposure, injury, causation, venue, or limitations requirements are satisfied. TrustedForm certificates can help preserve the advertisement and permission trail; they do not establish claimant identity or adjudicate duplicate representation. Use appropriately permitted LexisNexis identity services alongside source-document checks and lawyer-directed conflict procedures. Tokenize identifiers, limit access, and resolve disputed matches through human review. A shared household phone number is not sufficient evidence of fraud. Collect only necessary identifiers and avoid sending medical narratives into advertising audiences. Preserve respectful, trauma-aware contact practices for survivors rather than increasing contact frequency to hit a conversion target.

The Base cohort's acquisition arithmetic is $15 per inquiry, $75 per signed retainer, $107.14 per evidence-supported matter, and $125 per deficiency-free accepted matter. These are internal cost ratios, not prices charged for recommending a lawyer. Monthly contracted revenue is $58,750; dividing by 120 accepted matters yields $489.58 of attributed service revenue per accepted matter. The contract still pays for specified services and capacity, rather than legal recovery or referral success. Routine retrieval spending of $3,000 across 180 attempts implies $16.67 per attempt as a blended planning assumption. Actual retrieval charges vary with request route, custodian, format, scope, and state rules. Under 45 CFR 164.524, patient-access requests have defined response duties and fee limitations; do not assume every authorization-based third-party request inherits the same fee treatment. The Ciox litigation narrowed parts of the third-party directive framework. Track the request's legal basis and verify the applicable rule before estimating cost or promising timing. A request under 45 CFR 164.508 and a patient-access request are not interchangeable simply because both retrieve records. Escalate an incorrect fee through the proper route without misrepresenting the requester's status.

Evidence quality is an economic control, not merely a clinical annotation. Build the chronology around dated source pages, provider identity, medication dispensing, exposure facts, and the firm's written intake criteria. An LLM can extract candidate ICD-10 codes and timeline events, but each material fact needs a retrievable source reference and a human check. An ICD-10 code alone does not establish product exposure or legal causation. Configure contractual confidentiality, appropriate business associate arrangements when applicable, access controls, and retention rules before processing protected health information. Measure false-positive extraction rates on sampled files and count correction time inside delivery costs. If usable proof falls from 140 to 122 matters while downstream acceptance remains 120/140, expected accepted output becomes 104.57 matters. Acquisition cost per accepted matter rises from $125 to $143.44. That deterioration first threatens delivery capacity, replacement expense, and renewals; it does not automatically erase fixed-fee revenue already earned. Define replacement deadlines and objective defect categories before launch. A general-causation exclusion under Federal Rule of Evidence 702 can damage an entire docket, so stop incremental acquisition when counsel withdraws the governing criteria instead of manufacturing medical support for unusable files.

## 3. Finance the service interval, not the hoped-for settlement  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Profit is measured over an accounting period; survival depends on the cash calendar. Base monthly expenses are $36,425, including the replacement budget, against $58,750 of monthly earned fees. For a conservative funding approximation, assume those expenses must be financed throughout a 30-day contractual replacement hold followed by 45 days from invoice issuance to collection. The intervals are sequential in this example, creating 75 days of operating float; do not add them if the actual contract runs them concurrently. Using a 30-day planning month, the cash requirement is $36,425 multiplied by 75/30, or $91,062.50. This is a cost-carry approximation, not a claim that receivables equal that amount. Earned-fee receivables and unbilled contract assets depend on performance, invoicing, and revenue recognition. Build a weekly cash schedule with media debits, contractor payment dates, retrieval charges, taxes, and expected receipts. Contractor net-15 terms may reduce the peak, while advance media funding may increase it. Treat the approximation as a deliberately conservative starting point, then replace it with the actual timing of each cash line. Never use a client's trust funds to bridge the operator's working-capital deficit.

The advertised $12,000–$50,000 startup range and $2,500–$14,000 monthly opex range do not establish sufficient cash for these fully loaded cohorts. Their scope must be reconciled against acquisition, contractor delivery, retrieval, replacement costs, and collection timing; they cannot replace the complete expense waterfall. A $30,000 customer advance reduces external funding to $96,062.50, assuming the advance is available for contracted services and no refund restriction prevents its use. Receipt does not itself create earned revenue; account for the advance against performance obligations. Negotiate shorter invoicing intervals, transparent defect notices, and undisputed-amount payment rather than demanding an open-ended hold on the entire cohort. If five percent of a Base monthly invoice remains disputed, $2,937.50 is temporarily unavailable even when the other $55,812.50 arrives. Place that amount in the weekly cash schedule. Distinguish a delayed payment from a permanent concession and a disputed service from a genuinely uncollectible customer. **SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the cohort's contractual assumptions
   - Record fee scope, earned-service milestones, hold periods, invoice dates, and replacement obligations.
   - Label intervals as sequential or concurrent and distinguish a customer advance from earned service revenue.
2. Schedule actual cash commitments
   - Place media debits, contractor payments, retrieval charges, and expected receipts into a thirteen-week forecast.
   - Include opening balances and cohort overlap; calculate the lowest weekly cash balance rather than relying only on average DSO.
3. Resolve defects without hiding revenue adjustments
   - Separate corrective delivery expense, price concessions, credit losses, and temporary payment holds.
   - Attach every adjustment to its original cohort, supporting document, responsible party, and expected resolution date.
4. Release spend against verified liquidity
   - Compare the next cohort's peak funding requirement with unrestricted cash and committed financing.
   - Reduce acquisition volume or negotiate customer prepayment when liquidity is insufficient; exclude client trust balances entirely.
