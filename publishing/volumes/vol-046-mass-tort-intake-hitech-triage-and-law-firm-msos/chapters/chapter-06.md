## 1. Mine operating pressure rather than claimant identities  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with law firms whose public activity implies a specific administrative bottleneck, not a presumed willingness to buy claimants. Review the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation's published MDL statistics, then inspect the relevant federal court's case-management orders through PACER. Record census deadlines, plaintiff fact-sheet requirements, deficiency procedures, and newly appointed leadership counsel. These events create demand for document indexing, authorized retrieval, intake scheduling, and exception management; they do not establish that any individual qualifies for relief. Cross-check firm websites, attorney directories, LinkedIn hiring notices, Google Ads Transparency Center, and Meta Ad Library for active campaigns and intake hiring. A firm advertising across three jurisdictions while recruiting records coordinators deserves investigation before a dormant firm with a prestigious verdict page. Build a company-level research ledger containing source URL, observation date, jurisdiction, responsible business contact, operational hypothesis, and next verification question. Keep claimant names and medical facts out of this prospecting ledger. Public filing access does not justify republishing sensitive allegations or using a health condition to target an individual. Capture only the minimum business signal needed to explain why a defined service could remove a measurable queue.

Allocate the first research sprint to forty firms across two active dockets and one geographic operating cluster. Score each account on five dimensions worth twenty points apiece: documented deadline pressure, observable intake volume, records backlog, accessible operating sponsor, and ability to purchase a fixed administrative scope. Award twenty points only for direct evidence, ten for a corroborated inference, and zero when evidence is absent. Pursue accounts scoring at least seventy after a discovery conversation confirms budget and a responsible lawyer. A high public signal score never substitutes for buyer confirmation. Ask how many authorized requests remain open, the oldest unresolved request age, the current retrieval cost, and who decides whether evidence satisfies the firm's criteria. Request aggregate counts before requesting any patient information. Disqualify demands for purchased signatures, payment tied to recovery, undisclosed referral compensation, or unrestricted access to another firm's claimant database. An incumbent vendor is not a dead end: propose overflow capacity for one provider network or one document category with explicit ownership boundaries. Your strongest opening is a documented operational discrepancy, such as growing fact-sheet obligations without corresponding records staffing, followed by a narrow offer to measure and clear that discrepancy.

Convert each signal into an evidence-backed offer sheet before sending outreach. For a fact-sheet deadline, offer a ten-business-day document inventory and exception queue. For a provider backlog, offer an authorized request-status desk with daily aging reports. For an advertising expansion, offer consent-evidence reconciliation and duplicate detection before new contacts enter the dialing system. For a Rule 702 development, offer administrative reclassification under criteria supplied by counsel, never an independent prediction of causation or settlement value. Federal Rule of Evidence 702 and Daubert-related exclusions can collapse acquisition economics even when intake conversion remains strong. Require the firm to identify which campaigns should pause when a material order changes its strategy. Separate recurring service demand from speculative litigation demand by asking whether the task remains useful if new advertising stops. Indexing existing records usually does; purchasing another batch of unverified inquiries may not. Price the diagnostic as a fixed scope that produces a queue map, sample audit, and remediation estimate. Do not provide free patient-level processing merely to win access. The initial asset is a repeatable operating relationship with a solvent buyer, not an inventory of prospective plaintiffs.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the buyer behind the visible litigation signal**
> 
> Public activity earns a discovery question; verified operating friction earns a proposal. Preserve that distinction throughout sourcing.
> - Save the exact case-management order and deadline paragraph, then ask the operations sponsor which internal queue owns the resulting work.
> - Ask for an aggregate provider-aging histogram before negotiating retrieval pricing; a few unresponsive custodians can dominate labor cost.
> - Demonstrate chronology formatting with synthetic documents, then use a separately authorized sample to measure actual extraction quality.
> - Sell incumbent-vendor overflow by provider network or document type, with mutually exclusive ownership and a daily duplicate-request reconciliation.

## 2. Build a consent-first screening offer without selling permission  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play tcpa_litigator_scrub_honeypot_arbitrage belongs to TRACK_2, but its defensible implementation is a consent-first screening service, not a device for contacting people who appear unlikely to sue. Use lawfully accessed docket-derived risk signals only as a secondary review input alongside authoritative do-not-call suppression, revocation records, number reassignment checks where applicable, and the required consent evidence for the proposed technology and message. Never build deceptive honeypots or contact a person because they were absent from a litigation list. A plaintiff-name match neither authorizes contact nor proves abuse; common names, stale telephone numbers, and unrelated proceedings create obvious false positives. Restrict enrichment to a documented purpose, approved access method, and minimal retention period. When matching confidence is weak, disable uncertain enrichment and require independently verified consent and authoritative suppression checks. Record the reason for every blocked or released contact. TrustedForm can preserve evidence about a submitted form, but a certificate alone does not establish that the disclosure covered the actual seller, communication method, or later campaign. LexisNexis identity services likewise require appropriate access and permitted use; they do not provide universal visibility into competing law-firm retainers.

Package screening around processing capacity and documented work. An illustrative monthly scope covers ten thousand submitted contact records for a $2,400 fixed administrative fee. At an assumed $0.06 per record for permitted verification inputs, data costs equal $600; twenty analyst hours at $30 cost another $600; allocated tooling and secure hosting add $300. The resulting $900 contribution is 37.5% before other business overhead, not a replacement for the volume's canonical owner-profit scenarios. Supplier prices must be quoted before contracting. If ambiguous matches double analyst time, contribution falls to $300, or 12.5%; cap the exception queue and sell additional authorized capacity through a written scope change. The fallback is suppression and evidence verification without docket enrichment, not relaxed contact standards. Keep records of inputs, rule versions, dispositions, and manual corrections so the buyer can reproduce a decision. Do not publish accusatory labels about individuals or use the service to conceal unlawful outreach. A screening product is valuable because it documents lawful operating decisions and catches broken evidence chains, not because it promises immunity from a complaint or identifies people whose rights matter less.

Separate business-development outreach to firm personnel from claimant communications commissioned by the firm. For business email, use accurate sender details, truthful subjects, the required postal address, and a functioning opt-out under 15 U.S.C. § 7704; maintain suppression across campaigns. A publicly listed mobile number is not permission for automated marketing calls or texts. Apply 47 U.S.C. § 227, 47 CFR § 64.1200, and relevant state requirements to the actual channel and technology. Treat seller-specific, one-to-one consent as an operating standard rather than claiming that the FCC's vacated 2023 one-to-one amendment currently supplies a universal federal requirement. Preserve the precise disclosure, named recipient, timestamp, source page, and subsequent revocation history. Counsel should approve claimant-facing scripts and jurisdictional advertising disclosures before use. Nonlawyer personnel may collect facts and arrange attorney contact but must not assess legal rights, promise recovery, or pressure signatures. ABA Model Rule 5.4 is a model framework; binding state rules control the engagement. A licensed Arizona alternative business structure changes only what its actual authorization permits. It does not automatically extend fee-sharing authority across states or to an unaffiliated marketing vendor that invokes the structure in a proposal.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before a campaign receives operational capacity**
> 
> A buyer's urgency does not replace the evidence required to run the assigned channel, handle records, or invoice the promised service.
> - Verify state-specific fee, advertising, solicitation, and nonlawyer-role requirements; use fixed or cost-plus administrative pricing with no recovery participation.
> - Reproduce consent and suppression decisions for a sample, including revocations, seller identity, and the exact planned contact technology.
> - Confirm lawful record-request authority, applicable business associate agreements, approved subprocessors, and role-restricted access before receiving patient information.
> - Obtain counsel-owned evidence criteria, a signed scope, a funded payment schedule, and bounded correction periods before starting production.

## 3. Reconcile the entire evidence waterfall before quoting capacity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use one cohort ledger to track raw inquiry to signed law-firm retainer to usable HITECH EMR or pharmacy proof to deficiency-free accepted case to collected MSO/vendor fee. Keep the last stage at the service-contract level: fees compensate administrative work, not the presence of a retained claimant or a recovery. An illustrative cohort starts with 200 inquiries, loses forty duplicates or invalid submissions, and produces 84 retainers from 160 unique inquiries, a 52.5% signing rate. Request authorized records for all 84; forty-two produce usable proof, a 50% evidence yield. Counsel accepts thirty-four after eight files remain deficient, an 81.0% acceptance rate from usable proof and 17% from raw inquiries. The fixed administrative pilot earns $8,000 for the contracted service period; $6,000 has been collected and $2,000 remains receivable. Collection is therefore 75% of invoiced fees, not thirty-four case sales. Log retainer execution separately from legal acceptance, and never treat either as an administrative worker's legal judgment. Maintain dated exclusion reasons so the firm can distinguish duplicate records, deficient authority, missing exposure evidence, clinical mismatch, and outstanding provider responses rather than compressing every loss into a misleading lead-quality percentage.

Under 45 CFR § 164.524, distinguish an individual's access request from a third-party disclosure authorization under § 164.508. The usual access response deadline is thirty days, with one additional thirty-day extension when the required written explanation and completion date are supplied. Shorter applicable state deadlines and record-specific rules require separate tracking. Electronic delivery depends on the records held and the requested form being readily producible. Do not describe HITECH as an automatic extraction API or assume every law-firm request receives the individual-access fee limit. Ciox-related limits distinguish third-party directions from personal access, including the scope of electronic health-record directions; select the correct pathway and budget the actual permitted charge. Pharmacy histories may identify dispensing dates without establishing ingestion, dose adherence, or causation. Use an LLM chronology parser only within approved data-handling arrangements, with source-page citations and human verification of dates, product names, and negation. ICD-10 codes help organize records but do not independently prove exposure or case eligibility. Prohibit unsupported inference from a missing page. Route clinical ambiguity to qualified reviewers and eligibility decisions to counsel, while keeping the administrative queue moving on undisputed indexing tasks.

Budget the illustrative $8,000 pilot with $2,400 intake labor, $2,100 retrieval outlays at $25 for each of 84 requests, $800 quality review, and $300 screening tools. Total direct cost is $5,600, leaving $2,400 before other overhead; this 30% pilot contribution is distinct from annual net margin. Stress duplicates to 35% of inquiries: 130 unique inquiries yield approximately 68 retainers at the same signing rate. If usable proof falls to 35%, approximately 24 files have proof and nineteen reach acceptance. At $45 per request, retrieval costs become $3,060; add $800 rework to unchanged intake, review, and tooling costs for $7,360 total direct cost and only $640 contribution. A fourteen-calendar-day correction window should cover documented vendor processing errors, not free replacement of claimants whose evidence fails legal criteria. Canonical annual revenue bridges are twenty $8,000 service-months for $160,000, forty-seven $15,000 service-months for $705,000, and fifty $25,000 service-months for $1,250,000. These are illustrative capacity packages, not case prices. Formula v2.0 total costs remain $118,400, $437,100, and $625,000, yielding owner profits of $41,600, $267,900, and $625,000. Recognize agency pass-throughs net when required by ASC 606; exclude settlements, trust funds, and client advertising spend controlled and purchased directly by the firm.

## 4. Run a four-month cadence that closes paid administrative mandates  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run an account-based cadence against verified business contacts rather than a bulk claimant list. On day one, send a short email citing one public operating trigger and offering one bounded diagnostic. On day four, place a lawful manual call to the published business line and ask who owns the records queue. On day eight, share a synthetic example of an aging report or chronology exception register through the channel the contact accepts. On day fifteen, send a concise close-the-loop message with an opt-out and stop further campaign outreach when requested. Use LinkedIn for relevant professional engagement, not automated message flooding. Build inbound demand with a provider-delay calculator, a fact-sheet readiness checklist, and a webinar co-presented with a properly engaged lawyer who owns legal interpretations. Forms should request company and workflow information, not public uploads of medical records. Send sensitive documents only through the approved secure intake channel after authority is established. Attribute inbound inquiries to the originating content and the actual buyer role. A hundred downloads from claimants are not equivalent to three qualified conversations with operations directors who control budget and can authorize a paid diagnostic.

Use explicit planning benchmarks rather than presenting invented market averages. An initial four-month target is 120 researched firms, ninety with verified business contacts, twenty-seven substantive replies, eighteen discovery meetings, nine qualified scopes, six signed pilots, and five funded starts. Those assumptions imply a 30% reply rate from contacted accounts, 66.7% meeting conversion from replies, 50% scope conversion from meetings, 66.7% signing from scopes, and 83.3% funding from signed pilots. Five funded starts equal 4.2% of researched accounts. If the first thirty contacted accounts produce fewer than three substantive replies, inspect trigger quality, deliverability, and buyer selection before increasing volume. If meetings occur but scopes stall, require a budget owner and quantified backlog before drafting another proposal. Three wins can be reached with fewer productive accounts; ten requires stronger conversion, more qualified accounts, or a longer cycle, not weaker verification. Define a win as an executed administrative mandate with its required initial payment received. A verbal commitment, unsigned statement of work, or unfunded retainer is pipeline. Track days between each stage and record the buyer's stated loss reason to separate pricing problems from missing authority, procurement delay, and insufficient demand.

Close objections by changing scope or evidence, never by changing the compensation into a disguised legal contingency. When a firm requests payment only for signed cases, offer a fixed capacity block with transparent activity reporting and capped correction credits for administrative errors. When it already uses a vendor, isolate one overflow queue and reconcile ownership daily. When price is challenged, compare the buyer's documented labor and retrieval costs against a smaller pilot rather than asserting universal savings. When a buyer asks for unrestricted database access, demonstrate synthetic output and request a minimized, authorized sample through the secure channel. The statement of work should define deliverables, provider-delay exceptions, counsel-owned eligibility criteria, retrieval authorization, security responsibilities, invoice timing, termination, and data return. Schedule weekly operating reviews covering open requests, duplicate rates, evidence yield, accepted files, invoiced service dollars, and collected cash. In month one, validate demand and access controls; in month two, deliver paid pilots; in months three and four, convert successful pilots to recurring capacity. Keep startup deployment within the $12,000–$50,000 capital range and planned monthly operating spend within $2,500–$14,000 during launch. Those launch ranges do not replace the larger annual total-cost budgets required by the canonical operating scenarios.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the first forty-account evidence ledger
   - Research two active dockets and one operating geography during the first two weeks.
   - Attach dated public sources, score five twenty-point dimensions, and verify an operations sponsor before handling patient information.
2. Validate authority and sell the bounded diagnostic
   - Run the four-touch business cadence and qualify budget, backlog, and fixed-fee purchasing authority.
   - Present synthetic output, approve jurisdiction-specific scripts, and execute access and service agreements before production.
3. Deliver and reconcile the first paid cohorts
   - Track inquiries, retainers, usable proof, accepted files, invoices, and collections in separate linked registers.
   - Reconcile duplicate exclusions, actual retrieval charges, correction windows, and source-cited chronology checks during weekly operating reviews.
4. Convert funded pilots into recurring capacity
   - Use measured queue improvement and collected service revenue to close three-to-ten funded mandates within one-to-four months.
   - Treat the timing and win count as targets, update conversion assumptions from observed results, and expand only within funded capacity.
