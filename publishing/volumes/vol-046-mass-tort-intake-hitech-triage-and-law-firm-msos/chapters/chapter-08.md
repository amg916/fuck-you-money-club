## Executive Summary

Operators running mass-tort and PI volume on Filevine, Litify and Case Cube converge on one rule: the case-management platform must be the single system of record, with intake, medicals, liens, trust and settlement inside one controlled stack. LawAccounting shows a $2M IOLTA wire auto-allocated by demand into 12 receipts, a check run producing 48 checks, and fee codes that block progress unless they total 100%. ## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The baseline every serious operator agrees on is a single system of record. LawAccounting runs Case Cube and its trust ledger natively on Salesforce so case management and trust accounting share one source of truth, then layers role-built dashboards for attorneys, paralegals, bookkeepers and case managers on top [dqv7XkRgaQQ @ 00:00:45]. Their Mass Tort Portfolio view works as a command center that drills from docket to plaintiff group to individual matter, which is the shape a mass-tort shop needs once inventory stacks up across referring firms and settlement waves. How to Simple describes Litify the same way: a Salesforce-built platform carrying matters from intake to resolution with configurable workflows for PI, mass torts and corporate work, automated task assignment, and a CRM that fires client follow-ups [3bh0rzq79u8 @ 00:01:17]. SelectHub's Filevine-sponsored comparison draws the line sharply against AI-only layers: Harvey augments an existing system of record and offers no case management or billing, while Filevine positions itself as the operations-first record for mass torts with AI embedded throughout [RE04OZvO3RQ @ 00:03:20]. The operational takeaway is blunt. If billing and financial management are on the requirements list, SelectHub says Filevine is the only option in that matchup, and an AI drafting tool bolted onto spreadsheets cannot carry a docket or move settlement money.

The second point of agreement is that automation is only as good as the manual process underneath it. Stephanie at Vineskills, a paralegal with a workers' comp background, tells firms to exhaust Filevine's native tools first, meaning Doctrine, task flows and deadline chains, before paying for a third-party engine [YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:02:33]. Workato's integration user holds org-admin rights only, so it can only do what a user could do by hand, and the API documentation sets the real limits. Susie Lewis, who implemented Workato inside Filevine, frames the same rule: if a user can do it, it can probably be automated, provided the logic is consistent, and every build is unique to the firm's template with no copy and paste [9_hNat-thXA @ 00:08:09]. Both push restraint. Lewis's team offers free audits and warns against stuffing a template with automations just because they sound cool, while Stephanie makes every builder answer what happens if the automation fails, because a broken deadline chain can end in the m word, malpractice [YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:06:00]. Josh Hostilo of Filevine adds the staffing piece: hire a dedicated platform admin rather than handing the system to attorneys or paralegals, a lesson from a 97-person prior firm, and steer small firms toward managed-service consultants instead [9AejH9lLg90 @ 00:16:56].

Every practitioner who touched AI or bulk intake insists on a human checkpoint and a hard control at the front door. LawAccounting makes the conflict check mandatory on bulk intake; in their demo it flagged 4 conflicts across 4 plaintiffs that had to be dispositioned or dismissed before the CSV import would proceed, which they call scalable intake with controls intact [dqv7XkRgaQQ @ 00:04:49]. Daya Naef, a Filevine prompt engineer and former paralegal, demos AI medical chronologies that extract date of service by date of service and export to Excel, but says the output is a fairly good foundation for the work rather than a replacement for human judgment [1QCGIBQ7PQI @ 00:09:42]. Jay Stephanie of Levinson and Stefani keeps AI Fields output in its own section so the team can always tell machine work from manual work [TrEm-Mk2jeQ @ 00:14:03]. Tech Help Man, reviewing Litify's public product pages rather than a live login, calls the agentic case expert an ambitious promise and tells buyers to verify what each agent can access and change, where a person must approve, and to demo a representative workflow with human review defined before relying on AI [g6KQDADEgmI @ 00:11:20]. Clear approval rules, in their words, give each step an accountable owner.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

LawAccounting's mass-tort run is the most complete money workflow on record. Plaintiffs are grouped into cohorts by referring firm, injury type or settlement wave, such as a Camp Lejeune group, and loaded by CSV with each row mapped to a docket, group and fee code [dqv7XkRgaQQ @ 00:03:37]. The Fee Code Library splits each settlement among the primary firm, referring counsel and co-counsel, and the code must total 100%; edit one line and the 95% remainder blocks progress. The Mass Expense Allocator spread $12,000 of investigator fees across 12 matters, split equally, weighted by demand, or firm-only, and paid expenses post as disbursements while unpaid ones become payables [dqv7XkRgaQQ @ 00:06:03]. A $2M wire into IOLTA auto-allocated by demand; nudging one allocation from 175 to 170 left $500 unallocated before 12 receipts posted. The settlement check run used a $393,000 spread with a cap-override percentage and produced 48 checks, followed by pre-bill review, closing statements, e-signature, and approve-and-post-to-trust attorney remittances before bulk printing [dqv7XkRgaQQ @ 00:11:11]. On the Filevine side, David at Vineskills runs mass torts through parent/child project linking and a Lead Docket to Zapier to Filevine pipe for intake [oNoHY7832hw @ 00:29:25].

Automation cost is counted in tasks, and the engineers who build it give exact numbers. David, Vineskills' Zapier engineer for about two years and maintainer of the Filevine connector, says the connector itself is free, but the free Zapier plan likely cannot use it; the Professional plan runs about $30 a month on monthly billing for 750 tasks [oNoHY7832hw @ 00:31:13]. A case-numbering automation eats 2 to 3 tasks, so 750 tasks covers roughly 250 new cases a month, and three to five tasks is their working estimate per automation [oNoHY7832hw @ 00:32:40]. Mass-tort clients they have supported sit closer to a million tasks or more, which lands near $3,300 a month, so loops are expensive and code blocks are the lever for cutting task count. Stephanie applies the same discipline in Workato: prefer action-button or on-create triggers and avoid on-save and activity triggers that burn tasks every time someone touches a record [YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:16:34]. Workato itself is licensed and sold through Filevine as an annual usage-based add-on bought via the account manager, per Susie Lewis, and it reaches Google Suite and custom APIs when compatible [9_hNat-thXA @ 00:01:27]. Lewis's team bills hourly and optimization blocks month to month.

For medical records and liens, the working build is a collection section with one card per provider. Jay Stephanie, whose Chicago firm has run Filevine for about five years, uses that layout so reports can sum across cards, starting with provider, treatment dates, injuries and charged amount, then adding complaints, diagnosis codes, balance owed as a lien signal, and payors, because anything you want reported has to be a field [TrEm-Mk2jeQ @ 00:01:17]. Their medical bill summary exports to Excel with a SUM formula and gets pasted into interrogatories, demands and mediation submissions; records-past-due and bills-past-due reports email to paralegals weekly [TrEm-Mk2jeQ @ 00:10:26]. Their retrieval vendor auto-uploads records into the damages and medicals folders with #MedRecs and #MedBills tags and pushes invoices into expenses. Susie Lewis automates the handoff: an Intake send-to-Medical button creates a Medical item per provider, and a lien/LOP Yes answer spawns a Lien item mapped to the lien holder, cross-linked back to its Medical card [9_hNat-thXA @ 00:19:04]. Daya Naef shows AI Fields firing on upload of a roughly 70-page record with about 10 preconfigured prompt types such as chronology, pain scores and deposition questions [1QCGIBQ7PQI @ 00:06:29]. **SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Scope the automation target
   - Hunt for duplicate data entry and processes that work but feel manual or clunky.
   - Ask where effort is duplicated, such as an incident date typed into both Intake and Case Summary, and which consistent-logic steps feel clunky, such as revealing hidden litigation sections; log each candidate with status, priority, template, impacted sections and impacted fields [Vineskills — 9_hNat-thXA @ 00:04:48].
2. Pick a task-efficient trigger
   - Use an action button or on-create trigger instead of on-save or activity triggers.
   - Action-button and on-create triggers fire only when intended, while on-save and activity triggers burn tasks on every record touch; remember hidden sections cannot receive data [Vineskills — YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:15:15].
3. Build in order with error handling
   - Sequence get project, get form, create collection item, then add an error recipe and run test recipe.
   - Run the steps in order, attach an error recipe with email alerts so failures do not spam staff, rerun on a 504 timeout, and use test recipe before going live [Vineskills — YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:31:42].
4. Go live and document
   - Remove the test project ID, notify users, note each automated field, and export the tracker.
   - Strip the test project ID limit, tell users what changed, record the automation in each field's internal notes, and export the tracker collection as documentation instead of setting it and forgetting it [Vineskills — YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:50:49].

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Small Settings That Save Mass-Tort Teams Hours**
> 
> These are the field-tested moves practitioners use to keep automations contained, document productions clean, lead texting safe, and task assignments visible.
> - Fence every new recipe: set trigger conditions on section and field selectors, exclude the API user, and limit the run to a single test project ID until it is proven [Vineskills — YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:24:15].
> - Combine documents first, then Bates stamp, because stamping does not combine for you; copy the original before redacting, since saved redactions are permanent [Vineskills — XAU9uhQg6nk @ 00:17:03].
> - Auto-text web leads with a five-minute reply promise and the number you will call from, but use keyword stops so the system never auto-replies to injury photos [Lunch Hour Legal Marketing — 9AejH9lLg90 @ 00:15:28].
> - Reassign only uncompleted tasks; a completed task moved to another user stays invisible on their list, so start a fresh thread from the write-a-note field instead [Errol Janusz — mfKVI7JPrYc @ 00:00:37].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failures are silent ones. Errol Janusz, who runs IT and cybersecurity for law offices, shows that reassigning a completed Filevine task to another user transfers it but leaves it invisible on the new user's task list; the demo reassignment to Ivan stayed complete and never surfaced, so the fix is a fresh thread through the write-a-note field rather than subtasks hung on the old task [mfKVI7JPrYc @ 00:01:16]. Stephanie at Vineskills names malpractice as the explicit risk: ask what happens if an automation fails against a deadline, avoid on-save and activity triggers that burn tasks, and lock trigger conditions to a test project ID while excluding the API user, because a loose trigger means that where you expected one item you have ten thousand [YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:29:36]. Without an error recipe the system spams the admin inbox, a 504 timeout means a rerun, and too often teams set it and forget it because it worked once [YtXAzduQ3PE @ 00:51:21]. David at Vineskills adds that one trigger with a few actions is fair DIY territory, but bulk creates or deletes in a live environment are dangerous, with Zapier's replayable run history as the debugging lifeline [oNoHY7832hw @ 00:20:13].

Task metering is where the economics bite. David prices the Zapier Professional plan at roughly $30 a month on monthly billing for 750 tasks, warns the free plan likely cannot use the Filevine connector, and estimates three to five tasks per automation, so a two-to-three-task case-numbering build covers about 250 cases a month [oNoHY7832hw @ 00:32:24]. Mass tort volume changes the bill: clients near a million tasks or more run about $3,300 a month, loops are costly, and code blocks cut task use [oNoHY7832hw @ 00:31:30]. Trust math fails closed on purpose. In the LawAccounting demo, every fee code must total 100 percent, so trimming one line leaves 95 percent allocated and blocks progress, and cutting one settlement allocation from 175 to 170 on a $2M IOLTA wire left $500 unallocated before 12 receipts could post [dqv7XkRgaQQ @ 00:09:02]. On the time side, SelectHub's sponsored review, which credits Strong & Hanni with escaping $6,000 to $8,000 a month in storage overages, prices search friction at $66 lost per 10 minutes for a $400-an-hour attorney, and concedes Filevine may not suit solo practitioners with very low case volume or teams unwilling to invest in proper implementation [BBsBjTLqlDI @ 00:06:02].

Document handling carries irreversible traps. Libby at Vineskills, a former PI litigation paralegal, warns that redaction is permanent once saved, so copy the original first and hit the hidden save button; Bates stamping does not combine documents, so combine first, then stamp; share links require an expiration date, and deleting one erases its audit record [XAU9uhQg6nk @ 00:13:36]. Related-project note copying skips documents, document locks only release after about 24 hours, and a hundred tags buries the ten vitals the screen shows. Jay Stephanie of Levinson and Stefani, five years into Filevine, reports AI Fields still duplicating CPT codes while improving on ICD-10, so the firm orders records in three-month batches and deletes superfluous pages before upload, with a couple hundred pages taking five to ten minutes [TrEm-Mk2jeQ @ 00:15:30]. Susie Lewis flags that two date-of-accident fields of the native incident-date type auto-mirror and reject differing dates, and that automations cannot create UI features or popups [9_hNat-thXA @ 00:11:32]. On client messaging, Hostilo's rule is blunt: auto-text web leads a five-minute reply promise with the calling number, but never auto-reply to injury photos, and use keyword stops [9AejH9lLg90 @ 00:15:28].
