## Executive Summary

Operators working Ritchie Bros., IronPlanet, AuctionTime and BidSpotter agree on one core rule: on Ritchie's unreserved floor the hammer price is the price, so the edge belongs to the buyer who inspects in person, reads the terms, and sets a hard ceiling before the clock runs. ET Transport's Ronin used the Bid Agent Chrome extension and $500k of Ritchie credit to land a 2023 Cascadia at $76k against $81k-$85k comps. The same session walked away when a favored unit hit $102k. Street costs stack fast: 12-19% buyer's premiums, a 10% auction fee plus 13% HST, a 3-4% card surcharge, and wire-preferred payment. Operators split on gambling on inoperable iron and on buying sight unseen. Burned buyers report hidden rust, dead batteries and no returns, and one seller's results landed about $18k under expectation.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every Ritchie Bros. operator on record works from the same foundation: the auction is unreserved, so the hammer price is the price. BuildWitt's walk through the world's biggest sale lays out the rule that owners cannot bid on or buy back their own equipment. It calls these 'very clear, honest auctions' and shows timed lots going to the highest bidder regardless of price [VLza4LoUZfo @ 00:07:31]. The Ritchie rep interviewed by ET Transport repeats it to a nervous seller: everything sells on auction day. The September sale drew 7,800 bidders, including buyers hunting parts trucks, so the fear of no bids is answered by sheer crowd size [UnNSCtlW-0k @ 00:12:21]. Sobre Orugas carries the same doctrine into Mexico. There, Ritchie tells consignors there is 'no crystal ball' and that estimates come only from statistics on past results [xUze3OGayRk @ 00:11:37]. The operator consequence is blunt. Sellers surrender price control the moment they consign, and buyers face no hidden reserve, only rival bidders. That symmetry is why the practitioners treat historical results as the real pricing engine. The rep points sellers to free past-result browsing for benchmarking, and buyers lean on the same sold data before they ever touch a bid button [UnNSCtlW-0k @ 00:04:19].

Inspection is the second point of agreement, and it cuts across trucks, iron and shop tools. Ronin of ET Transport, after a live session buying 2023 Cascadias, tells buyers to walk the yard with a diesel mechanic. The yard opens about a week before the sale, and buyers should read the condition reports rather than bid blind [wzN9KKrhTBo @ 00:49:08]. Sobre Orugas reports Ritchie Mexico's own staff urging buyers to show up: 'In fact, what we recommend is that they come' [xUze3OGayRk @ 00:06:14]. The DIY Contractor toured a lot packed with trucks, paving equipment and car haulers. They frame it as a deal zone with one condition: 'Just make sure you check them out first' [iFwSrSGEDcQ @ 00:03:03]. I Am a Hardscaper confirms that even an online-only sale let buyers touch, see and test-drive equipment in the days beforehand. Their own preview turned up buggies that would not start and dump trucks that were mostly unsatisfactory [DZYNxJ75ASA @ 00:00:16]. The shared logic is that the listing is a starting point, not a verdict. Photos and inspection reports frame the lot, but the practitioners who walked yards found condition gaps that only a hands-on look exposed. They priced or passed accordingly before the clock ever started running on the lot.

The third consensus is the hard ceiling, set before bidding and defended against ego. ET Transport capped the favored 361,000-km Cascadia at $90k to $92k and stopped as it ran to $102k. They warned that most bidders 'don't know when to stop or where to stop' [PHnFlcmti2s @ 00:15:55]. I Am a Hardscaper set $12,000 as the ideal and $15,000 as the absolute top for a tight-access mini skid steer. The number was built from market comparables, closing fees, getting the trailer road-ready and reliability work, and it was set explicitly to restrain a competitive streak. The units went for $20,000-plus and they walked [DZYNxJ75ASA @ 00:04:36]. Christopher of Make Everything, after 30 to 40 lots across two years, tells buyers not to go in 'auction blind' and not to spend money they do not have [x7M1QNicFvc @ 00:11:49]. The Hardscaper math makes the ceiling all-in rather than hammer-only, because fees and road-ready costs land after the win. The ceiling also works in both directions. The discipline that kept ET Transport off the $102k truck put them on a higher-kilometre unit that the comparables priced below market. That made walking away from the first target the most profitable decision of the day.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Getting to the bid button runs through a credit gate. Ritchie Bros.' own IronPlanet onboarding for Europe and the Middle East issues an 'approved buying limit.' It demands VAT country and number from EU companies, may require a deposit from non-EU buyers, and promises processing in no longer than 30 minutes [oItxvAmXefg @ 00:01:07]. Graham of 613 Equipment Rentals received a $75,000 bidding limit after entering card data, a limit that is 'not tied to the credit card' [edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:02:04]. In Mexico, Sobre Orugas reports a $10,000 registration deposit, refunded in full if the buyer wins nothing, with no cost to participate [xUze3OGayRk @ 00:03:03]. Once inside, the clock rules everything. IronPlanet's featured auctions give each item up to a six-minute slot, with several items on the block at once. Buyers can preset a priority bid during a two-week preview. The sale extends while bidding is active and closes after two minutes with no bids [qHDv4e8rQQw @ 00:03:03]. That extension rule is where the money is decided. Every new bid pushes the close back, so the real auction happens in the final stretch. A buyer who has not preset a maximum ends up fighting a ticking timer instead of pricing against value.

ET Transport's live Cascadia buy is the most detailed mechanics session on record. Ronin ran Bid Agent, a third-party Chrome extension from bidagent.io with a free trial. It overlays past Ritchie sold comparables with a percentage match: 400,000 km at $90,000 with a 91% match, 394,000 km at $96,000, and 443,000 km at $100,000 [wzN9KKrhTBo @ 00:11:15]. The tool scrapes auction-to-auction data only and shows no retail price. It logged ET Transport's own win within about two minutes [ubGpPmWhLFE @ 00:42:10]. Funding came from $500,000 of Ritchie credit, approved on two years of financials and 60 days of account history. The line is usable on Facebook Marketplace and private deals, and banks were described as 'running away' from trucking on high default rates [ubGpPmWhLFE @ 00:15:08]. On the clock, rival max bids auto-stepped in $1,000 increments, each new bid added about two minutes, and most bids landed in the last three to four minutes. Graham of 613 Equipment Rentals read the same signals on smaller iron. Bids moved in $500 steps, quick-bids ran against preset max prices across several same-model lots, and an instant counter-bid meant a rival was sitting on a higher preset max [edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:07:05].

The win price is never the cost. Christopher of Make Everything pegs buyer's premiums at 12% to 19%. At 15%, a $100 lot costs $115 and a $1,000 lot carries $150 on top, state tax can stack above that, and across many purchases the premium can nearly equal the item cost [x7M1QNicFvc @ 00:01:05]. Graham's Ritchie invoice added a 10% auction fee to the subtotal, then 13% HST, then a $115 trailer title fee. Paying by card would have added roughly 3%, close to $800, so the wire was the play [edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:03:01]. Makers Acres itemized the full damage on three lots. A $2,500 trencher, a $1,600 tool cabinet and a $4,250 Kubota RTV picked up $835 in auction fees and $711.85 in tax, for a $9,896.85 total. That house charged a 4% card fee and took no cash [vUTKwba3CBk @ 00:13:49]. On trucks, ET Transport puts the buyer fee at about $1,500 and prices kilometres as time. Paying about $10,000 more for about 200,000 fewer km is worth it because 'that's going to give you one full year for $10,000' [wzN9KKrhTBo @ 00:36:14]. Stacked against Bid Agent comps, that math scored the $76,000 win at roughly $10,000 under value.

2. Build a Ranked Wish List and Pull Comps
   - Watchlist about 10 comparable units, rank them by lowest hours or kilometres, and score each against sold comparables.
   - Ronin ranked about 10 2023 Cascadias by lowest km and ran Bid Agent comps on each [ET Transport — wzN9KKrhTBo @ 00:00:57]. Add every target twice, because the heart watchlist does not carry into the auction watchlist [613 Equipment Rentals — edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:05:56].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Inspection is the first fault line. Sobre Orugas says the equipment must sit in the yard and the house wants buyers on site: 'In fact, what we recommend is that they come' [xUze3OGayRk @ 00:06:14]. ET Transport goes further for trucks, telling buyers to use the week the yard is open to walk units with a diesel mechanic and to read every condition report instead of bidding blind [ubGpPmWhLFE @ 00:49:00]. 613 Equipment Rentals ran the opposite play, buying three lots sight unseen about 5.5 hours away in Amaranth, a Home Depot-exclusive Misca trailer plus two Kubota KX8 excavators. The only snag at pickup was dead batteries, which the yard's boost truck cleared before the crew rolled out in about an hour [edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:00:17]. I Am a Hardscaper splits the difference. The online-only sale let bidders touch and test-drive machines in advance, but the advice is not to show up just to try a bunch of equipment, after buggies that would not start and dump trucks that mostly disappointed [DZYNxJ75ASA @ 00:02:50]. The split tracks ticket size and engine risk. The remote buyer took simple, rental-grade iron, while the truck lots ET Transport chased cleared between $76,000 and $102,000, where a missed engine problem wipes out the spread.

The second split is how to place the bid. IronPlanet sells the set-and-forget route. In featured auctions, buyers can lodge a priority bid, a preset maximum, during the two-week preview, and each item then runs in a slot of up to six minutes that closes only after two minutes without a bid [qHDv4e8rQQw @ 00:02:10]. 613 Equipment Rentals went manual, firing quick-bids against preset maximums across several same-model lots and reading an instant counter-bid as proof that a rival held a higher proxy ceiling [edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:06:14]. ET Transport layers data on top, running the Bid Agent Chrome extension for sold comps and capping the favored 361k-km Cascadia at $90-92k. They stopped cold when it ran to $102k, then won a 572k-km unit at $76k against comps of $85k, $82k and $81k [PHnFlcmti2s @ 00:08:22]. I Am a Hardscaper uses no software, just a hard number built from comparables, closing fees, trailer road-readiness and reliability work: $12k ideal and a $15k ceiling, set specifically to override a competitive streak. Those mini skid steers went for $20k plus, so the cap meant walking away and renting through another season [DZYNxJ75ASA @ 00:04:02]. Data-driven or gut-driven, every buyer here decided the stop price before the clock started.

Sellers get conflicting advice on where value comes from. Greg Cook of KDK Sales & Equipment pitches AuctionTime consignment as 'more of a retail type price versus a private party value.' That pitch comes only after screening whether the seller's expectations are realistic. KDK then runs lien searches, takes photos and details the equipment, turning distressed assets immediately and holding others for an optimal window [JxE8MNAqxhQ @ 00:01:12]. The Ritchie Bros. Mexico rep on Sobre Orugas refuses to promise a number. Auctions are unreserved, there is 'no crystal ball,' and estimates come from statistics [xUze3OGayRk @ 00:11:37]. BuildWitt frames that unreserved rule as the trust engine, since owners cannot bid on or buy back their own iron, and the yard ramps units to pull attention, more bidders and a top price [VLza4LoUZfo @ 00:07:15]. ET Transport tested the model with their own fleet and found the gap. They expected $250,250 and banked $232k. Their 2020 Cascadias went from $19k at 1.3M km to $42k at 988k km, and they concluded trucks should be sold under roughly 1M km in a used market they called tanking [UnNSCtlW-0k @ 00:22:56]. The buy-side read from the same channel cuts the other way: Facebook Marketplace asks run $25-30k above auction, which is exactly the spread a buyer harvests.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive trap is condition the listing does not show. Makers Acres paid $4,250 for a Kubota RTV1140 whose photos hid salt-rust wear that earlier auctions would have shot: rusted-out floor pans, a disconnected accelerator and no 4WD engagement. A yard forklift operator also lifted it by the front driveshaft, and there was no recourse because the policy is 'no take backsies' [vUTKwba3CBk @ 00:09:58]. Diesel Down Under gambled knowingly on a Komatsu WF22A-2 compactor that was 'running in the video when it was listed online.' They then found oil 2-3 inches overfull with water in it, a holed muffler and blow-by strong enough to eject the filler cap [cNP1VdOt1J4 @ 00:19:13]. At about a couple grand each, the crew capped the oil-cooler hoses with reusable JIC fittings, refilled fluids and still rated the outing a pretty good buy. Make Everything sets the baseline: no auction guarantees function, and preview-day panels are usually stripped of power. The pennies-on-the-dollar wins, like a 5hp three-phase SawStop at $900 against roughly $5,000 in value, belong to buyers who can absorb a dud [x7M1QNicFvc @ 00:11:49]. Price the repair before the bid, not after the gavel.

The second trap is fee stacking that turns a cheap hammer price into a mid-priced machine. Make Everything clocks buyer's premiums at 12-19% on BidSpotter houses. At 15%, a $1,000 win adds $150 before state tax, and across many purchases the premium can nearly equal what the items cost [x7M1QNicFvc @ 00:01:05]. Makers Acres itemized the damage. A $2,500 trencher, a $1,600 tool cabinet and a $4,250 RTV became $9,896.85 after $835 in auction fees and $711.85 in tax. A 4% surcharge waits for anyone paying by credit card, and no cash is accepted [vUTKwba3CBk @ 00:13:49]. 613 Equipment Rentals ran the Ontario math as subtotal plus a 10% auction fee, times 13% HST, plus a $115 trailer title fee. They avoided a card surcharge of about 3%, close to $800, by wiring, which still took an hour instead of fifteen minutes [edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:03:01]. In Mexico, Sobre Orugas lists a 3.85% admin fee with a $500 minimum and a $1,290 cap per lot, plus 16% VAT outside agricultural equipment. Buyers get five business days to pay, and nothing is released until 100% is paid [xUze3OGayRk @ 00:07:07]. Build the landed number first, then back into the max bid.

The third trap is logistics, where trips and loads get blown. Makers Acres nearly drove 1.5 hours for nothing because pickup had moved to appointment windows, a change learned only by phone. On the way home they pulled over after about one mile and found two straps already loose [vUTKwba3CBk @ 00:08:13]. Make Everything hit terms demanding $1M in insurance for pickup, yet every house allowed self-pickup when called in advance. For anything past your own limits, riggers and machinery movers hold first right of refusal and may quote better rates [x7M1QNicFvc @ 00:07:22]. Diesel Down Under widened the float to 3.5 m over the wheels and about 4.25 m over the blade, then watched the compactor slide on a deck with no rubber. They secured it with eight chains, two per direction per end, plus flags, lights and an escort [cNP1VdOt1J4 @ 00:51:36]. 613 Equipment Rentals rolled up to two excavators with dead batteries, struck out on a kill-switch check and a cross-boost, and needed the yard boost truck. Earlier they had discovered that the heart watchlist does not carry into the live auction watchlist, so every lot gets added twice [edx6wEhgoE8 @ 00:22:34]. Book the slot, plan for a dead battery, and recheck tie-downs early.
