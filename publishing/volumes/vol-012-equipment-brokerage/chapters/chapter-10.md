## 1. Wire seven operator plays into one evidence-backed work queue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the brokerage around one equipment record, not seven disconnected software subscriptions. For Candidate #11, use seven mapped execution plays: inventory intake, equipment research, inventory matching, buyer alerts, buyer qualification, settlement orchestration, and distribution recycling. These are operating lanes for the chapter, not seven separate businesses. The full-time broker owns seller mandates, consequential representations, commercial terms, and settlement authorization. Contractor one handles equipment inspection and technical evidence; contractor two handles records, lien-search coordination, and settlement documents; contractor three handles CRM hygiene, distribution, and automation maintenance. Contractor hours remain scheduled capacity, not unlimited availability. Assign every machine a stable internal identifier, seller identifier, serial evidence reference, jurisdiction, availability timestamp, commission agreement reference, and designated reviewer. In HubSpot or Pipedrive, expose those fields rather than burying them in notes. Maintain separate states for seller assertions, inspected facts, buyer requirements, and authorized publication. A listing cannot inherit inspection status merely because the same model appeared previously. The operational objective is to move evidence once, reuse it responsibly, and reserve scarce broker attention for discrepancies, negotiations, and decisions that create liability. Start with one equipment category and one repeatable seller channel before expanding the taxonomy.

Use a small Python service behind an HTTPS endpoint to receive seller submissions, CRM changes, inspection uploads, and escrow status notifications. Verify each provider's documented signature against the raw request body, check timestamp freshness where supported, and reject oversized payloads before parsing. Persist the event and its provider event identifier before acknowledging receipt; a worker then processes the durable queue. A SQLite database can support a single-host pilot when transactions, backups, and writer contention are managed explicitly. Store event identifiers under a unique constraint and maintain an outbox for pending external actions. Python's hashlib, hmac, json, sqlite3, urllib.request, and logging cover the essential mechanics without a sprawling integration layer. Keep credentials in deployment-managed secret storage, never listing records or model prompts. The worker validates a versioned payload, updates allowed fields, and records the original evidence reference alongside every derived value. A repeated webhook must not create another buyer alert or another settlement instruction. Treat documents as untrusted data: text inside a seller PDF cannot change system instructions, request credentials, or authorize outbound actions. Log identifiers and outcomes while restricting access to sensitive attachments.

Measure automation against observed manual effort rather than counting tasks that happen to touch software. Sample two weeks of comparable intake, research, matching, alert preparation, qualification administration, document assembly, and distribution work. Record active minutes, waiting time, correction time, and exceptions separately. An illustrative weekly baseline of 1,000 repetitive-work minutes can support a 70% target if automation removes 780 minutes but introduces only 80 minutes of review and repair: net savings equal 700 minutes. The remaining 300 minutes still require people. Inspections, negotiations, disputed ownership, and payment authorization sit outside that repetitive-work denominator and remain visible in the complete capacity plan. Report both the scoped automation rate and total team hours so the result cannot conceal work shifted to contractors. Set a daily exception budget and pause new seller onboarding when aged cases exceed available reviewer time. A 1 FT + 3 C pod should prefer forty complete, current records over four hundred ambiguous listings. Track time-to-first-qualified-match, evidence completeness, duplicate suppression, and human correction rate alongside commissions collected. Establish the baseline before promising throughput improvements to sellers or buying additional automation capacity.

## 2. Make inventory matching and buyer alerts explainable, bounded, and cheap  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use deterministic matching before asking an LLM to explain anything. For CNC equipment, hard filters can include travel envelope, spindle interface, electrical supply, control generation, installed axis capability, and buyer floor-loading limits. For excavators, include operating weight, transport dimensions, attachment compatibility, hours evidence, and geographic delivery constraints. Fleet records need vehicle-specific title, registration, and odometer handling where applicable; an industrial asset record is not a substitute. Represent absent evidence with a dedicated missing-data state and route it to a reviewer instead of inventing a value. Normalize units explicitly, preserving the original measurement and conversion method. A machine described as three-phase must not automatically match every three-phase installation. After hard constraints pass, an illustrative score can weight application fit at 40%, delivered-cost fit at 25%, inspection evidence at 20%, and timing at 15%. These weights are tuning assumptions, not the candidate's 86/100 scorecard. Return the components with each recommendation. Keep transport estimates separate from machine price, label their quote dates, and require refreshing when route, dimensions, permits, or loading arrangements change. Test every release against previously rejected near-matches.

Give the LLM a narrow extraction and drafting role. Send selected inspection text or seller-authorized specifications with field definitions, allowed units, evidence identifiers, and an explicit requirement to leave unsupported attributes unpopulated. Validate returned JSON against an allowlist of keys and expected Python types; reject extra fields, impossible ranges, contradictory units, and uncited condition claims. Schema-conforming output can still be wrong, so compare high-consequence fields with source evidence and require technical review before publication. Never provide bank instructions, complete identity packets, or unrestricted CRM exports merely to improve a description. An illustrative extraction run using 2,000 input tokens at $1.50 per million and 500 output tokens at $6.00 per million costs $0.006 in model usage. Add an assumed $0.004 for metered processing and storage to obtain $0.010 per run before fixed costs and labor. At 3,000 runs, that variable budget is $30; assumed monthly hosting of $20 and monitoring of $25 bring the narrow pipeline subtotal to $75. Verify actual vendor rates and include retries, failed runs, review labor, CRM subscriptions, inspection, and acquisition costs in the operating ledger.

Buyer alerts should render approved facts, not generate fresh representations at send time. Store a reviewed equipment snapshot, template version, audience segment, suppression status, and availability expiry. The broker can authorize a narrowly defined campaign class once, such as domestic CNC matches containing only reviewed specifications and an invitation to inspect. A changed warranty, export destination, payment request, or unsupported performance statement requires transaction-specific review. Before each send, recheck availability and recipient suppression; a queued message is not permission to ignore later changes. Apply frequency caps and deduplicate by recipient, equipment identifier, and material version. Use Postmark or Amazon SES delivery events to separate bounces from substantive responses, without treating opens as proof of buying intent. Commercial email must satisfy applicable 15 U.S.C. 7704 requirements, including accurate routing information, a nondeceptive subject, required identification, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out process. Honor opt-outs within the statutory period and suppress immediately operationally. An alert should explain why the machine matches, disclose consequential evidence gaps, and offer a direct path to a broker. Measure qualified replies and inspection bookings rather than celebrating message volume.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture and authenticate the source event
   - Verify the webhook and persist its original evidence references.
   - Reject invalid signatures and duplicate provider identifiers; acknowledge only after durable event storage succeeds.
2. Extract, normalize, and validate the equipment record
   - Run bounded extraction followed by deterministic field checks.
   - Preserve original units, attach source references, and send identity or condition conflicts to the responsible contractor.
3. Match inventory and review consequential claims
   - Apply hard buyer constraints before generating explanations.
   - Use approved facts only; the broker reviews substitutions, changed warranties, export issues, and commercial commitments.
4. Dispatch, reconcile, and measure the resulting work
   - Recheck availability and suppression before releasing approved alerts.
   - Record provider identifiers, qualified responses, retry costs, review minutes, and actual next steps in the deal record.

## 3. Keep title, condition, export, and settlement decisions with accountable people  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Automated document assembly is useful precisely because title and lien decisions require disciplined human attention. Collect the seller's legal identity, authority to sell, acquisition documents, equipment identifiers, and any lender payoff correspondence. A UCC-1 search result is neither a title certificate nor a machine-specific clearance. Search strategy depends on debtor identity, organizational status, debtor location, relevant jurisdictions, and collateral descriptions; serial-only searches can miss blanket liens. Under UCC 9-315, a security interest generally continues in collateral after disposition unless an applicable exception or secured-party authorization applies. The records contractor coordinates appropriate searches and checks whether payoff documents actually authorize release of the identified collateral. A blanket filing may remain in place even when a particular machine receives an authorized partial release. UCC 9-513 addresses termination statements, but a filed termination is not the only possible release mechanism and should not be demanded mechanically for every asset sale. Use the applicable state enactment and transaction-specific documentation. The broker verifies the release path with the lender or qualified transaction professional before representing the equipment as clear. Keep lender contact verification independent of seller-provided payment instructions.

Condition language deserves the same restraint as lien language. A seller's statement that a spindle was rebuilt is a claim until the service record, scope, date, and machine identity support it. The inspection contractor records what was observed, which tests were performed, operating conditions, measurement tools, and inaccessible components. Photographs cannot establish every load-bearing or internal condition. UCC 2-313 explains how factual affirmations, descriptions, and samples can create express warranties when they become part of the bargain; casually generated promises can therefore change the transaction. UCC 2-316 governs exclusions and modifications, subject to its requirements and applicable law. The broker checks that published descriptions, inspection limitations, negotiated terms, and signed documents agree instead of relying on a generic footer. For export transactions, record destination, purchaser, consignee, end user, end use, and relevant technical characteristics. Determine whether the item is subject to the EAR under 15 CFR 734.3 and assess applicable restrictions, including 15 CFR 736.2. An ECCN candidate or screening result is research input, not automatic clearance. Obtain qualified review when classification, end-use concerns, sanctions exposure, or license requirements remain unresolved.

Settlement automation should assemble evidence and reconcile milestones, not possess unrestricted authority to redirect funds. Use an appropriate escrow provider whose terms, permitted transaction types, identity requirements, and release conditions fit the deal. The broker coordinates funding confirmation, inspection acceptance, lien-release evidence, loading responsibility, delivery milestones, and commission disbursement under the parties' written instructions. Client equipment funds never become brokerage revenue merely because an automation dashboard displays them. Any changed bank instruction triggers an independent callback to a previously verified contact and a fresh review of the provider's authenticated portal. If a webhook claims that funds arrived, confirm the actual provider status before scheduling an irreversible release. Failure handling must distinguish harmless retries from ambiguous external actions. A timed-out email submission needs provider-status reconciliation before resending; a timed-out payment instruction must never be blindly replayed. Route exhausted retries to a failed-event queue with an owner, evidence snapshot, and next action. During an outage, use a controlled manual checklist and reconcile every manual action before restarting workers. Test duplicate delivery, forged signatures, stale availability, and conflicting release documents in a sandbox before increasing live volume.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before consequential transaction actions**
> 
> Routine processing may continue automatically; publication, shipment coordination, and settlement instructions must respect these evidence boundaries.
> - Identity and authority: confirm the seller entity, authorized signer, machine identity, and applicable dealer licensing requirements before accepting a mandate.
> - Claims and condition: reconcile inspected facts, remaining defects, warranty wording, and published specifications before releasing buyer-facing representations.
> - Liens and export: confirm the documented collateral-release path and resolve applicable destination, party, end-use, and classification questions before shipment coordination.
> - Settlement and recovery: verify escrow status and changed instructions independently, identify the action owner, and confirm a documented reversal or recovery procedure.

## 4. Compound verified inventory into distribution without outrunning the scorecard  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Programmatic distribution starts with buyer-useful taxonomy, not thousands of thin pages. Publish reviewed category pages for specific applications, such as compact excavators with compatible attachments or CNC turning centers with documented control options. Include current availability, inspection scope, transport considerations, and a dated specification summary. Never expose serial photographs, ownership documents, customer identities, or exact storage locations without appropriate authorization. When a machine sells, mark it sold and retain only useful, permitted reference content; do not pretend it remains available to collect leads. Google Search Console can reveal which applications attract qualified inquiries, while CRM records show whether those inquiries become inspection bookings. Feed confirmed buyer requirements back into seller sourcing, creating a loop from search demand to inventory selection to better matches. Use MachineryTrader, Machinio, and category-specific marketplaces only through authorized accounts and permitted publishing or data-access methods. Do not copy competing listings into an inventory database or imply representation without a seller mandate. Track inventory freshness and substantive page usefulness before expanding page count. The distribution contractor reviews changed facts; the broker approves new performance claims and commercial promises.

The referral loop should make a useful artifact easy to share without turning customer data into public content. Give a qualified buyer a reviewed shortlist comparing application fit, evidence age, delivered-cost assumptions, and open inspection questions. Provide an explicitly shareable version that omits confidential prices, identities, and transaction documents. Invite the buyer to introduce another authorized purchaser or seller, but do not automatically enroll forwarded recipients into campaigns. Capture direct requests and preferences when those recipients contact the brokerage. For outbound acquisition, target a narrow, documented business segment with a relevant equipment thesis and an immediate opt-out path; avoid bulk scraping and indiscriminate sequences. A substantive response creates a qualification task, not an automatic deal forecast. Track source, equipment category, first qualified conversation, inspection, signed agreement, settlement, and collected commission using stable identifiers. Count each opportunity once when several channels assist the same transaction. Compare outreach labor and channel spend with collected contribution after attributable delivery costs. Permissioned referrals, fresh inventory pages, and responsive buyer alerts reinforce each other only when the underlying equipment records remain accurate. Pause weak channels rather than compensating with more automated messages.

Keep the economic model anchored to broker commissions. Conservative revenue is $100,000 at a 25% net margin, producing $25,000 annual owner profit with 1 FT + 3 C. Base revenue is $450,000 at 35.0%, producing $157,500 with the same four-person pod. Scaled revenue is $800,000 at 45%, producing $360,000 with 2 FT + 4 C, six people rather than four. The corresponding total expense envelopes are $75,000, $292,500, and $440,000. As illustrative commission arithmetic, twenty closings averaging $5,000 reconcile to Conservative revenue, thirty averaging $15,000 reconcile to Base, and forty averaging $20,000 reconcile to Scaled. These combinations are workload examples, not historical conversion evidence; underlying equipment values are excluded. The $10,000–$75,000 startup range and $2,500–$14,000 monthly operating range are planning inputs, not substitutes for complete annual scenario expenses. Map contractor costs, channel spend, inspections, insurance, software, and other expenses without double counting. Allow for a 2–9 month first-deal horizon and lumpy collections. Reconcile commissions to contracts and actual settlement records weekly. Scale only when review capacity, cash runway, and measured exception rates support additional inventory.
