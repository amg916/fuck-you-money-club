## 1. Separate Commission Economics from the Machine's Hidden Liabilities  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The first fatal trap is treating equipment transaction value as brokerage revenue. A $250,000 excavator sold at a 6% commission produces $15,000 of operator revenue, not a quarter-million-dollar business. Three illustrative transaction mixes reconcile exactly to the scorecard: Conservative closes ten $200,000 machines at 5%, producing $100,000 commissions; Base closes thirty $250,000 machines at 6%, producing $450,000; Scaled closes forty $400,000 machines at 5%, producing $800,000. Their respective equipment transaction values are $2,000,000, $7,500,000, and $16,000,000, all excluded from recognized operator revenue. Annual operating costs implied by the canonical margins are $75,000, $292,500, and $440,000, leaving $25,000, $157,500, and $360,000 owner profit. These transaction mixes explain the arithmetic; they are not supplied deal forecasts. The stated $2,500–$14,000 monthly overhead range is not an all-in expense ceiling: contractor work, inspections, transaction support, and acquisition costs must still fit the annual expense envelope. Maintain separate ledgers for commissions, reimbursed expenses, seller proceeds, and deposits. Never finance payroll from buyer settlement balances. Establish the contractual earning event and the accounting recognition policy before posting a commission, especially when acceptance or cancellation provisions survive closing. A funded escrow account does not itself prove performance or eliminate reversal exposure.

The second trap is quoting a headline percentage without defining its base, deductions, and survival terms. A heavy-construction consignment at $200,000 and 5% yields $10,000; a $1,500 independent undercarriage inspection and $500 site visit leave $8,000 direct contribution before shared overhead. The fallback is a reduced-price, fully disclosed sale or withdrawal, not a promise that the broker will repair every defect. CNC matching at $250,000 and 6% yields $15,000; $2,500 for geometry testing and $1,000 for specialist travel leave $11,500. If the buyer requires tighter tolerances than the machine can demonstrate, reroute it to a suitable production application rather than relabeling its condition. Fleet remarketing of five $40,000 trucks at 4% produces $8,000; $2,000 of title and inspection expense leaves $6,000. Remove a title-defective unit and recalculate the fee instead of holding the entire fleet hostage. These are three of the seven chapter mappings. In each engagement, specify whether freight, taxes, attachments, rebates, and post-sale credits enter the commission base. Obtain signed seller authority before introducing buyers. An introduction without a defined protection period invites circumvention; an indefinite protection claim invites a different dispute and can make sellers refuse representation.

Three more mappings expose different asymmetries. Industrial liquidation of a $600,000 lot at 4% earns $24,000; $6,000 of cataloging and inspection support leaves $18,000 direct contribution. Separate blocked assets into an excluded schedule when liens or removal obligations cannot be resolved. Domestic inventory matching on a $120,000 machine at 5% earns $6,000; a signed 20% commission split and $800 inspection bill leave $4,000. Without a written split, competing intermediaries can each demand the entire fee. Export buyer sourcing on a $400,000 machine at 5% earns $20,000; $3,000 of classification, screening, and logistics support leaves $17,000 before overhead. If authorization or end-use evidence fails, suspend the shipment and seek a screened domestic buyer. Never convert that fallback into shipment through an undisclosed intermediary. The seventh mapping, imaging-suite brokerage, requires separating equipment value from operating authority. Across all seven, reserve for contractual reversals rather than spending the full receipt. In the Base case, a $22,500 fee refund plus $4,500 of unrecovered testing and $6,000 of dispute expense reduces annual owner profit from $157,500 to $124,500, assuming those costs are incremental. Record the refund as a revenue reduction where applicable; recording it again as an expense would count the same loss twice.

> - Maintain a trailing reversal ledger by seller, machine class, and contract clause so recurring defects change pricing and acceptance terms.

## 2. Prove Ownership, Machine Condition, and a Reversible Settlement Path  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Possession is not ownership, and a seller's invoice is not a lien release. Start with the seller's exact legal name, entity status, authority to sell, and the machine's serial number. Under UCC sections 9-301 and 9-307, debtor location commonly determines the jurisdiction governing perfection by filing; collateral-specific exceptions matter. Section 9-503 makes debtor naming consequential, so searching only a trading name is weak evidence. Obtain current searches through the relevant secretary of state or a professional search provider, examine blanket collateral descriptions, and investigate prior names and ownership transfers when the facts require them. A serial-number search alone will miss many security interests. Section 9-315 generally continues a security interest after disposition unless an applicable exception or secured-party authorization changes the result. Obtain a creditor-authenticated payoff and asset-specific release instructions tied to the actual collateral. A UCC-3 termination can remove an entire financing statement and may be inappropriate for a lender retaining other collateral. Conversely, a filed document does not cure an unauthorized filing. Certificate-of-title statutes can control vehicle perfection under section 9-311; DMV evidence therefore belongs beside, not beneath, the UCC search. Freeze disbursement whenever the seller, record owner, lender, and beneficiary identities cannot be reconciled.

Condition fraud often arrives as selective truth rather than an obviously fake machine. A warm engine hides cold-start problems; cleared CNC alarms hide intermittent servo faults; fresh paint obscures weld repairs; a photographed attachment may not belong to the seller. Require a continuous live walkthrough connecting the serial plate, machine location, hour meter, controls, and the person granting access. Then commission independent testing appropriate to the asset: oil sampling and hydraulic pressure under load for construction equipment, geometry and thermal stability checks for CNC, and maintenance records matched to vehicle identification numbers for fleet units. Inspectors should disclose seller relationships and deliver original measurements, not simply a green checkmark. UCC section 2-313 makes factual affirmations and descriptions potentially consequential as express warranties in sales contracts. Copying 'holds aerospace tolerances' from an old listing can therefore create a dispute that generic condition language will not resolve. Section 2-316 places conditions on warranty exclusions and does not turn inconsistent representations into harmless advertising. Match every material claim to dated evidence, identify who makes each warranty, and distinguish a broker's introduction service from seller obligations. Suspend publication when a serial discrepancy, inaccessible test, or material undisclosed repair prevents a buyer from evaluating the specific machine being offered.

Settlement fails when money moves faster than evidence. Use an independently verified escrow provider whose agreement identifies the property, parties, release conditions, dispute process, and fee waterfall. Provider eligibility, permitted transaction types, and licensing must fit the transaction; a familiar logo is not enough. A forwarded email changing bank details is a fraud event until verified through a previously established telephone number and a second authorized person. Treat a payment screenshot as no evidence of cleared funds. Wire payments have different reversal mechanics from card payments, but fraud investigations, contractual refunds, and court orders still create loss exposure. Specify lender payoff, seller net proceeds, broker fee, holdback, and taxes as separate settlement lines. Allocate rigging, disconnection, loading, transit risk, insurance, and buyer acceptance before machinery leaves its test location. A $15,000 commission cannot absorb an accidental $80,000 floor repair caused by an uninsured rigger. Verify coverage and exclusions with the relevant insurer or authorized representative, not merely a certificate attached by the seller. When an exception appears, pause the affected release rather than inventing a side payment. Preserve signed amendments and the settlement ledger so a later defect claim can be evaluated against the actual agreed condition and transfer sequence.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Bind identity to the actual asset
   - Create one transaction record linking seller authority, serial evidence, and physical location.
   - Use live inspection evidence and independently sourced entity records; quarantine conflicting identity or ownership documents.
2. Resolve encumbrances and measured condition
   - Complete the relevant debtor and title searches, creditor release process, and independent technical inspection.
   - Attach the exact collateral schedule and acceptance criteria; obtain written allocation of every material disclosed defect.
3. Authenticate the settlement waterfall
   - Confirm escrow eligibility, beneficiary instructions, payoff sequence, commission entitlement, and holdback terms.
   - Verify changes through established contacts and require the escrow provider to acknowledge the operative release conditions.
4. Release against evidence and archive
   - Authorize only the disbursements and physical transfers whose written conditions have been satisfied.
   - Retain inspection records, acceptance, creditor authorization, settlement statements, and shipment evidence for the documented retention period.

## 3. Isolate CON Authority, Export Restrictions, and Platform Enforcement  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mandatory catalog mapping is con_decommissioned_medical_imaging_arbitrage, Certificate-of-Need (CON) & Decommissioned Hospital Imaging-Suite Arbitrage, TRACK_1. Its dangerous beginner assumption is that ownership of a scanner includes permission to operate the former hospital's service. Equipment, premises, service approvals, payer enrollment, and operating licenses are distinct assets or permissions. North Carolina General Statutes section 131E-178 illustrates a state CON requirement for covered new institutional health services; definitions, thresholds, exemptions, and change-of-ownership facts determine the actual analysis. Do not price transferable capacity merely because a seller once operated the scanner. Obtain the original approval, conditions, ownership history, and written agency interpretation or required decision for the proposed transaction. In the brokerage version, a $260,000 equipment sale at 6% produces $15,600 commission. Deduct $3,200 independent inspection, $800 travel, and $500 transaction-specific insurance expense for $11,100 direct contribution. Assign zero broker revenue to unverified operating authority. If site authority cannot support the proposed use, an inspected equipment-only sale at $220,000 yields $13,200 commission and $8,700 contribution using the same costs. Those prices are illustrative, not appraisals. An outright equipment acquisition would create a different principal-risk model outside this commission-only scorecard; do not silently substitute resale turnover for brokerage fees.

An option or acquisition agreement for an imaging suite should make required authorization, technical inspection, site access, and removal feasibility explicit conditions with defined exit rights. Keep the broker's own obligations limited to the agreed service; a seller's option premium is not commission revenue. The fallback is exit under those contingencies or sell inspected equipment separately without representing transferable operating authority. MRI systems add magnet status, quench arrangements, cryogen logistics, floor loading, and specialist deinstallation risk. CT systems add tube condition, detector performance, radiation-control registration questions, and software entitlement verification. Nuclear-medicine assets can involve licensed radioactive material; 10 CFR 30.34(b) restricts license transfers absent the required written authorization, and Agreement State requirements may control. A facility shutdown is not proof that a source has been lawfully transferred or disposed of. Patient information can remain on workstations and storage media, so require documented sanitization and seller authorization before resale access. Verify whether service software, reconstruction packages, passwords, and OEM support contracts transfer under their actual terms. Do not promise access by bypassing a license control. If an inspection depends on unavailable proprietary diagnostics, disclose that limitation and restrict claims instead of converting an incomplete evaluation into a statement of clinical readiness.

Export and platform shortcuts produce correlated failures: the same false description that wins a click can invalidate a transaction file. Under the Export Administration Regulations, determine jurisdiction, classification, destination, end user, and end use before quoting an executable delivery commitment. Review 15 CFR parts 736, 744, and 746 as applicable; an EAR99 classification does not eliminate prohibited end-use, party, or destination concerns. Record screening inputs and resolution of potential matches rather than relying on a screenshot of a green result. Require the actual consignee and destination when an intermediary requests domestic delivery for subsequent export. Separately, check state dealer licensing for the specific activity and asset class: California Vehicle Code section 11700 is relevant to covered vehicle dealer activity, not a universal licensing rule for every industrial broker. On eBay, Machinio, or another marketplace, review current listing, account, fee, and communication terms before publishing. Stolen photographs, fabricated ownership, undisclosed duplicate inventory, fee circumvention, and replacement accounts after suspension can trigger removal or lasting exclusion under the applicable rules. Stop affected listings, preserve evidence, and use the platform's appeal channel. Never evade an account restriction through a contractor's identity. Keep an authorized CRM and owned buyer relationships so a platform interruption does not erase the entire qualified pipeline.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Pre-Flight Checks Before an Irreversible Commitment**
> 
> An unresolved material exception stops the affected commitment. A higher projected commission does not substitute for evidence.
> - Verify seller authority, exact serial identity, relevant lien searches, and authenticated release instructions before authorizing any seller disbursement.
> - Match independent inspection results to written buyer acceptance criteria and identify every material excluded component, software entitlement, and removal obligation.
> - For imaging transactions, separate equipment ownership from CON, radioactive-material, and site authority; retain the required decisions or an equipment-only scope.
> - For export or platform-sourced transactions, resolve actual end-user screening and account restrictions before committing shipment, publication, or commission collection.

## 4. Install Telemetry That Stops Leakage Before the Cash Account Notices  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Instrument the brokerage at transaction level using Airtable or HubSpot for workflow, QuickBooks Online for the commission ledger, and a controlled document repository for signed evidence. These tools organize records; they do not validate ownership or legal authority automatically. Maintain fields for serial number, seller entity, gross equipment price, contractual fee, referral share, inspection spend, expected acceptance date, earned date, collected date, reversal exposure, and unresolved exceptions. Every field should have a source document and a responsible person. Define fee realization as net commissions retained divided by originally contracted commissions for the same closed cohort. An illustrative warning below 95% over ninety days should trigger clause-level review, not automatic accusations of fraud. Define direct contribution using retained commissions less transaction-specific acquisition, inspection, referral, and settlement support costs; keep shared overhead outside that measure to avoid double counting. If direct costs exceed 30% of the contracted fee before acceptance, require a revised spend budget and an explicit continue-or-withdraw decision. Small samples distort rates, so display both dollars and counts. One large refund among two closings deserves attention even when a percentage dashboard looks statistically unstable. Reconcile each closed record to actual bank settlement and the accounting ledger weekly.

Early warnings must distinguish an aging deal from an impaired deal. Track days from completed inspection to buyer acceptance, days from earned commission to receipt, and days remaining until the seller's mandate expires. For an illustrative fourteen-day commission collection term, flag balances seven days past due and suspend new unsecured work for that counterparty at thirty days past due unless a documented recovery plan justifies exposure. Review any inspection-to-acceptance interval above twenty-one days for financing, condition, logistics, or documentation failure; do not assume all delays have the same remedy. Forecast unrestricted cash separately from escrow and seller balances. At $8,000 monthly fixed overhead, a ninety-day runway requires $24,000 before expected deal-specific commitments. That reserve fits within, but can consume a substantial portion of, the $10,000–$75,000 startup range. A two-to-nine-month first-deal timeline makes commission dependence dangerous at the lower end. Contract inspection work in stages, avoid speculative ownership, and cap unreimbursed site visits. Treat more than 25% of trailing commissions from one seller or more than 50% of qualified leads from one marketplace as review thresholds, not statutory limits. Diversification should mean additional verified relationships, not unqualified lead volume that consumes inspection capacity without improving collections.

The final circuit breaker is a repeatable response when facts deteriorate. A serial mismatch, unauthenticated bank change, unresolved material lien, prohibited export concern, or missing required authority creates an immediate hold on the affected action. A weaker contribution forecast creates an economic review, not a reason to conceal a defect. Assign one decision owner, preserve original records, notify the relevant contracting parties through established channels, and document what evidence permits resumption. For platform warnings, stop the implicated behavior and listings while assessing scope; do not destroy messages or open replacement identities. For suspected payment fraud, contact the bank's fraud team immediately and follow its recall and reporting process without promising recovery. Reforecast canonical profit against realized losses rather than quietly changing the scorecard: the Base benchmark remains $157,500, while the earlier $33,000 shock produces a $124,500 outcome. The Conservative $25,000 profit has especially little room for one major dispute. Review the exception register weekly with the owner and relevant contractors, recording closed issues as well as open ones. Inventory matching and buyer alerts should automate distribution only after authority and evidence checks; automation that broadcasts a defective listing merely multiplies representations faster than a small team can retract them.
