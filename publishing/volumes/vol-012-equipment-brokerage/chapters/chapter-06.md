## 1. Mine Seven Triggered Plays Without Buying Speculative Inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin with a buyer problem narrow enough to quote: a regional excavator needs two tracked machines below 4,000 documented hours; a job shop needs a specific CNC spindle taper and control; a fleet manager needs six replacement service trucks. Ask for application, required configuration, installed budget, delivery window, inspection standard and purchase authority. Create a buyer-wanted record before collecting listings. Mine SAM.gov award notices, municipal purchasing records, state WARN notices where published, company closure announcements and public auction calendars for dated events. A construction award signals possible equipment demand, not an equipment order. A WARN notice signals organizational change, not permission to sell machinery. Cross-check each trigger with an authorized manager before marketing anything. Use MachineryTrader, Equipment Trader, Machinio and OEM dealer listings for configuration comparisons; asking prices are hypotheses, while documented completed sales provide stronger evidence. Record source URL, observation date, machine category, named business contact and the next verification question in Airtable or HubSpot. Deduplicate by company, serial number and seller relationship. The scarce asset is a verified buying or selling decision with a deadline, not a spreadsheet containing thousands of scraped addresses.

Run six equipment plays with explicit fee arithmetic and reversible fallbacks. Fleet rotation can match a $200,000 excavator at an agreed 5% commission, producing $10,000 before brokerage expenses; if replacement delivery slips, keep the mandate conditional instead of promising immediate availability. CNC replacement can place a $150,000 machining center for $7,500 at 5%; missing parameter backups or failed spindle tests require repair, repricing or withdrawal. Plant closure can move a $300,000 machine package for $15,000 at 5%; if removal costs break buyer economics, split the package only with seller authorization. Rental refresh can place four $50,000 machines for $10,000 at 5%; uneven condition requires separate inspections and prices. Lender-directed disposition can produce $20,000 on $400,000 at 5%, but only after verifying sale authority and release mechanics; otherwise pause marketing. Buyer-wanted matching can earn $12,500 on $250,000 at 5%; an unfunded buyer returns to qualification rather than receiving a costly site visit. These are negotiated illustrations, not observed market rates. In every play, distinguish machine consideration from the broker's fee, identify who pays that fee, and document whether inspection, travel and advertising expenses are separately reimbursed before committing contractor time.

The seventh play, cell_tower_billboard_easement_strip_out, is an adjacent property-rights assignment, not an equipment resale. A closing industrial site may carry a tower, rooftop or billboard arrangement whose assignable rent and access rights interest specialist buyers. Separate the underlying land, installed structures, tenant equipment, lease obligations and proposed easement in a rights schedule. Illustratively, $24,000 annual rent less $2,000 annual owner obligations produces $22,000 before taxes and transaction costs; a specialist's assumed 8% capitalization rate implies $275,000 before contractual and title adjustments. A hypothetical 30% haircut for termination exposure reduces that indication to $192,500, not a promised bid. Model tenant termination, renewal options, escalation, access width, utility obligations and mortgage priority. A $30,000 reduction in residual-property value would reduce that owner's combined economic benefit to $162,500 before other costs. Use the appropriately licensed property specialist and obtain required owner, tenant and lender approvals; an equipment mandate conveys none of those powers. Fallback: retain the lease or negotiate a narrower term-limited assignment with all required approvals. Keep this transaction and any permitted compensation separate from equipment commission projections. Its sourcing value is recognizing a distinct right during an authorized site-disposition conversation rather than accidentally selling someone else's interest.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the Decision Behind the Listing**
> 
> Experienced brokers trade on verified timing and configuration advantages, not merely on being first to forward a photograph.
> - Ask the OEM dealer when the replacement machine is actually scheduled to arrive; obtain the seller's permission before discussing their order.
> - Request CNC option screens, control version and spindle hours before quoting comparables; identical model names can conceal materially different capabilities.
> - Ask plant managers which machine must leave last to preserve production; a buyer accepting that sequence can outperform a higher but inflexible bid.
> - Capture the included bucket, toolholder, fixture and software-license schedule separately; missing accessories can erase an apparent price advantage.

## 2. Turn Public Signals Into Qualified, Authorized Mandates  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a weekly signal desk rather than a perpetual browsing habit. On Monday, review selected public contract awards and regional business announcements for the chosen machine family. On Tuesday, inspect state WARN postings, authorized liquidation notices and auction catalogs for possible supply. On Wednesday, ask OEM dealers, riggers and independent technicians for permission-based introductions; agree any permitted referral compensation in writing and disclose material relationships. A rigger may know a removal deadline but cannot appoint you to sell the machine. A lender contact may discuss disposition without possessing authority to release every lien. On Thursday, reconcile fresh signals against existing buyer-wanted records. On Friday, call the most time-sensitive matches and archive stale claims. Capture the original record, its publication date, the inferred commercial event and the person who subsequently verified it. Use permitted site alerts, RSS feeds and manual review rather than bypassing access restrictions. Retain business contact data needed for the transaction, restrict exports from the CRM and remove unnecessary personal details. A productive research note ends with a specific question such as 'Does the replacement delivery free serial 4821 in October?' rather than an unsupported conclusion that a company must liquidate.

Score each prospective mandate out of 100 before spending on travel: 20 points for verified seller authority, 20 for matched buyer demand, 15 for economic spread after delivered costs, 15 for available condition evidence, 15 for a credible title-release path and 15 for timing alignment. Award full points only when the relevant document or accountable contact is recorded; use half points for partial evidence and zero for unsupported claims. A score of 75 earns deeper work only if authority and title-release feasibility are established. No aggregate score overrides an ownership dispute. For a $200,000 machine, add $8,000 freight, $4,000 rigging and $3,000 immediate service: the buyer's installed cost is $215,000 before applicable taxes and site modifications. Against a supported $230,000 installed alternative, the advantage is $15,000, not $30,000. At a seller-paid 5% fee, the broker earns $10,000 and the seller receives $190,000 before payoff and other seller costs. Explicitly identify which party pays freight and inspection. Reject apparent bargains that require hiding access costs, suppressing known defects or treating a speculative resale price as an executable buyer offer. Re-score after inspection, not just when the listing enters.

Qualification must establish authority and settlement feasibility without pretending that a database search proves clean ownership. Obtain the seller's exact legal name, entity jurisdiction, invoice chain, serial photographs and any vehicle title. Under UCC § 9-307, debtor location often drives the filing jurisdiction; registered organizations generally require attention to their organizing jurisdiction. Fixtures, titled vehicles and other exceptions require the applicable record system. Search the relevant official UCC index using correct debtor information, then examine blanket collateral descriptions, amendments and continuations. UCC § 9-315 generally preserves a security interest following disposition unless the secured party authorized a free transfer or another rule applies. Obtain authenticated payoff and collateral-release instructions from each relevant secured party; independently verify contact details. UCC § 9-513 concerns termination statements, but a filed termination alone is not a universal ownership certificate. The signed mandate should define identified assets, authority, asking strategy, fee payer, commission trigger, term, expenses, permitted disclosures and any narrowly defined post-term protection. Check state dealer or broker licensing for the actual asset and activity before solicitation. Fleet transactions involving road vehicles may invoke rules that ordinary industrial machinery sales do not.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Publishing a Deal-Ready Listing**
> 
> A high qualification score never replaces authority, evidence or a workable release path. Complete these checks before representing an asset as ready for a committed buyer.
> - Verify the seller's authority, exact asset identity and your signed marketing mandate; confirm required dealer or broker credentials for the transaction.
> - Obtain dated condition evidence and disclose known limitations; distinguish seller statements from independently verified inspection findings.
> - Document the relevant lien searches, vehicle-title or fixture issues, authenticated payoff contacts and an executable collateral-release sequence.
> - Confirm buyer purchasing authority, installed budget, inspection access and settlement instructions; use independently verified callbacks for account changes.

## 3. Run a Measured Cadence From First Contact to Inspection  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a 15-business-day cadence centered on one evidenced event and one useful question. On day one, send a short business email referencing the published trigger: 'Your new machining line appears scheduled for November. Are any vertical machining centers being released, and who owns that decision?' Do not claim a funded buyer unless one exists. On day three, call the published business number and ask for the person responsible for disposition. On day six, send a configuration-specific buyer brief or a completed-sale comparison with its limitations. On day ten, request a warm introduction through an authorized dealer or service relationship. On day fifteen, close the sequence with a clear invitation to respond when timing changes. Stop immediately after an objection to further contact and synchronize suppression across tools. Commercial email must satisfy 15 U.S.C. § 7704, including accurate routing information, a valid postal address and an effective opt-out process. Do not assume a business number makes automated calls or texts permissible; evaluate 47 U.S.C. § 227 and applicable state rules before using those methods. Manual, relevant outreach is enough to test this niche without building a mass-contact operation.

Inbound should collect specifications rather than applause. Publish one useful landing page per chosen machine family, showing the configurations you source, your inspection process and a buyer-wanted form. Ask for capacity, control or attachment requirements, location, installed budget and decision date; allow document upload through a restricted workspace. Publish actual authorized inventory with dated photographs and clear availability, then remove sold or withdrawn equipment promptly. Do not republish another dealer's inventory as your own. Offer buyer alerts only to contacts requesting them, matching required fields before optional preferences. In Airtable, require machine category, capacity and location compatibility before generating a draft alert; in HubSpot, log the human review and resulting conversation. A near-match should explain the mismatch instead of hiding it. Track which search phrase, dealer introduction or technical article produced a qualified specification, not merely a page view. A page explaining 480-volt installation requirements or excavator auxiliary hydraulics can filter poor fits before a call. Avoid unsupported operating claims: service records and independent tests establish condition, while the broker explains how to obtain those records and what remains unverified before a buyer commits.

Treat conversion benchmarks as planning hypotheses until your own cohorts replace them. Start with 300 researched company records over six weeks; suppose 180 are relevant and contactable, 54 yield substantive two-way conversations, 18 pass qualification, eight sign mandates and three close within the launch window. Those stage rates are 60%, 30%, 33.3%, 44.4% and 37.5%, respectively; the overall researched-record to close rate is 1%. They are operating targets, not industry measurements. Eight mandates and five open or lost files must remain visible after three settlements. Tag every loss: no authority, price gap, configuration mismatch, title problem, inspection failure or timing slip. If conversations are scarce, repair relevance and contact quality. If inspections fail, tighten evidence requirements before accepting mandates. If qualified buyers stall, revisit installed economics rather than increasing message volume. Answer 'Your fee is too high' by showing seller net proceeds and the specific matching, inspection coordination and settlement work purchased. Answer 'Send your buyer first' with a limited, signed introduction agreement and permission-based disclosure. Answer 'Another broker promised more' with comparable configuration and executable terms, not an inflated estimate. Do not substitute elaborate scripts for a demonstrably better transaction.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture a dated commercial trigger
   - Create a source-linked CRM record and identify the verification contact.
   - Separate the published fact from the inferred equipment opportunity; assign one next question and a follow-up date.
2. Verify both sides of the potential match
   - Collect seller authority, machine identity and a buyer-wanted specification.
   - Calculate installed costs, apply the 100-point qualification rubric and stop if authority or release feasibility fails.
3. Secure the mandate and coordinate inspection
   - Document the fee agreement before introducing protected information or booking paid diligence.
   - Obtain permission for disclosures, identify who pays contractors and schedule machine-specific independent tests.
4. Close through verified settlement instructions
   - Reconcile payoff, release, acceptance, fee and collection documents with the escrow provider.
   - Record collected commissions, final seller proceeds and the source cohort; retain unresolved files until explicitly lost or completed.

## 4. Close the First Wins and Reconcile Every Commission Dollar  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A first win is a completed transaction with collected commission, not a signed listing or refundable deposit. Convert buyer interest into a written offer identifying the machine, included accessories, price, inspection rights, deposit treatment, acceptance standard and collection deadline. Specify who bears loss before loading, during rigging and after carrier pickup. Independent inspection should match the asset: cold-start and hydraulic tests for construction equipment; geometry, spindle condition and control backups for CNC; title and service history for road fleets. Require buyer review of reports before waiving contractual inspection rights. UCC § 2-316 governs important aspects of warranty exclusion; a casual 'as is' label does not erase inconsistent express promises or excuse false condition statements. Use an established escrow provider able to handle the asset type, payoff instructions and agreed release sequence. The broker should not casually accept purchase funds into an operating account. Independently verify settlement contacts and account changes using previously established numbers. Release funds only under the written escrow instructions after required conditions are satisfied. Give the buyer the agreed transfer documents and arrange collection only when the settlement sequence authorizes it; possession and commission timing need not coincide.

Use the canonical scorecard without confusing machinery value with broker revenue. The Conservative case is $100,000 annual commission revenue at 25% net margin, yielding $25,000 owner net profit with 1 FT + 3 C. One illustrative bridge is ten $200,000 sales at 5%, totaling $2,000,000 equipment consideration and $100,000 commissions; implied annual costs are $75,000. The Base case is $450,000 at 35.0%, yielding $157,500 with 1 FT + 3 C. Thirty $300,000 sales at 5% create $9,000,000 equipment consideration and $450,000 commissions; implied costs are $292,500. The Scaled case is $800,000 at 45%, yielding $360,000 with 2 FT + 4 C, six people. Forty $400,000 sales at 5% create $16,000,000 equipment consideration and $800,000 commissions; implied costs are $440,000. These deal counts illustrate arithmetic, not mandatory production assumptions. Contractors are rostered specialists, not automatically full-time equivalents. The $2,500–$14,000 monthly operating-expense range is a planning input, not a substitute for total annual scorecard costs. Include variable contractor work, acquisition expense and the scorecard's other cost categories when reconciling the complete budget; never force profit by omitting transaction-related expenditure.

Sequence the 2–9 month launch around evidence accumulation. During the first month, select one equipment family, interview buyers, appoint inspection and rigging contacts, and establish mandate and escrow procedures. During months two and three, run the first researched cohort, seek eight qualified mandates under the illustrative funnel and schedule only funded inspections. During months four through nine, repeat the channels producing clean settlements and pursue three to ten cumulative wins as availability permits. Three $10,000 commissions produce $30,000 revenue, not the Conservative annual target or $30,000 profit. At $5,000 monthly recurring burn, six months consumes $30,000 before exceptional deal costs; size cash reserves against that timing within the $10,000–$75,000 startup range. Do not fund seller payoffs or buyer deposits from those reserves merely to rescue a weak match. Before cross-border offers, determine EAR jurisdiction and classification, destination, end user and end use; advanced machine tools can raise controls that ordinary domestic listings do not reveal. Track 15 CFR Part 744 restrictions and applicable licensing requirements before shipment commitments. Finish every settlement with a source attribution, defect review and permission-based referral request so the next deal costs less to originate.
