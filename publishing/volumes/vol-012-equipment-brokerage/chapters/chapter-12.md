## 1. Days 1–7: Define Seven Plays and Build a Verifiable Supply Wedge  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Launch Candidate #11 as an agent earning commissions, not as a dealer carrying inventory. This chapter maps seven operating plays: P1 heavy-construction resale, P2 CNC-machine resale, P3 fleet-equipment resale, P4 industrial-surplus resale, P5 buyer-side sourcing mandates, P6 seller-side disposition mandates, and P7 repeat-buyer inventory alerts. These are the chapter's working labels; the first four describe asset lanes, while the last three describe commercial motions across those lanes. A transaction receives one primary play and optional secondary tags, so overlapping mandates never create duplicate opportunities or commissions. During Week 1, nominate one primary asset lane within practical inspection distance and one adjacent lane with shared buyers. Score each lane on accessible owners, inspection cost, transport complexity, expected commission, and buyer specificity. Require ten identifiable sellers and ten plausible buyers before purchasing lane-specific marketing. Keep the remaining plays represented in the CRM, but do not launch seven separate campaigns. A used machining-center specialist can serve P2, P5, P6, and P7 through the same evidence packet; adding fleet vehicles immediately introduces different title, licensing, and transport requirements. The objective is seven usable routes to revenue, not seven simultaneous operating departments.

Assign the four-person pod before contacting owners. The single full-time operator owns seller interviews, buyer qualification, pricing, contracts, and cash decisions. Contractor one performs independent equipment inspections; contractor two coordinates entity records, lien searches, and closing documents with qualified counsel when interpretation is required; contractor three obtains rigging, freight, and pickup estimates. Contractors work against written scopes and spending limits, not assumed full-time availability. Reserve the owner's forty-hour week as twelve hours sourcing sellers, ten qualifying buyers, eight coordinating diligence, six managing contracts and negotiations, and four reconciling pipeline and cash. Configure Airtable or HubSpot with linked records for seller, asset, buyer, mandate, and opportunity. Give every asset a unique identifier tied to a serial photograph; fleet units also require VIN records where applicable. Separate seller asking price, expected transaction value, contracted commission, and earned commission into different fields. Require a named next action and dated evidence attachment before advancing any stage. Use DocuSign for scoped mandates and a permission-controlled Drive folder for inspection material. Record who supplied each claim, when it was checked, and whether the broker can substantiate it independently.

Set the cash boundary on Day 1. This illustrative sprint requires $24,000 of available operator capital, within the canonical $10,000–$75,000 startup range. Authorize $3,000 of one-time setup spending plus $4,500 for the first month's operations, producing a $7,500 Day 30 ceiling. Within monthly operations, budget $3,500 for recurring overhead and contractor availability, and $1,000 for approved asset-specific work. Additional fieldwork must replace another expense, receive a separately documented customer reimbursement arrangement, or wait. Limit subsequent monthly spending to $4,500, making cumulative Day 60 and Day 90 ceilings $12,000 and $16,500. This leaves $7,500 untouched if no commissions arrive. Count paid expenses and committed unpaid obligations against those ceilings; do not hide expenditure in contractor invoices awaiting payment. Customer deposits, equipment purchase funds, and escrow balances never fund operations. A founder starting with $10,000 must reduce the scope and publish a replacement budget before using this schedule. On Day 7, review the first seller records, contractor quotes, and state licensing analysis. Stop vehicle solicitation wherever the intended activity requires a license that the business does not hold; commission-only positioning does not erase dealer definitions.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy Evidence Before Buying Reach**
> 
> The strongest early advantage is a packet that survives an experienced buyer's inspection, lender review, and freight quote.
> - For CNC assets, request control model, spindle hours, alarm history, power requirements, and a cutting demonstration before advertising production readiness.
> - For construction machines, commission a witnessed cold start and undercarriage assessment; a warm engine and fresh paint can conceal expensive defects.
> - Ask whether rigging access changes after neighboring machines leave; removal sequence can turn an apparently cheap industrial lot into an uneconomic purchase.
> - Keep buyer requirement alerts separate from public listings, and send only assets that match documented dimensions, power, budget, and delivery constraints.

## 2. Days 8–14: Convert Listings Into Authority-Checked, Inspectable Opportunities  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Week 2 converts owner conversations into controlled inventory records. Obtain a written mandate identifying the legal seller, asset serials, commission basis, term, marketing permission, and circumstances that earn the fee. State whether the owner pays a percentage of the equipment price or a fixed amount, and exclude taxes, freight, and rigging unless the agreement expressly includes them in the fee basis. Document any buyer-side fee and disclose dual-side compensation before proceeding; never conceal it inside an inflated freight quote. P1 through P4 supply the assets, P6 creates seller authority, and P5 starts with a buyer's written specification rather than an unverified promise to purchase. Require a live seller conversation using independently sourced contact information, entity records matching the mandate, and evidence linking the seller to the asset. A photograph of equipment in a yard proves neither ownership nor authority. For consigned or leased machines, identify the actual owner and the party permitted to sell. Count an asset as verified supply only after identity, authority, serial evidence, location, condition disclosures, and lien-resolution status are documented, with no unresolved authority contradiction; a planned release is not a completed release.

Treat lien work as a jurisdictional investigation, not a search-engine task. Under UCC Section 9-301, the debtor's location generally controls perfection and priority rules for many security interests, subject to important exceptions. Use UCC Section 9-307 to determine that location and Section 9-503 for the correct debtor name; a trade name alone can miss filings. Search the relevant filing office and obtain underlying records rather than relying on a seller's screenshot. Review blanket collateral descriptions, amendments, assignments, and secured-party identities. Equipment may remain subject to a security interest after sale under UCC Section 9-315 unless a relevant exception or secured-party authorization applies. Obtain a payoff statement and asset-specific release instructions from the secured party through independently verified channels. A UCC termination under Section 9-513 serves a different purpose from a partial collateral release; do not demand termination of an entire credit facility merely to release one machine. Titled vehicles require the applicable certificate-of-title process, and other liens or ownership claims may require separate searches. Escalate ambiguous collateral, seller insolvency, and competing claims before inviting a buyer to commit funds.

Inspect only after the seller packet passes the inexpensive checks. Prioritize assets with a realistic buyer match, an expected fee sufficient to cover diligence, and access for an independent inspector. The report should separate observations, instrument readings, seller statements, and items not tested, with a reason and a proposed resolution for each untested function. For CNC machines, capture control version, spindle condition, maintenance records, tooling inclusion, and electrical requirements. For construction equipment, capture meter readings, hydraulic leakage, structural repairs, and wear measurements. For fleet units, reconcile VIN, title, configuration, and service history. P4 surplus lots need a count sheet and explicit boundaries around excluded accessories. Never describe an untested asset as fully operational. Record seller representations and buyer inspection rights in the transaction documents; applicable state enactments of UCC Sections 2-313 and 2-316 affect warranty creation and exclusion. By Day 14, target four verified assets, two inspection scopes scheduled within budget, and eight documented buyer interviews. Failure to obtain documents after two written requests within seven days removes an asset from active marketing, even when its apparent commission looks attractive.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Buyer Funds or Binding Delivery Promises**
> 
> Commercial momentum never substitutes for authority, condition evidence, clear release mechanics, or verified settlement instructions.
> - Verify seller identity, sale authority, applicable licensing, and serial or VIN agreement across mandate, asset photographs, and transaction documents.
> - Deliver the inspection report and unresolved defect list to the buyer; obtain written acknowledgment of material exclusions before signing.
> - Obtain secured-party payoff and release instructions, applicable title-transfer requirements, and an agreed sequence for clearing every identified claim.
> - Confirm the independent escrow provider's authority and safeguarding arrangements; verify payment instructions by callback and prohibit broker custody of purchase funds.

## 3. Days 15–21: Match Buyers, Rehearse Settlement and Prove Commission Economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Week 3 tests whether verified supply meets funded demand. A qualified buyer must have an identified decision-maker, an asset specification, a budget range, a funding route, and a stated purchase window. Financing interest is not lender approval; keep those stages separate. Build P7 alerts from explicit requirements rather than blasting every listing to every contact. Match on capacity, dimensions, control type, power, condition tolerance, geography, and delivered acquisition cost. Each alert should name the relevant asset, link the current evidence packet, disclose material defects, and ask for one specific next step. Count a buyer-asset match only when the buyer confirms technical fit and requests a quotation, inspection, or negotiation. Multiple alerts to the same buyer for the same asset remain one match. Maintain separate asset and opportunity identifiers when several buyers examine one machine; forecast only one successful sale of that machine. By Day 21, target twelve qualified buyers, four confirmed matches, two inspection events completed or firmly scheduled, and one written commercial proposal. These are local experiment thresholds, not historical conversion claims. They expose weak specifications, unrealistic asking prices, or inaccessible equipment without requiring an implausibly fast first closing.

Rehearse the closing with the document and logistics contractors before a buyer wires money. Select an independent escrow provider permitted to handle the transaction in the relevant jurisdiction, and inspect its engagement terms, safeguarding arrangements, fees, and dispute process. The settlement statement must distinguish equipment price, secured-party payoff, seller proceeds, taxes where applicable, freight, escrow charges, and broker commission. Put the conditions for disbursement, lien release, title documents, inspection acceptance, pickup, and risk transfer into coordinated written instructions. Agree who pays if removal is delayed by site access or missing lifting equipment. Verify changed bank instructions by calling a previously validated number; never use the number supplied in the change message. For potential exports, establish the item classification, destination, end user, and end use before promising shipment. The EAR general prohibitions in 15 CFR 736.2 and restrictions under Part 744 can apply beyond obvious military equipment; consult the relevant rules and obtain required permissions before export. Make the first sprint domestic unless the team can support the additional work. A clean escrow rehearsal should expose document and timing defects while no customer money is at risk.

Use unit economics to decide which opportunities deserve scarce inspection hours. An illustrative $300,000 machine sold under a 5% seller commission produces $15,000 of broker revenue, not $300,000. If asset-specific inspection, travel, and closing costs total $3,000, the deal contributes $12,000 before shared overhead and other annual expenses. Record reimbursable customer costs separately and apply the relevant principal-versus-agent accounting treatment rather than automatically inflating revenue. The canonical annual cases reconcile exactly: $100,000 commissions less $75,000 total costs yields $25,000 owner profit; $450,000 less $292,500 yields $157,500; $800,000 less $440,000 yields $360,000. At an illustrative constant 5% rate, those revenue cases could arise from twenty $100,000 trades, thirty $300,000 trades, and forty $400,000 trades respectively. Those deal counts are arithmetic examples, not launch forecasts. The first two cases retain one full-time person and three contractors; the Scaled case uses two full-time people and four contractors. The published $2,500–$14,000 monthly operating range does not replace the annual total-cost reconciliation. Keep overhead, variable transaction expense, and staffing assumptions visible, and never annualize one unusually large commission into a sustainable run rate.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the identity and asset packet
   - Reconcile seller authority, buyer identity, serials, condition evidence and licensing.
   - The owner records packet version and unresolved issues; any authority contradiction stops the opportunity before funds are requested.
2. Reconcile liens and the settlement statement
   - Match payoff instructions, asset release documents and commission terms to the proposed disbursements.
   - The document contractor obtains independently verified secured-party instructions and refers conflicting rights to qualified counsel.
3. Confirm inspection acceptance and physical transfer
   - Obtain written buyer acceptance and confirm rigging, pickup access and risk-transfer terms.
   - The inspector reports facts; the logistics contractor confirms practical movement; the contracting parties decide acceptance and allocation of risk.
4. Execute independent settlement and close the ledger
   - Let the escrow provider disburse under agreed conditions and collect the broker's earned commission.
   - Record equipment proceeds outside broker revenue, reconcile the actual fee and deal costs, and archive release and transfer evidence.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Reviews at 60/90: Kill Weak Plays, Fix Bottlenecks, Scale Evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use Days 22–30 to consolidate evidence rather than expand the advertising surface. On Day 22, refresh buyer specifications; on Day 23, test asking prices against recent comparable transactions where obtainable, distinguishing realized sales from auction estimates and advertised prices. On Day 24, resolve inspection defects and revised freight costs. On Day 25, have the document contractor audit lien and settlement packets. On Day 26, calculate contribution for each active proposal. On Day 27, reconcile paid and committed spending. On Day 28, test alert accuracy and duplicate suppression. On Day 29, prepare the decision packet; on Day 30, assign Kill, Fix, or Scale to each tested play and to the overall sprint. Report untested plays separately instead of declaring them successful or unsuccessful. The packet includes unique counterparties, verified assets, qualified buyers, confirmed matches, written proposals, actual fees collected, projected contribution, and owner hours. An active proposal requires a buyer-confirmed next event within fourteen days. Remove stale proposals from active counts until the buyer reconfirms. Zero Day 30 revenue is compatible with the canonical 2–9 month first-deal window; zero usable authority or demand is a different problem.

Apply decision rules in order: Kill overrides Fix, and Scale requires every listed condition for that checkpoint. Kill means stop new spending on the affected play, withdraw unreliable listings, and preserve records; it does not mean abandon existing contractual duties. A missed review-date burn ceiling freezes all new discretionary spending until commitments and cash are reconciled. Confirmed seller impersonation, fabricated ownership evidence, or an unresolved mandatory authorization also stops the affected activity immediately. For commercial testing, count only personalized attempts to relevant named counterparties, not bulk impressions. At Day 30, stop a tested play with forty seller approaches and zero verified assets, or thirty buyer approaches and zero qualified buyers. Otherwise, a shortfall against Scale thresholds receives one fourteen-day repair plan with one owner, one bottleneck, and a measurable target. Examples include securing two missing mandates, converting three qualified interviews into one confirmed match, or correcting delivered costs on two proposals. Do not solve documentary failure with more advertising. At later checkpoints, use the cumulative activity thresholds below and the dated repair record to distinguish an unproven market from a repeatedly failed execution process.

Scale at Day 30 means reallocating up to 20% of existing prospecting hours toward the strongest play, with no headcount increase and no budget increase. Day 60 Scale adds at most 25% more alert volume within the same four-person pod, provided the listed evidence and capacity tests pass. Day 90 Scale allows a separately budgeted expansion proposal, not automatic adoption of the $800,000 annual case. Define a late critical task as a scheduled authority check, inspection report, payoff confirmation, or settlement action more than two business days overdue. Keep this count at zero before scaling. For pipeline coverage, multiply each active proposal's expected commission by a conservative 20% planning weight and sum the results; require at least $9,000 of weighted commissions at Day 90. This is a liquidity stress proxy, not booked revenue or an empirically established close probability. Maintain at least three active proposals across three unrelated buyers and three separate assets, and preserve the $7,500 reserve. No closing by Day 90 triggers Fix rather than automatic Kill when demand remains credible. Authorize the additional full-time person and fourth contractor only through a new capacity and cash plan supported by collected fees and repeatable work.
