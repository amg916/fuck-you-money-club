## 1. Classify the Seven Plays Before Taking a Mandate  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an activity map, not a generic broker label. For this chapter, the seven-play operating map is seller representation, buyer mandates, heavy-construction resale, CNC matching, fleet remarketing, industrial liquidation, and export coordination. These are operating lanes, not seven separate revenue streams to add to the scorecard. Record whether the operator merely introduces parties, negotiates price, signs for a principal, takes title, conducts an auction, arranges transportation, or handles purchase funds. Each action can change the governing rules. Ordinary used machinery introductions do not carry a single universal federal equipment-broker license, but that observation does not settle state dealer, auctioneer, vehicle, escrow, or transportation requirements. California Vehicle Code sections 285 and 11700 illustrate why fleet transactions require separate analysis: negotiating vehicle sales for compensation can fall within dealer activity even without inventory ownership. An excavator on tracks and a road-registered service truck are not interchangeable licensing cases. Build a state worksheet for the operator's location, seller's location, equipment situs, buyer's location, and delivery destination. Record the specific activity, applicable exemption, regulator source, expiration date, and responsible reviewer before accepting a compensated assignment. Retain the underlying authority alongside the conclusion so another reviewer can reproduce it.

Use a four-person pod with explicit boundaries: one full-time principal owns mandates, pricing, disclosures, and settlement decisions; one contractor owns inspections; one owns transaction documentation; and one owns inventory matching and logistics coordination. Legal review, licensed escrow, and licensed transport brokerage remain purchased specialist services rather than implied capabilities of those contractors. Seller representation requires documented authority and disclosed compensation. Buyer mandates require a precise search specification and clarity about any seller-paid fee. Heavy-construction resale emphasizes serial identity, attachment ownership, and field inspection. CNC matching adds controller licenses, tooling, rigging, and acceptance testing. Fleet remarketing adds vehicle licensing, title transfer, and odometer analysis where applicable. Industrial liquidation adds landlord access, auction rules, and insolvency authority. Export coordination adds classification, destination, end-user, and shipment responsibilities. Do not let an inventory-matching contractor quietly negotiate vehicle sales or arrange regulated motor carrier transportation for compensation. Under 49 U.S.C. sections 13102 and 13904, transport brokerage has its own definitions and registration requirements. The machinery broker should distinguish a simple referral from transportation arrangement and use an authorized provider when the activity requires one. A contractor title does not exempt the underlying conduct from licensing rules. Document handoffs in the deal record.

Separate the sale contract from the service mandate. State-enacted UCC Article 2 generally governs sales of goods; the brokerage service agreement is not automatically a goods sale. Identify the actual seller, buyer, and broker on separate signature lines, with entity names matching authority records. Under UCC section 2-312, title and infringement warranties require attention distinct from condition warranties. Under sections 2-313 through 2-316, descriptions, affirmations, samples, and inspection statements can create express warranties; a broad condition disclaimer does not simply erase them. Merchantability and fitness exclusions have specific wording and conspicuousness rules, and state enactments control. Avoid advertising a machine as production-ready when the inspection only establishes that it powers on. Industrial business purchases differ from consumer-product sales, where additional federal and state warranty protections may apply. Allocate sales-tax collection, exemption certificates, and destination evidence without assuming the commission broker is always the taxable seller. Physical possession, title-taking, marketplace activity, and state nexus rules can change that result. When an industrial liquidation involves bankruptcy, verify the seller's authority against the actual order and any conditions under 11 U.S.C. section 363. A signed engagement letter cannot override a court restriction or manufacture clean title.

## 2. Make Lien Clearance and Escrow a Documented Settlement System  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat ownership and lien clearance as different investigations. An invoice can support acquisition history without proving that a secured lender has released its interest. Collect the seller's exact legal name, formation jurisdiction, former names, serial photographs, purchase records, leases, and secured-party contacts. Search the correct filing office using state-enacted UCC sections 9-301 and 9-307 for governing-law and debtor-location analysis; registered organizations are generally located in their state of organization. Do not assume the equipment's warehouse location determines the central filing office. Review collateral descriptions for all-assets and after-acquired-property coverage rather than searching only the machine serial number. UCC section 9-315 generally preserves a security interest after disposition unless an applicable authorization or other rule changes the result. Buyer protections under section 9-320 are fact-specific; buying a manufacturer's surplus mill is not automatically buying from a dealer in that kind of equipment. Certificate-of-title goods, fixtures, possessory liens, tax liens, and bankruptcy issues require additional records beyond a central UCC search. Have the documentation contractor produce a written search scope and exceptions list, then obtain transaction-specific releases instead of presenting a clean search as universal proof of unencumbered ownership. Record dates because intervening filings can change the closing position.

Ask each secured party for a payoff statement or authorized collateral-release arrangement that identifies the debtor, equipment, serials, good-through date, daily accrual if relevant, payment instructions, and release conditions. Authenticate the contact through a previously verified institutional channel rather than the email containing wire instructions. A UCC-3 termination is not always the correct instrument: a blanket facility may remain open while the lender releases only one machine. Obtain a signed release or disposition authorization covering the specific asset and any appropriate filing amendment. State-enacted UCC section 9-513 addresses termination statements, but the closing packet still needs a coherent link between payment and collateral release. Engage an independent escrow provider whose authority and operating rules fit the jurisdiction and transaction. Define whether the provider merely holds funds or also verifies specified documents; escrow does not inherently inspect machines or search liens. The principal should approve a written disbursement waterfall: secured payoffs, agreed taxes and charges, commission, then seller proceeds. Require matched serial identity, executed sale documents, agreed inspection disposition, and verified bank instructions before release. Never fund a seller's unrelated liquidity problem from a buyer deposit while the equipment remains encumbered. Keep client funds outside ordinary operating accounts.

Write delivery and acceptance as observable events. For a CNC machine, specify the powered test, spindle hours source, demonstrated tolerances, alarm history, included tooling, controller backup, and who performs deinstallation. For heavy construction, identify attachment serials, hour-meter limitations, hydraulic testing, undercarriage measurements, and inaccessible components. For fleet assets, reconcile VIN, title holder, recorded liens, and required odometer documentation. The sale agreement should distinguish inspection acceptance, escrow release, title passage, risk of loss, loading responsibility, and physical delivery. UCC sections 2-401, 2-509, and 2-510 provide rules that should be considered when drafting those allocations. A machine can be accepted while still awaiting a rigger, so acceptance alone should not silently assign every loading casualty to the buyer. Verify cargo and rigging coverage against the actual equipment and operation, including exclusions and deductibles. Preserve photographs before disconnecting utilities and after loading. Use DocuSign for executed versions and a restricted deal folder for evidence, with bank details visible only to necessary personnel. Require an independent callback for every changed payment destination. Inventory matching and buyer alerts can be automated; changed wire instructions and unresolved collateral exceptions cannot be treated as routine marketing events.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the asset and authority packet
   - Reconcile entity authority, serial identity, acquisition history, and applicable title records.
   - The documentation contractor records exceptions; the principal accepts the mandate only within a documented licensing scope.
2. Resolve liens and inspection exceptions
   - Obtain relevant searches, authenticated creditor instructions, and buyer-visible inspection findings.
   - The inspector owns technical findings while the documentation contractor links every release to the correct asset.
3. Approve the independent settlement instructions
   - Match executed documents, verified beneficiaries, fee entitlement, and conditional disbursements.
   - The principal and escrow provider reconcile the waterfall without routing equipment purchase funds through operating accounts.
4. Close and retain the evidence trail
   - Confirm authorized release, settlement completion, pickup condition, and commission receipt.
   - The logistics contractor preserves custody evidence; the principal closes the ledger using commission revenue rather than equipment value.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Habits That Prevent Expensive Closing Surprises**
> 
> Make transaction evidence useful to the next person handling the deal, not merely present in a folder. These tactics fit the existing 1 FT + 3 C pod.
> - Photograph the machine plate, controller screen, and included attachments together; reconcile serials against the release schedule before publishing buyer alerts.
> - Ask the lender for a collateral-specific release early; an open revolving facility often requires something narrower than terminating every filing.
> - Archive the exact listing sent to each buyer so later condition disclosures can be checked against the original express claims.
> - Run the escrow waterfall with actual payoff expiry dates before scheduling riggers; a missed pickup can also invalidate the payoff amount.

## 3. Draft Fee Protection Without Assuming Seller or Carrier Liability  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a brokerage mandate that names the principal, covered assets, territory, exclusivity, term, authorized marketing, negotiation limits, and prohibited commitments. State that the broker cannot bind the seller, promise machine performance, receive purchase funds, or vary sale terms unless a specific written authorization says otherwise and the activity is permitted. Define the fee mathematically: for example, six percent of the equipment purchase price actually paid, excluding separately stated freight, taxes, rigging, and buyer interest charges. State whether deposits, trade-ins, bundled tooling, or installment payments enter that base. Separate when the commission is earned from when it is payable, and specify the outcome for failed inspection, seller default, buyer default, and partial closings. A balanced trigger can earn and pay the fee upon completed settlement with cleared proceeds, with a separately negotiated remedy for deliberate circumvention. Have the escrow instructions acknowledge the commission allocation without assuming that escrow independently decides entitlement. Disclose any buyer retainer or referral payment and how it offsets or supplements the seller-paid commission. Keep the mandate consistent with the sale agreement; inconsistent fee definitions invite a dispute precisely when the team needs cash from a lumpy closing cycle. Avoid oral side agreements.

Protect introductions with a narrow, auditable tail rather than an unlimited restraint. For example, attach a list of introduced counterparties, define introduction evidence, exclude documented preexisting negotiations, and limit a post-termination tail to named assets and a negotiated period. Treat twelve months as an example to assess under governing state law, not an automatic enforceable duration. Any liquidated remedy should reflect anticipated loss rather than operate as a penalty. Allocate responsibility for seller statements, broker-authored descriptions, inspection work, data security, and physical handling separately. A negotiated liability cap can reference fees received, but exclusions for fraud, intentional misconduct, and other nonwaivable duties need state-specific review; some jurisdictions restrict additional limitations. Specify notice, cure, governing law, venue, and either litigation or a workable arbitration process, including cost allocation and access to provisional relief. Do not assume a chosen state's law defeats mandatory licensing rules elsewhere. For IP and data, obtain permission to use seller photographs and specification sheets, distinguish preexisting buyer relationships from seller-confidential records, and restrict access to controller backups containing proprietary programs. An OEM software license may require consent to transfer even when the machine itself changes ownership. State who requests that permission and pays associated charges.

Reconcile every fee discussion to the canonical scorecard rather than the equipment price. Conservative recognized commissions are $100,000; at a 25% net margin, owner profit is $25,000 and implied annual expenses are $75,000. Base commissions are $450,000; at 35.0%, profit is $157,500 and implied expenses are $292,500. Scaled commissions are $800,000; at 45%, profit is $360,000 and implied expenses are $440,000. Conservative and Base each use 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C. As an illustrative fee bridge, a $500,000 machine at six percent produces $30,000 commission, not $500,000 revenue. Fifteen such completed transactions produce the Base $450,000 commission total, without claiming that every deal will share that price or rate. The stated $2,500–$14,000 monthly operating range is not a substitute for the scorecard's complete annual expense envelope, which must accommodate deal-dependent costs and the modeled staffing economics. Budget specialist reviews, searches, travel, insurance, and contractor work within those envelopes. Maintain a separate cash forecast for the two-to-nine-month first-deal window and $10,000–$75,000 startup range. Do not treat an unsigned mandate, contingent fee, or escrowed purchase deposit as collected commission cash. Review realization monthly.

## 4. Run Lawful Outreach and Screen Export Deals Before Commitment  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build outreach around the channel actually used. CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. section 7704, applies to commercial business email as well as consumer email: use accurate headers and subjects, provide required advertising identification, include a valid physical postal address, and provide a working opt-out honored within ten business days. Use a centrally maintained suppression list in HubSpot or an equivalent CRM so buyer alerts cannot reactivate an opted-out recipient through a fresh import. A deployable email script is: 'Commercial equipment offer: We represent the owner of the listed CNC machine and are paid a seller commission if it closes. The attached sheet identifies the machine, inspection scope, and available records. Would you like the specification packet, or should we stop equipment emails to this address? Reply STOP to opt out.' Append the brokerage's actual identity and valid postal address before sending; that closing information is mandatory, not decorative. Do not claim exclusive inventory, a verified inspection, or immediate availability unless the deal file supports it. Log the source of each contact and its permitted use. Published contact information does not itself authorize automated calls or texts, and a purchased list does not establish the seller's claimed permissions.

For calls and texts, evaluate 47 U.S.C. section 227, 47 CFR section 64.1200, applicable do-not-call rules, and state statutes before choosing dialing technology. Manually placing a live call is not a universal exemption from solicitation restrictions, and business contacts often publish personal mobile numbers. Artificial or prerecorded voices, regulated autodialing, and marketing texts can carry different consent requirements; use the conservative operating default of individually reviewed live outreach until the channel has a documented legal basis. Check federal and state suppression requirements, calling windows, and company-specific stop requests. A live opening is: 'This is the equipment brokerage named in my introduction. We help industrial buyers source used machines. Is this an appropriate business line for a brief equipment inquiry? If you prefer no further calls, I will record that now.' After permission to continue, ask: 'What process, capacity, voltage, footprint, controller, delivery window, and inspection requirements are essential? Who can authorize the purchase, and will the equipment remain domestically?' Permission to continue this conversation is not blanket consent for later automated campaigns. Do not record calls without addressing the laws applicable to all participants, including California Penal Code section 632 where confidential communications are involved. Record discovery notes instead when recording authority is unresolved.

Export coordination starts before promising delivery. Determine whether the equipment is subject to the EAR, document its ECCN or supported EAR99 classification, and evaluate destination, end user, end use, and license requirements under 15 CFR parts 734, 736, 738, and 744. EAR99 does not mean every shipment is unrestricted. Advanced machine tools, related software, and technical information deserve specific review; defense articles can instead implicate ITAR jurisdiction and brokering rules in 22 CFR part 129. Screen parties against applicable government lists and consider OFAC ownership restrictions, including aggregate ownership under the 50 Percent Rule. A buyer-discovery script is: 'Please identify the purchasing entity, ultimate consignee, installation address, intended process, beneficial owners, and any intermediary receiving the machine. We will confirm classification and shipment requirements before committing to export terms.' Resolve red flags rather than accepting a freight forwarder's reassurance as a substitute for facts. Allocate exporter and agent responsibilities, powers of attorney, and Electronic Export Information duties under 15 CFR part 30; a routed transaction does not erase the parties' responsibilities. Screen again before shipment, retain required records, and protect controlled technical files from unauthorized access. The principal owns unresolved exceptions while contractors collect evidence.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Pre-Flight Checks Before Outreach Becomes a Closing Commitment**
> 
> The principal records a pass or an explicit unresolved exception for each check. An unresolved item blocks the affected action, not unrelated work on the transaction.
> - Verify the play, asset class, jurisdictions, and required licenses before offering compensated negotiation, auctions, transport arrangement, or custody services.
> - Verify seller authority, asset identity, relevant lien evidence, and authenticated release instructions before committing buyer funds to settlement.
> - Verify signed fee terms, inspection disclosures, risk allocation, escrow conditions, and data permissions before presenting the transaction as ready to close.
> - Verify outreach suppression and channel authority, then resolve applicable export classification, ownership, destination, and end-use checks before sending or shipping.
