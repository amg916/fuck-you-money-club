## 1. Price an accepted opportunity, then reconcile every revenue dollar  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with the economic object: a client buys account research, qualified introductions, and documented opportunity creation; the operator does not earn the buyer's million-dollar contract value. The canonical Conservative, Base, and Scaled revenue figures are $150,000, $675,000, and $1,200,000. The following underwriting bridge assumes five, fifteen, and twenty full-year client equivalents, respectively. These are illustrative cohort designs, not additional scorecard observations. Conservative invoices $25,000 of annual retainer plus four opportunities at $1,562.50 each per client. Base invoices $37,500 plus six such opportunities; Scaled invoices $50,000 plus eight. A blended four-percent allowance for contractual service credits and opportunity reversals reduces $156,250, $703,125, and $1,250,000 of face billings to the canonical revenue totals. Effective retained opportunity revenue is $1,500 each, while effective annual retainers are $24,000, $36,000, and $48,000 per client. Thus, twenty, ninety, and 160 retained-equivalent opportunities contribute $30,000, $135,000, and $240,000; retainers supply the balance. Fractional retained equivalents describe portfolio yield, not permission to invoice fractional meetings. Actual recognition follows delivered obligations and supportable consideration, rather than the date an invoice happens to leave the accounting system.

Define acceptance before the first campaign enters Salesforce or HubSpot. An accepted opportunity requires the agreed account profile, a named business problem, the relevant buying role, a completed discovery interaction, and a client-confirmed next step. Budget availability can be a qualification field without falsely claiming that every enterprise buyer has an approved purchase order. Store the account identifier, campaign identifier, source timestamp, meeting record, acceptance timestamp, and duplicate-search result together. A workable contract gives the client ten business days to reject an opportunity using enumerated reasons, then applies the agreed acceptance mechanism. Separate a missed meeting requiring replacement from an accepted opportunity that later loses to a competitor. The former may consume the credit allowance; the latter should not automatically reverse an earned origination fee. Match the lookback window to the client's actual sales cycle and existing account ownership rules. If an account already has an active opportunity, sell incremental research under the retainer instead of arguing over a duplicate introduction. Keep expected service credits separate from collection losses: a valid fee that remains unpaid is economically different from a fee never earned.

Read the waterfall as an annual portfolio statement with direct delivery payroll, contractor production, and delivery software above gross contribution. Conservative direct costs are $72,000 of loaded employee compensation, $18,000 of contractors, and $6,000 of tooling; the resulting $54,000 gross contribution is 36% of revenue. Base produces $337,500 at 50%, and Scaled produces $690,000 at 57.5%. Acquisition, administration, expected bad debt, and funding expense then reconcile exactly to owner profit. Compensation includes the modeled owner delivery wage; profit is the residual before the owner's personal income taxes. The Conservative payroll allocation is only $36,000 loaded per full-time role, an explicit weak point that requires location-specific wage and benefit validation. Adding $60,000 to normalize compensation turns its $30,000 profit into a $30,000 loss. Three or four contractors indicate intermittent specialists, not additional full-time salaries. Do not present this model as universally recruitable. The published $4,000–$20,000 monthly opex range can describe launch or narrow overhead, but cannot describe all-in mature spending: total annual deductions imply $10,000, $39,375, and $60,000 per month across these scenarios.

## 2. Separate scrub reserves, receivables, and spendable payout cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat scrub risk as a cohort measurement problem, not a percentage selected to make the profit column attractive. The four-percent allowance above is an underwriting assumption applied to face billings; replace it with observed contract-specific rates when sufficient cohorts mature. Track duplicates, incorrect account size, missing buying authority, disputed source attribution, service credits, and genuine no-shows as separate reason codes. Record both event counts and fee-weighted dollar losses because one large retainer credit can overwhelm many clean meetings. Freeze a monthly cohort after its contractual dispute period, while retaining a separate register for permitted later claims. QuickBooks or Xero credit notes should reference the original invoice and the CRM evidence packet. Do not deduct the same expected credit from recognized revenue and again from operating expense. Likewise, cash earmarked for refunds is a liquidity allocation, not a second profit deduction. The cash model below holds one average month's expected credits: approximately $521, $2,344, and $4,167. That buffer only fits a roughly monthly resolution cycle; extend it when contracts allow longer reversals or when disputes concentrate around quarterly procurement reviews.

Define DSO as invoice issuance to customer payment, then add only the separate interval between payment and usable bank cash. If the accounting report already measures through bank availability, do not add the payout lock again. For illustration, Conservative uses thirty days of DSO plus ten days of payout restriction; Base uses forty-five plus fifteen; Scaled uses sixty plus fifteen. These locks are stress assumptions, not standard terms attributed to any payment provider. Net cash operating expense excludes the noncash expected collection-loss charge but includes modeled funding fees, producing $117,000, $465,750, and $708,000 annually. Dividing by 365 gives approximately $320.55, $1,276.03, and $1,939.73 of daily burn. Multiplying by forty, sixty, and seventy-five days yields float requirements of $12,822, $76,562, and $145,479 before additional cushions. Adding the monthly credit buffer produces approximately $13,343, $78,906, and $149,646. This is a simple steady-state cash-expense coverage proxy with no supplier credit assumed, not an accounts-receivable valuation. Weekly cash forecasting must still reflect invoice milestones, payroll dates, tax remittances, restricted balances, and the difference between refundable advances and earned receipts.

Collect the retainer at the beginning of each service month and invoice accepted opportunities weekly where the contract permits. Advance collection improves liquidity but does not accelerate revenue recognition for work still owed. Pay contractors against completed research packets or accepted delivery batches, with clearly agreed terms rather than surprise delays imposed after the work. Maintain a thirteen-week cash forecast in which every projected receipt has an invoice, expected payment date, and named collection owner. Salesforce opportunity probabilities are not cash receipts and should never fund payroll. The $15,000–$100,000 startup capital range can support a lean launch, but the Scaled float proxy already exceeds its upper bound before onboarding costs or a payroll cushion. Close that gap through advance retainers, accumulated earnings, or an approved financing facility before increasing delivery commitments. Compare financing quotes using total dollars paid over the actual funded interval, including minimum fees and recourse exposure. A receivable buyer may reject disputed origination invoices entirely. Borrowed principal increases available cash and a liability, not revenue or owner profit; principal repayments likewise belong in the cash schedule rather than the margin waterfall.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the acceptance cohort
   - Export the week's accepted and disputed opportunities from Salesforce or HubSpot.
   - Match every fee to its client contract, source identifier, completed interaction, duplicate check, and acceptance timestamp.
2. Reconcile earned fees and credits
   - Tie invoice lines and credit notes to the cohort register.
   - Keep contractual consideration reductions separate from expected nonpayment, and document every change to the estimated credit rate.
3. Forecast usable cash by week
   - Update thirteen weeks of collections, payout restrictions, payroll, contractor payments, and funding obligations.
   - Deduplicate DSO and payout delays, exclude restricted balances from available cash, and show borrowed principal outside operating revenue.
4. Authorize capacity from funded runway
   - Compare available liquidity with the stressed timing requirement before committing new delivery work.
   - Record the advance retainer, retained earnings, or committed facility that funds each additional account; reduce intake when funding is absent.

## 3. Underwrite the three mapped plays without importing their headline transaction values  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

For telegram_channel_signal_scraping, buy signal processing capacity rather than a list of supposedly reachable members. An illustrative monthly lane spends $1,200 on permitted public-feed ingestion and normalization, $800 on analyst verification, and $1,000 on account research and outreach labor. Four accepted opportunities at the modeled $1,500 retained fee generate $6,000 and leave $3,000 before client acquisition, shared overhead, and retainer delivery. Two accepted opportunities merely cover the $3,000 direct lane cost; raw message volume contributes nothing by itself. Public OTC discussions and distress broadcasts provide hypotheses, not proof of buying authority or permission to contact every participant. Deduplicate reposted messages, verify the underlying company, and preserve source provenance without collecting unnecessary personal data. Never assume the official Bot API exposes every public channel's history or member roster. Its actual permissions determine what the bot can receive. The fallback is passive, read-only listening to public broadcasts through permitted access, coupled with inbound capture through official Bot API utility bots. If feed access becomes restricted, stop ingestion and substitute volunteered alerts or licensed sources; do not fund bypass work from the delivery budget.

For government_rfp_subcontractor_brokerage, distinguish a prime contractor's management spread from a lead operator's revenue. A $100,000 service award with $75,000 of subcontract fulfillment creates a $25,000 spread before supervision, insurance, bid expense, and collection costs; it does not create $100,000 of lead-generation fees. If this enterprise demand-generation business only supplies research and a qualified introduction, recognize its contracted retainer or permissible service fee instead. Principal-versus-agent treatment follows actual control and obligations, not the desired margin presentation. Use SAM.gov interfaces and permitted municipal portal access to identify solicitations, then cost the statement of work before matching a supplier. FAR 52.203-5 restricts contingent-fee arrangements where applicable, subject to its defined exceptions; do not assume an award-linked introduction fee is permitted. The 13 CFR 125.6 services limitation is generally measured against amounts paid by the government and payments to entities that are not similarly situated, rather than a universal headcount percentage. The fallback is unrestricted commercial or municipal work without federal set-aside limits, subject to local procurement rules, or eligible teaming with similarly situated entities and documented subcontracting calculations.

For out_of_network_dme_telehealth_billing_arbitrage, separate patient acquisition spend, provider reimbursement, and MSO service revenue before calculating anything resembling a margin. The mapped $85–$220 patient acquisition range means one hundred acquired patients consume $8,500–$22,000; it says nothing about clinical eligibility, covered benefits, completed documentation, or collected claims. A $900–$3,500 reimbursement illustration against $70–$220 hardware cost is provider-side unit math, not this operator's revenue. A fixed $6,000 monthly MSO services retainer cannot absorb that acquisition budget plus labor unless the service economics change. Have the client fund media directly and price documented marketing and software work at supportable fair market value. Use written personal-services terms evaluated against 42 CFR 1001.952(d); merely labeling a payment flat does not establish safe-harbor protection. Federal-program referrals implicate 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b), and laboratory arrangements can implicate 18 U.S.C. 220 across payer types. The fallback combines flat service retainers, independent clinical decisions, third-party chart review, and clinically supported recurring CGM resupply. Prioritizing commercial payers does not erase referral restrictions, state rules, or coverage requirements. Exclude clinical details from advertising event payloads and ordinary sales CRM records.

## 4. Stress the cohort before scaling headcount or distributing profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Stress rates against their correct denominators. Raising the contractual credit allowance from four to eight percent of unchanged face billings removes an additional $6,250, $28,125, and $50,000 from Conservative, Base, and Scaled revenue. With costs held constant, owner profit falls to $23,750, $174,375, and $430,000. A fifty-percent increase in operator acquisition cost instead adds $4,500, $33,750, and $48,000 of expense, leaving $25,500, $168,750, and $432,000. Acquisition here means winning paying enterprise clients, not the client's own paid media or the healthcare patient's acquisition cost. A twenty-percent increase in combined employee and contractor delivery labor adds $18,000, $59,400, and $90,000, producing $12,000, $143,100, and $390,000 of profit. Apply all three shocks together and profit contracts to $1,250, $81,225, and $292,000. These are deliberately static sensitivities: they do not assume instant price increases, staff reductions, or better lead quality to rescue the result. Keeping those responses outside the initial calculation exposes whether the current contract portfolio can withstand ordinary execution friction before management takes corrective action.

Measure acquisition payback using contribution, not gross invoicing. The annual gross contribution per client is $10,800 Conservative, $22,500 Base, and $34,500 Scaled. Dividing each by twelve produces monthly contribution of $900, $1,875, and $2,875; dividing respective CAC of $1,800, $4,500, and $4,800 by those amounts gives two, 2.4, and approximately 1.67 months of post-activation payback. These figures are not elapsed time from the first prospecting dollar. Enterprise sales cycles and the stated three-to-twelve-month time to first deal create a pre-revenue interval that the steady-state annual model does not fund automatically. Track acquisition cohorts from first spend through signed contract, activation, earned contribution, and collected cash. A fifteen-day collection delay adds approximately $4,808, $19,140, and $29,096 of required liquidity at unchanged daily cash expense, even if reported profit stays constant. Additional borrowing charges would reduce profit separately. Runway therefore needs both an activation budget and a collection buffer. Do not use the attractive post-activation payback number to justify hiring against unsigned pipeline or distributing cash that is already committed to delivery.

Concentration deserves a separate failure test because enterprise retainers are lumpy. Losing one equal-sized full-year client removes $30,000, $45,000, or $60,000 of recognized revenue across the three scenarios. If no costs can be removed immediately, annualized owner profit becomes zero, $157,500, or $420,000. Real losses depend on departure timing, prepaid obligations, replacement speed, and which contractors can actually be released. Track the largest client's share of collected cash as well as revenue; a slow-paying dominant account can consume more funding than its revenue percentage suggests. Before adding the third full-time role in Scaled, require a funded delivery schedule, a clean acceptance backlog, and account-level contribution evidence. Preserve contractor flexibility without misclassifying employee relationships or shifting unagreed collection risk onto workers. Distribute residual owner cash only after separating payroll obligations, tax liabilities, client advances, expected refunds, and facility payments. The operating target is not the largest pipeline dashboard. It is a repeatable cohort whose accepted fees survive credits, whose labor is paid on time, and whose collected contribution supports the next cohort without emergency financing.
