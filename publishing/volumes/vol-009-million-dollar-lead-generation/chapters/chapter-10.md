## 1. Build an evidence pipeline before buying more enrichment  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Organize the five-person team around decisions rather than applications. The first full-time operator owns account strategy, client definitions, and opportunity acceptance; the second owns integrations, observability, and recovery. Three contractors cover evidence research, editorial review, and campaign operations within explicit access boundaries. Create one event envelope containing tenant_id, account_id, event_id, source_url, observed_at, collected_at, permission_basis, schema_version, and payload_hash. Python workers can use urllib.request for authorized HTTP retrieval, hashlib for fingerprints, and sqlite3 for a single-host pilot ledger. Store timestamps in UTC and retain source excerpts sufficient to reproduce each conclusion. Use customer-approved Salesforce or HubSpot APIs for writes; never let an enrichment vendor become the authoritative account identity. Resolve legal entities and domains before requesting contact records, because subsidiaries, franchises, and acquired brands frequently share technology but buy independently. Treat retrieved pages as untrusted data, not instructions to the language model. Strip executable markup, limit response sizes, and pass only the excerpt needed for extraction. The model returns structured claims and evidence references; it receives no credentials and cannot authorize outreach, change billing, or browse arbitrary addresses supplied inside a page.

Measure automation against a fixed workload, including work that moves downstream. An illustrative batch of 100 account packets takes 1,000 minutes manually. Automated collection, normalization, deduplication, and first-pass qualification remove 800 minutes; retained decisions consume 200 minutes, additional QA consumes 60, and exception recovery consumes 20. The resulting 280 human minutes represent a 72% net reduction, not an 80% claim that hides review. Budget each automated run at $0.06 for retrieval, $0.08 for licensed enrichment, $0.03 for model inference, and $0.02 for queueing and storage: $0.19 before labor. One hundred runs cost $19; a $2 retry allowance and 280 minutes at an illustrative $45 hourly loaded labor rate bring the batch to $231, or $2.31 per packet. These are planning assumptions rather than vendor quotations. Instrument actual token usage, cache hits, failures, and reviewer minutes separately. A packet that requires three corrections is not a successful automated output. Evaluate a fixed weekly sample against source truth and track accepted packets per paid reviewer hour. Keep the target conditional on unchanged quality and contact-permission standards.

Make every side effect recoverable. For incoming webhooks, implement the provider's documented signature scheme using hmac where applicable, reject stale timestamps, and persist the verified envelope before acknowledging receipt. A unique constraint on tenant_id and event_id prevents ordinary replay from creating duplicate work. Run network requests outside database transactions and use a transactional outbox to record pending CRM writes. Persist the external record identifier after success; after a timeout, reconcile by the deterministic external key before retrying. Retry transient failures with bounded exponential backoff and jitter, honor Retry-After, and move exhausted jobs to an exception queue. Schema failures, revoked access, and absent permission require investigation rather than repeated execution. Prevent server-side request abuse by rejecting loopback, private, and link-local destinations and rechecking redirect targets. Keep secrets outside event payloads and redact personal contact details from ordinary logs. At pilot scale, one SQLite writer and short transactions are manageable; move to a managed transactional store before distributed workers introduce lock contention. Test restore procedures, not merely backups. A reviewer must be able to reopen the original evidence and resume one failed account without replaying an entire campaign.

## 3. Connect qualification, attribution, scheduling, and distribution  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Define a qualified opportunity as a contract object before teaching a model to recognize one. Required fields should include the accepted account, relevant buying role, evidenced business problem, mutually agreed next step, and client acceptance status. A calendar booking alone does not establish procurement authority, budget, or a payable opportunity. Keep raw source events immutable and derive first-touch, opportunity-sourcing, and influence views separately. Use Salesforce external identifiers and campaign membership, or equivalent HubSpot properties, to connect accepted evidence to the CRM opportunity without overwriting an existing owner. Record the attribution policy version on each fee-bearing event. Calendly webhooks should map creation, cancellation, and rescheduling to one meeting lineage; duplicate notifications must not produce multiple fees. Before displaying availability, check time zones, routing ownership, minimum notice, and contact eligibility. After the meeting, the client confirms attendance and qualification through a structured form. If the CRM is unavailable, hold proposed changes in the outbox and send an internal exception task, not an untracked spreadsheet invoice. Freeze disputed fees until the account owner resolves the evidence under the signed attribution rules. A transparent event ledger makes this resolution faster than competing dashboard screenshots.

Build programmatic distribution from approved account problems, not mass-generated claims. Publish useful migration checklists, procurement timelines, and benchmark pages only when a subject-matter reviewer can substantiate the content. Avoid customer-identifying examples without permission and remove thin pages rather than inflating the index. The loop is straightforward: aggregate recurring questions from accepted opportunities, produce an approved asset, distribute through search and customer-authorized channels, capture an explicit request, and return conversion evidence to the topic backlog. Illustratively, 20 pages at $150 production cost require $3,000. If they collectively attract 2,000 relevant visits, convert 3% into 60 requests, and yield six client-accepted opportunities, content cost is $500 per accepted opportunity before maintenance and sales labor. Treat these rates as hypotheses until cohorts mature. A voluntary shareable procurement worksheet can create a second loop: 100 recipients sharing at 15%, with two new visitors each and 20% request conversion, generate six additional requests, not six qualified opportunities. Use referral parameters that identify the asset rather than exposing personal identities. Measure incremental outcomes against a comparable holdout instead of crediting every later deal to the last downloaded document.

For outbound distribution, let automation select reviewable account cohorts and draft evidence-linked messages, while humans approve targeting rules and consequential claims. Use truthful sender identity and subject lines, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out mechanism for commercial email under 15 U.S.C. § 7704. Honor opt-outs within ten business days and propagate them immediately operationally rather than waiting for the statutory ceiling. Do not confuse email rules with permission for automated promotional texts or calls. Store channel-specific eligibility and enforce suppression again at send time, because approval yesterday cannot override revocation today. Start with small client-approved batches, review replies, and stop when complaints or delivery failures exceed the campaign's documented threshold. Feed genuine objections into qualification rules, not into tactics for bypassing recipient preferences. For covered California businesses, evaluate CCPA sale or sharing obligations and applicable opt-out signals before transferring contact data between clients or advertising systems. When a permission or suppression dependency fails, pause the affected channel while research and approved content work continue. That separation preserves useful throughput without turning a vendor outage into unauthorized distribution.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Register the evidence and permission envelope
   - Persist the account identity, source excerpts, channel eligibility, and deterministic event key before enrichment.
   - Reject cross-tenant references, unapproved retrieval destinations, missing source timestamps, and events whose permission basis cannot be evaluated.
2. Extract, corroborate, and review the proposed action
   - Run schema-constrained extraction, join corroborating sources, and send material claims to the assigned reviewer.
   - Record the reviewer, evidence version, decision, and rejection reason; a numeric model score cannot replace factual support.
3. Write once and route the meeting safely
   - Publish approved CRM changes through the outbox, recheck suppression, and create or update the meeting lineage.
   - Reconcile remote identifiers after ambiguous timeouts and preserve cancellation or rescheduling history instead of creating duplicate fee events.
4. Reconcile acceptance, costs, and learning
   - Obtain client acceptance, apply contract fee rules, and attach actual run costs and reviewer minutes to the cohort.
   - Resolve attribution disputes before invoicing and update extraction or targeting rules only after reviewing mature accepted-outcome evidence.
