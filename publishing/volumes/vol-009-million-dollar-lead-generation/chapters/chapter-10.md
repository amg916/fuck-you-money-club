## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator who touches law-firm intake lands on the same law: the lead decays in minutes, and whoever answers first wins the case. Loudr Media frames unconverted leads as ad budget poured into a 'bucket with holes,' notes that bad intake teams take 5 to 15 minutes to call back, and sets the house rule at 60 seconds ideal and 'five minutes max' [TJJfmZiIGW4 @ 00:01:34]. Ryan Samson of Accelerate Marketing pushes the bar lower still, claiming clients doubled booked calls by cutting response time from minutes to seconds, with an AI voice-and-text layer that answers Local Service Ads and Google Business Profile leads in under 30 seconds, after hours included [EJ36tZtFe4A @ 00:02:38]. The Local Service Ads channel runs the same play live: humans get 15 seconds to pick up, then AI takes the call, and the speaker claims you cannot rank in the top three without that coverage [I1FMlqBfPQc @ 00:04:56]. SMB Team lands the identical verdict from the Meta side, mapping the path as ad, then form, then follow-up, and warning that slow callbacks lose cases: 'The ad gets the click and the follow-up gets the client' [WZ82yYgND3M @ 00:11:52]. The ad spend buys attention; the response clock decides whether it becomes a signed case.

The second consensus is that a cheap lead that never becomes a viable signed case is the most expensive line in the budget. Katherine Hoffmann of Smart Reviews says the TV-and-radio era ran about 30% fallout, but today a lead that answers the phone proves nothing, because 'the frauds will always answer,' and problems often surface only 2 to 3 months later [dxqvZPB25eo @ 00:10:01]. Zero Risk Cases builds its pitch around the same threat: all lead generation is in-house, no cold calls, and a proprietary 'Scammer Scrub' screens every inbound and outbound lead against serial TCPA plaintiffs and 'lawsuit mill' litigators, updating in real time from court documents and the National Do Not Call list [bK2Wu8cOcRU @ 00:01:33]. The agency backs it with replacement 'no questions asked' when a case misses criteria or medical records contradict intake, against competitors who allow returns for only 3 to 7 days [z8D3CYz8Rxs @ 00:00:36]. Awais of Law Firm ROI reaches the same conclusion from the buyer side: shared leads at $50 to $150 get blasted to roughly 10 firms, so only exclusive live transfers are worth paying for, citing 4 leads for $2,000 that closed 2 clients at $5,000 each for a 10x return [gFY3JFigbao @ 00:52:39]. Exclusivity, verification, and replacement terms are what the money buys.

On the engagement side, the consulting operators agree that the retainer is the cure for revenue whiplash, and that it must be priced on value rather than hours. Consulting Success calls retainers the fix for the 'feast or famine' cycle, puts the market range at $2,000 to $50,000 a month with $5,000 to $25,000 typical, and insists on landing one premium client at $15,000 before chasing ten at $1,500 [78Np-tdJDW4 @ 00:03:11]. Dan Lok anchors high on purpose, claiming six figures a year for an hour a month of advising and pricing $10,000 a month as $120,000 a year or $100,000 paid upfront, pitched as '2 months free' [GyZuNsAtjIU @ 00:03:06]. Ana Leiman, drawing on 17 years on both buyer and seller sides, says scope-based work is the first thing a client cuts, so Leiman filters prospects through six criteria and only proceeds when at least five are met: a recurring problem, measurable value, work inside your moat, an outcome owner, capacity to pay, and trust already earned [ngCxldTE3UI @ 00:06:36]. eForms adds the structural baseline: a retainer reserves the contractor's time, it comes as pay-for-work or pay-for-access, and more than a third of digital marketing agencies already run on them [KhY7TkJppys @ 00:01:02].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

On Meta, the numbers are specific. Andrew Stickel ran Facebook lead ads for a Stuart, Florida client on the 3M earplug tort in May 2019 and pulled 77 leads at $40.55 apiece while reaching 67,000 people, with roughly half converting for about '$80 a case,' a figure Stickel admits is unverified [aAMJ5QeSrvU @ 00:02:03]. The ad sets split by branch: Army spent $863 for 20 leads at $43.19, Veterans $449 for 17 at $26, Navy $520 for 14 at $37, and Veterans plus interest expansion $305 for 10 at $30.59, geo-targeted to Miami plus 50 miles along Florida's east coast. Creative carried branch logos and called out the demographic in the first three words; the best Army ad pulled 8 leads at $35 [aAMJ5QeSrvU @ 00:05:27]. SMB Team's larger book backs creative over targeting: creative drove 47% of lift versus 9% for targeting, and retargeting ran $34 CPL against $55 cold, or $40 against $78 in personal injury [WZ82yYgND3M @ 00:16:06]. Their AI avatar ads, built in HeyGen and ElevenLabs from two headshots and an audio sample, produced 425+ leads at $39 CPL versus $64 for image ads, with personal injury at $108 versus $200+ [WZ82yYgND3M @ 00:19:05].

On Google, the Local Service Ads operators reverse-engineer spend from case value: on a $3,000 case, paying $300 to $1,000 per signed case is 'still a 3x ROI,' and cost per lead and cost per acquisition get tracked over time [I1FMlqBfPQc @ 00:02:15]. Ryan of Accelerated Marketing says LSA cases cost about half what other ad units cost because firms pay only for qualified calls, and Ryan disputes '10 and up to 50' of calls, getting that share of spend back in roughly a month and a half [abdd1rgCZ90 @ 00:01:11]. The setup runs through an MCC account with message leads on, 24/7 coverage for personal injury, a backup call service because 'google only gets paid when you answer,' a daily disposition sheet tagging missed and abandoned calls, and CallRail as mandatory tracking. Awais of Law Firm ROI splits paid budgets 30% Meta for awareness and retargeting and 70% Google to capture the 2 to 3% of the market ready to buy, and sets the floor at 3 to 4 times CPA, so a $2,000 CPA needs $8,000, possibly $6,000 [gFY3JFigbao @ 00:18:15]. SMB Team caps personal injury cold Meta campaigns at 5,000 a month in mid-size markets, non-PI at 2,500 to 3,000, and retargeting alone at 500 to 600 [WZ82yYgND3M @ 00:51:10].

Intake is where the case is won or lost, and the operators script it tightly. Loudr Media's framework runs five beats: warm rapport, empathy about what happened, next steps, clarity about the process, and commitment, meaning the retainer or a concrete follow-up before the call ends; they sign clients even if untreated and treat hanging up on untreated callers as a warning sign [TJJfmZiIGW4 @ 00:03:41]. Unsigned leads go into 30 days of 'rule of threes' follow-up, three touches a day across call, text, and email, worked by a dedicated, commissioned team treated 'like salesmen, not receptionists.' Zero Risk Cases signs claimants to the attorney's criteria, collects a digital retainer plus permission to forward it, and routes wet-signature documents to a third-party notary who returns ID photos and the notarized originals [bK2Wu8cOcRU @ 00:02:17]. Hoffmann's Smart Reviews then runs secondary QC after retainer, criteria check, and HIPAA are signed: background check, internal database search for dual representation and duplicate claims, a facility call to confirm treatment on medical torts, and a viability score of nonviable, potentially viable, or confirmed [dxqvZPB25eo @ 00:02:21]. After signing, Amy Goggins of Mass Tort Institute sends an update letter, written by Goggins and signed off by an attorney, every 6 months so no client goes longer without contact [0VBYF3XIGBE @ 00:00:53].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first fork is whether to buy signed cases or build the machine yourself, and the camps split hard. Zero Risk Cases sells signed mass tort cases nationwide. It promises to replace any case that misses criteria "no questions asked" and knocks competitors who allow returns for only 3 to 7 days [z8D3CYz8Rxs @ 00:00:36]. Its pitch is that all lead generation stays internal, no cold calls are made, and outbound dials go only to people who asked for them [bK2Wu8cOcRU @ 00:00:46]. Katherine Hoffmann of Smart Reviews pushes back on trusting surface signals. A lead answering the phone no longer proves legitimacy, firm feedback is "dangerous," and problems surface two to three months later. So she runs secondary QC after the retainer is signed: background checks, dual-representation searches and facility calls [dxqvZPB25eo @ 00:10:17]. Awais of Law Firm ROI goes further on the generic lead market. He says 95% of lead generators run cold email off Instantly, Apollo and ZoomInfo, and sell shared leads for $50 to $150 to roughly ten firms. In his view only exclusive live transfers are worth buying. His example: 4 leads for $2,000, two of them closed at $5,000 each, a 10x return [gFY3JFigbao @ 00:52:39]. Buyers who follow this camp demand replacement terms and run their own downstream QC instead of taking the vendor's word.

On when to turn on paid traffic, the practitioners run opposite playbooks. Andrew Stickel goes straight to Facebook lead ads. His Stuart, Florida 3M earplug campaign pulled 77 leads at $40.55 across 67,000 people, and about half converted for roughly "$80 a case", a figure he admits he never verified [aAMJ5QeSrvU @ 00:01:20]. SMB Team also leads with Meta. It reports 4,600+ leads at a $42 CPL (personal injury at $64), with retargeting beating cold traffic $34 to $55 [WZ82yYgND3M @ 00:07:08]. Ali Katz of New Law Business Model flips the order. Paid traffic is "like pouring gasoline on a fire," so it comes last, only after 50% of inquiries become planning sessions and 80% of sessions become $4,000 to $6,000 clients [mDqUwOQyPnc @ 00:24:38]. Awais splits the difference with 30% on Meta and 70% on Google. Meta handles awareness and retargeting, while Google captures the 2 to 3% of the market ready to buy. He also insists on a budget of at least three to four times CPA, so a $2,000 CPA means roughly an $8,000 budget [gFY3JFigbao @ 00:17:09]. In practice, Stickel and SMB Team test cheap leads fast and optimize from live numbers. Katz refuses to amplify any conversion process that has not hit its benchmarks.

Retainer structure is where the consulting voices split most sharply. Dan Lok always opens at a one-year term and never at three months. If the client balks, he downsells to six months and then three. He prices $10,000 a month, or $100,000 paid upfront as "2 months free." He then adds a percentage of revenue for B2B clients or advisory equity for software companies [GyZuNsAtjIU @ 00:01:13]. Consulting Success rejects the fixed opener. The minimum term should equal the time needed to show results, for example six months, because today's results reflect work done 60 to 120 days ago. Price should be tied to outcomes, not hour blocks that become "use it or lose it" [78Np-tdJDW4 @ 00:12:12]. Ana Leiman prices at 10 to 20% of the value created over the next twelve months. She never shows hours and recommends one option. Commissions can never be the safety net; the base fee must cover margin [ngCxldTE3UI @ 00:05:08]. eForms frames the split as pay-for-work versus pay-for-access. Pay-for-work suits newer contractors. Pay-for-access suits experienced ones, who reserve their time for the client and may have to turn down other work [KhY7TkJppys @ 00:02:22]. Lok stacks upside on top of the fee. Leiman protects the base and changes scope, never price.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The fraud trap hits hardest in mass torts. Hoffmann's line is blunt: "the frauds will always answer." She reports that Reddit groups circulate clean marketing links to farm claims. Feedback therefore has to be real-time, not the two-to-three-month lag firms typically deliver [dxqvZPB25eo @ 00:10:01]. Her fix is a viability score on every ingested case. A case is scored nonviable (with the reason, so firms can release it on ingestion), potentially viable, or confirmed, which counts as a bonus. The score lets marketers shut down any source that produces nonviables [dxqvZPB25eo @ 00:03:43]. Zero Risk Cases attacks the same problem upstream with Scammer Scrub. It screens every inbound and outbound lead against serial TCPA plaintiffs and lawsuit-mill litigators, updating in real time from court documents and the National Do Not Call list. Wet-signature documents are then notarized by a third-party notary who returns ID photos [bK2Wu8cOcRU @ 00:01:33]. The economics check is volume. Hoffmann says a marketer producing 1,000 NEC cases a week "doesn't make any sense." Depo cases rarely come in under $10,000 or faster than one to three a week. A firm demanding 30 Depo cases at $5,000 is told it is "not going to happen" [dxqvZPB25eo @ 00:06:32]. Juvenile abuse confirmation now drags 12 to 14 weeks.

The second trap is leakage between the ad and the signature. Loudr Media calls unconverted leads ad budget poured into a "bucket with holes." Bad intake takes 5 to 15 minutes to call a lead back. Loudr's target is 60 seconds, with five minutes as the maximum. Hanging up on an untreated caller is a warning sign, because the script signs them anyway. The follow-up runs 30 days, using a rule of threes: three touches a day across call, text and email. One client signed only after two weeks [TJJfmZiIGW4 @ 00:01:21]. Ryan of Accelerated Marketing warns that "google only gets paid when you answer." LSA firms therefore need a backup call service, 24/7 coverage for personal injury, and a daily disposition sheet that tags missed and abandoned calls [abdd1rgCZ90 @ 00:01:44]. The money check sits in disputes. Anywhere from 10 to 50 of the calls get disputed, and that share of spend comes back in about a month and a half [abdd1rgCZ90 @ 00:01:11]. SMB Team sees the same choke point on Meta. The path is ad, then form, then follow-up, and slow callbacks lose cases. Criminal and PI retargeting windows must stay short rather than running 90 to 120 days [WZ82yYgND3M @ 00:11:52].

The last traps are wasted spend and broken promises. Ali Katz watched lawyers burn tens of thousands of dollars on agencies that over-promise. Her conclusion from her own practice: "I didn't have a marketing problem." The real fault was clarity and conversion [mDqUwOQyPnc @ 00:07:41]. Awais reports that his own YouTube channel has produced one client since about March. He calls LinkedIn an "AI spam" bragging machine that is not worth it for law firms. He also calls agencies that sell GEO/AEO as a separate service a scam [gFY3JFigbao @ 00:41:55]. On the retainer side, Leiman lists the killers: dividing the project fee by three, scope creep, becoming "furniture," and leaning on commissions. Her rule is "be on the retainer, but not on the payroll" [ngCxldTE3UI @ 00:12:28]. After the signature, Amy Goggins of Mass Tort Institute names the classic mistake: feeding clients inaccurate information to relieve pressure. Her two golden rules are never to give an exact settlement or trial timeline and never to give even an estimate of value. She backs them with an update letter at least every six months, signed off by an attorney [0VBYF3XIGBE @ 00:02:05]. Leiman ranks weak boundaries as the biggest problem of all.
