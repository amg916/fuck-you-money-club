## 1. Sell an accepted buying opportunity, not a million-dollar story  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Million-dollar lead generation is an outsourced enterprise demand-generation business serving sellers whose customers can justify large, complex purchases. The operator identifies buying conditions, resolves the responsible account and people, earns a conversation, and transfers a documented opportunity into the seller's workflow. A million-dollar client contract describes the downstream commercial context, not the operator's invoice. The revenue boundary is retainers and qualified-opportunity fees; client contract values, property principal, employer benefit spending, and attributed pipeline remain separate measures. A $1,000,000 software agreement associated with one $5,000 accepted opportunity produces $5,000 of opportunity-fee revenue, plus whatever retainer service was earned. It does not produce $1,000,000 of agency revenue. Define an opportunity through observable facts: an eligible account, a relevant decision participant, an acknowledged business problem, a plausible purchase window, and an agreed next action. A calendar booking alone satisfies none of those conditions reliably. Salesforce or HubSpot should hold the evidence, while the contract specifies who accepts it and when a rejection is valid. This distinction turns a vague promise of access into a measurable service that procurement can buy, delivery staff can repeat, and the operator can defend during an attribution dispute.

Five archetypes organize the market: enterprise account-based marketing orchestration, filing-trigger origination, property-event demand capture, employer-benefit introductions, and specialist channel development. Each starts with a different signal and requires a different proof package. An enterprise infrastructure seller needs architecture change and procurement timing; a tax-appeal provider needs an assessment, ownership match, and an actionable filing deadline. An employer-benefit specialist needs evidence that the employer operates a self-funded plan, rather than merely having many employees. Do not combine these into one generic contact list. Select a sub-niche where one accepted opportunity has enough expected contribution to support research and a long sales cycle. For example, a seller earning $180,000 contribution per won account at a 10% accepted-opportunity close rate has $18,000 expected contribution per accepted opportunity before acquisition costs. A $5,000 opportunity fee may fit that budget; the same fee can destroy economics where contribution is $20,000 and conversion is 5%. Validate those assumptions from the client's actual cohorts, not a vendor benchmark. Use a retainer to fund reserved research capacity and charge the variable fee only against the contracted evidence standard. Exclusivity should identify accounts, territories, duration, and release conditions rather than claiming ownership of an entire industry.

The durable moat is a maintained decision system, not possession of Apollo, Clay, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, or a large language model. Build a versioned account map that links legal entities, operating brands, subsidiaries, buyer roles, filing identifiers, and prior client relationships. Attach source timestamps and reviewer decisions so another researcher can reproduce why an account entered the campaign. Automate account qualification, CRM attribution, and meeting scheduling only after those decisions have stable definitions. Clay can coordinate enrichment, Salesforce can record account ownership, and Calendly can route a meeting; none independently establishes purchase intent or permission to contact someone. Measure positive replies, held discovery sessions, accepted opportunities, subsequent stage progression, and reversals as separate events. Track rejected opportunities by reason instead of quietly replacing them and preserving an inflated success rate. A beginner often scales list volume when acceptance falls; a disciplined operator narrows the signal or repairs the evidence. Keep client concentration visible using both recognized revenue share and unpaid receivables. If one account supplies 40% of revenue, a delayed renewal can impair staffing even while the pipeline dashboard looks healthy. Candidate #21 earns its 86/100 score through leverage and specialization, not through immunity to long sales cycles or disputed attribution.

## 2. Reconcile earned revenue, delivery costs, and owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical Conservative case recognizes $150,000 annual revenue at a 20% net margin, yielding $30,000 annual owner net profit with 2 full-time people and 3 contractors. One illustrative fee bridge is five clients paying $2,000 monthly for twelve months, producing $120,000, plus ten accepted opportunities at $3,000, producing $30,000. The Base case recognizes $675,000 at 30.0%, yielding $202,500 with the same headcount: nine clients at $5,000 monthly produce $540,000, and twenty-seven accepted opportunities at $5,000 produce $135,000. The Scaled case recognizes $1,200,000 at 40%, yielding $480,000 with 3 full-time people and 4 contractors: twelve clients at $6,250 monthly produce $900,000, and fifty accepted opportunities at $6,000 produce $300,000. These are annual steady-state constructions, not promises about first-year ramp or prescribed price cards. They imply total annual expenses of $120,000, $472,500, and $720,000 respectively. Preserve those expense totals when testing hiring, enrichment, postage, software, and owner workload. Contractor headcount does not imply full-time contractor utilization. At the Conservative level, staffing is especially tight; a labor schedule must show how the actual compensation arrangements fit the envelope without treating unpaid effort as a scalable resource.

Recognition follows the promised service and the applicable accounting framework, not the invoice date or the excitement of a sales announcement. A monthly stand-ready research service can generally be recognized over its service period when the contract supports that treatment. An advance payment for six future months is not immediately six months of earned revenue. For opportunity fees, establish whether acceptance is a substantive performance condition, how disputes are resolved, and whether expected credits constrain the amount recognized. Keep billed amounts, cash collected, earned revenue, and refund exposure in separate ledger fields. If a client prepays $12,000 for six months of evenly delivered service, the illustrative monthly earned amount is $2,000 while the remaining obligation declines with delivery. If a $5,000 accepted opportunity later fails a contractual duplicate-account test, record the required credit rather than leaving both revenue and acceptance counts untouched. Do not recognize a percentage of a customer's unsigned purchase forecast. Where the operator arranges third-party services, assess principal-versus-agent treatment rather than grossing every pass-through payment into revenue. Maintain a written accounting policy with the accountant and reconcile the CRM acceptance ledger to invoices, credits, deferred amounts, and the general ledger every month.

The startup capital range is $15,000–$100,000, the published monthly opex range is $4,000–$20,000, and time to first deal is 3–12 months. Those ranges cannot replace the scenario expense arithmetic: the canonical annual expenses average $10,000, $39,375, and $60,000 monthly. The published opex band therefore does not describe total monthly expenses at Base or Scaled economics. Before committing capital, obtain a cost-category reconciliation that explains the difference rather than assuming the smaller range covers every delivery obligation. At $10,000 monthly cash consumption, $60,000 funds six months before collections, reserves, or additional setup spending; a twelve-month delay requires a different plan. Sell a bounded paid diagnostic or research pilot when procurement will not yet support an annual engagement, but recognize only what that pilot delivers. The Base construction averages 2.25 accepted opportunities monthly across nine clients, illustrating that high-value origination is not a mass-booking factory. Forecast researcher hours, client review latency, and rejection rates alongside revenue. If a client takes thirty days to review evidence, opportunity-fee collections can lag delivery even when the campaign performs. Use renewal timing, concentration limits, and a cash reserve to absorb that mismatch instead of funding permanent staff against unaccepted pipeline.

## 3. Choose a sub-vertical and bound each mapped play  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The play public_filing_trigger_mail_arbitrage uses daily Secretary of State formations and county deed or mortgage recordings to identify timely service needs. Its legitimate output is a clearly private offer for recurring workplace compliance updates, assessment-appeal services, or exclusive new-mover and B2B introductions. A filing is a trigger, not proof of employer size, property occupancy, or purchase intent. Resolve the entity and mailing address, filter registered-agent offices, and check recording corrections before buying postage through Lob or PostGrid. Illustratively, 1,000 pieces at $1.10 fully variable production and postage cost require $1,100; ten accepted introductions at $250 yield $2,500 in operator fees and $1,400 contribution before research, software, overhead, and reversals. These pilot figures are not additions to the canonical annual cases. If five introductions fail acceptance, contribution falls to $150 on the same mailing cost. Use private commercial branding and sell genuine services; never resemble a mandatory government poster bill or an official deed invoice. 39 U.S.C. § 3001 addresses deceptive mailed solicitations, including specified government-lookalike and invoice formats, while state rules can add restrictions. Tax-appeal contingent compensation belongs to the appropriately authorized provider; an operator should use a permitted service-fee structure when local referral restrictions prevent contingent lead compensation.

The play sec_edgar_8k_vendor_trigger_arbitrage translates Form D offerings and Form 8-K events into researched account hypotheses. Watch Item 5.02 leadership changes, Item 1.01 material agreements, and Item 2.01 completed acquisitions or dispositions; read the filing because an event label alone does not establish a relevant budget. A Form D notice does not prove that the full offered amount was raised or remains available. Use available SEC filing feeds and submissions JSON, preserve accession identifiers, and route high-value events to a reviewer for sub-hour outreach where appropriate. Apply an aggregate token bucket capped at five requests per second, identify the application in its User-Agent, cache responses, and honor backoff instructions. For historical lookbacks, mirror SEC nightly bulk ZIP archives where provided rather than repeatedly fetching the same records. An illustrative client paying a $4,000 monthly monitoring retainer and accepting four $2,000 opportunities produces $12,000 monthly operator revenue. Against $3,000 analyst cost, $1,000 data and software cost, and $1,000 allocated overhead, contribution is $7,000 before remaining business expenses. If no opportunities qualify, the same cost base loses $1,000 against the retainer. The fallback is narrower coverage, a resized service commitment, or repricing, not higher request volume or an invented funding claim.

The play erisa_self_funded_specialty_drug_importation_arbitrage targets employers with 100–5,000 employees, but its international sourcing premise cannot be treated as a ready-to-sell operating route. The mapped comparison uses $6,000–$15,000 monthly US PBM list prices against $800–$2,200 foreign prices, proposed $0 employee copays, and 25–35% shares of $50,000–$100,000 annual patient savings. List prices are not verified net plan costs, and manufacturer-sealed packaging or a foreign pharmacy license does not establish lawful US importation. ERISA does not exempt a plan from federal drug requirements. Section 804 importation programs under 21 U.S.C. § 384 have specific authorization requirements and exclusions, including biological products; personal-importation discretion is not a commercial distribution license. Illustratively, verified domestic net cost of $96,000 against a lawful alternative costing $24,000 gives $72,000 gross savings, with $21,600 representing a 30% provider fee before service costs. That fee is not automatically this lead operator's revenue. Use independent TPAs and transparent pass-through PBMs to validate lawful domestic options, manufacturer Patient Assistance Program eligibility, clinical continuity, and domestic bridge fills. Some assistance programs exclude these patients or arrangements. If an authorized pathway, eligible product, and clinical safeguards cannot be documented, omit importation entirely and sell only qualified introductions around lawful plan-cost management.

## 4. Install a repeatable acceptance, privacy, and delivery system  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Launch one narrow account cohort with a signed acceptance schedule before activating automation. Specify geography, company size, excluded sectors, eligible buyer roles, minimum evidence, existing-account exclusions, and how long account protection lasts. Give the client a defined review period and require coded rejection reasons supported by evidence. Do not rely on silence as acceptance unless that mechanism is explicitly agreed and workable for both teams. Store each signal with its source URL or filing identifier, observation time, entity match confidence, and researcher rationale. Use a stable account identifier to prevent one subsidiary, parent, and trading name from generating three fees for one buying project. Salesforce validation rules or HubSpot required properties can block incomplete handoffs without making the scoring model the final judge. Schedule discovery only after the recipient understands the commercial purpose and the proposed agenda. If the seller repeatedly misses meetings or delays follow-up, record the failure separately from lead quality. A weekly operating review should reconcile delivery, acceptance, stage progression, credits, and invoices. This creates an audit trail for disagreements and a feedback loop for targeting. It also exposes an uncomfortable but useful result: some clients lack the sales capacity to monetize additional opportunities and should not receive more volume.

Apply channel-specific rules rather than treating public records as universal outreach permission. Commercial email falls under 15 U.S.C. § 7704, including accurate headers, nondeceptive subjects, a valid physical postal address, and a functioning opt-out process. Honor opt-outs within the statutory period and synchronize suppression across the sending tools used for that campaign. Calls and texts require separate analysis under 47 U.S.C. § 227, implementing rules, and applicable state requirements; a business title does not remove every restriction. California privacy duties under Civil Code § 1798.100 depend on applicability and processing context, so document sources, purposes, notices, requests, and retention instead of assuming B2B records are categorically excluded. Collect only the data necessary to establish commercial relevance. Employer-benefit prospecting should use employer-level signals, not patient diagnoses, prescription histories, or individual claims exported into enrichment platforms. Keep sensitive clinical review inside the authorized provider's controlled workflow. Where federal health care program business or federally reimbursable items are implicated, examine 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b) before adopting referral compensation. For federal procurement, review FAR Subpart 3.4 and applicable contingent-fee clauses. The practical fallback is a properly scoped research service or another permitted compensation structure, not relabeling an impermissible referral payment.

Run a sixty-day pilot as a measurement exercise, with commercial renewal decisions based on evidence rather than a promised enterprise closing date. Weeks one and two establish the exclusion snapshot, source permissions, account taxonomy, outreach copy, acceptance criteria, and the cash budget. Weeks three through six test a bounded cohort and inspect every accepted opportunity for fit, duplication, and next-step quality. Weeks seven and eight reconcile CRM events with invoices and client feedback, then decide whether to renew, narrow, reprice, or stop. Enterprise contracts may close months later; distinguish early acceptance evidence from realized client wins. Assign one person to research quality, one to client delivery and acceptance, and contractors to bounded enrichment, production, or specialist review tasks. At Scaled size, the third full-time role should remove the demonstrated bottleneck rather than automatically adding another salesperson. Track acceptance cost as total cohort delivery cost divided by accepted opportunities, including rejected records in the cost numerator. If a $9,000 cohort produces three accepted opportunities, acceptance cost is $3,000 each before unallocated overhead. Compare that figure with earned fees and retainer allocation, then stress-test a lost client and delayed collections. Scale only the repeatable process whose quality, cash demands, and rejection behavior remain visible under that stress.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the account and acceptance baseline
   - Export exclusions and sign the cohort-specific qualification schedule.
   - Assign stable entity identifiers, document protected accounts, and identify the client's acceptance reviewer before sourcing begins.
2. Capture and validate the buying signal
   - Ingest permitted records and attach source-level evidence to each candidate.
   - Apply deduplication, entity matching, channel rules, and a human relevance review before the record enters production outreach.
3. Deliver a documented discovery handoff
   - Record buyer relevance, business problem, timing, and the agreed next action.
   - Route the evidence to the designated reviewer; retain acceptance, rejection, and correction timestamps without overwriting prior decisions.
4. Reconcile economics and revise the cohort
   - Match earned service periods and accepted opportunities to invoices and credits.
   - Report delivery cost, rejection causes, collection timing, concentration, and stage progression before changing price or expanding volume.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before the first production cohort**
> 
> Activate only the channels and offers supported by the evidence collected.
> - Verify the signed fee schedule, acceptance standard, duplicate-account baseline, rejection window, and credit mechanism against the configured CRM fields.
> - Verify actual source access terms, mail identity, email suppression, calling or texting requirements, and any sector-specific compensation restrictions.
> - Verify each benefits claim with the authorized provider; remove unsupported importation, universal assistance eligibility, and automatic zero-copay claims.
> - Verify the cash budget against $120,000, $472,500, or $720,000 annual scenario expenses, with client concentration and collection delays explicitly stress-tested.
