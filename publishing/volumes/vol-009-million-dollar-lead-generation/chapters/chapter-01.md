# Chapter 01: Define the Buyer and the Route to Market

## Executive Summary

The practitioners converge on one rule: define the buyer by the pain you fix, then cut the market until only accounts with active, budgeted, urgent pain remain. OGONJO Workbooks, Myk Pono and Connor Murray all build targeting from problems and outcomes rather than products or titles, while The Influence, WebFX and Misar AI put the company-level ICP filter ahead of persona work. The street mechanics are tight. Hunter's 31M-email data shows lists of 100 or fewer more than double replies. OGONJO scores 5 to 7 criteria and disqualifies 20 to 30%, and Dan Martell trims 50,000 accounts to 800 with technographics. The disagreements run on list size and route to market: Hunter's 50-to-100-person sequences against Jeff Borshawa's 10-to-12 whales, sales-led against marketing-led on Prairie Founders Club's $10K LTV line, and Neil McDonnell's federal notice-and-teaming path.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every serious operator here starts targeting from the pain, not the product. OGONJO Workbooks puts it bluntly: stop defining your company by what you do and define it by what you fix. A customer buys a solution to a problem, not a product, and each seat feels that problem differently. The CFO feels cost, the VP of Sales feels lost revenue, and the end user feels frustration [ZSqgCrydlAo @ 00:00:30]. Myk Pono runs the same logic as a funnel. The target audience is everyone who has the pain. Potential customers add fit and budget, meaning more than 25 employees or over $1M ARR. The true ICP is the slice where that pain is active and they are already looking for a solution [Y1u-ngsyH60 @ 00:01:46]. Connor Murray narrows in a fixed order of territory, then ICP, then accounts, then people. He prioritizes by industry, company size, use case, and the business outcomes and KPIs the buyer is measured on [DBGq--oFsQ8 @ 00:01:20]. Hunter's team adds a hard three-part test: a target must have hair-on-fire pain, budget and authority, and a message you can actually tailor to them. Miss any one of the three and the account drops out [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:16:35]. The shared operating rule is that if you cannot state the pain in the buyer's own words, you do not have an ICP yet. You have a product description pointed at a crowd.

The second point of agreement is that a smaller, sharper list beats volume. Hunter's state-of-email-outreach data, built on 31M outreach emails, shows that lists of 100 leads or fewer more than double replies. Meanwhile, 61% of decision makers call most outreach irrelevant, because a bigger audience forces a broader, weaker message [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:09:28]. lemlist frames the same trap in one line: reaching out to everybody means reaching nobody [7hSBNM6xnmc @ 00:00:12]. Jeff Borshawa, on Marketing in the Age of AI, takes it to the extreme. You need 10 to 12 people, not 30,000, and treating suspects as prospects is the core mistake. Ten, maybe 20, ideal clients will change a small business's trajectory. Jeff ties this whale-hunting approach to Chad Holmes's Dream 100 and a granular top-100 list, and warns that chasing minnows means small deals that carry no margin [M-Z0SPYJ9-w @ 00:19:38]. Dan Martell explains why focus compounds with the image of air pressure in a tire. A tight geographic focus builds a mini-brand: LA for music, New York and Bay Street for finance, the Midwest for farm [RqN41GVY6UQ @ 00:01:30]. Across the board, the operators treat narrowing as the lever that raises response, not as a cap on upside. Spray-and-pray signals such as a 10,000-name list, or the complaint that outreach just doesn't work, are the tell that the target is wrong.

The third consensus is that the ICP is a company-level filter that runs before any persona work, and its real job is to cut. The Influence opens with a cautionary case: perfect personas sitting inside companies that could not afford the product. It lands on a fixed order, with the ICP first as the filter and the persona second to personalize. In their words, the ICP tells you which doors to knock on and the persona tells you exactly what to say when somebody answers [_i5Xkz85mPA @ 00:04:59]. WebFX draws the identical split. The ICP is company traits and the persona is a fictional individual such as 'Sally, 54.' Both should be specific enough to name northern Kansas counties and a 20-to-90-employee band [V7x_5WjVUiw @ 00:00:57]. OGONJO Workbooks calls the ICP a disqualification tool outright and warns that a vague ICP is worse than having none at all [4S63IHiDhTk @ 00:01:07]. Misar AI builds that cutting power straight into the worksheet. Every row is one filter that exports to the CRM, and anti-ICP rows sit beside the positives as hard negatives: commodity manufacturing, low budget, no CRM [giIGT_MMd5k @ 00:01:58]. The working stack has two layers. Fit decides whether an account gets touched at all, and only then does the persona shape the words, the KPIs you speak to, and the sign-off authority you need to reach.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Hunter's build is the most concrete. Skip TAM, SAM and SOM for outreach, which Matt calls terrible for this job. Work in the obtainable ring instead and pick the segment you can dominate, where buyers are desperate [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:12:15]. Their Proposely example shows the precision expected: marketing and creative agencies with 10 to 15 employees, US-based, founded 2015 to 2022. Seed the search with your 10 to 20 best customers and run the funnel from market to company to person. Stop at 50 to 80 people, maybe 100. The validation check is that existing customers start showing up in your filters. Matt filters on headcount, not revenue, except for AI companies, because there is no good revenue data on any company that does not publish it [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:28:02]. In the live Discover AI demo, natural-language search took roughly 2,000 companies down to 337 people and then to 44 in California. Dan Martell runs the same compression on technographics. He mines BuiltWith and job posts for tech-stack clues and trims 50,000 accounts to 800 [RqN41GVY6UQ @ 00:03:24]. Misar AI's SaaS worksheet lands in a similar range: UK, Germany and France, 100 to 500 employees, $10M to $50M ARR, running AWS plus Zapier. It targets a Head of Ops or VP Product and yields 150 to 200 accounts [giIGT_MMd5k @ 00:03:03].

Once the list exists, the practitioners score it and hunt for timing. OGONJO Workbooks wants a one-sentence ICP statement backed by 5 to 7 criteria scored 0 to 5, including the health of the decision-making unit. Their benchmark is to disqualify 20 to 30% of opportunities and see roughly a three-times win-rate gap between high and low scorers. Their cybersecurity case used three criteria: revenue above $100M, a regulated industry, and a named security officer. It lifted SDR productivity 45% and acceptance 35% [4S63IHiDhTk @ 00:04:33]. For timing, Hunter's Matt points to hiring, funding, tech-stack and social signals. He warns to wait a couple of weeks after a new HR hire before pitching, since only 2 to 8% of any market is actively looking at a given moment [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:32:17]. lemlist converts the persona into a scrapable persona for LinkedIn filters. SDR headcount growth signals revenue goals, and a years-in-position filter tightens the cut further [7hSBNM6xnmc @ 00:04:48]. Misar AI applies negative filters first: competitor partners, data-law noncompliance and integration gaps. Only then does it layer SIC and NAICS codes, refreshing the list weekly [giIGT_MMd5k @ 00:06:39]. The net mechanic is to score, time and exclude before a single email goes out, so the send budget is spent only on accounts that clear every gate.

The route to market is where the numbers get specific. Hunter's sending rules start with a 15-per-day floor and a cap around 200 to 250 against a roughly 500 provider limit, with a recommendation of 50 or fewer per day. Run one sequence per quarter at 3 to 4 steps, and remember that progressive sending is not warm-up [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:51:28]. If you are sitting on 10,000 leads, split them into ten lists of 1,000 using one or two levers. Prairie Founders Club picks the motion with a 2x2 of B2B versus B2C and LTV over or under $10K. B2B above $10K is sales-led and B2C below $10K is marketing-led. LTV is total revenue divided by total customers, calculated on at least three years of data [VJjRWz_6Hq0 @ 00:02:08]. On paid, GrowthSpree, pitching its own $3,000-a-month flat model, argues that the tell is the feedback loop. Feed tiered CRM conversions back to Google, LinkedIn and Meta: MQL at $100, SQL at $900, opportunity at $3,000, closed-won at deal value. Their reasoning is that only 13% of MQLs become SQL [YOYPAYz4iOc @ 00:02:44]. For the federal route, Neil McDonnell treats SAM.gov, DIBBS, Unison, GSA eBuy and CPORT as a daily exercise, and his SAM walkthrough filtered 3,800 notices to 1,000 and then to 177 [YL3LT_x34Ms @ 00:21:57].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Pin down the problem you fix
   - Mine call notes, support tickets and surveys for the top three problems, then pick the most urgent as the core statement.
   - Write the problem in the customer's words, cascade it to the C-suite, department head and end user, and get a gut check from a sales colleague; target 1 to 2 problem statements in circulation [OGONJO Workbooks — ZSqgCrydlAo @ 00:03:13].
2. Clone your most profitable clients
   - Pull roughly your 10 most profitable clients and extract shared firmographic, technographic, cultural and recent-change attributes.
   - Use BuiltWith for tech stack, interview customers directly, then prioritize 5 to 10 attributes such as ready, urgent, willing, able and advocacy potential [lemlist — 7hSBNM6xnmc @ 00:01:25].
3. Write the one-sentence ICP and scorecard
   - Compress the attributes into one sentence backed by 5 to 7 criteria scored 0 to 5, including decision-making-unit health.
   - Pair firmographic and technographic data with an outcomes framework, avoid 20-point scorecards, and confirm the scorecard disqualifies 20 to 30% of deals [OGONJO Workbooks — 4S63IHiDhTk @ 00:03:24].
4. Build the list and sequence it
   - Seed the search with 10 to 20 best customers, apply excludes, and stop at 50 to 80, maybe 100, people per list.
   - Exclude wrong industry, contract lock-in and sanctioned locations, then run one 3-to-4-step sequence per quarter at 50 or fewer sends per day [Hunter — 35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:38:43].

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Small moves the operators use to sharpen targeting and reply rates**
> 
> These are the low-effort adjustments the practitioners call out as outsized: widen contacts inside an account, dig to the emotional driver, build rooms instead of pitching them, and settle money before paperwork.
> - Email more than one person per target company. Hunter's outreach data shows almost +50% replies, and editing templates plus body personalization adds about another +50% [Hunter — 35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:10:21].
> - Run the Five Whys on every problem statement, asking 'why does that matter?' five times until you hit the emotional reason the buyer will act [OGONJO Workbooks — ZSqgCrydlAo @ 00:08:35].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first split is where the definition starts. The Influence runs company-first: the ICP is the 'where' filter built from size, revenue above $2M, industry, location, tech stack and urgency such as a compliance deadline, and only after that filter does the persona layer decide what to say [_i5Xkz85mPA @ 00:04:11]. Myk Pono attacks the persona-first habit directly, warning that companies who jump in to create personas miss whole segments, and replaces it with a funnel: target audience is everyone with the pain, potential customers add fit and budget such as more than 25 employees or more than $1M ARR, and the ICP is only the slice whose pain is active and who are already looking for a solution [Y1u-ngsyH60 @ 00:00:47]. OGONJO Workbooks starts further upstream still, arguing a customer buys a solution to a problem, not a product, and cascading one urgent problem statement down to the CFO who feels cost, the VP of Sales who feels lost revenue and the end user who feels frustration before any firmographic row gets written [ZSqgCrydlAo @ 00:00:30]. Prairie Founders Club pushes deepest into the individual, logging hobbies and TV habits because people buy from those they like and trust [VJjRWz_6Hq0 @ 00:04:53].

The second fight is over whether to mine your existing book. lemlist tells operators to pick roughly the 10 most profitable clients and extract shared firmographics, technographics, culture and recent changes, then prioritize 5 to 10 attributes including ready, urgent, willing, able, acquisition efficiency, ascension and advocacy potential [7hSBNM6xnmc @ 00:01:25], and Jeff Borshawa on Marketing in the Age of AI says to look inside your 10 favorite clients and clone them because they already know your next favorite clients [M-Z0SPYJ9-w @ 00:20:55]. The counter-camp is loud. Myk Pono warns that analyzing only the highest-ASP or highest-LTV accounts reinforces the segments you already have, so other segments must be researched proactively [Y1u-ngsyH60 @ 00:02:42]. OGONJO Workbooks calls 'all our current customers' a backward-looking map and defines SOM as only what you can win in the next 12 months [bVYYwxZZ4dU @ 00:05:19]. OGONJO's own answer is to rank 3 to 4 segments, add a technographic layer, and commit 80% of effort to two verticals, which is how a procurement SaaS focused on US SaaS firms with 200 to 1,000 employees on Salesforce plus NetSuite lifted marketing ROI 50% and lead-to-opportunity 30%.

The third split is about sizing the hunt. Hunter's team calls TAM, SAM and SOM terrible for outreach and builds inside a prioritized obtainable market where buyers are desperate, landing on 50 to 80, maybe 100, people per campaign and using headcount over revenue, except for AI companies, because there's no good revenue data about any company that doesn't publish it [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:12:15]. Jeff Borshawa compresses harder, arguing you need 10 to 12 people, not 30,000, and that 10, maybe 20, ideal clients change a small business's trajectory [M-Z0SPYJ9-w @ 00:19:38]. On deal size, Dan Martell says start low or mid-market and move up, and uses a geography focus to build a mini-brand the way air pressure works in a tire [RqN41GVY6UQ @ 00:01:30]. Martell's technographic trim, using BuiltWith and job-post mining, cuts 50,000 accounts to 800. Jeff runs the opposite play, whale hunting rather than chasing minnows because big players' little problems carry decent budgets while small players' big problems have tiny ones. OGONJO Workbooks then contradicts Martell's geography lever, calling geography-only segments a trap and testing focus by whether a team can put 80% of effort into two verticals [bVYYwxZZ4dU @ 00:05:50].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most repeated failure is aiming at everyone. Hunter's team lists the warning signs verbatim, a list of 10,000 or a hundred thousand and a founder saying outreach just doesn't work, then backs it with data from 31M emails: 61% of decision makers call most outreach irrelevant, a bigger audience forces a broader message, and lists of 100 leads or less more than double replies [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:06:23]. lemlist frames the same trap as reaching out to everybody and reaching nobody [7hSBNM6xnmc @ 00:00:12]. Jeff Borshawa learned it coaching accounting firms, where general terms produced general results until the framing tightened to three to five partners and 10 to 20 staff; they also reject 'I serve everyone' using skincare's everyone-with-skin pitch as the example [M-Z0SPYJ9-w @ 00:05:13]. The conference variant is worse: at Nvidia GTC with about 30,000 attendees, only 3% are in market, a super connector meets no more than 500 people, the energy lasts 48 to 72 hours then goes quiet for 362 days, and closing a widget pitch there is dumb luck. GrowthSpree adds the downstream symptom, MQLs rising and CPL falling while pipeline stays flat, with only 13% of MQLs becoming SQL [YOYPAYz4iOc @ 00:00:36].

The second trap is mistaking a good-looking target for a buyer. The Influence opens on a cautionary case of perfect personas sitting inside companies that could not afford the product, and treats spreadsheet-run tech stacks as a sign a company is not ready [_i5Xkz85mPA @ 00:00:14]. OGONJO Workbooks says a vague ICP is worse than none, warns against 20-point scorecards and scorecards that just validate the existing pipeline, and sets the bar at 5 to 7 criteria scored 0 to 5, disqualifying 20 to 30% with roughly a three-times win-rate gap between high and low scores [4S63IHiDhTk @ 00:02:18]. Connor Murray carries the trap into the deal: it is very easy to get happy ears, intent is not execution, and in their example legal takes 6 weeks and security another 2, so you can't take anything for granted [DBGq--oFsQ8 @ 00:06:22]. Connor also admits 'I certainly made this mistake when I was new' in negotiation, which is why they insist on knowing discount bands and approval limits beforehand and trading any discount for a longer term, faster decision or upfront payment. GrowthSpree flags the measurement trap: dark-funnel touches get marked direct and under-credited, and the tell is whether tiered CRM values feed back: MQL $100, SQL $900, opportunity $3,000, closed-won at deal value [YOYPAYz4iOc @ 00:02:44].

The economics checks are blunt. Emanuel Rose warns that tiny contracts carry no margin while clients still expect $50,000-a-month results, because a project costs the same to spool up at any size [M-Z0SPYJ9-w @ 00:03:20]. Prairie Founders Club draws the line at $10,000 lifetime value: B2B above it is sales-led, B2C below it is marketing-led, and LTV is total revenue divided by total customers using at least 3 years of data [VJjRWz_6Hq0 @ 00:02:08]. In federal teaming, Neil McDonnell says one way or another you have to talk about rates early, flags a partner charging $200 against your $100 as the conflict to raise before signing the teaming agreement, warns about large primes demanding 49% of workshare, and runs a 1-to-5 requirements gap analysis per subtask where you prime only at an average of 4 and a score of 5 needs 3 past performances [d_oQ5UhlC0k @ 00:20:54]. Hunter's team caps the outbound engine too: a 15-per-day floor, a ceiling around 200 to 250 against a roughly 500 provider limit, a recommendation of 50 or fewer per day, and a reminder that progressive sending is not warm-up [35H3HVb8ymw @ 00:51:28]. They also say to wait a couple of weeks after an HR hire signal before reaching out.
