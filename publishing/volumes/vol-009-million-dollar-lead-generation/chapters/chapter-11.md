## 1. Assign five accountable seats before expanding to seven  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Organize Candidate #21 around one enterprise demand-generation pod, with the owner occupying the first full-time seat rather than appearing as an invisible sixth person. That seat owns selling, client qualification, commercial terms, renewal conversations, and escalation decisions. The second full-time seat owns account strategy, campaign delivery, CRM definitions, and acceptance evidence. Three bounded contractor scopes provide account research, campaign production, and CRM automation. Keep the complete register of 30 mapped plays, but assign every play a primary seat, backup reviewer, input specification, deliverable, acceptance test, and weekly capacity charge. The chapter does not rename or fabricate the underlying play catalog; its architecture applies ownership to all thirty existing entries. Use Salesforce campaign identifiers or HubSpot properties to connect each active play with its client, account cohort, and statement of work. Start with a deliberately narrow active portfolio while retaining ownership for inactive plays. Count preparation, review, rework, and client meetings against capacity. A researcher delivering five hundred unreviewed contacts has not created five hundred usable opportunities; the pod has merely accumulated inventory that its two employees must still validate before client delivery.

Use measurable workload rather than revenue excitement to authorize the seven-person configuration. Track delivery hours committed for the next four weeks, reviewer queue age, accepted opportunity volume, and gross contribution by client. As an internal planning rule, keep scheduled employee delivery work below eighty percent of available hours so renewal calls, exceptions, and quality correction have real capacity. Add the third full-time seat as client success and quality lead when the existing lead repeatedly exceeds that threshold and contracted, collectible work funds the incremental loaded cost. Add the fourth contractor for signal operations and scheduling, not as another unowned source of outbound volume. Separate event detection from qualification: a Bombora topic surge, job posting, or product launch can justify research but cannot establish budget or purchasing authority. Preserve the original delivery lead as the accountable systems owner. Require a named backup for acceptance review and billing evidence before adding accounts. Run a two-week absence exercise in which the owner does not attend routine delivery calls; record every decision that still waits for them, then convert those dependencies into documented authority limits.

Buy contractor outcomes through written statements of work that define the batch, evidence, revision allowance, access rights, and payment trigger. Illustrative buying terms might pay research at $45 per accepted account brief, production at $650 per approved campaign package, and CRM engineering at $85 per hour under a twenty-hour monthly ceiling. These are purchasing examples, not additional scorecard assumptions. A brief must include entity matching, dated source links, role rationale, and suppression checks; a package must include approved copy, routing tests, and version history. Review submissions within two business days, return a specific defect list, and require correction within two further business days when the defect falls inside scope. Pay undisputed accepted work on the contracted schedule. Do not make contractor payment depend on whether an enterprise prospect eventually signs a million-dollar agreement. Classification follows the actual relationship, including applicable federal and state tests, rather than the contractor label. Preserve genuine control over methods where appropriate, avoid employee-like scheduling requirements, and budget employee treatment when the facts require it. Written copyright assignments, confidentiality terms, and documented subcontracting permissions must cover every reusable deliverable.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the reviewer bottleneck before buying more volume**
> 
> Enterprise origination usually fails at qualification, evidence, or handoff before it fails at raw contact supply.
> - Attach the original dated trigger to every account brief; a refreshed intent dashboard must not overwrite the evidence supporting an earlier fee.
> - Price research rework by defect category, separating missing evidence from client-requested changes after the brief was accepted.
> - Maintain a second trained reviewer for the ten accounts closest to fee acceptance; a single absent employee should not stop billing.
> - Track owner rescue hours by mapped play, then rewrite the three most rescue-intensive handoffs before adding contractor production capacity.

## 2. Run one weekly scorecard from source evidence to accepted fees  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a forty-five-minute weekly operating review from a frozen CRM extract, not from contractor slide decks. Give every measure an owner, denominator, cohort date, source field, and corrective action. Account-brief acceptance equals accepted briefs divided by reviewed briefs; meeting attendance equals attended meetings divided by meetings scheduled to occur during the period. Qualified-opportunity acceptance equals client-accepted opportunities divided by submitted opportunities whose contractual review period has expired. Report pending submissions separately so delayed buyer responses do not artificially improve the ratio. Measure acceptance turnaround, first-pass defects, duplicate accounts, disputed fees, and aged receivables alongside production. Establish initial internal targets of at least ninety-five percent first-pass brief acceptance and ninety percent on-time evidence review, then recalibrate using actual cohort performance. These are operating targets rather than market benchmarks. Separate activity metrics from commercial outcomes: email opens are unreliable instrumentation, meeting bookings can conceal no-shows, and pipeline value belongs to the client. A million-dollar prospect contract remains a prospect contract even when the pod originated the introduction. The operator earns only the retainer and opportunity fees supported by its own agreement.

Resolve attribution before launch by defining eligible accounts, excluded existing opportunities, attribution windows, duplicate treatment, required buyer roles, and the client's acceptance authority. In Salesforce, preserve campaign membership, original source, opportunity creation time, submission time, and acceptance time as distinct facts. A correction should create an auditable history rather than silently replace the original field. Store the supporting email or portal decision with controlled access and a record identifier. Calendly attendance data can corroborate a meeting, but scheduling alone cannot prove enterprise qualification. Require a human reviewer to verify fit, need, buyer involvement, and the agreed next step before submission. Inspect all billable opportunities and a risk-weighted sample of nonbillable research outputs. If acceptance drops, split failures into wrong account, weak buyer role, missing evidence, duplicate opportunity, or client definition change. Assign corrections to the responsible workflow rather than increasing outbound volume. Compensate contractors for accepted deliverables within their control; a modest quality bonus can reward sustained low defects without paying for inflated pipeline estimates. Preserve a separate dispute register with amount, reason, responsible reviewer, and resolution deadline.

Treat outreach permissions and suppression as measurable delivery requirements, not a separate appendix that production ignores. For commercial email, 15 U.S.C. section 7704 governs matters including truthful transmission information, required identification, postal address, and opt-out handling. Process opt-outs internally within one business day, comfortably inside the federal ten-business-day deadline, and propagate them across sequencers and subcontractor lists. Calls and texts require channel-specific review under 47 CFR section 64.1200, including applicable consent and do-not-call requirements; a business contact label does not automatically resolve a mobile-number restriction. Where the California Consumer Privacy Act applies, map collection notices, purposes, retention, and service-provider terms to the actual processing relationship. Keep a permitted-use record for every data source and prohibit contractors from exporting client lists into personal prospecting accounts. The weekly review should surface suppression failures, unauthorized exports, and incomplete permission evidence as immediate stop-work conditions for affected records. Pause the relevant channel or cohort while preserving unaffected delivery. Sector-specific referral rules also matter: do not transplant a technology prospecting fee schedule into healthcare, securities, or federal contracting without evaluating the applicable compensation restrictions.

**Weekly Enterprise Origination Scorecard and Corrective Actions**

| Metric and accountable operating owner | Calculation and retained source evidence | Initial operating threshold or review trigger | Required correction when performance deteriorates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Meeting attendance rate owned by scheduling specialist | Attended meetings divided by meetings scheduled for that reporting period | Investigate material deterioration against the preceding eight comparable weeks | Audit confirmations, attendee roles, calendar friction, and source quality |
| Opportunity acceptance rate owned by client success reviewer | Accepted opportunities divided by submissions past contractual review deadline | Report overdue pending submissions separately from accepted and rejected outcomes | Escalate unresolved definitions and suspend disputed opportunity fee billing |
| Evidence review timeliness owned by designated backup reviewer | Packages reviewed within two business days divided by packages due | Target ninety percent timeliness while every billed package receives review | Reassign reviewer capacity before purchasing additional research production batches |
| Disputed fee exposure owned by commercial pod leader | Open disputed invoice amounts divided by total outstanding operator receivables | Review every dispute weekly with reason and accountable resolution date | Correct source evidence or issue documented credits under contract |
| Suppression execution quality owned by CRM systems engineer | Timestamped requests reconciled against all connected outbound sending systems | Internal one-business-day processing target with immediate affected-record suspension | Repair propagation, confirm removal, and investigate affected message history |
| Delivery contribution and capacity owned by delivery lead | Earned operator fees less attributable delivery costs and allocated labor | Investigate negative client contribution and sustained eighty-percent scheduling pressure | Reprice scope, reduce rework, or defer unprofitable campaign expansion |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly evidence set
   - Export the CRM cohort and accounting references at the same weekly cutoff.
   - Record extract time, reporting period, source identifiers, and unresolved acceptance decisions before discussing performance.
2. Reconcile deliverables to earned fees
   - Match accepted outputs to contract terms, invoices, and the dispute register.
   - Separate pending qualification, client pipeline value, deferred retainers, and earned operator fees into different fields.
3. Assign corrective work within existing capacity
   - Give each exception one accountable seat and a dated correction.
   - Reduce new production when review queues exceed capacity; never resolve missing evidence by changing acceptance definitions retrospectively.
4. Close the loop with client and contractor records
   - Publish the frozen scorecard and track corrections through the following review.
   - Send each counterparty only their authorized records, confirm disputed items, and preserve prior scorecard versions for diligence.

## 3. Reconcile canonical profits to accrual cohorts and concentration limits  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep the three canonical cases intact: Conservative revenue is $150,000 with $30,000 owner net profit at twenty percent; Base revenue is $675,000 with $202,500 profit at thirty percent; Scaled revenue is $1,200,000 with $480,000 profit at forty percent. Their implied total annual costs are $120,000, $472,500, and $720,000 respectively. Monthly revenue averages are $12,500, $56,250, and $100,000; monthly profit averages are $2,500, $16,875, and $40,000. Retainers plus earned qualified-opportunity fees must reconcile to those revenue totals without adding client contract values. The stated $4,000–$20,000 monthly opex range cannot be treated as the complete expense ceiling for every scenario: Base and Scaled imply average total monthly costs of $39,375 and $60,000. Build a cost bridge separating payroll, contractor purchases, data, software, selling expense, and overhead. The Conservative five-person configuration presents a genuine labor-budget constraint. Its $120,000 total cost envelope does not demonstrate that two market-rate full-time employees and three contractors are funded. Preserve the scorecard while requiring an explicit compensation and utilization plan; do not hide unpaid owner labor or outside subsidies inside apparent operating efficiency.

Close the books monthly using an accrual policy aligned with the actual promises in each contract. A retainer collected before delivery is initially deferred to the extent services remain unperformed; recognize it as the contracted service obligation is satisfied rather than assuming every retainer is automatically earned on receipt. Record opportunity fees when the agreed earning conditions and applicable revenue-recognition requirements are met. Assess variable consideration and reversals where acceptance or clawback terms create uncertainty. Accrue contractor costs for accepted work even when the invoice arrives later. Use QuickBooks Online or Xero tracking dimensions for client, service month, and acquisition cohort, with the CRM opportunity identifier retained as supporting detail. Reconcile beginning receivables plus invoices less cash and credits to ending receivables, separately reconciling earned unbilled revenue and deferred balances. Report client contribution after attributable delivery costs, then show shared labor and overhead below that line. Never label contribution as owner net profit. Review cohort retention using starting recurring fees, expansion, contraction, and churn, excluding new clients from existing-client retention calculations. Long enterprise sales cycles make signed backlog, recognized revenue, invoiced amounts, and collected cash four different operational facts.

Set a maximum twenty-five percent exposure to any customer group and any ultimate paying counterparty, measured separately on trailing-twelve-month earned fees, current receivables, and forward contracted fees. Aggregate affiliates under common control and identify the principal behind agencies or channel partners. Four equal clients technically meet the ceiling but leave no tolerance for one cancellation or uneven fee timing; six to eight balanced relationships provide more practical headroom. A new business with one or two clients will breach the policy, so label that condition explicitly, maintain a dated diversification plan, and do not describe the book as diversified or exit-ready. Freeze discretionary expansion of an over-limit relationship while pursuing other accounts; perform existing obligations rather than manufacturing diversification by relabeling invoices. Apply the same twenty-five percent ceiling to intermediary-controlled fee receipts so multiple end clients cannot disguise dependence on one reseller. Track origination-channel dependence separately and build replacement paths for critical vendors. Stress-test the loss of the largest customer using avoidable costs, notice periods, receivable timing, and retained payroll. Maintain a liquidity plan that covers the actual stressed cash deficit; a profitable accrual statement alone cannot fund a delayed enterprise payment.

## 4. Sell transferable earnings or retain a resilient cash business  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build valuation from verified maintainable earnings, not from client pipeline or a headline revenue multiple. Small owner-operated service firms may be discussed on seller's discretionary earnings, while businesses with independent management may be evaluated on adjusted EBITDA. Use two to three times verified SDE and three to five times transferable EBITDA as illustrative negotiating ranges, not observed transaction comparables for this candidate. Actual pricing depends on size, recurrence, growth quality, concentration, and deal structure. Canonical owner net profit is neither automatically SDE nor automatically EBITDA. Prepare a bridge covering owner compensation already expensed, replacement management, interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, and supportable nonrecurring items. If diligence establishes Base SDE of exactly $202,500, the illustrative two-to-three-times range produces $405,000–$607,500 of enterprise value. That conditional example does not prove the bridge. For Scaled, if the $480,000 owner profit needs $150,000 of incremental replacement management and all other EBITDA adjustments net to zero, transferable EBITDA is $330,000; three to five times yields $990,000–$1,650,000. Subtract debt, account for agreed cash and working-capital adjustments, and distinguish cash at closing from contingent consideration before estimating seller proceeds.

Make the data room an operating byproduct rather than a last-minute reconstruction. Maintain signed client contracts, amendments, renewal dates, termination rights, assignment provisions, acceptance logs, monthly cohort statements, and the complete thirty-play ownership register. Retain signed contractor copyright assignments satisfying 17 U.S.C. section 204(a) where a transfer is intended; payment alone does not transfer copyright, and commissioned work does not automatically qualify as work made for hire. Identify preexisting templates, licensed datasets, software subscriptions, and third-party assets separately so the seller cannot promise rights they do not own. Check whether data and vendor licenses survive a transaction and whether client assignment requires consent. Use company-controlled password management, named accounts, least-privilege access, and a documented offboarding checklist. Share diligence records through staged, access-controlled folders, redacting personal information until disclosure is necessary and permitted. Demonstrate transferability with backup-led client meetings, repeatable acceptance decisions, and a tested recovery procedure for CRM automations. A buyer should be able to trace a sampled fee from agreement to source evidence, acceptance, ledger, and cash without interviewing the founder to reconstruct what happened.

Negotiate quality of proceeds as carefully as the headline multiple. A large earnout tied to the buyer's future sales execution can transfer enterprise sales-cycle risk back to the seller. Define any contingent payment using auditable retained fees or specified accepted opportunities, with clear accounting policies, information rights, allocation rules, and dispute procedures. Prevent related-party charges or reassigned accounts from silently changing the measurement base. Model seller transition hours and any post-close employment separately from purchase price. For an owner who retains the business, the same discipline supports cash distributions: fund tax obligations, working capital, replacement labor, and downside liquidity before distributing the remaining cash. Compare a risk-adjusted hold case against net closing proceeds plus realistically collectible deferred consideration, rather than comparing annual profit with an unadjusted headline offer. Review the comparison quarterly without forcing a sale timetable. The thirty mapped plays become valuable intellectual property only when another authorized operator can execute them using documented inputs, budgets, acceptance standards, and exception rules. Premium outcomes come from reduced dependence and clean evidence; adding more contractors, campaigns, or theoretical pipeline does not substitute for transferable customer relationships and durable earned fees.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before scaling or presenting an exit-ready book**
> 
> Treat missing evidence as unfinished operating work. Expansion and buyer outreach should reflect the condition of the actual business.
> - Verify all thirty mapped plays have accountable owners, documented acceptance tests, and backup coverage inside the five- or seven-person configuration.
> - Reconcile the last twelve monthly closes to earned fees, contractor accruals, bank receipts, and the canonical scenario bridge without including client contract values.
> - Verify customer-group and ultimate-payer exposure is at or below twenty-five percent across earned fees, receivables, and forward contracted fees.
> - Confirm signed IP assignments, transferable contract rights, permitted data use, and a buyer-testable owner replacement cost before applying an earnings multiple.
