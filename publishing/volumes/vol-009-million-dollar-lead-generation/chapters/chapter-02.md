# Chapter 02: Build the Account-Based Marketing Operating System

*ABM versus demand generation, high-value account targeting, sales–marketing alignment, LinkedIn, ABM advertising, Claude-assisted outreach, and enterprise pipeline workflows*

## Executive Summary

Practitioners converge on one rule: ABM is a joint sales-and-marketing operating system aimed at a short, tiered account list, not a lead-volume campaign. TK Kader, AdConversion and Fullfunnel io all gate entry on deal size, with floors cited at roughly $20,000 ACV, CFO sign-off, or 30K ACV, and flag a thousand-account list or a $1M-in-30-days expectation as red flags. The street mechanics run ICP, messaging, a Dream 100, personalization, then sales outbound under paid and organic air cover, tracked in weekly account-level meetings. Reported numbers include terminus's 124% demo-to-close lift, Snowflake's 2-3% account-ad click-through against 0.09% display, and a 994-day enterprise journey. Practitioners split on automation: GrowthSpree pushes Claude-driven outreach to 100 contacts, while Fullfunnel io warns that volume beyond capacity kills the program.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every serious operator gates ABM on deal economics before touching tactics. TK Kader lays out the fit test: a larger ACV, multiple decision makers and long sales cycles, and flatly says that for a self-service SaaS business where a user signs up and swipes a credit card, ABM 'might be overkill' [rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:04:01]. AdConversion's Brandon Alysa Glue puts a number on it, roughly $20,000 ACV or more, or any contract that requires a CFO sign-off, and adds that if accounts are too small for title filters to work, they probably are not ABM-qualified at all [FPPnVM3_KPk @ 00:18:13]. Fullfunnel io sets its red-flag line higher for enterprise pipeline, listing ACV below 30K, an MQL-driven lead-gen focus, siloed teams, a 1,000-account list and an expectation of $1M in 30 days as red flags [glV72-awJAU @ 01:17:37]. Senator We Run Ads closes the low end entirely: ABM is not built for $2 to $10 per month tickets, and when it does fit, it deserves at least 25% of the media mix [Jx0muHw5NUY @ 00:13:30]. The shared logic is blunt: TK Kader calls ABM 'probably a little bit more expensive' than other motions, so it only pays where deal size justifies concentrated spend.

The second point of agreement is that ABM dies without sales and marketing operating as one unit, with leadership forcing it from the top. Craig Rosenberg of TOPO calls ABM 'a business decision' made top-down, with the VP of Sales and the CEO saying 'get us in... we got it,' and Sangram of terminus says pure marketing-only or sales-only efforts fail, pushing a single shared scorecard instead of 15 to 20 minutes of arguing over whose numbers are right [Co22pd-mVlk @ 00:39:16]. Sarah Challis is equally direct: if the sales team or senior management is not on board, 'it can be really hard to pull this off,' and marketing cannot run the strategy alone [nZS7xjR3bD0 @ 00:07:19]. Marketing Business Network describes the mechanics as an inverted funnel, where sales and marketing first agree on accounts by revenue potential and strategic fit [MNUNce5QMnc @ 00:00:44]. Fullfunnel io's diagnosis of failing programs lands in the same place, naming the absence of shared systematic planning with sales, where the only alignment is a list of territory 'biggest logos', as the first of five failure causes [glV72-awJAU @ 00:06:45]. Every operator here treats that alignment as the entry ticket, not a later optimization.

Practitioners also agree that the account, and the buying group inside it, replaces the lead as the unit of work, while demand generation stays switched on. Sangram puts it as 'we don't close leads we close accounts,' and Craig Rosenberg tells teams not to turn demand gen off ('the Titanic'), instead pivoting field events so 60% or more of the seats come from target accounts; that client grew pipeline 30% on 20% fewer leads [Co22pd-mVlk @ 01:03:22]. Rosenberg's replacement scorecard metric is TAP, target account pipeline. In Love With Marketing frames ABM as demand gen pointed at specific target accounts only, with the two sharing workforce, vendors, budgets and martech [CNTwxh9-1AI @ 00:02:38]. ATAK Interactive's Hannah Keeling uses the net-versus-spear image, says ABM breaks with 'a million accounts,' and argues the two run in tandem, with the only question being which lever to pull harder [FCu5IsBzx4U @ 00:03:46]. TK Kader warns against confusing ICP with TAM, citing a $12B TAM where the real ICP is a narrow slice, and says thousands of Dream 100 candidates mean the ICP needs refining [rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:05:13]. The consensus target list stays short, strategic, validated against closed deals, and owned jointly by sales and marketing.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

TK Kader's five-step build runs in strict order. First comes an ICP fleshed out across 29 points of firmographic, demographic and technographic data; then messaging that spans email subject lines, the first sentence of a cold call, ad copy and homepage hero text; then a Dream 100 built in a spreadsheet or CRM with key contacts; then real personalization, which at Tower meant researching public quarterly filings rather than 'insert school' tokens [rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:05:00]. Execution pairs sales outbound by email, DM and calls with marketing air cover from ads, organic social, SEM/SEO, intent tools and events, sized at 100 accounts times 5 people, or 500 contacts [rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:11:24]. Skip steps one through four and hand an agency a 'napkin' ICP, Kader warns, and you burn your brand. Prospeo's Cole tiers the list in Sales Navigator by headcount, 1,000+, 200 to 1,000 and 1 to 200, saves each as a named account list, and runs lead search for titles like 'head of marketing,' remembering Sales Navigator shows only 25 accounts per page [OqdXhRy5QNY @ 00:02:08]. Senator We Run Ads expands lists with Leadfeeder website-visitor identification or a one-time manual Google search, and segments 2,000-account lists by size or revenue [Jx0muHw5NUY @ 00:03:58].

Fullfunnel io turns that sequence into an operating system. Accounts split into three sublists: a Cluster ICP of cold accounts getting awareness, a Future Pipeline that sales touches only after an agreed engagement threshold, taking about 20% of sales time, and an Active Focus list on fully personalized playbooks with weekly touchpoints [glV72-awJAU @ 00:38:59]. Each playbook ships with an outline, success criteria agreed with sales such as 20 accounts and 25 buyers, time required, templates, AI prompts and a plan B, while reps run a one-hour daily slot against a checklist and the whole team holds weekly meetings plus 5 to 10 minute daily stand-ups early on; the full stack runs under $1,000 a month with Goldcast, FullEnrich connected to Claude, and Apify scraping LinkedIn engagement weekly [glV72-awJAU @ 01:14:21]. Snowflake's Lars Kristensen scores engagement in Engagio at 2 points per site visit, plus 2 for a high-value page, 10 for a white paper, 15 for a webcast and 30 for a physical event, then seeds new AE territories with the most-engaged accounts [Co22pd-mVlk @ 01:35:55]. Offers escalate by stage, from a dummies guide for unaware accounts to a live demo for engaged ones and a free trial for highly engaged ones.

Paid and outbound mechanics come with hard settings. Prospeo's LinkedIn ad build uses a lead-gen objective with an uploaded company list, which needs at least 300 valid contacts and 24 to 48 hours of processing before targeting goes live, narrowed by job title and run as warm-up, cover and wake-up ads; outbound starts with email in LGM because of LinkedIn's weekly connection limits [OqdXhRy5QNY @ 00:08:12]. Aqil Jannaty budgets 5,000 to 10,000 a month for LinkedIn awareness ads despite high CPMs, calls ZoomInfo very expensive, and favors a B2B podcast play where the prospect is invited on as a guest, warmed up on an intro call, and the content is repurposed [sYgF5NyLeo8 @ 00:07:18]. AdConversion reports that at Thought Industry a Google Ads question keyword, 'LXP vs LMS,' cost pennies on the dollar against roughly $75-a-click target keywords, with the next-closest campaign's cost per lead 123% higher [FPPnVM3_KPk @ 00:25:10]. On the AI side, GrowthSpree runs Claude co-work on the Pro plan or above plus the Chrome extension to pull ICP-matched people from a LinkedIn company page, research posts and company news, write a personalized drip for 100 contacts, and send the connection requests [gq_DLeTobXo @ 00:03:39].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Qualify Fit and Plan Jointly
   - Confirm ACV and buying-committee fit, then lock sales and marketing into a one-to-three-day planning session.
   - Run closed-won analysis on the highest-ACV and fastest deals, use lost deals for disqualification criteria, and match account count to team capacity [Fullfunnel io — glV72-awJAU @ 00:24:14].
2. Build and Tier the Account List
   - Split 'could buy' from 'likely to buy now' and concentrate budget on the smaller list.
   - Ask your best reps who belongs on it or mine the CRM; targeting 10 to 20 companies is fine when 5 to 10 accounts would otherwise eat the budget [AdConversion — LbEgZ7SpArs @ 00:01:00].
3. Map Personas and Messaging Pillars
   - Strip non-buyers from targeting and build messaging pillars for decision makers and influencers.
   - Exclude accounting, admin and HR, separate decision makers (CEO, procurement) from influencers, and build pillars on testimonials, USPs and pricing, and ease of migration and support [Senator We Run Ads — Jx0muHw5NUY @ 00:05:50].
4. Launch With Air Cover and a Monday Metrics Loop
   - Run sales outbound under ads, social and events, then review results weekly as one team.
   - Hold a Monday sales and marketing meeting tracking campaign to opportunity to customer, diagnose ICP, then messaging, then channel, and double down on the one channel that crushes it [TK Kader — rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:15:10].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first fight is over who owns the target account list. Sangram of Terminus tells marketers to be brave and build the list themselves instead of waiting for sales, because companies hire Account Executives, not lead executives [Co22pd-mVlk @ 00:15:41]. Aqil Jannaty reverses that split: sales builds the list, marketing builds the channels, and the starting unit is about 100 dream companies with mapped buying committees, ranked line by line with AI [sYgF5NyLeo8 @ 00:01:51]. Size is the second fault line. Lars Kristensen at Snowflake runs 3,000 target accounts and 200 one-to-one accounts with only two ABM staff. That list came from pushing 25,000 accounts through ICP mapping and letting each of about 50 reps pick roughly 60 [Co22pd-mVlk @ 01:32:09]. Fullfunnel io treats 1,000 accounts as a red flag on its own, alongside ACV below 30K, a lead-gen MQL focus, siloed teams, and an expectation of $1M in 30 days [glV72-awJAU @ 01:17:37]. Fullfunnel's own planning checklist ties account count to team capacity. Snowflake's 3,000 also sits on top of a far smaller one-to-one core of 200. Match the count to the people actually running the plays, then expand once the core is producing.

Automation splits the room hardest. GrowthSpree runs the entire outreach motion through Claude co-work and the Claude Chrome extension with no other automation tools. It builds the persona list from LinkedIn company pages, researches posts, comments and company news, and writes a personalized LinkedIn drip. It then scales from one prospect to all 100 contacts, with separate primary and secondary ICP sequences [gq_DLeTobXo @ 00:03:18]. Fullfunnel io pushes the other way. It warns that volume beyond team capacity will kill the program and that accounts which have never engaged get no sales pitch, because if they don't know us, probably they're not going to buy from us. Its Active Focus sublist gets fully personalized playbooks and weekly touchpoints, while cold Cluster ICP accounts get awareness only [glV72-awJAU @ 00:39:16]. Personalization depth is the second split. TK Kader says inserting a school name is not personalization; at Tower the team researched public quarterly filings before touching an account [rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:09:47]. Aqil Jannaty argues that overpersonalization is killing ABM. The better move, in that view, is finding the watering holes where each persona actually spends time, and Aqil notes that security buyers are closed to cold email entirely [sYgF5NyLeo8 @ 00:05:23].

On demand generation, nobody fully agrees. Aqil Jannaty dismisses it as spray and pray [sYgF5NyLeo8 @ 00:00:54]. Craig Rosenberg of TOPO gives the opposite tip number one: don't turn it off, because it is the Titanic. Rosenberg instead pivots field events so 60%+ of the seats come from target accounts, a move that grew pipeline 30% on 20% fewer leads [Co22pd-mVlk @ 01:03:22]. Hannah Keeling at ATAK Interactive refuses to pit the two against each other at all. In that framing demand gen is the net and ABM is the spear, and companies start with demand gen because it is quicker, then shift to ABM as they find their niche [FCu5IsBzx4U @ 00:04:30]. Paid media and tooling draw the same split. Aqil budgets 5 to 10,000 a month for LinkedIn ads, and Senator We Run Ads wants at least 25% of the media mix in ABM. Fullfunnel io, by contrast, calls ads a quantity distribution channel and runs its full stack for under $1,000 a month [glV72-awJAU @ 01:32:34]. Terminus runs 20+ tools and Snowflake about 25, with Snowflake piping everything into its own data warehouse. Craig Rosenberg's rule holds either way: strategy first, tech stack second.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The cheapest failure to avoid is running ABM on a business that cannot carry it. TK Kader calls ABM probably a little bit more expensive than other motions. A self-service SaaS where users sign up and swipe a credit card might find it overkill, because fit needs a larger ACV, multiple decision makers and long sales cycles [rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:04:01]. Brandon Alysa Glue on AdConversion puts the floor at about a $20,000 ACV, or any contract that requires a CFO sign-off. Glue adds that accounts too small for title filters are likely not ABM-qualified [FPPnVM3_KPk @ 00:18:13]. Fullfunnel io sets its red line higher and flags ACV below 30K as a reason to walk away [glV72-awJAU @ 01:17:37]. Senator We Run Ads rules out $2 to $10 a month tickets entirely [Jx0muHw5NUY @ 00:13:30]. Hannah Keeling adds the account-count version of the same check: ABM fails with a million accounts, and if you cannot identify targets or align sales and marketing, you are a demand-gen client. Sarah Challis supplies the budget sanity check for teams that clear the bar. Spend roughly 5% of deal value, which is £5,000 against a 100K deal, and do not get crazy ambitious relative to what the company is doing now.

The second trap is a clock that does not match the sales cycle. A Fullfunnel io client demanded $3M from a pilot within three months on a 15-month cycle, while Fullfunnel's own tracking shows 994 days from the first touchpoint to engagement [glV72-awJAU @ 00:24:14]. Their webinar poll found nobody rating ABM great, about 60% so-so and 40% frustrated. Short-term conversion playbooks and missing leading or lagging indicators were among the five causes they named. Russell Prue at AdConversion watched a client burn most of the budget on 5 to 10 accounts. Meanwhile, the list sales actually cared about for the next 3 to 6 months got only 20 to 50 impressions in 90 days [LbEgZ7SpArs @ 00:01:00]. Prue's diagnostic is blunt. No impressions means fixing distribution, no engagement means fixing content, and sales saying not sure at two months is a red flag [LbEgZ7SpArs @ 00:08:08]. Rob on the LinkedIn panel warns against buying programmatic before the list, database and sales orchestration are built, since that invites ROI questions that tarnish the whole initiative [Co22pd-mVlk @ 02:17:30]. Fullfunnel also refuses to chase early budget increases, because asking puts the gun in front of your head.

The ugliest failures happen at the moment of contact. TK Kader warns that skipping the ICP, messaging, Dream 100 and personalization steps and handing a napkin ICP to an agency or fractional CMO will burn your brand. A weak first impression earns the reply: we went with the other guy [rH2rc9-LY00 @ 00:13:24]. The LinkedIn panel catalogued what this looks like on the receiving end. Rob was told we sell to e-commerce companies like you despite running a B2B tech company. Matt received cupcakes with no sender name and a pizza box holding only a glossy sheet. Tai got a marshmallow fluff jar captioned last fluff you'll ever get from us [Co22pd-mVlk @ 01:52:08]. Matt's rule is that gifts sent to the wrong person just look like regular marketing with direct mail. The moderator's brother was hooked by a free-trial offer, but the telesales rep did not know about the trial and pushed an amazing deal today. The brother never bought, and the story spread among Seattle entrepreneurs, because you can't isolate those bad experiences anymore [Co22pd-mVlk @ 01:54:54]. Sarah Challis names the internal version: if sales or senior management are not on board, it is really hard to pull off [nZS7xjR3bD0 @ 00:07:19].
