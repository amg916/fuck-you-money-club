## 1. Find the budget owner, then price the loss they can measure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The enterprise check normally comes from a vendor selling expensive software, infrastructure, professional services, or regulated operating capacity, not from the prospect receiving outreach. A chief revenue officer sponsors pipeline creation; a demand-generation director administers the program; procurement negotiates the purchase order; accounts payable releases cash. Treat those as separate authorities even when one enthusiastic executive introduces everyone. Ask which legal entity signs, which cost center funds the work, who can accept an opportunity, and who can change bank instructions. A champion's Salesforce access establishes none of those powers. Their acute pain is a costly sales team waiting for credible buying conversations while an annual bookings target approaches. Illustratively, four enterprise sellers costing $250,000 each consume $1 million annually before implementation staff or media spend. A $90,000 annual origination retainer is therefore evaluated against productive seller capacity and expected contribution, not against the price of scraped email addresses. Build the buying case around an agreed account universe, reachable buying roles, a verified business problem, and evidence of a next step. Never present the prospect's million-dollar purchase as agency revenue or imply that a scheduled calendar event has already created collectible pipeline.

Decision-maker psychology changes with the budget source. A revenue leader wants defensible pipeline attribution before the next board meeting; a marketing leader wants acceptance from sales; a controller wants invoices that match a purchase order without manual exceptions. Show each person their own evidence rather than one inflated dashboard. Define a qualified opportunity as an eligible account, an identified buying participant, a documented need, an agreed commercial scope, and a completed discovery event with a recorded next action. Set any timing or budget threshold expressly instead of assuming every enterprise buyer reveals procurement plans during the first call. For an illustrative $1 million client contract at 40% contribution and a 10% close probability, expected contribution is $40,000 before selling costs, timing, and correlated losses. A $2,500 opportunity fee can fit that model, but the probability must come from the client's comparable cohort, not the operator's optimism. Use a limited paid pilot to establish acceptance rates. A regulator's deadline can intensify demand for qualified professional help, but it does not compel anyone to hire this agency. Sell timely, accurate routing and documented work, never privileged access, official affiliation, or an invented statutory obligation.

Screen collectability before launching expensive account-based marketing. Obtain the contracting entity's registration, tax form, billing address, purchase-order requirements, and authorized signer's name through an approved onboarding channel. Review available financial statements, payment references, commercial credit reports, and relevant public insolvency records; a recognizable brand can still contract through a thin subsidiary. Dun & Bradstreet and Experian Business reports are inputs, not substitutes for entity verification. For a young buyer, shorten billing intervals and collect the first service period before reserving delivery capacity. If the brand requires its subsidiary to sign, consider a negotiated parent undertaking rather than silently treating group revenue as subsidiary credit. Confirm that a purchase-order ceiling covers both retainers and variable fees. Map who may authorize escrow release separately from who approves an invoice; procurement cannot release funds it does not control. Start with a written credit limit and a suspension trigger tied to overdue undisputed balances. A $7,500 monthly retainer plus eight $2,500 opportunities creates $27,500 of exposure for that service month. Waiting two unpaid months can finance $55,000 of client activity before collection costs. Restrict additional delivery when that exposure exceeds the amount the business can absorb.

## 2. Choose the settlement arrangement without confusing custody and revenue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The eight arrangements below are commercially comparable, but they are not eight equivalent banking networks. Wire and ACH move money; retainers and milestone holds allocate payment timing; escrow adds third-party custody; factoring finances a receivable; statutory disbursement follows a legally constrained waterfall; platform checkout packages a processor's services. Choose the arrangement after defining the invoice event. For ordinary enterprise work, invoice a monthly retainer in advance and accepted opportunities on a regular schedule, with separate line items and evidence references. An ACH credit initiated by the buyer avoids the operator pulling funds, while an ACH debit requires appropriate authorization and remains exposed to applicable returns and disputes. Verify bank details through a previously established contact using a different channel from the change request. A fraudulent email announcing new wiring instructions can erase the economics of many successful introductions. Use a dedicated business collection account and reconcile receipts to invoices rather than treating every deposit as earned revenue. Advance cash can remain deferred revenue until the associated services are delivered. No payment method cures an unauthorized referral fee or transforms client settlement proceeds into unrestricted agency operating cash.

Use escrow only when both parties need custody protection and can specify an objective release condition. Verify the provider's legal authority for the transaction and the account structure, fees, dispute process, and treatment of insolvency before sending funds. Neither an agency's ordinary bank account nor a processor balance becomes escrow because a contract uses that word. For an illustrative $20,000 pilot, the buyer might fund $10,000 for setup and $10,000 for an evidence-backed delivery milestone; release instructions must identify the authorized signers and what happens when only part of delivery is disputed. Keep undisputed amounts payable on schedule. Milestone holds serve a similar negotiating purpose without necessarily involving independent custody, so they leave greater buyer credit exposure. Platform checkout through Stripe or another approved provider can simplify smaller retainers, but underwriting limits, reserves, chargebacks, and prohibited-business rules still apply. An illustrative 3% processing charge on $7,500 consumes $225 before fixed fees. Quote the service price and permitted payment terms clearly rather than adding an unexpected surcharge. For larger invoices, compare the actual bank and processor quotes against administrative effort, settlement speed, recovery risk, and the buyer's treasury policy.

Factoring belongs after a clean receivable exists, not before the parties agree that a meeting qualifies. A factor purchasing a $25,000 accepted invoice at an illustrative 3% discount leaves $24,250 before other charges; an 85% advance would initially release $21,250, with the reserve reconciled later under the agreement. Recourse terms can return non-payment risk to the operator. Review assignment restrictions, setoff rights, debtor notices, and applicable UCC provisions before selling invoices. Statutory disbursement is different: a legal settlement may require trust-account handling, lien resolution, court authorization, or client authorization before any attorney fee becomes distributable. Healthcare reimbursement likewise follows payer contracts and program requirements rather than a marketing agency's preferred split. Never ask a settlement administrator or pharmacy payer to route restricted proceeds straight into the agency's general account merely to accelerate collection. Require the professional business to pay its own lawful service invoice through an appropriate operating account. For government-contract introductions, examine FAR 52.203-5 when applicable before proposing award-contingent compensation; its covenant and exceptions require factual analysis. A fixed, documented research engagement is a practical fallback when the proposed success-fee structure cannot be supported.

**Eight Payment and Settlement Arrangements for Enterprise Origination**

| Arrangement and actual economic function | Authority and release evidence | Cost or collection exposure | Practical operator design and fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wire transfer through the buyer's verified treasury bank | Authorized treasury instruction matching the correct beneficiary account | Bank charges vary; fraudulent beneficiary changes threaten recovery | Independently verify instructions and reconcile the bank reference |
| Independent escrow holding funds against defined delivery milestones | Executed escrow instructions naming authorized releasers and evidence | Provider charges and disputed releases can delay availability | Use objective releases and separately resolve contested deliverables |
| ACH credit or authorized debit for recurring invoices | Buyer initiation or valid debit authorization with invoice references | Returns and bank rules differ across transaction circumstances | Prefer documented mandates and monitor settlement exceptions daily |
| Prepaid retainer funding reserved enterprise account development capacity | Signed service scope defining coverage period and deliverables | Advance receipts can remain deferred until services occur | Invoice before allocation and recognize revenue as earned |
| Legally constrained disbursement following settlement or program requirements | Applicable trust, lien, court, client, or payer documentation | Restricted proceeds cannot freely fund unrelated marketing operations | Bill the authorized business outside restricted custodial balances |
| Platform checkout processing card or supported bank payments | Processor-approved business activity and documented customer payment authority | Fees, reserves, disputes, and chargebacks reduce available cash | Check underwriting fit before routing large enterprise invoices |
| Factoring accepted business receivables into earlier operating liquidity | Assignable invoice supported by acceptance and debtor verification | Discounts, reserves, recourse, and setoff affect net proceeds | Finance clean invoices rather than disputed opportunity claims |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish payer identity and spending authority
   - Match the signed agreement, purchase order, tax details, and beneficiary records.
   - Record the legal entity, authorized buyer, accounts-payable contact, credit ceiling, and independent bank-verification channel.
2. Define the earned-fee event
   - Attach the opportunity rubric, exclusion register, acceptance deadline, and dispute process.
   - Distinguish scheduled meetings, completed discoveries, accepted opportunities, and downstream contract awards rather than billing them interchangeably.
3. Collect and reconcile through the selected arrangement
   - Issue an evidence-linked invoice and match the settled receipt to its open balance.
   - Track processor deductions, ACH returns, escrow holds, deferred revenue, and any reserve separately from earned service revenue.
4. Resolve exceptions before expanding exposure
   - Escalate disputed evidence and suspend additional delivery at the agreed credit limit.
   - Keep undisputed balances payable, preserve the audit packet, and resume work only under documented revised terms or cleared funds.

## 3. Route the three regulated plays through lawful payer economics  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The play arizona_abs_mass_tort_cocounsel changes the potential business owner, not merely the invoice label. Arizona Supreme Court Rule 31.1(c) and ACJA section 7-209 govern the licensed alternative business structure route; Arizona removed its own ER 5.4 prohibition, not the ABA model rule nationwide. Nonlawyer ownership requires the actual ABS authorization, responsible professionals, and applicable safeguards. Institutional sexual assault survivor matters involving juvenile detention, rideshare litigation, clergy, boarding schools, or medical settings demand trauma-informed intake and attorney evaluation of defendants, limitations, lookback windows, venue, and corroborating primary documents. Catastrophic mass torts likewise require substantive case assessment rather than purchased signatures. An illustrative $500,000 recovery with a permitted 30% fee produces a $150,000 attorney-fee pool before expenses and any lawful co-counsel allocation; a 40% allocation would be $60,000, not $500,000 of operator revenue. The mapped $50,000 to $400,000-plus settled-case fees describe potential legal-business economics, not a standard marketing bounty. Attorneys must satisfy applicable fee-division rules and client requirements. Outside the authorized structure, use a properly designed MSO providing genuine services at supportable compensation without controlling legal judgment or disguising fee sharing. Blend suitable single-event injury matters with longer institutional dockets for cash planning, without promising settlement timing or subordinating clients' interests to liquidity.

The play patent_trademark_office_action_arbitrage uses a public procedural event to identify timely demand for licensed trademark counsel. Poll permitted USPTO TSDR resources and Trademark Daily XML feeds within published access limits, then verify the current prosecution record before contacting an apparently unrepresented applicant. A non-final office action can involve substantive refusal grounds, identification defects, specimen issues, or other requirements; the operator should not diagnose the legal response. Most covered trademark office actions have a three-month response period with an available paid extension, while exceptions, including Madrid applications, require separate deadline treatment under 37 CFR 2.62. Mail must plainly identify the private sender, avoid government-like invoices, and distinguish optional attorney services from official USPTO charges. Illustratively, 100 verified records at $4 for research and mailing cost $400. If eight applicants independently request introductions, acquisition cost is $50 per request before platform labor. A law firm might instead buy a $2,000 monthly directory subscription with defined software services; payment must satisfy applicable advertising and recommendation rules, including 37 CFR 11.702 where relevant. Do not convert response rates into guaranteed legal outcomes or collect a percentage of attorney fees. The fallback is a transparent directory or software platform, with attorney selection and legal work remaining with the applicant and licensed professional.

The play section_340b_contract_pharmacy_spread_arbitrage requires especially careful separation between demand generation and drug economics. Eligible covered entities include qualifying health centers, Ryan White grantees, and qualifying hospitals; eligibility is category-specific, not established by a rural address alone. Section 340B, 42 U.S.C. 256b, sets ceiling-price mechanics and prohibits diversion and duplicate discounts. A universal 25% to 50% discount and full billed commercial reimbursement are not entitlements. Model actual acquisition cost and allowed reimbursement: $12,000 collected less $8,000 drug cost, $500 dispensing and administration, and $300 other attributable charges leaves $3,200 before remaining entity overhead and adjustments. That residual belongs to the appropriate pharmacy and covered-entity economics, not automatically to the lead generator. An illustrative TPA contract paying $4,000 monthly for documented split-billing, reconciliation, and reporting yields $48,000 annual service revenue, not the prescription principal. Flat fees alone do not resolve the federal anti-kickback statute; assess fair market value, actual services, referral influence, and applicable safe-harbor conditions. Preserve EHR encounter eligibility checks and Medicaid duplicate-discount controls. An entity-owned specialty pharmacy can reduce dependence on contract-pharmacy access, subject to licensing and payer requirements. Automating 340B ESP submissions may address particular manufacturer policies, but cannot universally unlock restricted NDCs or replace current legal and operational review.

## 4. Protect earned fees and reconcile the three canonical outcomes  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Fee protection begins before the introduction, with a contract that defines what was sourced and how acceptance becomes observable. Record the legal account name, relevant domain, buying unit, introduction timestamp, participant role, and source evidence in Salesforce or HubSpot. Establish an existing-opportunity exclusion register and a process for parent-subsidiary overlaps. Agree whether the fee covers an account, a business unit, or a discrete buying project; otherwise one multinational can generate endless duplicate claims. Specify a reasonable review period, written rejection reasons, and a cure process for missing evidence. Any deemed-acceptance provision must be expressly negotiated rather than hidden in an invoice footer. A narrowly defined non-circumvention obligation can address deliberate routing around an earned fee, but should not claim perpetual ownership of every future customer transaction. Define introduced accounts, covered conduct, duration, exclusions, and proportionate remedies under the governing law. Preserve timestamped delivery packets and acceptance records outside editable CRM fields, while limiting personal data to what the service requires. For commercial email, implement the applicable CAN-SPAM requirements, including truthful headers, sender identification, postal address, and functioning opt-out handling. Attribution evidence is useful only when its collection and retention are themselves supportable.

Reconcile the canonical scorecard with a service ledger rather than a pipeline dashboard. Conservative revenue can be illustrated as three clients paying $3,000 monthly, producing $108,000, plus 42 accepted opportunities at $1,000, producing $42,000. Total revenue is $150,000; $120,000 of modeled cost leaves $30,000 owner net profit at 20%, with 2 FT + 3 C. Base revenue can be illustrated as five clients paying $7,500 monthly, producing $450,000, plus 90 opportunities at $2,500, producing $225,000. Total revenue is $675,000; $472,500 of modeled cost leaves $202,500 at 30.0%, again with 2 FT + 3 C. Scaled revenue can be illustrated as eight clients paying $10,000 monthly, producing $960,000, plus 80 opportunities at $3,000, producing $240,000. Total revenue is $1,200,000; $720,000 of modeled cost leaves $480,000 at 40%, with 3 FT + 4 C. These are illustrative revenue constructions matching the required totals, not additional scorecard assumptions. Their opportunity fees describe permissible enterprise engagements, not automatic pricing for legal or healthcare referrals. None of the client's contract value, settlement principal, or prescription reimbursement enters these agency revenue totals.

Cash planning must also expose the difference between baseline overhead and total scenario cost. The stated $4,000 to $20,000 monthly operating range cannot represent every expense in the Base and Scaled cases: their implied total monthly costs are $39,375 and $60,000 respectively, compared with $10,000 Conservative. Treat the range as an initial planning reference and build a complete cost schedule for payroll, contractors, data, media, travel, processors, losses, and administration. Contractor counts do not establish full-year utilization or compensation. Similarly, the $15,000 to $100,000 startup-capital range must be tested against a three-to-twelve-month first-deal cycle rather than treated as sufficient runway by itself. At $10,000 monthly cash burn, six months consumes $60,000 before unusual setup costs. Maintain a thirteen-week cash forecast showing invoice dates, expected clearance, disputed balances, and committed delivery spend. Stress customer concentration by removing the largest account's receipts and its avoidable costs separately. If one of five equal Base retainer clients stops paying, $7,500 monthly disappears before any associated opportunity fees. Reprice delivery, tighten credit, or replace the account; do not hide the loss by counting unsigned pipeline as earned revenue or factoring a disputed receivable.
