## 1. Mine Buying Events Before Buying Contact Data  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin with the distinction that prevents most sourcing waste: your customer is the enterprise vendor purchasing demand-generation services, while the target account is that vendor's potential buyer. A million-dollar downstream contract creates commercial relevance; it does not become your revenue. Choose one vendor category with expensive customer acquisition and observable buying events, such as industrial cybersecurity integrators serving manufacturers. Interview ten vendor sales leaders about installed-base exclusions, minimum project size, geographic coverage, and the actual reasons opportunities fail. Ask for anonymized examples of five accepted opportunities and five rejected meetings before proposing delivery volume. Build a two-sided account register: fifty prospective paying vendors and an initial two hundred downstream accounts that those vendors could legitimately serve. Record corporate domain, operating locations, relevant business unit, current supplier evidence, and the date each assertion was observed. An acquisition filing can suggest integration work, but it cannot establish budget approval. Treat every public signal as a research hypothesis until a buyer confirms the business consequence. The first offer should therefore sell a bounded research-and-origination mandate, not exclusive access to imaginary purchasing intent. Preserve the source URL and a short factual excerpt so another operator can reproduce the qualification decision without trusting your intuition.

Run three daily collection lanes rather than an indiscriminate scraping job. The public-record lane checks SEC EDGAR filings, SAM.gov notices, municipal permit portals, and state procurement calendars for category-specific changes. The first-party lane reviews webinar registrations, requested assessments, product documentation visits where collection is permitted, and replies to named experts. The partner lane captures explicit introductions from implementation firms, insurers, equipment distributors, and industry associations. Store collection method, permitted use, publication date, event date, and account-match confidence in separate fields. Use documented APIs and published access limits; an accessible page is not permission to defeat access controls or reuse personal information without evaluating applicable requirements. Normalize domains and parent-subsidiary relationships before enrichment through a licensed provider such as ZoomInfo or Apollo. A headquarters contact rarely owns a plant-level replacement decision. Assign a seven-day freshness window to hiring or engagement signals and a ninety-day review window to capital-project records as initial operating rules, then calibrate them against actual response. Route expired evidence back to research instead of letting an automation repeat an obsolete claim. Record negative signals too: a cancelled permit, frozen requisition, or awarded contract can save more seller time than another apparently promising lead.

Prioritize intersections of evidence, not single dramatic headlines. A manufacturer announcing an acquisition, posting integration-security roles, and requesting a regional integrator has a stronger research case than a company appearing on one generic intent list. Assign separate evidence grades: direct buyer statement, official public record, attributable partner report, and uncorroborated third-party inference. Never present inferred page visits as knowledge of an individual's private browsing. For government demand, distinguish a sources-sought notice from a funded solicitation and a solicitation from an award. SAM.gov publication alone does not authorize contingent-fee arrangements; examine FAR 52.203-5 and the applicable contracting facts before designing compensation. In the initial weekly cycle, let one researcher collect sixty events, deduplicate them into twenty-four accounts, and advance twelve accounts for manual qualification. These are workload assumptions, not market conversion benchmarks. The seller should review the rejected twelve as carefully as the accepted twelve to detect an overly narrow filter. Package the strongest five examples into a vendor-facing evidence brief showing trigger, likely business consequence, missing confirmation, and proposed outreach angle. That brief is the first sales asset: it demonstrates a repeatable research method without pretending that a public event is already a sales-ready opportunity.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make the Evidence More Valuable Than the Contact List**
> 
> Experienced originators sell verified commercial context and preserve the evidence needed to defend every accepted opportunity.
> - Save the original procurement identifier and revision history; amended deadlines can change the outreach angle before an enrichment feed updates.
> - Ask each vendor for its excluded-account export before research begins, then match parent entities as well as email domains.
> - Separate event date from collection date; a freshly indexed article can describe a project that ended months earlier.
> - Trade five annotated account hypotheses for a qualification workshop instead of giving away a reusable bulk contact export.

## 3. Qualify Accounts and Run a Bounded Multichannel Cadence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a one-hundred-point qualification rubric with hard exclusions outside the score. Award twenty-five points for target-account fit, twenty for corroborated trigger strength, twenty for buyer-confirmed economic relevance, fifteen for access to the responsible function, ten for plausible timing, and ten for delivery fit with the paying vendor. Define full, half, and zero credit for each dimension in the CRM; for example, economic relevance earns full credit only after the buyer describes a measurable business consequence. Require at least seventy points for seller research priority, not automatic opportunity acceptance. An accepted opportunity additionally needs the contracted minimum facts, such as account eligibility, a relevant role, a documented problem, and an agreed next discussion. Hard exclusions include suppressed contacts, existing vendor opportunities, prohibited fee arrangements, and unsupported damage assertions. Missing budget is a discovery task, not a field an enrichment model should invent. Configure HubSpot or Salesforce with separate research-qualified, conversation-qualified, and client-accepted stages, plus coded rejection reasons. Have a second person audit ten records weekly against their underlying evidence. If two reviewers consistently disagree, rewrite the criterion before increasing volume. This separates automation's useful ranking function from the human judgment required to represent a genuine commercial opportunity.

Run a five-touch, twenty-one-day cadence with channel eligibility checked before every action. Day one sends a short business email connecting one verified event to one relevant operating problem and asking whether that problem belongs to the recipient. Day four offers a useful comparison or technical question through an allowed business channel; a public discussion should remain useful without requiring a private sales exchange. Day eight uses a manually placed business call where applicable rules permit it. Day fourteen sends a permissioned case example with concrete scope and delivery conditions, not a fabricated customer endorsement. Day twenty-one closes the loop and stops the sequence unless a new, material event or requested follow-up justifies contact. Commercial email must satisfy 15 U.S.C. Section 7704, including accurate routing and subject information, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out handled within the statutory period. Business recipients are not categorically excluded. Phone and text workflows require separate analysis under 47 U.S.C. Section 227 and implementing rules; email eligibility does not establish calling permission. Synchronize suppression across the CRM, sending tools, and contractor work queues. When California privacy law applies, evaluate collection notices and sale or sharing rights rather than assuming business contact data falls outside its scope.

Use objections to locate missing evidence rather than to overpower resistance. When a vendor says, 'We already have an agency,' ask which segment or buying event remains undercovered, then propose a nonoverlapping pilot. When they say, 'We only pay for closed deals,' distinguish your influence over accepted opportunities from their control over pricing, sales execution, and implementation capacity. Offer a fixed research retainer plus a defined acceptance fee where permitted, rather than taking unbounded downstream sales risk. When attribution is challenged, produce the source record, first-contact timestamp, exclusion-list check, buyer response, and client acceptance event; a tracking link alone proves little. A request for proof deserves an anonymized evidence pack and a bounded sample, not an invented logo wall. A budget objection deserves a smaller named-account scope with unchanged evidence standards, not a promise to manufacture meetings cheaply. If the buyer requests removal, suppress the contact immediately as an operating practice and propagate the change. Record objection codes by segment and channel so a repeated timing objection changes account selection. After twenty qualified conversations, compare proposal progression and rejection reasons before rewriting copy. Weak fit cannot be repaired by adding touches, and an uninterested prospect is not an attribution asset worth preserving.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify These Four Conditions Before Activating a Sequence**
> 
> A high score never overrides a missing contact right, unsupported claim, delivery constraint, or unresolved compensation restriction.
> - Verify source provenance, current account ownership, permitted data use, and the absence of unsupported buyer-intent claims.
> - Check channel-specific permissions, suppression status, platform rules, and the exact automation capabilities authorized for the account.
> - Confirm vendor capacity, excluded accounts, opportunity acceptance evidence, and a written process for attribution disputes.
> - For storm or government work, verify licensing and applicable compensation restrictions before making introductions or quoting fees.

## 4. Turn the First Cohort into Signed Mandates and Reconciled Economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Define the first three-to-ten wins as signed, paid demand-generation client mandates, not downstream meetings or enterprise purchase orders. Start with a planning funnel of six hundred vendor-side signal records deduplicated to 240 prospective client accounts. Qualify 120 accounts, establish sixty substantive conversations, complete twenty-four discoveries, issue twelve proposals, and sign six mandates. The successive account-stage assumptions are 50%, 50%, 40%, 50%, and 50%; the final mandate yield is 2.5% of unique accounts. Signal-to-account deduplication is a data-quality ratio, not a sales conversion rate. These are explicit experiment targets, not external industry benchmarks. Allow 3–12 months because vendor procurement, proof requirements, and campaign learning can dominate message volume. During month one, complete the research register and qualification workshops. During months two and three, sell narrow pilots and capture rejection evidence. During months four through six, expand only the segments producing accepted opportunities and collectible fees. Use months seven through twelve for renewals and additional mandates when buying cycles require them. Review cohorts by first-contact month so recent accounts do not depress apparent conversion unfairly. If fewer than twelve of sixty conversations reach discovery, repair positioning or eligibility before increasing traffic; if proposals stall, inspect scope, procurement, and proof.

Offer a ninety-day pilot with named-account scope, weekly evidence delivery, a maximum active-account count, and an explicit acceptance process. A simple illustration is a $4,000 monthly retainer plus $1,500 for each accepted opportunity. Three monthly retainers and two accepted opportunities total $15,000 earned revenue if the services and acceptance conditions are fulfilled. That pilot illustration is separate from the annual scorecard and should not be silently annualized. Define duplicates, preexisting opportunities, rejection evidence, dispute response times, and any replacement remedy in the statement of work. A scheduled meeting is not automatically an earned opportunity fee. Store client acceptance with an immutable timestamp or auditable CRM history, then reconcile it to the invoice line. Collect retainers on an agreed schedule and model slow collections independently from recognized revenue. Track researcher hours, seller hours, enrichment cost, messaging cost, and rework for every pilot. For an illustrative $15,000 pilot, $9,000 of direct delivery expense leaves $6,000 before allocated overhead and other expenses; that contribution is not owner net profit. Limit concurrent pilots to available delivery capacity, and make one person accountable for both opportunity quality and dispute closure. Concentration risk is visible when one renewal determines whether payroll can be funded.

Reconcile growth decisions to the canonical annual cases rather than to headline client deal size. Conservative revenue is $150,000, expenses are $120,000, and owner net profit is $30,000 at 20% with 2 FT + 3 C. Base revenue is $675,000, expenses are $472,500, and owner net profit is $202,500 at 30.0% with 2 FT + 3 C. Scaled revenue is $1,200,000, expenses are $720,000, and owner net profit is $480,000 at 40% with 3 FT + 4 C. Illustrative annual revenue bridges are three client-years at $50,000, nine at $75,000, and twelve at $100,000 respectively; those bridges are arithmetic allocations, not promises that early pilots renew. Client-years represent full-year equivalent service, so staggered starts require a monthly recognition schedule. The $4,000–$20,000 monthly operating range cannot substitute for total scenario expenses; include delivery labor, contractors, acquisition, and allocated overhead without double counting. Validate staffing costs against local compensation before committing hires, especially in the Conservative case. Keep the $15,000–$100,000 startup-capital range separate from annual earnings and stress-test delayed collections. Automate account qualification, CRM attribution, and scheduling only after manual evidence checks work. Your scaling signal is repeatable accepted demand with defensible margins, not an expanding queue of unverified contacts.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the two-sided evidence register
   - Select one vendor category and research fifty prospective paying clients.
   - Maintain a separate downstream account universe; retain source dates, entity matches, exclusion checks, and the business hypothesis for each record.
2. Qualify and activate a bounded cohort
   - Apply the one-hundred-point rubric and review channel eligibility manually.
   - Advance only evidence-backed accounts, enforce hard exclusions, and run the twenty-one-day cadence with centralized suppression.
3. Sell a measurable ninety-day mandate
   - Specify named-account scope, earned fees, acceptance evidence, and dispute handling.
   - Record client acceptance separately from meeting scheduling and keep downstream contract values outside operator revenue.
4. Reconcile the cohort before expanding
   - Review conversion, delivery hours, collections, rework, and customer concentration weekly.
   - Compare monthly recognized revenue and total expenses with the canonical annual cases before committing additional people or software spend.
