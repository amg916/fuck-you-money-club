## 1. Mine purchasing signals, not patient identities  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one service polygon defined by drive time, receiving hours, and backup coverage, not an entire state. Build a 120-account research universe from state Medicaid transportation manuals, broker provider-network pages, public procurement portals, CMS Care Compare dialysis listings, and CMS CLIA laboratory records. Record the facility address, public purchasing contact, procurement vehicle, service window, source URL, and retrieval date. A CLIA certificate identifies a laboratory footprint; it does not reveal shipment volume, purchasing authority, or specimen classification. A dialysis station count indicates potential recurrence, not a sellable trip entitlement. Read county purchasing minutes for expiring transport contracts, hospital expansion announcements for discharge pressure, and laboratory job postings for evening accessioning shifts. Cross-check each signal by calling an administrative contact and asking who purchases transportation for that specific population. Keep patient names, diagnoses, and appointment rosters out of prospecting systems. Store only organization-level research in HubSpot or Airtable until an executed agreement and necessary access controls support operational data exchange. An attractive map becomes a lead only when a buyer confirms an unresolved service requirement, a purchasing route, and a decision date.

The first four chapter plays target recurring passenger demand. Play one, Medicaid standing orders, begins with the contracted broker's network team: facility enthusiasm alone cannot authorize reimbursable trips. An illustrative 60 authorized one-way legs weekly at $42 produces $2,520 billings and $1,134 retained contribution at 45%, before overhead. Play two, dialysis corridors, packages compatible treatment windows: 36 weekly legs at $48 yield $1,728 and $777.60 respectively. Never promise pooling when mobility needs, benefit rules, or discharge timing make it unsuitable. Play three, hospital discharge overflow, prices scarce evening coverage: 12 completed legs at $85 yield $1,020 billings and $459 retained contribution. Play four, workers' compensation appointments, approaches the authorized adjuster or transportation network: 20 approved legs at $65 yield $1,300 and $585. These are independent quoting examples, not additions to the canonical annual scenarios. Confirm whether waiting, empty repositioning, tolls, escorts, and cancellations are separately payable. If authorizations are unreliable, offer a buyer-funded limited coverage block with explicit payment responsibility, or decline the route. Do not transfer unpaid authorized-service risk to the passenger through improvised charges or represent a facility referral as payer approval.

The next four plays pursue specimens and usable capacity. Play five, laboratory milk runs, joins collection sites with compatible accession cutoffs: ten weekly route runs at $140 produce $1,400 billings and $630 retained contribution. Play six, STAT backup coverage, sells a defined response envelope: eight completed dispatches at $95 generate $760 and $342, excluding any separately negotiated availability fee. Play seven, rural specimen consolidation, needs a real receiving destination and validated transport conditions: six runs at $180 generate $1,080 and $486. Play eight, owner-operator capacity matching, fills verified service gaps rather than reselling an unapproved subcontract: 15 authorized legs at $50 generate $750 and $337.50. For every example, 55% driver settlement is a screening assumption, not a promise that every lane supports that split. Measure loaded time, empty miles, parking, cleaning, packaging handling, and return obligations before quoting. If specimen stability conflicts with consolidation, revert to dedicated transport. If a broker prohibits subcontracting, pursue direct credentialing or reject the assignment. If backup capacity disappears, reduce the offered geography instead of selling a response window that dispatch cannot deliver. The ninth, adjacent employer-service play appears separately in the conversion economics.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the gap between the published schedule and actual coverage**
> 
> Experienced dispatch sellers investigate time-window failures before offering lower prices.
> - Ask dialysis administrators for aggregate late-return counts by weekday, then confirm trip authority with the contracted broker before proposing coverage.
> - Call laboratory receiving operations before collection sites; the final accession cutoff determines which apparent route combinations actually work.

> - Ask prospective owner-operators to price the complete duty cycle, including empty repositioning, rather than quoting only loaded mileage.

## 2. Qualify authority, route contribution, and payment float before quoting  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a 100-point qualification score before spending time on a custom proposal. Award 20 points for verified purchasing and assignment authority, 20 for recurring addressable volume, 20 for measured route contribution, 15 for acceptable collection terms, 15 for credentialed primary and backup capacity, and 10 for a documented buying event. Require at least 75 points for a proposal, while treating absent trip authority, unsafe handling, and prohibited subcontracting as disqualifiers regardless of score. Full volume points require a buyer-confirmed aggregate schedule, not a facility census. Full contribution points require a driver quote for the complete route and a dispatch cost estimate. Full collection points require invoice acceptance rules, dispute ownership, and a credible payer. Ask who signs, who issues the order, who accepts proof of service, and who releases payment; these can be four different organizations. Record the answer to each question in separate CRM fields. Avoid calling the deal qualified because a case manager likes the concept. If the score is below threshold, specify the missing evidence and a next review date. An unresolved credentialing dependency belongs in nurture, not the committed sales forecast.

Translate the canonical scorecard into a purchasing filter. Conservative gross billings of $250,000 less $137,500 driver settlements leave $112,500 retained revenue; $82,500 of remaining operating costs leave $30,000 owner profit. Base billings of $925,000 less $508,750 settlements leave $416,250 retained revenue; $249,750 remaining costs leave $166,500 profit. Scaled billings of $1,600,000 less $880,000 settlements leave $720,000 retained revenue; $336,000 remaining costs leave $384,000 profit. Thus gross-billings margins are 12%, 18.0%, and 24%, while retained margins are 26.67%, 40.0%, and 53.33%. Preserve the supplied 2 FT + 2 C, 2 FT + 2 C, and 3 FT + 3 C staffing assumptions. Do not reinterpret those headcounts as a universal fleet-capacity limit; validate actual driver availability against each schedule. The model uses retained dispatch revenue as its net presentation; actual ASC 606 principal-versus-agent assessment depends on the contracts and control of the promised service. Keep gross billings, driver settlements, retained contribution, and owner profit in distinct ledger fields. Reject proposals that substitute underlying medical charges or workers' compensation claim values for transport revenue. Route contribution is not owner profit.

Payment float can eliminate a superficially attractive deal. At the Base case, $508,750 annual driver settlements equal approximately $1,393.84 daily using 365 days. A 45-day interval between paying drivers and collecting accepted invoices ties up approximately $62,723 before overhead, disputes, or reserve cash. Weekly driver terms may shorten that interval, while rejected invoices can lengthen it; model actual dates rather than assuming Net-45 means exactly 45 days from pickup. This exposure nearly consumes the $65,000 upper startup-capital case, making an immediate Base-scale launch unsuitable without additional liquidity or staged volume. The supplied $3,500–$18,000 monthly opex range is a planning indicator, not the complete residual cost bridge: Base remaining costs average $20,812.50 monthly. Budget every residual category explicitly rather than forcing that total into the narrower range. Start with one bounded route, invoice promptly, and reconcile paid versus accepted service before expanding. Use 837P EDI only where the payer actually requires that transaction; other buyers use broker portals or ordinary invoices. RouteGenie or MediRoutes GPS evidence can support service verification, but neither replaces authorization, required signatures, or the payer's specified trip record.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before accepting the first paid assignment**
> 
> A signed pilot is useful only when the dispatch desk can execute and collect.
> - Verify the ordering party, payer, service authorization, permitted subcontracting structure, and required Medicaid enrollment for the specific state and contract.

## 3. Run a measurable outbound cadence and convert evidence into paid pilots  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build outreach around one observed constraint, one credible capacity statement, and one small next step. A laboratory email can read: your public hours show collection through 18:00; we are evaluating a dedicated 18:15 pickup to your receiving laboratory, subject to your transport requirements; who owns that route? A broker email instead names the specific county, vehicle class, and verified coverage window. Do not claim network approval before it exists. On day one send a short individual email; on day three call the published business number; on day six send a one-page coverage map; on day ten offer a route review; on day fifteen close the loop and ask permission for a later check-in. Use accurate sender details, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out for commercial email under 15 U.S.C. 7704. Avoid unsolicited automated texts or prerecorded calls; screen calling methods against 47 U.S.C. 227 and applicable state rules. Log objections and suppress opt-outs across all campaigns. Public contact details are a starting point for relevant outreach, not permission to flood every employee at the same institution.

Treat conversion benchmarks as planning hypotheses, not industry averages. For one 120-account cohort, target 84 verified purchasing contacts, 42 substantive conversations, 21 qualified route assessments, 12 written proposals, six paid pilots, and three signed recurring assignments. The successive conversions are 70%, 50%, 50%, approximately 57%, 50%, and 50%; the overall win rate is 2.5%. A stretch cohort of 200 researched accounts can yield 140 contacts, 70 conversations, 35 assessments, 20 proposals, 14 pilots, and ten wins, implying 5% overall conversion and materially stronger pilot performance. Keep these funnels separate rather than averaging incompatible assumptions. Assign one person research and contracting ownership and another dispatch feasibility ownership; batch approximately ten new accounts per working day during the research phase. Use weeks one through three for mapping, weeks three through eight for conversations and credentialing, and weeks eight through twenty for pilots and recurring awards. Existing network access may compress this schedule; new enrollment can extend it beyond five months. Review losses weekly by authority, price, timing, capacity, or payment terms. Fix the largest measured bottleneck before increasing outreach volume or buying another contact list.

Design inbound material to answer the questions a real buyer asks before requesting coverage. Publish separate pages for wheelchair-capable NEMT coverage, laboratory scheduled routes, and STAT backup, using only capabilities actually supported by the operator network. Show service geography, staffed dispatch hours, vehicle or handling limitations, and a clear organizational inquiry form. Do not collect patient names, diagnoses, or specimen identifiers through that form. Link prospects to a secure operational intake only after qualification. Offer a paid ten-business-day pilot covering one route or one bounded service window, with written rates, acceptance criteria, exception reporting, and exit terms. For example, twenty completed courier runs at $140 produce $2,800 billings, $1,540 driver settlements, and $1,260 retained contribution before dispatch overhead. A proposed 98% on-time target cannot be meaningfully estimated from twenty runs: nineteen timely runs equal 95%, while twenty equal 100%. Report raw counts alongside percentages and investigate every late event. The recurring agreement should follow evidence of executed service, invoice acceptance, and backup performance. Count a win only when a signed recurring assignment exists; a directory listing, verbal referral, or unpaid demonstration is not a won contract.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the evidence-backed account cohort
   - Research 120 organizations inside one executable service polygon.
   - Capture public source URLs, retrieval dates, purchasing contacts, service windows, and an organization-level demand hypothesis.
2. Verify authority and score the route
   - Complete the qualification scorecard before preparing custom pricing.
   - Require at least 75 points plus resolved authorization, safety, and subcontracting conditions; obtain complete primary and backup driver quotes.
3. Run the fifteen-day contact sequence
   - Use individual email, business calls, and a specific coverage map.

4. Convert a paid pilot into recurring scope
   - Execute a bounded pilot and review service evidence with the buyer.
   - Reconcile completed work, exceptions, accepted invoices, and backup performance before signing recurring volume or expanding coverage.

## 4. Close objections, isolate the tax-service adjacency, and protect delivery  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Answer objections with narrower operating commitments rather than indiscriminate discounts. When a broker says its network is full, ask whether a specific evening window or accessible-vehicle gap remains uncovered and request the formal supplier process. When a laboratory says the incumbent is cheaper, compare complete pickup windows, exception response, receiving cutoffs, and temperature evidence, not unsupported claims about clinical outcomes. When a hospital requests immediate unlimited coverage, offer a limited geography with a named escalation path and measurable capacity. When a buyer refuses an availability fee, sell scheduled completed work without promising exclusive standby resources. A $95 STAT dispatch with $52.25 driver settlement leaves $42.75 before overhead; an unpriced hour of dedicated standby can erase that contribution. If a prospect demands unpaid work, propose a smaller paid test rather than normalizing free transport. Do not pay per-patient referral bounties to discharge staff or facility employees. Remuneration intended to induce federally reimbursable business raises issues under 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b); any marketing arrangement needs its own factual assessment. A procurement introduction does not justify a hidden commission. Record the objection, revised scope, economic effect, and buyer response before issuing revised terms.

The ninth mapped play is wotc_hourly_payroll_tax_credit_contingency_engine, an adjacent employer service rather than a transportation revenue source. Offer employer-authorized Form 8850 workflow support only after checking 26 U.S.C. 51 authorization for the actual hire date, state workforce agency procedures, and the generally applicable 28-day certification submission deadline. Because the prior authorization covered workers beginning work before January 1, 2026, verify any extension before marketing credits for later hires. Contractor settlements do not become qualifying employee wages. For an illustrative authorized cohort, ten certified employees each meeting the applicable 400-hour requirement and $6,000 eligible wage cap could produce ten times $2,400, or $24,000, before employer-specific limitations; target groups and hours can change those amounts. A hypothetical permitted 10% outcome fee would be $2,400, but professional fee restrictions, including 31 CFR 10.27 where applicable, control the engagement. Separately evaluate Section 41 research activities, business components, uncertainty, experimentation, qualified expenses, and wage coordination; buying dispatch software does not establish research eligibility. Exclude unsupported claims. The fallback is independent tax review and fixed-fee documentation, such as twelve substantiated work hours at $125 for $1,500, without a credit promise or transport referral condition. Keep all such fees outside the canonical transport scenarios.

Close the transport agreement with an evidence package that matches the actual service. Where the relationship creates business-associate duties, execute the required HIPAA agreement under 45 CFR 164.502(e) and 164.504(e); do not assume every courier relationship has identical status or that a narrow conduit exception automatically covers dispatch software storing patient data. Apply minimum-necessary access where required and separate sales records from operational records. For specimens, have the responsible shipper establish classification and packaging; Category B UN3373 requirements under 49 CFR 173.199 are not a universal rule for every medical shipment. Address employee exposure under 29 CFR 1910.1030 where applicable, with training, exposure controls, and incident response. CLIA certification belongs to laboratory operations and does not credential a transport company. Use barcode handoffs, timestamps, receiving confirmation, and specified temperature evidence without claiming that a scan proves specimen integrity. Review owner-operator classification under applicable state law and federal standards; a signed contractor agreement alone settles nothing. After the first three wins, review collections, missed windows, driver concentration, and backup failures every week. Expand only the service patterns whose collected contribution and operational evidence support the next commitment.
