## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Operators across the board agree the vehicle is the business: whatever you park in the driveway decides which manifests you can accept. RouteGenie frames the minivan as the workhorse, citing Indiana Medicaid data where 63.5% of trip requests are ambulatory and 30.3% are wheelchair, which is why a wheelchair-accessible minivan can complete up to 90% of all contracts and is the minimum recommended platform for scaling with brokers [Lh999r_-mi0 @ 00:05:17]. Nemt University's host maps a six-month path from personal car to wheelchair van and is blunt that adding a wheelchair accessible vehicle on top of ambulatory ramps up revenue and is essential if you want to scale [VGWUx-p8MbM @ 00:12:56]. The Wheelchair & Stretcher Transportation Services operator pushes the logic to the ceiling: a Ford Transit fits a stretcher, two wheelchairs, and one ambulatory passenger in one vehicle, and running Caravans on broker work alone is not how you scale [svdIU5f-6tA @ 00:00:39]. NEMT Growth Machine bought into the same thesis with real money, choosing a van with full ambulatory and wheelchair capability because the owners wanted to shoot for as much revenue as possible [nSB43YYfRs0 @ 00:07:35]. The shared rule: buy capability that matches the highest-value trips in your market.

The second consensus point is that cramped wheelchair vans quietly bleed trips. Jasmine and Monica of MJ Business Academy, five years in business in California, report that rehabilitation-center patients often ride with leg extenders, and a smaller wheelchair van like the ProMaster City cannot fit them, so that trip gets turned away; the same tight cabin blocks multi-loading and blocks hauling a husband and wife who are both in wheelchairs [P_pN6quM3G4 @ 00:03:02]. On a live ride-along they watched a competitor load a reclining wheelchair and called it proof that full cut vans are the best, since a typical side entry van likely could not accommodate that client [ohTDpW0zUS8 @ 00:06:54]. Their own full cut Dodge Caravan carries two ambulatory riders plus one wheelchair with the seats out, or two wheelchairs with the fold-away chairs collapsed [rIDRpTROLM8 @ 00:00:23]. They also flag a regulatory trap that reinforces the same choice: California has been denying rear entry vehicles, and one of their clients with a rear entry wheelchair van was approved by Medicaid only for ambulatory trips because the van lacked an emergency exit, even with a side door [P_pN6quM3G4 @ 00:11:02]. Size, layout, and exit configuration decide your book before marketing ever does.

Baseline equipment is equally settled. RouteGenie lists the non-negotiables as airbags, seat belts, ABS brakes, maintained lights, brakes, and tires, a first aid kit, a fire extinguisher, and GPS tracking, with accessible units needing a 56-inch door opening, ramps or lifts, gear shift interlock, and four tie-downs with lap and shoulder belts; some states bar vehicles older than 15 years from manufacture, and the most common age range cited is 5 to 10 years [Lh999r_-mi0 @ 00:01:26]. MJ Business Academy adds that the fire extinguisher must be mounted inside the van and that a carpet-free floor is a plus because clients have accidents and bodily fluids have to be cleaned fast [rIDRpTROLM8 @ 00:01:54]. NEMT Growth Machine carries disposable gloves, N95 masks, a first aid kit, and a bloodborne pathogen and bodily fluid spill kit in a duffel in the back [nSB43YYfRs0 @ 00:04:26]. On the human side, RouteGenie's driver profile demands a valid state-issued license, the ability to operate a wheelchair lift, sometimes lifting 50 pounds or more, and training in HIPAA, CPR, and first aid, because this is not taxi or Uber work [qiZqLls0G-E @ 00:00:50]. Skip any of these and the van is not ready to run a manifest.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

NEMT Growth Machine published the cleanest unit economics. Their 2025 Ford Transit 350 XL with a rear loading wheelchair lift cost $87,900 after taxes from Mobility Works in Charlotte, with a warranty; they put $6,000 down and financed the rest at roughly $1,600 a month [nSB43YYfRs0 @ 00:00:00]. RouteGenie's 2026 price sheet, built on 2019 used units under 100,000 miles, gives the cheaper on-ramps: a used Grand Caravan runs $13,000 to $16,000, a used Toyota Sienna $21,000 to $24,000, and a used Ford Transit $25,000 to $30,000, against $50,500 new [uJV2f3MOJbI @ 00:01:06]. Nemt University tells beginners to set a hard budget first, whether $7,500, $10,000, or $20,000, then pick cash, financing, or a lease, paying cash if you can so there is no note, and financing only with decent credit and room to cover the payment plus insurance [VGWUx-p8MbM @ 00:04:37].

Inside the box, the mechanics are physical and repeatable. NEMT Growth Machine runs a floor track system: pull a lever and the seats slide to any layout, so a wheelchair rider and caregiver can sit together; every wheelchair gets four track locks, two front and two back, that hook the chair, then two lap belts join in the middle and a shoulder belt completes a full seat belt [nSB43YYfRs0 @ 00:01:44]. Their add-on list is cheap: a roughly $30 windshield phone mount so drivers stay hands-free, a dash cam plus interior camera at about $180 to $200 that records whenever the van is on, Clorox wipes for every high-touch surface after each rider, an umbrella for door-to-door pickups in the rain, a jack and spare, and three reflective triangles set roughly 10, 100, and 200 feet behind a breakdown [nSB43YYfRs0 @ 00:02:40]. MJ Business Academy slides a TJ Maxx or Home Goods storage bin into the limited storage area for driver lunches and equipment [rIDRpTROLM8 @ 00:02:16]. For stretcher work, AMS Vans installs a gurney with a quick release lock in a rear entry long channel: lower the gurney all the way, roll the front casters into the antler cradle, and pin it; unbolt the floor mount with a 9/16 ratchet to flip the van back to wheelchair service [-DCWDiGaTn0 @ 00:01:02].

Daily execution starts before dawn. RouteGenie's driver routine begins as early as 4 or 5 a.m. to hit pre-procedure hospital arrivals: clock in through the NEMT app, check fuel, oil, tire pressure, brakes, lights, wheelchair lifts, and securement straps, then run surgery, dialysis, and physical therapy pickups across an 8 to 12 hour shift that closes with a final inspection logged in the app [qiZqLls0G-E @ 00:03:06]. Wait time is billable leverage: on a ride-along, Jasmine and Monica's private client had a 10:30 pickup and had agreed that if the client was not outside at 10:30 the clock starts and wait time gets charged, and they pulled the client's number from the app to call directly [ohTDpW0zUS8 @ 00:05:59]. Cancellations are the bleed. LearnCargo says nine times out of 10 you discover a cancellation on your own at the last minute, sometimes after the broker failed to notify you; the fix is to call the broker and get a note on the client account after a wasted 30-mile run [MOdO2Zzw1wI @ 00:01:02]. RouteGenie's fee model uses a 24-hour free cancellation window, with an example of a $25 fee on a $100 trip canceled inside 24 hours, backed by a written, consistently enforced no-show policy [d8ocN3ERJag @ 00:04:22].

Even the money math splits, sometimes inside the same channel. The playbook that reconciles these positions is Nemt University's own six-month plan: start on a personal vehicle, then step up to a wheelchair van if business goes well, with market research deciding the timing.
