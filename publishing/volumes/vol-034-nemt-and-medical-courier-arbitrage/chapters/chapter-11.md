## 1. Assign four real seats before selling nine operating plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the initial pod around two employee seats and two genuine owner-operator contractor seats. FT1 owns dispatch, customer relationships, credential monitoring, incident command, and the weekly capacity plan. FT2 owns authorization matching, invoice preparation, remittance posting, collections, and the service-month close; they also provide trained dispatch relief during scheduled breaks. C1 supplies qualified NEMT vehicle capacity and documented passenger service. C2 supplies qualified medical courier capacity and specimen handoffs. Neither contractor becomes a disguised employee dispatcher merely because the organization chart needs coverage. Define each contracted service by accepted assignments, equipment requirements, delivery evidence, and lawful performance outcomes, while testing the actual relationship against applicable federal and state classification rules. At scale, FT3 becomes the dedicated dispatch and exception coordinator, releasing FT1 to supervise accounts and service quality; C3 supplies a qualified flex route within their actual credentials. These are four and six people respectively, not counts that quietly omit permanent assistants. If the founder fills FT1, identify that fact in payroll and valuation schedules. If outside carrier companies supply additional capacity, disclose their drivers separately and stop describing total delivery manpower as only six people.

Use one nine-play map to prevent the pod from fragmenting into unrelated service lines: Medicaid standing-order rides, recurring dialysis rides, hospital discharge rides, workers' compensation appointment transport, scheduled laboratory sweeps, STAT specimen pickups, interfacility specimen transfers, pharmacy delivery routes, and contracted overflow coverage. This chapter's operational labels organize the nine mapped plays; they do not establish nine separate revenue forecasts. Tag each accepted job with its play, payer, ordering customer, service class, and assigned contractor. Dialysis work can sit inside a Medicaid standing order, so choose one primary play for revenue attribution and retain other characteristics as secondary tags. FT1 owns commercial commitments for every play, while FT2 owns evidence sufficient to collect each commitment. C1 normally carries passenger assignments and C2 specimen assignments; pharmacy and overflow work require specific qualification rather than automatic eligibility. A pharmacy delivery contract does not itself authorize controlled-substance handling. Each play must fit the same dispatch board, evidence store, and billing ledger. Do not advertise continuous STAT coverage when two contractors cannot cover simultaneous jobs and rest periods. Sell defined service windows, explicit acceptance deadlines, and documented alternatives when capacity is unavailable.

Size the next seat from timed work, not the attractiveness of the Scaled case. For four weeks, record assignment handling minutes, authorization exceptions, pickup-window collisions, collection touches, and actual contractor vehicle-hours. An illustrative hiring trigger is six consecutive weeks above 80% of available dispatch minutes, coupled with contracted incremental contribution sufficient to cover the new employee's loaded compensation and operating buffer. Separately require a vehicle-hour capacity model before adding C3: passenger dwell time, wheelchair securement, laboratory receiving windows, and deadhead mileage all consume capacity even when billings appear adequate. RouteGenie or MediRoutes can support scheduling and GPS evidence, but verify the selected product's contracted features before promising integrations. Use a barcode workflow for specimen events that does not expose patient information on public labels. FT2 must be able to retrieve the supporting event history without asking the founder to interpret private messages. Cross-train the two employee seats on essential exceptions, then test a planned founder absence. A dispatcher should know whom to contact, what service may be accepted, and when the customer must receive a refusal rather than an unsupported promise.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make the smallest pod difficult to disrupt**
> 
> Protect scarce dispatch attention by designing repeatable boundaries before accepting additional service complexity.
> - Attach each dialysis standing order to its current authorization dates; flag renewals before recurring trips continue beyond the authorized period.
> - Obtain each laboratory's receiving cutoff and rejected-package escalation number; a timely arrival at a locked entrance is not a completed handoff.
> - Reserve flex vehicle-hours explicitly instead of counting the same contractor as simultaneous passenger backup and STAT specimen coverage.
> - Export a weekly evidence index with service IDs and document locations so a replacement employee can locate records without searching the founder's phone.

## 2. Price contractor outcomes and run an exception-first weekly scorecard  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Write compensation schedules by service type, with an accepted base trip or route amount, mileage treatment, waiting-time rules, cancellation terms, and documented accessorials. The canonical 55% driver settlement share is an aggregate financial assumption, not a universal trip-level entitlement. A dense laboratory sweep may support a different settlement ratio than a long wheelchair ride; route-mix totals must still reconcile to the modeled retained share. Pay for performed, documented services on the agreed cycle rather than automatically passing a broker's delayed payment to contractors. State exactly how a disputed custody event or missing trip record is investigated, what evidence the contractor may submit, and which deductions the agreement and applicable law permit. A missing signature is not permission to confiscate an entire week's earned settlement. Specify customer-facing outcomes without scripting every movement of a purported independent business. IRS common-law analysis, FLSA employment standards, and state tests can produce different obligations; California Labor Code section 2775 is particularly important where its framework applies. A contract title, business entity, or 1099 form does not settle classification. If the actual role requires employee-like control, budget employment rather than forcing the economics through a contractor label.

Run a thirty-minute Monday scorecard using the previous completed service week, with a separate aging view for unresolved exceptions. Define on-time pickup as arrival within the contractual window divided by eligible completed pickups; show customer-caused exclusions separately instead of removing inconvenient events silently. Set illustrative internal targets of at least 98% on-time pickup, at least 99% complete service evidence, and at least 97% first-pass claim or invoice acceptance, then substitute stricter contractual requirements where necessary. Report counts beside percentages because one miss among ten STAT pickups is materially different from one among a thousand scheduled rides. For STAT work, measure acceptance-to-arrival and arrival-to-handoff against the specific purchased service window, never an invented universal clinical deadline. FT1 assigns every serious exception a named investigator, customer communication deadline, and closure evidence. FT2 reports denial reasons by service cohort, unbilled completed work, and receivables past terms. Review custody breaks immediately rather than waiting for Monday. Contractor scorecards should distinguish facts from allegations, allow corrections, and track recurring patterns. Avoid blending safety-critical exceptions into an average score that rewards volume while concealing a failed handoff.

Maintain two evidence tracks because passenger transportation and specimen transport have different failure modes. For NEMT, match the authorized rider, service date, origin, destination, assistance level, and required trip verification to the payer's current rules. Use 837P only where the payer requires that transaction; many broker relationships settle through portals or contractual invoices instead. For specimens, connect the package barcode to collection handoff, custody transfers, delivery recipient, and packaging or temperature exceptions without treating the courier as the laboratory's clinical decision maker. Under 49 CFR 173.199, Category B infectious substances have specific packaging and marking requirements; not every medical package belongs in that category. OSHA's bloodborne-pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030, applies to covered occupational exposure and requires an employer exposure-control approach, not merely a signed driver checklist. Where the dispatcher acts as a HIPAA business associate, execute the required agreement and control access to protected information; evaluate any conduit argument against actual storage and service functions. FT1 checks qualification before assignment, while FT2 checks collection evidence before billing. A clean invoice cannot cure an unqualified vehicle, mishandled specimen, or materially inaccurate trip log.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the completed service cohort
   - Export the prior week's completed jobs and unresolved exceptions.
   - Preserve original timestamps and service identifiers; corrections remain separately attributable and dated.
2. Reconcile operational evidence to money
   - Match each job to authorization, invoice status, and contractor settlement.
   - Separate unfinished evidence, disputed pricing, rejected claims, and cash already collected.
3. Assign corrective work with an owner
   - Review denominator-based KPIs and assign deadlines for material exceptions.
   - Investigate patient-safety and specimen-integrity events immediately, outside the ordinary weekly meeting.
4. Rebalance the next service week
   - Adjust accepted capacity, renewal priorities, and collection escalations.
   - Record the decision, supporting evidence, accountable seat, and next review date in the shared operating log.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before releasing an assignment**
> 
> A dispatch release requires current evidence, actual capacity, and a collectable service definition.
> - Verify payer authorization, service eligibility, agreed rate, and the exact evidence required for the accepted passenger or courier assignment.
> - Verify driver and vehicle qualifications, insurance scope, equipment suitability, and the applicable service-category credential expiration dates.
> - Verify packaging responsibilities, receiving location availability, custody capture, and incident contacts for each specimen service.
> - Verify that the assignment fits available vehicle-hours, preserves promised pickup windows, and does not push a counterparty above the 25% exposure policy.

## 3. Reconcile retained contribution and cap every economic counterparty  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep the Formula v2.0 bridge visible in every management pack. Conservative gross contracted billings of $250,000 less $137,500 in driver settlements produce $112,500 retained contribution; subtract $82,500 in remaining modeled costs to reach $30,000 owner profit. Base billings of $925,000 less $508,750 produce $416,250 retained contribution; subtract $249,750 to reach $166,500. Scaled billings of $1,600,000 less $880,000 produce $720,000 retained contribution; subtract $336,000 to reach $384,000. Gross-billings profit margins are therefore 12%, 18.0%, and 24%, while retained margins are 26.67%, 40.0%, and 53.33%, rounded as shown. The supplied monthly opex range of $3,500–$18,000 cannot represent every remaining modeled cost in every scenario: those annual residuals average $6,875, $20,812.50, and $28,000 monthly. Reconcile its scope before using it as a total-cost budget. Do not subtract driver settlements twice or describe underlying medical charges as brokerage revenue. For ASC 606 presentation, document whether the operator controls the promised service before transfer. The scorecard uses a 45% retained-share basis, but actual principal-versus-agent presentation depends on contractual facts; a spreadsheet ratio alone does not establish the accounting conclusion.

Close by service month, customer, primary play, and contractor cohort. Accrue earned billings for completed qualifying services, related contractor obligations, payroll, insurance, software, and identifiable credits in the same period. Maintain separate schedules for unbilled completed services, submitted receivables, disputed balances, and cash receipts. Treat denials according to their cause: a contractual price adjustment is different from a valid receivable becoming uncollectible. FT2 should reconcile the dispatch service register to the billing register and general ledger, then reconcile contractor settlement batches to payables and bank movements. QuickBooks Online or Xero can hold the ledger, but avoid placing patient details in accounting memo fields; use restricted service identifiers linked to the authorized evidence system. For a simple Base cash sensitivity, daily average billings are $925,000 divided by 365. Thirty days of evenly distributed unpaid billings imply about $76,027 receivables; forty-five days imply about $114,041. The additional fifteen days absorb approximately $38,014 before considering payment timing elsewhere. This is a receivables illustration, not a complete cash forecast. Model weekly contractor settlements, payroll dates, taxes, customer credits, and available reserves explicitly; initial capital of $15,000–$65,000 does not automatically finance the mature Base collection cycle.

Set a maximum 25% exposure policy for each customer and economic counterparty, measured across trailing-twelve-month gross billings, retained contribution, and outstanding receivables. Aggregate affiliates and contracts sharing a common broker payment source; four facilities routed through one payer can still create one collection dependency. Also track forward contracted run rate so a new award cannot hide behind trailing diversification. At Base, 25% equals $231,250 of annual gross billings and $104,062.50 of annual retained contribution. At Scaled, those limits are $400,000 and $180,000 respectively. Receivables receive their own percentage test rather than borrowing an annual revenue dollar limit. Early launch will often breach the cap because the first customer represents the entire book; label that as an unresolved exception with a time-bound diversification plan, not as compliance with the target. Pause incremental awards to an overweight counterparty when practical and pursue independent payment sources within existing service geography. Avoid superficial diversification through longer routes that destroy contribution. Stress-test loss of the largest account, a forty-five-day payment delay, and one contractor becoming unavailable together. The pod needs enough liquidity and executable capacity to survive the combination, not merely each event in isolation.

## 4. Transfer the book, normalize earnings, and choose sale or distributions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build a buyer-ready operating book before soliciting buyers. Include executed customer agreements, renewal calendars, rate amendments, assignment restrictions, termination rights, payer enrollment requirements, and any required change-of-control notifications or approvals. A relationship with a hospital employee is not an assignable revenue contract, and Medicaid participation may require separate steps that an asset purchase does not bypass. Maintain a credential register, insurance history, incident log, contractor agreements, and dated evidence of how service exceptions were resolved. Put company domains, dispatch subscriptions, barcode workflows, and operating documentation under company administration with recoverable credentials and role-based access. Require written intellectual property assignments for custom scripts, training materials, and other owned deliverables where ownership has not otherwise vested. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a signed writing; paying an outside developer alone does not establish ownership. Separate owned materials from licensed vendor software and document license transfer restrictions. Limit early diligence to aggregated operating evidence and appropriately redacted examples. A prospective buyer's interest does not create unrestricted access to patient information. Route any necessary protected-data access through a documented permitted basis, appropriate agreements, and minimum-necessary controls where applicable.

Price earnings only after deciding what the buyer must replace. For an owner-operated small book, use an illustrative negotiating sensitivity of 2.0–3.0 times verified seller's discretionary earnings, or SDE; this is not a claim about observed transaction comparables. If normalized Base SDE actually equals the canonical $166,500 owner profit, that sensitivity produces $333,000–$499,500 before debt, cash, working-capital adjustments, and transaction costs. Do not assume owner profit and SDE are identical without a reconciliation. For a genuinely manager-run operation, examine an illustrative 3.0–5.0 times normalized EBITDA sensitivity rather than applying an SDE multiple to a different earnings definition. As a separate Scaled sensitivity, assume $384,000 is the earnings starting point and an unexpensed owner role requires $110,000 in annual loaded replacement cost. If no other adjustments apply, the resulting $274,000 produces $822,000–$1,370,000 at those multiples. These assumptions do not alter the canonical profit case. If management replacement is already expensed, subtracting it again understates earnings. Reconcile interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, owner compensation, and genuine nonrecurring items explicitly. A buyer will reject unsupported add-backs and discounts that disappear only while the founder works unpaid.

Earn the upper end of a negotiated range through evidence of durability, not a glossy route map. Demonstrate stable service-month collections, counterparty concentration at or below 25%, qualified backup capacity, repeatable dispatch coverage, and customer retention after the founder stops handling routine calls. Explain any adverse cohorts before diligence finds them: retroactive authorization denials, specimen rejection disputes, contractor classification exposure, and expensive renewal terms can all change sustainable earnings. Present debt-like obligations, unresolved clawbacks, and a working-capital target based on the actual collection cycle. In an earnout, define qualifying revenue, contractor cost allocation, customer transfers, reporting access, and dispute resolution so the buyer cannot change the calculation merely by moving accounts. If retaining the business, apply the same discipline to distributions. Release cash only after funding scheduled settlements, payroll, known taxes, reasonable claims reserves, and a stressed collection buffer. Test at least two consecutive weeks of ordinary founder absence before calling the book transferable; include a renewal, a late payer, and a service exception in the observed operating period when feasible. A durable cash cow and a saleable platform require the same core asset: documented earnings that survive another competent person taking the controls.
