## 1. Identify the entity that owes payment, not merely the person requesting transport  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start every opportunity with three names: the service requester, the person authorized to commit spending, and the legal entity obligated to pay. A dialysis scheduler wants fewer missed treatments but usually cannot bind a Medicaid transportation broker. A hospital discharge coordinator wants beds released but may lack purchasing authority for a private-pay ride. A laboratory accessioning manager wants specimens before cutoff, while procurement signs the route agreement and accounts payable releases funds. Medicaid transportation assurance under 42 CFR 431.53 creates a state program obligation, not an automatic receivable for any dispatcher accepting a request. Identify the contracted broker, managed-care arrangement, or enrolled provider pathway before booking. Ask who issues the authorization, which entity appears on remittance, and whether subcontracted owner-operators are permitted. Obtain the executed agreement, current rate exhibit, credentialing confirmation, and escalation contact rather than treating a referral email as purchasing authority. For private laboratory routes, inspect the purchase order and its service period, locations, spending ceiling, and invoice address. The buyer's acute pain is continuity of care or processing throughput; your collectible asset is an accepted contractual obligation supported by performance evidence. Keep those concepts separate when a persuasive clinical contact demands immediate service without an identified payment source.

Sell the budget consequence that each decision-maker can actually control. Broker network managers value reliable coverage in difficult ZIP codes because repeated failures consume rescue capacity and threaten contract performance. Laboratory operations leaders value stable pickup windows because delayed specimens can require recollection or invalidate an assay's handling conditions. Workers' compensation adjusters value authorized attendance and a defensible claim file; an employer's supervisor does not necessarily control the carrier's transportation budget. Quote different deliverables accordingly: a completed authorized leg, a reserved route window, a documented STAT pickup, or a defined coordination service. Never sell patient referrals or a percentage of testing reimbursement as the dispatch product. Use time, mileage, stops, capacity, and documented service requirements to support pricing. Under 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b), remuneration intended to induce federal healthcare program referrals creates serious exposure; a coordination label does not cure a referral-payment arrangement. Record who approves accessorial charges and who bears cancellation risk before assigning drivers. Nine chapter-mapped operating plays appear below; their rates illustrate purchasing mechanics rather than additional scorecard forecasts. Each play requires its own contract permission, service evidence, and fallback. Combining them commercially does not combine their authorizations, clinical handling requirements, or reimbursement rules into one universal permission to dispatch.

The mapped play gsa_municipal_set_aside_facility_ghost_brokering must operate as disclosed coordination, despite its historical label. Match an eligible facility-service prime with qualified transportation or courier subcontractors only where the solicitation and prime contract permit that scope. The prime owns the government relationship; the coordinator earns a documented fee for sourcing, scheduling, and evidence administration, not for lending eligibility credentials. An illustrative $20,000 monthly subcontract package can support a separately priced $1,500 coordination fee when the prime expressly purchases that work and the pricing avoids duplicate recovery. Only the $1,500 service fee is coordinator revenue if subcontractors bill the prime directly; the $20,000 package is neither coordinator revenue nor the volume's 45% dispatch model. Inspect federal limitations on subcontracting under 13 CFR 125.6 where applicable, relevant solicitation clauses, and actual performance responsibilities. Municipal preferences follow their own local rules, not an assumed federal template. Obtain required subcontract disclosures and verify that the prime performs its required share. Never manufacture set-aside status, hide the actual performer, or submit an invoice implying unperformed work. If eligibility or performance requirements cannot be satisfied, withdraw the noncompliant bid and operate as a disclosed subcontractor on unrestricted work. Preserve the sourcing record, deliverables, and acceptance evidence supporting every coordination invoice.

## 2. Select a settlement rail that matches purchasing authority and evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A payment rail moves money; it does not establish entitlement to that money. Medicaid-related work may require a broker portal, proprietary trip file, or an 837P professional claim according to the payer's companion guide. Do not submit 837P transactions simply because dispatch software can export them. Match authorization identifiers, rendering or billing identifiers where required, service dates, permitted codes, units, and pickup evidence to the enrolled contractual pathway. Track technical acceptance separately from adjudication and payment: a clearinghouse receipt or 999 acknowledgment is not a promise to pay, and a 277CA acceptance still does not establish final reimbursement. Reconcile available 835 remittance advice or broker settlement reports against the invoice ledger, including adjustments and recoupments. Laboratory accounts commonly use purchase-order invoices instead of healthcare claims; do not force their courier charges through medical billing rails. Ask when the payment clock begins: receipt, acceptance, correction, or the next scheduled payment cycle. Net-30 language can produce substantially longer elapsed time when documents are rejected. Store invoice submission timestamps, portal receipts, and acceptance records. Those records resolve disputes about whether the collection problem is missing performance evidence, a payer processing delay, or genuine inability to pay. Assign a named owner to each exception rather than repeatedly resending the entire batch.

Use ACH as the default for recurring business-to-business settlements when the payer supports it, but verify enrollment through a known contact and reconcile bank deposits to remittance identifiers. ACH can return or reverse under applicable rules; a displayed credit is not the same as an irrevocable settlement commitment. Wire transfers suit large deposits or urgent prefunding, with callback verification for new instructions because recovery after fraudulent redirection is difficult. Never accept a changed beneficiary solely through an emailed invoice. Private laboratory buyers can fund a refundable service advance or capacity retainer if the agreement defines earned amounts, cancellation treatment, and replenishment. Distinguish a deposit liability from revenue earned through service. Medicaid transportation arrangements may prohibit side charges, so do not make covered passengers the substitute source of working capital. Apply the provider payment-in-full requirements of 42 CFR 447.15 to the relevant enrolled arrangement and check state-specific member-charge restrictions. Platform checkout is suitable only for an authorized private purchaser and a permitted service; disclose pricing and refund terms without exposing patient details to payment metadata. Keep banking access separate from dispatch permissions, require dual review for beneficiary changes, and match each settlement to the legal entity named in the underlying agreement.

Escrow, factoring, and milestone holds solve different problems and should never be presented as interchangeable guarantees of collection. A genuine independent escrow arrangement can secure a private route-launch deposit, but release conditions must reference objective deliverables and a funded account. Your ordinary operating account is not escrow merely because a spreadsheet labels it that way. Factoring accelerates eligible receivables after discount, reserves, and contractual conditions; recourse can return a disputed trip invoice to your balance sheet. Check restrictions on assignment, offset rights, and any public payment limitations before promising a factor access to collections. UCC Article 9 addresses receivables and security interests, but it does not erase program-specific rules or make a rejected claim collectible. Statutory disbursement means payment under an applicable public contract or program, not a universal faster rail. The federal Prompt Payment Act framework does not automatically give a subcontractor direct rights against the government. Milestone holds can protect a laboratory during route onboarding, yet broad holds let minor documentation disputes immobilize an entire month's cash. Negotiate acceptance deadlines, payment of undisputed amounts, and a bounded correction process. The comparison below shows where each mechanism belongs and which evidence should exist before dispatch capacity is committed.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Verify the contracting debtor
   - Match the signed agreement, purchasing authority, and remittance entity before accepting volume.
   - Record the authorization pathway, credit ceiling, permissible subcontracting, purchase-order balance, and verified accounts-payable contact.
2. Prove the submission pathway
   - Validate the payer's required invoice or claim format using a sample packet.
   - Use the relevant companion guide or supplier instructions; distinguish technical receipt, adjudication, acceptance, and scheduled payment.
3. Capture evidence during performance
   - Collect authorization references, GPS events, custody scans, and exception acknowledgments as work occurs.
   - Minimize patient information, restrict access, and link each service record to the exact invoice line without altering original event history.
4. Reconcile cash and exceptions
   - Allocate deposits using remittance detail and assign every deduction to an accountable reviewer.
   - Track appeal deadlines, return events, accepted balances, driver settlements, and any deposit liability until fully resolved.

## 3. Protect earned fees through precise deliverables and defensible service evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Fee protection begins with an exact purchased service, not an aggressive non-circumvention paragraph. Define the billable unit, included mileage or stops, waiting-time increments, cancellation windows, rescue obligations, and the person authorized to approve exceptions. State whether a round trip contains two payable legs and whether an unsuccessful pickup is compensable under the actual payer rules. Never bill a completed trip merely because the driver arrived. Use RouteGenie or MediRoutes event records to compare scheduled times with arrival, boarding where applicable, departure, and destination events; investigate implausible distances and overlapping assignments before submission. GPS is corroboration, not proof of every contractual condition. Keep original records and documented corrections rather than silently editing events to match a preferred invoice. For laboratories, distinguish package acceptance from specimen suitability: the receiving laboratory determines whether testing conditions were met. Define what happens when an incomplete requisition, leaking package, or receiving-site closure interrupts service. A usable agreement allocates the response, documentation, and permitted charge without inventing completed performance. An agreed exception ticket can preserve an earned pickup fee even when a laboratory rejects the specimen, but only if the contract permits that payment and the courier actually performed the billed obligation.

Protect introductions and coordination work with named accounts, an identified scope, and a reasonable payment mechanism that survives ordinary personnel changes. For a laboratory route introduction, a disclosed setup fee tied to documented implementation tasks is easier to substantiate than an indefinite percentage of every future specimen. If ongoing dispatch earns a recurring coordination charge, specify scheduling coverage, exception resolution, reporting, and acceptance criteria. A narrowly drafted account protection clause can address intentional bypass of an earned commercial fee, but enforceability depends on governing law and the actual relationship. Do not restrain a patient's choice, prevent emergency continuity, or impose a healthcare referral bounty. Public procurement terms may restrict contingent fees, subcontract arrangements, or assignment; review the applicable solicitation rather than copying a private-market clause. Keep your contractual fee separate from driver compensation and disclose authorized subcontracting to the buyer. The owner-operator agreement must reflect actual operational independence and applicable labor standards; a 1099 form alone does not determine classification. Where required control, exclusivity, scheduling, or equipment arrangements make the contractor structure unsuitable, use employment or a qualified carrier subcontract instead. Preserve the coordination deliverables so the fee remains supported even if the buyer later changes its transport provider.

Evidence quality must include privacy and handling controls because an unusable record cannot reliably support payment. Determine the HIPAA relationship for each workflow rather than assuming every courier is automatically a business associate. Dispatch access to protected health information may require a business associate agreement under 45 CFR 164.504(e), while a narrow conduit function requires separate analysis. Where the business associate relationship applies, execute the agreement and required downstream terms before exchanging patient data. Use minimum-necessary fields where applicable and role-based access; route instructions should not expose diagnoses that drivers do not need. For specimen logistics, barcode custody events should identify the package, accepting person, timestamp, location, and recorded condition without unnecessarily reproducing clinical contents. Apply 49 CFR 173.199 when shipping Category B infectious substances; not every clinical specimen belongs in that category. Confirm classification and packaging responsibilities with the shipper, including required UN3373 markings where applicable. OSHA's 29 CFR 1910.1030 governs covered occupational exposure, requiring an appropriate exposure-control program rather than a generic driver checklist. Link each invoice to permitted evidence while keeping detailed clinical or custody records in controlled systems. Contract retention periods, payer audit rules, and applicable law determine preservation, including records needed to contest later trip-log clawbacks.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before committing capacity**
> 
> Release a route only when purchasing authority, service eligibility, evidence readiness, and cash capacity align.
> - Verify the debtor's legal identity, delegated signer, active agreement, remaining purchase-order value, and independent callback contact.
> - Verify broker enrollment, permitted subcontracting, active trip authorization, qualified drivers, and the required vehicle or specimen-handling capability.
> - Verify the invoice packet, privacy arrangements, GPS or barcode evidence, exception workflow, and payer-specific submission deadline.
> - Verify available liquidity against the collection-delay stress case, driver payment commitments, concentration limits, and a documented continuity plan.

## 4. Underwrite counterparty credit and reconcile contribution before expanding volume  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Screen the entity that actually owes your invoice, not the recognizable healthcare brand on its website. Confirm legal name, registration, physical operations, tax documentation, and bank-account ownership through appropriate business verification. For government-facing primes, review SAM.gov registration and exclusions where relevant, contract scope, and documented award authority; registration alone proves neither solvency nor available funding. For private laboratories and intermediaries, request trade references, recent payment experience, and proportionate credit information. A broker can have a major payer contract and still delay subcontractor settlements. Read offset, recoupment, assignment, termination, and dispute provisions before setting a credit ceiling. Track exposure as unpaid invoices plus performed unbilled work plus committed service costs, net of usable customer funds. Separate accepted receivables from disputed balances so apparent growth does not hide weak collections. Review aging by debtor and service line, not only consolidated days outstanding. Set escalation thresholds when a promised payment date slips, authorizations repeatedly fail, or deductions change without explanation. Increase private-client deposits or reduce new discretionary commitments where permitted. Existing patient obligations require an orderly, contract-compliant continuity plan rather than an abrupt cutoff. Credit discipline means controlling new exposure early, while there is still time to arrange replacement capacity and resolve disputed documents.
