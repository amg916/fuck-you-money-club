## 1. The Practitioner Consensus: Contracted Routes, Clean Paperwork, Relentless Follow-Up

The operators converge on one structural claim: medical courier money comes from contracted routes, not from chasing app pings. Giganomic Era frames it as running a route rather than chasing orders, the same pickup and the same drop-off at the same time every single week, and argues these shippers struggle to find reliable contractors because most drivers do not know the lane exists [VOBDHU3FYtA @ 00:00:24]. The same channel treats the barrier to entry as protection: the DoorDash driver cannot even get in the door on stem cell and radiopharmaceutical freight [VOBDHU3FYtA @ 00:01:28]. Lavish Life goes further, stating they have never used apps and never will, because app work is not run under your business, the pay is inconsistent, and the apps push 20 to 30 plus stops a day for a little bit of pay; they do not even let their own employees run that many stops [_GYJDRIHqL4 @ 00:00:00]. Daeron Myers drives the same point through the company website itself, telling drivers to skip the employment openings and click vendor opportunities, because being an employee is what 99% of the world already knows how to do and the goal is the 1% [DMJuDqVtjtU @ 00:02:55]. The shared posture is to treat each courier company as a client relationship, not a gig login.

Baseline paperwork is nearly identical from company to company. Giganomic Era lists On Track Transport's bar as 21 or older, a valid license, a clean driving record for the past three years, a reliable insured vehicle and the ability to lift up to 50 pounds, with HIPAA and handling training supplied during onboarding [7k3sVGSbmRA @ 00:01:39]. Side Hustle 247 reads the Strategic Delivery Solutions checklist: 21 or older, three years of U.S. driving experience, own your vehicle, 100/300K insurance minimums, an LLC where the state requires one, plus a criminal background check and drug screen, all of which they call standard stuff [WVwEyiww470 @ 00:03:39]. For Diligent, Giganomic Era stresses that the HIPAA requirement is two free online courses, privacy and security, a few hours of time rather than a license, and that being HIPAA trained separates a driver from most gig workers, opens facilities others cannot touch and lets them charge more [VOBDHU3FYtA @ 00:05:07]. Vehicle size then sets the ceiling. Diamond Hunter Enterprises prices a four-door sedan at a $200 daily minimum, cargo vans and sprinters at $400 to $500, and box trucks at $800 to $1,200 per day, with stat routes paying more [kDbf0ylOUNw @ 00:01:00]. The sedan gets a driver in the door; the bigger box gets the bigger check.

The third point of agreement is that the application form is the start of the work, not the end of it. Giganomic Era says to follow every form with a short professional email stating you are an independent contractor operating in their market, HIPAA aware and ready to discuss route availability, then get the dispatcher's or regional manager's name, use it, and follow up every five to seven days, which is how applied turns into starting Monday [VOBDHU3FYtA @ 00:06:38]. Boss up with Kae tells drivers to ask for the hiring manager, driver manager or driver coordinator by title, and to mine the company's LinkedIn to find who is actually in charge [6vnVGK7rSFQ @ 00:01:36]. Sarah Davis says a company that is not hiring in your area should never be crossed off: call, introduce yourself or your business, give your area and vehicle, ask to partner when routes open, and keep following up, because the opportunity may simply not be posted yet [XX29XpCCZO4 @ 00:20:14]. The flip side is silence. Giganomic Era warns that when nobody calls back after a medical courier application, the company often just does not have available routes in that market [7k3sVGSbmRA @ 00:04:03]. Silence is a market signal rather than a rejection, and the operator answer to it is another call.

## 2. Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Advertised rates cluster tightly by vehicle and route type, and the operators read them as ceilings rather than promises. Associated Couriers pays by miles plus stops instead of a flat rate; Eann shows one medical listing at 87 or 89 cents per mile and notes the rate changes by location [tidUl8-lql8 @ 00:05:28]. On the heavier end, Sarah Davis lists Capstone Logistics in Duncansville, Pennsylvania at $400 to $480 a day for extended cargo vans, sprinters and 10 to 16-foot box trucks, on Monday through Friday local routes that start between 4:00 and 7:00 a.m. and run 8 to 11 hours, home daily with weekly pay [XX29XpCCZO4 @ 00:08:37]. The app layer pays less but fills dead time between contracted work. Giganomic Era ranks Dispatch as the best app for rates, consistency and fairness where it operates, reporting $1,000 to $1,500 weeks from a cargo van and $700 to $900 from an SUV, while framing it as a commercial platform rather than a guaranteed medical gig [FzfSZhrc8rM @ 00:04:52].

The scaling mechanics run through vendor and broker channels rather than driver portals. **SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Match vehicle and insurance to the listing
   - Filter openings by the vehicle, insurance limits, location and availability you already have before applying anywhere.
   - Giganomic Era warns a driver can qualify without medical experience and still get turned down because the vehicle fails age requirements or the insurance limits are too low, so target listings that match what you run [Giganomic Era — 7k3sVGSbmRA @ 00:05:27].
2. Stack the credentials that separate you
   - Complete the free HIPAA privacy and security courses and set up an EIN and LLC before submitting applications.
   - Giganomic Era calls HIPAA training a few hours of free online modules that open facilities other drivers cannot touch [Giganomic Era — VOBDHU3FYtA @ 00:05:07], and Kae says an EIN is optional on the Spoke Logistics form but puts you ahead of drivers who lack one [Boss up with Kae — 6vnVGK7rSFQ @ 00:02:35].
3. Submit complete applications with a sharp narrative
   - Fill every field, check every availability day, and write a two to three sentence experience narrative.
   - Diligent only contacts applicants who complete the full form, so do it right the first time [Giganomic Era — VOBDHU3FYtA @ 00:05:53]; Diamond Hunter Enterprises uses ChatGPT to tighten a two to three sentence narrative on work experience and availability before hitting apply [Diamond Hunter Enterprises — kDbf0ylOUNw @ 00:02:46].
4. Follow up by name, then price the offer like a business
   - Email and call the dispatcher or regional manager every five to seven days, then judge any offer on profit, not revenue.
   - Follow-up cadence comes from Giganomic Era [Giganomic Era — VOBDHU3FYtA @ 00:06:38]; before accepting, Sarah Davis checks mileage, schedule, stop count, paid versus deadhead miles, fuel, insurance and operating costs because revenue is not profit [Sarah Davis | The D&G Way — XX29XpCCZO4 @ 00:23:09].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split runs between operators who refuse apps outright and operators who treat them as a paid on-ramp. Lavish Life says flatly, "I don't use apps. Never have, never will," because app work is not under your business, pay is inconsistent, and apps "will have you doing 20 to 30 plus stops a day for a little bit of pay" [_GYJDRIHqL4 @ 00:00:18]. That operator does not even let employees run that many stops. Giganomic Era takes the opposite line: Roadie is "the easiest app to get on with," roughly a 90% shot using what you already have and an excellent gap filler, while Dispatch has paid them $1,000 to $1,500 a week in a cargo van and $700 to $900 in an SUV, ranked best for rates, consistency, and fairness [FzfSZhrc8rM @ 00:03:12]. No Boss Blueprint sits in the middle: pharmacy apps pay more than Uber Eats and DoorDash, but deliveries come few and far between, so keep more than one app loaded [99Brd_naNps @ 00:00:12]. Daeron Myers sequences it instead of choosing: apps first so there is money on the phone, then local contracts in your city, then subcontracting national contracts without physically doing the work [DMJuDqVtjtU @ 00:09:43].

Background policy is where the advice openly collides. Diamond Hunter Enterprises pitches EKW Transport in Chicago as a second-chance medical courier opportunity for people with a felony background, and tells anyone with a felony to drop what they are doing and apply to American Logistics Authority [rPBtFcgIPmQ @ 00:13:04]. Sarah Davis | The D&G Way points to L&D Logistics in Houston actively encouraging applicants with criminal records, and says that when a listing is silent you should inquire and be honest, because a record "doesn't automatically disqualify you" [XX29XpCCZO4 @ 00:12:16]. Boss up with Kae pushes back hard: "The medical courier industry is heavily vetted," a sketchy background blocks a TWIC card, and those applicants get steered toward auto parts and other non-medical deliveries instead [6vnVGK7rSFQ @ 00:04:54]. The same split hits paperwork. Diamond Hunter Enterprises says you must have an LLC, C Corp, or S Corp to submit the Accurate Courier Services contact form, while Side Hustle 247 reads the Strategic Delivery Solutions requirement and notes that in Georgia you do not have to have an LLC, though there is nothing wrong with it and it protects you [WVwEyiww470 @ 00:03:59]. That same listing still demands a criminal background check and drug screen, which Side Hustle 247 calls standard stuff.

Operators also split on how fast to scale past one seat. Sarah Davis | The D&G Way says level two subcontracting is always the best place to start for the completely new, but flags AME Pharmacy, advertised at $850 to $1,500 a week, as potentially worth more than higher-paying listings because with three drivers it could reach $10,000 to $18,000 a month in revenue [XX29XpCCZO4 @ 00:07:53]. Daeron Myers skips the solo stage on Lab Express vendor work: a Boston contract demanding seven to 10 drivers across 22 routes averaging $8,000 a week, chased from Baltimore because "even if we're not in that market, we can get the business" [DMJuDqVtjtU @ 00:04:59]. On credentials, Boss up with Kae says carrying HIPAA and bloodborne pathogens certifications, commercial insurance, and a TWIC card lets you jump the line on everyone who has to go get them after the video [6vnVGK7rSFQ @ 00:10:03]. Giganomic Era counters that some companies provide training during onboarding, so check whether a company requires certifications before applying or supplies them after acceptance before paying for multiple certifications [7k3sVGSbmRA @ 00:06:56]. The fork is real: front-load credentials to win priority, or let the contracting company carry the training.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure on record is a disclosure failure. Diamond Hunter Enterprises recounts a student who lost a multi-million dollar federal courier contract at Fort Sam Houston because the student was not truthful during the background disclosure process the speaker calls a UAR, and warns anyone who has failed a background check or carries a felony not to repeat that mistake [rPBtFcgIPmQ @ 00:02:15]. The second trap is confusing approval with work. Giganomic Era warns that some companies let you fill out an entire application without telling you they are not accepting drivers in your area, and that silence after applying often just means no available routes in your market [7k3sVGSbmRA @ 00:04:03]. On apps the same channel says applying and being approved are not the same as receiving consistent work, and five approved apps can still leave you struggling [FzfSZhrc8rM @ 00:09:20]. Side Hustle 247 adds the timeline: the first few weeks normally do not pay much unless you get stuck right into a route, but people running routes in that same area were making $250 to $350 a day [WVwEyiww470 @ 00:07:27]. Judge a company further down the road, not on the first few weeks.

Rate math is where new operators bleed. Lavish Life says dispatchers, brokers, and companies who see you lack knowledge of rates, pay, and mileage are going to lowball you, sets a floor of over $600 for 400 miles out and 400 back before diesel, and reminds operators that cargo van breakdowns and driver payroll still hit regardless [_GYJDRIHqL4 @ 00:03:32]. Sarah Davis | The D&G Way insists an advertised weekly number means nothing until you check mileage, stops, deadhead, fuel, insurance, and operating expenses, because "revenue is not the same thing as profit" [XX29XpCCZO4 @ 00:23:30]. Giganomic Era runs the same check per order: a 30-minute wait at pickup counts, and a $40 delivery that works in a sedan may not make sense in a cargo van burning fuel for an hour or two [FzfSZhrc8rM @ 00:06:44]. No Boss Blueprint shows the gig-food version: an $18 order whose $6 tip gets pulled for cold food drops to $12 for the hour and about $9 after gas and miles, which is why they block out a full hour for every delivery regardless of the estimate [99Brd_naNps @ 00:06:03]. Medical deliveries pay base only, with no tips to lose.

Listings themselves hide traps. Boss up with Kae sent a US Pack listing in Macon, Georgia to a buddy who got skeptical because it showed $0 and 0 miles; the read is a negotiable package of two or three routes where the company is probably trying to lowball you at first, so always negotiate a fuel surcharge that bumps pay when gas tops $4 [6vnVGK7rSFQ @ 00:09:14]. Giganomic Era warns you can qualify with no experience and still get turned down because your vehicle misses age rules or your liability limits fall short, with MDS demanding the 100/300K minimum, so confirm with your insurance agent whether paid delivery needs a business-use endorsement or commercial policy [7k3sVGSbmRA @ 00:05:27]. Ello Hunny Bun flags vague pay as a red flag and warns that fake postings and scam emails now appear even on Indeed and ZipRecruiter, so look up the sender address, the company number, and reviews [wv1CfFvEa7I @ 00:03:35]. No Boss Blueprint adds that Roadie gigs are not guaranteed: you choose a job, then wait to see if you were accepted, and Pills To Me background checks can take a few days to a week [99Brd_naNps @ 00:04:08].
