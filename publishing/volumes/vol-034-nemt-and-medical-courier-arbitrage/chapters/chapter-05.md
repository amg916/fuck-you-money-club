## Executive Summary

Medical courier pay is a ceiling-versus-floor game. GoShare advertises car work at up to $45 an hour, but drivers juggling six companies report $300 days only when everything lines up, and Dean Medical Courier Academy's $3,500 weeks came only during driver shortages and bad weather. The operators who clear real money price routes themselves: Giganomic Era builds a $175 flat daily rate from time, 70-cent mileage, and a 25% markup, charges $65-plus stat runs, and converts steady clients into a $2,300 monthly retainer. Sarah Davis and Dj Mo refuse to judge any rate without miles, hours, and volume attached. The split comes on structure: some stack app gigs and bids, while others insist the money lives in stacked direct contracts, subcontractor rights, and relationships that never hit a job board.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The first thing every serious operator here agrees on: the advertised number is a ceiling, not a paycheck. Samuel B. walked through GoShare's published rates of up to $45 an hour for a car, $70 for a pickup, $105 for a cargo van, and $168 for a box truck, then pointed out that the fine print says 'up to' and that pay varies job to job [X99grw6uMuY @ 00:04:16]. The real drivers he quoted were blunter: one working six companies full-time said $2,500 a week is almost impossible and $300 a day only happens when everything lines up, while another clears $300 a day after expenses only by multi-apping 12 to 14 hours [X99grw6uMuY @ 00:05:02]. Bruce of Dean Medical Courier Academy has personally hit $3,500 in a week, but only because the carrier was short on drivers and the weather was bad, and Bruce says you cannot build a business on extenuating circumstances [q25UyvVRKew @ 00:04:37]. Shawn in Town replayed a TikTok newcomer who chased the '$700 to $1,000 a week' pitch onto Senpex and landed a $20 air-conditioner delivery that cost about $20 to handle; Shawn's verdict was that the hype skipped the part about stacking contracts with real medical companies [LqGuhE6C6_g @ 00:02:18].

Second point of consensus: a rate quoted in isolation tells you nothing. Sarah Davis gets asked constantly whether $2 a package, $1.25 a mile, $250 a route, or $1,000 a week is good, and the answer is always 'it depends' until you know the miles, hours, stops, and volume [hxoZsGym9DU @ 00:00:21]. Her side-by-side math is the cleanest out there: a $250 route that runs 80 miles in 4 hours can beat a $350 route that eats 250 miles and 10 to 12 hours, and $2 a package across 200 daily packages is $400 while $5 a package across 15 packages is $75 [hxoZsGym9DU @ 00:09:27]. Dj Mo refuses to publish a per-mile number at all because every contract is negotiated and the variables differ by vehicle, tires, and miles, and warns that companies throw '$1,200 a week' at newcomers without mentioning the wear and tear [twmmoYWAs5Y @ 00:05:15]. Corey at Giganomic Era lands in the same place from the client side: pharmacies are buying reliability, not 12 miles of driving, and five courier companies will give five different quotes for the exact same request because their costs, insurance, and service levels differ [zKn-xcqMKhg @ 00:01:34]. The shared rule: profit, not revenue, decides.

Third consensus: the money sits in stacked, direct, and recurring work, not in whatever an app pushes at you. Shawn in Town tells newcomers never to 'try' medical courier; you commit to the hours, stack contracts, and learn the business long-term [LqGuhE6C6_g @ 00:06:32]. Bruce at Dean Medical Courier Academy says they have never run with fewer than two contracts outside their first three or four months, and that many routes never hit Indeed or Google because carriers hire subs from within and now let drivers bring their own [ZJagcSpBYaQ @ 00:06:24]. Surereal Cooper went from one contract to three within the first two weeks of driving independently, mixing a $4-a-stop prescription contract with lab and Red Cross blood runs dispatched from home [kFqNxDvajsU @ 00:00:22]. Corey at Giganomic Era frames the end state: one pharmacy that calls consistently can be worth more than dozens of random deliveries, and once a client trusts you, the conversation moves to scheduled routes, overflow coverage, backup courier service, and stat work [J9XCEoIfTU4 @ 00:05:55]. Every one of these operators treats a single delivery as an audition for a client relationship.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The cleanest pricing formula comes from Corey at Giganomic Era's flat daily route model for fixed, same-stops, same-time work: estimated time plus mileage at the 70-cent IRS standard rate plus your desired hourly rate sets the floor, then a 20 to 30 percent markup sets the price [EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:01:05]. The worked example is a 2.5-hour, 45-mile morning lab route: $31.50 mileage plus $100 of time at $40 an hour is $131.50, a 25 percent markup lands at $164, and Corey rounds to a clean $175 per service day pitched as 'no hidden fees and no fuel surcharges' [EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:01:40]. Compare that to what carriers actually hand out. Bruce's IDEXX veterinary route for Delivery Solutions of America starts at 11:00, runs about 12 clinic stops in roughly an hour and 45 minutes to two hours, and pays $90 a day; a Saturday US Pack grocery run paid $163 for just over four hours [dhyibOQPAlA @ 00:01:31]. Side Hustle 247 found a part-time Stat Courier evening route posted at $220 to $230 per day, 1099, Monday through Friday from about 4 p.m., with a backup driver expected, and notes route time shrinks from eight hours toward six as you learn the stops [NjZBEatuBlc @ 00:05:18].

Unscheduled and per-piece work gets priced differently. Corey's stat model is a $65 minimum per run plus $1 per mile beyond the first 10 miles, quoted with a 30-minute to one-hour response window Monday through Friday, and stat availability is bundled into route deals as a value-add so the client never shops for a backup [EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:02:33]. With a Bronx pharmacy, Corey charges $45 per pickup and delivery within 20 miles but drops to $35 each when the pharmacy hands over several at once, because three orders on one pickup is $105 without three trips back to the counter [J9XCEoIfTU4 @ 00:01:01]. The guardrail: per-delivery pricing only fits predictable work, and anything involving waiting, unknown stops, or open-ended availability shifts to hourly, route, mileage, or waiting-time charges so a $35 delivery never eats two to three hours [J9XCEoIfTU4 @ 00:03:45]. Carrier-side, Dj Mo's own invoices show $11 flat add-ons for multi-stops under 10 miles, so a two-mile hop still pays the full amount and three of them stacked to $33; a single 234-mile run paid $210.60, and the week of February 28 through March 4 totaled $891.60, with Dj Mo daring viewers to work out the per-mile figure themselves [Wc0Dykqpw-Q @ 00:04:52].

Once a client is steady, Corey upgrades to a monthly retainer after 60 to 90 days of proven service: estimate their monthly use, multiply by your daily rate, and give back 10 to 15 percent for the commitment. A client using 15 days a month at $175 is $2,625, so Corey offers $2,300, invoiced on the 1st and paid by the 15th, covering 'up to 18 service days' with anything beyond billed at the standard daily rate to block scope creep [EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:03:50]. The negotiation sequence: send the service agreement within 24 hours of the first successful run, name your price first so a $150 counter gets negotiated down from $175 instead of up from zero, refuse to match a predecessor's $120 a day, and close on a start date rather than a yes-or-no [EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:04:57]. Bruce's new carrier docks roughly $50 a week before the wheels turn: a $6.30 administrative fee, $5 tech data, $15.85 occupational accidental insurance, $4.65 cargo insurance, and $25 commercial auto liability [q25UyvVRKew @ 00:03:50]. Gilsar Vergara says to track spending through apps, budget trackers, or saved receipts each pay period and wall that amount off as operating costs; Gilsar needed another oil change two weeks after the last one [_1Xl9_H-PX8 @ 00:06:02].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Know your cost per mile
   - Calculate fill-up cost, miles per gallon, cost per mile, and daily miles driven.
   - At a minimum, know what it costs to fill your vehicle, your miles per gallon, your estimated cost per mile, and what it costs to complete each contract [Sarah Davis | The D&G Way — hxoZsGym9DU @ 00:11:47]
2. Build the floor and mark it up
   - Add route time at your hourly rate to mileage at 70 cents, then mark up 20 to 30 percent.
   - A 2.5-hour, 45-mile route gives $31.50 mileage plus $100 time, $131.50 floor, $164 at 25 percent, rounded to $175 per day [Giganomic Era — EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:01:19]
3. Interrogate the offer before quoting
   - Ask deliveries, stop distance, miles, time, recurrence, and total contract value.
   - Run the numbers on the client's existing structure first; if their $30 per delivery or flat route rate is profitable, roll with it instead of reinventing it [Giganomic Era — J9XCEoIfTU4 @ 00:02:38]
4. Anchor high and close on a start date
   - Name your number first, defend it on reliability, and ask when to start.
   - Send the agreement within 24 hours, counter a lower predecessor rate with on-time routes, insurance, and current HIPAA, then say 'I can have this in place by Monday' [Giganomic Era — EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:05:26]

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The second split is where the work comes from. Overhead and credentials are a live dispute too. ## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The fastest way to lose money is a low-paying first job taken on hype. Shawn in Town replays a TikTok creator who got approved on Senpex within two days, hauled an air conditioner the customer already owned, and called it 'a $20 job that's costing me $20 to even handle'; Shawn's diagnosis is that the creator never stacked contracts or got with a real medical company [LqGuhE6C6_g @ 00:02:18]. LearnCargo describes the carrier-side version: newcomers get long runs like 30 miles for $11 or $9 and are told to work up the ladder before earning a set schedule, which LearnCargo calls losing all the way around the board [9AYrPh0PlLE @ 00:00:59]. LearnCargo also watched drivers take a 1 p.m. hospital load of 38 drop-offs and finish near midnight zigzagging between neighborhoods, when sorting packages into zip-code piles before leaving would have collapsed the route [9AYrPh0PlLE @ 00:01:51]. Pedro Santiago adds the app-side leak: Pedro accepted 15 deliveries, the pharmacy only had 14 ready, the cancellation trimmed the payout by about $10, and Roadie warns that a delivery mistake can get your account suspended and your pay withheld [BhO0ZqkVHFk @ 00:12:22]. Every one of these losses traces back to accepting the job before checking distance, sequencing, and stop count.

Gross numbers hide the cost side, and the veterans show the receipts. Samuel B. runs the arithmetic plainly: $40 an hour with $8 an hour of gas is really $32, before registration, insurance, and maintenance, and some couriers are putting 60,000 miles a year on a car [X99grw6uMuY @ 00:03:30]. Bruce's $8,700 January with US Pack, SDS Rx, and Via took an average of 10 hours a day in the vehicle, and the mileage forced a jump from one oil change a month to two using high-mileage oil, a couple hundred dollars monthly [q25UyvVRKew @ 00:03:07]. Bruce's $3,500 weeks only happened when drivers were short and the weather was bad, and Bruce refuses to build a business on extenuating circumstances. Gilsar Vergara's hospital double ran from a 5:30 a.m. wake-up to a 3:30 a.m. finish the next day, needed an oil change two weeks after the previous one, and Gilsar warns that sleep deprivation in this work can be fatal [_1Xl9_H-PX8 @ 00:08:00]. Surereal Cooper's Norcross prescription contract starts 37 miles from home, demands a 6 a.m. load sort, and runs 200 to 300-plus miles a day, burning a full tank on that single contract [kFqNxDvajsU @ 00:01:09]. Revenue that ignores these line items is fiction.

The contract and route-math traps are where even experienced couriers get burned. Corey at Giganomic Era flags three clauses to reject in hospital and lab paperwork: an exclusive service clause unless they pay a premium, unlimited liability for specimen loss regardless of cause, and at-will termination with no notice, where Corey pushes for 30 days minimum [EgbN0cCrzpk @ 00:06:05]. Corey also warns that a per-delivery rate on unpredictable work can lock you into a $35 job that consumes two to three hours, so waiting, unknown stops, and out-of-area driving belong on hourly, route, mileage, or wait-time pricing instead [J9XCEoIfTU4 @ 00:03:45]. Sarah Davis shows how headline rates deceive: a $350 route covering 250 miles and 10 to 12 hours can lose to a $250 route of 80 miles and 4 hours, and $5 per package on 15 packages is $75 while $2 per package on 200 packages is $400 [hxoZsGym9DU @ 00:09:27]. Dj Mo's own invoice week shows what real settlements look like: $891.60 across February 28 through March 4, including a 234-mile day paying $210.60 and $11 flat multi-stops for anything under 10 miles [Wc0Dykqpw-Q @ 00:05:59]. Read the clauses first, then run the miles against the money.
