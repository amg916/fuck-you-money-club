## 1. Days 1–7: Select One Operating Wedge and Price the Cash Exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a bounded service territory, one buyer class, and one dispatch window. Candidate #34 supports nine mapped operator plays; this chapter labels them P1 recurring Medicaid NEMT standing orders, P2 scheduled discharge NEMT, P3 workers' compensation appointment transport, P4 recurring laboratory specimen routes, P5 STAT specimen pickups, P6 broker overflow coverage, P7 consolidated rural NEMT routes, P8 after-hours laboratory coverage, and P9 contracted replacement-driver recovery. These are operating lanes within the candidate, not nine businesses to launch simultaneously. P9 means replacing failed contracted transport capacity, never emergency medical response. Select P1 or P4 as the initial wedge only after buyer interviews establish credentialing requirements, route density, and payable evidence. Keep the remaining plays in a scored backlog. Map P2, P3, P6, and P7 to the passenger workflow; map P5 and P8 to the specimen workflow. Both workflows may use P9, but passenger vehicles and specimen packaging are never treated as interchangeable resources. Assign FT1 to buyer development, contracts, and cash; assign FT2 to dispatch, evidence, and collections. C1 supplies qualified operating capacity and C2 supplies independent backup capacity under properly classified arrangements. The four-person pod has no spare overnight desk.

On Day 1, FT1 builds twenty named buyer accounts using state Medicaid transportation directories, broker contracting portals, hospital discharge departments, workers' compensation administrators, and laboratory outreach teams. By Day 3, complete five discovery conversations and capture the actual purchaser, covered geography, settlement terms, cancellation rules, and required transport evidence. A discharge coordinator's interest is not a payer contract. A laboratory's CLIA certificate under 42 CFR Part 493 does not credential your courier or authorize testing. For Medicaid work, verify the state's transportation model and the broker's enrollment instructions before quoting a start date; 42 CFR 431.53 establishes the state transportation assurance, not universal acceptance of a new vendor. For P3, identify the authorizing payer and applicable state workers' compensation transport rules rather than copying Medicaid rates. Record wheelchair capability, passenger assistance boundaries, loading time, specimen temperature requirements, and receiver hours as separate fields. By Day 7, choose a lane only if two buyers confirm a specific recurring need and one supplies a written onboarding checklist. Otherwise continue discovery without software upgrades, vehicle purchases, or an unsupported promise of immediate service. Preserve interview notes without collecting patient identifiers that the sales process does not require.

Build the cash sheet before buying dispatch software. The canonical startup capital range is $15,000–$65,000, monthly core operating expense is $3,500–$18,000, and first-deal timing is two to five months. Set this sprint's unrecovered cash-outflow ceiling at the lesser of $12,000 or 40% of committed startup cash. Thus a $15,000 launch permits only $6,000 of unrecovered outflow during the first thirty days; a $65,000 launch still caps it at $12,000. Include payroll, insurance deposits, software, training, legal review, and driver settlements less collected receipts in that measure. Existing unpaid commitments also consume the ceiling. Do not solve a cash shortfall by treating employee labor as free or delaying agreed contractor payments. Separately reserve sixty days of forecast settlements plus thirty days of other cash expenses before adding live volume. At Base annualized billings, monthly driver settlements equal $42,395.83, so sixty days alone requires approximately $84,791.67 under a thirty-day planning convention. That exceeds the startup capital ceiling before overhead. Base therefore describes a mature run rate, not a funded first-month launch. Negotiate shorter collections, obtain committed working capital, or start with materially smaller route volume.

## 2. Days 8–14: Credential Capacity and Rehearse the Evidence Chain  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 8–10 convert the selected wedge into a documented eligibility file. For each owner-operator, obtain the credentials actually required by the state, payer, service class, and contract: operating authority where applicable, license, vehicle inspection, insurance endorsements, background screening, training, and exclusion checks when required. Verify coverage with the producer rather than assuming a certificate proves coverage for every proposed activity. Passenger assistance and wheelchair securement require demonstrated competence when offered; specimen transport requires the packaging and handling competence appropriate to the material. Use the federal economic-realities framework under the Fair Labor Standards Act and applicable state tests when reviewing contractor status. A contract label does not override actual working conditions. California Labor Code section 2775, for example, creates an ABC framework subject to statutory exceptions that require specific analysis. If the planned relationship fails the applicable test, use employment or a properly structured qualified carrier arrangement and reprice the lane. Do not preserve the 55% driver-settlement assumption by ignoring payroll obligations. C1 and C2 must be genuine usable capacity, not names on a spreadsheet, and neither should be assigned simultaneous passenger and specimen commitments that cannot both be met.

Configure RouteGenie or MediRoutes only after checking the selected vendor's current functions, contract, access controls, export options, and handling of protected health information. Execute HIPAA business associate agreements where the relationship requires them under 45 CFR 164.502(e) and 164.504(e); do not assume every courier relationship has identical status. Apply minimum-necessary access under 45 CFR 164.502(b) where applicable. FT2 needs dispatch details, not unrestricted clinical records. Use individual accounts, prompt access removal, device locks, and documented evidence retention based on payer, privacy, and state requirements. GPS verifies location, but location alone does not establish eligible transport or a valid claim. Link authorization, service date, origin, destination, timestamps, qualified vehicle, required signature, and exception reason to one trip identifier. For specimens, link collection identifier, pickup, custody transfers, temperature evidence when required, and receiver acceptance without putting diagnostic details into ordinary text messages. Test records must be synthetic or properly de-identified. A barcode scan demonstrates a recorded event, not specimen integrity by itself. The receiver's acceptance and the ordered handling conditions complete the evidence chain. Export a complete sample packet before relying on any dashboard.

Days 11–14 are rehearsal days unless contracts, credentials, and operating controls already permit live work. Run twenty synthetic events across ordinary service, late arrival, cancellation, unreachable passenger, rejected specimen, and replacement-driver scenarios. For passenger work, rehearse the broker's escalation window and prohibit backfilled signatures or invented timestamps. For specimens, obtain the shipper's classification and packaging instructions; Biological substance, Category B shipments use 49 CFR 173.199 when applicable, while Category A material requires a different transport framework. Never relabel material to fit the available vehicle or training. Determine hazardous-materials employee training duties under 49 CFR 172.704 based on actual functions. OSHA's bloodborne-pathogens rule, 29 CFR 1910.1030, applies to covered employees with occupational exposure and drives exposure controls, training, and response planning. Record who supplies compliant packaging and who rejects a damaged package before pickup. Test C2's backup response without creating unpaid standby expectations that contradict the chosen working relationship. Require twenty complete synthetic evidence packets, zero unresolved critical handling defects, and a timed failure-response drill. Rehearsal success proves workflow readiness only; it does not prove market demand, collection reliability, or permission to carry passengers and specimens.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Validate the service order
   - Match purchaser authorization to the contracted lane before dispatch.
   - Check service class, covered geography, timing, required assistance or handling, and the party responsible for payment.
2. Release qualified operating capacity
   - Assign an eligible driver and vehicle with an available backup path.
   - Check current credentials, coverage, equipment, workload, and acceptance of the specific service before sharing necessary details.
3. Capture the complete service event
   - Record required timestamps, signatures, custody transfers, and exceptions.
   - Escalate delays and rejected handoffs under the buyer's written rules; preserve original records and label later corrections.
4. Reconcile evidence, invoice, and settlement
   - Compare the payable packet with the driver settlement and buyer submission.
   - Route defects for correction, record submission acknowledgment, and forecast cash using contractual due dates rather than invoice optimism.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Any Live Passenger or Specimen Movement**
> 
> A commercial opportunity becomes dispatchable only when all four checks pass for the specific service, driver, vehicle, and payer.
> - Confirm written service authority, active required credentials, service-specific insurance, and a valid purchaser authorization; sales enthusiasm cannot substitute for any item.
> - Confirm qualified primary and replacement capacity, applicable worker classification, and a duty schedule that does not exceed the pod's covered service window.
> - Confirm applicable HIPAA agreements, restricted data access, complete trip or custody fields, and specimen packaging responsibilities where relevant.
> - Confirm positive quoted contribution, agreed contractor settlement dates, and funded cash coverage for sixty days of settlements plus thirty days of other expenses.

## 3. Days 15–21: Run a Capped Pilot and Reconcile Every Dollar  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Release a pilot during Days 15–21 only when the four pre-flight checks pass; otherwise perform the same steps as controlled rehearsals and buyer onboarding work. The canonical two-to-five-month first-deal window makes a rehearsal-only first month entirely plausible. Cap an authorized pilot at ten completed service events per day, one active route per available driver, and the smaller of contracted capacity or funded settlement capacity. This is an illustrative starting limit, not a statement that every route can support ten events. Define an event as one contracted one-way passenger leg or one separately billable specimen movement; do not inflate reliability denominators with each barcode scan. Record miles, paid time, waiting, cancellations, invoice value, settlement obligation, and direct exception cost. FT2 reviews every evidence packet before submission, while FT1 compares the service to the purchaser's authorization. For Medicaid claims, use 837P only when the payer or broker actually requires that transaction and the operator is eligible to submit it. Other arrangements use broker portals, trip files, or commercial invoices. An accepted clearinghouse file is not an adjudicated claim, and an adjudicated claim is not collected cash.

Keep the scorecard bridge visible beside the pilot ledger. Conservative annual gross contracted billings are $250,000, driver settlements are $137,500, and the 45% retained contribution is $112,500. Other modeled costs of $82,500 leave $30,000 owner profit: 12% of gross billings and 26.67% of retained contribution. Base gross billings are $925,000, settlements are $508,750, and retained contribution is $416,250. Other modeled costs of $249,750 leave $166,500 owner profit: 18.0% of gross and 40.0% of retained contribution. Scaled gross billings are $1,600,000, settlements are $880,000, and retained contribution is $720,000. Other modeled costs of $336,000 leave $384,000 owner profit: 24% of gross and 53.33% of retained contribution. Conservative and Base use 2 FT + 2 C; Scaled requires 3 FT + 3 C. The stated monthly core expense range does not replace these all-in annual cost totals. Under the scorecard's ASC 606 agency presentation, retained amounts represent operator revenue; actual principal-versus-agent presentation requires contract-specific assessment. Patient healthcare spending, laboratory testing revenue, and insurance claim value are never dispatch revenue. Publish gross billings and retained contribution as separate ledger measures.

Use a $100 payable-event example to expose fragile pricing: the canonical settlement allocation is $55 and retained dispatch contribution is $45 before other costs. At Base economics, allocating $27 of other modeled cost leaves $18 owner profit per $100 of gross billings. That allocation is a portfolio ratio, not proof that a particular rural trip earns $18. An unreimbursed $20 exception would erase that illustrated profit. Track exceptions against their originating lane instead of burying them in general dispatch expense. Count on-time performance against the contractual pickup window, and count STAT performance against the separately agreed priority window. Record customer cancellations separately from operator failures, while retaining both in the operational log. Measure first-pass packet acceptance only for reviewed or acknowledged submissions; pending files cannot enter the numerator. Maintain a daily receivables aging report with submission date, acceptance date, contractual due date, disputes, and receipt date. During the pilot, review every rejected packet within one business day and prohibit automated resubmission without correcting the underlying defect. RouteGenie or MediRoutes can support dispatch discipline, but neither software selection nor an EDI scrubber transfers responsibility for accurate service evidence.

## 4. Days 22–30: Close the Evidence Pack and Apply Day-30/60/90 Decisions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

During Days 22–30, consolidate the operating record rather than opening all nine plays. FT1 closes the cash ledger and obtains written next steps from buyers; FT2 closes defects and documents dispatch workload. P1 can expand into P6 only when overflow windows fit existing coverage. P4 can extend into P5 or P8 only with funded, qualified priority or after-hours capacity. P2 requires discharge coordination, P3 requires its own payer authorization, and P7 requires route-level density evidence. P9 remains a bounded recovery capability rather than unlimited standby. Measure scheduled workload as committed service and administrative minutes divided by paid, covered working minutes for each employee, and separately measure accepted workload against each contractor's available capacity. Keep planned utilization at or below 75% before expanding. The remaining 25% absorbs delays, evidence corrections, and backup coordination; it is not free inventory to sell twice. If one employee's routine shift already requires persistent overtime, repair pricing, workflow, or staffing before adding routes. The four-person pod cannot silently become the six-person Scaled case. Record added staff cost and capacity explicitly before using that scenario as a planning target.

Apply the following thresholds as management rules, not observed industry benchmarks. At Day 30, Scale means authorizing the next bounded onboarding or pilot stage, not assuming mature profitability. Require five completed buyer interviews, two confirmed recurring needs, one written onboarding path, two qualified capacity files, twenty complete rehearsal packets, zero unresolved critical defects, and spending within the cash ceiling. Live volume remains prohibited until all pre-flight checks pass. At Day 60, expansion requires at least sixty completed live events across four operating weeks, at least 98% on-time service, at least 98% first-pass acceptance among thirty or more reviewed submissions, zero missed STAT commitments, and at least 45% realized retained contribution before other costs. Require one paid invoice and the settlement reserve before adding volume. At Day 90, require 150 completed events across eight operating weeks, at least sixty reviewed submissions at 98% acceptance, 98% on-time service, zero missed STAT commitments, and two invoices paid by their contractual due dates. Apply the 45% retained-contribution floor again. With insufficient samples or no payment history, label the decision Fix and hold volume; zero complaints does not establish reliability.

Kill applies immediately to an unauthorized movement, an uninsured assignment, fabricated evidence, or a critical unresolved passenger or specimen safety defect: stop the affected activity, preserve records, and follow required reporting and corrective procedures. A missed STAT commitment freezes new STAT bookings until the cause is corrected and ten consecutive timed rehearsals pass. Commercial Kill applies to a lane with negative contribution after driver settlements and attributable direct costs across twenty comparable events, followed by two documented unsuccessful repricing or routing attempts. It also applies when contracted obligations exceed available cash and committed funding in the rolling thirteen-week forecast; honor existing obligations while stopping new unfunded bookings. Fix means one named owner, one measurable defect, and a seven-day correction deadline, without volume growth. At Day 90, Scale additionally requires four consecutive weeks at or above 12% fully loaded owner-profit margin on gross billings, no undisputed receivable more than fifteen days past due, and utilization within the 75% limit. Increase weekly volume by at most 25%, then recheck after two weeks. No contract by Day 90 alone is not failure: continue a funded, documented onboarding path within the expected two-to-five-month deal window.
