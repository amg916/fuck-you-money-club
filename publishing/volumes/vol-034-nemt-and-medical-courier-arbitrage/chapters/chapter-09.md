## 1. Classify the service before accepting the route  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a service classification sheet, not a generic transportation agreement. Record the purchasing entity, payer, passenger assistance level, specimen category, vehicle ownership, dispatch role, and every state crossed. Medicaid transportation assurance under 42 CFR 431.53 and transportation provisions under 42 CFR 440.170(a) do not create a universal federal dispatch license. State Medicaid agencies, managed care organizations, and transportation brokers impose enrollment, screening, authorization, inspection, and documentation requirements through their respective programs. Determine whether your company contracts as the enrolled transportation provider, an authorized subcontractor, or an administrative dispatch vendor; those positions produce different billing and credential obligations. Section 1902(a)(87) of the Social Security Act, codified at 42 U.S.C. 1396a(a)(87), establishes specified Medicaid transportation provider and driver assurances, including exclusion screening and driver licensing requirements. Check the state's implementation rather than treating one broker's credential packet as transferable authority. A wheelchair trip, assisted ambulette trip, stretcher movement, and ambulance service may occupy different licensing categories. Reject an assignment requiring clinical monitoring when the available crew and vehicle provide transportation only. Preserve the written classification, current credentials, and buyer authorization together so dispatch cannot silently expand the service when a facility requests extra assistance.

Build a separate passenger and property authority matrix for interstate work. Federal property brokerage concepts under 49 U.S.C. 13102 do not automatically resolve passenger dispatch status; evaluate the actual arranging activity, compensation, operating authority, and applicable exemptions. Interstate for-hire passenger carriers can face FMCSA registration and insurance requirements even when the appointment itself is medical. California passenger-carrier authority, local vehicle rules, and Medi-Cal enrollment must be assessed independently; California Labor Code sections 2775 and 2776 also make contractor status a separate analysis. New York Medicaid ambulette requirements, transportation manager instructions, and applicable Vehicle and Traffic Law Article 19-A driver rules require another lane-specific review. Workers' compensation transportation follows the relevant state claim authorization and purchasing arrangement rather than automatically inheriting Medicaid rates. Assign one owner to update the matrix whenever a new county, payer, vehicle class, or border crossing appears. Retain official source links, effective dates, and written agency responses where the classification is unclear. The fallback is a properly credentialed carrier with a permitted subcontracting arrangement, or declining that lane; relabeling a carrier as a technology partner does not change the underlying activity. Recheck insurance territories before approving the replacement.

HIPAA status follows the information function, not the word courier. A carrier performing only transient transmission may fit the narrow conduit treatment described by HHS, but a dispatch company storing patient schedules, addresses, appointment details, and trip records will often perform business associate functions. Where that relationship exists, execute the agreement required by 45 CFR 164.502(e) and 164.504(e), including appropriate downstream subcontractor terms. Apply minimum necessary access under 45 CFR 164.502(b), role-based permissions, device controls, incident reporting, and contractually defined retention. Keep patient identifiers out of sales prospecting systems. Specimen transport adds a different classification tree: the shipper must determine whether material is exempt, Category B under 49 CFR 173.199, or subject to Category A requirements. UN3373 is not a universal label for every specimen. Require documented packaging, temperature limits, receiving windows, and a damaged-package escalation process before pickup. OSHA's 29 CFR 1910.1030 applies to covered employers with occupational blood exposure, requiring an exposure control plan and associated protections. CLIA primarily regulates laboratory testing; a courier-only operation does not acquire laboratory testing authority by transporting specimens. Have the laboratory define specimen acceptance criteria and document every rejected handoff.

## 2. Make fee triggers, liability, and retained economics auditable  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a master services agreement plus lane schedules, not one price sheet covering every passenger and specimen movement. Define scope by geography, operating hours, assistance level, authorized vehicle, specimen class, and excluded activities. A fee trigger should require the buyer's authorization identifier, the agreed service event, and an enumerated evidence packet. State whether a completed trip, documented attempted pickup, approved waiting time, or canceled standing order earns payment; a driver's arrival alone must not become a completed Medicaid claim. Specify invoice delivery, dispute deadlines, undisputed payment timing, correction rights, and access to audit records. For a $100 completed service, the illustrative settlement is $55 to the driver and $45 retained before overhead. A $20 waiting charge earns nothing unless the contract and payer rules authorize that charge and the actual wait is documented. Separate commercial service credits from payer recoveries, and allocate recoveries according to the party's documented error rather than automatically charging every denial to the driver. Where the buyer requires 837P transactions, confirm its companion guide and enrollment pathway; other buyers use portals or invoices. Never promise that a scrubbed claim will be paid simply because it passes format validation.

Preserve the scorecard's distinction between gross contracted billings and retained dispatch contribution. Conservative billings of $250,000 produce $137,500 driver settlements and $112,500 retained contribution; subtracting $82,500 of remaining modeled costs leaves $30,000 owner profit. Base billings of $925,000 produce $508,750 settlements and $416,250 retained contribution; $249,750 remaining costs leave $166,500. Scaled billings of $1,600,000 produce $880,000 settlements and $720,000 retained contribution; $336,000 remaining costs leave $384,000. Profit divided by gross billings is respectively 12%, 18.0%, and 24%; profit divided by retained contribution is 26.67%, 40.0%, and 53.33%. Apply the supplied 45% retained-share convention in the operating model, while documenting the actual ASC 606 principal-versus-agent conclusion from control of the promised service. Gross billings are not automatically recognized dispatch revenue. The stated $3,500–$18,000 monthly opex range is not a substitute for the complete annual cost bridge: Base remaining costs average $20,812.50 monthly. Resolve that scope difference explicitly in budgeting. At Base volume, 45 days of driver settlements alone consume approximately $62,723 using a 365-day year, before other cash outflows. A $65,000 startup budget therefore leaves little collection delay capacity without deposits, staged growth, or committed funding.

Negotiate risk allocation around controllable events. A liability cap can reference twelve months of fees, but specify separate treatment for bodily injury, confidentiality breaches, willful misconduct, and other liabilities that the parties cannot or will not cap. Match insurance to passenger transport, hired and nonowned vehicles where applicable, cargo or specimen loss, cyber events, and employer exposures; an insurance certificate alone does not establish every coverage term. Make indemnities fault-linked, state the defense process, and prohibit unilateral settlement that creates new obligations for the other party. A narrow anti-circumvention clause can protect documented commercial introductions for a defined period, but must not restrict patient choice, interfere with payer networks, or become a disguised referral payment. Address the federal Anti-Kickback Statute, 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b), when remuneration could induce federally reimbursed business; do not pay discharge personnel for referrals. Define escalation, mediation or arbitration, venue, governing law, and emergency remedies. Reserve preexisting software and routing methods to their owner while granting necessary service rights. Allocate record custody, access, export, retention, and deletion duties without claiming ownership extinguishes patient rights or statutory access. Require an orderly transition when either party terminates the agreement.

## 3. Convert driver and specimen requirements into dispatch evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

An owner-operator agreement is evidence of intent, not a substitute for worker classification. Federal wage-hour analysis, federal employment tax rules, state unemployment and workers' compensation laws, and state wage statutes can apply different tests. Document actual investment, business independence, opportunity for profit or loss, scheduling practices, vehicle ownership, and the relationship's permanence. California's business-to-business provision has conditions; an LLC, invoice, or signed contractor declaration does not establish the exception by itself. Avoid drafting terms that promise independent businesses while operating exclusive, continuously supervised shifts with no meaningful discretion. Required safety standards, payer credentials, and trip verification remain necessary, but assess how the complete operating relationship functions. Where the model requires employee control, budget payroll taxes, overtime exposure, workers' compensation, and supervision instead of disguising wages as settlements. The scorecard's 2 FT + 2 C and 3 FT + 3 C describe the modeled operating team, not the total available driver network or a classification ruling. Keep each carrier's business records, applicable coverage, vehicle inspection, credential expiration, and signed service terms current. If a carrier loses coverage, remove that carrier from assignment availability rather than relying on a promise to renew after completing today's routes.

Configure RouteGenie or MediRoutes around the evidence the buyer actually requires, and verify the purchased product configuration before promising features. Capture assignment acceptance, arrival, passenger boarding or package receipt, departure, delivery, and exceptions as distinct events. GPS proximity supports a trip record but cannot establish that a passenger boarded or a specimen reached an authorized recipient. Pair location evidence with permitted signatures, recipient identifiers, or barcode scans, and preserve corrections with actor, reason, and timestamp. Use service identifiers rather than diagnoses on driver screens wherever possible. For recurring dialysis transport, confirm each service date remains authorized; a standing schedule is not proof that every trip occurred. For specimens, associate the laboratory accession or approved transport identifier with package count, seal condition, temperature evidence when required, and receiving acknowledgment. Do not photograph exposed labels into ordinary messaging applications. Separate damaged packaging, missed pickup, passenger absence, recipient closure, and temperature excursion codes because their remedies differ. A missed STAT pickup needs immediate buyer notification and a clinical decision by the responsible laboratory or care team, not a dispatcher guessing whether the specimen remains usable. Never backfill a pickup timestamp to preserve the service metric.

Design recovery procedures before selling redundancy. Maintain a lane-specific backup roster whose carriers already satisfy the buyer's subcontracting, vehicle, insurance, and information requirements. An uncredentialed vehicle nearby is not usable backup. For an illustrative $200 specimen route, a $110 driver settlement leaves $90 before overhead; if a permitted recovery costs another $60 without a buyer-approved surcharge, retained contribution falls to $30. Repeated recovery costs can therefore erase the modeled 45% retention even while gross billings remain unchanged. Record recovery expense against the original service and review the responsible failure mode weekly. For passenger distress, drivers follow the emergency response procedure and contact emergency services when appropriate; dispatch must not improvise medical triage. For spills or suspected exposure, isolate the hazard within training limits and activate the employer and shipper response instructions. Billing review should reconcile completed events, authorized units, driver settlements, buyer remittances, and denial reasons before releasing final profitability reports. Preserve records for the longest applicable contractual or statutory period, using separate schedules for trip records, required HIPAA documentation, employment records, and specimen logs. HIPAA's six-year documentation rule does not create one universal retention period for every operational record.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify the buyer and service
   - Complete the lane authority and information flow worksheet.
   - Identify payer, contracting role, state rules, vehicle class, assistance limits, specimen category, and business associate responsibilities before pricing.
2. Bind responsibilities and verify capacity
   - Execute buyer schedules and qualify primary and backup carriers.
   - Match fee triggers, credential records, insurance terms, packaging duties, escalation contacts, and authorized subcontracting to the actual assignment.
3. Run a documented test assignment
   - Validate event capture and exception handling with synthetic records.
   - Use synthetic passenger or specimen identifiers until access controls are verified; test timestamps, scans, recipient acknowledgment, and export fields.
4. Reconcile service, settlement, and collection
   - Release billing only after evidence and authorization agree.
   - Track disputed units separately, assign correction ownership, preserve the audit trail, and update cash forecasts using actual remittance dates.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the evidence before protecting the margin**
> 
> Experienced dispatch operators prevent small documentation shortcuts from becoming repeated payer recoveries or unusable specimens.
> - Test one complete buyer evidence packet before onboarding a whole recurring route; verify its portal, signature rules, and remittance reference fields.
> - Track credential expiration by carrier, driver, and vehicle separately; a current carrier policy does not renew an individual driver's qualification.
> - Give laboratory receivers a distinct closed-door exception code and escalation number so arrival scans cannot conceal failed delivery.
> - Review retained dollars after recovery expense by lane each week; a busy route with repeated rescues can destroy the 45% retained-share assumption.

Then ask: 'What happens when the passenger is absent, the receiving laboratory is closed, or the approved driver cancels?' These questions expose authorization and recovery risk before rate negotiation. Never describe pending enrollment as approved network status, and never promise clinical capability merely because a driver has healthcare customers.

For multi_inbox_free_workspace_outbound_clusters, separate the arithmetic from permitted service use. Three hundred inboxes sending 15–25 messages each over 20 business days yield 90,000–150,000 monthly messages; 150,000 is the upper endpoint, not a minimum or an inbox-placement prediction. At hypothetical retail prices of $6–$12 per seat, 300 seats cost $1,800–$3,600 monthly before other services. Use Microsoft 365 developer or partner benefits only when their current terms expressly permit the intended production activity; eligibility does not establish unrestricted commercial sending rights. Cloudflare Email Routing is inbound forwarding, not a substitute for an authorized outbound mail service. Use properly licensed SMTP service, authenticated domains, truthful sender identity, and monitored replies. A voluntary limit of two or three domains per tenant may simplify administration but does not authorize otherwise prohibited activity. Remove tracking pixels, validate addresses using permitted services, and recognize that catch-all SMTP responses cannot verify every recipient. Do not rotate domains to evade provider enforcement. The fallback is lower volume, better account selection, and permitted paid capacity, not an instant identity swap.

Apply CAN-SPAM requirements under 15 U.S.C. 7704 to commercial email, including business-to-business campaigns: accurate headers, truthful subjects, required advertising identification, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out honored within ten business days. Maintain suppression across all sending identities, not just the mailbox receiving the request. Telephone and text outreach requires a separate review under 47 U.S.C. 227, 47 CFR 64.1200, and applicable state requirements; an email address does not establish consent for automated marketing calls or texts. Use this laboratory discovery script: 'Which specimen classes do you release, who packages them, what temperature limits apply, and who decides acceptance after a delayed handoff?' For commercial terms say: 'Our quote separates scheduled service, authorized waiting, cancellation, and recovery. Can we review your evidence requirements and payment timetable before selecting a pilot lane?' Close with: 'If this is outside your remit, please direct me to vendor qualification. Reply stop and we will suppress further marketing.' Include the remaining required sender disclosures in the actual message. Start with one measured lane; calculate customer acquisition cost from collected contribution, including onboarding labor, rather than counting replies as profitable contracts.
