## 1. Map twelve sourcing plays to rights, deadlines, and funded demand  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with three separate inventory lanes, not one undifferentiated ticket list. Multi-exchange resale involves individually identified tickets, exchange selling fees, replacement exposure, and platform-specific payout eligibility. Suite seat-splitting begins with a suite agreement whose permitted guests, resale rights, catering minimums, parking allocations, and access credentials must survive subdivision. Tentpole hospitality combines admission with separately documented services around the Super Bowl, World Cup, F1, Masters, or Final Four; a hotel booking or sponsor relationship does not establish permission to resell admission. Give each opportunity a HubSpot record with event identity, supplier authority, inventory evidence, acquisition deadline, transfer availability, buyer deadline, and expected cash-release date. Request redacted ownership evidence before asking for complete transferable credentials. Do not circulate live barcodes in prospecting decks. Rank supply by a specific reason for release: unused sponsor allocations, corporate attendance reductions, expiring suite commitments, or an authorized broker's aging stock. A cheap listing without transferable rights is not inventory. A verified release without a reachable buyer is merely a watchlist item. Concentrate the opening month on one venue cluster and two buyer segments so travel knowledge, seating expertise, and supplier references compound instead of fragmenting across unrelated events and jurisdictions.

Map twelve plays explicitly. Plays one through eleven are sponsor-allotment releases, suite seat-splitting, Super Bowl corporate bundles, World Cup hospitality bundles, F1 hospitality bundles, Masters hospitality bundles, Final Four corporate bundles, NFL seat-license playoff strips, NBA seat-license playoff strips, authorized multi-exchange inventory distribution, and corporate cancellation recovery. Play twelve is b2b_commercial_vcc_interchange_rebate_arbitrage, a separate TRACK_1 accounts-payable settlement adjacency rather than ticket resale income. For each, record both the commercial trigger and the permission that makes it usable. An NFL stadium seat license, sometimes marketed as an SSL or PSL, may confer purchase rights without permitting every proposed transfer; read its actual agreement. NBA playoff strips similarly require round-specific acquisition obligations and refund treatment. Model three authorized playoff purchases at $800 each as $2,400 committed, not as three certain games or three certain sales. If elimination refunds only unplayed rounds, played-game losses remain yours. For sponsor releases, contact the person empowered to dispose of the allocation, not simply someone wearing the sponsor's badge. Treat a publicly announced sponsorship as a lead signal, never as proof of surplus inventory. Require event-specific confirmation before describing any package as official.

Mine demand from public company event calendars, conference exhibitor directories, corporate sponsorship announcements, venue premium-seat pages, and SEC filings identifying regional expansion or material customer concentrations. Municipal procurement portals can reveal convention transport or hospitality requests, but a public buyer's request does not authorize gifts to its employees. Separate that procurement channel from ordinary client entertainment. Review source access terms and use permitted exports, manual research, or authorized APIs; never bypass ticket queues, purchase limits, or access controls. The BOTS Act, 15 U.S.C. §45c, reaches prohibited circumvention and specified resale involving tickets obtained through it. Record a URL, observation date, relevant sentence, and proposed buyer need for every signal. A pharmaceutical conference beside an NBA arena suggests schedule fit, not automatic suitability for healthcare entertainment. Qualify the buyer's recipient restrictions before building that offer. Run a weekly release calendar at thirty, fourteen, seven, and three days before each supplier's genuine decision deadline. Ask which unsold allocation can be returned without penalty, which requires full payment, and which cannot be split. Prefer a documented conditional hold while matching demand; if the supplier requires speculative cash immediately, reduce quantity or walk away.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Work the release clock before competing on public asking prices**
> 
> The useful edge is documented availability matched to a specific buyer deadline, not a mysterious source of cheap seats.
> - Ask suite managers for the exact catering commitment date; an earlier guest-count deadline can reveal divisible inventory before public discounting.
> - Ask sponsor operations for written guest-allocation release dates and the employee authorized to approve transfers; marketing contacts alone rarely establish disposal authority.
> - Save seating-map versions and photograph permitted sightline evidence; compare contiguous seats and restricted views before treating two prices as equivalent.
> - Offer agencies a timestamped supplier-backed quote with an expiry and substitution rule; never advertise a hold that the supplier has not actually granted.

## 2. Turn intent signals into a scored, permission-aware outreach machine  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build two linked pipelines: suppliers who can convey rights and buyers who can commit budget. Use Airtable for inventory evidence and HubSpot for account activity, connected through a unique opportunity identifier rather than attendee names. A research record becomes a qualified buyer only after a discovery conversation establishes event, date flexibility, headcount, seating configuration, all-in spending ceiling, contracting entity, decision maker, and payment timing. Score one hundred points across verified budget, schedule urgency, decision authority, documented inventory fit, transfer feasibility, and payment readiness. Award points for evidence, not confident language. Require seventy-five points for a priced proposal, with mandatory rights and transfer checks regardless of score. A ninety-point prospect cannot cure prohibited resale. Below seventy-five, send a bounded discovery request rather than reserving expensive inventory. Track supplier records separately for identity verification, authority, successful delivery history, cancellation terms, and banking verification. Confirm changed remittance instructions through a previously verified telephone number. Avoid collecting full guest identity documents until the actual issuer or service provider requires them through an appropriate channel. The system should let an operator explain why this buyer, this allocation, and this payment schedule belong together without searching personal messages for critical evidence.

Run an account-specific cadence over twelve business days. On day one, email the relevant procurement, partnerships, or executive-assistant contact with the observed trigger, a plausible attendance use case, and one qualification question. On day three, use a manually placed business call where permitted; leave a short message about the same event rather than changing the offer. On day six, send a LinkedIn note referencing a public professional signal. On day nine, email a substantive seating or package comparison only if the lead remains suitable. Close the sequence on day twelve with a clear invitation to return when headcount and budget are approved. Suppress opt-outs across every channel immediately. Commercial email must satisfy 15 U.S.C. §7704, including truthful routing, identification requirements, a postal address, and a working opt-out. Do not use automated promotional texts or prerecorded calls without establishing the applicable consent requirements under 47 U.S.C. §227 and 47 CFR §64.1200. Publish inbound pages for specific venue configurations, transfer procedures, and total-price comparisons. Forms should request business contact, event, party size, and budget range, while clearly distinguishing verified inventory from sourcing services. Answer qualified inbound requests the same business day with either a supported next step or a precise limitation.

Use a testable funnel rather than a promised conversion rate. An illustrative opening cohort starts with 240 researched accounts, of which 180 pass contact-quality and relevance checks. Forty-five substantive responses represent 25% of contacted accounts. Twenty-four discovery conversations represent 53.3% of responders; twelve qualified opportunities represent 50% of discoveries. Eight written proposals convert at 66.7% of qualified opportunities, and four signed mandates or paid sales convert at 50% of proposals. The resulting account-to-win rate is 1.67%. A signed sourcing mandate is a commercial win, but it is not ticket revenue unless its contractual earning conditions are met. Tag mandates and delivered sales separately. At unchanged yield, 180 researched accounts produce three expected wins and 600 produce ten; neither arithmetic result establishes actual demand. Review rejection reasons every Friday before increasing volume. Poor response means weak targeting or messaging; good discovery with few proposals usually means unsuitable supply or budget; proposals without closes suggest trust, payment, or total-price friction. Track cohorts by first-contact week and allow their buying windows to mature. Do not inflate conversion by removing silent accounts or counting multiple follow-ups as new prospects. Keep acquisition labor and paid lead costs attached to the same cohort.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Research a bounded account cohort
   - Build 240 source-linked accounts across one venue cluster and two buyer segments.
   - Deduplicate contracting entities, record public intent triggers, and separate supplier records from buyer records.
2. Qualify demand before committing inventory
   - Contact the 180 accounts that pass relevance and contact-quality checks.
   - Use the twelve-business-day cadence, score evidence consistently, and suppress opt-outs across channels.
3. Convert fit into a conditional written offer
   - Issue proposals only after the seventy-five-point threshold and mandatory rights checks.
   - Specify seats, inclusions, total price, quote expiry, transfer timing, cancellation treatment, and payer obligations.
4. Reconcile the cohort through delivery and settlement
   - Track signed mandates, paid orders, delivered inventory, and settled owner contribution separately.
   - Review lost-deal reasons weekly and compare actual cash-release dates against the original funding assumptions.

## 3. Price the funding gap and reconcile every offer to retained contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Reconcile sourcing decisions to Formula v2.0 before celebrating turnover. The canonical gross bases are $220,000, $1,010,000, and $1,800,000; acquisition costs consume 60%, leaving retained amounts of $88,000, $404,000, and $720,000. Owner profits of $26,400, $191,900, and $468,000 therefore require remaining expense envelopes of $61,600, $212,100, and $252,000. These produce gross-basis margins of 12%, 19.0%, and 26%, and retained margins of 30%, 47.5%, and 65%. The table allocates those envelopes illustratively across selling fees, inventory leakage, cancellation reserves, funding cost, and overhead without changing the canonical totals. Normalize exchange statements first: if a payout already deducts seller fees, recover the corresponding gross sale and fee entries rather than subtracting that fee twice. Likewise, recognize actual markdowns in actual realized proceeds and unsold inventory through the applicable loss treatment; the planning bridge's leakage allowance must not become a second deduction. The supplied 40% retained-share convention is a planning basis, not an automatic ASC 606 conclusion. Actual gross-versus-net presentation depends on principal-versus-agent facts and control of the promised service. Keep client package billings, supplier pass-through amounts, retained contribution, and owner profit visible as separate ledger fields. Do not describe customer spending as the operator's earnings.

Underwrite each inventory lane with its own cash calendar. A multi-exchange ticket bought for $1,200 and targeted at $2,000 leaves $800 before selling and operating costs. Deduct $200 exchange fees, an $80 leakage allowance, a $60 cancellation allowance, $20 funding cost, and $100 allocated labor and overhead: planned profit is $340. That illustration is not a substitute for the annual scorecard's blended margins. Suite splitting might commit $12,000 for twenty seats offered at $900 each, but only $18,000 of full-capacity proceeds exist before any empty seats, fees, or services. The table's $1,800 leakage stress reduces that ceiling before calculating profit. Tentpole bundles require separately funded admission, rooms, transport, and hosting, often with different cancellation rights. Never assume a refundable hotel makes the whole package refundable. Record contractual payout timing for each exchange rather than assuming instant settlement. If an illustrative $12,000 suite is paid forty-five days before an event and proceeds arrive seven days afterward, $12,000 remains tied up for fifty-two days. At 18% annual simple funding cost, that is approximately $308, replacing any smaller budgeted funding allowance. A postponement extends the clock; rerun profitability and peak cash exposure rather than merely moving the event date.

Treat the VCC play as an adjacent B2B operating business with its own contracts and ledger. The offer embeds a free automated AP and instant vendor-settlement interface inside freight brokerage, construction general contracting, medical supply, property management, or media agencies, replacing eligible checks and ACH payments with single-use Visa or Mastercard commercial virtual cards. Free describes the buyer-facing software offer, not an absence of acceptance, funding, or servicing costs. Market instant settlement only where the actual provider delivers that timing. At $100,000 eligible monthly invoice volume, a contracted 140–195 basis-point rebate yields $1,400–$1,950 before costs. Subtract an illustrative $700 customer revenue share and $200 servicing allocation to leave $500–$1,050. At $100 million annual eligible volume, the same gross rebate range is $1.4 million–$1.95 million, not $100 million of operator revenue. Confirm eligibility, tiers, reversals, exclusions, and whether the contract actually has no cap. Obtain vendor acceptance instead of forcing card conversion. Inject accurate Level-3 line-item invoice metadata where supported; it does not assure a particular interchange outcome. For card-averse vendors, offer mutually accepted 1.5%–2% ten-day early-pay ACH discounts and document who receives the savings. Focus on fragmented long-tail vendors, but keep every VCC dollar outside this volume's canonical ticket-profit cases.

## 4. Close the first three-to-ten wins without buying avoidable failure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Close with a written scope that eliminates the buyer's most expensive ambiguities. Name the event, venue, date, section, row when available, seat adjacency, accessible-seating requirements, hospitality inclusions, excluded services, transfer channel, and delivery deadline. Disclose material limits before payment, including credentials released near the event. Under 16 CFR Part 464, applicable live-event ticket offers must display the required total price upfront, subject to the rule's specified treatment of excluded charges; do not bury mandatory service charges behind a low headline. Apply relevant state requirements as well, including New York Arts and Cultural Affairs Law Article 25 where the transaction and business fall within its scope. Respond to price objections with equivalent inventory and a complete price comparison, not imaginary scarcity. Respond to trust objections with verified business identity, references shared with permission, documented rights, and appropriate payment protections. If the buyer requires certainty that the supplier cannot support, offer a different allocation or decline that configuration. A sourcing mandate should identify search scope, spending authority, fee earning conditions, cancellation rights, and whether the broker may commit funds. Never let an informal request to explore options become presumed authority to purchase nonreturnable inventory.

Before broadcasting inventory, test the actual connected stack. SkyBox, Automatiq, and Logitix are named workflow options, not interchangeable promises of universal atomic locking. Verify the contracted integrations for StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, and TickPick, including acknowledgment latency, quantity updates, order ingestion, and emergency delisting. Require real-time auto-hold behavior where available and an operator stop rule wherever synchronization becomes uncertain. If one order arrives while another exchange still shows availability, quarantine the remaining quantity and confirm the delisting acknowledgment before accepting additional fills. Do not continue selling while waiting for a dashboard to refresh. Stress-test a contractual replacement charge equal to 200% of a $1,000 sale as $2,000 of exposure, while checking whether that charge replaces or supplements other remedies in the actual agreement. Avoid double-counting the same penalty when calculating the loss. Maintain a permitted manual transfer path, but never bypass issuer controls to rescue an order. Store transfer confirmations, timestamps, recipient acceptance where provided, and reconciliation references. One contractor can verify delivery while the owner handles pricing and exceptions; a second contractor can reconcile invoices and settlements. Explicit responsibility matters more than adding another disconnected tool.

Run the first ninety days as a sequence of controlled commitments. During days one through fourteen, establish the supplier evidence register, venue knowledge, total-price templates, and payout calendar; research the first account cohort concurrently. During days fifteen through thirty, complete discovery, test transfer operations, and seek conditional mandates or small matched transactions. During days thirty-one through sixty, deliver the first orders and reconcile actual selling fees, substitutions, funding days, and support labor against each quote. During days sixty-one through ninety, repeat the strongest source-and-buyer pairing rather than adding speculative events. The stated one-to-three-month first-deal window is an operating target, not evidence that demand has already been secured. Keep startup commitments inside the $10,000–$50,000 capital range, with a separate reserve for clawbacks and outstanding supplier obligations. Model monthly overhead within the supplied $1,800–$11,000 range and measure the actual burden rather than using revenue as a staffing trigger. At each weekly review, report researched accounts, qualified buyers, proposals, signed mandates, paid sales, fulfilled orders, settled proceeds, and owner contribution. Release buying capacity only after cash availability and remaining liabilities are reconciled. Three profitable, delivered, settled deals teach more than ten unsigned proposals or a large inventory balance whose buyers have not materialized.
