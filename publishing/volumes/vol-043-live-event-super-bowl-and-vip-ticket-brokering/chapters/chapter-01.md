## 1. Separate three inventory businesses before selecting five operator archetypes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Live-event brokering purchases or arranges access rights and sells a better combination of availability, location, certainty, and service. The asset is a conditional admission entitlement, not ownership of the event, stadium, or customer's travel budget. Multi-exchange resale uses SkyBox, Automatiq, or Logitix workflows to distribute eligible inventory through connected marketplaces such as StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, and TickPick. Supported integrations, reservation semantics, and settlement terms must be verified for the actual account. Stadium suite splitting instead commits capital to a room or block whose individual seats can be sold only when the suite agreement permits that activity. Tentpole hospitality bundles seats with separately contracted hotel, transport, catering, or club benefits; each component creates another delivery obligation and cancellation exposure. These businesses share demand forecasting but differ in inventory divisibility, supplier deadlines, and payout timing. A $30,000 suite deposit cannot be treated like thirty liquid $1,000 tickets if the contract prohibits individual transfers. Likewise, an impressive corporate itinerary is worth little when the admission credential is personal, revocable, or unavailable until an uncertain release date. Start with rights verification, then choose distribution, rather than sourcing attractive seats and discovering the restrictions after accepting payment.

Five archetypes describe how a small team earns its spread: the exchange inventory operator, suite seat-splitter, corporate hospitality assembler, playoff-option specialist, and comparison-led booking agent. The exchange operator benefits from fast reservation propagation and accurate seat mapping. The suite operator needs a documented right to divide seats, allocate catering, and transfer parking independently. The hospitality assembler needs reliable suppliers and buyers who value one accountable itinerary. The playoff specialist prices contingent events and opponent uncertainty rather than assuming every purchased strip becomes a home game. The booking agent converts qualified demand into authorized inventory without pretending referral traffic is ticket revenue. None requires owning a stadium relationship on day one, but each requires a repeatable sourcing explanation. A sponsor allocation may be attractive because a business cannot use its seats; it is saleable only if the sponsor, issuer, and contract permit the proposed transfer. Team or player-family provenance does not itself establish resale permission. For NFL or NBA season-seat licenses, distinguish the license, season inventory, and conditional playoff purchase rights. An SSL transfer can carry separate consent requirements and future payment obligations, so price the actual entitlement being acquired, not the shorthand printed on a seller's spreadsheet.

Candidate #43 receives 24 points for revenue potential, 25 for profit-margin potential, 20 for team efficiency, 13 for startup accessibility, and 13 for automation potential, totaling 95/100. That ranking describes the operating opportunity, not an exemption from capital constraints. The $10,000–$50,000 startup range fits controlled tests, deposits backed by enforceable terms, and modest inventory batches; it does not automatically fund a large tentpole book with delayed payouts. The expected first-deal window is 1–3 months, while monthly operating expense ranges from $1,800 to $11,000. A solo full-time operator with two contractors can divide sourcing, fulfillment, and reconciliation, but contractors still need explicit authority limits and event-day coverage. Unlike wholesale electronics buying groups, an unsold ticket loses essentially all admission utility when its event begins. Postponement can immobilize cash without creating an immediate refund right, while cancellation can reverse previously expected proceeds. Durable advantage therefore comes from verifiable supply, reliable transfer execution, and buyer relationships that survive a bad event. Beginner hype mistakes a rising championship market for skill, counts package markup as profit, and scales inventory before measuring failed transfers, refund latency, and cash committed beyond the next supplier deadline.

## 2. Reconcile gross commercial volume, retained contribution, and spendable cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical revenue basis combines gross secondary ticket exchange payouts, suite-splitting sales, and VIP hospitality package billings, with a 40% retained share after face-value ticket and suite acquisition cost. Preserve those scorecard labels while separating economic measurement from accounting presentation. Gross buyer consideration, exchange deductions, cash disbursed, supplier costs, and operator commission belong in different ledger fields. An exchange bank deposit may already exclude seller fees; do not subtract those fees again or call the deposit gross billings. For model comparison, normalize transactions to a consistent pre-deduction commercial basis and retain the bridge back to actual settlement statements. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent assessment considers control of each specified good or service before transfer. A principal may recognize gross revenue with acquisition cost separately; an agent generally recognizes its fee or commission. The scorecard's 40% factor is an analytical retained-revenue convention, not a universal accounting election. Separately assess admission, hotel, and transport promises when their control arrangements differ. Customer cash received before performance can remain a contract liability, while cancellation rights and variable consideration affect recognition. Stadium attendance GMV, client travel budgets, and sponsor marketing spend are never substitutes for the operator's own earned consideration.

Formula v2.0 reconciles without changing any canonical result. Conservative gross volume of $220,000 less $132,000 acquisition cost leaves $88,000 retained revenue; $61,600 of subsequent costs leaves $26,400 owner profit. Base gross volume of $1,010,000 less $606,000 acquisition leaves $404,000 retained revenue; $212,100 of subsequent costs leaves $191,900 owner profit. Scaled gross volume of $1,800,000 less $1,080,000 acquisition leaves $720,000 retained revenue; $252,000 of subsequent costs leaves $468,000 owner profit. Gross-basis margins are 12%, 19.0%, and 26%; retained-basis margins are 30%, 47.5%, and 65%. An illustrative Base cost allocation assigns $60,600 to seller fees, $35,000 to fulfillment, $40,400 to inventory markdowns and write-offs, $20,200 to cancellation and clawback provisions, $10,000 to settlement funding, and $45,900 to operating overhead. Those amounts total $212,100 exactly; they are an explanatory allocation, not vendor quotes. The overhead component averages $3,825 monthly, within the supplied operating range. Charge each loss once: when inventory is already expensed at acquisition, a lower realized sale price reduces revenue rather than creating an identical second expense. Maintain separate planning reserves and booked actuals so provisioning does not silently become duplicate loss recognition.

Profit and liquidity diverge most sharply around marquee events. Suppose an illustrative package sells for $10,000 and costs $6,000 to acquire. Deduct $600 seller fees, $350 fulfillment, $400 expected markdown or inventory loss, $200 cancellation exposure, $100 funding cost, and $450 allocated overhead. Owner contribution is $1,900, not the $4,000 headline spread. Those allowances must be recalibrated by event, supplier, and sales channel rather than copied as universal industry rates. If $6,000 remains funded for sixty days at a 10% simple annual rate, financing costs approximately $98.63, explaining the rounded $100 allowance. The locked $6,000 itself is working capital, not another expense. A post-event payout hold lengthens the funding period even when admission transfers successfully. Size available liquidity against dated supplier outflows, collectible customer receipts, refunds, and credible payout delays; do not use recognized revenue as a cash proxy. Maintain a downside calendar in which a postponed event produces no immediate exchange proceeds and a second event still requires its deposit. At Scaled volume, improved purchasing and utilization must explain the higher margin; the spreadsheet cannot simply remove seller fees, contractor costs, or cancellation exposure to manufacture efficiency.

## 4. Build the control loop that survives double sales, statutory duties, and delayed payouts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A small-team moat begins with one authoritative inventory record per transferable entitlement. Record event identity, date, section, row, seats, adjacency, restrictions, supplier, cost, and transfer deadline before publishing. Configure supported SkyBox, Automatiq, or Logitix connections to propagate reservations and removals across StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, and TickPick, but do not assume a common dashboard creates an atomic lock between independent exchanges. Test actual order acknowledgments, latency, partial fills, retry behavior, and outage recovery. If a connection cannot reliably reserve scarce inventory, allocate that inventory to one channel or a partitioned quantity rather than broadcasting every seat everywhere. Replacement exposure can be modeled at 200% where the specific contract supports that penalty assumption; it is not a universal marketplace tariff. Store completed transfer evidence without duplicating sensitive barcodes into contractor chat. Use least-privilege accounts, strong authentication, and an exception queue for rejected transfers. Automated barcode delivery is appropriate only through authorized mechanisms supported by the issuer and marketplace. At event time, an accountable person must own unresolved admission problems, because a successful API response does not establish that the buyer holds a valid, usable credential.

The federal BOTS Act, 15 U.S.C. §45c, prohibits circumventing covered ticketing security measures or purchase controls and addresses resale of tickets obtained through prohibited circumvention under its statutory conditions. Automated repricing and authorized inventory synchronization differ from bypassing purchase queues, CAPTCHAs, or account restrictions. Require suppliers to document lawful acquisition and investigate credible warning signs rather than treating a supplier warranty as proof. For covered live-event transactions, 16 CFR Part 464 requires prominent total-price presentation including mandatory fees, subject to its specified exclusions and disclosure rules. Optional ancillaries must be genuinely optional; compulsory processing charges cannot appear only at the final payment step. New York Arts and Cultural Affairs Law Article 25 adds ticket-reseller requirements, including licensing under §25.13 where applicable. Determine the relevant jurisdictions and account for licensing, disclosures, and refund rules before publishing offers. Comparison pages also need accurate affiliation statements: 15 U.S.C. §1125 addresses misleading representations and confusion. Text-only references are a useful design choice, not automatic permission for misleading claims. Engineering should preserve displayed prices, source timestamps, accepted terms, and order versions so disputes can be resolved from evidence rather than reconstructed recollection.

Operate a daily event-level reconciliation rather than waiting for the monthly bank close. Match supplier commitments, live inventory, reservations, completed sales, delivery evidence, marketplace deductions, and cash receipts using stable internal identifiers. Separate postponement from cancellation because refund obligations, supplier recoveries, and payout schedules can differ. When an event becomes uncertain, freeze affected repricing automation, inspect marketplace instructions, and prevent accidental sale of obsolete dates or invalid credentials. Offer a replacement only when it is authorized and the buyer accepts it; otherwise process the required refund and pursue supplier recovery separately. Unwind refundable hotel and transport reservations before their deadlines instead of waiting for the ticket dispute to finish. Track inventory age, unsold exposure, reservation propagation time, transfer failures, net contribution, refund aging, and peak cash draw by event cohort. Use those observations to revise purchase limits and price-drop curves, not merely to produce retrospective dashboards. Expansion from one full-time operator and two contractors to two full-time operators and three contractors should follow demonstrated fulfillment capacity. A repeatable $191,900 Base profit with funded obligations is more durable than a nominal Scaled forecast whose next refund batch exhausts available operating cash.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Register the actual entitlement
   - Create one authoritative inventory record and attach the controlling supplier documents.
   - Capture seat identifiers, transfer restrictions, suite divisibility, ancillary scope, acquisition cost, and the last permissible delivery time.
2. Underwrite contribution and dated cash
   - Calculate expected proceeds after acquisition, fees, fulfillment, losses, reserves, funding, and overhead.
   - Maintain a separate cash timeline through post-event payout; stress postponement, cancellation, partial sell-through, and supplier refund delays.
3. Publish controlled inventory
   - Verify permitted marketplace connections, reservation propagation, and complete buyer-facing price disclosure.
   - Exercise a supported test workflow before broad distribution and partition scarce inventory when reliable shared reservation is unavailable.
4. Fulfill and reconcile the event cohort
   - Confirm authorized transfer, resolve exceptions, and match settlement statements to the transaction ledger.
   - Close supplier balances, process required refunds, release justified reserves, and update event-specific purchasing limits from realized contribution.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight verifications before accepting a live order**
> 
> An offer becomes sellable only when its rights, inventory state, displayed price, and fulfillment funding all match the proposed transaction.
> - Verify written resale, suite-splitting, credential assignment, and ancillary transfer rights for the exact event and buyer-facing package.
> - Demonstrate reservation propagation and withdrawal on every connected channel; use exclusive allocation wherever cross-channel locking cannot be established.
> - Validate applicable reseller licensing, BOTS Act sourcing controls, total-price disclosure, and documented cancellation terms.
> - Fund supplier deadlines and modeled refund exposure through the stressed payout date, including financing cost in contribution calculations.
