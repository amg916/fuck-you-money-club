## 1. Build one inventory authority before automating twelve operator plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin with a single inventory authority, not three independent exchange bots. Select SkyBox, Automatiq, or Logitix after verifying contracted API access, supported marketplaces, webhook behavior, and actual cancellation semantics; these products are alternatives or contracted integrations, not automatically interchangeable rails. Map each ticket to an immutable inventory identifier plus event, section, row, seat, ownership evidence, transfer restrictions, and acquisition lot. A Python receiver validates the provider's documented signature against the raw request body, checks replay limits where supported, and commits the event before acknowledging delivery. Use a unique provider-event key and a transactional outbox so retries cannot create a second reservation. The lifecycle is available, reserved, sold, transfer pending, transferred, and settled; releases require explicit reversal evidence. An order from StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, or TickPick triggers a local hold and immediate withdrawal requests everywhere else. Local atomicity cannot make external exchanges atomic. Measure acknowledgement latency, reconcile residual listings, and stop new exposure whenever a marketplace cannot confirm withdrawal. A double-sale contract can impose replacement liability approaching 200% in the supplied risk model; read the actual agreement rather than treating that percentage as a universal platform rule.

Treat the twelve mapped plays as one workload ledger with explicit owners. The full-time operator owns purchasing, exposure limits, exceptional prices, and corporate promises. Contractor one handles transfer exceptions and event-day support; contractor two handles approved account research, CRM quality, and invoice preparation. Inventory synchronization, probability repricing, transfer orchestration, and invoice reconciliation form the transaction spine. Account research and qualification feed programmatic event pages, buyer referrals, suite replenishment triggers, outbound clusters, creative testing, and exception digests. Every run records its input snapshot, rule version, output, reviewer, and eventual commercial result. Measure automation using time, not the number of enabled integrations. An illustrative weekly baseline is 50 repetitive hours across the team. Removing 39 hours through straight-through processing while adding four hours of exception handling saves 35 hours, exactly 70%. If exceptions consume eight hours, savings fall to 62%, even with identical software coverage. Keep negotiation and acquisition judgment outside that repetitive-work denominator, but publish their hours separately to prevent selective reporting. Sample twenty completed records each week and compare actual handling time with the baseline. Reopen the measurement whenever event mix or contractor responsibilities materially change.

Keep three commercial lanes distinct inside the same system. Multi-exchange resale requires owned or contractually controlled tickets, exchange-specific delivery deadlines, and cash capacity through the contractual payout date. Suite seat-splitting commits to a suite block before individual seats necessarily sell; track catering minimums, parking allocations, admission rights, and each unsold seat separately. Tentpole corporate hospitality for the Super Bowl, World Cup, F1, Masters, and Final Four combines tickets with precisely described services and supplier deposits. Do not describe sponsor allotments as freely transferable until written rights support that claim. Corporate deposits are cash receipts with remaining obligations, not automatically earned contribution. A $24,000 suite split into twenty seats needs $1,200 per seat merely to recover the suite acquisition before catering, selling fees, reserves, or operating costs. Sixteen seats sold at $1,800 produce $28,800, leaving only $4,800 before those deductions. By contrast, a $3,000 resale ticket locks a smaller absolute amount but may still settle after the event. Hospitality may demand several supplier deposits months earlier. Separate cash calendars prevent a profitable ticket dashboard from concealing a suite funding shortage. Never route these obligations through the wholesale electronics buying-group assumptions used in Volume 041.

## 2. Make repricing, fulfillment and invoice automation bounded and recoverable  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the repricer around realized contribution rather than scraped asking prices. For each lot, store acquisition cost, applicable exchange fee, expected markdown loss, cancellation reserve, delivery expense, and payout carrying cost. A simple floor is acquisition cost plus dollar reserves and required contribution, divided by one minus the percentage selling fee; add fixed selling charges to the numerator. At $600 acquisition, $40 combined reserves and carrying cost, $60 target contribution, and a 10% fee, the floor is $777.78 before customer taxes and any separately applicable charges. Round upward, not downward. Estimate drop curves by event, seating zone, remaining inventory, and hours until transfer cutoff. Use permitted feeds or licensed market observations instead of bypassing access restrictions. For NFL or NBA SSL playoff-option strips, model each possible home game separately and verify what the seat license actually conveys. A 60% chance of $300 contribution and a 40% chance of a $200 sunk loss produces only $100 expected contribution. That value is not spendable cash. Injury, elimination, venue changes, and rainouts require fresh scenarios and human review before buying additional exposure.

Run deterministic checks before asking a language model to explain anything. A repricing proposal carries the current floor, observation age, market depth, and maximum permitted change. Illustratively, permit reductions up to 3% per cycle only inside the approved floor and inventory budget; larger moves become review tasks. Do not average missing observations into fabricated market depth. Use the model to summarize why a proposal changed, extract package inclusions from supplier documents, and draft corporate responses from verified fields. Treat supplier PDFs, customer messages, and web content as untrusted material: they cannot change tool permissions or issue transfer instructions. Require structured output with permitted fields, source references, and explicit validation errors. Reject unsupported amenities rather than polishing them into convincing copy. Illustrative model pricing of $0.50 per million input tokens and $2 per million output tokens makes a 1,200-input, 300-output extraction cost $0.0012. Adding $0.0008 orchestration yields $0.002 per run, or $200 for 100,000 runs. Vendor subscriptions, licensed feeds, messaging, and human correction remain separate costs. Cache stable supplier facts, but invalidate transfer conditions whenever the issuer changes them.

Transfer orchestration must respect the issuer's delivery mechanism. Rotating mobile barcodes are not reusable image attachments, and a screenshot is not proof of accepted delivery. Where an authorized transfer API exists, send through that interface and store the transfer identifier, recipient confirmation, and acceptance state. Where it does not, generate a checklist for the fulfillment contractor rather than automating around account security. Avoid storing raw barcodes in model prompts or outbound analytics. Invoice automation similarly matches facts rather than inferring payment from an email. Use decimal amounts, currency codes, invoice identifiers, bank settlement references, and allocated seat quantities. A $12,000 suite invoice with a $4,000 deposit and $8,000 balance remains partially collected until the second bank receipt clears; supplier payment and revenue recognition are separate entries. Retry transient errors with bounded exponential backoff and jitter. Route persistent failures into an exception queue with event-date priority. After a timeout, query the remote operation's status before retrying a transfer or credit. Daily reconciliation catches missed notifications, while a tested replay procedure reconstructs state from durable events without duplicating customer actions. An on-call person must own incidents during delivery windows.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture and authenticate
   - Validate the provider's documented authentication mechanism and persist the original event.
   - Record receipt time, provider identifier, payload digest, and replay result without placing ticket secrets in general logs.
2. Reserve and calculate
   - Apply the inventory transition and compute contribution using versioned fee and reserve rules.
   - Commit the transition and outbound intent together; conflicting reservations become exceptions rather than silent overwrites.
3. Review consequential exceptions
   - Route purchases, exceptional prices, transfer conflicts, and changed payment instructions to the named owner.
   - Show the underlying evidence, cash exposure, delivery deadline, and exact proposed action before execution.
4. Execute and reconcile
   - Dispatch authorized actions, verify remote status, and match settlement against the original obligation.
   - Retry only safe operations; unresolved outcomes remain open until a person documents the actual external result.

## 3. Scale distribution through verified demand, not mailbox-count theater  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play multi_inbox_free_workspace_outbound_clusters proposes hundreds of secondary domains and subsidized Microsoft 365 developer or partner mailboxes, Cloudflare Email Routing, and low-cost SMTP relays. Separate arithmetic from eligibility and delivery claims. Three hundred mailboxes sending 25 messages on twenty business days produce 150,000 monthly messages; at fifteen daily messages they produce 90,000. Neither volume establishes primary-inbox placement. Developer subscriptions are for permitted development uses, and partner benefits have their own terms; verify current production and commercial sending rights before assigning any mailbox to outreach. Cloudflare Email Routing forwards incoming mail and is not an outbound SMTP service. Use a sending provider that expressly permits the intended activity, with authenticated domains and truthful sender identities. Avoid claiming $6–$12 retail seat savings until the eligible mailbox cost is documented. Two or three domains per tenant can simplify administration but do not authorize quota multiplication or restriction evasion. If eligible subsidies cannot support production, budget paid infrastructure and reduce volume. Never rotate identities to defeat recipient blocking, provider enforcement, or unsubscribe requests. The commercial objective is qualified corporate conversations, not a manufactured count of nominally free accounts.

Replace the proposed promise of 100% SMTP catch-all verification with layered address checks and honest uncertainty. Syntax and domain checks identify obvious errors; approved verification services can flag risk, but catch-all domains do not prove a particular person has a deliverable mailbox. Quarantine ambiguous addresses or confirm them through an existing business relationship. Send plain-text messages without open pixels or tracking redirects, identify the business accurately, include a valid postal address and working opt-out, and synchronize suppression across every domain. Under 15 U.S.C. §7704, commercial email requirements apply to B2B messages too; other jurisdictions may impose additional permission requirements. Keep standby sender infrastructure only for legitimate service continuity, carrying the same identity and suppression records, never for bypassing restrictions. A measured funnel might produce 150,000 messages, a 0.4% qualified-response rate, and 600 qualified responses. If 10% become substantive opportunities and 10% of those close, the result is six deals, not six hundred. At $2,000 incremental contribution each, that is $12,000 before campaign labor and infrastructure. Illustrative monthly sending and verification costs of $900 and $450 equal $0.009 per message, excluding mailboxes, domains, and human handling.

Build distribution loops around evidence that gets better with each completed event. Programmatic pages should answer specific buyer questions about seating configuration, accessible entry, package inclusions, transfer timing, and all-in prices when an offer is displayed. Generate drafts from verified inventory and venue records; do not manufacture availability or mass-produce indistinguishable event pages. A corporate purchaser who receives accurate delivery and a clean invoice can introduce another department, creating a referral loop without exposing attendee information. Ask permission before publishing testimonials or identifying corporate buyers. Suite replenishment uses actual attendance, purchasing lead time, and budget windows to trigger a relevant follow-up, not inferred personal characteristics. Research and qualification summarize company-level fit; creative testing compares approved offers while preserving the same factual claims. An illustrative search cohort of 4,000 visits at 2% inquiry conversion yields eighty inquiries; 25% qualified and 15% closed yields three deals. Deduplicate these against outbound and referral records before adding contribution. The exception digest reports stale pages, undelivered transfers, unanswered qualified inquiries, and overdue balances, linking directly to evidence. Retire campaigns when fully loaded contribution turns negative, even when traffic and message counts continue rising.
