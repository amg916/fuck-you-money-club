## 1. Build a jurisdiction and acquisition-rights file before listing  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate lawful inventory automation from prohibited primary-market access. The BOTS Act, 15 U.S.C. §45c, prohibits circumventing specified ticket issuer security measures or access controls that enforce posted purchasing limits or ordering rules; it also reaches selling tickets obtained through prohibited circumvention under the statute's participation, control, or knowledge standards. Its covered-event definition includes venues with capacity exceeding 200 people. A SkyBox, Automatiq, or Logitix integration does not authorize queue bypass, CAPTCHA defeat, identity rotation to evade limits, or purchasing through a supplier doing those things. Require supplier identity, original acquisition evidence, event identifiers, purchase timestamps, quantity, transfer status, and a representation describing lawful acquisition. A representation supplements evidence; it does not erase contradictory facts. Quarantine lots offered with implausible volume, unverifiable accounts, or instructions to conceal provenance. Distinguish inventory held in an issuer account from inventory actually transferable to a buyer. Record delivery restrictions and release dates in the inventory ledger before promising fulfillment. For NFL or NBA SSL playoff-option strips, document exactly which postseason games can arise, exercise deadlines, incremental purchase obligations, and treatment when qualification fails. Never describe an unexercised option as an issued admission credential.

Create one jurisdiction record for the operator's business location, event location, targeted buyer location, and each sales channel. New York Arts and Cultural Affairs Law Article 25 supplies ticket-specific requirements; §25.13 addresses reseller licensing, while §25.07 addresses ticket sale requirements. Read the currently effective provisions, exclusions, licensing instructions, security requirements, and renewal dates together rather than treating a marketplace account as a statewide license. Record the actual license holder and operating entity on contracts and invoices. Evaluate other states separately: California Civil Code §1770(a)(29) addresses advertised consumer prices, while local ticket-reseller rules can add requirements outside a national platform's onboarding. Geographic targeting creates questions that a remote office does not answer. Suite splitting also requires the venue agreement to permit individual-seat resale, guest substitution, and any advertised food or beverage access. Corporate hospitality can add travel-seller, alcohol-service, transportation, or lodging obligations when the operator supplies those components. Use properly authorized suppliers for those services and specify responsibility in the package contract. For World Cup inventory, confirm tournament-specific transfer and hospitality conditions; ordinary ticket ownership does not automatically confer commercial hospitality packaging rights.

Apply 16 CFR Part 464 to covered live-event ticket offers: the prominently displayed total price must include mandatory charges, subject to the rule's specified exclusions and disclosure requirements. The rule reaches covered business offers as well as consumer offers, so a corporate buyer is not a reason to hide compulsory service fees. Explain excluded government charges and permitted shipping charges before payment and identify the final payment amount. A mandatory delivery fee cannot become an optional add-on merely because the checkout names it differently. Describe obstructed views, restricted access, hospitality location, and whether seating is contiguous wherever those facts affect the offer. Preserve the advertisement, checkout presentation, invoice, and versioned terms together. Federal pricing rules do not eliminate stricter applicable state requirements. Treat channel rules as a separate layer: StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, and TickPick each have their own fulfillment and seller-remedy provisions. A possible 200% replacement penalty belongs in a channel-specific stress case, not in a claim that every exchange imposes identical damages. Assign someone to review amendments, license renewals, and issuer transfer changes before the next campaign, and stop affected offers when the underlying rights cannot be demonstrated.

## 2. Contract for transferable rights, matched remedies, and settlement timing  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start the contract with a product schedule precise enough for another operator to fulfill without a sales call. State event, venue, scheduled date, section, row when known, quantity, contiguous seating commitment, access class, transfer method, delivery deadline, and every included hospitality component. Where an allocation does not identify a row, describe the enforceable category and substitution boundaries instead of inventing a seat. Distinguish an owned ticket, a sourced-to-order procurement service, an exercisable playoff option, and a reserved suite. Set the fee trigger at the contracted performance milestone, not simply when a salesperson sends an invoice. Specify whether a deposit funds acquisition, holds capacity, or compensates a completed service, with refund treatment for each component. A corporate purchase order is evidence of an obligation, not collected cash. For a $12,000 suite commitment sold as twelve $1,400 seats, gross billings are $16,800 and acquisition spread is $4,800 before selling costs, unsold seats, reserves, and labor. Require written permission to split the suite and state whether catering minimums, parking, gratuities, and taxes sit inside the quoted total. If splitting is prohibited, sell only a permitted whole-suite allocation.

Match upstream and downstream remedies before accepting payment. Define cancellation, postponement, venue relocation, shortened programming, and loss of a hospitality component separately. Explain who elects to keep postponed-event access, what applicable law or channel policy requires, and when refunds become payable. Avoid promising a cash refund within five days when the supplier only releases funds after an indefinite issuer process unless the business can finance that mismatch. Identify substitution standards using measurable access and seating attributes; a different experience is not equivalent merely because its list price is higher. Specify the cure sequence for failed delivery, including customer contact, replacement procurement, and refund processing. Negotiate a liability cap appropriate to the transaction while preserving remedies that mandatory law does not permit the parties to waive. Address fraud, deliberate misconduct, confidentiality, and payment obligations explicitly rather than burying exceptions in a catchall sentence. Put governing law, forum, escalation contacts, and any arbitration procedure in readable terms. Review consumer arbitration separately from negotiated corporate agreements. A seller contract cannot rewrite an exchange's penalties, card-network dispute rights, or statutory refund duties, so maintain a remedy map that shows which obligation controls each transaction.

Use a narrow anti-circumvention clause for introduced corporate accounts or authorized suppliers: identify covered relationships, covered transactions, duration, and an evidence-based fee remedy. Do not use it to suppress ordinary market competition or override a venue's transfer restrictions. Separate that commercial protection from the absolute prohibition on circumventing issuer purchasing controls. Allocate intellectual property and customer information deliberately: the operator owns its original proposals and workflow materials, while neither party acquires league marks, issuer credentials, or unrestricted rights to attendee information. Limit guest data to fulfillment needs, define permitted recipients, and establish deletion and incident-notification responsibilities. Keep ticket barcodes and account credentials out of shared outreach tools. For exchange resale, require a single inventory authority, reservation states, idempotent order handling, and acknowledged removal from other channels before treating an order as safely allocated. Real-time auto-hold across StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, and TickPick is an operating requirement to implement and test, not a capability to assume from a vendor logo. Where an integration cannot confirm reservation or delisting, reduce exposure by using one selling channel for that lot. Document manual incident ownership and the time at which replacement procurement begins.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify the product and transaction footprint
   - Identify the resale model, operating entity, event jurisdiction, and intended buyer locations.
   - Record whether the order concerns exchange tickets, individual suite seats, or a corporate package with additional services.
2. Collect rights and acquisition evidence
   - Obtain issuer records, supplier representations, transfer conditions, and applicable license evidence.
   - Reject undocumented splitting or hospitality rights and assign a named owner to every expiring authorization.
3. Match obligations and fund the timing gap
   - Compare supplier, customer, exchange, and payment-provider remedies against the cash calendar.
   - Calculate replacement exposure, refund reserves, and financing costs before accepting the customer's delivery promise.
4. Test fulfillment and preserve accepted terms
   - Verify inventory reservation, all-in pricing, transfer evidence, and the final signed schedule.
   - Archive the offer version and channel acknowledgments, then reconcile settlement against the same order identifier.

> - Obtain written permission before marketing sponsor allocations; a sponsor employee's possession of tickets is insufficient evidence of resale rights.
> - Test one low-exposure order through each enabled exchange and retain hold, transfer, delisting, and payout evidence before expanding inventory.

## 4. Run permission-based outreach with exact scripts and measurable fallbacks  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Implement high_velocity_dm_outbound_systems as a permission-based discovery workflow, not a cold-message volume contest. Publish a useful event planning prompt and invite an explicit comment keyword, a clearly described hand-raiser reaction, or an inbound message requesting information. A generic like is not automatically a request for sales contact. Record the initiating action, source post, timestamp, assigned inbox, and requested topic. Send the first automated message only where the platform permits that action, and send external links only after the prospect replies. Route warm signals among authorized team inboxes for workload ownership, not to evade sending limits or continue contacting someone who declined. Treat PeerFloodError, account warnings, and silent Message Request accumulation as reasons to stop automation and use permitted inbound handling. Suppose 200 explicit triggers produce 80 replies, 20 qualified briefs, and four collected orders. At $250 contribution per order after acquisition, selling fees, inventory loss allowances, and carrying expense, that produces $1,000 before campaign labor. Six contractor hours at $30 plus $70 of tooling leave $750 campaign contribution. These are illustrative funnel assumptions, not scorecard additions. Tie each order to settlement and reserve movements before calling the campaign profitable.

Use language that requests a real procurement brief without implying privileged access. Public prompt: 'Planning client hosting for the Super Bowl, F1, or a playoff game? Comment HOST if you want our seating-versus-hospitality comparison. We will message only people who request it.' First message: 'You requested the hosting comparison. Are you considering tickets only, individual suite seats, or a complete hospitality package? Reply with the event and approximate guest count; I will keep this conversation here until you request a proposal.' After a reply: 'For a useful quote, what is your total budget including mandatory fees, preferred access level, decision date, and contracting entity? May I send a link to a written proposal?' Discovery follow-up: 'Do you require adjacent seats, accessible seating, named-guest changes, parking, catering, or a cancellation option? Which person can authorize the deposit and purchase order?' Describe options from documented inventory and disclose restrictions before requesting funds. Do not claim official sponsorship, league affiliation, or guaranteed access to restricted hospitality without the corresponding rights. For sponsor allocations, ask the supplier for written resale authorization before contacting buyers. If rights are unresolved, offer a documented alternative rather than promising later paperwork.

Keep channel permissions distinct. A requested social message does not establish permission for promotional texts, prerecorded calls, or unrelated email campaigns. For commercial email, 15 U.S.C. §7704 requires accurate transmission information, nondeceptive subjects, required identification, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out process; business recipients are not a blanket exemption. Honor statutory opt-out deadlines and maintain a suppression record. Apply 47 U.S.C. §227 and 47 CFR §64.1200 according to the calling technology, message purpose, recipient, and applicable consent or do-not-call requirements before adding phone outreach. Review state calling and privacy rules separately. Use a clear closeout script: 'Understood. We will stop sales follow-up on this channel. Contact us directly if your event plans change.' Do not restart contact from another inbox. For procurement, send: 'The proposal identifies the total price, delivery deadline, included access, and event-change terms. Please confirm the contracting entity and authorized signatory before we issue the payment request.' Keep trigger evidence, accepted terms, delivery confirmation, and opt-out records together with restricted access. Measure qualified briefs, funded orders, realized contribution, complaints, and delivery incidents; raw message count rewards precisely the behavior that destroys channel access.
