## 1. Turn eight mapped plays into one controlled shipment lifecycle  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a narrow service promise: scheduled dry-van replenishment between two distribution clusters, with named pickup windows, equipment requirements, and exception ownership. Candidate #12 ranks #19 because attractive billings conceal purchased transportation, labor, and working capital. The canonical Base operation has three full-time staff and two contractors, not an unattended matching bot. Assign commercial intake and credit to one employee, carrier procurement and dispatch to another, and tracking plus accounting coordination to the third; contractors provide defined coverage and bookkeeping capacity. The owner must have an explicit place in that staffing budget. Map eight plays across this workflow: niche acquisition, shipper credit, carrier identity defense, load matching, shipment tracking, invoice reconciliation, float management, and customs first-sale coordination. These are chapter-level operating mappings, not seven invented catalog identifiers accompanying the one supplied identifier. Each play requires a measurable unit, an accountable operator, and a fallback that preserves service without surrendering payment controls. A signed tender becomes work only after credit exposure, carrier identity, and capacity are checked; a sales conversation alone creates neither a shipment nor recognized revenue.

Establish active FMCSA property-broker authority, process-agent designation, and the required $75,000 BMC-84 surety bond or BMC-85 trust arrangement before arranging covered transportation. Apply 49 U.S.C. § 13904, § 13906, 49 CFR § 387.307, and Part 366 to the actual entity that signs contracts. The security amount is not automatically a $75,000 cash expense: a bond has underwriting, premiums, and possible collateral; a trust has its own funding requirements. Review current FMCSA implementation requirements rather than relying on an old onboarding checklist. Verify carriers through FMCSA Licensing and Insurance and SAFER, then use Highway or RMIS as additional identity and document-workflow evidence. A vendor badge does not establish coverage for the particular commodity or defeat identity theft. Independently confirm dispatch contact and payment instructions using previously validated channels. Bind the authorized carrier, driver, tractor, trailer, and pickup reference to the shipment record before release. When a last-minute substitute appears, stop the release instruction, call the validated carrier, and rebook verified capacity if necessary. The commercial penalty for a missed pickup is usually smaller than the combined cargo and duplicate-payment exposure.

The required customs_first_sale_rule_valuation_arbitrage mapping belongs at the importer's customs workstream, not inside domestic freight revenue recognition. Under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a, 19 CFR § 152.103, and the Nissho Iwai framework, an earlier sale needs substantiated transaction-value eligibility, including a bona fide sale for export to the United States. At an illustrative 25% ad valorem rate, one million units valued at $18 produce $4.5 million duty; an eligible $8 value produces $2 million, saving $2.5 million, or 55.56%, before required valuation adjustments. That example does not establish a universal 35–55% saving: assists, royalties, related-party issues, product-specific duties, and inadequate destination evidence can change or eliminate the result. For merchandise programs spanning $10 million to $250 million, model entry-level eligibility before aggregation. Use a confidential, encrypted customs-broker evidence vault to protect factory invoices from casual commercial disclosure while preserving required importer record access and lawful CBP access. Obtain a written customs-counsel analysis; it supports decisions but does not bind CBP. If evidence fails, retain the supportable valuation and separate any agreed coordination fee from duty savings.

## 2. Implement six ledger modules with serialized, idempotent state changes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the following six modules as a sequential, copy-pasteable SQLite reference, executed in one Python process with a local database file. Module one opens a connection in explicit transaction mode; every business mutation subsequently uses BEGIN IMMEDIATE before reading mutable balances or states. SQLite therefore serializes competing writers, including separate connections to that same local database. Unique shipment sources prevent duplicate intake, while immutable request bodies distinguish legitimate retries from conflicting reuse of an idempotency key. Balanced integer-cent postings replace spreadsheet adjustments. The reference deliberately contains no bank API calls, carrier booking calls, or message publication inside transactions. Its race protection covers local ledger operations, not an impossible promise of exactly-once execution across unrelated external systems. Keep the database on supported local storage, assign one connection per worker, and retry a busy transaction using the identical request key. Do not share the connection concurrently between threads or move the file onto a network share. For Postgres, preserve the request-key constraint and lock the shipment row before checking exposure or applying cash; changing databases without preserving those semantics would reintroduce duplicate payments and overcollection races.

Module two scores intake deterministically and records only qualified shipments. The score is an operational margin screen, not the candidate's 85/100 portfolio score. An illustrative $12,500 bill and $10,500 carrier commitment produce a sixteen-percent purchased-transportation spread and pass the threshold. Module three progresses qualified, verified, reserved, dispatched, delivered, billed, and settled states with evidence references attached to the event. Verification requires authority, identity, insurance, and credit evidence; reserve requires capacity and liquidity evidence. These references must resolve to reviewed artifacts in the deployed application. A string alone cannot verify an insurance policy. Customs escrow means document custody here, not an unlicensed movement of customer money, and its evidence remains outside the freight state machine unless the shipment actually requires customs coordination. Billing posts receivables and gross shipper revenue, plus carrier expense and payable, in the same transaction as the state change. This deliberately simplified model bills immediately after delivery and fixes accepted prices. A deployed system needs separately controlled credit notes, accessorial changes, disputes, and delivery-period accruals; never edit historical postings to pretend those events did not happen.

Module four records confirmed collections, carrier payments, and operating expenses; it does not initiate transfers. Use a bank transaction identifier as the request key, not a random identifier generated on every retry. The shipment lock implied by the writer transaction makes balance checks and postings indivisible: two callers cannot both spend the same remaining payable. Module five exposes shipment balances and aggregate profit without counting collections as new revenue. Module six runs assertions against an isolated in-memory fixture, verifies retry behavior, rejects excessive collections, and checks settlement and ledger balance. Those checks are provided as executable reference tests, not represented as already executed. The implementation omits authentication, authorization, backups, and external delivery adapters; supply those before operational deployment. Restrict direct database access because application-level posting invariants assume writers use these functions. External dispatch should use an outbox plus the provider's idempotency facility; ambiguous bank responses require statement lookup before any resend. Preserve shipment evidence under 49 CFR § 371.3 and apply separate retention rules to customs records. The practical architecture is a small deterministic accounting core surrounded by explicitly fallible integrations, rather than an automation that silently treats every webhook as both authentic and economically final.

## 3. Execute dispatch, evidence custody, billing, and exception recovery  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Production work starts before the first API event. The intake owner captures the shipper's legal entity, payment contact, purchase-order format, commodity, declared value, origin, destination, appointment windows, and special handling. The procurement owner confirms whether the proposed carrier's insurance actually addresses the commodity, deductible, exclusions, and requested limit; a certificate is evidence to investigate, not a promise that a future claim will be paid. Record which employee reviewed each artifact and when it expires. Before dispatch, compare the pickup driver's identity and equipment against the carrier's validated instructions and the facility's release process. Never email an unrestricted pickup reference to an unverified replacement dispatcher. The tracking owner then watches pickup confirmation, appointment changes, and delivery proof, escalating missed events through a documented call tree. At delivery, collect legible proof, count exceptions, temperature records where relevant, and signed accessorial authorization. A clean packet should reach billing on the delivery day in this model. Measure the hours from delivery to accepted invoice, because every avoidable billing delay extends the cash bridge even when the shipper technically pays within their agreed terms.

Separate three incidents that often become one vague accounting dispute. Identity fraud means the accepted counterparty or payment beneficiary may be false; a cargo claim means the goods suffered an alleged loss; a billing dispute means the contracted amount or documentation is contested. For suspected double brokering, freeze unconfirmed release or remittance instructions, preserve messages and identity evidence, and contact the shipper and verified carrier through independent channels. For cargo damage, preserve delivery exceptions and insurer notification evidence while applying the actual contracts and claim procedures. 49 U.S.C. § 14706 addresses carrier liability for covered transportation; it does not make every broker a carrier or settle contractual allocation automatically. For disputed accessorials, segregate the challenged component and pursue the supported invoice amount. Do not erase the original invoice or create a second shipment to make collections look cleaner. Route corrections through an authorized reversal and replacement journal in the deployed ledger. One $2,500 absorbed claim consumes 1.25 standard $2,000 load contributions before overhead. That sensitivity belongs in the risk review, not as an unrecorded deduction hidden inside the canonical Base figures.

Close every day with three queues: shipments missing evidence, invoices awaiting acceptance, and bank movements awaiting allocation. Assign a person and next action to every item rather than counting raw webhook volume as productivity. Match bank deposits to remittance details; where one payment covers several invoices, use a controlled allocation batch whose components total the deposit. The reference implementation deliberately handles one shipment per collection event, so extend it before accepting multi-invoice remittances or customer credits. Likewise, validate factoring notices and beneficiary changes independently before changing a carrier payee. The weekly treasury review combines committed but unbilled loads, open receivables, scheduled carrier payments, payroll, and usable funding. A $100,000 revolving facility is not $100,000 of available cash if its borrowing base excludes disputed invoices. Model actual advance rates, reserves, fees, and recourse. Keep permission to book freight separate from permission to change payment details, even when one employee covers both desks temporarily through documented review. Broker transaction records under 49 CFR § 371.3 should explain who arranged the movement, what was charged and paid, and how the retained evidence connects the commercial agreement to the cash.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Qualify the tender and reserve exposure
   - Capture shipment requirements, approve shipper credit, and lock accepted commercial terms.
   - Record the source tender identifier and include booked but unbilled commitments in the external credit-control calculation.
2. Verify capacity and execute the movement
   - Authenticate carrier identity, confirm equipment, release pickup details, and monitor milestones.
   - Escalate substitutions through independently validated contacts and preserve each release decision with reviewer identity.
3. Match evidence and post the invoice
   - Reconcile tender, rate confirmation, delivery proof, and authorized accessorials before billing.
   - Post receivable, gross revenue, purchased transportation, and carrier payable in one balanced transaction.
4. Allocate bank cash and close balances
   - Apply confirmed collections and carrier payments using stable bank transaction identifiers.
   - Settle only after receivable and payable balances reach zero and unresolved operational claims remain separately visible.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before the first live shipment**
> 
> Require evidence that the business can arrange, execute, finance, and reconcile the movement.
> - Verify active broker authority, required process-agent filing, and current bond or trust security for the contracting entity using official FMCSA records.
> - Authenticate the carrier's authority, identity, relevant insurance, dispatcher contact, and proposed pickup equipment before distributing release instructions.
> - Approve shipper exposure and a dated carrier-payment cash forecast that includes committed loads, payroll, and funding restrictions.
> - Exercise duplicate intake, conflicting request keys, interrupted transactions, and excess-payment rejection against a test database before connecting production adapters.

## 4. Walk the 90-day cohort from qualified leads to reconciled cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The cohort begins after authority, staffing, shipper onboarding, and carrier procurement are ready; it is an operating quarter, not a claim that a cold start reaches maturity immediately. The stated first-deal window remains two to six months. Each month produces forty screened opportunities, thirty qualified opportunities, twenty-five quotes, and twenty-two accepted tenders. Two accepted tenders are scrubbed before dispatch without cancellation charges, leaving twenty completed shipments. Each completed shipment bills $12,500 and costs $10,500 in purchased transportation. Monthly gross shipper billings therefore equal $250,000, carrier cost equals $210,000, and contribution before operating expenses equals $40,000. Budget $26,250 monthly cash operating expenses: $17,500 payroll and employment costs, $4,000 contractors, $1,500 systems and communications, $1,250 insurance and authority-related costs, and $2,000 sales and administration. These are illustrative budget allocations, not quoted vendor prices. They produce $13,750 monthly owner profit, exactly 5.5% of billings. This quarter assumes no realized cargo loss or credit write-off; scrubbed opportunities create no revenue, carrier commitment, or cash loss. Keep losses explicit rather than quietly declaring every rejected lead an expense.

Use $250,000 opening unrestricted operating cash, separately from any restricted trust or bond collateral. Assume every invoice generated in a month is collected in the next month. Assume half of each month's carrier charges is paid during that month and half during the next month; this is a monthly cash-bucket convention, not a claim that all individual invoices have identical payment terms. Operating expenses are paid as incurred. Month one collects nothing, pays $105,000 to carriers and $26,250 overhead, and closes with $118,750 cash. Month two collects $250,000 from the first cohort, pays $210,000 across the first and second carrier cohorts, and pays $26,250 overhead, closing with $132,500. Month three repeats those flows and closes with $146,250. Month-end balances alone do not prove intramonth liquidity: schedule actual collection dates against payroll and carrier maturities before accepting the cohort. If receipts arrive after concentrated payment dates, stagger accepted loads, negotiate supported terms, or arrange usable funding. The illustrative opening cash also excludes prelaunch spending, so a business needing the full reserve plus setup costs can exceed the stated startup range.

At day ninety, cumulative billings are $750,000, carrier expense is $630,000, operating expense is $78,750, and owner profit is $41,250. Collections total $500,000 and carrier cash payments total $525,000; after overhead, cumulative operating cash flow is negative $103,750. The reconciliation is exact: $41,250 profit minus $250,000 ending receivables plus $105,000 ending payables equals negative $103,750 cash flow. Opening cash plus that movement equals $146,250, and ending cash plus receivables less payables equals $291,250, or opening capital plus earned profit. Annualizing the operating volume produces $3 million billings, $2.52 million carrier costs, $315,000 overhead, and $165,000 owner profit, matching the canonical Base case with three full-time staff and two contractors. The Conservative case remains $1 million at 3%, or $30,000 profit; Scaled remains $5 million at 8%, or $400,000 with four full-time staff and three contractors. Those cases require different productivity and cost structures, not mechanical reuse of this Base budget. Finally, an extra month of collection delay adds roughly $250,000 receivables at this throughput before any offsetting terms changes, illustrating why profitable growth can still exhaust operating cash.
