## 1. Reconcile every load from shipper billing to owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Begin with the scorecard's measurement boundary: revenue means gross shipper billings including purchased transportation, not the broker's retained spread. Actual financial statements require a separate principal-versus-agent assessment; a net presentation changes the revenue denominator without manufacturing additional cash or profit. The illustrative annual cohorts contain 500, 1,500, and 2,500 delivered loads, each averaging $2,000 billed. Carrier purchases average $1,500, $1,600, and $1,560 per load, producing gross spreads of $500, $400, and $440. Those differences represent different customer and lane mixes, not a promise that procurement improves automatically with size. Conservative deliberately assumes a concentrated, higher-spread niche whose small volume absorbs fixed labor poorly. Base broadens the book at a thinner spread; Scaled gains density and purchasing discipline. Preserve separate load identifiers for original tenders, rebooks, accessorial amendments, carrier settlements, and shipper credits. Otherwise, a canceled booking can leave phantom revenue while a replacement carrier creates a second payable. The waterfall below is an explicit cost allocation constructed to reconcile the canonical outcomes, rather than additional scorecard data or vendor pricing. Its owner-profit residual follows all listed business expenses; personal taxes and owner distributions sit outside this operating model.

Labor must remain visible even when an owner dispatches after hours. Annual loaded employee budgets are $150,000, $240,000, and $360,000 for three, three, and four full-time positions respectively. These imply average loaded costs of $50,000, $80,000, and $90,000; validate local wages, payroll taxes, benefits, and the owner's working-role compensation before accepting them. Contractor delivery support adds $24,000, $48,000, and $84,000, equivalent to 800, 1,600, and 2,800 purchased hours at an illustrative $30 hourly rate. The two, two, and three contractors are fractional resources, not additional full-time equivalents. Assign their hours to check-calls, appointment recovery, document collection, and invoice exceptions. Contractor expense per delivered load is consequently $48, $32, and $33.60. Keep commissions inside the stated labor buckets or disclose a replacement allocation; quietly adding commissions later breaks the reconciliation. Employee and contractor classification depends on the actual relationship, not the spreadsheet label. If the Conservative employee budget proves $30,000 too low, its entire annual owner profit disappears. Track after-hours coverage and exception minutes alongside shipment count so nominal automation savings do not merely become unpaid owner labor.

Acquisition expense is $12,000, $36,000, and $60,000, supporting six, eighteen, and thirty newly acquired shipper accounts at $2,000 incremental CAC each. These are acquisition counts, not an assumption that every load comes from a new account. For a new Base account contributing twenty loads, gross spread is $8,000; subtracting $640 of contractor support and $400 of expected losses leaves $6,960 before allocated employee, platform, financing, and other costs. That contribution covers the $2,000 incremental acquisition outlay after roughly six comparable loads, but collection timing still controls cash payback. A fully loaded CAC report should add attributed employee selling time while eliminating the corresponding double count from payroll when reconciling totals. Keep software and integration costs at $12,000, $24,000, and $42,000; loss provisions at $10,000, $30,000, and $50,000; financing at $6,000, $24,000, and $40,000; and other overhead at $6,000, $33,000, and $64,000. These deductions produce exact owner profits of $30,000, $165,000, and $400,000. Divide by delivered loads to obtain $60, $110, and $160 of owner profit, rather than mistaking the much larger gross spread for distributable earnings.

## 2. Scrub invoices, reserve reversals, and price eight operating plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat scrub as the process that converts an operational event into an enforceable, supported invoice, not as a mysterious percentage withheld from every shipment. Match the shipper tender, signed rate confirmation, delivery evidence, carrier invoice, and authorized accessorial record before billing. For detention, capture arrival, release, appointment, free time, and the applicable contractual rate. Four hours on site with two free hours at $75 per compensable hour supports $150, not $300. If the broker owes the carrier $100, retained incremental spread is $50 before collection and handling expense. Never assume tracking pings alone prove the customer's detention obligation. The annual one-percent loss provision can be analytically divided into 0.50% shipper credit loss, 0.30% unsupported billing or credit adjustments, and 0.20% unrecovered carrier settlement or claims-related costs. Base therefore assigns $15,000, $9,000, and $6,000 to those categories. Financial reporting must classify revenue reductions separately from credit expense where appropriate; this management waterfall combines them only to expose economic leakage. Reconcile opening reserve plus current provision less utilized amounts to closing reserve. A posted credit must not also become a second loss when the invoice finally closes.

The supplied canonical play is freight_detention_audit_contingency, covering automated parcel, LTL freight, and tariff overcharge contingency auditing under TRACK_1. The remaining seven labels in the matrix are chapter-local operating mappings, not additional asserted catalog identifiers. Obtain shipper-authorized, read-only UPS, FedEx, and LTL billing feeds through supported APIs, EDI delivery, or controlled exports. Obtain CBP Form 7501 entry summaries from the authorized importer or its customs service provider; do not assume a universal public API exposes entry records. Reconcile invoice lines to negotiated tariffs, dimensions, weights, service commitments, and actual delivery events. A $25,000 realized recovery at a 40% contingency produces $10,000 of operator fee revenue, not $25,000 and certainly not the underlying freight spend. Deduct $2,000 specialist labor, $1,000 acquisition, $500 data handling, and a $1,000 reversal reserve to obtain $5,500 contribution before allocated fixed overhead. The 35–50% fee range produces $8,750–$12,500 revenue on that same recovery. These are illustrative sidecar economics, excluded from the canonical brokerage totals unless an explicit offsetting allocation incorporates them. Require account-level proof that collected benefits belong to the contracted customer.

Refund eligibility and drawback eligibility require different evidence. Parcel service guarantees may be suspended or contractually waived; LTL classifications depend on the shipment and governing tariff. When service-refund claims fail, pivot toward duplicate charges, dimensional-weight errors, LTL freight-class corrections, and contract-rate benchmarking recoveries. Target defensible billing errors where service-guarantee waivers do not control the claim, while still checking billing-dispute deadlines and contractual limitations. Duty drawback under 19 U.S.C. 1313 requires a qualifying statutory pathway and supporting import, export, or destruction records; Form 7501 alone establishes no refund entitlement. Route regulated customs business through appropriately licensed or otherwise authorized participants under 19 U.S.C. 1641 and 19 CFR 111.2. Contract contingency fees against actually realized refunds or usable credits and define clawback treatment. A ninety-day recovery cycle can require the $3,500 of delivery, acquisition, and data spending before any fee arrives; the additional $1,000 reserve is retained protection, not immediate vendor spend. Use carrier identity controls from Highway or RMIS alongside independent FMCSA checks and verified bank-change callbacks. A clean platform status does not establish who actually collected the shipment or who controls the remittance account.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the spread before celebrating the shipment**
> 
> Small operational exceptions become material when they repeat across an entire shipper cohort; instrument the repeatable failure first.
> - Export detention events weekly with appointment and release evidence; bill only compensable hours under the customer's actual terms.
> - Require an independently sourced callback for carrier bank changes; an emailed voided check is supporting evidence, not identity verification.
> - Age audit recoveries from documented customer realization, not from the date an analyst first flags an apparent overcharge.
> - Code every credit by original load and root cause so sales concessions, document failures, and shipper insolvency remain distinguishable.

## 3. Fund the collection gap without treating security as spendable cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Model working capital with a daily convention before quoting a growth target. Assume uniform annual activity over 365 days, shipper DSO of 45, 45, and 50 days, and effective carrier payment timing of 15, 15, and 14 days after delivery. DSO here includes delivery-to-invoice delay; operational dashboards should separately show that delay and invoice-to-cash aging. Carrier payment timing must likewise reflect actual settlement, not contractual terms that the business routinely overrides. The carrier-cost bridge equals annual carrier purchases divided by 365, multiplied by DSO less payment days. It is approximately $61,644 Conservative, $197,260 Base, and $384,658 Scaled. This bridge answers how much purchased transportation is funded between carrier settlement and corresponding customer collection; it does not include every business cash requirement. A document-related payout lock is a specific, contractually supported settlement condition, not an unlimited financing option. Track invoice receipt, proof-of-delivery completeness, exception resolution, and due date independently. Valid carrier obligations remain obligations even when the shipper disputes its own invoice. Repeatedly stretching carriers can remove trusted capacity precisely when a growing brokerage needs reliable service and stronger procurement leverage.

A complementary balance-sheet measure is gross receivables less carrier payables. Multiply shipper billings by DSO over 365, then subtract carrier purchases times payment days over 365. The resulting trading working capital is approximately $92,466, $271,233, and $535,342. It exceeds the carrier-cost bridge because billed spread is also awaiting collection. These are alternative views of the same operating cycle; never add the two totals. For a planning cushion, add fourteen days of noncarrier cash operating expense, excluding the noncash loss provision. Annual cash expenses on that definition are $210,000, $405,000, and $650,000, producing cushions of roughly $8,055, $15,534, and $24,932. Trading working capital plus those cushions is therefore about $100,521, $286,767, and $560,274. Round illustrative accessible-liquidity targets upward to $110,000, $320,000, and $610,000 before separately assessing concentrated defaults, seasonality, or restricted security. These targets are planning choices, not lender commitments. Build a thirteen-week cash schedule to capture actual invoice batches, payroll dates, tax payments, deposits, and facility draws; steady-state averages can hide a severe Friday settlement shortfall. Undrawn facilities count only to the extent their borrowing conditions are actually satisfied.

The $50,000–$250,000 startup capital range does not automatically fund the Base or Scaled receivables book. A $250,000 cash contribution falls $70,000 short of the illustrative Base liquidity target before restricted collateral or formation spending. FMCSA property-broker authority and financial responsibility are separate requirements under 49 U.S.C. 13904 and 13906 and 49 CFR 387.307. The $75,000 BMC-84 surety bond or BMC-85 trust security amount is neither automatic credit availability nor necessarily a $75,000 expense. Bond premium is expense; required collateral consumes liquidity; trust assets cannot simultaneously fund carrier payroll-like settlement runs. Verify current instrument, provider, replenishment, and authority-status requirements before operating. Compare a bank receivables line with factoring using advance rate, eligible collateral, concentration limits, recourse, reserve release, minimum fees, and notification requirements. At an 85% advance, Base gross receivables of about $369,863 produce at most $314,384 before exclusions and facility limits. That is below the $320,000 planning target even when every invoice qualifies. A recourse factor accelerates cash without necessarily transferring shipper default risk. Keep financing budgets provisional until actual balances, rate tiers, and fee schedules reproduce them.

> 
> Authorize additional shipment exposure only when the shipment-level records and the treasury schedule support the promised settlements.
> - Reconcile delivery-to-invoice delay and invoice-to-cash aging to the assumed DSO; investigate excluded disputes and delivered unbilled shipments.
> - Confirm carrier due dates and quick-pay elections against available cash, rather than assuming every carrier accepts the modeled payment interval.
> - Verify current FMCSA authority and $75,000 financial-security support; exclude restricted trust assets and pledged collateral from usable liquidity.
> - Recalculate lender availability after debtor concentration, aging, disputes, reserves, and recourse exposure; fund the remaining gap before accepting more tenders.

## 4. Stress the cohort and turn exceptions into a weekly cash SOP  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Stress purchase cost and collection quality independently before combining them. A two-percentage-point increase in carrier cost as a share of gross billings removes $20,000, $60,000, and $100,000 from annual profit when customer pricing stays unchanged. Owner profit falls to $10,000, $105,000, and $300,000. An additional one-percentage-point loss provision removes another $10,000, $30,000, and $50,000. Together those operating shocks leave $0, $75,000, and $250,000 before any financing effect. Do not label a two-point revenue deduction as a two-percent carrier-price increase: the denominators differ. On Base carrier purchases of $2.4 million, a literal two-percent purchase increase is $48,000, whereas two points of $3 million billings is $60,000. Use both views during bid review and state the chosen denominator in the customer cohort report. Concentration produces a different shock: a single $50,000 unrecovered shipper balance exceeds Conservative annual profit before any offsetting reserve release. Model such events against existing provisions so the same expected loss is not charged twice. Future volume can be repriced; completed shipments need a collections and settlement plan rather than optimistic replacement bookings.

Extend DSO by fifteen days without changing carrier payment behavior. Additional gross receivables become approximately $41,096, $123,288, and $205,479. If a lender funds the entire incremental balance at an illustrative 12% annual rate and the extension persists throughout a full year, incremental annual interest is $4,932, $14,795, and $24,658. A temporary delay requires interest based on actual duration, not this sustained annualization. Funding availability is a separate question: an aging invoice may lose eligibility just when cash is needed most. Combining the carrier-cost shock, extra losses, and sustained collection delay produces approximately negative $4,932, positive $60,205, and positive $225,342 in annual owner profit. Compare that with replacing the entire original financing budget with a simple 2% fee on every gross invoice. Annual fees would be $20,000, $60,000, and $100,000, yielding profits of $16,000, $129,000, and $340,000 after removing the old financing expense first. This fee-only comparison excludes additional interest and service charges; actual proposals must be modeled from their complete schedules. Never add a replacement financing fee while retaining an expense it explicitly replaces.

Run the operating review from one immutable weekly shipment extract, then reconcile it to accounting and bank activity. Segment by shipper, lane, equipment, salesperson, carrier, invoice month, and exception reason. A Base load yielding $400 spread but requiring five extra contractor hours at $30 consumes $150 before employee escalation or credit loss. If that pattern repeats across one hundred loads, $15,000 of contribution vanishes even though headline gross margin appears healthy. Separate these exceptions from normal delivery support to determine whether the modeled $48,000 contractor budget still holds. Use TMS event timestamps, accounting payment traces, and bank reconciliation rather than salesperson estimates. Maintain the transaction records required by 49 CFR 371.3, including compensation and freight-charge information, and preserve applicable access rights. Assign each exception an owner, evidence requirement, recovery amount, and next action date. Escalate unresolved identity conflicts separately from ordinary missing paperwork. Finish each review with a signed cash forecast that states which customer exposure can grow, which needs prepayment, and which should stop receiving new tenders. Profit is usable only when the corresponding cash survives collection, settlement, and correction.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze and reconcile the delivered-load cohort
   - Export the prior week's deliveries, amendments, invoices, credits, and settlements into a versioned reconciliation workbook.
   - Tie shipment counts and gross billings to the TMS and ledger; retain separate identifiers for canceled loads, replacement tenders, and disputed accessorials.
2. Rebuild the contribution and reserve waterfall
   - Allocate purchased transportation, contractor time, acquisition spending, and expected loss adjustments to the correct shipper cohort.
   - Reconcile reserve opening balance, new provision, utilization, and closing balance; remove duplicate charges between revenue credits, loss expense, and actual write-offs.
3. Refresh thirteen-week settlement and collection liquidity
   - Update expected receipt dates, carrier due dates, payroll, lender availability, and restricted security balances.
   - Run fifteen-day DSO deterioration and concentrated-customer default cases; exclude ineligible invoices and unavailable collateral from the funding sources column.
4. Assign corrective actions before accepting incremental exposure
   - Set customer-level prepayment requirements, revised tender limits, repricing actions, and exception-resolution owners.
   - Record dollar exposure, evidence still required, next review date, and settlement responsibility; verify completion against subsequent bank and shipment records.
