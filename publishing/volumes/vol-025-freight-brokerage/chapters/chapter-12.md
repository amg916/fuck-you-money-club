## 1. Days 1-7: Assign Eight Plays and Build the Authority, Credit, and Cash Foundation  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the first week as construction of a controlled brokerage, not a race to tender freight. Candidate #12 requires three full-time people plus two contractors before the Base case becomes a credible operating target. Assign the founder-commercial lead to P1 niche selection and P2 shipper acquisition; assign the operations lead to P4 carrier verification, P5 load matching, and P6 shipment tracking; assign the finance-account lead to P3 pricing, P7 invoice reconciliation, and P8 float management. These eight chapter play labels are the execution map, not eight independent businesses. Contractor one provides documented after-hours tracking coverage; contractor two handles bookkeeping and document preparation without releasing payments. Publish a coverage calendar showing named backups, escalation numbers, and maximum simultaneous exceptions. The finance lead must independently verify payment changes that operations receives. Start with domestic, nonhazardous, full-truckload dry-van freight in one corridor with repeat industrial shippers. Exclude temperature-sensitive, high-theft, household-goods, and cross-border work from this initial design. Those exclusions narrow equipment, claims, documentation, and security requirements without pretending they remove ordinary freight risk. Each play receives one owner, one weekly output, and one measurable failure trigger.

On Day 1, select the legal entity, operating name, corridor, and capital commitment. Day 2 produces an authority checklist covering FMCSA property-broker registration under 49 U.S.C. 13904, financial security under 49 U.S.C. 13906, and process agent designation under 49 CFR Part 366. A submitted application is not active authority. Confirm active status through FMCSA Licensing and Insurance before arranging transportation as an independent broker, and check applicable Unified Carrier Registration obligations separately. Day 3 obtains written BMC-84 surety quotations or a BMC-85 trust proposal. The $75,000 security requirement is not a $75,000 operating expense when a bond is used, and a bond premium is not available working capital. A funded trust reduces usable liquidity; collateral demanded by a surety does too. Check current 49 CFR 387.307 requirements, provider eligibility, and replenishment procedures before committing funds. Days 4-7 complete shipper and carrier agreement review, credit applications, bank permissions, and record-retention design. Apply 49 CFR 371.3 transaction records and three-year retention requirements to the TMS export structure. Put every unresolved authority item on a dated blocking list rather than quietly converting it into a sales assumption.

Use a deliberately funded launch model: $150,000 of unrestricted equity, a separately priced bond, and no assumed borrowing availability. This sits inside the canonical $50,000-$250,000 startup range but does not make its lower endpoint suitable for this staffing plan. Limit first-month operating spending to $18,000: $14,000 for three full-time roles including employer costs, $2,000 for tightly scoped contractor coverage, and $2,000 for software, communications, and administration. These are planning allowances requiring actual local quotations; if compensation cannot fit, increase documented capital and reapprove the plan before committing employment. Allow another $6,000 for formation, filings, bond premium, and initial professional work, producing a $24,000 total launch ceiling. Exclude carrier pass-through settlements from operating burn but track them in a separate daily liquidity schedule. After launch spending, $126,000 remains before freight settlements or collateral restrictions. Ring-fence $54,000 for three additional months of operating expense, leaving $72,000 for settlement exposure and contingencies. Set cumulative spending checkpoints at $8,000, $13,000, $18,000, and $24,000 for Days 7, 14, 21, and 30. These are ceilings, not instructions to consume unused budget.

> 
> A fast sales cycle never substitutes for active authority, verified counterparties, executable service, or settlement cash.

> - Demonstrate that stressed carrier settlements leave the $54,000 operating reserve intact without relying on disputed receivables.

## 2. Days 8-14: Build a Qualified Lane Pipeline and Rehearse Fraud-Resistant Execution  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Day 8 builds a list of forty named shipper accounts inside the chosen corridor, with shipping locations, likely equipment, and a plausible transportation decision-maker. Day 9 begins individualized outreach, and Day 10 turns responses into structured lane interviews rather than unpriced promises. Target twelve completed interviews by Day 30, including origin, destination, weekly frequency, appointment constraints, cargo category, accessorial history, and payment process. Count an interview only when the shipper confirms actual procurement responsibility or introduces the responsible buyer. P2 does not receive credit for scraped contacts or unopened messages. Day 11 creates three written pilot proposals with explicit equipment assumptions and quote expiration times. Use DAT or Truckstop market observations alongside actual carrier quotations; neither screen replaces a carrier's commitment to a specific movement. P3 records the quote timestamp, expected carrier buy, accessorial assumptions, and credit exposure in the TMS. No salesperson may promise unlimited detention reimbursement or guaranteed capacity from an unconfirmed network. With a canonical two-to-six-month first-deal window, a credible procurement next step is meaningful progress even when the customer's routing-guide process cannot finish this month.

Days 12-13 build the carrier bench and attack its weakest identity assumptions. Target six verified carriers across the corridor, with at least two plausible options for each proposed pilot lane. Highway or RMIS can surface identity, insurance, and network signals, but the operations lead owns the acceptance decision and must investigate discrepancies. Check FMCSA Licensing and Insurance for authority and SAFER for operating identity and available safety information; a clean snapshot alone does not establish control of a truck. Obtain insurance evidence through an independently established insurer or producer contact, then check relevant cargo limits, exclusions, and effective dates. An ordinary property carrier's cargo certificate is not equivalent to a universal federal cargo-insurance filing requirement. Call the carrier using a previously verified channel, reconcile dispatch identity, and confirm driver and equipment details near pickup. An unexplained second intermediary, mismatched domain, last-minute beneficiary change, or refusal to identify the performing carrier stops that tender. Where a legitimate inter-broker arrangement exists, require explicit contractual permission and a documented chain of responsibility. Do not normalize concealed reassignment because a load is running late.

Day 14 runs three tabletop shipments from quote through settlement: one clean load, one identity substitution, and one delivery with disputed detention. Use synthetic customer and driver details, not real documents uploaded into an unapproved trial service. P5 must show a timestamped tender and accepted rate confirmation. P6 must demonstrate pickup confirmation, an agreed tracking method, late-arrival escalation, and a failed-tracking fallback using a verified contact. P7 must assemble a bill of lading, proof of delivery, approved accessorial support, shipper invoice, and carrier payable under the same load identifier. P8 must calculate the earliest carrier payment date against the latest plausible shipper receipt, including a dispute delay. Require all three exercises to finish without an uncontrolled payment release or an unresolved identity substitution. The after-hours contractor practices two escalations but cannot accept a substitute carrier or alter bank details. The bookkeeping contractor assembles the invoice packet while the finance lead releases funds. Record elapsed handling minutes and each manual repair. A rehearsed control is useful only if the five-person pod can perform it during an actual pickup exception.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Veteran Habits That Protect a Small Brokerage**
> 
> Preserve independent evidence before urgency makes a weak counterparty look like the only available solution.
> - Save the original carrier onboarding contact and independently sourced callback number before a dispatcher requests any last-minute change.
> - Price appointment rigidity and detention history into the lane worksheet; attractive linehaul spread can disappear during one uncompensated wait.
> - Ask shipper accounts payable to validate invoice submission requirements before pickup, including portal fields and purchase-order matching.
> - Run Friday's settlement forecast before accepting weekend freight; a Monday delivery does not eliminate a Saturday carrier-cash obligation.

## 3. Days 15-21: Validate Per-Load Economics and Size the Shipper-to-Carrier Float  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Days 15-17 convert pilot interest into executable credit and pricing decisions. Day 15 obtains each prospective shipper's credit application, trade references where available, billing entity, and accounts-payable contact. Day 16 establishes a written exposure limit covering delivered unpaid invoices plus committed loads not yet billed. Day 17 tests a representative $2,000 shipper charge against a $1,700 carrier buy: $300 spread, or 15% of shipper billings. Subtract illustrative variable costs of $30 for transaction and tracking expense and $20 for a management risk allowance, leaving $250 contribution before fixed overhead. The allowance supports planning; it does not determine the accounting treatment of an actual claim. At $18,000 monthly fixed operating expense, seventy-two such loads cover that expense before additional unmodeled costs. This is a launch sensitivity, not a replacement for the canonical scorecard. Record accessorial reimbursements and carrier accessorial payables separately; a $200 customer reimbursement matched by a $200 carrier payment adds billings without adding spread. Stop quoting below a $200 modeled contribution floor unless the founder documents a bounded experiment and its loss budget.

Day 18 stress-tests cash rather than celebrating annualized billings. The canonical Base case is $3,000,000 annual gross shipper billings, or $250,000 monthly on average. With the illustrative 85% carrier-cost assumption, monthly carrier payouts are $212,500. Under steady flow, a forty-five-day shipper collection cycle and fifteen-day carrier payment cycle create thirty days of funding gap: $212,500 using a thirty-day month. Add the $54,000 operating reserve and the requirement already exceeds the launch model's $126,000 remaining cash, before a stress buffer. With genuinely contracted and observed twenty-eight-day collections and twenty-one-day carrier payments, the seven-day gap requires approximately $49,583; a 20% buffer raises that to $59,500. Adding the reserve gives $113,500. That comparison does not authorize delaying carriers or assuming customers pay early. Validate terms with counterparties and replace averages with dated invoice cash flows. If factoring is considered, price recourse, reserves, fees, debtor limits, assignment notices, and bank-control effects. An unsigned facility contributes zero available liquidity. Reject the next load when its stressed settlement schedule breaches the protected operating reserve.

Days 19-21 prove that the operating pod can protect those economics. Day 19 measures hands-on minutes for quoting, verification, dispatch, tracking, documentation, and collections using the tabletop records or authorized live pilots when all prerequisites are satisfied. Day 20 configures TMS alerts for appointment risk, missing delivery evidence, duplicate invoices, and overdue customer balances. Automation may propose load matches and reconcile documents; it cannot independently accept a suspicious carrier identity or release a changed beneficiary. Day 21 reviews every open exception with all three leads. Preserve at least 25% of staffed operating capacity for disruptions rather than filling every hour with theoretical load throughput. Assign contractor tracking coverage by booked hours and escalation scope, not by an assumption of unlimited availability. Reconcile the scorecard exactly: $1,000,000 at 3% produces $30,000 annual owner profit; $3,000,000 at 5.5% produces $165,000; $5,000,000 at 8% produces $400,000. These are gross-billings scenarios including carrier costs, not brokerage spread presented as additional revenue. Actual principal-versus-agent accounting presentation requires a separate assessment. The Scaled case adds a fourth full-time person and third contractor; it is not the five-person pod working indefinitely beyond capacity.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the counterparty and pricing record
   - Save the approved shipper entity, credit headroom, carrier identity, and timestamped rate assumptions under one load identifier.
   - A changed customer entity or performing carrier requires a fresh check before any tender proceeds.
2. Calculate the incremental settlement obligation
   - Enter carrier pay, accessorial exposure, payment date, and stressed customer receipt date into the daily cash forecast.
   - Include existing commitments and restricted funds; an attractive load cannot consume cash already owed elsewhere.
3. Release only an executable shipment
   - Confirm active authority, verified counterparties, accepted rates, tracking coverage, and sufficient unrestricted settlement cash.
   - The operations lead owns tender release; the finance lead independently confirms credit and liquidity.
4. Close the shipment and preserve the evidence
   - Match delivery evidence, approved accessorials, customer invoice, carrier invoice, and eventual cash receipts and payments.
   - Report disputes immediately and retain required brokerage transaction records for at least the applicable three-year period.

## 4. Days 22-30 and Reviews 30/60/90: Separate Readiness, Repair, and Funded Expansion  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Day 22 refreshes every outstanding proposal and records a buyer-confirmed next action. Day 23 finishes twelve qualified lane interviews or documents the shortfall by account. Day 24 obtains three written pilot proposals with named procurement contacts and requested decision dates. Day 25 repeats carrier identity checks for likely pilot capacity and confirms two options per lane. Day 26 tests the actual invoice submission path with each willing shipper's accounts-payable team. Day 27 rehearses the contractor handoff through a missed appointment and failed tracking signal. Day 28 refreshes the thirteen-week cash forecast, separating unrestricted cash, restricted security, receivables, and executable credit facilities. Day 29 freezes the evidence packet and assigns an owner and deadline to every unresolved defect. Day 30 makes the documented Kill, Fix, or Scale decision against the matrix. An independent launch still awaiting active authority can finish research and administrative preparation, but cannot dispatch merely to satisfy a calendar milestone. No completed load in thirty days is compatible with the canonical first-deal window. Missing authority, cash, or counterparty integrity is fundamentally different from a qualified buyer following a slow procurement calendar.

Define the decision vocabulary before emotions attach to pipeline value. Kill means stop new commitments, preserve records, collect receivables, and settle existing obligations; it does not mean abandoning freight or withholding carrier payments. Fix means a bounded fourteen-day remediation cycle with no capacity increase and a named owner for each failed metric. Scale means only the next specified increment, not immediate pursuit of $5,000,000 annual billings. At Day 30, Scale authorizes a controlled pilot program when authority, liquidity, carrier checks, and commercial evidence are complete. At Day 60, operational expansion requires twenty completed loads and ten matched customer-receipt and carrier-payment records. At Day 90, require fifty completed loads and thirty matched settlements before widening volume. Insufficient samples produce Fix, not invented percentages. Define on-time service against the contractual appointment window; count a load as compliant only when both pickup and delivery qualify. Use every completed load in the trailing thirty-day measurement period. Contribution includes actual variable costs and the planning risk allowance, while collections and credit exposure remain separate tests. Unresolved identity or payment diversion incidents override attractive average margins.

Review the matrix in priority order: Kill conditions override everything, Scale requires every listed condition, and all remaining cases receive Fix. At Days 60 and 90, distinguish a slow but documented procurement cycle from an exhausted corridor thesis. Do not kill solely because the first deal has not arrived before the canonical six-month outer window; kill continued spending when the funded runway or repeated market evidence no longer supports it. Each Fix cycle specifies cash cost, completion date, and observable success. A second failed cycle triggers corridor reassessment before further discretionary acquisition spending. Protect the reserve even when reducing launch activity: payroll, contract termination, existing freight, and customer collections still need an orderly plan. Limit an approved capacity increase to 25% above the trailing four-week average weekly completed loads for the next two weeks, rounding down to whole loads. Recheck cash and coverage before each tender. If the existing pod cannot retain 25% workload headroom, fund staffing before expanding. Move toward four full-time people plus three contractors only with documented recurring demand and affordable coverage. Annual owner-profit scenarios remain planning endpoints, never a substitute for collected cash and reconciled obligations.
