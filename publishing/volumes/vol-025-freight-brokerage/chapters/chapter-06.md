## 1. Map Eight Demand Plays to Freight You Can Actually Control  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a lane thesis, not a purchased directory of transportation managers. Choose one equipment class, two origin clusters, and a delivery problem that a small verified carrier bench can solve repeatedly. A workable initial thesis is palletized, nonhazardous packaged merchandise moving from New Jersey import warehouses to Pennsylvania and Ohio distribution centers. Exclude temperature control, unusually high cargo values, and regulated products until coverage and operating competence match those exposures. Mine municipal planning agendas for warehouse openings, company hiring pages for transportation coordinators, port statistics for changing commodity flows, and exhibitor directories for manufacturers adding distribution. Public ocean manifest data can identify some importers, but confidentiality requests, intermediary names, and missing shipment modes make it incomplete. Treat ImportYeti as discovery, not proof of present purchasing authority. Confirm each signal through the company's published switchboard or website before adding a contact. Record the dated source, likely shipping facility, probable equipment, and specific question that could falsify the opportunity. A warehouse lease alone does not establish shipping volume, control over routing, or creditworthiness. The useful lead is a named operating buyer who can explain which loads they control and what failure currently costs.

The eight-play map below contains one supplied cross-sector identifier and seven chapter-local freight sourcing plays; those seven names are operating labels rather than additional canonical candidate IDs. Each play sells transportation coordination, not ownership of the customer's merchandise. Import replenishment captures recurring inland moves after customs release. Facility launches capture temporary imbalance before routing guides stabilize. Appointment recovery sells execution against a documented receiving constraint, while seasonal overflow sells bounded reserve capacity rather than unlimited availability. Industrial service parts justify urgency only when commodity, weight, dimensions, and pickup readiness are verified. Reverse logistics works when inspection, packaging, and disposition responsibilities are explicit. Complementary backhauls work when genuine carrier positioning lowers cost without forcing missed appointments elsewhere. Quote these offers through the same worksheet: shipper charge, carrier commitment, expected variable service cost, maximum cargo exposure, payment timing, and fallback capacity. For illustration, a $2,500 shipper charge against $2,100 carrier cost produces $400 spread, not $400 owner profit. Tracking, sales labor, payment costs, claims, and fixed overhead still consume that spread. The table's prices are lane hypotheses for quote testing, not current market rates or substitutes for the volume's canonical annual scenarios. Reject a play when its required service cannot survive the stated fallback.

The supplied authorized_distributor_grey_market_diversion_arbitrage play uses a hypothesized 35–60% regional merchandise price gap; that is a sourcing premise for the importer, not a brokerage margin. A foreign purchasing entity buying authentic goods does not resolve trademark, customs, labeling, contract, or product restrictions. Kirtsaeng concerned copyright exhaustion for lawfully made copies under 17 U.S.C. § 109(a); it does not provide blanket clearance for branded cosmetics, watches, or medical consumables. Source freight prospects among established importers holding documented purchase orders, a responsible importer of record, customs-broker support, and product-specific admissibility evidence. Do not structure concealed routing to defeat distributor restrictions or misstate origin, value, or ownership. A $100,000 merchandise order with $4,000 inland freight yields $4,000 gross shipper billings for this model, not $104,000. If carrier cost is $3,300, the initial freight spread is $700 before other expenses. The fallback is globally identical universal-packaging SKUs sold through direct B2B orders to off-price chains or regional wholesalers, with lawful entry and product requirements still independently checked. Universal packaging reduces some mismatch risks but does not establish admissibility. If documents or authority are deficient, decline the load and pursue ordinary domestic replenishment from the same qualified buyer.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the Controllable Load Behind the Public Signal**
> 
> The profitable information is the shipment constraint that a buyer can authorize and a carrier can execute.
> - Ask who chooses the carrier under the purchase order; an importer with attractive volumes may receive entirely supplier-controlled freight.
> - Request five redacted recent tenders and their accessorial outcomes before quoting a recurring lane; averages hide detention-heavy receivers.
> - Ask verified carriers where they consistently finish empty, then prospect compatible origin facilities without promising those carriers exclusive freight.
> - Offer a paid recovery pilot on one ordinary, insurable load before accepting a high-value fragrance or medical-consumables shipment.

## 2. Turn Public Signals into a Credit-Qualified, Lane-Specific Account Queue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a weekly research batch of fifty accounts using one repeatable record structure in HubSpot or a controlled spreadsheet. Save company legal name, operating address, source URL, signal date, switchboard, buyer role, equipment hypothesis, and next verification action. Separate an account from its contacts and lanes so three employees do not inflate demand counts. Match the billing entity against the relevant secretary of state's business registry, then confirm whether the actual shipper, parent, or purchasing intermediary will sign and pay. Corporate registration establishes identity clues, not solvency. SAM.gov opportunities can reveal logistics demand, but bid requirements and procurement calendars make many unsuitable for the first pilot. State permit records and local economic-development announcements are often faster signals because they name operating facilities before national prospecting lists update. Use job advertisements as corroboration, not permission to scrape personal contact details or represent a hiring need as a shipping emergency. Refresh the highest-ranked signals weekly and archive stale accounts after a defined period. Every lead should end with a buyer-verifiable hypothesis: which facility, which load type, which timing problem, and why the incumbent arrangement might leave an economically serviceable gap.

Score qualified accounts on six dimensions totaling one hundred points: twenty for routing authority, twenty for equipment and lane fit, twenty for payment quality, fifteen for a quantified service problem, fifteen for credible recurring volume, and ten for shipment readiness. Award full routing points only when the buyer identifies their tender authority and contracting entity. Give lane-fit points only after dispatch checks equipment, geography, appointments, and at least two viable carrier options. Payment quality requires an internally approved exposure limit supported by trade references, credit information, or prepaid terms; a famous customer name alone earns nothing. A seventy-five-point threshold can prioritize interviews, but cannot override a failed identity check or insufficient cash. Ask for origin and destination postal codes, commodity description, declared value, dimensions, weight, pickup readiness, accessorial history, payment terms, and weekly frequency. Request a redacted bill of lading and tender where available. Record whether the prospect controls only emergency loads or a recurring routing allocation, because those are different revenue opportunities. Disqualify evasive requests to conceal the performing carrier, change beneficiaries without verification, or dispatch before written terms. Concentrate selling time on loads that can be priced, funded, and delivered without improvising core controls.

Create a carrier bench alongside the account queue rather than after a shipper accepts a quote. Highway or RMIS can organize onboarding and identity signals, but neither replaces independent verification of the specific operating carrier. Cross-check FMCSA Licensing and Insurance for authority and filings, use SAFER for identity and operating context, and confirm cargo coverage directly with the insurer or authorized agent through independently sourced contact details. Review exclusions, deductibles, commodity restrictions, geographic limits, and the actual named insured. A federal insurance filing is not proof that the contemplated cargo is covered. Call the carrier through an independently verified number to confirm dispatch authority, equipment, driver assignment, and payment instructions. Escalate sudden email-domain changes, mismatched entity names, recent bank changes, or refusal to identify the performing carrier. Contractual restrictions on rebrokering must be supported by pickup verification and shipment-level monitoring. At pickup, compare the arriving carrier and equipment with the dispatch record through the facility's established contact channel. If the identity chain breaks, pause release and notify the shipper. Replacement capacity is cheaper than funding a load whose custody, insurance, and eventual payment recipient cannot be reliably established.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Required Checks Before the First Tender**
> 
> A strong sourcing score never substitutes for authority, identity, insurability, or funded payment obligations.
> - Verify the brokerage's active FMCSA authority, process-agent designation, and required $75,000 BMC-84 bond or BMC-85 trust arrangements before arranging covered interstate transportation.
> - Match the shipper's contracting entity, authorized signer, routing control, approved credit limit, and independently verified accounts-payable contact.
> - Verify the actual performing carrier, active authority, shipment-specific insurance suitability, dispatch identity, and independently confirmed payment instructions.
> - Reserve cash for carrier obligations under the pilot's actual collection assumptions, and secure written rate, accessorial, appointment, and exception terms.

## 3. Run a Measured Outreach Cadence and Close a Bounded Paid Pilot  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use a six-touch, twenty-one-day cadence that adds information rather than repeating a generic capacity pitch. On day one, send a short business email naming the observed facility change and one lane-specific hypothesis. On day three, call the published office number to identify the person who controls that lane. On day six, send a useful artifact: a pickup readiness checklist, appointment exception template, or clearly dated indicative quote with explicit assumptions. On day ten, use a professional network message to the verified role without implying an existing relationship. On day fourteen, ask whether a single paid pilot would be useful; on day twenty-one, close the loop and offer a relevant follow-up date. Commercial email must use accurate headers and subjects, identify its commercial nature as required, include a valid postal address and opt-out method, and honor opt-outs within the statutory period. Keep a suppression list across systems. Use manually placed business calls; automated calls and texts introduce additional federal and state requirements. Stop when the recipient declines. Publish one substantive landing page for the chosen lane cluster with a shipment form that requests operating facts, not sensitive banking information.

Model the first cohort before buying contact data or increasing outreach volume. An illustrative eight-week batch of four hundred unique accounts might produce three hundred verified contacts, sixty substantive conversations, twenty-four qualified lane opportunities, twelve written pilot proposals, six signed pilot agreements, five completed paid pilots, and three repeat customers. Those imply contact-to-conversation conversion of 20%, conversation-to-qualified conversion of 40%, proposal-to-signature conversion of 50%, and raw-account-to-paid-pilot conversion of 1.25%. They are planning assumptions, not observed industry benchmarks. Define a win here as one distinct shipper whose pilot shipment is completed and paid; multiple loads from that shipper remain one account win. Separate signed agreements, completed loads, collected invoices, and repeat orders in the CRM. With thirty-day shipper terms, an eight-week selling cohort may require another month before collection confirms the win. Three to ten wins within two to six months therefore require overlapping cohorts, disciplined follow-up, and capacity to execute what sales closes. Diagnose the narrowest stage first: weak conversations suggest poor targeting, weak proposals suggest inadequate qualification, and signed but unshipped pilots suggest missing trust, timing, or commercial detail. Do not solve every failure by adding leads.

Handle objections with a narrower commitment and evidence, not a discount that destroys the service budget. When the buyer says the incumbent is cheaper, compare the same appointment window, fuel treatment, accessorial schedule, equipment, and payment terms; quote differences explicitly rather than attacking the incumbent. When they already have brokers, offer one defined overflow lane without demanding replacement of the routing guide. When authority is newly established, provide verifiable registrations, documented procedures, and genuine staff experience without borrowing another company's operating history. When the buyer wants sixty-day terms, offer prepaid pilots or a smaller exposure cap before committing carrier cash. When guaranteed capacity is requested, specify reservation duration, cancellation terms, and the replacement procedure instead of promising unlimited trucks. Close with a written pilot covering one to three loads, named facilities, commodity, value, equipment, service windows, rates, accessorial triggers, tracking expectations, claims contacts, and payment terms. Record who can authorize changes and how detention evidence is captured. A quote becomes an operational commitment only after carrier acceptance and the required checks. Review the pilot with the buyer using arrival timestamps, exception handling, invoice accuracy, and collection results; ask for a small recurring allocation only when those facts support expansion.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Research one fifty-account cohort
   - Collect dated public signals and verify company identity, operating facility, and likely routing owner.
   - Deduplicate by contracting entity and distinguish separate contacts from separate potential customers.
2. Qualify the controllable shipment
   - Apply the hundred-point scorecard and collect actual lane, commodity, timing, value, and payment facts.
   - Advance scores of at least seventy-five only when all mandatory identity, authority, and cash checks pass.
3. Contract and execute the paid pilot
   - Agree one to three loads with explicit rates, accessorial rules, appointments, tracking, and payment terms.
   - Confirm performing-carrier identity and shipment suitability before dispatch, then record pickup and delivery evidence.
4. Collect and earn the next allocation
   - Reconcile the invoice, confirm collection, review exceptions with the buyer, and request a bounded recurring lane.
   - Count a paid account win once; monitor additional loads separately as retention and volume expansion.

## 4. Fund the Float, Reconcile the Scorecard, and Expand Only Proven Lanes  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Underwrite the payment gap before treating a promising lane as a sales success. Assume ten weekly loads billed at $2,500 each, with carriers owed $2,100 per load. Weekly gross billings are $25,000 and carrier obligations are $21,000. If carriers are paid seven days after delivery and shippers pay thirty-five days after delivery, the steady-state timing difference is twenty-eight days, requiring approximately four weeks of carrier cost, or $84,000, before separate reserves. A further two-week collection delay adds approximately $42,000. This simplified model assumes level volume, prompt invoicing, and no disputes; actual daily cash forecasting must incorporate shipment dates and individual terms. The canonical $15,000–$80,000 startup capital range therefore does not support every growth plan equally, especially with monthly operating expense of $6,000–$30,000. A $75,000 broker bond is security, not unrestricted working cash; bond premiums and collateral differ from its penal amount. Trust funding also consumes resources according to the arrangement. Establish customer exposure limits covering unpaid invoices and committed, uninvoiced loads. If a customer exceeds the limit, pause new tenders, request payment, or reduce the allocation rather than quietly financing additional volume.

Keep the canonical scenarios separate from experimental lane spreads. Conservative means $1,000,000 annual gross shipper billings, a 3% net margin, and $30,000 annual owner net profit with three full-time staff and two contractors. Base means $3,000,000 gross billings, a 5.5% net margin, and $165,000 owner net profit with the same staffing. Scaled means $5,000,000 gross billings, an 8% net margin, and $400,000 owner net profit with four full-time staff and three contractors. At an illustrative constant $2,500 average invoice, those totals require 400, 1,200, and 2,000 annual loads respectively. The load counts illustrate throughput; they do not establish that each scenario shares the same carrier spread or cost structure. Annual total costs implied by the canonical profit figures are $970,000, $2,835,000, and $4,600,000 respectively, including carrier costs and other operating expenses. Maintain distinct ledger fields for shipper billings, carrier payables, brokerage spread, operating costs, and owner net profit. Accounting presentation can differ under principal-versus-agent analysis, so preserve a reconciliation to this scorecard's gross-billings basis. Never add merchandise purchase value to freight billings, and never annualize a successful first pilot as though seasonality, losses, and collection delays disappear.

Run a weekly account review that joins commercial activity to execution and cash. Track verified leads, conversations, qualified lanes, proposals, signed pilots, completed loads, collected pilots, repeat accounts, gross spread, accessorial recovery, receivable aging, and identity exceptions. Measure pickup and delivery performance against agreed windows rather than a vague on-time label. A three-account launch can be healthier than ten uncollected pilots with disputed invoices. Use load matching to shorten search time, shipment tracking to expose exceptions, and invoice reconciliation to catch rate discrepancies; retain human review for carrier identity changes and customer credit expansion. Store the transaction records required by 49 CFR § 371.3 for three years and support the inspection rights specified there. Document the performing carrier and all commercially relevant exceptions so a later claim does not depend on a salesperson's memory. Offer a recurring allocation only after the pilot's service, payment, and contribution are visible. Increase weekly volume in steps that the cash forecast and backup carrier bench can support. If claims, nonpayment, or identity anomalies appear, stop incremental exposure and resolve the root cause before restarting. Sourcing quality is ultimately demonstrated by repeat collected freight, not the number of names entered into a CRM.
