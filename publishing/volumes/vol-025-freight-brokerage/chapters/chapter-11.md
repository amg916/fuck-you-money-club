## Executive Summary

Candidate #12, Freight Brokerage, ranks #25 with a 62/100 Money-to-Manpower Score: this is a working-capital business, not passive software income. Structure three employee seats and two bounded contractor engagements around eight mapped plays: niche acquisition, shipper credit, carrier verification, pricing and matching, shipment control, billing and collections, claims recovery, and transferable governance. Expand to four employees and three contractors only when collected contribution supports the additional capacity. The canonical annual owner profits are $30,000 Conservative, $165,000 Base, and $400,000 Scaled, against gross shipper billings of $1,000,000, $3,000,000, and $5,000,000. Weekly evidence, concentration limits, clean accruals, and signed intellectual-property assignments make those earnings more durable and give buyers something transferable to purchase beyond the founder's telephone.

## 1. Assign eight plays to five seats before buying more capacity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the Base pod around accountable outcomes rather than interchangeable dispatch labor. Count the working founder inside the three full-time seats; otherwise the staffing model quietly understates labor. Seat one owns commercial strategy, shipper credit decisions, and treasury. Seat two owns carrier procurement, verification, and load execution. Seat three owns customer operations, billing, collections, and the accounting close. Two contractor engagements provide bounded tracking coverage and document reconciliation, respectively. The chapter's eight-play operating map is P1 niche acquisition, P2 shipper credit, P3 carrier verification, P4 pricing and matching, P5 shipment control, P6 billing and collections, P7 claims recovery, and P8 transferable governance. These labels organize the eight required plays into executable workstreams; they do not add eight departments. Assign one employee owner and one trained backup to every play. Use a TMS such as AscendTMS or Tai to preserve load histories, and keep the accounting ledger authoritative for posted balances. Contractors receive scoped access rather than shared employee credentials. No contractor independently changes carrier banking, releases payments, or approves a credit extension. Those restrictions protect the shipper-to-carrier float and prevent a busy operations queue from becoming an alternate route around financial controls.

Contract for measurable deliverables, not an employee schedule disguised by an invoice. An illustrative tracking engagement pays $18 per accepted shipment packet covering pickup confirmation, agreed milestones, exception escalation, and delivery evidence. At 100 accepted packets, the fee is $1,800; this is a purchased service expense, not incremental brokerage revenue. Define acceptance through timestamps and evidence completeness, with correction of defective submissions included in the stated fee. For a reconciliation specialist, an illustrative $7 accepted invoice package requires the rate confirmation, proof of delivery, accessorial support, and matching ledger reference. Pay uncontested accepted work promptly and document disputed items separately. Measure contractor-controlled response time rather than making compensation depend on whether a carrier actually arrives on time. The written SLA can require critical exceptions to reach the employee owner within fifteen minutes of detection during purchased coverage, with ordinary document defects corrected within one business day. Employee status depends on actual working conditions and applicable federal and state tests, not the contract heading. If the engagement requires continuing personal supervision, mandatory individual shifts, and employee-like integration, redesign it as a genuine vendor service or budget an employee. Keep classification review separate from the commercial decision about which deliverables the pod needs.

Expand to four full-time employees and three contractors by adding an employee account manager and a bounded carrier-research service, not another founder-dependent salesperson. The account manager absorbs routine shipper communication and develops second relationships inside existing accounts. The research contractor assembles carrier evidence; the procurement employee still decides whether a carrier may receive freight. Highway and RMIS support verification workflows but do not replace direct checks of authority, insurance evidence, identity, and suspicious changes. Require coverage plans before hiring: who answers a delayed pickup, who confirms a bank change through an independently sourced contact, and who releases a disputed invoice? Under 49 CFR 371.2, an independent dispatch business arranging transportation can raise broker-authority questions; define the contractor's permitted actions and assess the actual arrangement. For the FMCSA-licensed broker itself, maintain required registration and $75,000 financial security under 49 U.S.C. 13906(b) and applicable regulations. The security instrument is neither operating cash nor a substitute for paying carriers. The Scaled scenario's seven people must cover the eight plays through documented handoffs. Add capacity only after a rolling contribution forecast, a funded cash calendar, and observed queue pressure support its cost without assuming that every new load converts immediately into collected cash.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy bounded capacity without outsourcing judgment**
> 
> Contractor leverage comes from repeatable evidence packages, while employees retain commercial commitments, financial authority, and exception decisions.
> - Price tracking packages by milestones and coverage windows; a live refrigerated exception is not equivalent to an ordinary dry-van status update.
> - Use Highway or RMIS findings to create a review queue; independently confirm suspicious carrier contacts before issuing a rate confirmation.
> - Keep contractor compensation outside carrier-payable accounts so invoice reconciliation never disguises labor expense as purchased transportation.
> - Run one founder-absence drill monthly: the backup must locate rate terms, escalation contacts, and collection history without a private message.

## 2. Run one weekly scorecard with hard denominators and concentration limits  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run the weekly operating meeting from a frozen Monday export covering the previous service week, with later corrections visible in a separate adjustment column. Every metric needs an owner, denominator, source, and corrective action. P1 measures qualified shipper opportunities and contribution retained by cohort, not calls placed. P2 measures credit exposure and overdue receivables against approved limits. P3 reports the percentage of dispatched loads with complete carrier-verification evidence, plus attempts stopped before tender. P4 measures accrued contribution per load and quote-to-tender conversion by lane. P5 measures pickup and delivery performance against the contractual appointment definition, with customer changes separately identified. P6 measures invoice cycle time, first-pass acceptance, and days sales outstanding. P7 measures open claim exposure, evidence age, and recoveries actually collected. P8 measures close timeliness, procedure coverage, and founder-only dependencies. A shipment whose final carrier invoice has not arrived still belongs in operating contribution using a supportable accrual. Do not reward a procurement lead for margin that disappears when detention arrives. Finish the meeting with named actions due before the next review, rather than a list of observations nobody owns. Keep the same definitions when results deteriorate; changing the denominator destroys trend credibility.

Set an internal concentration cap of no more than 25% for each shipper group across trailing-twelve-month billings, accrued contribution, and receivables; evaluate each measure independently. Apply the same ceiling to each carrier group across purchased transportation spend and outstanding payables. Aggregate commonly controlled entities, because separate legal names do not create independent risk. Use a 20% early-warning threshold to start diversification before a growing account breaches the ceiling. A new brokerage may initially exceed the target with its first customer; record that launch exception, reduce absolute credit exposure, and show a dated acquisition plan rather than describing the book as diversified. Once the operating book matures, stop increasing an over-limit exposure without a documented reduction plan and funded loss capacity. Include factoring counterparties and shared payment destinations in fraud analysis, while avoiding the mistake of treating every carrier using one factor as commonly owned. Carrier fraud, claims, and shipper nonpayment can strike different lines of the same load. Track those risks separately so an insured cargo incident does not conceal an uninsured collection problem. Weekly concentration reporting should reconcile to the ledger, not to a salesperson's preferred account list.

Make compensation reinforce those definitions. For an employee commercial incentive, an illustrative 8% of eligible collected contribution can work only after carrier costs, approved accessorials, customer credits, and attributable claim adjustments are reconciled. Define earning dates and adjustment rules in writing and observe applicable wage-payment requirements; do not invent retroactive deductions after a bad month. Contractor fees should instead follow accepted deliverables under the service agreement, with invoice disputes supported by specific defects. Review every identity exception, banking change, and material invoice variance, then sample at least ten ordinary completed loads weekly, or all loads if fewer than ten closed. That sample is a workload rule, not a statistical assurance claim. Score evidence completeness separately from business outcomes: a delayed truck can still have excellent exception handling, while an on-time truck can conceal an unauthorized substitution. Escalate repeated defects to revised scope, retraining, or vendor replacement. Record who reviewed the work and which source established acceptance. A buyer should be able to reproduce a week's contractor bill and employee incentive from stored records without asking the founder what the parties informally agreed. This discipline also protects a durable cash cow from quiet margin leakage.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the operating population
   - Export the prior week's delivered, canceled, and still-open loads.
   - Record the extraction time and reconcile shipment identifiers to TMS and ledger control totals.
2. Validate evidence and exceptions
   - Review all high-risk events and the ordinary-load QA sample.
   - Inspect carrier identity, bank changes, invoice support, contractor acceptance, and concentration by controlled group.
3. Reconcile economic performance
   - Update carrier accruals, customer credits, claims estimates, and cash timing.
   - Separate billings, load contribution, operating expenses, owner profit, and cash movements before discussing incentives.
4. Assign corrective work
   - Publish an owner, due date, and closure evidence for each material exception.
   - Carry unresolved items into the next scorecard and test completed fixes against source records.

## 3. Reconcile load accruals, working capital, and the canonical profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Preserve the scorecard's economics exactly: Conservative is $1,000,000 of annual gross shipper billings at 3% net margin, producing $30,000 of annual owner net profit with three employees and two contractors. Base is $3,000,000 at 5.5%, producing $165,000 with the same five people. Scaled is $5,000,000 at 8%, producing $400,000 with four employees and three contractors. Corresponding total expenses are $970,000, $2,835,000, and $4,600,000. These are scenario totals, not claims that purchased transportation alone equals those amounts. The canonical revenue basis includes carrier costs; a broker's accounting presentation can differ following its principal-versus-agent assessment. Maintain a reconciliation from gross billings to the financial-statement presentation and never label the entire shipper invoice as earned brokerage spread. As a load-level illustration, a $2,500 shipper charge less $2,100 carrier cost yields $400 before other load costs and overhead. An additional $50 expected accessorial reduces that contribution to $350 unless a supportable customer charge offsets it. Neither contribution amount is owner profit. Test actual staffing costs against the scenario expense envelope, particularly at Conservative scale, where $30,000 leaves little room for omitted founder labor, insurance increases, or collection losses.

Close by service period using a consistent revenue-recognition policy and shipment cutoff, not by whichever invoices reached the inbox first. Link each recognized customer amount to earned service evidence and accrue the matching carrier obligation from the accepted rate and supportable adjustments. Reconcile completed-but-unbilled loads, carrier invoices awaiting receipt, disputed accessorials, credit memos, and claim estimates every month. Track customer cohorts by first shipment month, service niche, lane group, and acquisition source. Show gross billings, purchased transportation, contribution, credit losses, and attributable service expense for each cohort without allocating the same overhead twice. Preserve revisions to estimates so apparent improvement cannot arise from quietly releasing prior reserves. Under 49 CFR 371.3, maintain the required brokerage transaction records for three years and support transaction parties' applicable review rights; a TMS export should connect to the underlying documents. Retention for taxes, contracts, litigation holds, or insurance can require a longer schedule. Use QuickBooks or another ledger with controlled posting permissions and a documented close checklist. A contractor can prepare a reconciliation, but an employee reviews the proposed journal entries. That separation makes both diligence and ordinary error correction faster.

Model liquidity separately from profit. For a clearly illustrative Base cash calculation, assume carrier purchases equal 85% of $3,000,000 annual billings, or $2,550,000, customer collections average 45 days, and carrier payments average 21 days. Steady-state receivables are approximately $369,863 and carrier payables approximately $146,712, leaving $223,151 tied up before other current assets and liabilities. This balance-sheet calculation is more complete than multiplying carrier spend by the payment-day gap because receivables include the brokerage spread. It is not a revised canonical margin: the assumed $450,000 contribution before other expenses must still cover $285,000 to reach Base owner profit of $165,000. Weekly cash planning must also consider seasonality, disputed invoices, quick-pay discounts, factor reserves, and claim payments. Compare the resulting cash requirement with the stated $50,000–$250,000 startup capital range and actual committed facilities, rather than treating that range as automatic funding. Maintain a thirteen-week cash forecast with named receipts and payment obligations. The stated $6,000–$30,000 monthly operating-expense range is planning context; carrier disbursements and working-capital growth can exceed it materially. Never use the $75,000 broker security requirement as the cash forecast's available balance.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before increasing the pod's load commitments**
> 
> These checks authorize internal operating capacity only; they do not replace the responsible employee's shipment-level judgment.
> - Verify active broker authority and required $75,000 security through official records; confirm current financial-responsibility obligations under 49 CFR 387.307.
> - Verify that shipper and carrier group concentration remains within each applicable 25% ceiling, or record and fund a specific reduction plan.
> - Verify that the thirteen-week cash forecast covers carrier obligations under delayed customer collections without counting the security instrument as cash.
> - Verify carrier identity, signed contractor scope, employee backup coverage, and reproducible load-level accruals before increasing weekly tender volume.

## 4. Sell transferable earnings or retain a genuinely durable cash cow  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A buyer pays for earnings that survive a transfer, not a directory of shippers who answer only the founder. Maintain at least two substantive contacts at each material account, company-controlled email and telephone channels, written service expectations, lane-level history, and account plans another employee can execute. Review customer agreements for assignment and change-of-control provisions; an asset sale and an equity sale can trigger different requirements. Confirm how broker registration, financial security, insurance, and vendor subscriptions will be maintained through the proposed transaction rather than assuming everything moves automatically. Require signed intellectual-property assignments covering contractor-created procedures, integration code, dashboards, and training material. Under 17 U.S.C. 204(a), transfers of copyright ownership generally require a signed writing; payment alone does not supply that instrument. Distinguish assigned deliverables from preexisting vendor tools and obtain sufficient continuing licenses where ownership stays with the vendor. Commercial relationships and factual shipment data raise different contractual rights from copyright ownership. Keep customer data rights, confidentiality obligations, and export permissions explicit. A controlled diligence room should contain executed agreements and redacted examples first, with sensitive shipment and counterparty information released only under appropriate transaction arrangements and documented access controls.

Use valuation multiples as underwriting sensitivities, not quoted market facts. For an illustrative small owner-operated brokerage, test 2.0–3.0 times normalized seller discretionary earnings; for a genuinely transferable, management-covered operation, test 3.0–5.0 times adjusted EBITDA. These are different earnings definitions and buyer populations, not interchangeable labels. Scale, concentration, fraud history, claims, contractual durability, and credit exposure can move a bid outside either range. Build a bridge from the canonical owner net profit to the selected valuation measure, identifying interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, owner compensation, replacement labor, and supportable nonrecurring items only where the underlying accounts justify them. Do not simply multiply $400,000 by five and call the result a defensible price. If diligence independently establishes adjusted EBITDA of $250,000, the illustrative 3.0–5.0 range yields $750,000–$1,250,000 of enterprise value. That example does not replace any canonical scenario or assert that its earnings bridge has been proven. Negotiate the normal working-capital target separately, including treatment of carrier payables, disputed receivables, and claims-related balances. Establish which liabilities are ordinary working capital and which are debt-like items so the same obligation is not deducted twice.

Compare seller proceeds with the cash-cow alternative using explicit ownership labor and capital requirements. Enterprise value converts to equity proceeds through cash, debt, debt-like adjustments, transaction expenses, and the negotiated working-capital true-up. Escrows and earnouts change timing and collection risk; a headline price is not closing cash. For retention, the Scaled case's $400,000 annual owner net profit is the starting point, not automatically distributable passive income. Deduct incremental replacement management expense if the owner stops working and that cost is absent from the scenario, then consider required reinvestment and cash retained for growth or claims. For a sale, prefer earnout definitions tied to verifiable collected contribution with clear cost attribution, reporting access, and limits on discretionary post-close reallocations. Buyers may prefer retention measures, but gross billings alone can reward unprofitable freight. Prepare twelve months of clean monthly closes, concentration trends, contractor acceptance records, and backup-drill evidence before testing interest. A premium becomes more plausible when the next owner can observe stable customers, funded carrier payments, and functioning employees without relying on an extended founder rescue period. The same evidence lets the existing owner retain the business with fewer emergencies and a clearer view of actual distributable cash.
