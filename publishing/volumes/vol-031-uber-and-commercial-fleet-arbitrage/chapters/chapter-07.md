# Chapter 07: Off-Platform Rentals: Building a Private Car Rental Business Without Turo

*Switching from Turo to private rentals, $1,500-per-car monthly models, 10 cars in 30 days, 500-car and 70-car fleet case studies, consignment cars with zero capital, B2B rental contracts, scaling to 50 cars, step-by-step 2026 roadmaps, and launching a local rental business*

## Executive Summary

Private rental operators who left Turo converge on one model: cheap cash-bought economy cars rented weekly to Uber, Lyft and DoorDash drivers who hold them for months, not days. Kel King prices $350-450 per week on $3K-4K cars and demands a 90-day payback; King of Rentals nets about $1,000 per car monthly versus roughly $500 on Turo; Alex Auto runs 50+ cars at 40-50% margins. Leads come from Facebook Marketplace posts titled 'cars for rideshare,' Google Business profiles and $10/day Messenger ads, screened by $3-5/hr overseas VAs, with Spireon GoldStar trackers and kill switches enforcing payment. The fault lines are insurance and payments: Kel pitches personal policies with permissible-driver framing while Demario Mccowan's guest Mo calls that suicide, runs commercial cover, files zero collision claims and takes only Cash App, Zelle and cash.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator who walked off Turo landed on the same customer: the rideshare and delivery driver who needs a car to earn and keeps it for months. Kel King says these renters hold cars six, nine or twelve months, against Turo's three-to-four-day turns, airport runs and dead off-seasons [BD6Xfj5F-Fk @ 00:01:10]. King of Rentals, run by brothers Max and Zach for five years, puts the average gig rental at 60 days versus three on Turo, a 20x gap that drops cleaning from seven $15 turns a month to roughly $7.50 [DOrgMXhPDXs @ 00:04:23]. Alex Auto's first private renter in Baltimore kept one car 16 months, and that single car 'practically bought me four cars' [RSpmJqhFEIw @ 00:01:13]. Kel frames turnover as the real enemy: 'Turnover is where fleets bleed money,' because every gap between renters means re-cleaning, re-listing and re-screening, and a renter who leaves after two weeks costs more than one who pays for eight months straight [EVlxhXnHHH8 @ 00:03:23]. The pricing logic follows directly: weekly billing, unlimited miles, insurance and maintenance bundled into the rate, and the operator keeps 100% of revenue because no platform skims a cut. Kel puts per-car earnings at $1K-2K a month on that basis.

The second consensus is the asset: cheap, boring, cash-bought economy cars. Kel King caps spend at $6,000 all-in, prefers $3K-4K, calls $4,500-5,500 'still fine,' and says never finance and never spend $10K-15K; the shortlist is a Ford Fusion 2.5L or Prius, with Nissans and BMWs banned [BD6Xfj5F-Fk @ 00:11:47]. Alex Auto calls financing the very first mistake of the journey, because the loan keeps billing during downtime and a 2015 rents for about the same as a 2022; Alex's buys run $3,000-5,000 [RSpmJqhFEIw @ 00:03:21]. Mo, the Detroit CEO interviewed on Demario Mccowan's channel, went all-cash after $7,200 a month in car notes plus insurance squeezed the business, and tells anyone holding $3K to buy one $3,000 car and bank its income for the next [taXFg2qEMew @ 00:09:37]. The reinvestment discipline is identical everywhere: Alex's first car made $26,000 in a year and funded three more, and Mo climbed from $10K to $20K to $60K a month while still driving Lyft to cover the bills. Mo's advice to beginners is to start with one car and keep the day job, which Mo did until the fleet reached 60 cars. The yardstick everyone repeats is a 90-day payback; the operator panel on Kel's channel calls it 'the golden rule for this business' [7l7A5buuie0 @ 00:41:31].

Under the model sit four prerequisites the operators keep naming. First, recovery hardware: Kel King runs Gold Star trackers by Spireon, and Arthur Fandunyan, renting since 2020 with 2,000+ reservations, says 'kill switch is main priority,' pairing a hardwired GPS cutoff with an OBD backup on a splitter, a lockbox and an after-hours VA procedure [d5fglf-rFOc @ 00:11:49]. Second, paper: Fandunyan lists an LLC at $100-300, a state rental license, commercial fleet insurance, an EIN, a bank account and an attorney-drafted agreement, 'not ChatGPT.' Car Rental Coach builds a conversion notice into the contract, adds $250 smoking and $200 cleaning fees, prorated late returns and open-ended improper-return charges, and keeps physical plus digital copies [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:11:07]. Third, insurance that actually permits private rental: Car Rental Coach warns that off-rental policies from Tint, Rumbly and ABI void private trips, so the operator needs an off-trip policy that allows it, such as GMI [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:22:23]. Alex Auto pays $86 a month per car for commercial cover and calls it 'super cheap' [RSpmJqhFEIw @ 00:11:05]. Fourth, documented handoffs: Fandunyan shoots 80+ timestamped photos per handoff, and Car Rental Coach takes 50-60 photos with deposits of about $500 on economy cars and $1,000 on pricier ones.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Kel King's buying script is specific: shop Facebook Marketplace, not auctions, demand a clean title and bring a mechanic [BD6Xfj5F-Fk @ 00:13:53]. Mo adds the inspection detail learned over eight years at a Chrysler dealership: target 2010-2012 Fusions with the 2.5L, a '$250 engine' if it dies, walk away from creamy oil because it signals a blown head gasket, itemize needed repairs to negotiate the price down, and match the title VIN to the door tag or windshield tag [-HtlfCgxxtg @ 01:27:39]. Kel's own case car cost about $3,500, rented from December 2024 and earned $18,000+ against roughly $1,300 in expenses, including a $500 valve-cover gasket. Pricing bands converge tightly. King of Rentals charges $350-450 a week for sedans, with $375-425 the safe range, Model 3s at $400-450, Model Ys at $500-600, San Diego and LA running higher, and daily rates of $65-75 sitting about 20% above weekly [DOrgMXhPDXs @ 00:02:04]. A 2010 Civic bought for $2,500-3,000 rents at $250-300 a week in Kel's LA fleet, while a $3K-6K Fusion gets $350-450 [UCJIebqFEj0 @ 00:11:17]. King of Rentals' unit math: $1,500 revenue minus $500 costs leaves $1,000 net per car, against roughly $500 on Turo. Maintenance runs about $125 a month per rideshare car and insurance roughly $100.

Lead flow runs through three channels. Kel King's free Marketplace post is titled 'cars for rideshare,' with 'unlimited miles, insurance included, and maintenance included' in the body plus a walkaround video, and the instruction is blunt: 'Don't try to remix it' [UCJIebqFEj0 @ 00:13:53]. Kel says the format pulls hundreds of leads. Car Rental Coach skips Marketplace rental listings and runs Facebook Messenger lead ads with AI copy and images inside a 15-20 mile radius, where $10 a day returns two to three qualified leads daily, backed by a Google Business profile carrying the city name, 10+ photos per car and reviews [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:20:33]. Arthur Fandunyan works the Hertz, Enterprise and Budget counters with business cards and pays body shops $25-50 per referral [d5fglf-rFOc @ 00:14:25]. Fandunyan warns that gig drivers put 1,500-3,000 miles a week on a car, so they need hard vetting. Screening is where the models split hardest. Kel's overseas team, paid about $3 an hour, filters every lead because 'They're not accepting everybody.' Mo runs no background checks at all, saying they would wipe out 95-98% of the clientele, and requires only a driver's license, age 23+, a flat $300 deposit plus the first week up front, and a gut-feel call on renewals [taXFg2qEMew @ 00:35:57].

Collections run weekly and up front. Mo's fleet bills weekly only, no dailies, through Connect Pro, built by Mo's wife, which fires automated Monday texts asking renters to return or extend; before that, at about 50 cars, the team sent manual invoices and scheduled 9 a.m. texts, and a missed invoice meant 'Most actually will not pay' [-HtlfCgxxtg @ 01:40:41]. After losing a PayPal chargeback to a renter who paid with a third party's card, Mo cut cards and went straight to Cash App, Zelle and cash. Apple Pay, Mo adds, kicks accounts off above $100K a year. Car Rental Coach calls card disputes 'a 50/50' and takes the first payment by Zelle or ACH with a $1 hold on a card kept on file [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:27:50]. Software sits on top: Kel uses HQ as the CRM, Mike_Thebusinessman runs Fleet Finesse at $97 a month standard or $147 premium with license verification, tolls and insurance sales, and King of Rentals points to iFleet at $99 a month after a 14-day trial [pXYcnDt9ASA @ 00:01:46]. Staffing scales by fleet size: Fandunyan hires a $3-5/hr VA at 10-20 cars, a fleet coordinator next and an operations manager at 50+ [d5fglf-rFOc @ 00:19:29]. Kel says one person can solo 12 cars at $20K a month.

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split in private rentals is how the car gets insured. Kel King teaches the low-cost route: a personal policy, the renter framed as a permissible driver, and no mention of rental. Bonzah is the pricier fallback and GMI the commercial option [BD6Xfj5F-Fk @ 00:14:07]. Detroit operator Mo, now running 300-plus cash cars, calls that exact play his biggest early mistake and labels it lying. His insurer came back asking, "Does this person live with you?" and his fix was commercial insurance "right off rip" [-HtlfCgxxtg @ 01:34:09]. In his second Demario Mccowan sit-down he goes further and calls renting on personal insurance "suicide" [taXFg2qEMew @ 00:28:48]. Car Rental Coach adds a third layer that most Turo converts miss. Off-rental policies built for platforms, including Tint, Rumbly and ABI, void private rentals. The operator needs an off-trip policy that explicitly allows private rentals, with GMI as his example. The operator then either sells coverage to the renter or verifies the renter's own policy through Canopy or a call to the insurer, and uninsured renters get pushed to Turo [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:22:23]. Mo rejects that last option outright. He never uses a client's insurance because most of his customers have none. He self-insures collision and lets the carrier handle liability only.

Payment rails and rental length split the room just as hard. Mo runs weekly rentals only, no dailies. After a renter won a dispute by paying with a third party's card, he "completely cut that out" and moved to Cash App, Zelle and cash only. He also warns that Apple Pay drops you above $100K a year and that Cash App disputes can reverse funds [-HtlfCgxxtg @ 01:44:42]. Car Rental Coach calls card disputes "a 50/50" and splits the difference. He takes the first payment by Zelle or ACH, then keeps a card on file with a $1 hold [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:28:04]. His own first win cuts against Mo's weekly-only rule, though. He billed a 2008 Kia Spectra at $60 a day through a daily Square invoice for a full year and cleared roughly $20K in revenue and $14K net. Mike_Thebusinessman goes the opposite way from cash-only and runs card payments through Fleet Finesse. He flags that "The software does not create a Stripe account for you," so operators have to connect their own [pXYcnDt9ASA @ 00:04:42]. King of Rentals keep both doors open and use price as the lever: "Daily's always more expensive." They charge $65 to $75 a day against $350 to $450 a week, so dailies run about 20 percent higher [DOrgMXhPDXs @ 00:02:04].

Who gets the keys is the third fault line. Mo runs no background checks because they would cut 95 to 98 percent of his clientele. He asks only for a license, age 23-plus and a flat $300 deposit with the first week up front, then goes with his gut on renewals. Only a handful of his renters drive for Uber or Lyft [taXFg2qEMew @ 00:34:25]. Alex Auto, at 50-plus cars in Baltimore, does the opposite. He runs background checks, verifies licenses and sends 30-day maintenance reminders, because negligence and renting to felons are the exceptions that break Graves Amendment protection [RSpmJqhFEIw @ 00:21:10]. Kel King filters every lead through an overseas team paid around $3 an hour. "They're not accepting everybody," he says, because a renter who leaves after two weeks costs him more than one who pays for eight months straight [EVlxhXnHHH8 @ 00:03:38]. Steve Ludwig of Select Auto Rentals rejects the gig-driver thesis entirely. A Tesla run 10,000 miles a month lands near 120,000 miles after one year of lease-to-purchase, which he calls "not sustainable." His 277-vehicle St. Louis fleet rents vans and Suburbans to churches, schools and transportation companies instead [W_GJwoORfVw @ 00:28:50].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The losses start with insurance that does not pay. Car Rental Coach rented a 2008 Kia Spectra to a 20-year-old through Lula. The renter totaled it during a DUI and the insurer refused cover, so it took roughly $1,000 in legal fees to force a $9K payout [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:05:31]. Mo's first renter totaled a car he had sunk $5,000 to $6,000 into, while it was on the personal policy he now calls his biggest mistake [-HtlfCgxxtg @ 01:33:15]. Then the carriers themselves disappeared. HyreCar and Lula both went bankrupt, HyreCar with about two to three days' notice. Lula went unreachable for a month, so Mo started switching insurers in October, months before Lula closed in March [-HtlfCgxxtg @ 01:03:35]. Working coverage carries a trap too. Every claim lands on the loss run report, and an operator paying $120K a year in premiums while the insurer pays out $80K is "getting dropped." Mo's bad loss run forced him to open a new LLC and transfer 100-plus cars into it. He now files claims for liability only, never collision, and tells operators to "Think and act like self-insured" [taXFg2qEMew @ 00:31:00]. He lost 12 to 13 cars by April of a single year.

Theft and recovery failures teach the hardware lesson. Car Rental Coach rented a 2012 Kia Optima at $300 a week with no deposit. The renter told a cancer story and had the GPS and kill switch professionally removed. Police called it "civil," and only a $500 bounty hunter got the car back, which left the deal roughly break-even [sP0LfwE7elc @ 00:07:56]. Kel King lost a car for three months before standardizing on the GoldStar by Spireon tracker. He now kills a car two hours after it goes overdue [UCJIebqFEj0 @ 00:20:08]. Another of his cars was abandoned by a scammer with a $25,000 fraudulent check inside, then towed and impounded. He negotiated the $5,600 impound bill down to about $4,000, and his moral was "trust your gut" [7l7A5buuie0 @ 00:31:09]. Mo's repo protocol is built from scars like these. The trigger is 24 hours late with no communication, and pickup happens first thing in the morning. He does a drive-by first to check whether the car is blocked in and enables the kill switch early because it lags. He sends two to three people and they do not knock. Belongings go back at the police station lot only after the key is handed over, and he offers $50 to $100 for withheld keys [-HtlfCgxxtg @ 01:51:02].

The economics checks are where cheap mistakes compound. King of Rentals list the beginner errors in order. "First thing is they buy the wrong car," meaning luxury units, followed by mispricing such as $200 a week, no rental agreement, and the wrong insurance with no vetting [DOrgMXhPDXs @ 00:06:42]. On Kel King's cash-or-trash panel, the Nissan Sentra rates trash because "It's the CVT transmissions," while the Ford Fusion 2.5L and the Prius rate cash. Kel also breaks with Mo Breezy and Niger Morgan by calling the Chevy Cruze trash [7l7A5buuie0 @ 00:15:56]. Kel's scaling math depends on keeping cars occupied. "Turnover is where fleets bleed money," he says, because that gap is where every car gets re-cleaned, re-listed and re-screened, and every day a car sits empty costs money [EVlxhXnHHH8 @ 00:03:23]. Steve Ludwig's own value calculator showed gross looking better than net. His fleet rule is blunt: a new $50,000 to $60,000 van does not rent for more than a 2018, so an emotional purchase becomes a loss. "You cannot get emotional," he says [W_GJwoORfVw @ 00:30:26]. Alex Auto and Mo both found that financing widens every one of these gaps. The note keeps running during downtime, and $7,200 a month in notes and insurance is what drove Mo to buy all-cash.
