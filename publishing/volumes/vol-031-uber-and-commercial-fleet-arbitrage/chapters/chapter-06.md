## 1. Mine ten specific demand pockets before sourcing vehicles  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a demand ledger, not an auction watchlist. Pick one metro, one vehicle class, and one operating window: weekday airport transfers in licensed Black SUVs, for example, rather than every rideshare category. Download current business-facing records from NYC Open Data where relevant, including TLC bases and licensed for-hire vehicle datasets; record publication date, license status, and operating entity rather than treating an old listing as available inventory. Outside New York, use the applicable state carrier lookup, airport ground-transportation directory, and municipal business-license portal. A new carrier registration, a newly opened hotel, or a fleet advertising replacement drivers is a research trigger, not proof of unfilled trips. Cross-check each trigger through a business website and a direct conversation. Ask for dates, shift windows, eligible vehicle classes, payment timing, and evidence of requests the prospect could not fulfill. Never extract private driver identities from motor-vehicle records; 18 U.S.C. § 2721 limits disclosure and use of protected personal information. Store only necessary business contact details in Airtable or HubSpot, with source URLs and a deletion schedule. Rank prospects by recoverable idle capacity, not by the apparent size of their fleet.

The ten mapped plays below are chapter-level sourcing routes, not ten independently verified profit engines. Nine directly serve the fleet model; the tenth preserves the specified local-service routing play as a separately tracked adjacent channel. Begin with driver replacement demand and investor vehicles already eligible for the chosen service, because their operating gaps can be validated before capital moves. A driver wanting a $450 weekly lease needs more than enthusiasm: establish intended mileage, work window, eligible platform account, deposit capacity, and the exact insurance arrangement. An investor offering an idle SUV needs a title and lien review, written permission for commercial use, a maintenance history, and a realistic depreciation reserve. Ask authorized repair shops, fleet insurance brokers, and commercial dealers to introduce customers who request introductions; do not purchase private claim lists or driver records. The usable signal is a dated operational mismatch: vehicle ready but driver absent, driver qualified but vehicle unavailable, or dispatch demand exceeding licensed shift capacity. Log each introduction against one primary play so the same SUV does not appear as three separate opportunities. Pay referral amounts only under documented terms after checking applicable restrictions.

For rank_and_rent_pay_per_call_routing, build city-specific foundation repair, water restoration, probate liquidation, or commercial roofing hubs with original calculators and useful service explanations. This is a local-service lead product, not permission to charge fleet operators $150 for an ordinary ride request. Route tracked numbers through Ringba or Twilio to contracted service businesses. A sample month with 20 accepted calls at $150 produces $3,000 gross call billings; subtract $900 traffic, $150 tracking, $300 review and refunds, and $600 partner fulfillment allocation to leave $1,050 channel contribution before shared overhead. Those assumptions are illustrative and are excluded from the canonical fleet scenarios. A call exceeding 90 seconds must also meet location, service, uniqueness, and buyer-acceptance rules; duration alone invites disputes. Sell at the specified $75–$300 range only after buyers validate lead economics. Require prepaid auto-refill wallets, deduplicate repeat callers, disclose recording where required, and pause routing when funds are exhausted. Anchor traffic in organic city and calculator pages that remain usable if a Google Business Profile is suspended. Co-verify Maps only through real, eligible partner physical addresses; reject fabricated locations and stop paid traffic when accepted-call contribution turns negative.

## 2. Qualify the payer, the vehicle, and the retained contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Apply a 100-point qualification score before sending a proposal: verified near-term demand earns 25, asset eligibility and coverage 25, payer collectability 20, unit contribution 20, and operational fit 10. Score demand fully only when a prospect identifies a service window, expected volume, and an accountable decision-maker; a social-media comment earns no equivalent credit. Asset evidence includes VIN, registration, inspection status, commercial-use permissions, and eligibility under current platform and local rules. Collectability requires an identified contracting entity, authorized signer, agreed payment method, and funded initial obligation. Operational fit considers handoff distance, maintenance access, shift overlap, and contractor coverage. Advance scores of 80 or higher, investigate scores from 65 through 79, and archive lower scores with the reason recorded. These are internal triage thresholds, not measured industry predictors. Any missing required coverage, prohibited vehicle use, or unsafe handoff overrides the numeric result. If using consumer reports for driver screening, establish a permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b and follow applicable notice and adverse-action procedures. Employment-purpose reports add specific authorization and pre-adverse-action requirements. Have the screening provider configure the actual relationship rather than blindly applying an employment template to every lease.

Reconcile every proposal to the canonical Formula v2.0 bridge. Conservative gross billings of $180,000 retain $99,000 at 55%; subtract $73,800 of remaining costs to reach $25,200 owner profit. Base billings of $690,000 retain $379,500; subtract $234,600 to reach $144,900. Scaled billings of $1,200,000 retain $660,000; subtract $324,000 to reach $336,000. Gross-basis margins are 14%, 21.0%, and 28%; retained-basis margins are 25.45%, 38.18%, and 50.91%, respectively. The 45% removed before retained contribution represents the specified investor splits and pass-through fleet costs; do not deduct those same items again. The residual cost bridge is arithmetic implied by the scorecard, not a supplied vendor-level expense schedule. The stated $2,500–$15,000 monthly operating range therefore does not fully explain every annual residual cost total, and must not be presented as doing so. Under the supplied ASC 606 retained-share convention, distinguish gross collection volume from recognized operator revenue; actual presentation follows contractual principal-versus-agent analysis. Underlying driver fares, passenger spending, vehicle purchase values, and customer loan principal are not additional operator revenue. Keep the call-routing channel in a separate ledger rather than adding its sample contribution to these fixed annual cases.

Use weekly unit economics to reject attractive-looking inventory before committing the $12,000–$65,000 startup capital envelope. At $500 gross weekly billing, the 55% convention leaves $275 retained contribution. If incremental servicing costs outside the already deducted pass-through bucket are $95, the illustrative unit leaves $180 before shared overhead. Two lost billing weeks remove $550 of retained contribution; a separate $1,500 collision deductible can consume more than eleven such $180 operating weeks when combined with that lost contribution. This comparison is a stress test, not a complete claims forecast, and assumes no offsetting cost savings during downtime. Demand premium quotations for the actual driver, VIN, territory, and activities; a personal policy declarations page does not establish commercial livery coverage. California Public Utilities Code § 5433 addresses TNC insurance periods, while charter-party activity may invoke a different authority and insurance structure. Check airport access separately from platform eligibility. Set reserves from actual deductibles, expected repair timing, tires, and scheduled service. With OBD-II GPS or starter-interrupt hardware, use disclosed tracking and documented lawful procedures; never interrupt a moving vehicle or strand occupants. If safe, permitted recovery cannot be established, omit remote interruption and use conventional collection and recovery processes.

## 3. Run a permission-aware cadence that converts evidence into paid trials  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a 100-record weekly research queue only after defining the service radius and evidence fields. One full-time operator can review twenty records per weekday if contractors handle authorized document collection, inspections, and bookkeeping. Do not confuse this staffing allocation with three additional full-time employees; the Conservative and Base teams remain 1 FT plus 3 C. Deduplicate by business entity, telephone, and vehicle where relevant, then separate driver, owner, dispatcher, and call-buyer pipelines. Each record needs a source URL, dated trigger, contact channel, proposed unit, expected decision date, and next action. Use a concise first message: 'Your business advertises airport SUV service; do you have a recurring weekday overflow window? We can assess one eligible vehicle against your coverage and dispatch requirements.' Avoid asserting that the prospect is short of vehicles unless they actually said so. For commercial email, follow 15 U.S.C. § 7704 requirements including accurate headers, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out. For calls and texts, assess 47 U.S.C. § 227, implementing rules, applicable state rules, and suppression lists. A publicly posted mobile number is not blanket permission for automated marketing messages. Prefer relevant manual business outreach and introductions over bulk automation.

Use a five-touch, twenty-one-day sequence with a specific reason for every contact. Day one sends the observed trigger and one qualification question. Day three uses a permitted manual business call to identify the decision-maker and operational window. Day seven sends a one-page calculation using the prospect's stated weekly volume, explicitly separating billings, retained contribution, and remaining expenses. Day fourteen offers a limited paid trial with defined availability, payment timing, and exit terms. Day twenty-one closes the loop and moves silent records into a lower-frequency nurture list only where continued contact is appropriate. Stop immediately on an opt-out, and do not switch channels to evade the request. For inbound demand, publish one focused page per actual service and territory: eligible driver leases, investor co-hosting, or licensed overflow dispatch. Use Twilio numbers with source tags, web forms with minimal fields, and a human callback target of fifteen minutes during staffed hours. This is a service target, not a promise of continuous coverage. Attribute every booked assessment to its original source and latest meaningful touch. Organic pages provide a durable acquisition asset, but new pages may take longer than the first ninety days to produce dependable demand.

Set funnel benchmarks as planning hypotheses and replace them with observed cohort results after four weeks. An illustrative campaign begins with 200 raw records, validates 140 reachable and relevant businesses, engages 56 in two-way conversations, qualifies 28, issues 14 written proposals, signs seven agreements, and activates five after funds and operating evidence clear. Those transitions equal 70%, 40%, 50%, 50%, 50%, and approximately 71.4%; five activations represent 2.5% of raw records. A win means a unique activated counterparty with a funded first obligation, not a reply, an unsigned quote, or three vehicles under the same initial relationship. At that illustrative raw-to-active rate, three wins require about 120 records and ten require about 400; actual yield may differ materially. If the 200-record campaign costs $2,000 including research labor and tracking, acquisition cost is $400 per activation. Against the earlier $180 weekly unit contribution before shared overhead, acquisition payback takes about 2.22 operating weeks, excluding startup costs and reserve funding. Use median days between stages to diagnose delays. Weak conversation rates suggest bad targeting; strong conversations but weak qualification suggest missing eligibility; signed agreements without activation usually expose collection, insurance, or handoff problems.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the evidence queue
   - Select one metro and service class, then collect 200 deduplicated business records over the opening campaign.
   - Attach source URL, observation date, operating trigger, permitted contact route, and one primary sourcing play to every record.
2. Score before proposing
   - Run the 100-point qualification rubric and document every mandatory operating condition.
   - Advance scores of at least 80 only after required coverage, asset permissions, payer identity, and safe handoff procedures are established.
3. Offer a bounded paid trial
   - Present one vehicle, one shift window, or one prepaid call-routing territory with explicit acceptance rules.
   - Set initial funding, billing cadence, incident ownership, dispute evidence, and termination mechanics before any possession transfer or billable routing.
4. Measure activation and repeatability
   - Reconcile collected revenue, retained contribution, defects, and acquisition cost every week.
   - Expand only the source cohorts producing funded, serviceable relationships; document each lost opportunity and stop channels with negative incremental contribution.

## 4. Close the first three to ten relationships without buying hidden liabilities  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Handle objections by exposing the disputed variable rather than discounting the entire arrangement. When a driver says the weekly lease is expensive, compare included mileage, approved use, maintenance responsibility, insurance charges, and downtime terms; never promise earnings to justify rent. When an investor wants a higher split, show the waterfall and offer a trade between minimum availability, maintenance obligations, and payout timing instead of quietly removing reserves. When a dispatcher demands immediate capacity, request the actual service window and explain which vehicle-specific evidence is still needed. When a call buyer disputes quality, examine service category, geography, duplication, connected duration, and agreed acceptance criteria before issuing a contract-defined credit. These are the operator's own customer-account adjustments, not changes to any software platform's billing or entitlements. Use a one-page proposal followed by the applicable lease, dispatch agreement, or co-hosting mandate; a marketing page cannot carry the entire allocation of risk. Identify who contracts with the passenger, who controls dispatch, who holds required operating authority, and who bears refunds and claims. For regulated carrier work, confirm whether the planned role itself requires authority rather than assuming that calling it co-hosting changes the underlying activity.

Sequence the first ninety days around dependencies. During days one through fourteen, establish the service territory, insurer pathway, contract package, research fields, and referral relationships; obtain indicative asset costs without buying vehicles. During days fifteen through thirty, complete qualification calls, inspect promising units, verify authority, and secure narrowly scoped conditional commitments. During days thirty-one through sixty, activate the first three paid relationships if the evidence and funding support them. During days sixty-one through ninety, pursue seven additional activations only where collections and service quality remain stable. This schedule targets the specified one-to-three-month first-deal window without treating calendar dates as proof of readiness. Keep investor co-hosting attractive by avoiding owned inventory, but remember that zero owned metal does not mean zero working capital. Inspection fees, deductibles, hardware, contractor visits, and customer acquisition still consume cash. Separate refundable deposits from earned revenue and maintain the applicable contract and jurisdictional handling requirements. Document toll identification, charge timing, administrative fees, and dispute evidence before charging drivers. Do not assume Uber Fleet Partner collections establish universal first-priority payment rights; verify the actual account features, permissions, payout mechanics, and signed driver agreements.

After each activation, reconcile the first four weeks before multiplying the arrangement. Review invoiced gross billings, cleared collections, investor allocations, pass-through costs, retained contribution, remaining expenses, downtime, and reserve movements as distinct fields. Reserves affect available cash but are not automatically current-period expenses; use consistent accounting treatment. A base portfolio must ultimately support $57,500 monthly gross billings to annualize to $690,000, with $31,625 monthly retained revenue and $12,075 monthly owner profit under the canonical case. The Conservative and Scaled gross monthly equivalents are $15,000 and $100,000, respectively. These are portfolio run rates, not earnings attributed to the first five clients. Assign contractors to inspection and recovery support, qualified maintenance, and bookkeeping according to actual agreements; protect the full-time operator's time for qualification, collections oversight, and partner retention. At the Scaled case, use the stated 2 FT plus 4 C team rather than quietly assuming unlimited contractor capacity. Stop adding units when overdue collections, downtime, or incident response exceed documented operating limits. The repeatable edge is a clean sequence from source evidence to funded obligation to retained cash, with an exit path for every asset and counterparty that stops fitting.
