## 1. Identify the economic buyer before offering the vehicle  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first check usually comes from a working driver who needs an eligible, insured vehicle before the next earning window. Their acute pain is lost earning capacity, not vehicle ownership as an aspiration. A $450 weekly lease becomes intelligible when compared with that driver's documented receipts, fuel, tolls, tax provision, and living withdrawals; it does not become affordable merely because a salesperson cites busy airport queues. Verify recent platform statements supplied through an authorized process and separate recurring earnings from temporary incentives. Ask who actually owns the payment account and who can authorize its debits. A spouse's card, an unrelated business account, and a driver's own account create different authorization risks. The agreement should identify the vehicle, permitted use, weekly billing period, included mileage, damage process, and return procedure. A refundable security deposit remains a liability until a valid contractual application occurs. Keep it outside operating revenue and track it by driver. If verified disposable operating cash cannot support the recurring charge, reduce the commitment or decline the placement rather than turning a collection problem into a remote vehicle-control problem.

The second buyer is an enterprise travel manager, executive assistant, hotel transportation desk, or corporate procurement team purchasing reliable arrival capacity. The person arranging a ride may not control the budget. Establish the contracting entity, purchase-order requirement, spending limit, invoice recipient, and accounts-payable calendar before dispatch. A travel coordinator values an arriving passenger more than a beautiful utilization chart; procurement values a supplier whose invoice matches an authorized reservation. Price waiting time, parking, flight disruption, cancellations, and substitution explicitly. For example, twelve independently booked airport transfers at $150 produce $1,800 of customer billings, not $1,800 of owner profit. Driver compensation, vehicle expense, insurance, and any subcontractor settlement still require funding. Local authority is a service requirement rather than a transferable marketing label: California TCP authority, New York City TLC licensing, and airport access permissions address different jurisdictions and activities. Corporate clients pay to avoid failed pickups and purchasing from an ineligible operator. They do not purchase immunity from airport rules. When a permit, vehicle qualification, or driver credential fails verification, stop assigning that resource and offer a separately verified licensed and insured substitute.

The third buyer is the vehicle investor, whose pain is unmonitored downtime, unexplained deductions, and declining asset value. Their management agreement authorizes specific services and distributions; possession of their vehicle does not authorize unrestricted withdrawals from their bank account. Specify who approves maintenance, who funds collision deductibles, how reserve balances are held, and when statements become payable. An investor may accept a $300 monthly management fee for documented placement, maintenance coordination, and reconciliation, but will challenge an unexplained percentage of vaguely defined proceeds. Use a distribution statement that opens with collected receipts, lists contractually permitted deductions, and ends with the owner's payable balance. The ten mapped plays below are chapter-level operating applications across the supplied lease, dispatch, and co-hosting edges; only the first carries the supplied canonical play identifier. Their prices illustrate contracts, not market quotations or additional scorecard revenue. Several applications serve the same vehicle and must not be counted twice. A toll recovery, for example, can settle a receivable without creating fee income. Treat each application as a distinct buyer obligation with a measurable service, an authorized payment source, and a workable fallback.

## 2. Match the settlement mechanism to the obligation  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Payment rail selection starts with the obligation, not the processor's logo. A weekly driver lease benefits from scheduled collection and clear authorization; a large investor funding transfer benefits from verified bank instructions; a disputed asset handover may justify independent escrow. ACH settlement does not eliminate returns, and card capture does not eliminate chargebacks. Keep separate ledger states for invoiced, initiated, processor-settled, bank-reconciled, disputed, and refunded transactions. Never release owner distributions merely because an online dashboard shows an incoming payment. Establish reserve timing from the processor agreement, payment type, observed return experience, and promised distribution schedule. For recurring debits from consumer accounts, Regulation E requires appropriately authenticated authorization and a copy to the consumer; variable amounts can trigger advance-notice requirements. Business accounts and network rules require their own analysis rather than copying consumer language. A processor such as Stripe or an ACH provider can transport an authorized instruction but cannot manufacture consent or validate the underlying lease. Use hosted payment collection where available, keep card credentials out of dispatch notes, and never ask drivers for platform passwords to make collection more convenient.

A fleet waterfall is a contractual allocation implemented through whatever supported payout configuration actually exists. Do not sell automatic top-of-waterfall deductions before confirming the fleet account's country, product, agreements, permissions, and available statement exports. Where a supported arrangement permits collection of the operator's charge, reconcile the driver's entitlement and every deduction explicitly. Where it does not, invoice the driver through a separate authorized rail. Platform passenger funds are not a general-purpose treasury account for corporate bookings, damage assessments, or unrelated investor distributions. Likewise, an independently sourced airport transfer belongs in its own booking and settlement workflow; never solicit a platform passenger to cancel a trip and pay privately. Contractual retainers and milestone holds describe when money becomes payable, while ACH, wire, and card checkout describe how it moves. Escrow describes independent custody and release conditions. Factoring changes ownership or financing of receivables. Keeping these categories distinct prevents a dangerous sales claim that a retainer is inherently protected or that a wire-funded invoice is immune to fraud. The comparison matrix treats all eight requested mechanisms as settlement choices without pretending they are technically identical.

Corporate accounts often demand payment terms longer than drivers and backup carriers can finance. If a $10,000 approved invoice is factored at an illustrative 3% fee, the total proceeds before other charges are $9,700, potentially delivered through an initial advance and later reserve release. Confirm recourse, dilution, assignment restrictions, customer notification, and collection control before accepting that liquidity. A disputed pickup can remain the fleet's exposure under a recourse agreement. Retainers solve a different problem: a $2,000 advance funds reserved service but ordinarily remains deferred until the contracted performance occurs. Milestone releases can fit a multi-day event, with documented daily completion controlling each installment. Statutory disbursement is not an ordinary fleet revenue channel; it means a payment governed by a specific court process or legal entitlement, such as a properly established claim distribution. Record the actual authority and recipient rather than inventing a statutory priority for your lease fee. For high-value wires, independently call a previously verified contact before changing beneficiary details. A convincing email thread is not proof that the investor or corporate treasury approved a replacement bank account.

## 3. Protect the fee without seizing money or disabling mobility  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Credit screening should predict whether the specific payer can satisfy the specific obligation. For a driver, verify identity, permitted driving credentials, authorized vehicle use, recent earnings, existing lease burdens, and ability to fund the first billing period. Obtain motor vehicle records through an authorized provider such as SambaSafety or another appropriate screening service, with the required permissible purpose and authorizations. If a consumer report drives a denial or less favorable terms, evaluate applicable Fair Credit Reporting Act notice duties rather than treating the vendor's score as an unexplained veto. For an enterprise, verify legal entity, signer authority, procurement enrollment, trade references, disputed invoice history, and payment calendar. A genuine company domain does not establish that an assistant can bind its treasury. For an investor, verify ownership, financing restrictions, insurance responsibilities, reserve funding, and authority to place the vehicle in commercial service. Record a credit limit, review date, and named exception owner. Limit exposure using delivered but unpaid service, not merely overdue invoices; a customer can exhaust credit before the first invoice reaches its due date.

Fee protection begins with measurable performance and narrow deduction rights. Define when a lease week starts, when a transfer counts as completed, which party bears a no-show, and how disputed tolls or damage are reviewed. A fleet cannot establish a damage charge simply by calling it an administrative fee. Pair delivery and return photographs with inspection timestamps, odometer readings, repair estimates, and the contract's allocation of responsibility. Keep routine lease collection separate from contested assessments where the agreement or law requires it. For corporate work, obtain reservation acceptance before dispatch and preserve pickup evidence without spreading passenger information across uncontrolled spreadsheets. UCC Article 9 can matter when receivables or other collateral secure an obligation, but a contract sentence alone does not establish attachment, perfection, or priority; determine the applicable state enactment and collateral rules. Avoid assuming a lien exists on platform proceeds merely because your company supplied the car. If a bankruptcy filing becomes known, escalate collection and possession decisions immediately because the automatic stay may restrict otherwise available remedies. Operational speed cannot repair an invalid collection right after money has moved.

Telematics should reduce uncertainty, not convert a billing dispute into a safety incident. Use an authorized OBD-II or hardwired system to document mileage, service intervals, and permitted location information under a disclosed data policy. A starter-interrupt device is not a substitute for collection controls. Any contemplated intervention requires a lawful contractual basis, applicable notices, reliable confirmation that the vehicle is parked and unoccupied, and a safe recovery procedure; never interrupt propulsion or immobilize an occupied or moving vehicle. When payment fails, first distinguish bank error, authorization dispute, temporary liquidity shortfall, and outright refusal. Send an itemized statement, offer a documented cure path, stop extending new exposure when permitted, and arrange a safe return or qualified recovery process. Do not repeatedly debit a revoked mandate or hold a driver's unrelated earnings hostage. For corporate buyers, preserve undisputed collection while investigating a disputed booking. For investors, withhold only amounts supported by the management agreement and governing rules. The durable deterrent to circumvention is reliable dispatch, transparent accounting, and enforceable service terms, not credential sharing, intimidation, or hidden deductions.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before dispatch or vehicle release**
> 
> A completed payment instruction cannot compensate for an unauthorized payer, ineligible vehicle, or unsupported deduction.
> - Verify the contracting entity, actual signer authority, account-holder authorization, payment amount, and first collection status before accepting recurring exposure.
> - Confirm the specific vehicle, driver, operating authority, airport permission, and coverage for every planned use; TLC and TCP credentials are not interchangeable.
> - Confirm the written waterfall, reserve ownership, fee basis, and available platform functionality before promising automatic deductions or investor distributions.
> - Confirm the backup carrier, incident contact, safe return procedure, and dispute workflow before any occupied trip or driver handover.

## 4. Reconcile the shift economics to retained contribution and cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Model commercial_tlc_tcp_plate_shift_dispatch at the vehicle level before aggregating the business. Two separately staffed ten-hour shifts provide twenty scheduled vehicle hours, not permission for one driver to work twenty hours. Assume each shift completes four eligible trips averaging $87.50 in customer fares: eight trips produce $700 daily fare volume. Assume $140 of applicable platform or booking charges, $280 of driver compensation, and $180 of vehicle, fuel, insurance, and maintenance allocations; the illustrative operating remainder is $100. These assumptions are a dispatch sensitivity test, not the chapter's annual forecast. If your contract earns only a $70 dispatch fee while another principal sells transportation, record the $70 fee basis rather than claiming all $700 as operator revenue. Do not add that fee to the $100 remainder without establishing whose costs and receipts each number represents. Four lost trips remove $350 of fares while many costs persist. Underwrite normal demand, lawful operating hours, airport queue delays, and maintenance downtime without guaranteed surge pricing. When coverage or authority fails, remove the affected vehicle or driver and fulfill accepted work through independently verified licensed and insured backup operators.

The annual scorecard uses a different, expressly defined boundary: gross operator lease, dispatch, and co-hosting billings multiplied by a 55% retained share. Conservative billings of $180,000 leave $99,000 after $81,000 of owner splits and pass-through fleet costs. Subtracting $73,800 of remaining modeled costs produces $25,200 owner net profit. Base billings of $690,000 leave $379,500 after $310,500 of those deductions; $234,600 of remaining costs leaves $144,900. Scaled billings of $1,200,000 leave $660,000 after $540,000 of deductions; $324,000 of remaining costs leaves $336,000. The corresponding gross-basis net margins are 14%, 21.0%, and 28%; retained-basis margins are 25.45%, 38.18%, and 50.91%, rounded to two decimals. Remaining costs are residual totals required by the supplied model, not an invented vendor budget. Do not force them into the separate $2,500–$15,000 monthly opex planning range or subtract that range again. The supplied ASC 606 retained-share convention supports this comparison; actual gross versus net presentation still depends on the principal-agent assessment of each performance obligation. Neither passenger fare volume nor vehicle purchase funding becomes fee revenue simply because it crosses an account you administer.

Cash control requires a daily bridge from completed service to collected funds and a weekly bridge from collected funds to distributable balances. For every receipt, retain payer, contract, service period, vehicle or booking reference, gross amount, deductions, processor identifier, and bank match. QuickBooks or another suitable ledger can track receivables and liabilities, while exported platform statements and processor reports establish the external evidence. A settlement file is not reconciled until its net deposit and fees agree with the bank and the individual allocations agree with the contracts. Keep refundable deposits, unearned retainers, owner payables, and claims reserves visible rather than sweeping them into available operating cash. Conservative and Base staffing remain one full-time operator plus three contractors; Scaled remains two full-time staff plus four contractors. Design exception queues around that capacity: failed payments, unmatched tolls, expiring coverage, disputed damage, and overdue corporate invoices need named owners and aging. Automation should close matched items and expose exceptions, not silently assign contested charges. Before distribution, rerun the waterfall using collected amounts, preserve required reserves, and deliver a statement that lets the driver or investor reproduce the calculation independently.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish payer authority and contract boundaries
   - Record the signer, account holder, approved service, fee basis, and maximum exposure.
   - Attach procurement authority or driver authorization, signed terms, vehicle eligibility, and the applicable operating permissions before dispatch or handover.
2. Collect through the permitted settlement mechanism
   - Issue an itemized invoice or authorized payment instruction linked to the service record.
   - Use supported platform functions only for permitted transactions; separate independent corporate bookings, retainers, refundable deposits, and contested assessments.
3. Match performance, processor settlement, and bank movement
   - Reconcile booking or lease evidence to the external settlement report and bank entry.
   - Assign failed debits, chargebacks, unmatched tolls, and disputed service to named owners; preserve original evidence and avoid duplicate recovery.
4. Apply the waterfall and publish reproducible statements
   - Calculate permitted deductions, required reserves, earned fees, and owner or driver payables.
   - Distribute only supported balances under the agreed timing; report gross operator billings, 55% modeled retained contribution, and owner profit as separate measures.
