## Executive Summary

Two plate regimes, one playbook: control the scarce credential and rent or operate around it. In NYC, the for-hire cap keeps plates scarce, so holders rent whole cars at $250+ a week or lease the plate alone at $250-$500 a month, while FastTrack locks plate-plus-insurance at $289 a week for 56 weeks against a $1,000 deposit. In California, BusinessRocket's Alex and Khushnud Numonov agree the TCP belongs inside an LLC or corporation. It runs $1,000 for Class B or $1,500 for Class A, and it dies on sequencing errors: no commercial registration, a blocking lienholder, or insurance bought before a pending TCP number exists. Fleet operators disagree hard on economics. I Touch claims roughly 10% margins, Michael calls that too optimistic, and Emil says plates are worthless next to cash flow.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

New York's for-hire plate market runs on artificial scarcity, and every NYC practitioner builds around it. CBS New York reports more than 120,000 licensed vehicles. It also reports an app-car cruising limit that forces empty time down from 41% to 31%, and a temporary cap on new for-hire vehicles extended for another year [o74VMchARlA @ 00:02:20]. ESCAPE THE MATRIX states the consequence bluntly: the city is not issuing new plates, so a plate already in hand earns money whether or not the holder ever drives [NtZcNRbIgUo @ 00:00:35]. The same channel says part-timers probably should not chase a plate just to drive and should work Long Island or Westchester instead. Non-drivers, however, are pushed to register under a corporation anyway and then sell or rent the plate [Y8ppyxLqw9E @ 00:00:59]. Fleet owners on AutoMarketplace price that scarcity directly. A one-plate corporation changes hands for $30,000 to $40,000, and Michael argues that plate value tracks policy much like a medallion [kuB7bxLZKa0 @ 00:59:24]. The shared operating posture follows from that. Treat the plate as a capital asset parked inside an entity. Monetize it through rental, through resale, or through a fleet that rents cars to drivers by the week, rather than letting it sit idle.

On the California side, the consensus starts with the wrapper. Alex of BusinessRocket calls the TCP, governed by the CPUC, the only legal way to run your own drivers and vehicles without paying commission. That is how the 40% the platforms take out ends up in the operator's own pocket [MTc0vheuAy8 @ 00:01:28]. Because the TCP is non-transferable, Alex holds it inside an LLC and sells the company rather than the permit. A company with a clean loss run fetches about $5,000 to $12,000, and the LLC lets drivers come aboard as partners instead of W-2 employees for about $200 to $300 [yv_URA4bMDw @ 00:01:47]. The trap for skipping the entity is concrete: a TCP held personally means restarting the entire process just to add a spouse or partner [s51s14MY4hI @ 00:01:13]. Khushnud Numonov lands in the same place from a different direction. Their attorney recommended an S Corp, and a sole-proprietor viewer reported success without an entity. Khushnud still steers operators to an LLC or corporation because they cannot promise the sole-prop route will work [67saVKNzHxc @ 00:01:55]. Every California voice treats the entity as step zero. It is the asset that carries the permit, the insurance history, the credit-building, and the eventual resale value of the whole operation.

The third consensus point is that the vehicle's paperwork, not the vehicle itself, decides whether the operation launches. Alex's first pitfall is registration. Converting a regular registration to commercial after purchase is extremely difficult. Commercial does not mean registered in the company name; it means the DMV recognizes the car as business-use. So buy the car commercially registered from day one [s51s14MY4hI @ 00:00:14]. Khushnud Numonov is equally blunt that a financed personal vehicle will not work. The owner either pays it off or converts it to a commercial loan, which Khushnud says is hard without perfect credit [eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:08:16]. In New York the same discipline applies to plates. Jeremy Mosvich of FastTrack insists that registration and insurance must match the LLC holding the plate, which is why FastTrack co-registers each car with the customer as second registrant [DiLCf_drK0E @ 00:14:20]. Coverage is specialized on both coasts. Soniclove21 warns that mainstream carriers such as State Farm cannot write the TLC coverage a plated car needs, so operators buy through a TLC broker or app [TwvZcpkzAxo @ 00:02:04]. The lesson is to align the entity, registration, and correct commercial policy before money goes into the car. Every mismatch forces a rebuy, a refinance, or another trip to the DMV.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Khushnud Numonov's portal run is the cleanest do-it-yourself blueprint. Agents charge $400 for the TCP and another $400 for the LAX permit, which Khushnud counts as roughly two days of work, so they filed alone using a copyable cheat sheet. In the portal, create an account as a Transportation Carrier and declare majority ownership. Then enter the California Secretary of State file number and DBA, and order the DMV EPN. The EPN costs about $15, takes one to two weeks, and blocks progress, so Khushnud says to start it early [67saVKNzHxc @ 00:07:16]. Class B costs $1,000 and caps trips at 125 miles pickup-to-drop-off. Class A costs $1,500, is unlimited, and is the tier for routine Los Angeles to San Francisco runs [67saVKNzHxc @ 00:10:24]. The nine portal sections include a projected P&L, where Khushnud entered $100,000 in revenue, $9,000 in insurance, and zero workers' comp. Payment is ACH only with a 2.6% fee, for a total of $1,026. Approval landed two to three days after submission, and the certificate arrived by email [67saVKNzHxc @ 00:19:10]. Alex quotes a slower clock of five to seven weeks for the whole PUC process at the same $1,000 base price. Alex also says most people do not need the $1,500 advanced tier [MTc0vheuAy8 @ 00:02:02].

The California registration mechanics are cheap but unforgiving. Alex prices the commercial conversion at a $5 or $25 fee through a scale certificate, with a turnaround of about 30 days. Alex also warns that a lienholder can refuse the conversion, leaving refinancing as the only fix [yv_URA4bMDw @ 00:04:00]. Khushnud Numonov breaks the process into physical steps. Weigh the vehicle at a public scale for a nominal fee, and never walk into the DMV without the certificate of unladen weight. Then file for Type 31 commercial registration, which covers vehicles under 11,500 pounds, and add party buses only after the TCP is in hand [eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:12:12]. Khushnud also reads out a line-item budget: $500 to $1,000 for professional filing help, about $300 for a DBA or up to several thousand for an S-corp or LLC, and $1,000 to $1,500 for the PUC filing. The DMV pull notice costs $5 and the weight certificate $20. The total comes to under $10,000 without a vehicle, or about $5,000 for someone who already owns an unfinanced, unleased car. Khushnud's advice is to start with one driver and one inexpensive vehicle [eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:05:12]. Airports come last. LAX, John Wayne, and Ontario require permits while Burbank does not, and Khushnud warns that unpermitted pickups bring huge fines, driver arrest, and impound [eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:17:23].

New York mechanics split into plating your own car and renting plates out. Soniclove21's plating sequence starts with holding a TLC permit and getting a base letter from Uber, because Lyft does not issue one. Next, buy TLC liability through a broker and collect the FH-1 and declaration. Then apply on the TLC site for about $650, with roughly a two-week turnaround. Calling daily confirms receipt but does not speed anything up. The DMV visit within 20 days costs about $425 to $450 [TwvZcpkzAxo @ 00:03:13]. ESCAPE THE MATRIX gives plate holders two options. Rent the whole car at $250 a week against $500 monthly insurance plus $100 in maintenance, netting the stated $400 a month. Or lease the plate alone for $250 to $500 a month so the renter's own car takes the wear [NtZcNRbIgUo @ 00:02:13]. FastTrack institutionalizes the plate-only model. It charges $115 a week for the plate plus $174 for its own insurance policy, $289 locked for 56 weeks behind a $1,000 deposit, with registration and TLC renewal fees included [DiLCf_drK0E @ 00:08:31]. Owners pausing a plate can use the storage route AutoMarketplace describes. Storage lasts up to 90 consecutive days once per two-year cycle. Coming out costs a $125 transfer, plus the $625 renewal if the license shows fail to renew [8QnFsW_-2_0 @ 00:09:21].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Form the entity and set up the business rails
   - Register an LLC or corporation, then open a business checking account with the entity's number.
   - Khushnud Numonov considers an LLC or an S or C corporation the easiest wrapper. The entity file number and DBA are required inside the TCP portal, and a corporation lets you add partners without refiling [Khushnud Numonov — eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:07:12].
2. Weigh the vehicle and lock commercial registration
   - Weigh the vehicle at a public scale and take the unladen weight certificate to the DMV for Type 31 commercial registration.
   - Never go to the DMV without the weight certificate, or you will make a return trip. Type 31 covers vehicles under 11,500 pounds, and a financed personal vehicle must be paid off or moved to a commercial loan first [Khushnud Numonov — eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:11:55].
3. File the TCP and enroll in drug testing
   - Open the TCP portal as a Transportation Carrier, choose Class B ($1,000) or Class A ($1,500), and upload the drug program agreement.
   - Complete all nine portal sections, including the projected P&L, and pay by ACH with the 2.6% fee. Khushnud's Class B payment came to $1,026, and approval arrived by email two to three days after submitting [Khushnud Numonov — 67saVKNzHxc @ 00:19:10].
4. Bind the TCP policy once a pending number exists
   - Buy commercial insurance through a livery broker only after the pending TCP number is issued, then finish the TCP.
   - Alex's order is to open the company, start the TCP, get your insurance, and finish the TCP. A policy written without a pending TCP number is the wrong policy nine out of 10 times and has to be cancelled and rebought [BusinessRocket — yv_URA4bMDw @ 00:05:22].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Khushnud Numonov filed their own TCP through the online portal rather than pay agents $400 for the TCP plus another $400 for the LAX permit, calling $400 roughly two days of work, so they built a copyable cheat sheet and did it themselves, and they report approval two to three days after submitting, with the certificate arriving by email [67saVKNzHxc @ 00:00:17]. Alex of BusinessRocket runs the opposite playbook: hire a consultant, because doing the steps out of order means overspending by thousands of dollars [yv_URA4bMDw @ 00:01:10]. Timelines split along the same line. Alex tells Torsten the whole PUC process takes anywhere between five and seven weeks right now, and that the license itself costs $1,000 [MTc0vheuAy8 @ 00:02:02]. Kush, in a second walkthrough built on a hollywoodlimousine.com checklist they repeatedly say they are not affiliated with, budgets 30 to 60 days just for the drug-testing enrollment notice and then another 30 to 60 days for the permit, up to four months end to end [eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:17:04]. The operator read is blunt: the DIY route saves the $800 agent bill and can clear in days when every document is staged, while the consultant route buys correct sequencing from a shop that says it has handled over 10,000 licenses.

The entity question produces near-consensus among the California operators, but with different reasoning. Khushnud Numonov's attorney advised an S Corp, and a sole-prop viewer reported that route worked, but they still steer operators to an LLC or corporation because they cannot promise the sole-prop path holds [67saVKNzHxc @ 00:01:55]. Alex of BusinessRocket goes further: a TCP license is non-transferable, so the only exit is selling the company that holds it, and a company with a clean loss run can probably sell for about $5,000 to $12,000 [yv_URA4bMDw @ 00:02:52]. Insurance timing is the sharpest split. Kush's nine-step checklist puts commercial insurance last, bought right before the PUC application through a livery-expert broker, with workers' comp skipped for a solo owner-driver but required once non-officer drivers come aboard [eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:13:18]. Alex's own sequence settles it in practice: open the company, start the TCP, get your insurance, finish the TCP, and never let a broker bind a policy before the pending number exists.

In New York the disagreement is over how to monetize a capped plate. Jeremy Mosvich of FastTrack says peer-to-peer plate deals are exactly where renters get burned: the owner can reclaim the plate, and because in the government's eyes you may be the title holder but not the registrant, the renter cannot retrieve an impounded car. FastTrack instead co-registers the vehicle and charges $115 a week for the plate plus $174 for its own insurance, locked at $289 for 56 weeks [DiLCf_drK0E @ 00:18:16]. ESCAPE THE MATRIX's 2022 playbook skips renting altogether by plating a wheelchair-accessible Toyota Sienna, where TLC waives the application fee and rides pay an extra $25 [Y8ppyxLqw9E @ 00:02:13]. On valuation, Emil says plates are worthless and values a business on cash flow, while Michael counters that plate value depends on policy, much like a medallion [kuB7bxLZKa0 @ 00:40:48].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive California trap is the registration. Alex warns that converting a regular registration to commercial after the fact is extremely difficult, that commercial does not mean registered in the company name, and that you must tell the dealership outright, 'I want commercial registration' [s51s14MY4hI @ 00:00:14]. On Rideshare Professor's walkthrough Alex adds that a lienholder can refuse the conversion, a mistake that can cost you $50,000 to $60,000, with refinancing as the only fix [sYJ0eQpgXks @ 00:05:27]. Kush layers on the DMV mechanics: a financed personal vehicle will not work, transferring an owned car into the business may be treated as a taxable sale, and you should never walk into the DMV without the certificate of unladen weight or you will be making a return trip [eXUCLdNVhYc @ 00:11:55]. BusinessRocket's vehicle breakdown adds the platform trap: the CPUC sets no vehicle requirement at all, but Uber, Lyft, and third-party vendors do, and they do not care how nice the car looks; if it falls outside their parameters they decline it, forcing a resale and a restart [JmrjPmk_Gpo @ 00:01:22]. The economics check is simple: call the platform and an insurance broker before you shop, because stretch limos, shuttles, and Sprinters can insure cheaper than a Prius.

New York fleet owners on AutoMarketplace's leasing panel put hard numbers on how thin the business runs. I Touch, with about 400 cars near JFK, walks $500 of weekly rent down through $150 of insurance, a $150 car payment, and $50 to $75 of other costs until, as they put it, now we're left with 200 bucks, roughly a 10% margin [kuB7bxLZKa0 @ 00:29:36]. Michael, running about 200 cars in Sunset Park, calls 10% too optimistic, reports 5% for 2023, and says they have lost at least $1 million over ten years to staged accidents and theft, plus $18,000 in EZPass collections from a single driver at $100 per unpaid toll violation [kuB7bxLZKa0 @ 00:14:48]. Emil books 2 to 3 total losses every two months, carries liability only, and caps driver damage exposure at $1,000 [kuB7bxLZKa0 @ 00:20:16]. Policy pressure stacks on top: the TLC extended the temporary cap on new for-hire vehicles another year and set a cruising limit, with app companies fined $350 for each 100 miles over the limit logged by each car [o74VMchARlA @ 00:02:00]. Officials say that could add up to hundreds of thousands of dollars in fines, and drivers interviewed fear lower earnings.

New York paperwork deadlines are where idle plates die. AutoMarketplace's storage walkthrough caps storage at 90 consecutive days once per two-year cycle, and warns that the TLC UP portal shows red not-eligible text until you surrender plates at the DMV for an FS-6; coming out, start at least two to three weeks before the deadline, because the transfer takes three to five business days at $125, plus $625 if the license sits in fail-to-renew [8QnFsW_-2_0 @ 00:06:27]. Both that walkthrough and Soniclove21 flag the 20-day window: once the plate letter arrives you have 20 days to reach a DMV in the five boroughs with the letter, proof of insurance, and FH-1, and pay roughly $425 to $500 [TwvZcpkzAxo @ 00:03:54]. Soniclove21 also warns that regular carriers like State Farm cannot provide the coverage you need, so the FH-1 has to come from a TLC insurer bought through a broker or app, and calling the TLC daily confirms receipt but does not speed the roughly two-week review [TwvZcpkzAxo @ 00:02:04]. On the driver side, Driver Study Guides drills the rules that cost licenses: no one else may drive your FHV except in an emergency, driving impaired means revocation, and late renewal is allowed only up to 180 days after expiry, with a fee [I6r7SzCicUo @ 00:00:49].
