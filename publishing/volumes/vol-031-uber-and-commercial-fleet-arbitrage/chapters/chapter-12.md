## 1. Days 1-7: Select One Market, Assign Ten Plays, and Protect Launch Cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start Day 1 with one metropolitan market, one vehicle class, and one primary customer promise. Candidate #31 contains three commercial engines: Uber Fleet Partner driver leases, properly authorized TLC/TCP Black SUV shift dispatch, and investor vehicle co-hosting without operator vehicle ownership. For this sprint, map those engines into ten executable plays: P1 weekly driver leasing; P2 commercial shift dispatch; P3 investor co-host acquisition; P4 contractual collections and waterfall allocation; P5 driver screening and onboarding; P6 insurance and operating-authority verification; P7 OBD-II location and safe vehicle recovery; P8 toll reconciliation; P9 preventive maintenance and downtime recovery; and P10 investor settlement and retention. This is an execution decomposition, not ten independent revenue streams. Select P1 or P3 as the initial sales offer; keep P2 inactive until its separate requirements are met. The full-time operator owns pricing, contracts, cash, and exceptions. Contractor C1 handles sourcing and driver coordination, C2 handles vehicle inspection and maintenance coordination, and C3 handles bookkeeping and document administration. Use an Airtable register with a named owner, deadline, evidence link, and backup contact for every play. Contractors remain accountable for contracted deliverables rather than assumed unlimited availability.

Days 2 and 3 turn a promising market into a documented operating boundary. Ask the insurer to identify the named insured, scheduled vehicles, authorized drivers, permitted rental or lease use, commercial livery activity, deductibles, exclusions, and coverage during each relevant rideshare period. A personal policy plus a platform screenshot is not a complete coverage file. California Public Utilities Code section 5433 addresses transportation-network coverage periods; section 5371 addresses charter-party operating authority. Neither substitutes for a New York City TLC license or an airport's separate pickup authorization. Compare the proposed contract with applicable rental disclosures, including California Civil Code section 1936 where the transaction falls within its scope. Verify investor title, lien restrictions, lease restrictions, and written permission for the intended commercial use. C3 maintains a dated requirements register, while the operator resolves classification questions with the relevant authority and insurer. Do not advertise airport service before confirming the airport's current commercial access rules. These checks attach to the particular vehicle, driver, service, and jurisdiction, not merely to the business entity. By Day 3, every unresolved dependency must have an accountable person, an expected response date, and a prohibited-until-resolved operating activity.

Days 4 through 7 establish the spending boundary before deposits become difficult to recover. The canonical startup range is $12,000-$65,000; use $12,000 as this illustrative pilot's opening unrestricted cash, not as a universal minimum for commercial vehicles. Cap first-month unrecovered launch outflows at $7,500, leaving at least $4,500 untouched. Allocate $2,500 to setup and recurring administration, $2,000 to quoted coverage and authority costs, $1,000 to inspections and telematics, $1,000 to sourcing and screening, and $1,000 to contingency. Customer deposits, investor distributions, and restricted balances cannot fund these envelopes. Count prepaid premiums and refundable vendor deposits against the ceiling while cash remains unavailable; cash consumption and accounting expense are different measures. Require remaining cash to cover both the largest applicable deductible and thirty days of unavoidable operating payments. If actual quotes exceed the envelope or the reserve test fails, do not deploy: obtain additional committed capital or shrink the pilot. The minimum-cash example supports only a narrowly priced co-hosting experiment, not vehicle purchases. On Day 7, sign a spending ledger, two conditional vehicle prospects, and a coverage checklist before authorizing the next week's work. Record commitments when signed, rather than waiting for card settlement.

## 2. Days 8-14: Contract the Pilot and Test Every Collection and Recovery Path  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Day 8 converts owner interest into a vehicle-level agreement rather than an informal promise of passive income. Specify who contracts with the driver, who controls pricing, who bears damage and collection risk, and who pays insurance, maintenance, registration, cleaning, and downtime. P3 cannot remain zero-owned-metal if the operator quietly guarantees an investor's debt service regardless of utilization. P10 must show a worked settlement with owner distributions, allowable pass-through items, retained operator amounts, and dispute deadlines. Day 9 then converts driver demand into a screened, documented application. Obtain a permissible purpose for consumer reports and follow the procedures applicable to the relationship. Employment-purpose reports trigger the specific disclosure, authorization, and pre-adverse requirements in 15 U.S.C. 1681b(b); other eligible arrangements still require a valid purpose, and report-based adverse action can trigger 15 U.S.C. 1681m(a). Do not assume every contractor arrangement follows the same screening workflow. A screening vendor such as Checkr can support report ordering, but the operator owns the decision process. C1 checks license class and experience against insurer criteria, while C3 records report status without exposing unnecessary personal data to the entire pod. Keep applications separate from the operating dashboard and restrict access to assigned reviewers.

Days 10 and 11 test P4 before a vehicle leaves custody. Use Uber Fleet Partner features only where the account, market, and contract actually support the intended workflow; confirm current payout controls rather than assuming every market permits automatic lease deductions. A top-of-waterfall lease is a contract and settlement design, not permission to seize unrelated driver earnings. If platform settlement cannot implement the agreed allocation, use a separately authorized payment method with clear invoices and lawful collection terms. Stripe Billing or a bank-supported debit service can schedule collections, but test settlement delays, returned payments, disputes, and account ownership. Build a four-line sample: $650 weekly lease billing, $292.50 aggregate owner and pass-through allocation, $357.50 retained operator revenue, and separately recorded operator expenses. The 45% allocation is the canonical aggregate assumption, not an invented promise about a specific owner's payout. Where actual contract allocations differ, calculate actual retained share and flag the variance. Customer security deposits remain liabilities until properly applied; they are neither lease revenue nor owner profit. C3 reconciles invoice, payment processor, bank receipt, and allocation ledger using a shared transaction identifier. The operator signs the first successful settlement and the first simulated failure.

Days 12 through 14 establish custody, maintenance, and recovery discipline for the first two pilot vehicles. C2 records dated photographs, odometer, fuel level, tire condition, warning lights, keys, and existing damage in a signed handover packet. Samsara or Geotab can supply location and diagnostic workflows where the selected device and plan support them; verify compatibility rather than treating every OBD-II dongle as an immobilizer. Configure location access narrowly, document the authorized purpose, and establish retention limits. Starter interruption requires compatible equipment, lawful contractual authority, and a verified safe stationary state; never disable a moving vehicle, interrupt active passenger service, or treat delayed telemetry as proof of safety. Prefer coordinated voluntary return and qualified recovery personnel over remote intervention. For P8, register each vehicle's toll account and transponder assignment before deployment, then define evidence and dispute handling for pass-through charges. For P9, reserve service windows using actual mileage intervals and inspection findings. Run a tabletop collision exercise covering emergency response, insurer notification, evidence preservation, replacement availability, and deductible funding. Day 14 ends with either a signed two-vehicle pilot release or a written list of blocked items; sales pressure does not change the release criteria.

## 3. Days 15-21: Measure Collected Economics Without Confusing Billings and Profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The third week tests whether the service survives contact with actual drivers, vehicles, and settlement delays. On Day 15, activate only eligible pilot units and record the first scheduled lease period. On Day 16, inspect collections and driver communication; on Day 17, reconcile toll evidence; on Day 18, inspect device health and mileage; on Day 19, perform the first owner statement; on Day 20, review downtime; on Day 21, close the operating week. Two vehicles at an illustrative $650 weekly lease produce $1,300 gross weekly billings and $715 retained revenue at 55%, before operator expenses. Four fully billed weeks would produce $5,200 gross and $2,860 retained, but deployment after Day 14 does not create four completed billing weeks by Day 30. Count only service actually delivered and collections actually settled. This pilot tests mechanics, not the annual Base case. Do not add passenger fares, investor vehicle values, refundable deposits, or loan principal to operator revenue. P2 dispatch revenue enters the ledger only under the actual commercial arrangement, with the gross-versus-net presentation assessed separately. Track a unique service identifier so the same activity cannot appear once as a lease and again as an invented dispatch fee.

Reconcile the canonical scenarios using the same bridge every Friday: gross billings multiplied by 0.55 equals retained revenue; retained revenue minus all remaining operator costs equals annual owner net profit. Conservative is $180,000 gross, $99,000 retained, $73,800 remaining costs, and $25,200 profit. Base is $690,000 gross, $379,500 retained, $234,600 remaining costs, and $144,900 profit. Scaled is $1,200,000 gross, $660,000 retained, $324,000 remaining costs, and $336,000 profit. Profit divided by gross gives 14%, 21.0%, and 28%; profit divided by retained revenue gives approximately 25.45%, 38.18%, and 50.91%. The scorecard's $2,500-$15,000 monthly operating-expense range is not a substitute for this complete cost bridge. Base residual costs average $19,550 monthly, so the operator must separately identify routine overhead and other costs rather than force total costs into that range. Do not subtract owner splits or pass-through fleet costs twice. The 55% retained basis is the supplied ASC 606 modeling convention; actual principal-versus-agent presentation depends on contract control and the service promised. Maintain gross billings as a separate operating measure even where recognized revenue is presented net. C3 documents every classification and the operator reviews unexplained differences before accepting the close.

Define the dashboard before interpreting a successful week. Collection rate equals settled cash applied to invoices due in the measurement window divided by those invoices' billed amounts; exclude deposits and future prepayments from both sides. Utilization equals paid contracted vehicle-days divided by scheduled pilot capacity days, including service days lost to maintenance or deployment failures. Unplanned downtime equals unplanned unavailable hours divided by scheduled service hours. Realized retained share equals actual retained revenue divided by gross billings for the same delivered services. Vehicle contribution equals retained revenue less attributable operator costs not already deducted, including paid contractor work, repairs, and insurance allocated consistently. Separately show owner-profit-equivalent margin after all remaining operator costs, using a consistent accrual treatment for earned revenue and incurred costs. Reserve transfers reduce available cash but are not automatically expenses. Record total operator hours and exceptions requiring intervention; automation that merely shifts unpaid work to contractors is not efficiency. Require at least two completed collection cycles for early operating conclusions and four active vehicle-weeks for a Day 30 economic sample. A vehicle-week is seven days of pilot exposure, not seven billable days. If the sample is smaller, classify economics as Fix for insufficient observation rather than extrapolating a lucky first payment.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Habits That Protect Fleet Contribution**
> 
> Experienced operators investigate the small reconciliation failures before they become vehicle, owner, or cash disputes.
> - Run toll matching by custody timestamp, not current driver name; delayed toll posting often belongs to the previous contract.
> - Schedule collections early enough to observe settlement before owner distributions, while keeping the contractual due date explicit.
> - Keep a dated tire and body-photo baseline at every handover; compare new damage against that packet before assigning a charge.
> - Ask the broker to price the next vehicle before promising its economics; one VIN or driver change can alter the deductible and premium materially.

## 4. Days 22-30 and Reviews at 60/90: Kill Structural Losses, Fix Friction, Scale Evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 22 through 24 repair the largest observed constraint instead of adding another offer. Rank exceptions by cash lost, service hours lost, and operator minutes consumed. A toll mismatch usually needs better attribution; uninsured activity requires immediate suspension of the affected service. A negative vehicle contribution needs a documented price, cost, or contract change before another unit repeats the loss. Day 25 retests the failed workflow with C3 observing the evidence. Day 26 checks reserve adequacy against current deductibles and unavoidable payments, rather than the original spreadsheet. Day 27 asks each owner whether the settlement statement is understandable and reconciles any dispute. Day 28 freezes new commitments while the operator closes the month. Day 29 assembles the review packet, and Day 30 assigns Kill, Fix, or Scale using the matrix below. First deal timing remains the canonical one to three months; an undeployed but properly progressing pilot is not automatically a failed business. It receives Fix, with lower spending and dated dependencies, until enough operating evidence exists. However, market interest does not justify indefinite spending. If the burn ceiling is reached without release readiness, stop new commitments and require a separately funded, explicitly bounded extension.

Apply decision precedence consistently: critical safety or authority failures override commercial metrics; Kill conditions override Fix; Scale requires every stated threshold; all other cases receive Fix. Kill means stop new commitments and wind down the affected pilot or structurally defective offer, honor existing obligations, return vehicles safely, and reconcile owner and driver balances. It does not mean remotely disabling an occupied vehicle. At Day 30, Scale permits one additional vehicle only after two collection cycles, four active vehicle-weeks, positive aggregate vehicle contribution, at least 95% collections, 75% utilization, no more than 10% unplanned downtime, and at least 55% realized retention. At Day 60, demand four cycles and twelve vehicle-weeks, then tighten collections to 97%, utilization to 80%, and downtime to 8%, with owner-profit-equivalent margin of at least 14% of gross. At Day 90, require eight cycles and twenty-four vehicle-weeks, 98% collections, 85% utilization, 5% downtime, and at least 21% gross-basis owner-profit-equivalent margin. Use trailing thirty-day economics at each review, with cumulative exposure for sample thresholds. These are proposed operating decision rules, not canonical scorecard outputs. At every Scale review, retain full coverage, positive reserves, no unresolved owner statement dispute, and a feasible contractor schedule.

Protect the four-person pod by making capacity a measured constraint. The full-time operator should spend no more than forty hours weekly across sales, exceptions, cash review, and owner communication; require a rolling two-week average within that limit before expansion. C1, C2, and C3 must have contracted capacity for the next increment, with a named substitute for essential tasks. Do not infer unlimited hours from the label contractor. Day 30 permits one added vehicle; Day 60 permits at most two; Day 90 permits at most two per fortnight, each contingent on current evidence and reserves. These increments are managerial limits rather than revenue forecasts. The Base scenario remains 1 FT + 3 C; the Scaled scenario explicitly requires 2 FT + 4 C, so establish those funded roles before pursuing its workload. A Fix decision creates one owner, one measurable correction, a fourteen-day deadline, and a retest without fleet growth. By Day 90, two failed correction windows for the same structural contribution problem trigger Kill for that offer. Preserve productive vehicles only where their own contracts and economics remain sound. Expansion follows reproducible cash collection and complete cost recognition, not gross billings, investor enthusiasm, or a platform dashboard that omits the operator's actual obligations.
