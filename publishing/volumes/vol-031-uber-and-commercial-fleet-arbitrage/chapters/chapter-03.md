## 3. Fund settlement gaps without spending investor or driver money  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Model cash timing independently from margin. The liquidity example assumes the operator collects the full billed amount, pays the 45% investor and pass-through allocation seven days after service, and realizes cash collections fourteen days after service. Those are planning assumptions, not representations of a universal Uber payout schedule. Use gross billings divided by 365 to establish daily throughput, then multiply by days sales outstanding, or DSO. Fourteen-day receivables are $6,904, $26,466, and $46,027. Seven-day contractual payables offset only $1,553, $5,955, and $10,356. This offset is legitimate only when the agreement actually permits that payment timing; an already overdue investor distribution is not free working capital. Track service date, invoice date, processor settlement date, bank availability, and investor due date separately. A payout displayed as pending cannot fund a premium debit tonight. Reconcile daily using bank-available balances, not the larger processor dashboard balance, and age disputed amounts separately from routine receivables to expose true collection delays before the next distribution run.

Add thirty days of operating cash buffer, excluding only the modeled scrub provision because the fourteen-day receivable model assumes ordinary collection timing and the provision is not an immediate cash payment. Annual non-provision costs are $70,200, $220,800, and $300,000; thirty days equals $5,770, $18,148, and $24,658. This buffer deliberately treats other budgeted costs as cash exposure, even when individual repair invoices arrive unevenly. Add immediately accessible deductible liquidity of $10,000, $20,000, and $30,000, representing illustrative $5,000 claims capacity across two, four, and six concurrent events. These amounts are balance-sheet funding requirements, not additional annual expense deductions. The resulting operating liquidity requirement is approximately $21,121, $58,759, and $90,329: receivables less permitted payables plus operating buffer plus deductible liquidity. Restricted security deposits, investor distributions awaiting remittance, and tax collections cannot fill the gap. Do not subtract expected loss reserves from receivables to manufacture cash availability; persistently unpaid balances require a separate bad-debt and collection forecast. Recalculate the buffer weekly from the next thirteen weeks of actual premium, payroll, repair, and investor payment dates rather than treating the thirty-day approximation as a permanent treasury policy.

The published $12,000–$65,000 startup range therefore supports only selected launch configurations, not automatic funding of the Scaled case. Setup deposits, installer charges, licensing, and coverage down payments sit above the operating liquidity estimate unless already included in its expense schedule. Negotiate collection-linked investor distributions where commercially acceptable, stage new placements, or secure a committed facility before assuming rapid expansion. **SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Close the vehicle-week billing ledger
   - Match service evidence, contractual rates, and collection ownership before issuing each invoice.
   - Assign vehicle, driver, investor, service interval, and billing-lane identifiers; separate refundable deposits from earned charges and preserve source statements.
2. Post the retained contribution bridge
   - Calculate investor allocations first, then deduct only operator-borne costs and expected losses.
   - Reconcile the aggregate to 55% retained revenue for the scorecard comparison without forcing individual 25%–35% co-host contracts into that percentage.
