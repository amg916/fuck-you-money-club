## 1. Define the Revenue Unit Before Pricing the Rights  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use one active licensee agreement for one measured year as the economic unit, with separate schedules for territories, permitted uses, reporting periods, and collection dates. The illustrative Conservative cohort contains two agreements generating $25,000 each after deductions; Base contains ten generating $42,500 each; Scaled contains sixteen generating $50,000 each. Those cohorts reproduce the canonical revenue totals without treating licensees' merchandise sales as operator revenue. Base starts with $8 million of eligible licensee sales at a five percent royalty, producing $400,000 of royalty billings, plus $50,000 of separately earned fixed license fees. Contract credits of $15,000 and an expected reversal allowance of $10,000 reduce $450,000 of billings to $425,000 of recognized revenue. A $200,000 recoupable minimum already credited against those royalties is a collection mechanism, not another revenue line. Advance cash remains deferred where the associated performance has not occurred. Maintain separate fields in PostgreSQL or Airtable for underlying sales, billings, earned consideration, receivables, and cash. A dashboard that collapses these fields can show growth while quietly spending money owed for future delivery. Require a contract identifier on every adjustment so the audit trail survives staff changes and spreadsheet exports.

Map the first three operating plays to rights packages with different delivery burdens rather than applying one royalty percentage everywhere. Patent field-of-use licensing can price 100,000 qualifying units at $0.20 each plus a separately earned $10,000 implementation license, yielding $30,000 before deductions; invalidity allegations or disputed claim coverage trigger a narrow evaluation license instead of an expensive exclusive grant. Territorial brand licensing can combine a $20,000 annual minimum with four percent of $750,000 in eligible sales: total royalty consideration is $30,000, not $50,000, when the minimum is fully recoupable. Its fallback is a shorter, nonexclusive territory with documented product quality inspections. Catalog-and-dataset licensing can sell four permitted-use packages at $7,500 each, also yielding $30,000, while charging separately for additional refreshes only when the contract permits them. If provenance is incomplete, remove affected assets and reprice the remaining package rather than promising unrestricted downstream rights. These are chapter-local pricing examples, not extra scorecard revenue. Copyright transfers require the signed writing specified by 17 U.S.C. 204; patent assignments follow 35 U.S.C. 261. Dataset ownership does not automatically supply privacy permissions or rights in every embedded work.

Treat customs_first_sale_rule_valuation_arbitrage as an adjacent licensing opportunity only when the operator actually licenses a valuation workflow, evidence taxonomy, or controlled software package. Under 19 CFR 152.103 and the Nissho Iwai framework, an eligible factory sale must be substantiated as a bona fide sale for export to the United States at an acceptable transaction value; a lower invoice alone proves nothing. At an illustrative ten percent duty rate, moving an eligible unit's valuation from $18 to $8 reduces duty from $1.80 to $0.80, a $1 saving. That is 55.56 percent on eligible units before valuation additions, not an automatic portfolio saving. Eligibility covering 63 to 99 percent of otherwise comparable import value produces approximately 35 to 55 percent aggregate reduction under these assumptions. At $10 million of importer-invoice value and 90 percent eligibility, modeled annual duty savings are $500,000. A $25,000 annual workflow license captures five percent of that benefit; only the fee enters operator revenue. For $250 million programs, validate entry-level evidence rather than extrapolating eligibility. The fallback is encrypted factory invoices in confidential customs-broker escrow with full CBP access, backed by a formal customs-counsel opinion letter; unsupported entries retain the defensible valuation.

> - Attach a numeric minimum-guarantee example showing exactly how each payment recoups against subsequent royalties.

## 2. Reconcile Every Deduction to the Canonical Owner Profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the waterfall from gross contractual billings rather than from cash deposits. Conservative billings of $55,000 less $3,000 of credits and $2,000 of expected reversals produce $50,000 revenue. Base billings of $450,000 less $15,000 and $10,000 produce $425,000. Scaled billings of $840,000 less $24,000 and $16,000 produce $800,000. Credits represent identified returns, canceled permissions, and contractually valid true-ups; the reversal allowance covers supported estimates of additional price adjustments. Do not mix either category with unpaid but valid receivables: credit losses require their own assessment and accounting treatment. The illustrative gross-billings scrub rates are 9.09 percent, 5.56 percent, and 4.76 percent respectively. Better reporting can reduce leakage, but these rates are assumptions rather than evidence that scale automatically improves contracts. Give each deduction a reason code, source document, owner, and resolution date. Preserve the original statement and post a linked correcting record instead of overwriting history. For quarterly royalties, estimate earned amounts using defensible reporting data and reconcile promptly when the licensee statement arrives. Collection timing changes cash availability; it does not by itself determine whether the licensed performance has been completed.

Subtract direct contractor delivery labor of $12,000, $70,000, and $90,000, then third-party rights participation of $7,500, $55,000, and $80,000. Gross contribution becomes $30,500, $300,000, and $630,000, equivalent to 61 percent, 70.59 percent, and 78.75 percent of recognized revenue. The labor budgets correspond to 120, 700, and 900 hours at a $100 blended hourly rate, covering technical rights packaging, metadata remediation, reporting setup, and scheduled quality reviews. These are purchased hours, not three or four full-time contractor salaries. Rights participation is modeled as an expense because this example assumes the operator controls the licensed rights and acts as principal; an agency arrangement may require net presentation instead. Obtain an accounting assessment before reusing this presentation for represented creators. Log contractor time by agreement and task so remediation cannot hide inside platform overhead. Separate revenue-share obligations from recoupable advances paid to rights owners. An advance can consume cash before its expense is recognized. Require the contract to state whether participation is calculated on invoiced, earned, or collected fees, because that distinction determines who finances a late-paying licensee and whether deductions can be passed through.

Below gross contribution, charge acquisition spending of $6,000, $35,000, and $45,000; platforms and operating infrastructure of $4,000, $18,000, and $24,000; counsel, audit, and insurance of $5,000, $26,375, and $31,000; and retained staffing and administrative support of $3,000, $40,000, and $50,000. Total operating costs are $37,500, $244,375, and $320,000, leaving the required $12,500, $180,625, and $480,000 owner profits. Net margins therefore remain exactly 25 percent, 42.5 percent, and 60 percent. Support spending covers non-delivery work and, in Scaled, the second full-time role; allocate time once rather than duplicating it in contractor delivery. This illustration treats the founder's residual as owner earnings before personal taxes, without a separate imputed replacement salary. Validate whether the support budget can actually purchase the proposed staffing locally. Conservative and Base use one full-time operator plus three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time people plus four contractors. The stated $2,000 to $12,000 monthly opex range describes the platform's operating envelope, not an all-inclusive cap on royalties and delivery costs. Base all-in monthly costs average $20,364.58 and Scaled $26,666.67; budgeting only recurring overhead would materially underfund both cohorts.

## 3. Fund the Collection Gap Without Spending Deferred Cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Measure the cash gap from payment of delivery obligations to unrestricted receipt, not simply from invoice date. For a comparable steady-state planning screen, assume costs are paid as incurred, no supplier credit, and a uniform daily cost rate. Conservative uses 90 days sales outstanding plus a separate 30-day payout lock; Base uses 75 plus 15; Scaled uses 60 plus ten. Here the lock begins after the licensee pays into a collecting intermediary, so adding the periods does not double-count the same delay. A direct bank receipt without a contractual restriction has no such lock. Applying total annual costs divided by 365 to gaps of 120, 90, and 70 days yields operating bridges of $12,328.77, $60,256.85, and $61,369.86. Add designated liquidity buffers of $2,000, $10,000, and $16,000 to obtain $14,328.77, $70,256.85, and $77,369.86 of modeled funding. Those buffers mirror the reversal allowances for planning convenience but are cash allocations, not second expenses. Quarterly settlement cliffs, upfront acquisition payments, and disputed statements can require substantially more cash than this daily-average screen. Build a dated receipts-and-disbursements schedule before committing the operator to a minimum guarantee owed upstream.

Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast with separate columns for unrestricted bank cash, intermediary-held cash, receivables, unearned advances, rights-owner payables, and contractor commitments. QuickBooks can hold the accounting ledger while a controlled spreadsheet carries contract-level settlement dates; reconcile both to bank statements every week. A $100,000 license advance does not create $100,000 of distributable profit when refresh obligations or recoupable royalties remain outstanding. Similarly, accrued royalties may be profitable without financing next Friday's contractor payments. Negotiate staged fixed fees at signed delivery milestones, minimum guarantees paid before the licensed selling season, and rights participation payable after collection where commercially achievable. Do not rewrite existing payout obligations through an internal forecast assumption. Model withholding taxes separately from contractual royalty deductions, including any treaty documentation and recoverability assessment relevant to cross-border collections. An unrecoverable withholding reduces economics; a recoverable amount can still extend the cash gap. Keep customs duty savings completely outside this treasury schedule because that cash belongs to the importer. Only the operator's earned license invoices and associated costs belong in the licensing cohort. Restrict bank-account changes to verified counterparties using an independent callback rather than an emailed instruction.

Startup capital of $12,000 to $75,000 must finance both origination and the collection bridge, with first deals potentially taking six to 24 months. At $2,000 monthly predeal overhead, six months consumes $12,000 and 24 months $48,000 before acquisition spikes or rights purchases. At $15,000 monthly overhead, those amounts become $90,000 and $360,000, so the long-delay, high-burn combination exceeds the stated upper capital range even before working capital. Do not present all endpoints as jointly affordable. A six-month low-burn launch plus the Conservative bridge already requires $26,328.77, slightly above the minimum startup amount. Reduce committed overhead, obtain an earned milestone payment, or secure committed funding before accepting that path. For Base, a 30-day collection slip adds $20,085.62 to bridge needs without changing recognized profit if collectibility remains intact. Track disputed balances separately because an eventual price concession changes revenue as well as cash. Prepare a weekly concentration view showing the cash forecast with the largest licensee delayed. A profitable portfolio can still fail when one quarterly remittance finances nearly all payroll and the operator has distributed last month's unrestricted cash too early.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the statement population
   - Export contract, invoice, royalty, adjustment, and settlement records for the same reporting cutoff.
   - Retain source files and immutable identifiers; reconcile beginning balances before calculating current-period economics.
2. Rebuild earned consideration
   - Apply permitted-use pricing, minimum recoupment, documented credits, and supported reversal estimates by agreement.
   - Keep underlying licensee sales separate from operator fees and prevent advances from appearing twice.
3. Schedule every cash obligation
   - Place contractor payments, rights participation, overhead, expected receipts, and lock releases into thirteen weekly columns.
   - Use actual contractual dates where available and separately identify disputed or conditional receipts.
4. Set the distribution ceiling
   - Retain committed operating cash and designated reserves before calculating available owner distributions.
   - Re-run the largest-licensee delay case and document the funded response before releasing surplus cash.

## 4. Stress the Cohort and Price the Failure Modes  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Calculate acquisition efficiency at the cohort level before celebrating the headline net margin. Assuming every modeled agreement is newly acquired and all acquisition spending belongs to that cohort, Conservative CAC is $6,000 divided by two, or $3,000; Base is $35,000 divided by ten, or $3,500; Scaled is $45,000 divided by sixteen, or $2,812.50. Annual contribution before acquisition and overhead is $15,250, $30,000, and $39,375 per agreement. Dividing CAC by monthly contribution gives approximately 2.36, 1.40, and 0.86 months of contribution-equivalent payback, but these are not elapsed cash payback periods. Origination may consume six to 24 months, and collection delays follow delivery. Include failed pitches, technical evaluations, and negotiation expense in CAC rather than assigning costs only to signed accounts. If renewals dominate the portfolio, separate new-customer CAC from retention costs instead of dividing by every active agreement. Annual owner profit per active agreement is $6,250, $18,062.50, and $30,000. Use these figures to prioritize reporting automation and renewal alerts, not to assume each account has identical profitability. One catalog client demanding weekly manual exports can consume the contribution generated by several clean annual licenses.

Run sensitivity cases without changing the canonical baseline. A ten percent reduction in recognized revenue with operating costs held fixed reduces annual owner profit to $7,500, $138,125, and $400,000. This intentionally assumes no immediate participation savings, as may occur with fixed upstream minimums. A twenty percent contractor labor overrun instead reduces profit to $10,100, $166,625, and $462,000. Combining both shocks produces $5,100, $124,125, and $382,000. Keep collection stress separate until there is evidence of a concession or credit loss; adding 30 DSO days increases required liquidity by $3,082.19, $20,085.62, and $26,301.37 rather than automatically reducing earnings. For audit economics, a five percent royalty on $200,000 of substantiated omitted sales supports a $10,000 claim. If recovery probability is 80 percent and audit cost is $6,000, expected recovery less cost is $2,000 before collection delay and relationship damage. The risk fallback is a targeted records request before a broad audit. Do not book an unagreed audit claim as ordinary recurring revenue. Require documented access rights, a defined lookback period, and contractual cost-shifting thresholds before assuming the licensee will pay investigative expenses.

Translate legal boundaries into observable operating checks rather than ceremonial contract language. Verify signed copyright transfers under 17 U.S.C. 204, patent assignment records under 35 U.S.C. 261, and the trademark quality control implications of 15 U.S.C. 1127. A trademark assignment also requires attention to associated goodwill under 15 U.S.C. 1060. Territory and field restrictions require competition analysis under 15 U.S.C. 1 rather than an assumption that ownership permits every restraint. For customs-related licenses, test whether royalty payments must be added to transaction value under 19 U.S.C. 1401a(b)(1)(D); a workflow fee's label does not settle its customs treatment. These checks affect price, reserves, and whether delivery can proceed at all. Use automated obligation tracking to flag expiring rights, missed reports, inspection dates, and territory changes, but assign a named human to each exception. Before scaling, reproduce the Base waterfall from original contracts and bank records, demonstrate a funded delayed collection case, and show contractor capacity for the proposed reporting calendar. When title or permitted use fails verification, suspend the affected grant and offer a narrower documented package. More volume cannot repair rights that the operator never possessed.
