## 1. Pair Every Rights-Supply Signal with a Buyer-Side Trigger  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with four mapped plays, not a miscellaneous collection of assets: patent commercialization, brand category licensing, catalog reuse, and dataset licensing. Each play needs a rights source, a buyer trigger, and a measurable economic advantage. For patents, inspect university technology transfer listings, USPTO Patent Center prosecution records, and Assignment Center transfers; pair a relevant asset with an announced product platform or engineering bottleneck. For brands, compare trademark registrations and owner catalogs with retailers' assortment gaps. For catalogs, match archive inventories against publishers' release calendars and producers' reuse requirements. For datasets, connect documented collection rights with procurement requests describing coverage, freshness, or evaluation needs. Create one Airtable record per asset and intended use, rather than one record per owner. Capture the source URL, observation date, rights contact, territory, likely buyer, trigger date, and unresolved ownership question. A patent assignment is a research lead, not proof that every necessary right transferred; a downloadable file is not permission to sublicense. Reject leads whose only thesis is that intellectual property looks cheap. The relevant discount is the gap between an obtainable license and a specific buyer's documented cost of delay, substitution, or internal creation.

Mine intent on the buyer side before seeking broad exclusivity from an owner. Read SEC EDGAR product descriptions, company engineering vacancies, trade show exhibitor announcements, retailer category plans, and SAM.gov solicitations for concrete application language. A robotics launch can justify investigating sensing patents; it does not establish infringement. A publisher expanding localized editions can justify asking about translation and territorial rights; it does not establish budget. For datasets, identify required fields, update intervals, provenance evidence, and whether buyers need internal analytics, redistribution, or model training. Those uses are separate negotiation variables. Record an exact quotation from a public source and formulate one falsifiable hypothesis: the buyer will pay to obtain a stated capability before a stated milestone. Test it in an interview without disclosing restricted asset materials. Favor triggers younger than ninety days unless a longer procurement cycle is visible. Maintain a suppression field for stale triggers, explicit refusals, and already licensed categories. The founder reviews twenty new asset-use hypotheses weekly while the diligence contractor prepares source packets. Stop adding records when review backlogs exceed two weeks; additional names cannot repair weak demand evidence.

Seek undervalued rights where coordination costs obscure an otherwise credible application. University portfolios may have neglected industry segments; established brands may lack a manufacturer in one category; regional archives may hold well-indexed material without an active licensing desk; specialist data owners may serve users manually without a reusable license package. Interview the owner about existing grants, reserved channels, obligations to funders, restrictions on exclusivity, and willingness to support diligence. For federally funded inventions, investigate applicable government rights and obligations under 35 U.S.C. §§ 202–204 rather than assuming unrestricted exclusivity. Define a narrow first offer covering one field, one territory, one buyer class, and a clear expiration. Do not purchase a portfolio merely to make outreach sound authoritative. Use a documented representation mandate or a conditional rights arrangement that specifies who can negotiate and who must sign. Keep owner authorization separate from buyer qualification in the CRM. With startup capital of $25,000–$250,000 and a 6–24 month first-deal window, avoid tying the entire budget to speculative rights acquisition. Reserve capacity for document review, buyer evaluation, and reporting setup, because those activities convert a promising introduction into a collectible contract.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Trade Narrow Rights Packages for Faster Buyer Decisions**
> 
> Experienced operators reduce uncertainty before expanding the asset package. These tactics improve evidence quality without adding another full-time role.
> - Ask university licensing staff which applications have received repeat inquiries but no completed license; investigate why each buyer stalled.
> - For brand opportunities, obtain the owner's existing category exclusions before approaching manufacturers, preventing conflicts with incumbent licensees.
> - For catalogs, request a rights ledger for ten representative works before evaluating thousands of files; missing contributor documentation often repeats.
> - For datasets, offer an authorized, purpose-limited evaluation sample with field definitions and provenance notes rather than circulating the complete asset.

## 2. Score Rights, Demand, and Collection Risk Before Spending  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a hundred-point qualification score with six weighted dimensions: ownership and licensing authority, twenty-five; buyer urgency, twenty; permitted-use fit, twenty; contribution economics, fifteen; implementation readiness, ten; and reporting observability, ten. Score evidence, not founder enthusiasm. A named buyer with an approved project merits more urgency credit than a market-size report. A complete assignment chain earns more ownership credit than an owner's verbal assertion. Advance records scoring at least seventy-five only when critical defects are resolved; a high total cannot compensate for absent licensing authority or prohibited use. Keep fifty-five through seventy-four in a targeted research queue, and archive lower scores unless a specific missing document could change the result. These thresholds are internal operating choices, not measured industry averages. The founder owns buyer evidence, the IP counsel contractor reviews title and scope, the diligence contractor checks asset fitness, and the royalty operations contractor models reporting and collection. That is Candidate #19's one-full-time, three-contractor pod. Contractor capacity is purchased by deliverable, with work-in-progress limits that protect the founder from supervising four different speculative negotiations at once. Log every score change with its supporting evidence.

Build a diligence packet appropriate to each play instead of applying a single ownership checklist. Patent packets require family coverage, maintenance status, assignment instruments, existing licenses, and a technical mapping to the intended application. Under 35 U.S.C. § 261, patent assignments require a written instrument; recorded documents remain inputs to review rather than a complete title conclusion. Brand packets require territory and goods coverage plus workable quality standards, sample approvals, and inspection rights supporting controlled use under 15 U.S.C. § 1127. Catalog packets separate copyright ownership from music, performer, privacy, publicity, and embedded-material rights; 17 U.S.C. § 204 addresses signed writings for transfers of copyright ownership. Dataset packets distinguish copyrightable selection or arrangement from underlying facts and separately assess contracts, trade secrets, personal information, and source restrictions. Never label raw facts exclusively owned simply because someone assembled a spreadsheet. If a proposed dataset contains sensitive personal information, pause distribution until purpose, access, retention, and applicable requirements are resolved. Require the seller to identify precisely what they can grant, and require counsel to flag gaps before a buyer receives a definitive rights representation.

Qualify commercial fit through a thirty-minute discovery meeting that ends with a named decision and next date. Ask what the buyer would build or buy without the license, which use is indispensable, which territory generates value, who approves expenditure, and what could block deployment after signature. Request a bounded volume estimate rather than a flattering total market forecast. For a hypothetical royalty, $2 million of eligible licensee product sales at three percent produces $60,000 of operator royalty revenue, not $2 million. If a $30,000 advance is fully recoupable against that same obligation, total royalties remain $60,000 rather than $90,000. Cash timing and revenue recognition still depend on contractual performance and the applicable accounting treatment. Have the royalty contractor test definitions for returns, discounts, bundled products, affiliates, currency conversion, and reporting periods. Calculate expected contribution after owner participations, specialist review, support, and collection effort. A large headline minimum with extensive customization can be inferior to a smaller repeatable license. Route unusual engineering or clearance burdens back into pricing before presenting terms, and document whether the operator acts as licensor or agent so revenue is counted on the correct basis.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify Four Conditions Before Sending Definitive Terms**
> 
> The founder records each decision in the asset-use record. A score does not override a failed authority, use, economics, or reporting check.
> - Verify the granting party, signed authority, existing grants, co-owner interests, and any security interests affecting the proposed rights.
> - Verify exact field, territory, duration, sublicensing permissions, and exclusions against the buyer's actual deployment plan.
> - Verify a named budget owner, measurable buyer benefit, delivery costs, and a minimum payment structure the pod can support.
> - Verify sample reporting, royalty definitions, audit access, payment mechanics, and practical remedies for missing or inaccurate reports.

## 3. Run a Permissioned Outreach Cadence and Measure Real Conversion  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Operate two coordinated queues: owners who can authorize a package, and buyers whose projects can absorb it. Do not imply that an owner has retained the operator before the mandate is signed. Start buyer research with a neutral problem interview, then use authorized materials once representation is documented. A practical fifteen-business-day cadence begins with a short email on day one citing the public trigger, a narrowly relevant capability, and one question. On day four, send a professional-network connection request without attaching restricted materials. On day eight, offer a one-page rights brief through the established channel. On day twelve, call a published business number when appropriate; on day fifteen, send a concise close-the-loop message and stop unless the recipient engages. Honor refusals across channels. Commercial email must follow applicable requirements under 15 U.S.C. § 7704, including accurate headers, nondeceptive subjects, required identification, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out mechanism. Use HubSpot suppression lists and dated CRM tasks rather than purchased bulk sequences. The founder personally handles replies because early objections reveal whether the asset, use case, or buyer selection is wrong. Delegate preparation, not judgment.

Use a cohort model to plan capacity, then replace assumptions with observed results. An illustrative 6–24 month cohort starts with two hundred raw asset-use leads, of which eighty have credible rights and demand evidence, forty produce two-way conversations, twenty reach qualified discovery, twelve request a scoped evaluation or terms, eight enter substantive negotiation, and five sign paying licenses. The corresponding stage conversions are forty percent, fifty percent, fifty percent, sixty percent, roughly sixty-seven percent, and roughly sixty-three percent; overall conversion is two and one-half percent. These are planning benchmarks, not historical results or expected market performance. Count each lead once, tie multiple contacts to one opportunity, and distinguish an owner mandate from a paying buyer license. A signed mandate establishes authority but does not establish license revenue. Track days in stage, reasons lost, and overdue next actions alongside conversion. Below-target replies suggest poor targeting or weak relevance; strong discovery with weak evaluation suggests unclear rights or poor asset fit; stalled negotiations suggest economic, reporting, or scope friction. Do not solve every shortfall by increasing message volume. Review the oldest five opportunities weekly and either schedule a buyer decision or return them to nurture.

Build inbound around evidence buyers can inspect without receiving the underlying restricted asset. Publish one authorized application brief per active play, describing permitted uses, excluded uses, territory, delivery format, evaluation process, and reporting requirements. A patent brief should describe the problem and application without asserting that prospects infringe. A brand brief should show approved category concepts rather than imply unrestricted endorsement. Catalog previews require appropriate display permission; dataset previews should use authorized samples or clearly labeled synthetic examples. Place these briefs where demand already gathers: university licensing directories, relevant trade associations, industry newsletters, and owner-approved website pages. Offer a scheduled technical clinic instead of a vague contact form. Ask inbound prospects for their intended use, territory, launch date, budget range, and reporting capability before allocating specialist time. Handle objections by isolating the disputed variable. For price, compare scoped alternatives and reduce breadth rather than automatically discounting. For rights concerns, provide the relevant diligence evidence under suitable confidentiality terms. For evaluation requests, define access limits, success criteria, and an expiration. For exclusivity, trade a narrow field and measurable performance commitments rather than surrendering every future market for one uncertain buyer.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create an evidence-linked sourcing cohort
   - Enter twenty asset-use hypotheses weekly across the four mapped plays.
   - Record the public trigger, source date, rights contact, buyer role, specific use, and next research question; stop intake when review work exceeds the pod's two-week capacity.
2. Qualify authority and buyer urgency
   - Apply the hundred-point score and resolve all critical defects.
   - Founder checks demand; counsel checks authority and scope; diligence contractor checks fitness; royalty operations contractor checks unit economics and reportability.
3. Run the five-touch sourcing sequence
   - Use days one, four, eight, twelve, and fifteen for relevant outreach.
   - Personalize around a documented trigger, use authorized materials, honor channel restrictions and opt-outs, and end the sequence without escalating pressure when the prospect does not engage.
4. Convert evaluation into a scoped license
   - Attach acceptance criteria, commercial terms, and a sample royalty report.
   - Schedule a buyer decision, obtain the correct signatures, verify payment setup, and assign obligation tracking before counting the opportunity as a signed paying license.

## 4. Land Three-to-Ten Wins Without Losing Revenue Discipline  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the first three licenses to establish repeatability rather than maximize the number of different asset types under management. The four mapped plays define the sourcing universe, but the founder should concentrate delivery on whichever one or two produce the strongest qualified demand. During months one through three, assemble rights evidence and conduct buyer discovery; during months four through six, seek mandates and bounded evaluations; during months six through twenty-four, convert qualified opportunities into paying licenses as diligence and procurement permit. These phases overlap and do not establish a promised closing date. Make evaluation access purpose-limited, time-limited, and distinct from production rights. The definitive agreement should state licensed rights, exclusions, territory, duration, deliverables, payment obligations, reporting, audit procedures, renewal, termination, and treatment of retained materials. Brand agreements additionally need operational quality controls. Have counsel assess exclusivity, territorial restrictions, tying, and other competitive restraints under applicable competition law, including 15 U.S.C. § 1; intellectual property does not create automatic immunity for restrictive arrangements. Do not let a buyer's requested launch date erase unresolved authority or reporting questions. A delayed clean grant is more useful than a rapid signature the operator cannot properly administer.

Reconcile the scorecard with earned license economics, not licensee turnover or the face value of multiyear contracts. Conservative annual gross revenue is $50,000; at a twenty-five percent net margin, costs are $37,500 and owner net profit is $12,500. An illustrative three-win allocation is $15,000, $15,000, and $20,000 of annual recognized revenue. Base annual gross revenue is $425,000; at a forty-two and one-half percent net margin, costs are $244,375 and owner net profit is $180,625. Five licenses contributing $85,000 each illustrate that revenue total. Scaled annual gross revenue is $800,000; at a sixty percent net margin, costs are $320,000 and owner net profit is $480,000. Ten licenses contributing $80,000 each illustrate the scaled total. These allocations are scenario decompositions, not recommended prices or predicted first-year receipts. Count advances and minimum guarantees consistently with their recoupment and recognition treatment, never twice. If the operator merely represents an owner as agent, count the operator's earned fee rather than passing the owner's entire receipt through the scorecard. Keep the Conservative and Base teams at one full-time person plus three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time people plus four contractors.

Install collection and renewal mechanics before the first reporting period closes. The royalty operations contractor maps contract definitions to a CSV template with license identifier, reporting period, product or dataset identifier, territory, eligible sales or usage, permitted deductions, rate, currency, and amount due. Test one sample report against source records and record the reconciliation method. Use Airtable or HubSpot for obligation dates, QuickBooks Online for invoices and cash matching, and calendar alerts for renewals and audit windows. Automation should surface exceptions rather than silently accept every submitted number. Flag missing periods, unexplained deduction increases, territory mismatches, and usage growth without corresponding royalties. Store contract versions and supporting reports with access restricted to the pod members who need them. After each close, obtain permission before publishing a case study or naming the licensee; confidential terms remain private. Ask satisfied buyers and owners for one adjacent introduction tied to the same use case. Expand toward ten wins only when existing reporting arrives reliably, renewals have accountable owners, and exception work fits available contractor hours. A sourcing system becomes high-margin when every new contract reuses proven evidence, negotiation boundaries, and collection routines.

8. 15 U.S.C. § 7704 — Requirements for commercial electronic mail: Governs commercial email practices including header accuracy, subject lines, identification, postal addresses, and opt-out handling in the sourcing cadence. — <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7704>
