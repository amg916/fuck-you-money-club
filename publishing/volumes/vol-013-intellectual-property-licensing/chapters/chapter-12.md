## 1. Days 1–7: Bound the Rights, Assign the Pod, and Cap the Burn  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Day 1 establishes a licensing operation, not an invention studio or an IP acquisition fund. The full-time lead owns buyer discovery, pricing, negotiation, and the cash ledger. Contractor one is IP counsel; contractor two handles rights research and commercial evidence; contractor three handles royalty operations and data security. These are fractional assignments, not three full-time salaries disguised as contractors. Reserve eight counsel hours, twenty research hours, and twelve operations hours for the first month, with written scope limits and escalation prices. Map four plays into one register: patent field-of-use licensing, controlled brand licensing, catalog rights packaging, and permissioned dataset licensing. Record one candidate asset in each lane, then select one primary lane and one backup by Day 7. Each asset needs an identified owner, buyer problem, permitted use, territory, proposed term, and evidence location. A register entry is not evidence of ownership. Link signed assignments, employment agreements, commissioning documents, registrations, and existing licenses separately. Where a document is missing, create a named remediation task and suspend external rights claims. The lead should be able to explain the proposed grant in sixty seconds without asserting broader exclusivity than the documents support.

Days 2–4 convert that register into a rights boundary a buyer can evaluate. For patents, use USPTO Patent Center and Assignment Center to investigate status and recorded transfers, then have counsel examine the actual instruments and applicable claims. Record patent numbers, expiration assumptions, maintenance status, fields of use, and existing grants; owning a patent does not establish freedom to operate. For brands, examine USPTO TSDR records alongside actual use, ownership documents, product categories, and a workable quality-control protocol. For catalogs, distinguish composition, recording, image, text, performer, and publicity permissions instead of selling an undifferentiated bundle. Copyright transfers require attention to 17 U.S.C. § 204, while statutory termination under sections 203 and 304 can affect older grants. For datasets, separate database structure, individual content, contractual access restrictions, personal information, and upstream redistribution permissions. No general label such as public data resolves those questions. Build a redacted evidence room in SharePoint or Google Drive with named access, expiring invitations where supported, and an access log. Do not upload raw personal records, trade secrets, or unpublished technical details to prospecting software. Counsel records unresolved limitations before the researcher produces any buyer-facing asset sheet.

Days 5–7 establish the economic boundary and the first decision record. Authorize at most $6,000 of incremental cash expenditure through Day 30: $2,400 for eight counsel hours at $300, $1,000 for twenty research hours at $50, $900 for twelve operations hours at $75, $400 for software and records, $500 for buyer interviews and sample preparation, and $800 contingency. This is a sprint budget, not the complete startup requirement. Candidate #19 retains a $25,000–$250,000 startup capital range and $2,000–$15,000 monthly operating range. Asset purchases, litigation, registration campaigns, and founder living costs are outside this sprint authorization and require separate funding before commitment. Track founder hours even when no cash salary is drawn. Use a weekly cash-plus-committed spend report so an unpaid invoice cannot conceal a breached ceiling. Require the primary asset to have no unresolved material defect in the proposed grant before buyer outreach describes it as available. Otherwise switch to the backup or stop. Finish the week with a one-page rights brief, a forty-account target list, a four-week task board, and a signed internal budget. Do not buy another asset to avoid confronting weak evidence on the first one.

> - Confirm that samples contain only material the operator may disclose; separate catalog components and remove restricted personal records.
> - Reserve the contractor hours and cash needed to resolve documented exceptions without exceeding the $6,000 sprint commitment ceiling.

## 2. Days 8–14: Test Buyer Demand Without Giving Away the Asset  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 8–10 test a commercial hypothesis with evidence the owner can safely disclose. Prepare four distinct offer patterns, but conduct most outreach for the primary lane. A patent offer specifies a product application and field of use, supported by a counsel-reviewed technical summary rather than an infringement accusation. A brand offer specifies product category, territory, channel, sample approval, and measurable quality obligations. A catalog offer specifies individual assets, media, duration, territory, and which underlying permissions travel with the license. A dataset offer specifies fields, refresh cadence, provenance, permitted processing, redistribution limits, and whether model training is included. Use synthetic records or a legitimately distributable sample for evaluation. The researcher builds the account evidence; the lead sends tailored messages and conducts calls. Enter contacts in HubSpot or a structured spreadsheet, with one row per account and separate contact records. Email opens, downloaded decks, and polite replies are not qualified demand. Count an account only when a named contact confirms a relevant use case, a decision process, and a plausible purchasing window. Record the exact obstacle in the buyer's words. This prevents the team from mistaking enthusiasm for authority, budget, or a deployable rights package.

Days 11–12 turn interviews into comparable licensing terms. Ask buyers what they currently pay to solve the same problem, which product or workflow would use the asset, who approves the grant, and what evidence their review team requires. Request a specimen royalty-report structure or procurement checklist before sending a complex proposal. For a running royalty, define the denominator: covered units, qualifying receipts, permitted returns, discounts, taxes, affiliate transfers, and bundled products. For a minimum guarantee, state whether it is recoupable against royalties, the measurement period, payment timing, and treatment of unused amounts. A $25,000 annual minimum credited against $40,000 of earned royalties produces $40,000 of total contractual consideration for that period, not $65,000. Recognition timing still depends on the agreement and applicable accounting treatment. Separate evaluation access from production rights and define deletion or return obligations when evaluation ends. Do not promise unlimited exclusivity to obtain an interview. Narrow exclusivity by field, territory, duration, and performance milestones, with counsel reviewing competition concerns under 15 U.S.C. §§ 1–2. The buyer should see a usable economic proposition, while the operator retains a clear boundary around what remains unlicensed.

Days 13–14 require disciplined synthesis rather than another outreach blast. Set the Day 30 discovery target at six completed interviews from forty individually selected accounts, with at least three qualified accounts and two written requests for a defined diligence item, sample, or draft scope. These are operating thresholds for this sprint, not claimed market conversion rates. A qualified account must identify a decision owner, application, procurement path, and purchasing window within the next twenty-four months. A written diligence request must name an actual next task; an automated acknowledgment does not count. At the midpoint, aim for three interviews completed and six more scheduled or actively being arranged. If fewer than three interviews occur, inspect delivery, account selection, and the opening proposition before cutting price. Have the researcher code objections into rights uncertainty, weak utility, integration burden, price, timing, and procurement friction. Select one variable to change in the next outreach batch and preserve the previous version. The lead should spend roughly half of available working time on buyer conversations and follow-through, not designing collateral. Contractors prepare evidence asynchronously so scarce counsel hours address specific objections instead of attending every introductory call.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect Leverage While Making Diligence Easy**
> 
> Experienced licensors reduce review friction without transferring commercial value before the contract supports it.
> - Request the buyer's actual royalty-export headers early; discovering an unavailable denominator after signature can make audit rights expensive to use.
> - For brand licenses, price the work of sample review, corrective action, and periodic inspection instead of treating quality control as a decorative clause.
> - For catalog packages, attach asset-level permission records so one disputed recording or image can be removed without collapsing the entire offer.
> - For dataset trials, use a versioned synthetic or permitted sample and require separate written scope for training, redistribution, and production access.

## 3. Days 15–21: Rehearse the Contract, Royalty Ledger, and Annual Economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Days 15–17 convert the strongest use case into a contractable scope and an operational rehearsal. Counsel works from a term sheet containing the parties, rights schedule, territory, field, duration, exclusivity conditions, consideration, reporting dates, inspection rights, breach remedies, and exit treatment. For patents, isolate the royalty treatment of patent rights after expiration and distinguish any separately priced know-how. For brands, assign actual inspection responsibilities and corrective deadlines rather than relying only on reserved rights. For catalogs, specify the licensed versions, editing permissions, credits, and handling of withdrawn assets. For datasets, define schema versions, refresh obligations, permitted users, security controls, deletion requirements, and downstream restrictions. The operations contractor creates an obligation register with an owner, due date, evidence artifact, and escalation rule for every recurring promise. Use DocuSign for execution when authorized and Airtable or a controlled spreadsheet for obligations; neither system determines whether the grant is valid. Send counsel a consolidated exception list instead of fragmented questions. Preserve the executed agreement and amendments as the authority, with automation reading only reviewed structured fields. A failed extraction must create a review task, never silently replace a contractual deadline or reporting obligation.

Days 18–19 test royalty reporting using a small, deliberately difficult transaction file. Include one return, one affiliate transfer, one foreign-currency sale, one bundled product, one duplicate row, and one transaction outside the licensed territory. Preserve source transactions and calculate adjustments separately so the audit trail survives recalculation. Reconcile opening minimum-guarantee credits, earned royalties, cash received, credits consumed, and closing receivables. Define exchange-rate source and date in the agreement instead of choosing whichever rate improves a monthly result. A repeatable Python csv script or spreadsheet should reproduce the approved expected amounts with zero unexplained variance; any rounding rule must be explicit. Inspect report completeness as well as arithmetic: a perfect calculation on omitted products still understates royalties. Require contractually appropriate access to supporting records, retention periods, audit frequency, and cost allocation when underreporting exceeds a negotiated threshold. The test uses synthetic transactions, not an unauthorized export from a prospect's systems. Set renewal alerts at 120, 90, and 60 days before the relevant action deadline, adjusting for the actual agreement. Track notice receipt and permitted delivery methods separately; an internal reminder does not itself exercise a renewal option or terminate a license.

Days 20–21 reconcile the operating plan to the canonical annual scorecard before discussing expansion. Conservative revenue is $50,000, with $37,500 of implied total costs and $12,500 owner net profit at a 25% margin. Base revenue is $425,000, with $244,375 of implied total costs and $180,625 owner net profit at a 42.5% margin. Scaled revenue is $800,000, with $320,000 of implied total costs and $480,000 owner net profit at a 60% margin. Those revenues consist only of recognized license fees and royalties, excluding licensees' underlying product sales. For simple volume illustrations, two licenses averaging $25,000, five averaging $85,000, and eight averaging $100,000 reproduce the respective revenue totals; these are arithmetic constructions, not assumed deal counts or forecasts. Do not add credited minimum guarantees a second time. Conservative and Base retain one full-time operator plus three contractors; Scaled requires two full-time people plus four contractors. The $6,000 sprint cap cannot be annualized into those cases: launch work, recurring expenses, rights costs, and total scenario costs have different boundaries. Maintain a bridge showing each actual expense against its intended category. Neither a proposal nor a signed expression of interest creates recognized revenue or funds the next month's work.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the grant and reporting specification
   - Version the reviewed rights schedule and royalty-field definitions together.
   - Record the source clause, owner, effective date, and amendment reference before enabling any recurring automation.
2. Validate a difficult transaction sample
   - Calculate expected royalties independently for the six exception cases.
   - Retain original rows, adjustment reasons, exchange-rate evidence, and explicit rounding behavior in the rehearsal package.
3. Reconcile obligations to money movements
   - Tie earned royalties and guarantee credits to invoices, receipts, and receivables.
   - Keep cash collection, contractual consideration, and recognized revenue distinct; investigate every unexplained variance before billing.
4. Release only the reviewed recurring workflow
   - Assign reporting, renewal, inspection, and exception tasks to named pod members.
   - Require human review of changed contract fields and retain a recoverable prior version when an amendment changes the workflow.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Day 60/90: Decide from Evidence, Not Pipeline Theater  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 22–24 close outstanding evidence gaps and request bounded next steps from the strongest buyers. Day 22 sends the reviewed scope and permitted sample; Day 23 asks the buyer to identify its technical reviewer and procurement owner; Day 24 records the remaining questions and an agreed review date. Do not demand a signature simply to decorate the sprint report. Candidate #19 has a 6–24 month first-deal horizon, and a credible diligence sequence is a more appropriate early milestone. Day 25 repeats the reporting rehearsal with an altered transaction file. Day 26 checks evidence-room access and removes expired evaluation permissions where the agreement allows. Day 27 reconciles paid and committed expenditure against the $6,000 ceiling. Day 28 scores each active asset against rights, demand, reporting, and capacity thresholds. Day 29 assigns any repair experiment a single owner, a maximum fourteen-day duration, and a measured outcome. Day 30 records Kill, Fix, or Scale with linked evidence and the next cash authorization. A lane may pass while another fails; do not blend four weak propositions into one apparently healthy portfolio. Preserve rejected hypotheses so later outreach does not repeat the same unsupported claims or commercial assumptions.

Apply the decision matrix in order. A material rights defect that cannot be cured within authorized scope, an unauthorized disclosure, or a breached cash ceiling stops the affected activity regardless of buyer interest. Demand-based Kill requires the specified outreach and interview evidence; an unfinished experiment is Fix, not proof that the market does not exist. By Day 30, Scale requires six interviews, three qualified accounts, two written diligence requests, a clean rights boundary, and a passing royalty rehearsal. By Day 60, require ten cumulative interviews, five qualified accounts, and three active diligence tracks with buyer-owned next actions dated within thirty days. By Day 90, require fourteen interviews, six qualified accounts, three active tracks, and two written scope acknowledgments that identify the buyer's review owner and purchasing window. Acknowledgment is not contractual acceptance. These cumulative thresholds measure progress, not revenue. If the primary lane passes, expand account coverage by at most twenty additional accounts per month and keep simultaneous document-heavy negotiations at three. Do not hire into the six-person Scaled configuration because three prospects requested drafts. The lead must demonstrate funded workload and repeatable reporting before adding permanent capacity or making a larger rights commitment.

Authorize extensions explicitly: cumulative incremental cash plus commitments may not exceed $12,000 through Day 60 or $18,000 through Day 90 under this launch plan. Neither extension is automatic, and the lead must show six months of runway at the proposed forward monthly cash burn, after existing commitments and excluding prospective license receipts. At $6,000 monthly burn, that reserve is $36,000; an operator starting with $25,000 cannot meet it without lowering burn or obtaining additional committed capital. A Fix decision reallocates the existing authorization rather than adding an unbudgeted rescue spend. Examples include narrowing a dataset's redistribution scope, removing a disputed catalog asset, replacing an unmeasurable royalty denominator, or changing the target product category. Each repair gets one testable hypothesis and a fourteen-day review; after two failed repair cycles on the same bottleneck, stop that lane and reallocate only if the backup has independent evidence. Killing a lane means ending new pursuit and observing existing contractual duties, not abandoning records or ignoring confidentiality. The Day 90 output is a funded, documented licensing pipeline or a deliberate stop. Annual profit cases remain the scorecard's scenarios until executed agreements, recognized revenue, and actual costs substantiate a different operating result.
