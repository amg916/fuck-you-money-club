## 1. Identify the owner, the economic buyer, and the person authorized to pay  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The treasury is normally the asset custodian, not the buyer of your service. A named owner, authorized estate representative, or corporate successor hires the recovery firm and owes its permitted fee. Separate three roles before quoting: entitlement to the property, authority to sign the service agreement, and authority to release payment. A corporate controller may sponsor a claim while treasury controls incoming funds and accounts payable controls your invoice. An estate beneficiary may want speed but lack authority to bind the estate. Request the actual delegation, letters of appointment, or corporate signing policy rather than accepting an impressive title. No statute generally forces an owner to hire a finder. The acute pain is an unreconciled receivable, an estate that cannot close cleanly, missing merger evidence, or staff time diverted from higher-value work. State explicitly that the owner can claim directly through the state's official process without hiring your firm. Then sell a defined evidence package, submission support, and status tracking. A $40,000 corporate claim at an otherwise permitted 10% fee produces $4,000 of service revenue, not $40,000. The remaining $36,000 belongs economically to the owner after that fee.

Decision-maker psychology changes the sales process more than the size of the public listing. Individuals first ask whether the letter is a scam; corporate staff ask whether the asset really belongs to their entity; fiduciaries ask whether the expenditure can be supported in their accounting. Lead with an independently verifiable property record, the official agency contact, and a plain explanation of your separate business. Never request banking credentials to prove ownership. For a corporate uncashed warrant, determine whether the issuing agency can still reissue the warrant or whether custody has transferred to the state. The correct route changes the evidence, payee, and payment clock. Ask the controller to identify the general-ledger account, vendor-onboarding requirements, purchase-order threshold, and invoice approver before work starts. Ask an estate representative how recovery expenses are approved and whether distributions are already scheduled. Do not manufacture urgency from a nonexistent expiration date. Real urgency comes from a documented estate hearing, dissolution timetable, expiring supporting records, or a customer's reporting deadline. Put that date beside the agency's actual processing estimate, not beside a salesperson's promised settlement date. A trustworthy explanation of delay often converts better than a larger headline recovery estimate.

Screen willingness and ability to pay separately from entitlement. A valid owner can still be insolvent, disorganized, or determined to avoid the fee. Check entity status through the relevant Secretary of State, review authorized bankruptcy records through PACER when indicated, and obtain the customer's vendor-payment instructions directly. Public records are leads, not a complete credit opinion. Bankruptcy can change who controls an asset; pause owner-directed collection until counsel resolves estate ownership and representative authority. For ordinary corporate customers, confirm whether accounts payable will accept a contingent invoice supported by the treasury remittance advice. A purchase order documents internal purchasing authority but does not redirect treasury funds or override a finder statute. For individuals, agree on the invoice trigger and a lawful payment method without conditioning assistance on surrender of the entire recovery. Estimate collection exposure before buying records: a $1,000 expected fee with $250 acquisition cost, $200 document labor, and a 20% noncollection assumption leaves only $350 before general overhead. If the economics fail, decline the engagement or offer a lawful, clearly scoped alternative. Do not solve weak underwriting by taking control of a claimant's identity or account.

## 2. Select a payment rail that preserves owner control and supports collection  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Design two distinct money movements: custodian to lawful owner, then customer to recovery firm. The default should be direct state payment to the owner, followed by an invoice tied to the engagement's permitted earning event. Some jurisdictions provide a specific process for paying an authorized representative or allocating a permitted fee; use it only when the agency's rules and accepted documents support that exact route. Never assume a power of attorney creates an assignment, grants unlimited endorsement authority, or obligates a treasury to split a check. Record the claim identifier, approved amount, actual receipt date, invoice identifier, and fee calculation as separate fields. A claim marked approved is not collected cash, and a check mailed to an old address is not evidence that the owner received usable funds. Define the contractual payment trigger around verifiable receipt or another lawful, clearly specified event. If the agency pays by paper check, document deposit and settlement through an appropriate customer confirmation, not unrestricted account access. Preserve a narrow remittance extract rather than the owner's entire bank statement. That separation keeps client principal out of the firm's operating account and makes disputed calculations easier to resolve.

Choose the customer-to-firm rail by ticket size, payer type, reversibility, and administrative friction. ACH usually suits a business invoice, but a debit requires valid authorization and applicable network and account rules; an ACH credit initiated by the customer avoids storing debit instructions. Consumer preauthorized electronic transfers introduce specific requirements under 12 CFR 1005.10, including authorization and stop-payment rights where applicable. A card checkout through Stripe can simplify small invoices but adds processing charges and dispute exposure. Verify processor acceptance of the actual business model before using it. Fedwire is useful for larger business payments, although bank cutoffs, account verification, sanctions screening, and fraud procedures still matter. Do not describe any rail as eliminating all recovery or litigation risk. An independent escrow arrangement can coordinate an authorized settlement, but ordinary treasury claims rarely require an operator-controlled escrow account. Have the custodian confirm whether the proposed destination is permitted before promising escrow-based fee protection. For changed wire instructions, call a previously verified number and require independent internal review. Email alone is insufficient evidence of a new beneficiary account, even when the sender appears to be the established controller.

Retainers, factoring, and milestone holds are commercial structures rather than interchangeable bank networks. A retainer collected by ACH is still subject to the rules governing the underlying recovery agreement, including any restriction on advance compensation. Calling a recovery charge a research deposit does not remove a statutory fee ceiling. Milestones may support permitted fixed-scope document work, but they cannot turn a prohibited early fee into a lawful one. Factoring transfers or finances an eligible operator receivable; it does not create ownership of the customer's treasury claim. A factor will examine invoice validity, assignment terms, debtor credit, disputes, and recourse. On a $4,000 earned invoice, an illustrative 3% financing charge costs $120 before other contractual charges; that expense reduces contribution rather than increasing recovery revenue. Escrow has its own administrative costs and release conditions, so reserve it for permitted cases where the coordination benefit exceeds those costs. Establish a fallback before submission: if the agency rejects split disbursement, the owner receives the full award and pays through the agreed invoice rail. Do not reroute proceeds through an employee, nominee, unrelated affiliate, or newly opened account merely to preserve your expected fee.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Map both money movements
   - Document custodian-to-owner payment separately from customer-to-firm payment.
   - Record the lawful payee, approved destination, permitted earning event, invoice approver, and fallback rail before submitting the claim.
2. Verify payment instructions
   - Confirm customer and beneficiary details through an established independent channel.
   - Use a previously verified telephone number for changes; do not rely on reply email or request the customer's banking password.
3. Invoice against the agreed evidence
   - Calculate the fee from the actual eligible recovery and attach the required support.
   - Match remittance evidence to the claim and contract; apply statutory limits, exclusions, and any agreed adjustment before requesting payment.
4. Close only after reconciliation
   - Match the received fee to bank or processor settlement and update exceptions.
   - Track reversals, refunds, partial receipts, and outstanding balances separately; never mark a case collected solely because the state approved it.

## 3. Protect the fee through enforceable scope, verified identity, and disciplined collections  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Fee protection begins with a jurisdiction worksheet, not an aggressive noncircumvention paragraph. Identify the custody program, applicable finder registration, permitted contracting date, compensation ceiling, required disclosures, and any assignment restrictions before presenting terms. California Code of Civil Procedure section 1582 regulates recovery agreements, including timing and compensation; its 10% ceiling cannot be bypassed by splitting an invoice into discovery and administration charges. Texas Property Code section 74.507 supplies its own agreement requirements and compensation limitation. Apply the actual provision to the relevant property and transaction rather than copying a national contract template. Keep the official statutory text and agency instructions with a dated review record. If the applicable waiting period has not elapsed, do not secure a prohibited agreement now and simply postpone the invoice. Where registration is required, confirm that the firm and the individual performing regulated activity satisfy the relevant rules. The contract should identify the property or covered portfolio precisely enough to distinguish your assignment from unrelated assets. Define the fee base, treatment of partial payments, expenses, termination, and evidence of receipt. Avoid claiming perpetual rights over every future recovery an owner may discover independently.

Circumvention usually starts as an evidence problem: the customer believes the firm only copied a free listing, while the firm believes it built a complete entitlement package. Record the contracted deliverables and their delivery dates. Use DocuSign or Adobe Acrobat Sign to preserve signed terms and delivery records, but remember that an electronic signature does not cure an unlawful agreement. Give the customer a claim-specific progress log and copies of documents they are entitled to receive. A narrowly drafted fee obligation may remain payable when the owner receives the covered property directly, if local law and the agreement support that result. It should not penalize the owner's statutory right to claim without a finder. Distinguish a genuine refusal to pay an earned invoice from a dispute about identity, scope, calculation, or prohibited charges. Send a calculation with the initial invoice, a concise reminder after the agreed due date, and a written resolution proposal before escalating. Have counsel assess collection options and applicable consumer rules where relevant. Do not threaten criminal action, impersonate the treasury, or assert a lien that the firm does not possess. Clear documentation protects margin better than pressure tactics that create another dispute.

Identity quality determines whether the payment rail can work at all. A name match is an intake signal, not proof of ownership. Match historical addresses, entity identifiers, predecessor relationships, and claim-specific documentation against the agency's requirements. Resolve same-name conflicts before sending sensitive documents or quoting a recovery as certain. Collect only what the filing requires, use restricted-access storage, and keep identity evidence out of ordinary CRM notes. Airtable can track case status, while a separately controlled document repository holds sensitive attachments; assess the actual configuration rather than assuming a product name supplies security. Define retention periods around agency requirements, contractual evidence, and applicable privacy duties. Limit contractors to the cases and documents needed for assigned work. A counterparty credit review should also identify suspicious payment changes, third-party payment requests, and attempts to redirect an estate payment into a beneficiary's personal account without authority. Escalate ownership conflicts and suspected fraud instead of improving the paperwork around a questionable story. When the verified recovery is too small to cover document costs, provide the official direct-claim route and close the opportunity. Rejecting a low-value or mismatched claim preserves both customer trust and the team's available filing capacity.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before a claim becomes an engagement**
> 
> Proceed only when the owner, agreement, receiving account, and collection economics are supported by actual case evidence.
> - Confirm the claimant's identity and chain of entitlement, including estate, trust, merger, or winding-up authority where the named owner differs.
> - Verify finder registration, contracting eligibility dates, aggregate fee limits, and direct-claim disclosures under the specific custody program.
> - Confirm the custodian's accepted payee and destination; independently verify any escrow, representative payment, or changed bank instructions.
> - Approve the case's expected fee, unreimbursed document costs, payer credit exposure, invoice trigger, and practical fallback if split payment is unavailable.
