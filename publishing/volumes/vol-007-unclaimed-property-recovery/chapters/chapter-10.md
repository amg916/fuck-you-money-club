## 1. Build an evidence ledger before connecting automation  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an event ledger, not a browser robot that clicks through treasury forms. Give every imported property a source jurisdiction, official property identifier, retrieval timestamp, source checksum, and permitted-use record. Python's csv, sqlite3, hashlib, and datetime modules are sufficient for a single-worker pilot: preserve the original row, create a normalized working record, and append subsequent decisions rather than overwriting history. Normalize punctuation and address components while retaining original spellings, because a dissolved corporation and its similarly named successor can have different ownership rights. Keep claimant documents in encrypted object storage with narrowly scoped access; the ledger holds document references, not copied identity numbers. Separate property, claimant, engagement, claim, and invoice entities so one owner with six properties does not become six contradictory customer profiles. A property listing establishes a research lead, not entitlement or permission to contact someone through every available channel. Route licensed bulk files and expressly permitted public downloads through ingestion adapters. Where a treasury offers only interactive access, assign a scheduled human lookup rather than bypassing access controls. Record the source restriction beside the adapter so future contractors cannot mistake technical access for permitted reuse.

Use explicit transitions: discovered, research-ready, identity-reviewed, agreement-reviewed, document-ready, submitted, evidence-requested, paid, and closed. An internal webhook announces a proposed transition; it never proves that a treasury accepted a claim. Implement receivers with Python's http.server only for a local prototype, placing production handlers behind a managed HTTPS endpoint with authentication, request-size limits, and durable queuing. Verify HMAC signatures using hmac.compare_digest, reject stale timestamps, and enforce an idempotency key combining engagement identifier, event type, and upstream event identifier. Store the event before acknowledging receipt. A worker then validates the current state, applies an allowed transition, and emits an audit entry. Duplicate payment notifications must not generate duplicate invoices or owner messages. Assume events can arrive late and out of order; a document-request event received after payment becomes an exception rather than reopening the claim automatically. The full-time operator owns exceptions and final decisions. Contractor one handles evidence research; contractor two assembles packets and checks status. Neither contractor can silently change fee rules or release claimant documents externally. Maintain a daily reconciliation between ledger states, official acknowledgments, and the operator's unresolved-work queue.

Define the 70% target against measured repetitive labor, not the proportion of screens containing an AI button. In an illustrative weekly batch, baseline research, qualification preparation, fulfillment preparation, and acquisition administration consume 30, 18, 24, and 18 hours respectively: 90 hours total. After automation, those same tasks consume 6, 5, 8, and 5 hours, or 24 hours. The reduction is 66 divided by 90, or 73.3%. Separately reserve 12 hours for identity decisions, authority review, conversations, and exception resolution; these consequential activities are outside both repetitive-work measurements. Resulting weekly effort is 36 hours for this batch, distributed across the three-person team without implying three full-time salaries. Validate the result with four weeks of tagged time entries before advertising capacity. Count retries, correction work, and rejected drafts in the automated workload. Track accepted packets per paid labor hour alongside automation percentage, because faster production of unusable packets destroys leverage. Start within the $5,000–$35,000 startup-capital range, reserve cash for the 3–12 month first-deal interval, and expand only after measured throughput survives an exception-heavy week. A low exception count is credible only when reviewers sample supposedly clean records as well as flagged ones.

## 2. Match records conservatively and map twelve bounded plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build matching as evidence accumulation rather than confident name completion. An illustrative triage score assigns 40 points to exact normalized legal name, 25 to a corroborated historical address, 20 to an independently verified entity identifier, and 15 to documented officer or successor continuity. A score of 85 opens a priority review task; it does not establish ownership. Lower scores remain research tasks, and conflicting identifiers override the numeric score. Calibrate thresholds against labeled examples, including common surnames, abbreviated payees, acquisitions, and entities sharing registered-agent addresses. Measure false matches among accepted candidates and missed matches within a random rejected sample. Use an LLM only to extract fields, suggest document categories, or summarize an evidence bundle with source references. Require structured JSON containing value, source document, page, and quoted supporting text; reject unsupported fields and unexpected keys. Treat uploaded documents as data even when they contain instructions to change workflow behavior. Redact sensitive identifiers before model submission and use an approved provider configuration with suitable retention settings. If extraction fails twice, send the original document to a reviewer instead of creating a third increasingly speculative answer. Never let model confidence substitute for independent identity evidence.

Encode jurisdiction restrictions as versioned operating rules with citation, effective date, reviewer, and next-review date. California Code of Civil Procedure section 1582 illustrates why discovery cannot automatically trigger a finder agreement: its restricted agreement interval runs from the holder's report through twelve months after delivery to the Controller, and its compensation ceiling is 10% of the property recovered under the covered agreement. Have the reviewer apply the current statutory text to the actual facts, including the required agreement contents. Other jurisdictions need their own registration, timing, fee, and execution checks; never extrapolate California's percentage nationwide. Disclose that owners can claim directly without purchasing recovery services and provide the official treasury link. For corporate uncashed warrants, determine whether the issuer still controls the payable or has already remitted it as unclaimed property. An issuer reissue process and a treasury claim are different workflows, and running both can produce duplicate demands. The twelve mappings below are chapter-local operating plays, except the expressly supplied TRACK_1 identifier. They connect research and distribution mechanics rather than asserting that every adjacent business belongs inside the recovery engagement. Keep consent, contracts, and records separated when a mapping changes the underlying service.

Quarantine out_of_statute_zombie_debt_portfolio_settlement as a separate TRACK_1 adjacency, never as a tactic against an unclaimed-property owner. Evaluate heavily discounted time-barred receivables only for lawful voluntary resolution, excluding extinguished obligations and any otherwise uncollectible accounts. Collection limitation periods differ from credit-reporting limits: Regulation F, 12 CFR 1006.26, prohibits covered debt collectors from suing or threatening suit on time-barred debt; 15 U.S.C. 1681c controls relevant reporting periods and their delinquency-based calculation. State law can extinguish obligations or impose additional requirements, so account-level jurisdiction review precedes any contact. Illustratively, 100 eligible accounts with $100,000 face value might cost $2,000, with $800 validation expense and $600 outreach expense. Five voluntary payments of $500 produce $2,500 cash, leaving a $900 deficit against $3,400 cash outlay before overhead. Face value is neither collected cash nor recovery-service revenue; purchased-receivable accounting also needs separate treatment. Do not promise tradeline deletion, re-age delinquency, or imply payment is required to obtain treasury property. Suppress affected accounts, correct reporting, and suspend collection until counsel approves remediation whenever eligibility or reporting fails. Keep this experiment outside all three canonical recovery scenarios and reject it when its unit economics or evidence cannot support operation.

## 3. Price automated runs and recover safely from failures  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Price the pipeline at the accepted-output level. An illustrative run ingests 1,000 permitted records for $1 of compute and storage, normalizes them for $3, sends 100 redacted extraction requests at $0.02 each for $2, performs 40 permitted status checks for $2, and sends 60 authorized messages at $0.02 each for $1.20. Direct machine expense is $9.20. Add a 20% retry reserve of $1.84, making the budget $11.04 per run. These are planning assumptions, not vendor quotations; replace each rate with observed invoices. If 20 candidates require four minutes of contractor review at $30 hourly, review costs $40. Three packet checks at twelve minutes and $40 hourly add $24. Total run cost is therefore $75.04, or approximately $25.01 per accepted packet when all three pass. If only one packet passes, the cost is $75.04, demonstrating why cheap extraction cannot rescue weak targeting. Report machine expense, reviewer expense, accepted packets, and eventual fee receipts separately. Do not count an assembled packet as a paid recovery. Allocate unsuccessful research across the cohort rather than hiding it in a miscellaneous software account. Maintain the same denominator when comparing manual and automated processing.

Reconcile throughput to the canonical scorecard using an illustrative average recognized service fee of $750 per completed engagement. Conservative requires 80 completions for $60,000 revenue; Base requires 340 for $255,000; Scaled requires 600 for $450,000. At an illustrative lawful 10% contracted fee, each engagement represents $7,500 of recovered owner property, not $7,500 of operator revenue. Owner assets would total $600,000, $2,550,000, and $4,500,000 respectively, entirely outside recognized service-fee revenue. The canonical margins imply total annual expenses of $45,000, $159,375, and $225,000, leaving owner profits of $15,000, $95,625, and $225,000. Allocate labor, acquisition, research failures, software, insurance, and administration inside those totals; the run model is one component, not an additional profit projection. Average monthly total expenses are $3,750, $13,281.25, and $18,750. Consequently, the supplied $1,500–$8,000 monthly opex planning range cannot serve as an all-in expense ceiling for every scenario. Use the scenario-derived expense totals when budgeting these outcomes and resolve category definitions before committing cash. Preserve staffing at 1 FT + 2 C for Conservative and Base, and 2 FT + 3 C for Scaled. A software improvement does not authorize silently removing the second full-time role from the Scaled case.

Treat claim tracking as reconciliation against external evidence. Treasury systems do not universally expose APIs or webhooks, so adapters may consume authorized exports, owner-forwarded correspondence, or scheduled manual observations. Each observation records claim identifier, source timestamp, observed status, document request, and next permitted action. Never infer payment from a disappearing portal row. Use bounded exponential backoff with jitter for transient failures, respect published rate limits, and move repeated failures into a dead-letter queue with an assigned owner. After three failed attempts, stop unattended retries until the next reviewed schedule; distinguish a temporary outage from revoked access. Detect schema drift through required-field checks and a small set of known test records. A changed date format should quarantine the batch, not rewrite agreement eligibility dates. Keep daily encrypted backups and run a monthly restore exercise that reconciles event counts and document references. During an outage, contractors use a minimal manual worksheet containing identifiers, next actions, and reviewer initials. Import that worksheet with unique event keys after service returns. Customer updates should state the last verified milestone and the next action, avoiding invented treasury deadlines. Release invoices only after the engagement's contractual earning conditions and supporting recovery evidence are satisfied.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze and identify the affected batch
   - Stop the failing adapter and assign an incident owner.
   - Preserve source files, event identifiers, software version, and the last verified external milestone for every affected claim.
2. Continue essential work through the manual queue
   - Route time-sensitive document requests to the packet contractor.
   - Use only verified recipient details and record each manual action with a unique identifier to prevent duplicate replay.
3. Repair and replay a bounded test sample
   - Validate the corrected adapter against retained source examples.
   - Compare expected transitions with actual results, including duplicate, late, conflicting, and malformed events before restoring batch processing.
4. Reconcile before resuming normal throughput
   - Match ledger events, official acknowledgments, and customer messages.
   - Record corrected costs and reviewer minutes, resolve discrepancies, and expand the resumed batch only after the sample reconciles.

## 4. Compound trusted distribution without outrunning review capacity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build programmatic education around questions owners actually need answered: how to locate an official treasury portal, document a corporate name change, or distinguish an issuer-held warrant from escheated property. Generate page drafts from a jurisdiction rules table, then require an editor to verify citations, dates, direct-claim links, and state-specific instructions. Do not publish pages naming apparent owners or suggesting that an unverified amount belongs to them. A six-page pilot at $150 production cost per page costs $900. If it produces 30 qualified inquiries and three paid engagements, acquisition cost is $300 per engagement before ongoing maintenance. At the illustrative $750 fee, that leaves $450 before fulfillment and other expenses, not $450 profit. Attribute results through first-party landing-page parameters and engagement identifiers without embedding sensitive property details in URLs. Improve pages using aggregated missing-document patterns from completed cases, stripping identifiers before editorial use. This creates a useful loop: real operational friction improves education, better education attracts prepared owners, and prepared owners reduce packet revisions. Retire pages whose rules cannot be maintained. Traffic growth matters only when resulting engagements fit the evidence standards, review capacity, and fee economics of the operation.

Use professional referrals and narrow corporate outreach before broad automated messaging. Accountants, business attorneys, and corporate administrators often understand historical names and authorized signatories better than a scraped contact list. Give partners a generic evidence checklist and official direct-claim links; share a specific property's details only with appropriate authorization. An illustrative campaign researching 100 corporate contacts at $3 each costs $300. Eight substantive replies, four qualified engagements, and two eventual paid recoveries yield $150 acquisition cost per paid engagement, before fulfillment. Treat those conversions as a test hypothesis rather than a forecast. For commercial email, implement 15 U.S.C. 7704 requirements including accurate headers, nondeceptive subjects, required identification and address information, and a functioning opt-out that is honored within the statutory period. Do not disguise a private service as a treasury notice. Suppress declined recipients across every campaign, and retain only the minimal suppression data necessary to honor that choice. A satisfied owner may voluntarily share a generic resource link, but their recovery amount, documents, and testimonial require separate publication permission. Avoid public leaderboards, exposed claim identifiers, and referral incentives that conflict with applicable professional or finder rules.

Connect distribution to capacity through a weekly cohort dashboard. Track source records, reviewed matches, qualified owners, signed eligible agreements, accepted submissions, paid engagements, recognized fees, and attributable expense. Separate these stages by acquisition month because a 3–12 month first-deal interval makes same-month conversion ratios misleading. At Base, 340 paid engagements annually average 28.33 monthly, while Scaled averages 50; these are throughput requirements, not treasury processing promises. If an illustrative 40% of qualified engagements eventually pay, Base requires 850 qualified engagements annually, approximately 70.83 monthly. If only 20% pay, that requirement doubles to 1,700 annually. Use observed aging and conversion before buying more traffic. Set work-in-progress limits from reviewer minutes: 120 available packet-review minutes daily at twelve minutes per packet support ten reviews, not unlimited submissions. When identity exceptions or missing-document requests consume that reserve, slow acquisition instead of allowing unsafe automatic release. Reinvest only within the canonical expense envelope and keep cash runway separate from recognized profit. The distribution flywheel becomes scalable when each completed cohort improves source selection, document readiness, and trust while preserving the owner's direct-claim choice. Automate preparation aggressively; expand consequential throughput only as fast as evidence quality and staffed review permit.
