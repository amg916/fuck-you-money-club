## 1. Classify the asset before choosing the state contract  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin every file with asset classification, not a nationwide finder agreement. Record the property identifier, reporting holder, custodial state, apparent owner, owner address history, asset type, and actual reporting or delivery dates available from the custodian. A corporate uncashed warrant still held by an issuing agency may follow that agency's reissuance procedure rather than the state treasury's finder statute. An estate claim requires authority from an executor, administrator, or qualifying successor; a dissolved company's claim requires a supported chain of corporate authority. California Code of Civil Procedure section 1582 addresses recovery agreements, including a restricted contracting interval and a ten-percent compensation ceiling. New York Abandoned Property Law section 1416 imposes its own agreement requirements and fifteen-percent ceiling. Neither number is a national default. Determine the governing provision, its current text, the relevant property category, and the event that starts each waiting period before presenting terms. If a required date cannot be established, move the record into a research queue and do not seek a signature. A promising balance cannot repair an agreement executed during a prohibited interval. Store the source document and reviewer decision alongside the eligibility calculation so another operator can reproduce the conclusion.

Build a versioned jurisdiction register with separate fields for finder registration, occupational licensing, bonds, required disclosures, permitted compensation, expense treatment, cancellation rights, and authorization format. Florida Statutes section 717.135 supplies recovery-agreement requirements, while section 717.1400 addresses registration of specified professional categories participating in claims representation. Read those provisions together with the Department of Financial Services' current instructions; a business filing alone does not establish eligibility to represent claimants. Investigative work can introduce another boundary: California Business and Professions Code section 7539 defines private investigation activities, subject to statutory exceptions. Determine whether the actual locating and investigative services require a licensed provider before selling them. Ordinary document collection also differs from representing an estate in a contested proceeding or advising a creditor about litigation strategy. Route disputed heirship, adverse ownership claims, and contested court appearances to appropriately authorized counsel. Use a separate engagement and identify who pays for that work. Review the register before entering a new state, changing a contract, or expanding a service. Archive the effective date and official source rather than relying on an aggregator's summary. Disable affected templates when a rule changes; training alone will not prevent staff from reusing obsolete documents.

Identity evidence should prove entitlement without becoming a reusable identity dossier. Collect the claimant's relationship to the named owner first, then request only the documents required for that relationship and custodian. An old address match can support research but cannot establish current signing authority. A corporate file should connect the reported name to the present entity through incorporation records, merger documents, assumed-name filings, and an authorized signer. Use a restricted Microsoft SharePoint library or equivalent controlled repository for sensitive records, with multifactor authentication, role-based access, and an access log. A CRM should hold document status and links rather than full identity scans. California Civil Code section 1798.81.5 establishes reasonable-security duties for covered personal information; section 1798.82 addresses breach notification when its conditions apply. Federal Trade Commission Act section 5 also matters when privacy promises or security representations are deceptive or unfair. Other privacy regimes depend on business thresholds, data categories, and location, so assess applicability rather than claiming universal coverage. Establish retention periods by document class, required records, disputes, and litigation holds. Give contractors only their assigned files, prohibit local downloads where practical, and revoke access when assignments end. Preserve decision evidence without keeping unnecessary identity material indefinitely.

## 2. Price the service and make every contract trigger observable  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The contract should name the legal provider, claimant, custodian, identified property, and precise service scope. Describe record research, document organization, status tracking, and submission assistance only to the extent the provider may perform them. State prominently: 'You may claim this property directly from the government custodian without hiring or paying our service. Our fee purchases optional assistance, not access to a government benefit.' Use any additional state-required wording without dilution. Define the fee base as the specified property actually recovered, subject to the applicable statute, rather than every future asset discovered under the owner's name. Define when a fee is earned and when payment becomes due; a portal status change is not the same event as cleared proceeds. Prefer payment to the owner followed by a transparent invoice, unless the custodian authorizes another lawful mechanism. Do not add processing charges that defeat a compensation ceiling. Include required cancellation mechanics and a plain description of what happens when the custodian denies a claim. A narrow authorization should identify permitted communications and submissions. It should not silently authorize endorsing checks, changing payment destinations, settling disputes, or transferring ownership. Have the signer receive the completed agreement and every incorporated fee schedule.

Treat anti-circumvention language as attribution protection, not ownership of the claimant's relationship with the state. Identify the property and documented work that could support an earned fee under applicable law. A direct filing, required cancellation right, or unrelated recovery must not become an automatic penalty. Avoid perpetual exclusivity, multiples of the expected fee, and clauses demanding compensation before a legally permitted trigger. A liability limitation should address ordinary service errors with a reasonable negotiated allocation while preserving nonwaivable rights and identifying exclusions appropriate to fraud, intentional misconduct, and sensitive-data incidents. State which forum and governing law apply, but do not assume a chosen state displaces mandatory protections elsewhere. Explain mediation, court access, arbitration if used, cost allocation, and any small-claims carveout in readable terms. For data ownership, distinguish the owner's documents and claim evidence from the operator's preexisting templates and internal matching methods. Give the owner usable copies of their file and define retention and deletion responsibilities. Prohibit contractors from reselling lead lists or using identity evidence for unrelated work. Public availability of a property listing does not create permission to commercialize every document subsequently collected. Have local counsel review the actual template and service model, not merely a generic engagement letter.

Keep service economics separate from recovered principal in every proposal and ledger. For a simple planning model, assume a lawful ten-percent fee and an average completed recovery of $10,000. Each paid completion generates $1,000 of recognized service revenue, not $10,000. Sixty completions produce the Conservative $60,000 revenue case; 255 produce Base revenue of $255,000; 450 produce Scaled revenue of $450,000. Their underlying client recoveries are $600,000, $2,550,000, and $4,500,000 respectively. Those recovery totals are illustrative throughput assumptions, not extra income. The canonical owner-profit cases remain $15,000 at 25%, $95,625 at 37.5%, and $225,000 at 50%, implying total annual costs of $45,000, $159,375, and $225,000. Conservative and Base staffing is one full-time operator plus two contractors; Scaled uses two full-time staff plus three contractors. Do not assume every jurisdiction permits this example fee or that every signed file pays. A $1,200 recovery produces only $120 at ten percent; two hours costing $45 each plus $20 of verification expense leave $10 before overhead. Offer the direct-claim route instead of absorbing structurally uneconomic work. Track earned, invoiced, collected, disputed, and written-off fees separately. Treasury processing delays affect working capital even when the expected fee remains collectible.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the fee by reducing ambiguity before submission**
> 
> Experienced recovery operators preserve evidence of what was authorized, what was recovered, and why the resulting invoice is correct.
> - Attach a property-specific schedule to each agreement; separate identical owner names across different custodians.
> - Capture the relevant reporting and delivery dates before spending on identity research; an ineligible contracting interval can eliminate the near-term opportunity.
> - Use a corporate authority packet covering name changes, mergers, and signer delegation rather than repeatedly requesting unrelated identity documents.
> - Reconcile each invoice to cleared recovery evidence and the approved fee formula; investigate partial payments before issuing a full-fee demand.

## 3. Use outreach that survives owner skepticism and channel rules  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate the legality of finding a record from permission to contact a person through a particular channel. A public treasury listing does not supply consent for automated marketing texts. The Telephone Consumer Protection Act, 47 U.S.C. section 227, and 47 CFR section 64.1200 impose requirements that depend on technology, destination, content, consent, and applicable exemptions. Document those facts before using a dialer, prerecorded voice, or text platform. Screen applicable telephone campaigns against national and company-specific do-not-call requirements, observe permitted calling hours, and evaluate state restrictions separately. A manually dialed call does not erase other telemarketing duties. Commercial email must satisfy CAN-SPAM requirements under 15 U.S.C. section 7704, including truthful routing and subject information, required identification, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out mechanism. Honor opt-outs within the statutory period and prevent later lead imports from restoring suppressed recipients. Use a central suppression table across staff, contractors, and vendors. Before recording discovery calls, assess applicable recording-consent law and obtain the required permission; notes are a practical alternative when recording is unnecessary. Vendor settings and a purchased contact list are inputs, not evidence that a campaign is permitted. Preserve the screening result and script version with each outreach batch.

Use this first-contact letter verbatim after adding the provider's actual identity, postal address, and official custodian address in the letterhead: 'We are a private recovery service, not a government agency. A public unclaimed-property record may relate to you or your business; a name match alone does not establish ownership. You can search and claim directly through the government custodian without paying us. If you prefer optional assistance, we can explain our services and any permitted fee before you decide. Please do not send identity documents, bank information, or your Social Security number in response to this letter. You may independently locate the custodian's official website and contact details. To stop future contact from us, reply with stop or use the contact method printed above.' For email, add the functioning unsubscribe mechanism and required commercial identification; the script is content, not a replacement for channel controls. Do not print a full property value, former home address, or identity clue on the envelope. Avoid treasury-style seals, official-looking case numbers, fabricated deadlines, and statements that funds will disappear unless the recipient hires you. A clean letter may convert fewer impulsive replies while producing better-informed clients and fewer disputed signatures.

After the recipient agrees to discuss the service, use this discovery opening: 'Before we discuss documents, are you the named owner or someone authorized to act for that owner? Please describe your relationship without giving account numbers or identity-document details. We will explain the evidence the custodian requires and provide a secure submission method if you choose our service. You can also claim directly without us. Would you like the official direct-claim instructions first?' For a corporation, continue: 'What is the current legal entity name, and who can authorize this engagement? Has the reported business merged, dissolved, or changed names?' For fees, say: 'Our written agreement identifies the property, the permitted fee calculation, and when payment becomes due. We will not ask you to sign until those terms and your authority are clear.' If the person declines, say: 'Understood. We will record your request and stop further marketing contact through the channels you identify.' Do not turn that exchange into another sales pitch. Log dispositions without speculative comments about age, vulnerability, or wealth. Measure verified-owner conversations, signed eligible files, completed recoveries, and paid fees separately. High response rates are meaningless when identity mismatch or unsupported authority prevents a valid submission.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Qualify the record and selected channel
   - Classify the asset, confirm the custodian, and evaluate the proposed contact method before adding the lead to outreach.
   - Save the source record, jurisdiction review, technology assessment, and applicable suppression or permission evidence.
2. Send the owner-first introduction
   - Use the approved channel-specific script with actual provider identity and the free direct-claim route.
   - Keep sensitive identifiers out of the message and preserve the exact script version associated with the communication.
3. Establish authority and release the correct agreement
   - Confirm the claimant relationship, applicable contracting interval, provider eligibility, and permitted compensation.
   - Use the property-specific schedule and obtain only the authorization required for the services actually being provided.
4. Reconcile recovery, fees, and record retention
   - Match the custodian outcome to recovery evidence and the contractual fee trigger before invoicing.
   - Resolve partial recoveries, synchronize contact restrictions, and apply the document retention schedule after closure.

## 4. Keep bankruptcy claim purchases on a separate underwriting rail  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The mapped play chapter11_trade_claim_convenience_class_rollup belongs to TRACK_1 and requires a different legal and economic model from treasury recovery. It purchases documented small trade claims below risk-adjusted expected plan distributions. The seller transfers a claim rather than hiring a finder to recover state-held property. Use PACER and the court-designated claims agent to examine the petition, Schedule E/F, claims register, bar-date orders, disclosure statement, proposed or confirmed plan, and claim-objection docket. Schedule E/F is a research source, not conclusive evidence of an allowed claim or its current owner. Under 11 U.S.C. section 1111(a), a scheduled claim may be deemed filed, subject to the exception for claims scheduled as disputed, contingent, or unliquidated; Bankruptcy Rule 3003 supplies additional filing requirements in Chapter 11. Match invoices, delivery evidence, credits, debtor entities, and proof-of-claim records before pricing. Section 502 governs allowance and objections, while section 1122(b) permits qualifying administrative-convenience classification. It does not promise a premium distribution to every small claim purchaser. Read the actual class definition, election deadline, reduction mechanism, related-holder language, and treatment of acquired claims. Never manufacture eligibility by assuming that splitting one invoice or aggregating multiple sellers changes the plan's enforceable classification rules.

Consider an illustrative portfolio with $100,000 face value purchased for $12,000. Assume verified plan treatment would distribute 30% of face on allowed claims, but underwriting supports only an 80% probability of that modeled allowance and payment outcome. Expected receipts are $100,000 times 30% times 80%, or $24,000. Subtract $12,000 purchase consideration and $4,000 of diligence, transfer, and servicing costs to obtain an $8,000 expected contribution before overhead, taxes, and the cost of tying up capital. This arithmetic is an expectation, not an observed collection or an assumption that every claim fails independently. Correlated debtor objections can affect the entire portfolio. If supported general-unsecured treatment instead yields 10% of face at the same 80% probability, expected receipts fall to $8,000 and contribution becomes negative $8,000 at the original purchase price. Stop affected purchases, enforce documented seller recourse where available, and price only supported general-unsecured recoveries. For a $2,000 target contribution under that fallback and unchanged $4,000 costs, maximum purchase consideration is $2,000. If sellers reject that price, pass. Do not import this portfolio's face value, proceeds, or trading spread into the volume's canonical service-fee scorecard.

Use a purchase agreement that identifies the debtor, case number, claim number when available, face amount, supporting documents, purchase price, and exactly what rights transfer. Obtain representations addressing ownership, prior assignments, payments, credits, disputes, and liens, with document delivery and further-assurance obligations. Separate recourse for a seller's breached representation from ordinary debtor insolvency risk; broad language is worthless when the seller cannot perform. Bankruptcy Rule 3001(e) provides different procedures depending on transfer timing and whether the transfer is for security. For a qualifying transfer other than for security after a proof of claim has been filed, the rule provides notice and a twenty-one-day transferor objection period. Follow the applicable procedure and court orders rather than treating a private signature as the entire transfer process. Track substitution, objections, distribution instructions, and any plan-specific election independently. Counsel should handle contested transfer or allowance proceedings and assess specialized rights, sanctions, tax, and solicitation issues when relevant. Keep purchase cash, acquired-claim accounting, and seller payments in a distinct ledger from service fees and owner recoveries. At each review, compare actual collections and remaining supported distributions with acquisition cost and unresolved objections. An attractive spreadsheet cannot override adverse docket evidence.
