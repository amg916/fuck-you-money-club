## 1. Mine Twelve Evidence-Rich Plays Instead of Buying Name Lists  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one state and one ownership pattern: operating companies with stale addresses, dissolved entities with documented successors, or estates with appointed representatives. Search the official treasury portal first; use MissingMoney only as a participating-state discovery layer, then return to the custodian for instructions. Public visibility does not establish permission for bulk extraction. Follow portal terms, rate limits, and published download procedures; where permitted, request a dated public-record extract rather than repeatedly querying individual names. Capture the property identifier, holder, owner spelling, reported address, asset category, amount visibility, and retrieval date. Preserve the original record alongside normalized fields. A matching engine can strip punctuation and expand business suffixes, but it must never turn a similar name into a verified claimant. Combine treasury evidence with Secretary of State filings, SEC EDGAR merger exhibits, county probate dockets, and corporate contact pages. Rank leads by recoverable fee opportunity and document accessibility, not advertised property totals. Three apparently valuable entries can represent the same ownership problem or entirely different people. The best first account usually has an active controller, a traceable historical address, and one custodian with a clear corporate claim checklist. That combination shortens evidence collection without relying on privileged access.

Use the twelve plays in the matrix as distinct sourcing hypotheses, not twelve interchangeable datasets. Corporate warrants, vendor overpayments, payroll balances, and utility deposits begin with different holders and require different proof. A city check register can reveal an uncashed warrant before treasury transfer, but the city may retain the obligation or apply a separate stale-check procedure. Determine custody before describing it as escheated property. Merger remnants, closed branches, and dissolved companies reward historical entity research; their failure mode is assuming that a familiar trade name proves succession. Estates, trust distributions, and insurance proceeds reward representative relationships, but a public docket does not authorize disclosure of sensitive beneficiary information. Securities and escrow balances introduce valuation and entitlement questions that can overwhelm a first-time operator. Prioritize cash claims with accessible records before pursuing those complications. For each ordinary recovery lane, calculate the prospective fee using a rate actually permitted for that property, jurisdiction, and agreement date. A $40,000 cash balance at an allowable 10% produces $4,000 of potential service revenue, not $40,000 of revenue. If the permitted rate is 5%, the same evidence burden must fit inside $2,000. Recalculate acquisition spend before contacting the owner.

Treat hospital_lien_medical_receivables_arbitrage as the required TRACK_1 adjacency, not a treasury-claim shortcut. Its operating name is Out-of-Network Medical Claim & ERISA Appeal Contingency Recovery. Partner with surgical centers, air/ground ambulance operators, and specialty clinics to review denied or underpaid commercial claims, but segment each service by payer, plan, facility status, and dispute eligibility. Federal No Surprises Act payment disputes are not a universal route for every out-of-network bill; ground ambulance services generally sit outside its federal balance-billing framework. For eligible disputes, track the 30-business-day open-negotiation period and ordinarily the four-business-day IDR initiation window that follows, while checking applicable extensions and current agency instructions. Automate evidence assembly and deadline reminders, not unsupported eligibility judgments. An illustrative $100,000 incremental payer recovery at a contractually permitted 25% contingency generates $25,000 in service fees; a 20–35% range yields $20,000–$35,000 before case costs. Keep this model separate from treasury finder caps and the canonical volume forecast. Execute BAAs where required, encrypt EDI transfers, and restrict access to minimum-necessary claim data. If IDR timing has lapsed, evaluate a timely ERISA administrative appeal independently; group-health adverse benefit determinations generally allow at least 180 days to appeal. An appeal is not an automatic substitute for a payment dispute.

## 2. Qualify the Claim, Representative, and Permitted Fee Before Outreach  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a qualification scorecard that separates ranking from mandatory eligibility. Award up to 30 points for ownership evidence, 20 for document accessibility, 20 for fee contribution, 15 for representative reachability, and 15 for timing clarity. An exact entity name plus a historical address can justify research, but the custodian still determines acceptable ownership proof. Require 75 points before personalized outbound and 85 before spending on complex records; these are internal starting thresholds, not measured industry benchmarks. Independently stop any case with disputed authority, an ineligible agreement date, a prohibited fee, or an unresolved identity collision. A high score cannot override those conditions. Create a state rules sheet recording finder registration, licensing, permissible contract timing, fee basis, required wording, cancellation provisions where applicable, and direct-claim disclosures. California Code of Civil Procedure section 1582, for example, makes certain agreements during the reporting-to-post-delivery interval invalid and limits finder compensation. Read the actual statutory sequence rather than assuming that an old-looking listing is immediately contractable. Record the source URL, review date, and reviewer beside each rule. If the rule depends on delivery timing that the public record omits, ask the custodian before soliciting a paid engagement.

Translate the score into a spending ceiling with explicit uncertainty. Suppose a documented $50,000 cash claim permits a 10% fee, yielding $5,000 if collected. Assume, for illustration, a 60% probability of collection after signing, $650 of expected processing labor, $150 of expected document costs, and $300 of allocated acquisition work. Expected contribution is $3,000 minus $1,100, or $1,900, before shared overhead and tax treatment. These costs must represent probability-weighted spend across successful and failed cases; using only successful-case costs overstates the result. A $4,000 claim under identical assumptions produces $240 of expected fees and loses $860. Either redesign that workflow around materially lower costs or send the owner to direct claiming. Do not conceal several small claims inside a large headline balance. Track each property separately and aggregate only where ownership, contract coverage, and custodian procedures allow it. For medical work, subtract IDR administrative and entity fees, specialist review, and unrecovered case costs under the engagement's allocation terms. A promising payer balance can still be uneconomic after eligibility screening. Apply a maximum research budget per lead and require a specific missing fact before extending it.

Design intake so that trust improves while sensitive-data exposure falls. Keep discovery records in a permissioned CRM such as HubSpot or Airtable, but store identity documents in a separately controlled repository with access logs and retention settings. Those product names do not establish suitability for health information; medical work requires the appropriate service configuration, contractual protections, and BAAs where applicable. Avoid collecting Social Security numbers during prospecting. Send a secure upload request only after the representative understands the claim, the fee, and the purpose of each document. Confirm corporate authority through custodian requirements, not merely a business email domain. For estates, distinguish the person discussing the claim from the person authorized to sign. Preserve name changes, address history, and supporting records as an evidence chain rather than silently editing the original identity. Default to payment from the custodian directly to the owner, followed by a service invoice under the agreement. Do not redirect recoveries into the operator's account simply to improve collection speed. Document fee collection rights, permitted payment mechanics, and dispute handling in advance. If a claimant will not use secure intake, offer custodian-direct document submission instead of accepting identity images through casual messaging.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before a Paid Mandate**
> 
> Advance only when the file supports the claim, the agreement, the economics, and the document-handling method.
> - Verify owner identity and signing authority against independent records and the custodian's actual evidence requirements.
> - Verify finder registration, agreement timing, fee limits, required terms, and the owner's disclosed direct-claim option for the specific jurisdiction.
> - Verify positive expected contribution using permitted fees, probability-weighted labor, acquisition costs, and a capped exception budget.
> - Verify secure intake, restricted access, retention rules, and required medical BAAs before receiving sensitive documents or EDI feeds.

## 3. Convert Evidence into Conversations with a Restrained Multi-Channel System  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run outbound as a verification offer, not a windfall announcement. On day one, send a short business email or sealed letter identifying the operator, the official custodian, the historical entity or address match, and the owner's ability to claim directly without hiring a service. Avoid displaying full property details on postcards or voicemail. State the service plainly: ownership research, document coordination, submission support, and status tracking where permitted. On day four, place one manual call to an independently sourced business number; ask who handles legacy receivables rather than requesting identity documents. On day ten, send an evidence-led follow-up with an official portal link and a redacted example of the document checklist. On day twenty-one, close the sequence with a clear opt-out and pause further prospecting unless the recipient reengages. Honor rejection immediately across every channel. Apply CAN-SPAM requirements to commercial email, including accurate headers, a postal address, and a functioning opt-out. Assess TCPA and applicable state restrictions before calling or texting; do not assume a public number authorizes automated outreach. Treat this cadence as a testable operating hypothesis, not permission to contact every listed owner repeatedly. Track replies by source and representative role.

Build inbound around questions that reveal document friction: 'claiming property after a merger,' 'old company address on a treasury record,' and 'estate claim representative documents.' Publish jurisdiction-specific checklists that link to the official agency and distinguish free owner claiming from paid coordination. Avoid pages that imitate treasury branding or imply government affiliation. A useful page names the ownership problem, lists the records usually requested, explains what varies by custodian, and offers a short eligibility conversation without requesting sensitive identifiers. Use Search Console to identify relevant queries and a simple CRM source field to connect inquiries with signed and collected cases. Public search demand is a sourcing signal, not evidence that any particular visitor owns property. Develop referral relationships with bookkeepers, corporate secretaries, probate practitioners, and business wind-down specialists who encounter missing-record problems. Give each partner a one-page referral checklist and a secure introduction process. Disclose and verify any referral compensation before offering it, particularly where professional conduct rules or finder-fee restrictions apply. Start with unpaid reciprocal education rather than assuming a referral commission is permissible. One controller introduction covering five related entities can produce better-qualified work than hundreds of anonymous impressions.

Handle objections by reducing information asymmetry rather than increasing pressure. When an owner asks whether this is a scam, provide the official portal, business identity, written scope, and an independently verifiable contact route; never ask them to trust a screenshot alone. When they ask why they should pay, explain the specific historical records and coordination tasks involved, then acknowledge that uncomplicated claims may be better handled directly. When they say the money is theirs, agree: the engagement purchases administrative work, not ownership of their asset. When a controller already has a recovery vendor, ask whether an uncovered entity or jurisdiction exists without requesting another provider's confidential files. Quote fees only after checking the property and contract rules. Avoid artificial deadlines, guaranteed payout dates, or claims that the owner will lose money merely by declining. End the conversation with one concrete next step: confirm the representative, review a redacted checklist, or receive the direct-claim link. Record objection categories so the weekly review can distinguish distrust, weak economics, unavailable documents, and timing restrictions. Each category demands a different correction; rewriting subject lines cannot fix an ineligible agreement or an inaccessible ownership archive.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create an evidence-backed weekly cohort
   - Research fifty raw records within one jurisdiction and ownership pattern.
   - Deduplicate property identifiers, preserve source dates, and score ownership, documents, contribution, reachability, and timing.
2. Release only eligible prospects
   - Review every selected record against the jurisdiction rules sheet.
   - Confirm permitted solicitation and agreement timing; prepare a direct-claim link and a specific evidence-based reason to contact the owner.
3. Run the four-touch acquisition sequence
   - Use the day-one, day-four, day-ten, and day-twenty-one cadence.
   - Record replies and objections, honor opt-outs across channels, and move interested representatives into secure qualification rather than open-ended follow-up.
4. Reconcile acquisition with collected outcomes
   - Review cohort progression and actual case costs every week.
   - Separate contacted owners, signed mandates, complete packets, accepted claims, owner payments, fee collections, and recognized service revenue.

## 4. Engineer the First Three-to-Ten Wins and Reconcile the Annual Cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Define a win as an owner-paid recovery with the operator's contractual fee collected; track signed mandates separately. For an initial planning cohort, start with 500 raw records, deduplicate and qualify 200, reach 80 verified decision-makers, hold 24 substantive conversations, sign 12 mandates, complete nine claim packets, and target six paid wins within the 3–12 month planning window. These are illustrative test assumptions, not observed conversion benchmarks or agency processing commitments. They imply 40% raw-to-qualified, 40% qualified-to-reached, 30% reached-to-conversation, 50% conversation-to-mandate, 75% mandate-to-complete, and approximately 67% complete-to-paid conversion. Overall raw-to-paid conversion is 1.2%. At that assumed yield, three wins require approximately 250 raw records and ten require approximately 834. Do not extrapolate until a matured cohort supports the rates. A recent submission awaiting review is different from a rejected claim; report aged cohorts and unresolved cases explicitly. Separate state, property category, referral source, and representative type so one unusually easy corporate cluster does not inflate every channel forecast. If conversations are strong but packet completion is weak, improve evidence intake before adding leads. If packets are complete but payments lag, investigate custodian requests and realistic cash timing rather than intensifying outbound.

Reconcile the annual economics to recognized service fees, never to the owner's recovered principal. One illustrative Conservative bridge is 30 completed engagements averaging $2,000 in recognized fees: $60,000 revenue, $45,000 operating costs, and $15,000 owner net profit at 25%, staffed by 1 FT + 2 C. The Base bridge is 51 engagements averaging $5,000: $255,000 revenue, $159,375 costs, and $95,625 profit at 37.5%, also 1 FT + 2 C. Scaled is 75 engagements averaging $6,000: $450,000 revenue, $225,000 costs, and $225,000 profit at 50%, staffed by 2 FT + 3 C. These engagement counts explain the fixed scorecard; they are not additional performance promises. At a uniformly allowable 10% fee, the implied recovered principal would be $600,000, $2,550,000, and $4,500,000 respectively. Different lawful rates change principal requirements, not the canonical fee totals. Keep the medical adjacency outside this bridge unless a separately reconciled operating plan replaces the illustrative engagement mix. The first six wins at $2,000 each produce $12,000 of fees, not the Conservative annual target. Maintain separate dates for owner payment, operator collection, and accounting recognition under the adopted revenue policy.

Sequence execution around working capital and case maturity. In month one, establish the jurisdiction rules sheet, secure intake, a fifty-record research batch, and two professional referral conversations. During months two and three, run weekly batches, test the outreach sequence, and submit only complete authorized files. During months four through twelve, service custodian requests, invoice earned fees, and expand only the source cohorts with demonstrated contribution. The canonical startup capital range is $5,000–$35,000; its $1,500–$8,000 monthly opex range is a planning envelope, not a ceiling on all annual case-related costs. The Base and Scaled total cost budgets exceed that range when annualized, so explicitly bridge fixed overhead, contractor processing, acquisition work, and other variable costs rather than hiding the difference. A founder can handle owner relationships while contractors research records and assemble packets, with access limited by task. Add the second full-time role only when collected contribution and documented backlog support it. Automate record matching suggestions and claim-status reminders first; require human review for identity collisions, authority changes, and fee exceptions. Close each month by reconciling the CRM, custodian correspondence, invoices, and bank receipts. Expand the machine only when those four records tell the same story.
