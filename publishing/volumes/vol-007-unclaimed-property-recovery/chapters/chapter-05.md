## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator working business-name recoveries starts from the same premise: this is earned revenue that slipped through operational cracks, not windfall cash. GardenShop.Pro's Tennessee breakdown frames it as uncashed vendor checks, returned electronic transfers, unreturned security deposits and forgotten insurance claims that land with the state after an office move or personnel change, with vendor payments sitting one to three years and government or municipal credits escheating after a single year [bUxdjo_bs0g @ 00:01:02]. Как се прави's North Dakota run puts hard dormancy clocks on the most common business assets: mineral proceeds and royalties at three years, vendor payments and credit memos at two, utility refunds at one, which means a missed final utility deposit after a relocation hits the state twelve months later [7MUtjq4bwU0 @ 00:01:48]. The custody model is equally consistent across states. South Carolina holds escheated assets in perpetual custody with zero statute of limitations, so funds turned over twenty years ago are as claimable as funds turned over two years ago [KJU4iu_xIgw @ 00:01:37], and Colorado runs the identical no-expiration rule, with a five-year general dormancy and just one year for unclaimed payroll [2_XKdBsmJ5c @ 00:01:21]. The operator read is simple: the clock never runs out on the owner, so old entities, acquired subsidiaries and decade-old addresses are all live inventory worth sweeping.

The second point of full agreement: the state search itself costs nothing, but a single portal never covers the whole map. GardenShop.Pro's Missouri walkthrough is blunt that the treasurer never, ever charges a fee to search, claim or return property [fntI1mKZpK0 @ 00:04:04]. Как се прави's Virginia session pushes past the state line, pairing vamoneysearch.gov with missingmoney.com because an out-of-state incorporation or a national vendor base routinely parks money in other jurisdictions [RtDPQUDYTy0 @ 00:03:47]. Garden Shop Pro's corporation-name guide adds the jurisdictional split that catches most teams: federal agencies are exempt from state escheatment, so IRS refunds, bankruptcy-court distributions, closed-bank FDIC deposits and matured savings bonds will never show on missingmoney.com and need a separate federal track, starting with IRS Form 3911 for an undelivered corporate refund [7Fc0D2RuIJE @ 00:03:14]. Even inside one state the online index leaks. Rhode Island's findrimoney.gov only displays properties valued above one dollar, so micro-balances and certain restricted asset classes require a direct call to the Unclaimed Property Division at 401-462-7676, while bankruptcy dividends sit in the separate U.S. Bankruptcy Unclaimed Funds Locator for the District of Rhode Island [an41g6w5nlU @ 00:03:11]. Operators therefore treat state, multi-state, federal and phone-only channels as four distinct sweeps rather than one search.

Proof is the third consensus pillar, and every speaker stresses that business claims face a heavier burden than individual ones. Как се прави's Minnesota guide lays out a four-part package: federal tax ID verification through a W-9 or CP575, a letter of authorization on company letterhead signed by an officer such as the CEO or CFO, proof tying the business to the old address on the record through bank statements, leases or vendor contracts, and a government photo ID for the signer [NPH35-KUMjQ @ 00:04:20]. GardenShop.Pro's Connecticut breakdown names the same three tests, legal identity, corporate authority and asset nexus, and insists the nexus document show the business operated at the exact address tied to the funds [mTurQziolOk @ 00:03:02]. Georgia's version layers on a current Secretary of State annual registration to prove active status, calling the historical address record the main defense against corporate identity theft [WHTvjELqLw0 @ 00:04:09]. Structural history changes the stack. Under Delaware section 278, former officers can claim freely inside a three-year post-dissolution survival window; after that, section 279 requires a Court of Chancery petition to appoint a receiver, though the Kraft Murphy ruling held that residual assets still legally belong to the company [7Fc0D2RuIJE @ 00:04:16].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Execution starts with the field trap that multiple Как се прави walkthroughs flag as the number-one reason business searches come back empty. On North Dakota's unclaimedproperty.nd.gov, the company name goes in the last-name field and the first-name field stays completely blank; typing even a space or an initial forces the algorithm to filter out commercial entities, returning zero matches while the state holds thousands of dollars [7MUtjq4bwU0 @ 00:02:53]. Once the field is right, the variant stack does the heavy lifting. The Minnesota guide has operators search DBAs, common misspellings, versions without Inc. or LLC, former names of absorbed predecessors, and every name containing an ampersand or hyphen both with and without the punctuation, repeating the sweep a couple of times a year as new property lands [NPH35-KUMjQ @ 00:03:36]. Colorado's breakdown explains why this matters: financial institutions truncate long names and drop designators before remitting, so Acme Manufacturing Incorporated must also be run as Acme, Acme MFG and Acme Manufacturing [2_XKdBsmJ5c @ 00:03:25]. GardenShop.Pro's Missouri tip closes the loop by stripping leading articles, searching Smith Corporation instead of The Smith Corporation, on the business tab of showmemoney.com [fntI1mKZpK0 @ 00:03:17]. City, zip code and state-issued property ID filters then narrow bloated result pools down to the branches that actually match.

The filing mechanics are technical, and rejections come from format errors as often as from missing proof. Colorado's portal requires the unique numeric claim ID generated at initiation, accepts only PDF, TIFF, PNG or JPEG, caps each file at exactly 10 megabytes, refuses password-protected or encrypted files, and takes five documents per upload session, so a large merger packet goes up in sequential batches under that same claim ID [2_XKdBsmJ5c @ 00:04:44]. Minnesota adds file names under 100 characters using only letters, numbers, dashes or underscores, while original stock certificates and safe deposit box contents still have to be mailed physically [NPH35-KUMjQ @ 00:05:34]. Payout rules vary just as sharply. Georgia quotes roughly 30 to 90 days of standard processing once documents are uploaded or mailed, and pays approved monetary claims exclusively by physical paper check to the registered address, with direct deposit and electronic transfers strictly prohibited [WHTvjELqLw0 @ 00:04:39]. That check carries its own trap: South Carolina refund checks void 90 days from the date of issue, which bounces the funds back into the central database and forces a full re-filing of the manual claim form and every verification document from scratch, so treasury teams are told to cash the disbursement immediately on receipt [KJU4iu_xIgw @ 00:05:27].

David Church of Surplus Institute works the same databases from the opposite chair, as a locator chasing business balances for a fee. His workflow: get registered in a locator-friendly state, download its unclaimed list, drop the spreadsheet into a paid ChatGPT account and prompt it to separate the highest-paid business claims, for example the top 40 [EFuzXe23Q7I @ 00:09:41]. He then has AI trace corporate ownership and goes after someone in the accounting department or the president with a plain pitch: hey, I found some unclaimed property, you have quite a bit, I do this, I would love to work with you, sometimes sent as a letter. His math is pure volume: line up 15 to 20 corporations, local banks included, and expect to collect on two or three [EFuzXe23Q7I @ 00:15:44]. State fee rules bound that model. Missouri voids any locator agreement signed in the first 12 months of state custody, then caps fees at 10% in year two, 15% in year three and 20% after 36 months [fntI1mKZpK0 @ 00:05:23]. Georgia goes further at the search stage itself, forcing users to electronically certify a valid legal or equitable interest under OCGA 44-12-215, which restricts speculative third-party queries aimed at corporate records [WHTvjELqLw0 @ 00:03:19].

Practitioners also split on how to fire the first query. One camp starts tight. Как се прави's North Dakota walkthrough says the engine uses exact string matching first, so operators should run the full legal name including LLC or Incorporated, then a truncated root like Acme, then Acme Ind, then vendor misspellings, former trade names, and acquired entities [7MUtjq4bwU0 @ 00:05:58]. The other camp strips names down from the start. Как се прави's Virginia company guide tells searchers to drop LLC, Inc., or Corp. on the first pass to cast a better net. Georgia's version goes further. In practice the stripped-down and exact passes end up run back to back, because each catches records the other misses.

Cadence and scale are the third fault line. The corporate-explainer camp treats searching as internal hygiene on a timer. ## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most common self-inflicted miss is the first name box. Как се прави's North Dakota walkthrough warns that typing even a space or an initial there makes the search algorithm filter out commercial entities entirely. The result is zero matches even when the state is holding thousands for the company [7MUtjq4bwU0 @ 00:02:53]. The Minnesota guide calls it the absolute wrong way to search, because it forces the database to treat the query like a human being's name [NPH35-KUMjQ @ 00:03:06]. The Oklahoma guide says that if operators take away one thing, it should be that the entire legal business name goes in the last name field with the first name left empty [M3F5IgHI7Ow @ 00:02:51]. The second trap is trusting one database to hold everything. Garden Shop Pro's corporate guide stresses that federal agencies are exempt from state escheatment, so money owed by the IRS or a federal bankruptcy court will never appear on the multi-state portal [7Fc0D2RuIJE @ 00:03:14]. That side runs on separate tools: IRS Form 3911 for an undelivered corporate tax refund, the bankruptcy courts' unclaimed funds locator for a debtor that went under, FDIC checks for deposits from closed banks, and a separate lookup for matured corporate savings bonds. Skip that track and the federal checks never surface at all.

Paperwork is where found money stalls. Как се прави's Colorado walkthrough says a standard cash claim under $5,000 needs an online form, a W-9, a photo ID, and proof of authority, and takes 90 to 150 days. Merged or dissolved entities need certified articles, a certificate of good standing, and a 6 to 12-month audit of the ownership chain [2_XKdBsmJ5c @ 00:03:58]. Their upload rules punish sloppiness: PDF, TIFF, PNG, or JPEG only, 10 megabytes per file, and five documents per session. Any password or encryption gets the file rejected. Georgia's guide shows the acquisition trap with a forgotten $10,000 utility deposit sitting under a dissolved target. The buyer cannot claim it with its current FEIN. It takes FEINs for both the target and the surviving entity, plus the certificate of merger [WHTvjELqLw0 @ 00:05:23]. Garden Shop Pro flags the Delaware clock. After the 3-year survival window under section 278 closes, a dissolved corporation needs a Court of Chancery receiver under section 279 to sign for and collect the funds [7Fc0D2RuIJE @ 00:04:16]. GardenShop.Pro's Tennessee guide adds that payouts for dissolved companies split by historical equity. A 60% owner collects 60% only if the operating agreements and stock ledgers survived [bUxdjo_bs0g @ 00:03:32].

Even approved claims die on the back end. Как се прави's South Carolina guide warns that state refund checks carry a strict 90-day validity window. Miss it and the check voids, the funds bounce back into the central database, and the team must refile the entire manual claim and verification package from scratch [KJU4iu_xIgw @ 00:05:27]. GardenShop.Pro's Connecticut breakdown gives automated CT Big Match warrants for single-owner property under $2,500 just 180 days before they go void [mTurQziolOk @ 00:03:59]. On the locator side, David Church reports the economics are lumpy and leaky. Owners go around the locator once they learn the money exists, like a buyer who shops at one dealer and signs at another. One Florida woman sitting on 108,000 called the police on the letters until a detective confirmed the funds, and she collected with no locator in the middle [EFuzXe23Q7I @ 00:01:35]. Their own client had already been denied on part of a claim that never should have been denied. Their prospecting stack also breaks. ChatGPT cut them off for searching too much, YouTube pulled a business spreadsheet as personal information and they lost the appeal, and a live Sun Holdings lookup turned up only small amounts not worth chasing [EFuzXe23Q7I @ 00:20:12].
