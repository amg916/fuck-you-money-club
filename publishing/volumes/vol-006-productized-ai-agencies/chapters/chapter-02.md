## 1. Find the budget owner before designing the workflow  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The check normally comes from an operating company whose department already owns the cost being removed. A chief operating officer buys faster order handling; a revenue-operations director buys cleaner qualification; a customer-support leader buys a smaller exception queue. The enthusiastic employee testing prompts is a champion, not necessarily a purchaser. During discovery, identify the legal buyer, budget code, delegated signing limit, purchase-order requirement, acceptance owner, and accounts-payable contact. Ask what happened during the last comparable software-services purchase and request the actual vendor-onboarding checklist. An executive may approve $25,000 while procurement requires competitive quotes above $20,000; that mismatch changes the schedule, not the invoice wording. Never split invoices to evade purchasing controls. Frame value around a measured baseline: 2,000 monthly tickets multiplied by six minutes equals 200 staff hours. At $40 per loaded hour, the existing workload costs $8,000 monthly. Recovering half those hours creates $4,000 of potential capacity, not automatically $4,000 of cash savings. The buyer must specify whether capacity replaces overtime, avoids a planned hire, or improves service. Sell a bounded implementation against that operating decision, with a named person accountable for measuring the result after deployment.

Acute pain becomes payable when delay has a visible owner and a credible cost. A backlog that blocks invoicing can command funding before an attractive dashboard, because the controller sees aged receivables every week. Statutory obligations can sharpen urgency, but they do not require purchasing AI. For a covered California business, California Civil Code section 1798.100(d) creates concrete contract requirements when personal information is disclosed to specified recipients. For HIPAA-regulated deployments involving electronic protected health information, 45 CFR 164.308(b) and 164.504(e) make business-associate arrangements material to system design. Price the data inventory, access boundaries, logging, and documented processing instructions as deliverables when applicable. Do not claim a chatbot itself satisfies those duties. The security reviewer can stop access without controlling budget; the controller can stop payment without judging model quality. Interview both before committing a delivery date. Write a responsibility schedule covering client-provided accounts, sample records, reviewers, and test decisions. Consequential outputs should reach a qualified human before action. The practical sales question is therefore which costly workflow the buyer can authorize, which data the agency may process, and which measurable acceptance event releases funds under the buyer's actual purchasing procedure.

Keep the scorecard anchored to agency fees. One illustrative Conservative mix is four $15,000 implementations plus five $8,000 annual support packages: $60,000 + $40,000 = $100,000 revenue. With $80,000 total modeled costs, owner net profit is $20,000 at 20%, using 2 FT + 3 C. Base can comprise ten $25,000 implementations and ten retainers at $2,500 monthly for twelve months: $250,000 + $300,000 = $550,000. Costs of $385,000 leave $165,000 at 30.0%, again with 2 FT + 3 C. Scaled uses sixteen $25,000 implementations and twenty twelve-month $2,500 retainers: $400,000 + $600,000 = $1,000,000. Costs of $600,000 leave $400,000 at 40%, with 3 FT + 4 C. These are annual reconciliation examples, not a forecast that every new retainer starts in January. Contractor counts do not establish full-year utilization, and the Conservative case requires especially tight staffing economics. Client transaction volume, recovered labor capacity, and incremental merchant sales never enter agency revenue. Treat the $5,000–$60,000 startup-capital range and $2,500–$18,000 monthly operating range as planning inputs requiring a detailed cost bridge, not substitutes for total annual scenario expenses. Allow one to four months to win the first deal.

## 2. Translate three mapped plays into measurable paid work  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Scope authorized ingestion from public Telegram broadcasts, public OTC deal-room feeds, wholesale distress posts, and niche message streams. A public username does not establish permission to copy member lists or automate outreach. Use a permitted read-only listener where platform rules and access allow it; preserve message links, timestamps, source permissions, and short factual extracts. A sample $15,000 sprint can price connector setup at $5,000, event classification at $6,000, and reviewer routing at $4,000. Support at $1,000 monthly covers source-health checks and a defined exception allowance. If reviewers formerly inspected 6,000 posts monthly at two minutes each, the baseline is 200 hours. Reducing manual inspection to 1,500 posts saves 150 hours of review capacity, worth $6,000 at $40 per hour before verification and tool costs. Validate precision on a labeled sample rather than promising profitable trades. When access, privacy, or signal quality fails, restrict ingestion to passive public broadcast listeners and obtain prospects inbound through an official Telegram Bot API utility bot. Inspect visible citations in ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews across a fixed set of buyer prompts, recording date, locale, wording, and observed sources. ## 3. Choose the payment arrangement and prove each release event  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A payment rail moves money; a retainer, milestone hold, or statutory payment obligation determines when money is owed. Keep those concepts separate when comparing the eight arrangements below. The commercial chain is signed statement of work, authorized purchase order when required, proper invoice, payer approval, transfer initiation, bank availability, and residual dispute exposure. A screenshot proves almost none of that chain. For a new $25,000 implementation, an illustrative schedule collects $10,000 before kickoff, $10,000 after an accepted staging demonstration, and $5,000 after documented handoff. Match each invoice to its milestone, purchase order, legal entity, currency, and tax treatment. Record deposits as liabilities until the applicable revenue recognition criteria are satisfied; collection is not itself proof of earned revenue. The client should normally own production vendor accounts and pay its model and cloud providers directly. If the agency resells or administers usage, determine principal-versus-agent accounting and show the charging method explicitly. Do not net merchant receipts against agency fees through improvised custody. Use the buyer's established payable process and a processor or bank account belonging to the contracting agency. Reconcile invoice status to actual ledger entries, including refunds, processing charges, and any withheld reserves.

Wire transfers suit larger deposits when the buyer accepts bank fees and the account details have been independently verified. Domestic commercial wire rights commonly depend on applicable state enactments of UCC Article 4A and bank agreements; do not treat a bank's displayed balance as a universal statement of legal finality. ACH credits let the customer originate payment, while ACH debits require proper authorization and remain subject to applicable return rules. Do not apply consumer dispute assumptions to every business account or promise that every ACH entry is irrevocable after a fixed number of days. Stripe Invoicing or a comparable checkout can collect cards and supported bank payments while retaining invoices and receipts, but payout timing, reserves, and disputes depend on the account and method. Escrow separates funding from release; verify provider eligibility for the specific services transaction, fees, dispute procedure, and release instructions before describing funds as protected. A recurring retainer should define included volume, overage prices, renewal terms, cancellation notice, and the underlying payment method. Require independent callback verification whenever bank details change, using a previously established number rather than contact information supplied in the change request. Never route payment through an employee's personal account.

Statutory disbursement is a special payment context, not a shortcut to public money. For an eligible federal prime contract, the Prompt Payment Act, including 31 U.S.C. 3902, and applicable FAR provisions establish payment and interest rules. FAR 32.904 addresses due dates, subject to its conditions and exceptions; proper invoicing and acceptance records matter. A commercial agency serving a government contractor does not automatically inherit the government's payment clock. Factoring instead sells eligible receivables to a financier. On a $20,000 invoice, an illustrative 85% advance provides $17,000 immediately; a flat 3% fee costs $600, leaving $2,400 from the reserve after full collection, assuming no other charges. Total proceeds are $19,400, and recourse terms may return nonpayment risk to the agency. Check assignment restrictions and debtor notification before proceeding. Milestone holds leave money with the customer until specified evidence exists; cap the final holdback so the agency does not finance the entire deployment. An escrow balance is not operating cash, a factor advance is not extra revenue, and a customer-held balance is not settled payment. Track those states separately in the receivables ledger and weekly cash forecast, especially when payroll precedes acceptance.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Validate the payer and purchasing route
   - Match the signed buyer entity to its purchase order and payable account.
   - Record signer authority, invoice destination, currency, applicable taxes, and the individual authorized to certify acceptance.
2. Issue a milestone-specific invoice
   - Attach the contracted deliverable, amount, due date, and release evidence.
   - Use stable invoice identifiers and established payment instructions; independently verify any requested beneficiary change.
3. Verify funding before committing the next tranche
   - Check bank or provider records instead of accepting transfer screenshots.
   - Distinguish initiated, available, escrowed, returned, disputed, and released funds; assess remaining exposure for the selected method.
4. Reconcile collection and preserve proof
   - Match gross invoice amounts to fees, reserves, refunds, and deposited proceeds.
   - Keep acceptance records with the invoice and recognize earned fees separately from deposits, merchant receipts, and financing proceeds.

## 4. Screen credit and protect fees without trapping the client  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Credit screening starts with identity and payment behavior, not a promising website. Match the contract entity to the relevant secretary-of-state registry, verify the signer's authority through established company channels, and confirm that the payable contact uses a credible business domain. Registry status alone does not prove solvency. With appropriate authorization and a legitimate business purpose, obtain trade references and commercial credit information from providers such as Dun & Bradstreet or Experian Business. For material exposure, request recent statements, review accessible insolvency records, and inspect relevant UCC filings without mistaking every financing statement for distress. Ask references about actual days to pay and disputed invoices, not whether the customer is pleasant. Set an exposure ceiling equal to unpaid earned fees plus unbilled work in progress plus noncancelable vendor commitments, less applicable collected deposits. A buyer owing $10,000 while consuming $6,000 of work and creating $2,000 of commitments exposes $18,000 before any usable deposit. If the agency's ceiling is $12,000, require another $6,000 payment or reduce the next work tranche. Avoid double-counting deposits already applied to invoices. New entities, slow references, and unexplained payer substitutions justify smaller milestones, advance funding, or declining credit while still offering prepaid delivery.

Fee protection belongs in specific commercial mechanics. The statement of work should define inputs, outputs, excluded integrations, evaluation fixtures, latency targets where relevant, reviewer obligations, and an acceptance period with a documented defect process. A client must be able to reject work for specified failures without turning every new idea into an unpaid correction. Separate defects from changes: a broken agreed Salesforce field mapping is a defect; adding a second CRM is a change order. Price changes before execution and identify schedule effects. State ownership of client data, preexisting agency components, third-party materials, and newly commissioned deliverables. Tie any agreed transfer of eligible deliverable rights to payment without pretending that every model output necessarily carries exclusive copyright. Keep client production accounts client-owned, retain authorized access only, and provide an export and handoff procedure. If invoices age beyond the contracted cure period, suspend future agency services through the agreed process rather than deleting records, disabling unrelated systems, or withholding client credentials. Where an introduction fee exists, define the introduced account, covered transaction, earning event, duration, reporting rights, and dispute procedure. Collect for provable work and agreed introductions rather than relying on an expansive restriction against doing business elsewhere.

Run a weekly receivables and quality review using the same project identifiers in the delivery tracker, invoicing system, and bank reconciliation. QuickBooks or Xero can hold the ledger; Jira or Linear can hold acceptance tickets; the contract determines which evidence actually earns payment. Track days sales outstanding, overdue value, disputed value, support hours, model spend, and rework per account. A $2,500 monthly retainer with $400 model expense, $600 contractor labor, and $200 allocated tooling leaves $1,300 before other overhead and owner compensation. That 52% contribution calculation is not the canonical 30.0% Base net margin. If rework adds $900, contribution falls to $400; inspect evaluation failures before adding more customers. Use client intake workflows, deployment checklists, and usage and quality monitoring to control the three dominant risks: scope creep, unreliable outputs, and client data exposure. When payment fails, establish whether the cause is a rejected invoice, missing acceptance, genuine defect, cash shortage, or suspected fraud. Correct administrative errors promptly, document technical remediation, and negotiate staged payment only within the exposure ceiling. Preserve agreed export and incident-response duties during disputes. A collectible, repeatable retainer is worth more than a large disputed invoice supported only by an impressive demonstration.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before funded work becomes production work**
> 
> Treat these checks as delivery controls with named owners and recorded evidence. A sales promise cannot substitute for any check.
> - Verify that the contracting entity, authorized signer, payable contact, purchase order, and selected payment arrangement all describe the same transaction.
> - Verify that required deposits are actually funded and that unpaid fees, work in progress, and vendor commitments remain inside the approved exposure ceiling.
> - Verify client-system authorization, least-privilege accounts, applicable processing terms, permitted source access, and human review for consequential outputs.
> - Verify objective acceptance tests, change-order pricing, recurring charge terms, cancellation mechanics, and the client-data export and handoff procedure.
