## 1. Give every play one accountable seat and a funded capacity envelope  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build Candidate #29 around two employee seats and three bounded contractor work packages. FT1 owns commercial qualification, scope, pricing, collections, and customer relationships; FT2 owns architecture, technical acceptance, access authorization, and production accountability. C1 implements approved integrations, C2 independently tests outputs and security boundaries, and C3 maintains monitoring, runbooks, and scheduled support coverage. Contractors are not three additional unlimited employees hidden inside a retainer. Maintain the existing 23-play register with its authoritative identifiers, then allocate primary accountability across these seats: four plays to FT1, six to FT2, five to C1, four to C2, and four to C3. This is a work-allocation model, not a replacement taxonomy for the mapped plays. Every register entry needs one accountable owner, one backup, an acceptance artifact, a client-data boundary, and an estimated monthly load. Keep sales promises, implementation work, and final acceptance visibly separate even when a small team shares meetings. FT2 may reject C1's delivery without renegotiating commercial scope; FT1 may sell an extension only after capacity and pricing are recalculated. Store the register in Linear or Jira and attach versioned evidence in the agency's authorized GitHub organization.

Size work from available delivery hours rather than impressive headcount. As an explicit planning example, reserve 40 weekly hours across the two full-time seats for delivery after sales, administration, and leadership, and contract another 45 hours of specialist capacity. At 70% planned utilization, that creates 59.5 scheduled delivery hours while preserving 25.5 hours for variance, investigation, and interruptions. A sprint requiring 45 delivery hours consumes roughly three quarters of that weekly envelope; two simultaneous starts would exceed it. Measure actual effort for four weeks before raising concurrency. The Conservative scorecard's $100,000 revenue and $20,000 owner profit leave only $80,000 for total annual costs. That amount generally cannot sustain two market-paid full-time employees, three contractors, and operating overhead. Preserve the canonical 2 FT + 3 C label, but flag its staffing feasibility instead of silently treating employees as fractional. Owner compensation, local wage requirements, payroll burden, and startup funding need an explicit reconciliation. Funding supplies cash, not profit, and founder labor still has a replacement cost. Do not launch a payroll commitment until actual contracts and available capital cover the resulting obligations.

Expand to 3 FT + 4 C by adding FT3 as delivery manager and customer-success owner, plus C4 as a second implementation specialist with recovery capability. Transfer four existing play accountabilities to FT3 and two to C4; rebalance the original seats so the register still totals exactly 23, rather than manufacturing new plays to justify hiring. The third employee should remove founder coordination and renewal dependence before adding another technical bottleneck. Require eight weeks of signed, contribution-positive backlog, demonstrated support demand, and a cash forecast that survives the largest customer paying thirty days late. These are internal planning thresholds, not claims about every agency's demand cycle. Use named backups for each privileged system, but never shared credentials; maintain individual accounts, least-privilege roles, and documented emergency access. Contractor classification depends on actual working conditions, not the label in a statement of work. Assess applicable wage, tax, and state tests, including California Labor Code section 2775 where relevant. Specify deliverables and service windows without assuming that compulsory employee-like schedules become independent contracting merely because invoices arrive through Deel or another payment platform.

## 2. Pay for accepted work and run one evidence-backed weekly scorecard  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Contract compensation should make quality observable without forcing specialists to absorb unlimited client ambiguity. An illustrative $6,000 integration package can pay $1,200 after approved design, $3,600 after independently verified staging acceptance, and $1,200 after production handoff with a complete runbook. Define invoice dates, review deadlines, written rejection reasons, and dispute handling; pay undisputed amounts when due under the agreement and applicable law. Price scope changes separately rather than withholding earned fees to obtain extra features. Establish a five-business-day acceptance review window and a thirty-day correction period for reproducible defects against the frozen specification. New model behavior, upstream API changes, and customer process changes require triage before anyone assigns responsibility. Keep QA compensation independent of the number of releases approved, and never reward a reviewer for hiding defects to accelerate collections. As a sprint illustration, a $15,000 implementation fee less $6,000 implementation, $1,200 independent QA, $300 attributable usage, and $1,500 allocated employee delivery cost produces $6,000 delivery contribution, or 40%. That contribution still must cover selling, administration, support obligations, and other overhead before it becomes owner profit.

Publish Friday's scorecard from source systems, not a manually polished slide. Linear supplies accepted milestones and cycle times; GitHub supplies reviewed changes; Harvest supplies effort; the accounting ledger supplies accrued revenue and costs; authorized monitoring supplies usage and incident events. Track first-pass acceptance as accepted first submissions divided by all first submissions, with the sample count beside the rate. Use an initial 90% target, but investigate small samples rather than ranking contractors on one unusual ticket. Track rework hours divided by total delivery hours, targeting below 10%, and on-time milestones divided by milestones due, targeting at least 95%. Define a blocker before measuring it and report its age and accountable decision maker. Pair automation completion with error severity, human override rate, and the proportion of consequential decisions reviewed by an authorized person. A faster workflow that silently sends inaccurate customer communications is not a quality improvement. Review contribution by project, retainer consumption, overdue receivables, and committed capacity in the same meeting. Each adverse signal gets one owner, one corrective action, and a dated evidence requirement rather than another status discussion.

Write service levels around coverage the pod can actually supply. An illustrative managed retainer can include weekday coverage from 09:00 to 17:00 in the named client timezone, one-business-hour acknowledgement for a production blocker, and four-covered-hour acknowledgement for degraded service. Acknowledgement is not restoration. Define containment, customer updates, escalation, and recovery objectives separately; do not promise continuous response from three part-time contractor packages. For an exposed data path or unauthorized action, empower the designated responder to disable the affected automation, preserve necessary evidence, and notify FT2 immediately through the documented channel. Applicable customer contracts and incident laws determine external notification duties. Test the fallback with synthetic records, including expired credentials, model timeouts, duplicate webhook delivery, and malformed structured outputs. Use idempotency keys and transaction boundaries where supported instead of assuming a retry is harmless. The Monday review approves only the corrective work that fits purchased capacity and records whether a client change order is required. Persistent misses trigger narrower scope, additional purchased coverage, or a replacement supplier after an orderly handoff, not concealed unpaid overtime or optimistic changes to the scorecard definitions.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the measurement interval
   - Close the weekly delivery window at the agreed Friday cutoff.
   - Preserve original due dates, first submissions, accepted changes, covered incident hours, and source-system links.
2. Reconcile labor and acceptance
   - Match contractor milestones and employee effort to accepted project artifacts.
   - Accrue earned but uninvoiced delivery costs and separate defects from newly requested customer functionality.
3. Resolve exceptions with named owners
   - Review quality, contribution, concentration, and capacity in one thirty-minute meeting.
   - Assign each exception a responsible seat, corrective action, deadline, and evidence needed to close it.
4. Publish the next capacity commitment
   - Approve only the following week's work that fits purchased coverage and available cash.
   - Send authorized customer updates, record priced scope changes, and archive the scorecard for cohort and buyer review.

## 3. Reconcile accrual books, cohort retention, and concentrated exposures  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Keep implementation revenue, managed support revenue, client business outcomes, and cash receipts in separate fields. Candidate #29 earns fees and retainers; a client's increased sales, reduced payroll effort, or processed transaction value is not agency revenue. The canonical annual bridges are exact: Conservative revenue of $100,000 less $80,000 total costs equals $20,000 owner profit; Base revenue of $550,000 less $385,000 equals $165,000; Scaled revenue of $1,000,000 less $600,000 equals $400,000. Those are respectively 20%, 30.0%, and 40% net margins, not delivery contribution percentages. Do not turn the $2,500-$18,000 monthly operating-expense planning range into a substitute for complete annual costs; document which direct labor and other costs sit outside that narrower budget. An illustrative Base revenue bridge is twenty $15,000 implementations plus ten accounts averaging $25,000 recognized annual support fees. A Scaled bridge is twenty-five $20,000 implementations plus twenty accounts averaging $25,000 support fees. Both reconcile exactly, but neither demonstrates delivery feasibility until hours and service obligations fit the relevant pod. Conservative could comprise four $15,000 implementations and four $10,000 annual support accounts, subject to the staffing cost constraint already identified.

Use accrual schedules that explain when services are earned, not merely when Stripe pays the bank. For a $24,000 annual stand-ready support arrangement collected in advance, recognizing $2,000 monthly is appropriate only when the service obligation is satisfied evenly through that period. Record the unearned balance as a contract liability. Implementation recognition depends on the contractual performance obligations and the applicable accounting framework; a deposit or invoice milestone alone does not establish that delivery has occurred. Have the accountant document that policy and apply it consistently. Accrue accepted contractor work before the invoice arrives, allocate attributable model and hosting usage, and reconcile bank, receivable, payable, and contract-liability balances monthly. Maintain customer cohorts by first support-service month, recording starting recurring fees, expansion, contraction, churn, contribution, and support hours. Gross revenue retention excludes expansion; net revenue retention includes it, while both exclude new customers from the starting cohort. Report implementation fees separately so a large sprint cannot disguise shrinking recurring support. Preserve signed scope changes and credit notes alongside the ledger entries so a buyer can reproduce both revenue timing and delivery economics.

Set a maximum 25% customer concentration policy against trailing-twelve-month recognized revenue, forward contracted fees, and gross receivables, testing each denominator separately. Aggregate entities under common control; five subsidiaries of one sponsor do not create five independent revenue risks. At Base revenue, the trailing annual customer limit is $137,500; at Scaled it is $250,000; at Conservative it is $25,000. During startup, the first customer necessarily breaches a diversified revenue limit. Record that exception with a dated diversification plan, and restrict new fixed commitments until exposure falls within policy. Apply the same 25% maximum to material reseller or referral counterparties using attributable recognized fees. For critical delivery suppliers, measure non-substitutable delivery exposure rather than pretending low invoice spend means low dependency. A sole model provider may support every workflow despite costing little; tested fallbacks reduce this exposure only when authorized and operational. Track contractor replacement time and cash held with payment intermediaries separately. A cap does not repair a breach automatically: require an owner, an exposure-reduction action, and evidence that the alternative works before describing the book as diversified.

## 4. Build transferable earnings before negotiating an exit multiple  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Transferability begins with ownership and permissions, not a broker presentation. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a signed writing from the owner or authorized agent. Obtain signed contractor assignments covering commissioned code, prompts where protectable, documentation, tests, and other protectable deliverables; do not rely exclusively on a work-made-for-hire label for contractor software. Separate preexisting tools, third-party components, and client-owned materials in schedules with the actual licenses needed for continued operation and transfer. A contractor cannot assign rights they never owned, and an agency cannot promise exclusive ownership of every machine-generated output. Document customer access authority, processing instructions, subprocessors, retention limits, and deletion procedures. California service-provider arrangements, where applicable, require contract terms and practices consistent with Civil Code section 1798.100(d) and relevant implementing rules. Review customer assignment and change-of-control clauses before modeling a sale. Maintain human review for consequential decisions and retain evidence supporting performance claims under the FTC Act's prohibition on deceptive practices. These records protect ongoing delivery and let a buyer identify rights they can actually acquire.

Use valuation multiples as negotiation sensitivities, not as automatic rewards for mentioning AI. An illustrative owner-operated agency might be tested at 2.0-3.0 times normalized seller's discretionary earnings, while a manager-run agency with durable retention and transferable contracts might be tested at 3.0-5.0 times normalized EBITDA. These are planning ranges, not observed transaction comparables. Actual bids depend on scale, customer quality, growth, market conditions, and deal structure. The canonical $165,000 Base owner profit is neither automatically SDE nor EBITDA. Build a bridge showing owner wages already expensed, genuine discretionary costs, interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, and required replacement management. If that bridge independently supports $165,000 SDE, the illustrative 2.0-3.0 range implies $330,000-$495,000 enterprise value. If a separately verified Scaled bridge supports $300,000 normalized EBITDA after replacement management, 3.0-5.0 implies $900,000-$1,500,000 enterprise value. The $300,000 example is an assumed valuation input, not a revision to the $400,000 canonical owner profit. Never combine an SDE add-back with an EBITDA multiple while omitting the employee needed to replace the departing owner.

Prepare a buyer room that reconciles monthly ledgers to contracts, cohort retention, bank activity, delivery evidence, and the 23-play register. Include the weekly scorecards, contractor agreements, signed IP chain, customer transfer restrictions, security incidents, recovery tests, and a schedule of unfulfilled prepaid services. Share redacted summaries first; disclose customer data only through authorized, necessary diligence access with controlled recipients and an access log. Run a four-week founder-absence exercise in which the pod handles renewals, deployment decisions, and incident triage without undocumented intervention. Measure delivery misses and replacement labor rather than declaring independence because the founder stopped attending standups. Enterprise value does not equal cash received: reconcile debt, cash treatment, normalized working capital, transaction costs, holdbacks, and contingent consideration separately. Prepaid support liabilities need explicit treatment so the seller cannot retain all collections while leaving unfunded service obligations behind. If bids undervalue durable earnings, retain the business with the same concentration limits and management coverage. Distributions should follow payroll, tax obligations, service reserves, and the cash forecast; recurring owner profit is valuable only when operations can keep producing it.
