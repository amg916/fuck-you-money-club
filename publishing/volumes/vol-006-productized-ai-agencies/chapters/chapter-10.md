## 1. Turn the fixed-scope sprint into a measured automation system  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a product boundary that the five-person team can defend: one intake source, one bounded transformation, one destination, and one measurable acceptance test per sprint. The delivery lead owns architecture and client acceptance; the operations lead owns intake, support, and account economics. Three contractors cover integration engineering, test execution, and domain review through explicit work packets rather than standing attendance requirements. Sell additional destinations, historical migrations, and new decision classes as separately priced changes. In the statement of work, name authorized systems, permitted data fields, service accounts, retention periods, model providers, output ownership, and incident contacts. Access must come from the client resource owner, not from instructions embedded in uploaded documents. Treat 18 U.S.C. 1030 as a concrete reason to record access authorization and stop when that authorization expires. Build an inventory of every credential and external publication surface before connecting production. The deliverable is a repeatable workflow with an observable result, not a promise that an agent will navigate arbitrary business exceptions. A new client should require configuration and acceptance fixtures, while genuinely new business logic receives its own estimate and delivery window.

Measure automation against labor-weighted repetitive work, not the number of boxes colored green in a workflow editor. Suppose the baseline contains 1,000 monthly repetitive hours: 200 in research, 150 in qualification, 450 in fulfillment, and 200 in distribution operations. A designed workflow leaves 50, 45, 100, and 55 hours respectively, or 250 hours altogether. That removes 750 hours and achieves 75% automation of the defined baseline. If the new system also creates 80 hours of review and incident handling, remaining total effort is 330 hours and net labor reduction is 67%, not 75%. Both figures belong on the client dashboard, together with volume, error severity, and sampling coverage. Do not describe the illustrative 1,000 hours as the agency team's available capacity; it can represent work distributed across client organizations. Instrument each task with start time, completion time, reviewer minutes, rework cause, and a stable workflow version. Compare like cohorts before claiming savings. Randomly inspect apparently successful runs because exception-only sampling misses silent errors. Freeze the denominator for a reporting period so changing task definitions cannot manufacture an improvement that the customer's staff never experience.

Implement intake as a small Python HTTP service behind a managed TLS endpoint. Verify the provider's webhook signature against raw request bytes with hmac.compare_digest, validate timestamps, and reject replayed event identifiers. A successful response means durable receipt, not completed business work: persist the event and an outbox record atomically, then let workers deliver outbox messages to Amazon SQS. Use a unique constraint on tenant, source, and event identifier to collapse duplicates. Validate JSON field types, payload size, allowed destinations, and client entitlements before any model call. The LLM receives only the minimum necessary fields and returns a typed proposal with evidence references; deterministic Python validates the proposal before a separate executor considers side effects. Customer text is data, never authority to expand tool access. Keep secrets outside prompts and retrieve them through scoped service identities. Attach tenant identity at ingestion and enforce it again when reading evidence, writing outputs, and opening review queues. A dedicated client pause switch must stop new external actions without destroying queued evidence. This separation lets operators repair a connector without asking the model to improvise a recovery procedure inside a live account.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Bind scope to authorized resources
   - Record client-approved systems, fields, identities, and permitted actions.
   - Create separate fixture and production configurations; issue no production credential before the resource owner authorizes access.
2. Capture the manual baseline
   - Time representative research, qualification, fulfillment, and distribution tasks.
   - Store task counts, handling minutes, error categories, and review effort so later automation percentages use a stable denominator.
3. Run deterministic and model evaluations
   - Replay normal, adversarial, duplicate, and dependency-failure fixtures.
   - Require evidence-linked outputs, tenant isolation, bounded tool permissions, and explicit review routing for consequential actions.
4. Release a reversible production slice
   - Enable one client workflow with bounded volume and active monitoring.
   - Check destination outcomes against receipts, rehearse the pause switch, and expand only after the accountable operator reviews observed results.

Build each dossier from evidence rather than persuasive model narration. Do not call that contribution owner profit. Create distribution through permissioned, redacted before-and-after dossiers, agency partnerships, and technical pages explaining evidence requirements for specific marketplaces. ## 3. Bound payment orchestration to approved merchants, routes, and responsibilities  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat high_risk_payfac_iso_mid_cascading_arbitrage as a demanding integration pattern, not permission to bypass underwriting. Its wholesale ISO and orchestration concept spans 25–60 domestic and offshore acquiring banks and high-risk merchant categories, including telehealth GLP-1s or peptides, nutra subscriptions, sweepstakes gaming, high-ticket coaching, and AI companions. Those labels establish neither legality nor processor eligibility. A category rejected by one provider may be accepted only by another provider that knowingly approves the actual business, geography, products, fulfillment, and descriptor. The productized agency should sell implementation and monitoring to authorized payment operators unless it separately holds the contracts and responsibilities required for another role. Collect each acquirer's written merchant approval, permitted transaction types, retry rules, settlement currency, and designated incident contact. Maintain a route registry that workers can read but an LLM cannot modify. New merchant identifiers require verified ownership and approved routing configuration. Offshore connectivity does not remove geographic, sanctions, consumer-protection, or product restrictions. When an approval is absent, the implementation stops at a sandbox demonstration rather than sending live transactions through an unrelated merchant account.

Expose the economics without confusing client payment volume with agency revenue. On $1,000,000 monthly merchant volume, a simplified 2.10% wholesale cost is $21,000. Merchant pricing of 5.50% produces $55,000 in charges and a $34,000 gross spread; 8.90% produces $89,000 and a $68,000 gross spread. These are payment-operator figures before additional network charges, refunds, losses, operating expenses, and contractual allocations, not this agency's recognized implementation revenue. A 10% rolling reserve withholds $100,000 of that month's eligible volume under the assumed reserve basis. Reserve balances remain restricted merchant funds or associated settlement obligations according to the actual arrangement, never free agency income. The agency instead might invoice a $25,000 deployment and $5,000 monthly support, already counted within the chapter's canonical revenue mixes. Build a merchant-level settlement ledger that distinguishes captured principal, processor fees, refunds, reserves, releases, and actual agency invoices. Reconcile provider statements against transaction identifiers daily. An unexplained difference triggers investigation and client notification, not an automatic balancing entry that hides the discrepancy or inflates reported service revenue.

Make payment routing deterministic and bounded. Tokenize card details through the authorized gateway; the agency workflow stores tokens and minimal transaction metadata, not raw card data in prompts or general logs. A decline classifier follows processor guidance: hard declines stop, while eligible soft declines receive only explicitly permitted retries on approved routes. Cascading must never disguise the merchant, evade issuer decisions, or send duplicate authorizations after a timeout without checking transaction status. For this mapped offer, require a documented 10% rolling reserve arrangement before enabling live routing, and enforce the contracted release schedule. Configure Verifi RDR pre-dispute refunds with merchant authorization, financial thresholds, and reconciliation; refund automation does not erase dispute obligations. Use supported 3DS2 step-up authentication for designated high-risk BIN and transaction cohorts, alongside broader fraud signals, and maintain recognizable merchant descriptors. Humans handle merchant onboarding, route changes, exceptional refunds, and consequential risk decisions. For covered recurring consumer electronic fund transfers, implement authorization and copy requirements under 12 CFR 1005.10(b); for covered online negative-option offers, address 15 U.S.C. 8403. Provider outages fall back to a pending state or controlled stop, never blind multi-bank retries.
