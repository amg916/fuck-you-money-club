## 1. Mine observable workflow changes rather than generic interest in AI  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A job advertisement seeking Salesforce migration experience identifies a possible project; it does not establish purchasing authority or budget. SEC EDGAR filings can reveal acquisitions and integration priorities, while SAM.gov notices describe actual procurement requirements. State business registrations establish entity facts, not intent. Require two independent signals before personalizing outreach, such as a migration job posting plus a newly published integration guide. Rank manual work with visible transaction volume above vague transformation announcements. The useful asset is an evidence-backed account brief that a delivery lead can inspect in five minutes. Delete stale hypotheses after thirty days unless a fresh observation supports them, and retain source facts separately from your commercial interpretation.

## 2. Score workflow economics, buyer authority, and deliverability before discovery expands  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a 100-point qualification scorecard that delivery staff can challenge. Allocate twenty points each to measurable workflow volume, verified economic pain, named buyer authority, accessible system boundaries, and repeatability within the standard sprint. Award zero, ten, or twenty points per category using recorded evidence, not salesperson confidence. A prospect with 2,000 monthly tickets, a sampled handling-time study, a budget owner, documented API access, and a reusable classification pattern can score 100. A persuasive champion without authority receives zero for that category until they arrange a buyer meeting. Route scores of eighty or above to discovery, sixty to seventy to further research, and lower scores to a dated nurture queue. Keep separate disqualifiers for unauthorized access, undisclosed sensitive datasets, and demands for unreviewed consequential decisions. A high commercial score never cancels those constraints. Obtain written authorization specifying systems, accounts, actions, and duration before inspecting client environments. Under 18 U.S.C. § 1030, public visibility is not permission to enter restricted resources. Use sanitized exports for initial analysis and client-controlled service accounts for delivery. Record the revocation owner, retention period, and incident contact before moving from sales evidence into operational data.

Quantify recoverable capacity before quoting a price. Suppose a team processes 2,000 tickets monthly, and a controlled sample supports reducing review effort by four minutes per ticket. That creates 8,000 minutes, or 133.33 hours, of potential monthly capacity. At a buyer-confirmed loaded labor rate of $45, the modeled value is $6,000 monthly. If only sixty percent becomes usable capacity because staffing and queue timing constrain redeployment, the working value becomes $3,600. A $2,500 monthly retainer leaves $1,100 before recovering the implementation fee. A $15,000 sprint would then require approximately 13.6 months to recover from that residual value, assuming steady performance and excluding additional client costs. That may be unattractive; reduce scope, target a larger queue, or decline instead of inflating savings. Distinguish released capacity from payroll reductions and exclude hypothetical client sales from agency revenue. Require a baseline sample containing routine, ambiguous, and failure cases. Measure accuracy, escalation frequency, review time, and cost per completed case. The buyer must name who absorbs exceptions and how work proceeds when the model or API is unavailable. Economics that omit supervision are usually sales fiction rather than a deliverable operating improvement.

Productize discovery around an input contract, an output contract, and a repair boundary. A $2,500 paid diagnostic can be credited against a $15,000 sprint, leaving $12,500 due under the implementation payment schedule; it is not an additional $2,500 of revenue when credited. Limit the sprint to one intake source, one destination system, three named routing outcomes, and a fixed evaluation set. Define what counts as an accepted record, how duplicates are handled, and which uncertain outputs require human review. State whether the client supplies software licenses and whether model consumption is included within a capped allowance. Put additional integrations, historical cleanup, and new business units into separately priced change orders. Data-processing terms should specify permitted uses, subprocessors, deletion, security responsibilities, and incident reporting. IP terms should distinguish reusable agency components from client-specific deliverables and third-party licenses. For consequential employment, credit, healthcare, or similar decisions, keep the initial offer limited to administrative preparation unless qualified reviewers and applicable requirements are explicitly addressed. Finish discovery with a mutual action plan naming the signer, technical owner, sample-data date, acceptance reviewer, and decision deadline. Without those owners, send a short evidence request rather than a speculative proposal.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four facts before accepting implementation money**
> 
> A signed order should describe work the agency is authorized, equipped, and economically able to deliver.
> - Verify a named budget owner, decision date, purchase process, and written acceptance authority for the specific workflow.
> - Verify written system-access authorization, permitted data uses, approved subprocessors, deletion timing, and a client-controlled revocation path.
> - Verify baseline cases, exception ownership, human review requirements, rollback behavior, and measurable acceptance thresholds.
> - Verify labor estimates, included usage limits, diagnostic credit treatment, change-order pricing, and the support retainer's actual service obligations.

## 3. Run a measured outbound cadence backed by useful inbound proof  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Organize outreach as a fourteen-business-day sequence with explicit stop conditions. On day one, send a short commercial email identifying the observed event, one plausible workflow problem, and a question about ownership. On day four, provide a relevant field-mapping checklist rather than another meeting request. On day seven, use a genuine LinkedIn profile for a manual contextual interaction when platform rules permit; do not automate connection spam. On day ten, call a published business number manually where applicable calling rules permit, identify the agency, and ask whether the workflow owner wants the material. On day fourteen, close the sequence with a clear stop option. A reply, objection, opt-out, or wrong-person report immediately changes the sequence. Commercial email must use accurate routing information and subjects, provide required advertising identification and a valid physical postal address, and offer a working opt-out under 15 U.S.C. § 7704. Honor opt-outs within ten business days and maintain a suppression record. Avoid purchased mobile-number blasting and automated calls or texts; 47 U.S.C. § 227 and 47 CFR § 64.1200 impose channel-specific restrictions. Track attempts by account so multiple contacts never receive conflicting stories or repeated unwanted pitches.

Use a planning funnel rather than borrowing conversion claims from unrelated agencies. Across an eight-week prospecting window, research 400 unique accounts and select one initial contact per account. Assume 380 delivered first emails, 38 positive replies, 23 held qualified meetings, 15 proposals, and six signed sprints. Those assumptions imply 95% delivery, 10% positive replies per delivered email, approximately 61% held meetings per positive reply, approximately 65% proposals per held meeting, and 40% wins per proposal. The complete raw-account win rate is 1.5%. Six $15,000 sprints create $90,000 in signed implementation fees, not immediate recognized revenue or owner profit. At sixty selling hours costing $75 each plus $600 in tools and data, campaign cost is $5,100, or $850 per signed sprint. Include founder selling time even when no payroll payment occurs, and avoid counting the same hours again in another acquisition model. Track signature dates, delivery capacity, deposits, and accounting recognition separately. At 40% proposal conversion, three to ten wins require roughly eight to twenty-five proposals, with actual results varying. If early reply quality misses the assumption, revise the segment and evidence rather than increasing unwanted message volume.

Make inbound assets answer the same questions discovery uncovers. Publish one specific workflow page describing supported systems, required inputs, exclusions, evaluation method, and price structure. Add a sanitized before-and-after trace showing a failed classification, the reviewer correction, and the final write to HubSpot. Offer a downloadable checklist through an optional signup rather than withholding every useful detail. Measure qualified conversations and signed work, not impressions or unverified attribution. Store first-touch source, last meaningful interaction, and the buyer's stated discovery path separately. Partner referrals need written boundaries covering introductions, fees, customer ownership, and disclosure; referral fees belong in acquisition economics. Under 15 U.S.C. § 45, performance claims need a defensible factual basis. The FTC's 16 CFR Part 255 guides explain material-connection and endorsement principles, while 16 CFR Part 465 prohibits specified fake-review and testimonial practices. Do not present a synthetic demonstration as a customer result or a paid promoter as an independent buyer. Review weekly whether prospects cite the proof artifact, whether it answers a real objection, and whether its claim still matches current performance. Remove unsupported claims and replace vague success language with the actual sample size, measurement period, workflow boundary, and observed failure rate.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the weekly evidence queue
   - Research fifty new accounts weekly and attach two independent workflow signals.
   - Keep observed facts, inferred intent, source dates, and permitted outreach channels in separate CRM fields.
2. Qualify before sequencing
   - Score volume, economics, authority, access, and repeatability using recorded evidence.
   - Route strong accounts to discovery, incomplete accounts to research, and disqualified configurations away from implementation selling.
3. Send one coherent multichannel sequence
   - Use the fourteen-business-day cadence and update the account immediately after every response.
   - Suppress opt-outs, stop incorrect hypotheses, and keep email, manual social activity, and permitted calls consistent.
4. Review conversions and delivery capacity together
   - Reconcile researched accounts, replies, meetings, proposals, signatures, and available sprint slots each week.
   - Diagnose the earliest weak stage before expanding outreach; never sell starts the delivery team cannot support.

## 4. Close bounded mandates and reconcile acquisition economics to the scorecard  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Handle objections by identifying the unresolved decision, not by promising broader capabilities. When the buyer says their internal team can build it, ask whether they want a tested connector, an evaluation harness, or temporary overflow capacity; sell the missing component or exit. When accuracy is questioned, propose a paid evaluation against a client-approved holdout set with human review and explicit stop criteria. When security blocks progress, offer a sanitized diagnostic and document the intended data path before seeking access. When price is challenged, compare the fee with buyer-validated recoverable capacity, then reduce scope rather than removing exception handling. When timing is vague, request the operational event that makes delay costly and schedule follow-up around that event. Finish each meeting with one named owner, one evidence request, and one dated decision. Send a proposal only after the buyer agrees on acceptance measures and the implementation boundary. A suggested $15,000 payment structure is $7,500 at signing, $3,750 after demonstrated integration, and $3,750 at acceptance. Specify the review window and defect process; silence should not conceal unresolved critical failures. Deposits finance work but do not automatically establish earned revenue. Track accounts receivable, delivery obligations, and cash separately from signed contract value.

Test sprint contribution before assuming the canonical annual margin is achievable. For an illustrative $15,000 implementation, budget $7,200 for loaded delivery labor, $600 for model and software consumption, $1,200 for independent quality review and rework, and $850 for allocated acquisition cost. The remaining $5,150 is 34.3% contribution after those listed costs, before remaining overhead and other annual expenses. It is not the Base scenario's net margin. A $2,500 monthly support retainer with $1,000 of direct monitoring and support cost contributes $1,500, or 60%, before acquisition and shared overhead. Define included incidents, response hours, usage allowance, and change-request treatment so this contribution is not consumed by unlimited development. If three new clients each require sixty delivery hours, the cohort needs 180 hours before support and selling. Reserve that capacity on a calendar rather than treating contractors as instantly available. Automate intake, deployment checklists, and usage monitoring only after owners and exception paths are established. Pause acquisition when promised start dates exceed staffed capacity. A waitlist with an honest start date protects trust better than accepting deposits against unstaffed work. Review realized contribution after each sprint and update the standard offer when repeated exceptions reveal a pricing error.

Reconcile annual planning to the supplied scorecard without turning first-cohort bookings into a forecast. Conservative revenue can comprise four $15,000 implementations plus four clients receiving ten months of $1,000 support: $60,000 plus $40,000 equals $100,000. At 20% net margin, annual owner net profit is $20,000 and total modeled costs are $80,000, with 2 FT + 3 C. Base revenue can comprise ten $25,000 implementations plus ten full-year $2,500 monthly retainers: $250,000 plus $300,000 equals $550,000. At 30.0%, owner net profit is $165,000 and costs are $385,000, again with 2 FT + 3 C. Scaled revenue can comprise twenty standardized $20,000 implementations plus twenty full-year $2,500 monthly retainers: $400,000 plus $600,000 equals $1,000,000. At 40%, owner net profit is $400,000 and costs are $600,000, with 3 FT + 4 C. These are illustrative reconciliations, not mandated price cards; full-year support assumes active service throughout the year and cannot be assigned to late-year starts. Contractor counts do not imply full-time utilization. The first 1–4 months should establish three to ten signed wins, observed conversion rates, and repeatable delivery evidence. Scale only the offer whose actual work supports its price, capacity plan, and continuing customer value.
