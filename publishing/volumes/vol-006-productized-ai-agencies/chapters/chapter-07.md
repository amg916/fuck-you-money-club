## 2. Build an auditable six-module production and ledger spine  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use the six fragments below as one sequential Python reference script; every dependency is standard library and every monetary amount is an integer number of cents. The reference deliberately covers a local control plane rather than pretending to deploy connectors, store credentials, or operate a payment institution. SQLite serializes writers through BEGIN IMMEDIATE, with foreign keys, durable commits, and database-enforced business uniqueness. Give each concurrent worker its own connection to the same local database file; never put that file on an unreliable shared network filesystem. A lock timeout fails the whole transaction so the caller can retry the same business key. Canonical payload comparison rejects a reused key carrying changed facts. One project identifier represents one billable milestone; recurring service requires a new identifier containing its service month. Application code is the exclusive writer, and deployment permissions prevent casual ledger edits. These assumptions remove check-then-write races within the reference's transaction boundary. They do not make an external email, payment, or CRM call atomic with SQLite. Production connector workers must use provider-supported idempotency and status lookup, or stop an ambiguous action for human resolution instead of blindly repeating it.

The verification and escrow state machine uses received, verified, reserved, released, and rejected states. Here reserved means an internal capacity allocation, not money held for another party; there is no escrow bank account, custody service, or automatic funds transfer. If a customer requires actual escrow, integrate the separately contracted provider and reconcile its statements without relabeling internal flags as safeguarded funds. Verification requires an evidence reference, and each transition records its expected prior state atomically. Release follows documented customer acceptance and enables invoice posting. A same-key retry returns the original transition result, while a different transition against an obsolete state fails. Release also creates a unique outbox notice inside the transaction. That notice is an inert work item, not permission to send anything externally. Keep model execution outside ledger transactions: store evaluation artifacts separately and reference their hashes or controlled locations. Never place raw mailbox content, secrets, or model prompts containing personal data inside accounting descriptions. Separate the engineer proposing a release from the reviewer accepting its evidence in the surrounding application, because the small reference demonstrates state integrity rather than a complete identity and permissions service.

Posting recognizes implementation revenue only after the defined milestone has been delivered and accepted; support revenue follows the service period. The invoice journal debits receivables and credits fee revenue. A bank-backed collection debits cash and credits that project's receivables, while a paid operating cost debits expense and credits cash. Unique bank identifiers prevent the same imported transaction from funding two postings, and an atomic outstanding-balance check prevents concurrent overcollection. This compact model accepts only exact, gross, single-project receipts and individually paid costs. Split settlements, processor fees, refunds, taxes, pre-delivery deposits, and currency conversion require explicit additional posting types; reject them rather than forcing them into receipt or cost. In the cohort, signing deposits arrive before acceptance but every project is accepted within its month, so monthly reporting has no remaining deposit liability. A live daily ledger must still add a customer-deposit liability account before recording those earlier deposits. Reconcile imported bank activity to posted cash and investigate every unmatched row. The supplied tests prove selected invariants and retry behavior; they are executable examples, not evidence that an installation has already passed a deployment test.

## 3. Walk a mature ninety-day cohort from lead scrubs to cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The Worked 90-Day Cohort Walk is a mature-quarter operating illustration, not a cold-start forecast. Enter Month 1 with ten active support accounts, $20,000 opening cash, and no opening receivables, payables, or customer deposits. Opening cash is working capital, not quarterly revenue. The support accounts remain active throughout the quarter and pay $1,250 each monthly for services delivered in that month. Sign and accept three $10,000 implementation projects in Month 1, three in Month 2, and four in Month 3. Each implementation pays $5,000 at signing and its remaining $5,000 in the following month. Completion within the signing month makes implementation revenue $30,000, $30,000, and $40,000; support adds $12,500 each month. Total recognized fees are therefore $42,500, $42,500, and $52,500. These assumptions require real reviewer capacity and timely acceptance, not merely an invoice date. An incomplete project keeps its appropriate deposit liability and recognition treatment until delivery evidence supports the accounting conclusion. Client revenue, client payroll savings, and the face value of capital raises remain outside every agency revenue line. New implementations do not automatically become additional retainers during this worked quarter.

Track acquisition cohorts separately from invoice cohorts so scrubbed leads cannot inflate conversion. Month 1 begins with sixty raw leads, removes twelve duplicates or unsuitable prospects, and advances twelve of forty-eight qualified records to discovery. Three sign and nine discovery opportunities close lost; thirty-six qualified records remain unadvanced. Month 2 starts with sixty-four raw records, scrubs sixteen, and repeats the twelve-call, three-win result. Month 3 starts with eighty raw records, scrubs sixteen, and converts sixteen discovery calls into four wins and twelve losses. Across the quarter, 204 raw records minus forty-four scrubs produce 160 qualified records; forty discovery calls produce ten wins, thirty discovery losses, and 120 qualified records that did not advance. These are distinct people or entities under a documented deduplication rule, not repeated feed events. Closed-lost means an acquisition outcome, not an accounting write-off. The illustration assumes zero refunds and zero bad debt, so any actual credit loss requires a separate variance. Record loss reasons such as absent reviewer capacity, unclear access authority, poor workflow repeatability, or unacceptable scope before purchasing more lead volume to conceal a delivery-fit problem.

Cash receipts equal $27,500 in Month 1, $42,500 in Month 2, and $47,500 in Month 3. ## 4. Run delivery, exception recovery, and the recurring close  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Operate the production sprint through visible evidence checkpoints. On days one and two, the delivery lead confirms access, exports a minimal permitted sample, and freezes a labeled evaluation set. On days three through five, the engineer builds the connector, deterministic preprocessing, model adapter, and exception routing behind a disabled write capability. On days six through eight, a reviewer measures class-level precision, missed urgent cases, unsupported statements, and required abstentions against the frozen set. On days nine and ten, the client reviews representative outputs and accepts the bounded workflow or receives an explicit defect list. Use Git for versioned configuration, SQLite for the reference control ledger, and the client's approved secrets manager for credentials. Pin model identifiers and record provider request identifiers without copying sensitive request bodies into general logs. A contractual test might require ninety-eight correct routes among one hundred labeled routine cases and zero unauthorized sends; that is a specific acceptance sample, not a universal accuracy claim. Measure risky classes separately because aggregate accuracy can conceal every urgent message being misrouted. Failed tests keep write operations disabled and send cases to the existing human workflow rather than quietly relaxing the acceptance threshold.

Run managed support as an exception service with explicit capacity. Each morning, inspect failed runs, unreviewed outputs, connector authorization changes, spend anomalies, and aging receivables. Each week, replay regression fixtures, sample apparently successful outputs, and compare actual review minutes against the retainer budget. Successful HTTP responses do not prove useful work: an empty extraction or confidently wrong category can pass transport checks. A provider timeout after a requested external action is an ambiguous result, so query the provider using the original idempotency key before retrying. If the provider cannot establish the outcome, quarantine the task for manual resolution. A worker lease is only a scheduling mechanism; its expiration does not prove the previous worker failed before acting. For data exposure, disable affected credentials, preserve access logs, identify impacted fields and clients, and execute the applicable incident process. Keep model inputs separate by client, restrict retrieval indexes by tenant, and test cross-client access denial using synthetic fixtures. Record recurring defects as priced engineering changes when they exceed the agreed support allowance. The fallback to human handling must remain usable throughout an outage, with named owners and a visible queue rather than an unmonitored mailbox.

Close the business with the same rigor used to release software. Reconcile bank statements, invoice aging, customer deposits, vendor obligations, and delivered service periods before publishing a margin. Keep implementation fees and support retainers in separate revenue accounts so project volume cannot hide a deteriorating recurring book. FTC Act Section 5, 15 U.S.C. § 45, makes deceptive commercial claims an operating risk: support performance statements with dated measurements, denominators, and relevant limits. When endorsements are used, evaluate material-connection disclosures under 16 CFR § 255.5 and review practices under Part 465. Scale only when measured delivery hours, review capacity, and exception rates support the next cohort. The scorecard remains three separate cases: $100,000 revenue with $80,000 costs produces $20,000 Conservative profit; $550,000 with $385,000 costs produces $165,000 Base profit; $1,000,000 with $600,000 costs produces $400,000 Scaled profit. Conservative and Base use two full-time staff plus three contractors; Scaled uses three full-time staff plus four contractors. These are annual scenario economics, not automatic outcomes of installing the reference script. Maintain loaded labor and contractor expense inside the model, and investigate cash shortfalls separately from genuine erosion in delivery margin.
