## 1. Reconcile every earned fee through the owner-profit waterfall  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with earned implementation fees and earned support retainers, not proposals signed, deposits received, client advertising spend, or revenue a client attributes to an automation. The illustrative Conservative cohort delivers eight $10,000 sprints and forty $500 support account-months, producing $100,000. Base delivers twenty $20,000 sprints and one hundred $1,500 account-months, producing $550,000. Scaled delivers twenty-five $28,000 sprints and one hundred fifty $2,000 account-months, producing $1,000,000. These are annual production mixes, not assumptions that every new customer purchases twelve months of support. Tie each account-month to a named customer, service period, and supported workflow. Stripe receipts, QuickBooks invoices, and bank deposits must reconcile through a separate schedule of receivables, customer advances, taxes collected, and earned fees. A $10,000 deposit against an unfinished $20,000 sprint creates cash and an obligation; it does not create completed delivery. Define acceptance evidence in the statement of work: approved test cases, connector permissions, deployment records, and the customer's designated reviewer. Store those artifacts beside the invoice identifier so a disputed milestone can be traced without reconstructing Slack conversations. The scorecard revenue totals remain fixed throughout this chapter.

The waterfall below supplies explicit planning allocations that reconcile exactly to the canonical profit cases; those allocations are assumptions, not additional scorecard facts. Contractor delivery, model and hosting consumption, internal quality review, and remediation provision constitute direct service costs. Subtracting them leaves $52,000, $306,000, and $600,000 of service contribution. Acquisition, payment collection, and fixed operating costs then leave $20,000, $165,000, and $400,000 for the owner. Contractor delivery excludes employees already allocated to quality review or overhead. Acquisition includes attributed sales labor and commissions, preventing apparently inexpensive organic leads from hiding founder time. Payment costs use a two-percent blended planning assumption across payment methods, rather than asserting any processor's current tariff. Track remediation provisions separately from cash earmarked for refunds: moving money between bank accounts does not create another expense. The modeled provision covers expected service recovery and delivery rework. Actual fee reversals require separate treatment and may reduce reported revenue; they cannot be silently absorbed while preserving both the original revenue and profit. Release unused provisions only against documented closure of the underlying service obligation, never merely to improve a monthly margin.

The staffing labels remain exactly 2 FT + 3 C, 2 FT + 3 C, and 3 FT + 4 C, but a roster is not a payroll budget. Conservative economics are especially fragile: only $26,000 sits in the combined quality-review and fixed-overhead lines, before separating software, insurance, administration, and non-owner employee compensation. That cannot establish the affordability of two market-rate technical salaries. Preserve the canonical scenario while explicitly flagging this capacity constraint rather than presenting unsupported hiring economics. For this model, owner distributions are the residual, with no additional replacement salary imputed for the owner; personal income taxes are outside the operating bridge. Actual employee wages, employer costs, entity-level taxes where applicable, and business insurance must fit their assigned expense lines or reduce the residual. Under 29 U.S.C. 206 and 207, covered nonexempt employees require applicable minimum wages and overtime; state requirements may be higher. Contractor labels also need classification review under the applicable rules. If replacing owner delivery requires another $80,000 annually, economic residuals become negative $60,000, positive $85,000, and positive $320,000. That sensitivity is distinct from the unchanged canonical owner-profit figures.

## 2. Price acquired-client cohorts and scrub disputed delivery before distribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use an acquired-client cohort as the commercial unit, with one sprint per newly acquired customer in this illustration. Divide annual recognized fees by eight, twenty, or twenty-five customers to obtain $12,500, $27,500, and $40,000 of recognized fees per acquired client. These figures include the year's associated support production, not a forecast of lifetime value. Contractor allowances become $3,750, $7,250, and $9,600 per client. At an illustrative blended $100 contractor hour, those allowances purchase 37.5, 72.5, and 96 hours across implementation and support. Instrument delivery with Harvest or Toggl project codes that distinguish connector installation, evaluation-set creation, deployment, support, and rework. A Base implementation consuming ninety contractor hours already exceeds its entire cohort allowance by $1,750 before support begins. Trigger a written scope amendment when the customer requests another data source, permission model, or consequential decision workflow. The fallback is a narrower supported workflow with an explicit manual queue, not unpaid engineering disguised as customer success. Track support account-months separately from active logos: one hundred Base account-months average five per acquired client, but individual customers may start later or receive different service durations.

The mapped play competitor_intent_comparison_hijacking belongs inside acquisition economics, not inside a promised conversion multiple. Build text-only comparison matrices, timestamped public-price citations, fee calculators, and migration guides for genuine competitor pricing, cancellation, and comparison queries. Define the proposed 10-18% conversion range as qualified landing visitors becoming booked discovery calls; it is a test assumption, not evidence of visitor-to-customer performance. With 1,000 qualified visitors, a twelve-percent booking rate, and a twenty-percent booked-call close rate, the funnel produces twenty-four customers. A $24,000 campaign therefore costs $1,000 per acquired customer before any omitted sales expense. At ten percent booking and fifteen percent closing, the same spend produces fifteen customers and $1,600 CAC. The canonical cohort budgets remain $1,500, $2,750, and $3,200 blended CAC, leaving room for other channels and sales effort. Assess nominative use under applicable trademark doctrine; 15 U.S.C. 1125 still addresses confusing affiliation and misleading commercial claims. The fallback is narrow, factual reference using brand names only, no copied logos or implied endorsement, and removal of unsupported comparisons. Measure paid invoices by source rather than rewarding a page for unqualified form submissions.

Scrub the delivery ledger weekly before treating booked margin as distributable cash. Match each sprint to acceptance evidence, outstanding defect tickets, contractor accruals, disputed invoices, and the remaining support obligation. A remediation provision should reflect expected extra labor and approved service recovery, while a processor hold belongs in restricted settlement cash and an unearned deposit remains a customer obligation. These are different balances with different release events. At Base, the $16,500 provision can absorb eleven incidents costing $1,500 each, or another documented combination; it cannot simultaneously fund those incidents and eleven fee refunds without another charge. If management instead grants $10,000 of fee credits with no offsetting expense reduction, reported net fees fall to $540,000 and owner profit to $155,000 under this sensitivity. Do not relabel that result as the canonical Base case. Retain the original invoice, credit memo, customer correspondence, and incident root cause in one case record. Repeated failures on a document extractor should tighten schema validation and exception routing before another sale, while repeated access-related failures should change onboarding evidence and permission testing rather than merely enlarging the reserve.

## 3. Fund collection delays without mistaking credits or client principal for cash earnings  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Model liquidity by calendar date, not by annual margin. Here DSO means invoice issuance to customer payment, and the additional lock means customer payment to unrestricted bank availability; this definition prevents double-counting processor settlement days. Assume DSO of thirty, forty-five, and sixty days, followed by locks of seven, seven, and fourteen days. Funding the full annual expense budgets over those combined delays requires approximately $8,110, $54,849, and $121,644, using expense divided by 365 times thirty-seven, fifty-two, or seventy-four days. This conservative liquidity proxy treats the remediation allowance as future cash spending; it does not claim the provision is a cash payment when booked. Adding illustrative next-payroll and incident buffers of $10,000, $25,000, and $40,000 produces $18,110, $79,849, and $161,644 liquidity targets. Actual weekly forecasts must replace this steady-state proxy when milestones cluster. Negotiate a fifty-percent sprint deposit, invoice the balance against objective acceptance, and bill support in advance where contracts permit. Subtract only available cash after covering its associated obligations, not every dollar called a deposit. The $5,000-$60,000 startup capital range is therefore not automatically sufficient for Base or Scaled collection delays.

The mapped play cloud_ai_startup_credit_aggregation_arbitrage must preserve genuine eligibility and separate normalized economics from temporary cash relief. If confirmed credits offset $22,000 of Base's $27,500 normalized compute budget, current cash payments fall to $5,500, but the recurring scorecard still carries $27,500. Maintain a paid-price shadow ledger and a runway alarm ninety days before expiry. The mapped play agency_ad_spend_card_multiplier_rebate_cycling is acceptable only for genuine client-authorized advertising purchases under eligible payment arrangements and explicit reward-allocation terms. ## 4. Stress the waterfall and close cash before committing the next sprint  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run sensitivity tests on separate tabs so the canonical scorecard never mutates into a more flattering forecast. A twenty-percent contractor overrun reduces annual profit by $6,000, $29,000, and $48,000, leaving $14,000, $136,000, and $352,000. A fifty-percent increase in normalized compute expense leaves $17,500, $151,250, and $380,000. A twenty-five-percent acquisition overspend leaves $17,000, $151,250, and $380,000, assuming the same customer count and production. Raising remediation cost by two percentage points of revenue leaves $18,000, $154,000, and $380,000. Combining those four shocks leaves only $6,500, $97,500, and $292,000. These calculations hold revenue constant and assume no overlapping charges; a lost customer or fee reversal requires a separate revenue bridge. Collection delays also remain separate: fifteen additional days require approximately $3,288, $15,822, and $24,658 more cash at baseline expense rates, without automatically changing profit. Interest changes profit only when borrowing is actually modeled. If Base DSO reaches ninety days while its seven-day lock persists, operating float becomes approximately $102,315 before the $25,000 buffer. Sales growth cannot cure that funding gap unless collection terms or available capital improve.

Make access authorization and output quality measurable cost controls. Before estimating delivery hours, identify the client's system owner, permitted datasets, service accounts, retention requirements, and restrictions on subprocessors. Where California Civil Code 1798.100(d) applies, the relevant service-provider or contractor contract must address specified processing purposes and the required privacy obligations. Price those duties rather than attaching them after a fixed-fee sale. Use scoped OAuth permissions, a secrets manager, redacted operational logs, and per-customer storage boundaries; retain approval evidence without copying credentials into project documents. Consequential decisions require an assigned human reviewer and a funded exception queue. A loan application summarizer should route uncertain extraction to review rather than silently deciding eligibility. Measure field-level errors against a maintained evaluation set and charge expected review minutes to the delivery budget. Performance advertising also has an economic control: 15 U.S.C. 45 prohibits deceptive practices, so claims about saved hours or increased conversions need retained measurement evidence. If evidence weakens, narrow the claim and adjust the forecast. A fabricated efficiency claim can generate both acquisition waste and expensive remediation.

Close the operating ledger every Friday and authorize owner distributions only against reconciled cash and remaining obligations. Begin with customer-level earned fees, then accrue completed contractor work even when an invoice has not arrived. Import usage charges, reconcile settlement holds, inspect overdue receivables, and update the remediation schedule. Maintain separate views for recognized operating profit, unrestricted bank cash, and forward cash commitments; none substitutes for another. The published $2,500-$18,000 monthly opex range should not be read as total spending at every scale: this waterfall implies average total deductions of approximately $6,667, $32,083, and $50,000 monthly, including variable delivery and reserves. For Base, the $75,000 annual fixed-overhead line averages $6,250 monthly, while the total expense load is much larger. Build a thirteen-week forecast with signed sprint starts, expected milestone acceptance dates, actual contractor payment terms, payroll dates, and service-credit expiry. Keep owner withdrawals outside operating expenses and display them as cash distributions. When the forecast breaches the required buffer, collect overdue invoices, renegotiate prospective terms, or defer new delivery commitments. Do not repair the spreadsheet by recognizing unearned deposits or borrowing client advertising principal.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Reconcile earned production
   - Match every recognized sprint and support account-month to its delivery record.
   - Tie acceptance evidence and service periods to invoice identifiers; separate advances, taxes, and client principal.
2. Accrue the entire delivery obligation
   - Book completed contractor work, usage, review labor, and incident exposure.
   - Check that contractor invoices, employee allocations, and remediation charges do not duplicate one another.
3. Refresh unrestricted cash availability
   - Rebuild the thirteen-week forecast using payment and settlement dates.
   - Show processor holds, payroll runs, customer obligations, compute-credit expiry, and available funding separately.
4. Authorize commitments from the stressed residual
   - Compare the combined downside case with cash buffers before distributing earnings.
   - Document the distribution amount, remaining obligations, next delivery commitments, and corrective action for any shortfall.
