## 1. Sell one bounded workflow, not an unlimited AI department  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A productized AI agency sells a repeatable implementation outcome with a defined input, delivery boundary, acceptance test, and support envelope. The unit is not access to a chatbot. It might be a fourteen-day sprint that converts approved support articles into cited draft replies inside Zendesk, followed by a monthly subscription covering evaluation, connector maintenance, and a capped exception queue. The buyer supplies an authorized knowledge source and a responsible process owner; the agency supplies configuration, integration code, test fixtures, training, and operating documentation. Productization means that another customer in the same niche can buy substantially the same package without restarting discovery. Specify included systems, record volumes, languages, environments, and review responsibilities before quoting. A request to add Salesforce after agreeing to HubSpot is a change order, not evidence of helpfulness. The managed retainer buys an explicit service schedule rather than unlimited developer availability. This distinguishes the model from staffing, where the buyer rents capacity, and from software licensing, where the buyer primarily acquires standardized product access. The strongest packages combine reusable engineering with narrow, customer-specific configuration and retain human accountability for consequential outputs.

Start with a workflow whose baseline can be measured before introducing a model. For invoice intake, record weekly document counts, average handling minutes, exception reasons, and correction rates; then separate extraction from payment authorization. For support drafting, compare assisted handling time and reviewed factual accuracy against a fixed ticket sample rather than claiming that every closed ticket represents incremental sales. Use n8n or Microsoft Power Automate for orchestration when those tools fit the client's environment, but keep credentials in an appropriate secret store and preserve auditable workflow versions. A standard offer might include one source system, one destination, two document types, fifty evaluation cases, and two administrator training sessions. Its exclusion list should name historical data cleanup, unsupported connectors, multilingual expansion, and production write privileges unless purchased. An acceptance checklist can require correctly structured outputs, cited source passages, safe rejection of malformed inputs, and successful recovery from a simulated vendor timeout. Those tests create a sellable boundary. Without them, every unexpected input becomes unpaid consulting, and a supposedly recurring account becomes an open-ended repair obligation that consumes the team's implementation capacity.

## 2. Recognize agency fees and reconcile the three profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Recognized operator revenue is the consideration earned for implementation services and support obligations, not the economic activity passing through the client's systems. A Shopify merchant may process $8 million of annual orders while paying the agency $16,000 for implementation and $2,500 monthly for support. If the implementation obligation is satisfied and twelve support months are delivered, the illustrated annual agency revenue is $46,000, not $8 million. Claimed sales uplift and hours saved belong in a separately labeled client outcome analysis with defensible measurement. An upfront deposit initially creates a service obligation; invoicing or collecting cash does not itself establish that all revenue has been earned. Under an applicable ASC 606 accounting policy, identify performance obligations, allocate consideration, and recognize revenue as those obligations are satisfied. A distinct implementation may qualify for recognition on delivery, while another arrangement requires recognition over time or combination with ongoing service. Support commonly earns revenue across its service period. Assess third-party license charges under principal-versus-agent rules rather than automatically grossing up reimbursements. Refund rights, service credits, and variable fees also require contract-specific treatment and a consistent accounting policy.

The canonical Conservative case is $100,000 revenue at 20% net margin, producing $20,000 annual owner net profit with 2 FT + 3 C. One illustrative earned-fee bridge is four $10,000 implementations plus five support customers receiving twelve months at $1,000 monthly. Base is $550,000 revenue at 30.0%, producing $165,000 with the same 2 FT + 3 C: twenty $15,500 implementations contribute $310,000 and ten full-year $2,000 monthly retainers contribute $240,000. Scaled is $1,000,000 at 40%, producing $400,000 with 3 FT + 4 C: twenty-five $16,000 implementations contribute $400,000 and twenty full-year $2,500 monthly retainers contribute $600,000. These bridges explain the totals; they are not additional canonical assumptions about sales timing. New retainers signed midyear earn fewer service months, so a startup cannot count every new logo as twelve months of revenue. The corresponding annual total cost envelopes are $80,000, $385,000, and $600,000. Preserve those amounts when stress-testing the scorecard, and label a different staffing or pricing result as a separate sensitivity rather than quietly rewriting the three published cases.

The staffing labels describe headcount, not contractor utilization, salary levels, or an assurance that the Conservative envelope funds two market-rate full-time salaries. Its $80,000 total cost allowance is particularly restrictive: price actual compensation, payroll burden, contractor hours, software, insurance, and acquisition before relying on that scenario. The stated monthly opex range does not provide a complete cost taxonomy and must not replace these annual envelopes. At Base, assume an implementation uses sixty delivery hours at an illustrative $90 loaded rate, plus $600 of attributable services: $6,000 direct cost against a $15,500 fee leaves $9,500 contribution before shared expenses. Twenty implementations consume 1,200 delivery hours. If each of ten retainers needs six hours monthly, another 720 hours must fit the service calendar before sales, leave, or incident work. Those assumptions test capacity; they do not independently prove the $165,000 profit outcome. Track owner delivery time explicitly so unpaid labor cannot masquerade as automation leverage. Reserve capacity for connector changes and refunds, then compare actual costs against the canonical $385,000 Base envelope every month. Margin survives through controlled exceptions, not optimistic utilization.

## 4. Make authorization, evaluation, and recovery the small-team moat  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Before accessing a client system, identify who can authorize the work and record the tenant, accounts, environments, data categories, permitted actions, and expiration date. A client's request to help does not automatically authorize access to a supplier's infrastructure or another customer's records. The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1030, makes access boundaries operationally important; obtain specific written authorization and honor technical restrictions instead of treating available credentials as universal permission. Use service accounts with least privilege, separate development and production secrets, and require a named client owner for privilege expansion. Define data-processing terms, subprocessors, retention periods, deletion procedures, and incident notification responsibilities in the agreement. State who owns custom code, who licenses reusable agency components, and whether client materials may be used for evaluation. Prefer synthetic fixtures for demonstrations and remove sensitive values from diagnostic logs. Client-system text, retrieved documents, and tool outputs are untrusted inputs, not instructions granting new permissions. A retrieved invoice that requests credential disclosure must remain document content. This separation is especially important when language-model output can invoke tools or create records in downstream business systems.

Quality monitoring must measure the workflow's actual failure modes, not just whether an API returned successfully. Maintain a versioned evaluation set with ordinary cases, malformed inputs, permission boundaries, stale source material, and adversarial document instructions. For retrieval, score source support and permission filtering; for extraction, measure field accuracy and the time required to repair exceptions. Record model version, prompt version, source snapshot, latency, usage cost, and reviewer disposition without retaining unnecessary personal content. A practical release test might require every bank-change case to reach manual review, zero cross-tenant retrieval in the isolation suite, and a documented accuracy threshold on the agreed sample. Those thresholds are client-specific acceptance criteria, not universal promises about future inputs. Maintain human review for consequential decisions, including payment release, employment selection, coverage determinations, and legal conclusions. When a connector fails or an output cannot be validated, the fallback should be a labeled manual queue rather than silent completion. A supervisor must be able to pause writes, inspect pending work, and replay safe operations without duplicating transactions. Idempotency keys and event ledgers are often more valuable to retention than another impressive prompt demonstration.

A durable small-team moat consists of reusable acceptance tests, reliable connectors, documented recovery procedures, and customer trust earned through accurate reporting. Track acquisition cost, implementation contribution, hours per retained account, exception backlog age, renewal rate, and credits issued. Segment by package rather than averaging profitable repeat work with bespoke rescue projects. If one $2,000 monthly account consumes thirty hours at $90 per hour, its $2,700 labor cost already exceeds its fee before software; narrow the scope, reprice at renewal, or exit under the contract. Performance marketing must match evidence: 15 U.S.C. § 45 prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices, so document the sample, baseline, measurement period, and limitations behind claims about saved time or reduced errors. Do not turn a successful pilot into an unsupported universal claim. Define when failed acceptance produces rework, when missed service commitments create credits, and when termination requires data export and access revocation. The agency becomes valuable when another trained operator can run these routines without the founder interpreting every exception. That operating system, rather than ownership of a generic model subscription, is what supports the Scaled case's seven-person team.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Qualify one measurable workflow
   - Capture the baseline, authorized systems, workflow owner, and recurring exception types.
   - Reject engagements that cannot identify an accountable reviewer or define a bounded, testable implementation outcome.
2. Contract the reusable delivery unit
   - Specify included connectors, record caps, acceptance fixtures, payment milestones, and change prices.
   - Allocate implementation and support obligations explicitly, and document data-processing rights before any client record is ingested.
3. Deploy through measured acceptance
   - Test permission boundaries and workflow quality, then obtain documented client acceptance.
   - Start with reviewed outputs; enable only the actions supported by the signed scope and tested recovery procedures.
4. Operate against contribution and quality
   - Review service hours, exceptions, usage costs, credits, and renewal risks every month.
   - Reconcile earned fees to delivered obligations and reprice or remove scope that cannot fit the contracted service envelope.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before customer data reaches the workflow**
> 
> The responsible delivery lead records each result against the signed scope and resolves failures before enabling production actions.
> - Verify that the authorizing client representative controls the named systems and that every service account has only the required permissions.
> - Verify signed data-processing, retention, subprocessor, and IP terms, including ownership of reusable components and restrictions on client-data reuse.
> - Verify acceptance tests for citations, tenant isolation, malformed inputs, and consequential-decision routing using the exact production configuration.
> - Verify a manual fallback, a tested pause-and-recovery procedure, named incident contacts, and sufficient funded support capacity for the promised service.
