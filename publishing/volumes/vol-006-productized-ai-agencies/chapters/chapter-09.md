## 1. Establish access authority before connecting client systems  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start every sprint with an authority schedule, not a shared password. Name the client entity, authorized signer, system owner, permitted datasets, allowed operations, production window, and expiration date. The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1030, makes access boundaries material; possession of credentials does not establish permission to enter every connected account. Separate client authorization from platform permission: a merchant can authorize work on its Salesforce tenant but cannot authorize bypassing Google, Yelp, or another provider's access restrictions. Use OAuth scopes, dedicated service accounts, and an access register that records who can read, modify, export, or delete each object. For Zapier, Make, n8n, and model APIs, document which service receives the payload and whether logs retain content. Prefer synthetic fixtures during development and minimized fields during production. Require a named client reviewer before consequential employment, credit, health, or eligibility decisions are released. That reviewer needs source evidence, correction authority, and enough time to disagree; a checkbox after an automatic rejection does not create meaningful review. End access promptly when the engagement ends, and retain revocation evidence without retaining the client's underlying sensitive records.

Translate privacy obligations into a data-processing schedule with concrete fields: purpose, categories, retention, subprocessors, security controls, deletion method, incident contacts, and permitted secondary uses. California Civil Code § 1798.100(d) requires specified contractual protections when a covered business discloses personal information to service providers, contractors, or third parties. Classification depends on actual conduct and contract terms, not the label printed above a signature. Colorado Revised Statutes § 6-1-1305 separately specifies controller-processor duties and contractual requirements. Assess each state's coverage, exemptions, sensitive-data rules, and consumer-request duties before reusing a national template. A dental workflow involving protected health information can require a business associate agreement under 45 CFR §§ 164.502(e) and 164.504(e), plus appropriate downstream arrangements. A public medspa review does not authorize confirming that the reviewer received treatment. Recording discovery calls also needs a state-specific analysis; California Penal Code § 632 protects confidential communications and can require every party's consent. Record the participants' locations, announce recording, and offer an unrecorded alternative. Avoid storing raw call transcripts merely because transcription is inexpensive; assign a deletion deadline and identify the person responsible for carrying it out.

There is no general federal license simply to implement a business automation workflow, but the underlying activity may cross a licensed boundary. An agency configuring appointment reminders is different from diagnosing patients, advising on securities, adjusting insurance claims, or providing legal representation. Describe the deliverable as software configuration and documented operating support when that is what the team actually supplies. Do not rename regulated professional judgment as an AI recommendation to avoid the applicable rule. Employment deployments need particular care: Title VII, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2, remains relevant when a tool screens applicants, and New York City Administrative Code §§ 20-870 through 20-874 imposes additional requirements on covered automated employment decision tools. Human involvement does not automatically remove a tool from local coverage. Build a jurisdiction-and-use register covering the client's locations, affected people, data flows, and decision categories. Route clinical or other licensed judgments to the client's qualified professional rather than agency contractors. When the intended use changes from summarizing documents to ranking people, pause that function and reprice the assessment. The original discovery budget should not silently absorb a new regulated product category.

## 2. Price bounded obligations and allocate ownership explicitly  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use a master services agreement for recurring commercial terms and a statement of work for each bounded sprint. The statement should name inputs, connectors, output formats, volume limits, supported environments, acceptance tests, and exclusions. Replace 'automate customer support' with 'classify up to 2,000 monthly tickets into twelve agreed categories, draft responses, and route every draft to a client reviewer.' Define a measurable quality test using a client-approved evaluation set, including ambiguous examples and prohibited disclosures. Specify that changes to categories, source systems, languages, or autonomous actions require a priced change order. A fee trigger can be 50% at booking, 30% when the sandbox passes named tests, and 20% at production handoff; payment milestones and accounting recognition are separate questions. Give the client a defined acceptance window and a written method for identifying reproducible defects. Do not make silence waive hidden security defects or mandatory statutory rights. Support terms must distinguish response time from restoration time and identify excluded third-party outages. Document client dependencies, especially credential delivery and reviewer availability, so their delay moves the schedule rather than creating unpaid standby work.

Allocate intellectual property by category rather than writing 'client owns everything.' List preexisting agency libraries, reusable templates, client-specific configuration, client source data, third-party components, and generated outputs separately. Under 17 U.S.C. § 204(a), transfers of copyright ownership generally require a signed writing; contractor work should not be assumed to qualify automatically as work made for hire. Obtain contractor assignments where appropriate and give the client a durable license to embedded reusable components. Do not promise exclusive copyright in purely machine-generated material or rights the model provider cannot convey. Data terms should prohibit using client records to train unrelated products without a separate, specific agreement. Address subprocessors, export formats, deletion, and transition support at termination. Negotiate a liability cap tied to a defined fee period, with express treatment of confidentiality, intellectual-property claims, fraud, and liabilities that applicable law will not allow parties to limit. A dispute clause should identify governing law, forum, escalation timing, and whether arbitration applies. Narrow any anti-circumvention term to identified introductions and deliberate fee avoidance; exclude preexisting relationships and independent inbound contacts instead of claiming ownership over the client's entire market.

Keep scenario economics tied to implementation fees and support retainers. An illustrative Conservative mix is ten $6,000 sprints plus five $8,000 annual support packages: $60,000 + $40,000 = $100,000 revenue. At 20% net margin, annual costs total $80,000 and owner profit equals $20,000. Base can comprise twenty-five $14,000 sprints plus ten $20,000 annual retainers: $350,000 + $200,000 = $550,000 revenue, with $385,000 costs and $165,000 owner profit at 30.0%. Scaled can comprise forty $15,000 sprints plus twenty $20,000 annual retainers: $600,000 + $400,000 = $1,000,000 revenue, with $600,000 costs and $400,000 owner profit at 40%. The canonical teams remain 2 FT + 3 C for Conservative and Base, and 3 FT + 4 C for Scaled. Contractor counts describe roster size, not automatically full-time commitments. These are annualized illustrations, not evidence that five market-rate salaries fit the Conservative budget. Reconcile actual compensation, API charges, insurance, acquisition, rework, and support against the cost envelope before hiring. Client sales influenced by automation, carrier settlement receipts, and customer transaction principal never become agency revenue merely because the agency dashboard displays them.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Translate discovery into measurable scope
   - List each input, connector, output, volume ceiling, and excluded action.
   - Attach a small evaluation dataset with expected routing and failure behavior before quoting the sprint.
2. Price dependencies and exception work
   - Estimate delivery hours, API use, reviewer effort, support load, and rework.
   - Compare total engagement costs with the applicable annual scenario envelope and price additional obligations explicitly.
3. Execute rights and processing schedules
   - Obtain signatures covering access, data handling, asset ownership, fees, and acceptance.
   - Confirm contractor assignments and applicable downstream agreements before deploying client-specific assets.
4. Handoff with revocable access
   - Deliver test evidence, operating instructions, escalation contacts, and termination procedures.
   - Demonstrate rollback, credential revocation, data export, and deletion using the actual supported environment.

## 3. Turn reputation monitoring into a defensible service, not suppression  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play review_gating_reputation_extortion_funnels requires a firm redesign around legitimate monitoring. For medspas, dental implant practices, roofers, and auto dealers, monitor new one-star reviews on Google Business Profiles, Trustpilot, and Yelp only through permitted interfaces, licensed feeds, or authorized manual review. Do not evade rate limits or assume public visibility creates permission for unrestricted extraction. Offer timestamped alerts, review-velocity reporting, response drafts, and evidence-based platform-policy assessments. An alert within minutes is a measured service objective only where the authorized feed supports that latency; otherwise state the actual polling interval. The Federal Trade Commission Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45, prohibits deceptive practices, while 16 CFR Part 465 addresses fake reviews, specified incentive practices, and review suppression. Never condition a refund, service recovery, or payment on removing an honest negative review. Never threaten exposure, fabricate policy violations, or claim a special platform relationship. The Consumer Review Fairness Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45b, also restricts covered form-contract terms that prohibit or penalize honest consumer reviews. A B2B agency contract should require client cooperation with these operating boundaries rather than quietly funding prohibited tactics.

Apply the fallback precisely: provide equal post-service review invitations to all eligible customers, without filtering by satisfaction, and flag only genuine platform-policy-violating spam or other documented violations. Equal treatment alone is insufficient if the solicitation violates the destination platform's rules. Google prohibits incentivized reviews, and Yelp discourages businesses from soliciting reviews; use a neutral private-feedback channel where direct solicitation is unsuitable. Recheck current platform rules before activating each destination. A Google invitation can read: 'Thank you for choosing our team. If you would like to share your experience, you can leave an honest review using the review link in this message. Positive, mixed, and critical feedback are equally welcome. Participation is optional and does not affect your service.' The system supplies the verified business link and uses an authorized communication channel. Do not send happy customers to a public review page while diverting unhappy customers into a private form. For a disputed review, preserve the original text, URL, timestamp, policy provision, and supporting evidence. Request platform review without promising removal. A healthcare response should invite private contact without confirming patient status or discussing treatment details.

Make the unit economics support patient, evidence-led work rather than coercive urgency. An illustrative $500 monthly monitoring package across twenty locations produces $10,000 monthly service billings, or $120,000 annualized if retained for twelve months. Budget $80 per location for authorized data and messaging, $100 for human review, and $70 for account operations: $250 direct cost per location and $5,000 monthly contribution before acquisition and shared overhead. This is an optional service-line illustration inside an agency portfolio, not an addition to the canonical scorecard or an owner-profit forecast. At sixty alerts monthly and twelve minutes of review per alert, the team needs twelve reviewer hours before escalations. If volume doubles, the contract must specify additional capacity pricing or a revised response target. The fallback is a lower-frequency monitoring and neutral-feedback service, not automated mass reporting. Measure alert latency, evidence quality, response turnaround, and client retention. Do not sell predicted star-rating increases or recovered sales without defensible measurement. For any performance claim, retain the baseline, measurement window, sample size, exclusions, and attribution limits. Never classify every negative reviewer as a competitor or every removed review as proof of agency-created revenue.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the margin by preserving evidence early**
> 
> Reputation operations become expensive when the team reconstructs facts after an angry client has already promised an outcome.
> - Store the exact platform-policy provision beside each proposed report so a second reviewer can distinguish spam from ordinary criticism.
> - Separate review-alert timestamps from response-draft timestamps; otherwise feed latency gets mistaken for contractor delay.
> - Set an included monthly investigation allowance and price evidence-heavy escalations before selling multi-location monitoring.
> - Maintain different templates for public replies, private service recovery, and platform reports; never reuse sensitive client facts across those channels.

## 4. Screen telecom economics and use evidence-based outreach scripts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play telecom_voice_traffic_pumping_8yy_sms_arbitrage describes revenue-sharing CLECs, international Tier-2/3 switches, free conference bridges, AI voice lines, radio call-in utilities, and OTP or A2P endpoints generating very large termination volumes. Treat that description as a high-risk screening case, not an implementation recipe. FCC access-stimulation rules include 47 CFR § 61.3(bbb) and § 51.914; ordinary customer demand does not automatically exempt a revenue-sharing arrangement. Require the carrier to identify the applicable service classification, settlement basis, access-stimulation analysis, and responsible contracting entities. An agency supplying software should not casually become the telecommunications provider of record. Depending on the actual service, federal authorizations, state public-utility obligations, registration, contributions, and other carrier duties may apply. Toll-free and messaging economics require separate analysis; voice attestation cannot validate SMS consent. Do not build artificial call generation, OTP abuse, or routing intended to obscure responsibility. The suggested fallback of distributing traffic across jurisdictions does not cure an unlawful arrangement. Replace it with a genuine user-requested utility funded by transparent subscriptions or documented service fees, using providers authorized for the actual routes and retaining attributable demand records.

Use simple economics to expose dependence on questionable settlements. At 20 million voice minutes monthly and an assumed $0.002 net settlement spread, the headline contribution is $40,000 before disputes, transport, fraud losses, and operating costs. A $0.002 change eliminates that entire spread. Those receipts belong to the relevant telecom operator, not automatically to the implementation agency. A safer illustrative agency engagement charges $12,000 for a bounded utility deployment plus $1,500 monthly support, producing $30,000 first-year fees over twelve support months. At $8,000 implementation cost and $800 monthly support cost, direct costs are $17,600 and contribution is $12,400 before shared overhead. Keep that illustration inside the canonical portfolio totals. Require genuine human initiation for the utility and verified caller identity where applicable. STIR/SHAKEN under 47 CFR § 64.6301 addresses caller-ID authentication duties for covered voice providers; it does not prove consent, lawful content, or lawful compensation. Use provider-documented attestation handling rather than inventing an agency certification. If carrier economics fail, retain the customer's legitimate workflow through subscription billing and ordinary metered transport, or discontinue the uneconomic feature. Never repair margin by manufacturing traffic or bypassing provider controls.

Keep outbound acquisition separate from deployed client messaging. Commercial email, including B2B outreach, is subject to CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. § 7704: use accurate headers and subjects, applicable advertising identification, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out. Honor opt-outs within ten business days and supervise contractors sending on the agency's behalf. The scripts below are message bodies; production email must append the agency's verified postal address and functioning unsubscribe mechanism. Do not invent an address to make a template look complete. Before calls or texts, classify the technology, number type, purpose, recipient location, and applicable consent requirements under 47 U.S.C. § 227 and 47 CFR § 64.1200, alongside state restrictions. A published business number is not blanket permission for an AI-voice campaign. Artificial or prerecorded voice rules can apply independently of whether a system is an automatic telephone dialing system. Log the precise consent language, number, timestamp, source, and revocation status when consent is required. A discovery conversation should establish authority, baseline workload, permitted data, and measurable acceptance before discussing savings. Use named deliverables and observed facts instead of invented urgency, removal promises, or carrier-income claims the agency cannot substantiate.
