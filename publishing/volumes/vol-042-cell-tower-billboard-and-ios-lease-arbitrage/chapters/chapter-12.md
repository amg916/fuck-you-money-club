## 1. Days 1-7: Build the rights ledger and choose one primary lane  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin with a $24,000 funded pilot, an operating bank account, and a parcel-level evidence register rather than three simultaneous acquisitions. That funding assumption sits inside Candidate #42's $12,000-$60,000 startup range and supports a realistic two-to-six-month first-deal cycle. Reserve $6,000 for at least two months of verified fixed obligations; increase the reserve if those obligations exceed $3,000 monthly. Authorize no more than $6,000 of nonrefundable spending through Day 30, including contractor invoices, subscriptions, travel, and diligence. A founder starting with $12,000 cannot adopt later spending ceilings automatically because the same reserve leaves only $6,000 deployable. The principal owns negotiation, underwriting, and capital decisions. Contractor one handles GIS and ownership research; contractor two is transaction counsel; contractor three coordinates site, environmental, and yard-operations diligence. Specialist surveys and environmental reports remain separately priced vendor purchases within the budget, rather than hidden additions to the standing pod. Use Airtable for the register, QGIS for spatial screening, and restricted Google Drive folders for evidence. Every record needs a parcel identifier, source date, owner entity, proposed right, counterparty, next action, spending authorization, and expiration. A prospect without a verifiable right stays outside the qualified pipeline.

Use the following eleven-play decomposition as this chapter's execution mapping, not as a claim that a missing source catalog supplied these exact labels. P01 sources existing tower-ground easement opportunities; P02 sources rooftop telecom rights; P03 underwrites perpetual easement buyouts; P04 packages institutional telecom exits. P05 screens highway billboard corridors; P06 negotiates sign-ground control; P07 verifies digital conversion and permit transfer paths; P08 packages billboard assignments. P09 sources heavy-vehicle IOS parcels; P10 structures conditional NNN master-leases; P11 preleases and operates unmanned fleet yards. These are connected workflows, not eleven independent revenue streams, and one opportunity must never appear as separate revenue under each workflow. By Day 3, research thirty records per asset lane, producing ninety screened records with source links and duplicate checks. FCC Antenna Structure Registration records help identify certain registered structures but neither establish ownership of lease income nor enumerate every rooftop installation. County deeds, tax records, recorded easements, and owner-provided leases complete the telecom inquiry. For billboards, combine state DOT annual average daily traffic layers with controlled-route maps and local sign ordinances. For IOS, intersect zoning districts, truck routes, parcel dimensions, drainage information, and practical turning access before requesting owner documents.

By Day 7, select one primary lane using verified ownership, reachable decision-makers, permission complexity, and available exit buyers. Maintain the other two lanes as research queues with no speculative deposits. Score each opportunity on five binary tests: identifiable grantor, documented existing or permissible use, plausible transferable rights, named downstream demand, and affordable diligence. Require four passing tests before owner outreach becomes a priority, while any confirmed prohibition overrides the score. A desktop-qualified opportunity additionally needs written source support for use and a preliminary spread calculation; an attractive aerial image does not qualify. Contact at least twenty distinct owner decision-makers during the first week and log delivery failures separately from successful delivery. Count a substantive conversation only when the decision-maker addresses rights, timing, price, or willingness to share documents. Counsel must evaluate the selected state's licensing rules before outreach scripts promise transaction services or success compensation. Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1101, for example, distinguishes brokerage activity from applicable exemptions; acting as principal does not excuse every activity surrounding a transaction. Finish the week with a signed contractor scope, a dated licensing analysis, ninety screened records, and a primary-lane decision explaining which assumptions still require evidence.

## 2. Days 8-14: Convert interest into conditional control and defensible unit math  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Week two replaces owner enthusiasm with documents that can support conditional control. Ask telecom owners for the executed lease, amendments, rent ledger, recorded easements, mortgage information, and correspondence concerning termination or assignment. For rooftop rights, separate access, equipment space, utility routing, structural responsibility, and roof-replacement obligations rather than purchasing an ambiguous income description. For billboards, obtain the ground lease, permit number, sign inventory record, display dimensions, digital-conversion history, and both state and municipal requirements. Under 23 U.S.C. 131 and applicable state implementation, a commercially attractive highway location can still fail spacing, zoning, or permit requirements. For IOS, request current zoning confirmation, permitted-use conditions, drainage documents, historic uses, utility information, and proposed vehicle classes. Use a written request list with a responsible party and response date for every missing document. By Day 14, target forty distinct delivered owner contacts, eight substantive conversations, and four desktop-qualified files across the pilot, not forty contacts in each lane. Send two nonbinding offers only after counsel confirms the transaction structure and the principal identifies a credible downstream counterparty. These cumulative targets protect the four-person pod from producing documents for opportunities whose sellers cannot convey the proposed rights.

Underwrite the purchased right rather than the property's headline value. For a telecom illustration, verified transferable annual rent of $24,000 acquired at sixteen times rent costs $384,000. A funded buyer indication at nineteen times rent implies $456,000 of exit proceeds, leaving $72,000 before transaction deductions. If title, counsel, seller adjustments, and other directly attributable deductions total $18,000, the illustrated net buyout spread is $54,000 before remaining operator overhead. The candidate's sixteen-to-twenty-two-times framing is a sensitivity range, not evidence of an available buyer or a universal valuation convention. A $24,000 startup account cannot finance the $384,000 purchase; use an assignable option or a separately funded closing structure with documented settlement mechanics. Require a buyer's written pricing basis and funding evidence before describing the exit as financeable. Model rent escalators, termination provisions, renewal control, taxes, closing delay, and discount-rate sensitivity separately. A nominal perpetual easement can carry income that ends with a tenant cancellation, so duration of title and duration of cash flow must not be conflated. The preliminary screen requires at least $20,000 of projected transaction contribution after all expected deal costs and a nonnegative stressed outcome.

Use equally explicit mechanics for billboard assignments and IOS spreads. A billboard buyer offering a $30,000 assignment fee against $8,000 of operator-paid entitlement and transaction costs creates $22,000 of projected contribution before shared overhead; require a transferable interest and documented permit procedure before counting it. Do not label an unissued permit an asset that the seller already owns. For an illustrative IOS yard, ten committed fleet spaces at $750 monthly produce $7,500 of tenant billings. Subtract $4,000 of master rent and $1,000 of operator-borne direct yard costs to obtain a $2,500 monthly retained spread before shared overhead. Screen IOS for at least $2,000 monthly retained spread and eighty percent of usable capacity under credible conditional tenant commitments before rent commencement. Stress twenty percent lower receipts and fifteen percent higher master rent and direct costs; this example retains $250 monthly before overhead. The cushion is thin enough to prohibit an unconditional lease without stronger commitments or renegotiated terms. Counsel should draft diligence access, assignment rights, financing conditions, landlord and lender consent requirements, and termination dates expressly. Promises of later reimbursement never substitute for a funded closing statement, and refundable deposits remain unavailable cash until their contractual release.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the exact right
   - Match the proposed acquisition or assignment to executed documents and the grantor's authority.
   - Record the document page supporting income, duration, access, assignment, and termination; unresolved conflicts remain open diligence items.
2. Price the transferable economics
   - Calculate the spread using documented buyer pricing or tenant commitments and explicit direct costs.
   - Run the twenty-percent receipts reduction and fifteen-percent cost increase without treating underlying property value as operator revenue.
3. Limit the commitment
   - Use counsel-drafted conditions, diligence access, expiration dates, and deposit-release mechanics.
   - Track every refundable deposit as encumbered cash and every nonrefundable fee against the cumulative spending ceiling.
4. Approve the evidence package
   - Have the principal sign a dated decision record before issuing the offer.
   - List the buyer or tenant, funding path, missing consent, maximum loss, next deadline, and person responsible for resolving each dependency.

## 3. Days 15-21: Resolve the fatal defects before activating obligations  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The third week tests whether the seller's proposed interest survives title, permitting, and physical diligence. Obtain a title commitment or an appropriately scoped title search, then have counsel identify the interests that could defeat the acquired right. A subordination, nondisturbance, and attornment agreement addresses particular priority and recognition issues; it is not interchangeable with landlord assignment consent or lender consent to a separate conveyance. Record each required instrument, signatory, delivery deadline, and closing dependency independently. For telecom, verify that access and utility rights remain usable after a sale or foreclosure and that tenant termination provisions match the valuation model. For billboards, distinguish existing lawful operation, proposed digital conversion, permit transfer, and ground-lease assignment. A state permit does not erase a local restriction, and municipal support does not establish state highway eligibility. Ask the responsible offices for written process guidance rather than extrapolating from a neighboring sign. By Day 21, the primary-lane file should contain a rights checklist, a dated consent log, and either a curable defect with a priced remedy or a recorded rejection. Pause the affected parcel when evidence contradicts the model; keep researching other prospects without advancing money into the defective file.

IOS deserves its own physical and operating workstream because a low-building strategy can still acquire substantial environmental and pavement exposure. Start with historical aerials, agency records, observed uses, drainage patterns, and a site visit, then commission a properly scoped Phase I environmental site assessment before executing the master-lease. Where federal liability protections are relevant, counsel and the environmental professional should address the applicability and timing of all appropriate inquiries under 40 CFR Part 312. A Phase I report alone does not resolve every tenant liability or operational condition. Determine whether proposed industrial activity triggers stormwater permitting under 40 CFR 122.26 and the applicable delegated state program. Confirm heavy-vehicle use, outdoor storage, operating hours, surfacing, fire access, fencing, lighting, and vehicle turning requirements through the appropriate authorities. Measure usable capacity after setbacks, drainage areas, and circulation lanes rather than billing theoretical acreage. Have the site contractor obtain priced remedies for any access, drainage, or surfacing defect and incorporate them into the spread calculation. Do not commence rent while waiting for a necessary use authorization. A landlord's statement that trucks previously parked there is evidence to investigate, not a substitute for the permission required for the planned fleet operation.

Demand validation now becomes a documented exit test rather than an informal market survey. For P04 and P08, send a controlled buyer package showing the exact transferable interest, economics, exceptions, and requested closing sequence. Count a qualified buyer response only when an identified authorized representative supplies written asset-specific pricing or a bounded pricing method, conditions, and credible funding information. For P11, obtain tenant letters naming vehicle counts, monthly rates, start dates, required access, and cancellation conditions. A conditional letter supports demand testing but is not collected revenue or a substitute for an executed occupancy agreement. Demonstrate RFID or license-plate access using a test account before buying permanent gate equipment. Document emergency entry, credential revocation, exception handling, payment reconciliation, and a human escalation contact. Stripe Billing can schedule recurring charges, while the yard ledger must still reconcile occupied spaces, master rent, recoveries, and deposits separately. Cap active deep-diligence files at three, with no more than one environmental report underway, so the principal can actually review exceptions. At the weekly review, require a specific buyer objection or tenant requirement for every active file. If nobody will name acceptable terms, move that file back to research instead of disguising silence as demand.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before an obligation becomes irreversible**
> 
> A passing pipeline metric never overrides a defective right, missing permission, or unfunded obligation.
> - Rights check: counsel identifies the exact grantor, transferable interest, assignment restrictions, mortgage priority, and required lender or landlord instruments.
> - Permission check: billboard state and local requirements are documented; IOS environmental screening, heavy-axle use, access, and stormwater applicability are resolved before lease execution.
> - Economics check: telecom and billboard contribution reaches $20,000 after deal costs, or IOS retained spread reaches $2,000 monthly, with nonnegative stressed contribution.
> - Funding check: purchase funding and settlement mechanics are documented, deposits are segregated in the cash model, and protected reserves survive the maximum contractual exposure.

## 4. Days 22-30: Reconcile the ledger and apply Day 30/60/90 decisions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The final nine days turn a promising pipeline into an auditable operating decision. By Day 30, target sixty delivered owner contacts, twelve substantive conversations, six desktop-qualified files, three conditional offers, and one executed conditional control agreement. These are proposed pilot thresholds, not historical conversion rates, and they apply cumulatively across the pod's selected primary lane and retained prospects. Control means an executed option or agreement with enforceable rights and explicit conditions, not an unsigned letter or owner expression of interest. Reconcile every invoice and bank transaction to a file, distinguishing operating expense, direct deal cost, refundable deposit, and restricted reserve. The initial $6,000 ceiling allocates $1,800 to counsel and title, $1,000 to GIS and ownership research, $1,700 to site and environmental work, $500 to systems and travel, and $1,000 to contingency. If a defensible environmental scope exceeds the allocation, defer that parcel or obtain separately funded authorization without breaching the total ceiling. Do not quietly reduce the scope of a professional report to fit the model. The principal signs a Day 30 decision memo containing actual funnel counts, unresolved defects, maximum exposure, and the next funded milestone. No closing is required merely to satisfy an arbitrary first-month deadline.

Keep the scorecard separate from the sprint ledger. Formula v2.0 specifies $120,000 of annual operator revenue at twenty-eight percent margin for $33,600 of owner profit; $535,000 at forty percent for $214,000; and $950,000 at fifty-two percent for $494,000. The corresponding annual cost envelopes are $86,400, $321,000, and $456,000. Those costs include more than the candidate's $2,000-$12,000 monthly operating-overhead range, so do not equate total annual costs with fixed monthly overhead. The scorecard's gross-revenue label refers here to its defined operator fee-and-spread basis, excluding institutional acquisition principal and underlying property value. Track telecom buyout spreads, billboard assignment fees, and retained IOS spreads consistently; gross tenant billings and refundable deposits do not become additional scorecard revenue. For the IOS example, annual tenant billings are $90,000, while the modeled retained spread is $30,000 before shared overhead. Do not deduct master rent twice after using that retained-spread basis. Actual external presentation under ASC 606 depends on the principal-versus-agent assessment, while lease arrangements also require the applicable lease-accounting analysis. The supplied scorecard contains no numerical retained_share parameter, so derive each contribution from documented inflows and deductions instead of inventing one. Keep an explicit reconciliation between management economics and the accountant's reporting treatment.

Apply Kill, Fix, or Scale in that order, with fatal rights defects overriding attractive funnel counts. Kill normally means closing a defective parcel or an unproductive lane, not abandoning viable unrelated opportunities; stop the entire pilot if reserves or contractual solvency fail. Scale means releasing one bounded tranche and repeating the proven workflow, not immediately hiring the scaled team or multiplying rent exposure. Keep the initial one-full-time-plus-three-contractor pod until recurring work and collected contribution justify expansion. Day 60 cumulative nonrefundable spending cannot exceed $10,000, and Day 90 cannot exceed $14,000, always subject to available unrestricted cash after deposits and the protected reserve. A Fix authorization is included inside those ceilings, never added above them. Maintain recurring pilot overhead near $2,000 monthly and reassess runway whenever a new obligation is proposed. By Day 90, require an executable exit package with identified funding, priced remaining conditions, and a documented path to closing within another ninety days. This accommodates the candidate's two-to-six-month first-deal window without allowing indefinite option collecting. Use one dated repair cycle per failed checkpoint, retain the original baseline, and prohibit resetting the clock by renaming a prospect. The table's Scale tests are conjunctive; falling short leads to Fix only when a bounded, funded remedy remains credible.
