## 1. Map eleven sourcing routes around verified exit demand  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with the exit, not a countywide owner blast. Select one freight corridor and its adjoining telecom and outdoor-advertising markets, then interview five infrastructure buyers, three billboard operators, and ten fleet dispatchers. Ask each buyer for acquisition geography, minimum remaining term, assignment restrictions, tenant concentration limits, and required closing documents. Ask fleet operators for actual overnight tractor counts, trailer dwell, access hours, turning dimensions, and their current cost per usable space. Record requests as dated demand briefs, with a named decision maker and an expiry date for pricing assumptions. A dispatcher describing congestion is a research source; a fleet manager accepting a specified yard rate and move date is a qualified prospect. Keep these categories separate in Airtable or a spreadsheet. The eleven routes below are this chapter's mapped sourcing plays, not eleven independent businesses to launch simultaneously. Choose one primary rail and one adjacent rail sharing the same geography. The opening objective is three independently verified demand briefs before paying for extensive title work or site engineering. If buyers refuse to specify a buy box, continue interviews rather than manufacturing inventory from public records. That sequence protects the $12,000–$60,000 startup budget from attractive parcels with no practical exit.

For telecom, download available FCC Antenna Structure Registration records, preserve registration identifiers, and spatially join coordinates to county parcel boundaries using QGIS. ASR identifies registered structures; it does not enumerate every rooftop installation or establish who owns the income stream. Cross-check assessor ownership, recorder easements, building permits, and the landlord's actual lease. Prioritize fragmented ownership, recent estate transfers, and leases whose access or assignment language can be clarified without impairing the carrier's operations. For billboards, combine state DOT outdoor-advertising inventories, AADT counts, municipal sign codes, and surveyed road geometry. A high traffic count does not create a digital conversion entitlement: spacing, lighting, interchange restrictions, and local sign prohibitions can destroy the apparent spread. For IOS, start with industrial parcels near documented dispatch bottlenecks, then inspect legal truck access, usable paved or stabilized area, drainage, and neighboring uses. Satellite imagery is a screening layer, never evidence of lawful storage. Create one immutable parcel identifier and attach each source's retrieval date. Resolve boundary ambiguity before mailing an owner, especially where a tower, access road, or proposed sign straddles separate tax parcels. A wrong-party conversation wastes scarce outreach capacity and can produce a worthless option.

Underwrite the rights you can convey, not the landmark you can photograph. Suppose an assignable telecom income interest produces $24,000 annually: a 16× acquisition costs $384,000, while a buyer accepting 18× would pay $432,000. The $48,000 difference is a preliminary spread before transaction costs, not $432,000 of operator revenue. A 22× exit is only a sensitivity until a buyer prices the actual tenant, term, escalators, and remedies. With startup capital far below the purchase price, negotiate conditional assignment rights or arrange a documented simultaneous closing; never assume the seller will finance an unfunded purchase. An illustrative billboard assignment paying $35,000 against $15,000 of documented project acquisition and direct costs leaves $20,000 on the scorecard fee basis. An IOS yard collecting $11,000 monthly against $6,000 of underlying rent leaves $5,000 before operator overhead and any unrecovered site expenses. Confirm who bears taxes, insurance, repairs, and stormwater costs rather than treating NNN language as an expense eraser. If assignment fails, fall back to an expressly permitted option extension or release the site. If the only remaining activity is arranging another party's transaction, verify the applicable brokerage authority before pursuing a fee.

## 2. Turn public signals into qualified opportunities without inventing intent  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Implement fmcsa_dot_inspection_trigger_arbitrage as a fleet-demand research workflow, not an assertion that every new carrier needs a yard. Schedule a daily job to check authorized FMCSA Census, Licensing & Insurance, and public SMS resources for new source versions; ingest only what each source actually publishes. Census extracts and SMS products have their own update schedules, and SMS measures are generally refreshed monthly. Do not describe a monthly snapshot as a live roadside violation feed or bypass restrictions on inspection details. Store USDOT number, relevant docket identifier, source date, authority status, operating geography, fleet size, and a reason for research. Deduplicate by USDOT number before resolving a business contact. Newly granted authority and recently applied-for authority are different events. The often-repeated mandatory 21-day insurance-filing window is not a universal federal deadline; verify application processing, protest timing, insurance filings, and activation requirements separately against the carrier's actual record. A fresh HOS or maintenance issue may justify asking about parking or inspection space, but neither proves that a yard solves the violation. Never imply that FMCSA selected your company, that your service activates authority, or that purchasing parking satisfies federal operating requirements.

Make the trigger economically testable. In an illustrative weekly batch, review 100 source changes, retain 30 carriers operating inside the selected corridor, identify 12 with a plausible parking constraint, and seek six held discovery calls. If research and outreach cost $600, acquisition expense is $100 per held call; if only three calls occur, it doubles. One resulting tenant taking 20 spaces at $175 monthly generates $3,500 of yard billings, not $3,500 of retained profit. Allocate underlying rent and incremental servicing costs before comparing that tenant with other demand sources. Use a transparent private compliance-agency identity only when that accurately describes the business, and otherwise identify the actual private yard operator. Offer a free BOC-3/MCS-150 status-check worksheet linking to official records, explaining that process-agent designation and registration updates are separate obligations. Collect contact details through a voluntary callback request rather than suggesting government affiliation. When public inspection detail is unavailable, use inbound hand-raisers, dispatch interviews, and carrier-requested location searches. Do not substitute scraped driver identities or sensitive incident narratives. Track both accepted and rejected hypotheses so that a weak trigger loses budget instead of becoming permanent campaign folklore.

Score every property against six evidence dimensions totaling 100 points: verified demand 25, legal control 20, use feasibility 20, economic spread 15, counterparty authority 10, and timing 10. An 80-point threshold earns paid diligence only if every fatal defect has been resolved or made an explicit, enforceable closing condition. A high aggregate cannot compensate for prohibited truck storage, a nontransferable sign permit, or a seller lacking authority. For IOS, confirm the specific use with the planning department, investigate environmental conditions through an appropriately scoped Phase I, and determine industrial stormwater exposure under the applicable permitting program. An environmental report does not itself authorize discharge or cure historic contamination. For telecom, reconcile the rent schedule with actual receipts and request lender subordination, non-disturbance, and attornment terms where necessary. For billboards, document state and municipal requirements independently under the Highway Beautification Act framework. Save the negative answer and its source alongside the attractive economics. Assign one owner to clear each exception, with a specific deadline and maximum expense. Freeze further spending when a dependency slips beyond the option period; negotiate an extension before ordering additional reports.

## 3. Run a measured contact cadence and answer objections with evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate owner outreach from buyer recruitment because their reasons to respond differ. An owner receives a specific observation about a verified asset and a proposed next conversation; a buyer receives a concise fit statement against an already documented acquisition brief. For a tower landlord, write that you purchase defined lease-income interests and would like to review remaining term, rent, and assignment provisions. For a billboard landowner, identify the parcel and explain that any proposal depends on permit transferability and local authorization. For a fleet, lead with location, measured spaces, operating hours, surface, and the proposed monthly rate, then ask whether those particulars solve an existing dispatch problem. Avoid invented urgency, guaranteed proceeds, and references to private circumstances inferred from public filings. Use business email, individually researched calls, postal letters, and permission-based introductions. Maintain suppression across every channel. Commercial email must satisfy 15 U.S.C. 7704, including accurate headers, required sender information, and a functioning opt-out. Telephone campaigns must account for 47 U.S.C. 227 and applicable state restrictions; a published number is not blanket permission for automated texts. Start manually so the operator can hear objections before automating a flawed message.

Use six purposeful touches over 21 days, stopping immediately after an opt-out and changing the next step when the recipient replies. The first message establishes parcel relevance; the second tests whether you reached the right person; the third supplies one useful observation or document request. Later touches offer a defined review slot, address the actual objection, and close the loop. Do not send six versions of an empty follow-up. If an owner says the price is too low, show the income, term, buyer multiple, direct costs, and retained spread assumptions; ask which input they dispute. If they refuse exclusive control, propose a short, clearly bounded conditional option only where that structure is lawful and useful. If a fleet says the yard is expensive, compare usable spaces and route miles rather than discounting an unmeasured parcel. If a lender delay blocks a tower transaction, request a dated consent workplan and adjust the option period instead of promising a faster closing. An objection is a request for evidence or a genuine rejection. Record the difference, and stop working a prospect whose stated requirements cannot fit the economics.

Publish inbound pages only for inventory characteristics you can substantiate: a corridor parking brief, an infrastructure income-rights document checklist, and a billboard feasibility intake form. A yard page should distinguish available inventory from locations being researched, with no fabricated availability. Ask for vehicle count, dimensions, arrival date, required access hours, and preferred contact method; avoid collecting driver credentials or unnecessary personal information. Send a useful response within one business day and route qualified requests to the same CRM stages as outbound prospects. Track source, message version, cohort date, contact outcome, qualified demand, site review, signed control, and collected proceeds. As an illustrative planning benchmark, 500 raw records might yield 150 desk-qualified prospects, 60 substantive conversations, 24 demand or acquisition briefs, 12 site packs, six signed conditional controls, and three completed monetizations. These are working targets, not observed market averages. The respective stage conversions are 30%, 40%, 40%, 50%, 50%, and 50%. Measure owner-side and buyer-side funnels separately before joining them at a matched site. Review results weekly by cohort; a large list cannot compensate for unresolved use restrictions or buyers who never accepted the price.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Assemble a source-dated lead batch
   - Research 100 records inside one corridor and deduplicate property and carrier identifiers.
   - Preserve public source dates, ownership evidence, contact provenance, and the specific reason each record entered the batch.
2. Qualify before beginning the cadence
   - Apply the six-part scorecard and select prospects with a plausible buyer or tenant match.
   - Exclude fatal use defects and distinguish tentative demand from written price, capacity, and timing requirements.
3. Convert replies into document-backed commitments
   - Run the six-touch sequence, then request a site review or precisely scoped conditional agreement.
   - Use lawful principal transaction structures or properly authorized brokerage arrangements; do not relabel brokerage work to avoid licensing requirements.
4. Reconcile the cohort and release weak opportunities
   - Review conversions, elapsed days, cash spent, outstanding conditions, and collected economic spread weekly.
   - Keep successful message variants, stop weak triggers, and terminate or extend expiring control before new diligence costs accrue.

## 4. Land the first wins without confusing signatures, principal, and profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Sequence the first 2–6 months around dependencies rather than a promised closing date. During weeks one and two, choose the corridor, interview buyers, configure source records, and produce three usable demand briefs. During weeks three through six, research the first cohort, run manual outreach, and complete inexpensive document checks. During weeks seven through twelve, concentrate on the few sites with verified control, feasible use, and a buyer or tenant willing to review terms. Reserve months four through six for lender responses, permit transfers, environmental work, and negotiated closings where needed. Track a signed mandate, conditional option, completed assignment, and occupied master lease as separate events. Three-to-ten signed wins can include conditional agreements, but a dashboard must never present those as three-to-ten paid transactions. A mandate counts only when the operator is authorized to provide the contracted service. Attach the actual conditions, termination rights, expiry date, and cash exposure to every agreement. If the first batch produces conversations but no document access, repair trust and specificity; if document access produces no viable sites, repair screening. Increasing message volume is the wrong response to defective inventory or unsupported buyer pricing.

Reconcile production targets to Formula v2.0 without treating underlying asset consideration as sales. The Conservative case is $120,000 annual scorecard revenue at 28%, yielding $33,600 owner net profit; the Base case is $535,000 at 40.0%, yielding $214,000; the Scaled case is $950,000 at 52%, yielding $494,000. The implied annual operating cost envelopes are $86,400, $321,000, and $456,000 respectively on that same economic basis. The illustrative production bridges below reconcile exactly but are not additional canonical assumptions. Their IOS components assume twelve productive months, so a yard opened halfway through the year earns only half that annual spread unless another transaction offsets the difference. Startup capital of $12,000–$60,000 and monthly baseline opex of $2,000–$12,000 are separate planning ranges; they do not replace the scorecard's full annual expense envelope. Keep deposits and acquisition principal on a distinct cash schedule. The scorecard uses retained transaction economics, not gross tenant collections or institutional purchase prices. Actual statement presentation requires contract-specific analysis, including ASC 606 principal-versus-agent treatment where applicable and ASC 842 for lease arrangements. Apply no invented retained-share percentage to figures already supplied on a retained basis.

Keep the operating team smaller than the opportunity list. At Conservative and Base, the prescribed structure is one full-time operator plus three contractors; at Scaled, it is two full-time operators plus four contractors. Assign the founder demand interviews, negotiations, and capital decisions, while contractors handle source research, specialist document review, and site diligence within defined scopes. A contractor slot is capacity planning, not permission for an unqualified person to perform regulated work. Automate source-version checks, parcel joins, consent-document assembly, and reminders before automating outbound volume. Install RFID access and recurring NNN billing only after the yard's lawful use, tenant obligations, and emergency access are settled. Every Friday, reconcile signed agreements to the cash ledger and list the next irreversible commitment. Require actual demand evidence before increasing fixed rent exposure. If 30 targeted conversations produce no acceptable price, revisit the corridor or offer; if counterparties agree on price but closings stall, fix consent packaging and control periods. The first repeatable win is not a lucky signature. It is an identifiable signal, a verified right, a documented counterparty need, and collected spread that survives all required costs.
