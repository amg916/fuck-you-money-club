## 1. Assign Fifteen Recovery Plays to Four Seats Before Adding Two  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Translate Candidate #8's 15 mapped operator plays into fifteen execution lanes for this chapter: creditor sourcing, judgment intake, docket screening, assignment verification, debtor identity resolution, lawful asset research, exemption screening, bankruptcy monitoring, lien-priority analysis, enforcement budgeting, attorney handoff, settlement administration, payment reconciliation, deadline renewal, and creditor reporting. These are working lane labels, not fifteen separate jobs. The baseline pod has one full-time owner and three contractors: a docket researcher, an asset-and-priority analyst, and independently retained enforcement counsel. Counsel remains responsible for legal judgments and representation within the engagement; the operator does not direct legal conclusions or sell unauthorized representation. Give every matter one owner, one stage, one next action, and one dated evidence packet. Airtable can hold workflow metadata, while a restricted document repository holds assignments, court orders, research provenance, and payment support. Keep sensitive identifiers out of general task descriptions. Separate a judgment's face amount from collectible assets and from the operator's fee entitlement. A large docket entry with a disputed assignment, exhausted collateral, or senior tax lien is a research exception, not productive inventory. Maintain a lane-to-seat register so no deadline depends on remembering which contractor last touched the file.

Buy contractor outputs rather than attendance. An illustrative research order pays $45 for an accepted docket packet containing the judgment, service history, last enforcement activity, and jurisdiction-specific renewal dates. An illustrative asset order pays $150 for a sourced identity-and-priority memorandum, with unresolved conflicts explicitly listed. These are purchasing assumptions to test locally, not market quotations or additions to the canonical profit cases. Require delivery within three business days after complete inputs, correction of attributable defects within two business days, and immediate escalation of a discovered bankruptcy or identity mismatch. Independently validate every proposed enforcement packet; sample routine research after acceptance and increase sampling when defects recur. Pay counsel under a separate engagement using locally permitted billing arrangements. Do not convert the research contractor into a percentage-paid legal intermediary or share attorney fees with a nonlawyer where prohibited. Contract labels alone do not establish independent status: review actual control, economic dependence, and applicable state tests with employment counsel. Track effective accepted-unit cost, including rework and supervision. Cheap packets become expensive when the owner spends an hour repairing identifiers or when a missed renewal destroys an otherwise collectible claim. Set purchase limits before issuing assignments, then reconcile accepted deliverables against invoices.

Scale to two full-time seats and four contractors by adding an operations lead and a reconciliation specialist, not another salesperson first. The owner retains creditor relationships, case economics, and exception decisions; the operations lead owns queues, service levels, evidence completeness, and contractor acceptance. The four contractors remain research, asset analysis, counsel, and reconciliation. Headcount describes seats, not four full-time contractor workloads. Counsel's capacity and conflicts must fit the chosen jurisdictions; narrow geographic intake rather than silently adding an unbudgeted legal bench. Use a six-week workload study before expansion: count accepted packets, owner exception hours, deadline backlog, payment exceptions, and realizable fees. Add the operations seat only when recurring contribution can fund its loaded cost through collection delays. The canonical startup capital range is $10,000–$75,000, and first-deal timing is 6–24 months, so apparent pipeline cannot substitute for liquidity. Treat the $2,000–$12,000 monthly operating-expense range as a planning input whose included categories must be reconciled with full scenario expenses. It cannot describe all scaled annual costs, which total $300,000. Cross-train the operations lead on creditor reporting and the owner on reconciliation review, while preserving independent approval of payment changes and disbursements.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect Scarce Owner Hours Before Buying More Leads**
> 
> A small recovery pod wins through clean evidence and selective case economics, not through accumulating impressive judgment balances.
> - Require the analyst to show senior claims beside each asset; gross property value without priority analysis misstates the collection opportunity.
> - Attach the source docket entry and calculation basis to every renewal date; a calendar reminder without its legal starting point is fragile.
> - Use a paid sample packet before a contractor receives a portfolio, and include a known identity collision to test escalation discipline.
> - Record the owner's repair minutes against each rejected packet; renegotiate or replace vendors using total accepted-unit cost rather than headline pricing.

## 2. Run a Weekly Evidence Scorecard That Cannot Reward Unsafe Volume  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a forty-five-minute weekly operating review from one frozen reporting extract. The owner chairs the baseline review; the operations lead prepares it after scaling. Report opening active matters, accepted intake, closures, blocked matters, and closing active matters so inventory reconciles. Divide completed on-time tasks by tasks actually due that week; do not improve performance by moving due dates after they pass. Define first-pass acceptance as packets accepted without material correction divided by packets delivered. Start with a 95% operating target, then calibrate against observed complexity rather than declaring it an industry benchmark. Track bankruptcy-check freshness, court-deadline coverage, payment-match aging, and fee contribution by cohort alongside throughput. Every exception gets an owner, next action, and date. Report pending enforcement separately from research-ready files, because ten queued levies can consume more counsel capacity than fifty preliminary screens. No contractor receives a volume bonus for unverified assets, letters sent, or dollars of judgment principal. Reward accepted evidence and resolved exceptions within authorized scope. Keep the raw extract and meeting decisions in a dated folder so a buyer can reproduce performance instead of trusting a dashboard screenshot. Discuss repeated misses as a capacity or specification problem before assigning more work.

Make bankruptcy and identity controls independent of the weekly meeting. Under 11 U.S.C. section 362, a bankruptcy petition generally stays specified collection and enforcement activity; counsel determines application, exceptions, and any required relief. Screen at intake and immediately before an enforcement instruction or disbursement decision affected by bankruptcy. Record the searched identifiers, timestamp, source, and reviewer. A new filing moves the matter into a restricted status and alerts counsel; automation must not issue the next demand while someone investigates. PACER supplies federal docket access, but account availability does not eliminate identity matching or court-specific source checks. Commercial focus also does not justify assuming every file lies outside consumer-debt rules. Document the underlying transaction and evaluate the definitions in 15 U.S.C. section 1692a when a personal guaranty, mixed-purpose obligation, or consumer transaction appears. Access to consumer reports requires a valid permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. section 1681b where that law applies. Vendor credentials are not permission to search any person. Route disputed authority, exemptions, service defects, and licensing questions to counsel before external action. Preserve the decision and supporting facts in the matter record, including why a seemingly attractive enforcement path was rejected.

Set a hard 25% ceiling for each creditor customer and each related debtor or payor group, measured separately across trailing-twelve-month recognized fees and probability-weighted expected fees on active matters. Group affiliates using documented common control; splitting one creditor into several account names does not create diversification. During startup, concentration may exceed the ceiling mechanically. Show the breach openly, pause additional discretionary exposure to that group, and assign a dated diversification plan rather than misstating compliance. Measure referral-source dependence separately, also capped at 25% of trailing recognized fees attributed to introductions. One law firm sending most cases can create commercial fragility even when underlying creditors differ. Use a second view of cash collections because one delayed large recovery can distort either measure. A base business with $280,000 annual recognized service fees therefore permits no group above $70,000 on the trailing-fee measure; the scaled ceiling is $125,000 against $500,000. Those are fee limits, not judgment-balance limits. Model the disappearance of the largest group and a ninety-day payment delay before adding fixed payroll. Keep concentration decisions connected to the fifteen execution lanes: sourcing changes the mix, intake controls acceptance, and reporting shows whether diversification actually reached earned fees.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the evidence-backed weekly extract
   - Export matter stages, task dates, accepted outputs, fee entries, and exceptions.
   - Preserve the extract with its reporting cutoff and reconcile opening and closing matter counts.
2. Separate safety exceptions from productivity misses
   - Review bankruptcy, identity, deadline, and payment-control exceptions first.
   - Restrict affected actions immediately; assign counsel or the responsible reviewer a documented next step.
3. Allocate contractor work against accepted capacity
   - Issue scoped work orders using backlog, defect rates, and approved purchase limits.
   - Keep contractor service levels output-based and preserve independent professional judgment where required.
4. Close the loop on earnings and concentration
   - Reconcile earned fees, cohort costs, affiliate exposure, and owner exception hours.
   - Record corrective decisions, accountable seats, and due dates in the same dated operating packet.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify Four Conditions Before Releasing Enforcement Work**
> 
> These checks apply to each matter, not merely to contractor onboarding. An unresolved result blocks the affected action while other authorized work continues.
> - Verify judgment identity, creditor authority, assignment chain, and counsel's representation scope against executed documents and the current docket.
> - Verify bankruptcy status using current searches, and obtain counsel's recorded direction on any stay, exception, relief, or uncertain debtor match.
> - Verify applicable licensing, debt classification, exemptions, data-access authority, and lien priority before selecting an enforcement method.
> - Verify the expenditure cap, estimated net collection waterfall, named reviewer, and approved payment instructions before releasing funds or instructions.

## 3. Build Accrual and Cohort Books That Reconcile to the Canonical Cases  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Maintain a matter subledger beneath the general ledger. Each matter needs creditor and debtor identifiers, engagement date, intake cohort, judgment principal, separately tracked interest, recovery receipts, client liabilities, fee terms, earned fees, direct costs, and remittance status. Judgment principal is operational reference data, not service revenue. For an illustrative collection, $100,000 received under a 30% earned service-fee agreement produces $30,000 operator revenue and $70,000 owed to the creditor before any separately authorized expense allocation. Do not post the entire receipt to sales and later call the remittance an expense. Use counsel or an appropriately authorized custodian for client funds where required; the operator's access and account structure must match applicable licensing and trust-account rules. The earned-fee trigger comes from the contract and the accounting policy reviewed by the accountant, including reversals, disputes, and amounts subject to refund. A signed recovery mandate or speculative asset estimate does not establish earned revenue. If a valid earned fee becomes receivable before cash arrives, record its basis and collectibility assessment. Reconcile bank activity, client liabilities, and fee entries monthly, with a different reviewer approving changes to creditor bank details. Never fund payroll with client balances awaiting remittance.

Use three reconciliations to keep the published scenarios intact: revenue to earned-fee entries, expenses to supported accruals, and profit to owner economics. Conservative revenue of $60,000 less $48,000 expenses produces $12,000 annual owner net profit at 20%. Base revenue of $280,000 less $196,000 expenses produces $84,000 at 30.0%. Scaled revenue of $500,000 less $300,000 expenses produces $200,000 at 40%. The first two cases use 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C. Do not change those totals to accommodate a preferred staffing narrative. An illustrative Base expense allocation is $72,000 contractor delivery, $44,000 unreimbursed enforcement and case costs, $24,000 systems and data, $20,000 acquisition, $18,000 insurance and professional support, and $18,000 other overhead. These six categories total $196,000; they are a budgeting allocation, not a second scorecard. Accrue accepted contractor work when incurred rather than shifting invoices into the next month to polish margins. Owner profit is not automatically buyer EBITDA or seller discretionary earnings. Document existing owner compensation and actual duties before making any replacement-cost adjustment, and avoid subtracting a salary already captured in expenses. Show every adjustment as a bridge, never as a silent rewrite.

Cohort reporting reveals whether the pod collects old wins while replenishing the book with weak cases. Group matters by intake quarter, creditor source, jurisdiction, and enforcement path. For each cohort, report accepted matters, cash recovered for clients, recognized service fees, direct expense, allocated delivery cost, elapsed months, and unresolved exposure. Compare cohorts at matching ages; a three-month cohort cannot fairly compete with a two-year cohort on lifetime collections. Track realized contribution separately from probability-weighted future fees and identify the assumption owner for each forecast. Insolvent debtors, bankruptcy stays, and enforcement costs are Candidate #8's central risks, so record write-down reasons rather than burying failures in a broad overhead account. A renewal deadline may preserve a claim without creating current fee revenue. A lien may improve position without producing spendable cash. QuickBooks Online can maintain the ledger, while a controlled spreadsheet or database carries matter-level waterfalls and cohort schedules. Lock closed periods, log adjustments, and reconcile exports before sharing them. Reserve cash against approved future case spending and fixed commitments, not against gross judgment balances. The owner's monthly decision is whether another dollar of authorized casework improves expected retained fees enough to justify delay and loss exposure.

## 4. Price Transferable Earnings, Not Judgment Face Value  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A buyer acquires enforceable relationships, reliable earnings, and an operating system; a folder of judgments alone does not establish enterprise value. Separate the company's sale from any transfer of creditor engagements, judgment assignments, servicing rights, or attorney relationships. Review anti-assignment provisions, change-of-control terms, creditor consent requirements, applicable licenses, and court-record requirements individually. Counsel evaluates whether substitution or additional filings are needed, including under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 69 where federal enforcement is involved. State procedure remains central: California Code of Civil Procedure section 683.020 generally addresses the ten-year enforcement period, subject to renewal and other applicable provisions. Do not turn that example into a nationwide deadline. Inventory contractor-created templates, scripts, research schemas, training materials, and reporting formats. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a transfer of copyright ownership generally requires a signed writing; paying an invoice alone is insufficient. Obtain express assignments of applicable rights, while identifying preexisting tools, licensed materials, and excluded third-party data. Vendor subscriptions and restricted research outputs may be nontransferable. Maintain a rights register that links each operational asset to its owner, permitted use, transfer terms, and supporting document. A buyer cannot inherit permissions the seller never possessed.

Use valuation multiples as explicit negotiation sensitivities, not asserted market evidence. For an illustrative owner-operated asset sale, test 2.0–3.0 times verified seller discretionary earnings only after an accountant reconciles that measure to the books. If, purely for sensitivity, normalized earnings equal the canonical owner profits, Conservative indicates $24,000–$36,000, Base $168,000–$252,000, and Scaled $400,000–$600,000. These are enterprise-value illustrations before debt, excess cash, working capital adjustments, taxes, and transaction costs. They are not quoted offers. For a manager-run buyer, first subtract any genuinely incremental replacement expense. If Scaled profit requires $80,000 of additional loaded management cost beyond existing expenses, adjusted earnings become $120,000; an illustrative 3.0–4.0 times sensitivity gives $360,000–$480,000. That comparison shows why a higher multiple can produce a lower price when the earnings basis changes. Use actual replacement duties and compensation quotes, not a convenient round add-back. Earnings volatility, single-case windfalls, weak assignment rights, unresolved licensing, and concentrated creditors can drive value below these ranges or make a sale impractical. Evidence of repeatable cohorts, independent management, and clean transfers supports negotiating toward the upper end; it does not mechanically earn a premium. Validate pricing against current comparable transactions before marketing.

Build the sale file quarterly even when the preferred outcome is continued ownership. Include monthly reconciled statements, fee-recognition policy, cohort schedules, concentration reports, contractor agreements, IP assignments, license records, court-deadline controls, and a schedule of disputed matters. Begin buyer diligence with aggregated and redacted information; release identifiable debtor or creditor records only under appropriate authority, confidentiality terms, and access restrictions. Log access and revoke it when discussions end. A cash-cow decision uses the same evidence: measure owner hours, maintenance spending, replacement case supply, collection delays, and downside liquidity. Do not compare a headline purchase price with annual owner profit while ignoring taxes, seller notes, contingent payments, and the labor needed to keep earning. If part of consideration depends on future collections, define qualifying cohorts, the retained service-fee basis, permitted deductions, reporting rights, collection conduct, and dispute procedures. Avoid an earnout based on gross debtor payments that rewards revenue inflation or uneconomic enforcement. Complete a controlled owner-absence rehearsal covering at least one reporting and reconciliation cycle. Record every intervention, fix the underlying dependency, and repeat. The strongest exit preparation is proving that all fifteen lanes continue functioning when the owner's inbox is no longer the system of record.
