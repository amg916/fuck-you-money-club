## 1. Define the mandate before valuing the judgment  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Judgment recovery is the business of helping a judgment creditor turn an existing court award into money through permitted investigation, negotiated payment, and authorized enforcement. The judgment establishes an obligation; it does not establish liquidity, lien priority, or an executable asset. A commercial supplier holding a $150,000 award against an empty corporation may recover nothing, while a smaller award against an operating business with reachable receivables may justify immediate attention. Start with the entered judgment, docket, party identities, service history, payment history, and any appeal, stay, satisfaction, or assignment. Then distinguish the named debtor from similarly named entities and from owners who are not personally liable. A corporate judgment does not automatically reach a member's house or an affiliate's bank account. The operator sells disciplined recovery work, not the assertion that every award has cash behind it. The client retains the underlying economic claim unless a valid transaction transfers it. Counsel handles legal judgments and appearances where required; sheriffs, marshals, and other authorized officials execute process under applicable procedure. An operator's practical contribution is organizing reliable evidence, controlling case spending, maintaining deadlines, and ensuring that each dollar received reaches the correct ledger and beneficiary.

Choose one contractual role before accepting documents or quoting fees. A research provider delivers factual records under a fixed scope. A collection agency pursues payment under the licenses, bonds, communications rules, and client-fund requirements applicable to its locations and activities. An assignee for collection needs a valid assignment and a jurisdiction-specific determination of who may enforce or appear. Assignment does not erase unauthorized-practice restrictions or guarantee that a business entity can represent itself. A counsel-support operator supplies records and administration without selling legal representation or taking an impermissible share of legal fees. An outright purchaser buys an economic asset and bears acquisition risk; that model requires accounting distinct from this volume's service-fee scorecard. Commercial debt also demands classification rather than a label supplied by a salesperson. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(5), the federal consumer-debt definition turns on the underlying transaction's personal, family, or household purpose. State collection laws can cover additional conduct or obligations. Record the original transaction, debtor type, geography, proposed contact methods, and operator role in an intake memorandum. If those facts change, reassess the operating requirements before treating the matter as another routine commercial file.

The defensible starting niche is commercial post-judgment work with documentary authority and identifiable execution routes, not a warehouse of stale awards. Screen the obligation's age, renewal history, debtor operations, asset location, creditor priority, expected expenses, and likely collection timing. PACER and official state portals can establish procedural events; county recorder systems and secretary-of-state records add ownership and lien clues. None independently proves that an asset is available to satisfy this creditor. A recorded mortgage, perfected security interest, statutory lien, exemption, or co-owner can materially change the result. For example, an illustrative $100,000 asset with $80,000 senior claims and $15,000 sale costs leaves only $5,000 before other deductions, not a six-figure opportunity. Use a written spend ceiling and a documented stopping condition for every file. A $900 investigation cannot be justified by multiplying judgment face value by an optimistic success rate without considering realizable proceeds and fee entitlement. The five archetypes below are different operating businesses, not interchangeable branding choices. Select the one whose authority, capital demands, and evidence workflow match the team, then refer unsuitable files rather than quietly changing roles after a promising asset appears.

## 3. Recognize earned fees and reconcile the three profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The scorecard's revenue boundary is recognized service fees, not client asset value. Maintain separate figures for judgment face amount, remaining enforceable balance, gross collections received, client liabilities, reimbursable case costs, earned operator fees, and operating expenses. A $100,000 judgment that produces a $20,000 collection under an illustrative 25% recovery-fee agreement creates $5,000 of operator fees, not $100,000 or $20,000 of revenue. Before separately agreed expenses, the other $15,000 belongs to the creditor. Recognize fees under the actual contract and applicable accounting policy when performance and entitlement support recognition; a contingent claim, pending appeal, disputed distribution, or reversible payment requires specific analysis rather than an automatic cash-equals-revenue rule. A refundable advance is not earned merely because it reaches the bank. Likewise, a settlement signed today does not justify booking the entire face amount of future installments. Have the accountant document treatment of contingencies, refunds, recoverability, and principal-versus-agent presentation. Route legal fees, filing charges, and creditor distributions to their proper owners and accounts. Fees legally awarded to counsel do not automatically become operator revenue, and a receipt labeled recovery can contain several different economic components.

Reconcile the canonical annual cases without changing their staffing or margins. Conservative recognizes $60,000 of service revenue, incurs $48,000 of total expenses, and leaves $12,000 annual owner net profit at 20%, using 1 FT + 3 C. Base recognizes $280,000, incurs $196,000, and leaves $84,000 at 30.0%, also using 1 FT + 3 C. Scaled recognizes $500,000, incurs $300,000, and leaves $200,000 at 40%, using 2 FT + 4 C, or six people. One illustrative collection bridge is $300,000 collected at 20% for Conservative, $1,000,000 at 28% for Base, and $2,000,000 at 25% for Scaled. Those rates are explanatory assumptions, not additional canonical inputs or promised market prices. Different contract mixes can produce the same recognized revenue. Contractor headcount does not imply three or four full-time equivalents; measure actual hours and matter-level commitments. Keep staffing, contractor charges, acquisition costs, software, insurance, and unreimbursed enforcement spending inside the expense envelope. Report owner labor compensation consistently with the adopted scorecard convention instead of silently moving it between expense and profit. The scale case requires improved selection and throughput; hiring one additional employee alone does not explain the margin increase.

Capital timing is a separate operating problem from annual profitability. The supplied startup range is $10,000–$75,000, recurring monthly opex is $2,000–$12,000, and time to first deal is 6–24 months. Do not treat those ranges as a single fully funded package. Even six months at $2,000 consumes $12,000 before startup purchases or case advances; twenty-four months at $12,000 consumes $288,000. Narrow scope, retain outside income, secure appropriately structured funding, or reduce fixed commitments when the runway arithmetic does not work. The annual scenario expense totals include more than recurring overhead, so they must not be forced into the monthly opex range. Track each matter's unreimbursed cash exposure separately from accounting margin. An illustrative case producing $4,000 of earned fees with $1,200 of unreimbursed process costs and $800 of contractor work contributes $2,000 before shared overhead. A subsequent $1,000 fee refund reduces that contribution to $1,000 and requires a corrected ledger. Maintain a case-level reserve policy for disputed funds, payment returns, and potential disgorgement. Client balances cannot finance payroll, and an apparent collection win is not available working capital until ownership and distribution rights are resolved.

## 4. Build a small-team moat around authority, priority, and cash controls  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The highest-value control is knowing when enforcement must stop. Under 11 U.S.C. § 362, a bankruptcy filing can stay enforcement against the debtor or estate property; exceptions and relief require actual legal analysis. Check PACER before material collection steps and escalate filing notices immediately rather than waiting for the weekly portfolio review. Bankruptcy can also create recovery or return exposure for transfers under provisions such as 11 U.S.C. § 547; counsel should assess timing, parties, and defenses before the operator treats proceeds as irreversible. Exemptions require similarly specific analysis. Federal limits under 15 U.S.C. § 1673 can restrict garnishment of an individual's disposable earnings, while state law may provide greater protection. A commercial origin does not erase exemptions applicable to the property or person pursued. Real-property judgment liens depend on the relevant recording and attachment rules, including California Code of Civil Procedure § 697.310 where applicable. For personal property, UCC priority analysis can determine whether a lien creditor is subordinate to a secured party. Obtain a dated priority worksheet and an estimated net-realization range before spending on execution. Publicly visible ownership without reachable equity is a research result, not a collection plan.

Lawful data access is part of the production system, not a purchase-order footnote. Use public court records, authorized client documents, properly obtained discovery, and vendors whose access conditions fit the matter. LexisNexis Accurint or TransUnion TLOxp access, where available, carries contractual and use restrictions; a subscription does not authorize every search or downstream disclosure. For consumer reports, document an applicable permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b rather than assuming that holding any judgment grants unrestricted access. Avoid pretexting, credential sharing, and access to another person's private accounts. Store only evidence needed for the mandate, restrict exports, log access, and define retention and deletion schedules. Automate court deadline tracking and payment reconciliation, the two canonical leverage points, while keeping independent review for calculated dates, identity matches, interest adjustments, and satisfaction filings. A calendar should retain the triggering event, jurisdiction, rule source, reviewer, and reminder history; a payment workflow should retain bank evidence, allocation logic, and exception resolution. An incorrectly automated renewal date or misallocated creditor payment scales damage faster than manual work. Measure exception age and correction rate alongside throughput so automation earns trust through observed accuracy.

A small team becomes durable when it repeatedly rejects attractive-looking but uncollectible files and closes the loop on accepted ones. Give the owner responsibility for intake economics, client authority, and expenditure decisions; assign factual research, process coordination, and bookkeeping according to documented competence and permitted roles. Counsel remains responsible for legal strategy and required representation, with written scope and direct escalation paths. Review a weekly queue showing next action, cash at risk, days since source verification, bankruptcy status, expected net proceeds, and unreconciled balances. Measure qualified-file acceptance, fee yield per research dollar, collection cycle length, unreimbursed cost per resolved matter, and client-fund reconciliation breaks. Do not substitute judgment face value added to the pipeline for an economic performance measure. Build repeat business through accurate status reports that distinguish observed facts, counsel decisions, and estimates. When a matter closes, verify final allocations, resolve remaining costs, and coordinate the required satisfaction or release with the responsible party. Feed the outcome back into screening criteria without exposing one client's records to another. The resulting moat is a documented judgment about which cases deserve attention, supported by reliable operations, rather than a secret database or aggressive collection script.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish authority and scope
   - Assemble the judgment, docket, mandate, assignments, and transaction history.
   - Classify the debt and operator role; resolve licensing, representation, and ownership questions before contact or execution spending.
2. Price the reachable equity
   - Build a dated asset, priority, exemption, and bankruptcy review packet.
   - Estimate proceeds after senior claims and costs, then set an explicit investigation ceiling and documented stopping conditions.
3. Execute the authorized route
   - Coordinate counsel, official process, permitted communications, and verified deadlines.
   - Refresh material facts before expensive steps; halt potentially stayed activity immediately when bankruptcy information arrives.
4. Reconcile and close the loop
   - Allocate receipts, resolve exceptions, distribute authorized amounts, and coordinate closure documents.
   - Report earned fees separately from client money, verify required releases or satisfaction, and update screening rules with actual outcomes.

> - Verify bankruptcy status, applicable exemptions, asset ownership, lien priority, and expected proceeds after realistic execution costs.
