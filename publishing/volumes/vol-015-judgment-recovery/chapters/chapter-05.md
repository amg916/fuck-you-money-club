## 1. Underwrite Collectible Priority Before Buying a Judgment Story  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is pricing the judgment's face amount instead of the debtor's reachable equity. A $500,000 judgment against an empty operating company is not a $500,000 asset pipeline. Begin with the certified judgment, service history, appellate posture, renewal calendar, recorded assignments, and exact debtor identity. Match the entity through Secretary of State records, including mergers and dissolved predecessors; an officer's address does not make that officer personally liable. Search the relevant county recorder, UCC filing office, and court dockets, then build an asset-specific distribution waterfall. Illustratively, a $300,000 realization less $210,000 senior secured debt, $30,000 sale expense, and $40,000 applicable protected value leaves $20,000 before junior claims and collection expense. A hypothetical 30% recovery fee produces $6,000 service revenue, not $300,000. Spending $8,000 to reach that result destroys contribution even if enforcement succeeds. Treat this as screening arithmetic, not a legal priority opinion: attachment, perfection, statutory liens, homestead rules, ownership interests, and sale procedures can change the waterfall. Require local counsel to validate the enforceable route before committing nonrecoverable expense. Record each valuation's date and evidence source so a stale broker estimate cannot silently become today's collectible-equity assumption.

The next traps are procedural rather than commercial: defective service, broken assignment chains, expired enforcement periods, unrecorded liens, stayed judgments, and an assumed right to act as counsel. A purchase agreement does not automatically authorize a recovery company to represent another party, appear through a nonlawyer, or bypass collection-agency licensing. Route filings, debtor examinations, garnishments, and contested priority issues through appropriately admitted counsel wherever required. Have that counsel identify whether the proposed assignment genuinely transfers ownership and which activities remain regulated. Contractor labels do not change the underlying activity. Keep separate dates for judgment entry, lien creation, lien expiration, renewal eligibility, service completion, and the next enforceable step; a single generic deadline field hides fatal differences. An Airtable or SQLite register can surface missing evidence, but the official docket and recorded instruments control. For every material date, store the jurisdiction, triggering event, rule citation, source document, and reviewer. Set internal reminders at 180, 90, and 30 days before verified deadlines, without treating those reminders as statutory periods. Reject intake when standing or service remains unresolved after the fixed diligence budget. Cheap defective paper becomes expensive only after someone starts spending against it.

Consumer-debt misclassification and identity shortcuts create another asymmetry: a small possible fee can expose the entire operation. Determine the underlying transaction's purpose rather than assuming every judgment against a company owner is commercial. The federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act defines covered debt by personal, family, or household purpose under 15 U.S.C. 1692a(5); state collection statutes can reach different activities or broader categories. A personal guaranty of business borrowing is not automatically consumer debt, but its label alone is insufficient evidence. Separate transaction classification from the operator's collector status and licensing footprint. Likewise, a judgment does not authorize unrestricted credit-report access, motor-vehicle searches, impersonation, or account sharing. Document a transaction-specific permissible purpose before using a consumer report, and validate separate restrictions for other datasets. Use public-record sources first, then licensed vendor products only within the contracted purpose. Never use stolen credentials, invented employment relationships, or a relative's identity to obtain debtor information. Suspend a matter immediately after an identity mismatch or unsupported access purpose. Those events are not ordinary research friction: they undermine evidence quality, invite vendor termination, and can create statutory exposure that dwarfs the expected recovery fee.

## 2. Treat Bankruptcy, Reversals, and Client Money as Separate Ledgers  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Bankruptcy is not merely a slower collection channel. Under 11 U.S.C. 362, a petition generally triggers an automatic stay covering specified collection and enforcement acts, subject to statutory exceptions. Do not wait for a mailed notice if reliable information indicates a filing. Check PACER and the relevant bankruptcy docket at intake, before each enforcement instruction, and whenever a debtor, attorney, or payment processor reports insolvency activity. A same-day check is an internal freshness standard, not protection against a petition filed moments later. Any credible filing signal pauses new collection activity pending counsel review. Counsel determines the debtor identity, case status, stay scope, applicable exceptions, and whether relief is needed. Section 362(k) provides damages for qualifying injured individuals after willful violations; other remedies may also matter. Separately, discharge under 11 U.S.C. 524 can bar collection of discharged personal liability even after the bankruptcy case closes. Surviving liens require their own analysis. Do not treat a closed docket, an existing writ, or a debtor's informal permission as blanket authority to resume. Record who reviewed the issue and exactly which action may proceed before restarting automated reminders or sending further instructions to enforcement personnel.

A cleared payment can still carry avoidance or restitution risk. Section 547 of the Bankruptcy Code permits avoidance of qualifying preferential transfers, generally examining a 90-day prepetition period and, for certain insider transfers, a longer period. That timing alone does not establish liability: the elements, defenses, creditor status, and transaction facts matter. Sections 548 and 550 address other avoidance and recovery questions, while section 546 limits particular avoidance actions. Never reduce these provisions to a universal refund deadline. A garnishment, settlement, or lien-related receipt needs counsel's transaction-specific analysis when bankruptcy emerges. Judgment vacatur, mistaken identity, duplicate payment, and settlement rescission can create additional return demands outside bankruptcy. Track gross receipts, beneficiary ownership, earned fee allocation, unresolved disputes, and potential reversal exposure separately. A contractual indemnity against an insolvent client is weak protection; insurance exclusions and coverage conditions need actual policy review. Set a documented cash buffer based on identified exposure and available liquidity, rather than assuming every distributed dollar is permanently earned. A buffer is a cash-management decision, not automatically an expense or a reduction of recognized revenue. Have the accountant distinguish restricted cash, client liabilities, fee recognition, and any supported loss provision.

The canonical cases use recognized service fees, never client principal: $60,000 revenue less $48,000 costs yields $12,000 owner profit; $280,000 less $196,000 yields $84,000; and $500,000 less $300,000 yields $200,000. Margins are respectively 20%, 30.0%, and 40%. Team counts remain 1 FT plus 3 contractors, 1 FT plus 3 contractors, and 2 FT plus 4 contractors; contractor counts are not full-time equivalents. Illustratively, $1,000,000 of collected client principal at a 28% earned fee generates the Base case's $280,000 revenue and a $720,000 client allocation before separately authorized adjustments. That collection volume is an explanatory bridge, not an additional scorecard assumption. Match bank receipts to matter, payer, settlement terms, earned fee, client payable, and distribution record. Use the applicable attorney trust account or other legally required segregated arrangement; never fund payroll from client allocations. In a stress test against Base, an additional $12,000 failed-enforcement expense, an $8,400 recognized-fee reversal, and $15,000 additional defense expense reduce profit to $48,600. This is sensitivity analysis, not a replacement scenario. The control objective is to catch those departures before distributions and spending make an otherwise manageable reversal impossible to absorb.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Contain the affected matter
   - Pause collection instructions and affected distributions immediately.
   - Preserve the triggering notice, docket references, payment identifiers, and action history; do not delete evidence or attempt an improvised transfer to another account.
2. Establish the controlling facts
   - Have counsel verify the order, debtor, transfer, and applicable restrictions.
   - Distinguish stay issues, discharge issues, avoidance demands, vacatur, and ordinary reconciliation errors because their remedies and response deadlines differ.
3. Reconcile exposure and available cash
   - Match every implicated receipt to fees, client liabilities, and distributions.
   - Prepare a recipient-level transfer schedule and a liquidity forecast; ask the accountant to classify any required adjustment without treating client money as operating capital.
4. Resume only the documented permitted work
   - Record counsel's action-specific direction and assign the next review date.
   - Update the docket register, vendor work queues, payment instructions, and responsible reviewer before restarting any previously paused activity.

## 3. Install Telemetry That Stops Margin Leaks and Access Failures  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Measure the denominator that actually drives profit. A recovery rate calculated against judgments purchased this month mixes fresh inventory with old receipts and produces false confidence. Build intake cohorts by month, jurisdiction, debtor type, enforcement route, and counsel; track cumulative realized fees against cumulative direct costs for the same matters. Keep open-case forecasts separate from realized contribution. Define direct contribution as recognized fees less attributable counsel, research, filing, service, investigator, and enforcement expenses. Do not call it owner profit because shared payroll and overhead remain. Illustratively, twelve matters producing $36,000 fees against $24,000 direct costs contribute $12,000 before overhead; a thirteenth matter consuming $5,000 without recovery cuts that contribution to $7,000. Use internal tripwires: review a mature cohort below 40% direct contribution margin, and stop new intake in that route after two consecutive mature cohorts fall below 25%. These are management thresholds, not industry benchmarks or scorecard promises. Define maturity before launch using the expected collection cycle, and retain censored open matters in the dashboard. Otherwise, closing bad files early while leaving optimistic files open manufactures an attractive success rate without improving cash generation or the quality of acquisition decisions.

Translate weak signals into bounded spending decisions. At intake, estimate reachable proceeds, contractual fee share, remaining direct expense, collection probability, and elapsed time; show the assumptions rather than only a blended score. A $20,000 reachable recovery at a 30% fee and a 50% collection probability produces $3,000 expected gross fees. If another $4,000 is required before receipt, reject the proposed step unless new evidence changes the inputs. Sunk cost is not a reason to continue. Trigger human review when cumulative matter spending reaches 75% of its authorized budget, and require renewed authorization before exceeding 100%. Flag asset evidence older than 30 days before a material new commitment, while using shorter intervals for volatile assets. Track verified-action latency, not merely emails sent: thirty days without a documented asset, docket, or settlement development should trigger a case review. The volume's $10,000–$75,000 startup capital, $2,000–$12,000 monthly operating expense, and 6–24 months to first deal can conflict sharply. Six months at $12,000 consumes $72,000 before exceptional enforcement spending. Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast and a longer runway model; stop discretionary intake below three months of committed operating cash unless funding is already secured.

Platform bans usually start with purpose drift, credential sharing, prohibited automation, or ignored warnings rather than an exotic technical failure. PACER, court portals, LexisNexis Risk Solutions, and TransUnion products have different access terms and permitted uses; a subscription does not authorize every workflow. Store the vendor product, named user, approved purpose, matter identifier, and access event in an auditable log without retaining unnecessary sensitive payloads. Use official exports or permitted interfaces, rate limits, individual accounts, and prompt offboarding. Never respond to suspension by rotating accounts, borrowing a lawyer's login, masking request origins, or buying leaked records. Stop the affected connector after one authorization warning, one unexplained identity mismatch, or repeated access-denied responses; obtain vendor clarification before resuming. Deadline tracking must also fail visibly. If a docket import fails, mark the affected calendar unverified and assign a manual source check rather than showing yesterday's green status. Reconcile each business day's receipts by the next business day, and investigate any unexplained client-balance difference immediately. Automate exception detection, not legal conclusions. The operator should see who owns each exception, what evidence resolves it, and whether spending, communication, or distribution remains blocked while the issue is open.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Veteran Habits That Preserve Optionality**
> 
> Experienced operators buy evidence in stages and keep every stage cheap enough to abandon when priority or authority fails.
> - Commission a narrow lien-and-ownership review before a broad investigator package; stop if the waterfall cannot support the next dollar of enforcement expense.
> - Require counsel invoices to identify matter, task, and result so failed service attempts and repeated motion drafting cannot disappear inside aggregate billing.
> - Keep bankruptcy notices and assignment documents beside the receipt ledger; reversal analysis becomes faster when ownership and transfer history are already linked.
> - Negotiate milestone-based research scopes with explicit stop instructions; a useful negative finding should end spending rather than trigger a larger automatic package.

## 4. Quarantine Adjacent Plays Before They Import New Liabilities  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The mapped play unclaimed_property_escheatment_arbitrage, State Treasury Escheatment & Unclaimed Corporate Warrant Recovery, uses TRACK_1 intake rather than ordinary judgment enforcement. Ingest state controller or treasury ledgers and municipal or corporate uncashed-warrant registries for items above $25,000, using authorized downloads and source terms. Cross-match corporate officers through Secretary of State filings, but require documentary authority from the actual owner or lawful successor before signing recovery agreements. An officer match is a lead, not proof of entitlement. The play's 10–25% contingent finder-fee range is subordinate to each state's statutory limits and timing restrictions. California Code of Civil Procedure section 1582, for example, imposes a 10% compensation ceiling and restricts agreement timing. For an eligible $40,000 claim at a permitted 10% fee, service revenue is $4,000; $1,200 document and processing expense leaves $2,800 direct contribution before overhead. The $40,000 asset never becomes operator revenue. State clearly that owners may claim directly for free. If the permitted fee is insufficient or the agreement falls within a restricted period, defer or decline rather than disguise compensation as document charges. Uncashed warrants may require separate issuer reissuance procedures before any escheat claim exists.

The second mapped play, state_film_historic_brownfield_tax_credit_brokerage, State Film, Historic Rehabilitation (HTC) & Brownfield Transferable Tax Credit Placement, also belongs to TRACK_1. Its danger is mistaking a certificate for an immediately usable transferable asset. Match only certified state credits whose program permits the contemplated transfer, and verify the buyer's tax position, permitted tax type, usage year, carryforward rules, transfer limits, filing steps, and potential recapture. State transferability does not establish that federal historic rehabilitation credits under 26 U.S.C. 47 can be sold outright. For an illustrative eligible state credit with $100,000 face value, a $92,000 negotiated purchase price and separately disclosed $3,000 placement fee yield $3,000 operator revenue, not $95,000 or $100,000. At $1,000 direct diligence expense, contribution is $2,000 before overhead; the buyer's initial total outlay is $95,000, subject to the agreed transaction structure. These figures are transaction assumptions, not market quotes or promised tax savings. If eligibility, certification, or transfer evidence fails, suspend settlement under written diligence contingencies. Rematch only verified eligible credits and taxpayers. Do not forward disputed certificates to new buyers, imply agency endorsement, or treat escrowed purchase funds as earned placement revenue.

Keep both adjacent plays outside the judgment collection ledger until their entitlement, statutory terms, and fee recognition conditions are established. They are two of the volume's 15 mapped plays, not evidence that every recovery method shares one license, contract, or settlement workflow. An unclaimed-property claimant may own an asset without owing the operator anything; a tax-credit buyer may pay a discount yet lack usable tax capacity. The same screening discipline applies across all three lanes: identify the actual rights holder, verify the transferable or enforceable right, calculate recognized fees, and cap the next irreversible expense. Illustratively, ten completed $40,000 property claims at lawful 10% fees produce $40,000 service revenue, while ten completed credit placements at $3,000 produce $30,000. Neither figure includes the underlying $400,000 property value or $1,000,000 credit face amount. These examples do not add revenue to the canonical scenarios without replacing another modeled fee source and accounting for incremental costs. Build separate contracts, evidence checklists, and reversal registers before expanding. If a new lane needs unfamiliar licensing, entitlement proof, or tax analysis, use qualified specialists and a small capped pilot rather than subsidizing uncertainty with client money from the core recovery business.
