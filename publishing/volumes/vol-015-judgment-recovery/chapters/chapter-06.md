## 1. Mine Creditor Pain and Asset Events Across Fifteen Sourcing Plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with creditors whose enforcement problem is visible, not debtors whose wealth merely looks attractive. Select two court systems and one commercial segment, such as equipment lessors, wholesalers, or commercial landlords. Use official state docket portals and PACER Case Locator for federal matters; CourtListener can surface leads, but confirm every material event against the court record. Capture judgment entry, creditor identity, debtor legal name, counsel, principal, interest basis, satisfaction entries, appeals, and recent execution activity. A final money judgment followed by twelve months without a recorded satisfaction is a research signal, not proof that nothing was collected. Ask the creditor for a payment ledger before quoting a recoverable balance. Public records also omit private settlements and pending disputes. Prioritize represented commercial creditors with several similar judgments, because one reliable relationship can supply repeat mandates without repeated acquisition expense. Schedule weekly searches rather than indiscriminate scraping, respect portal access terms, and record source timestamps. In Airtable or a SQLite register, separate the legal creditor, authorized contact, judgment debtor, and possible asset owner. That distinction prevents a common expensive error: pitching enforcement against property belonging to an affiliate whose similar name never made it a judgment debtor.

Map each lead to one of the fifteen sourcing plays below, then require an asset-specific reason to investigate. County recorder activity, renewed business registrations, a disclosed business sale, and a creditor's abandoned execution file can create useful timing signals. None independently proves available equity. A property transfer might reflect an exempt residence, a senior foreclosure, or an ordinary sale with no surplus. A revived entity might hold no operating assets. Build an evidence packet containing the docket event, ownership match, competing claims, proposed next inquiry, and estimated investigation cost. Send that packet to the creditor or existing counsel, never to the debtor's customers as leverage. The highest-margin channel is often a small litigation practice with enforceable commercial judgments but little appetite for post-judgment administration. Offer organized records, status reporting, and authorized investigation; licensed counsel retains responsibility for legal strategy and court work. Do not promise nonlawyer representation or divide attorney fees through a disguised marketing charge. Confirm the governing state's collection-agency, assignment, and referral restrictions before choosing the engagement structure. If authority is unresolved, keep the file in research status and refer the legal question to appropriately licensed counsel instead of collecting signatures prematurely.

The required play ucc1_expiration_refi_trigger_arbitrage belongs in a separate commercial-intent lane, not the collectible-judgment inventory. Monitor state Secretary of State UCC-1 statements, amendments, and continuations for operating businesses that may be four to nine months into high-cost Merchant Cash Advances or equipment notes. Filing age is only a prospecting proxy: a UCC statement does not establish an MCA, outstanding balance, interest cost, or contractual maturity. Under enacted UCC section 9-515, ordinary filings generally last five years, with continuation during the final six months; exceptions require review. Obtain borrower-confirmed agreements before labeling a refinance opportunity. Illustratively, 100 reviewed filings might produce 20 verified business contacts, six requested introductions, and two fundings. At an assumed lawful $750 referral fee per funding, gross referral revenue is $1,500; subtract $600 sourcing cost to leave $900 before overhead. A $50,000 advance is funded principal, never operator revenue. Track this adjacent experiment separately from the canonical recovery cases. Arrange live transfers only after the business requests an introduction. If transfer economics or permissions fail, use direct mail, B2B work-email sequences, and manual human-dialed outreach with applicable state commercial financing disclosures. Never condition judgment settlement on purchasing new credit.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy Better Evidence Before Buying More Leads**
> 
> Veteran recovery operators improve file quality before expanding territory. A smaller inventory with verified balances and ownership usually deserves attention before a large spreadsheet of impressive judgment amounts.
> - Read the most recent returned writ before researching assets; its return can explain precisely why the previous execution failed.
> - Ask referring counsel for three previously abandoned files and the reason each stopped; repeated obstacles reveal the best service niche.
> - Search exact legal names plus documented former names, then preserve the entity identifier used to distinguish similarly named businesses.
> - Request a creditor payment ledger with every judgment packet; unrecorded partial payments can change the balance and the outreach proposition.

## 2. Qualify Collectible Equity Before Selling a Recovery Mandate  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Score evidence, not optimism. Use a 100-point intake model: 25 points for enforceability and standing, 25 for verified debtor-owned assets, 20 for residual value after senior claims and exemptions, 15 for executable venue and procedure, and 15 for creditor economics and cooperation. Require 75 points for a paid investigation proposal and 85 for a recovery proposal; these are internal triage thresholds, not measured success probabilities. An active bankruptcy stay, disputed authority, an expired judgment, or unresolved licensing requirements overrides any score. Separate evidence into confirmed facts, dated third-party records, and hypotheses requiring verification. A screenshot of a successful business does not earn asset points without ownership support. Request the entered judgment, relevant orders, service history where material, assignments, payment ledger, current counsel details, and prior enforcement invoices. Check renewal and limitation rules in the actual enforcing jurisdiction. Interest calculations must follow the applicable judgment and law, including payment credits; do not apply a favorite percentage universally. Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 69, federal money-judgment execution generally uses state procedure unless a federal statute applies. That procedural bridge makes local counsel and jurisdiction-specific checklists more valuable than a national template claiming universal coverage.

Build a priority waterfall before estimating the operator's fee. Suppose a commercial asset could realize $150,000, documented sale and enforcement costs total $15,000, and senior claims consume $110,000. Only $25,000 remains before other applicable claims or exemptions. A $100,000 judgment does not turn that $25,000 residual into $100,000 of collectible value. If the engagement permits a 35% service fee on recovered funds, the illustrative fee ceiling is $8,750, not $35,000. Order UCC searches in the relevant filing jurisdiction, check county records where appropriate, and have counsel reconcile lien attachment, perfection, priority, and execution timing. A lapsed financing statement is not proof that the underlying debt vanished or that your creditor automatically moved ahead. Likewise, a tax lien search does not replace reviewing the governing priority rules. Screen bankruptcy using official records at intake and again before enforcement. Section 362 of title 11 can halt collection acts, and section 524 can prohibit collection of discharged personal liability. A dismissal, closing, or discharge entry needs order-specific interpretation. Where ownership or priority remains contested, cap investigation spending and obtain a written legal assessment before proposing an asset-specific recovery timeline to the creditor.

Set an investigation budget using expected contribution, with explicit uncertainty. For a file with $20,000 estimated collectible proceeds, a 35% fee would produce $7,000 if collected. At an illustrative 40% collection probability, expected fee revenue is $2,800. Subtract $500 acquisition cost and $900 expected unreimbursed investigation and enforcement expense to leave $1,400 before shared overhead and timing effects. This calculation is a screening model, not a valuation of the judgment or an instruction to purchase it. Stress the probability to 20%; expected contribution then falls to zero under the same assumptions. Prefer files whose downside remains manageable after such a stress. Document whether court costs are advanced by the creditor, recovered from proceeds, absorbed by the operator, or separately billed by counsel. Never count the same reimbursement twice. Use staged authority: initial document review, bounded asset investigation, then counsel-selected enforcement. Limit personal data to what the authorized task needs, and restrict access by role. Public availability does not authorize impersonation, pretexting for bank information, or unrestricted consumer-report access. If the only proposed asset lead requires unlawful access, reject that route and return to permissible records, creditor documents, or counsel-supervised discovery.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify Authority, Assets, Priority, and Spending Before Launch**
> 
> A signed prospect does not cure a defective file. Record the reviewer, source, date, and conclusion for each condition before authorizing the corresponding activity.
> - Verify the creditor's authority, assignment chain, current balance, and your required collection licenses before accepting collection work.
> - Confirm debtor identity, asset ownership, and current bankruptcy status before any debtor contact or enforcement instruction.
> - Have counsel review priority, exemptions, available remedies, and any consumer-debt implications before presenting a collectible-value estimate.
> - Obtain written spending limits, cost allocation, reporting duties, and client-funds handling instructions before incurring external costs.

## 3. Turn Verified Signals Into Conversations, Mandates, and Measured Wins  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run outbound toward the creditor's authorized decision-maker, not toward people surrounding the debtor. Build a six-touch sequence over thirty business days: a short work email, a manual telephone call, a mailed one-page process brief, a second email offering a record checklist, a final manual call, and a close-the-loop note. Use the public case reference only when necessary and avoid sensitive details in subject lines or voicemail. A useful opening is: 'We organize commercial post-judgment files for documented asset review and counsel-led enforcement. Would a short review of your inactive inventory be useful?' Identify the sender accurately and explain how to stop further marketing. Commercial email must follow applicable CAN-SPAM requirements, including accurate headers, a valid postal address, and an effective opt-out. Maintain suppression across channels and assess telephone restrictions before calling; manual dialing is not a universal exemption. Inbound should match the same narrow offer: an evidence checklist, a realistic timeline, and an authorized case study with identifying details removed where appropriate. Do not publish debtor dossiers to attract search traffic. Qualify each inquiry with creditor authority, commercial debt purpose, jurisdiction, judgment date, outstanding balance, and available documents before scheduling an expensive substantive review.

Use a transparent funnel to define the first ten mandates. In one illustrative six-month sourcing cohort, screen 300 raw docket leads, retain 120 eligible creditor records, reach 60 authorized contacts, hold 24 substantive conversations, receive 12 complete packets, and sign six mandates. Those transitions are 40%, 50%, 40%, 50%, and 50%, producing a 2% raw-lead-to-mandate rate. They are planning assumptions, not industry benchmarks. If three mandates later produce collected funds, the cash-producing rate is 50% of signed mandates and 1% of raw leads, measured only after sufficient observation. A $3,600 sourcing budget produces a $600 acquisition cost per mandate or $1,200 per eventual cash-producing file in this example. Track those cohorts separately because a fresh file has not failed merely because enforcement takes months. The canonical first-deal window is 6–24 months; three early signatures are not three realized recoveries. For an initial three-to-ten mandate target, 150–500 raw leads would be required at the assumed 2% rate. Do not extrapolate cash receipts from signatures. Diagnose losses by stage: poor contact data, weak relevance, missing creditor documents, unsuitable economics, or engagement terms that allocate costs unclearly.

Handle objections with bounded next actions. When a creditor says 'our attorney already tried,' ask for the returned writ, last asset report, and reason work stopped; offer only a changed-facts review. When they say 'there are no assets,' request permission to test one specific ownership lead under a fixed research cap. When they demand payment upfront for an assignment, distinguish a purchase from a service mandate: a purchase requires separate pricing, transfer review, capital allocation, and loss tolerance. Do not imply that a contingent service proposal includes an asset purchase. When they object to the percentage, show estimated net proceeds after documented costs and compare a smaller investigation scope, rather than inventing a collection certainty. For 'send references,' provide authorized creditor references or redacted process examples, never another client's private recovery file. The engagement should define scope, authority, fee basis, settlement decision rights, reporting frequency, expense limits, termination, and handling of proceeds. Keep counsel's engagement and the operator's permitted services distinct. Require secure document delivery through a configured client portal rather than broad email forwarding. Close the first conversation with one specific request and date: an authorized contact will deliver the judgment and ledger by Friday, or the file returns to nurture.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the weekly evidence queue
   - Screen a bounded batch of commercial judgments in the selected courts.
   - Deduplicate creditor and debtor entities, preserve source dates, and assign one of the fifteen sourcing plays to every retained lead.
2. Contact the authorized creditor representative
   - Run the thirty-business-day sequence with shared suppression controls.
   - Ask for the judgment, ledger, and previous enforcement history; do not contact unrelated debtor counterparties to pressure payment.
3. Price a bounded, evidence-supported mandate
   - Apply the qualification score and complete the priority waterfall.
   - Resolve overriding legal conditions, document assumptions, and state who funds each investigation and enforcement expense.
4. Measure signed and cash-producing cohorts separately
   - Track mandate conversion, acquisition cost, collected proceeds, and earned fees.
   - Review stage losses monthly and change one sourcing variable at a time so improvements can be attributed to actual operating changes.

## 4. Fund the First Wins and Reconcile Fees to the Canonical Scorecard  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat the first three-to-ten mandates as a learning portfolio with explicit spending ceilings. During months one through three, establish operating authority, counsel relationships, secure records handling, and the first court watchlists. During months four through six, test the creditor offer and document-transfer process without assuming a completed deal. During months six through twenty-four, convert qualified files, fund staged investigation, and allow actual enforcement timelines to determine receipts. The canonical startup capital range is $10,000–$75,000, while monthly operating expense ranges from $2,000–$12,000. These ranges are not interchangeable runway promises: $10,000 funds only five months at $2,000 per month before startup purchases or external enforcement costs. At $12,000 monthly spending, even $75,000 provides only 6.25 months before those additional uses. Match hiring to funded work, not gross judgment balances. The Conservative and Base teams are one full-time operator plus three contractors; the Scaled team is two full-time people plus four contractors. Contractor counts do not specify utilization or compensation. Define scoped deliverables for research, administration, and licensed services, then reconcile their actual costs to the annual case budget. If cash runway cannot cover the expected delay, reduce fixed spending or postpone expansion rather than pressuring unsuitable files into enforcement.

Reconcile the scorecard through recognized service fees, never through judgment face value or client receipts. One illustrative Conservative bridge is $200,000 of collected client proceeds at a 30% service fee, producing $60,000 revenue. Total modeled expenses of $48,000 leave $12,000 owner net profit and a 20% margin. The Base bridge uses $800,000 collected at 35%, producing $280,000 revenue; $196,000 total expenses leave $84,000 profit and a 30.0% margin. The Scaled bridge uses $1,250,000 collected at 40%, producing $500,000 revenue; $300,000 expenses leave $200,000 profit and a 40% margin. These fee percentages illustrate arithmetic, not required pricing or historical recovery performance. Actual contracts may allocate costs differently and require a different collection volume. The monthly operating-expense range describes a planning range for recurring operations, not a ceiling on all annual case costs. For example, $8,000 monthly recurring expense totals $96,000 annually, leaving $100,000 of the Base expense envelope for other modeled costs, without double counting. Keep referral experiments outside these bridges unless the model explicitly reallocates revenue. Recognize contingent fees only when earned under the applicable contract and accounting policy; a signed mandate alone creates no collected service fee.

Automate deadline reminders and payment reconciliation only after the underlying records are reliable. Maintain separate dates for judgment renewal, lien duration, writ returns, hearings, promised creditor documents, and any UCC continuation window. Import docket alerts as tasks requiring review, not instructions to execute process. A paralegal or attorney should validate legal deadlines against the governing rule and actual order. For money movement, assign a case identifier to every receipt, retain remittance evidence, and reconcile the client balance, earned service fee, approved expenses, and disbursement. Follow jurisdiction-specific trust-account or collection-account requirements; client money is not operating cash simply because it reaches an account you administer. Use QuickBooks or another properly configured ledger for accounting and a separate restricted case register for operational evidence. Review a monthly cohort report showing source, signed date, authorized cost ceiling, cash recovered, fee earned, expense incurred, and unresolved legal conditions. Stop a sourcing play when its mature-cohort contribution stays negative after realistic collection delays, or when missing records repeatedly prevent lawful execution. Expand only the channel that produces documented authority, collectible residual value, responsive creditors, and acceptable cash timing. A large pipeline of unverified judgments is inventory of questions, not a revenue asset.
