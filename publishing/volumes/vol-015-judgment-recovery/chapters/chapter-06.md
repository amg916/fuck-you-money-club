# Chapter 06: Judgment Liens, Abstracts, and Real Property

*Judgment vs. lien distinctions, abstracts of judgment, finding liens in courthouse and title records, liens on primary residences, homestead and bankruptcy effects, removing liens from preliminary title reports, and lien and encumbrance fundamentals from real estate exam prep*

## Executive Summary

Practitioners across title, collections, and real estate law agree that a money judgment is only a number against a person until it is recorded or docketed in each county where the debtor holds land. At that point it becomes a general, involuntary lien on every parcel there, including property acquired later. Bob Benjy walks through California's one-page abstract of judgment and multi-county recording. Hidden Power Files maps how to dig judgment liens out of grantor-grantee indexes and separate dockets. Nicholas Kralick sets a five-year revival window for Pennsylvania lien priority, and David Babel puts New York judgment interest at nine percent a year. The friction points are tax liens jumping senior, homestead and entireties shields, bankruptcy avoidance inside 90 days, and personal property that requires a sheriff levy or writ.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every practitioner who works title or collections draws the same hard line: a judgment is a number against a person, and a lien is a hook in an asset. The In Rem Podcast attorney says flatly that an entered judgment 'alone does not create any lien against any real or personal property' and that, until further steps are taken, it is 'just the number on a piece of paper or a number in a computer system' [pHI86CVm1RQ @ 00:01:10]. Hidden Power Files makes the same point from the records side. The final judgment gives the creditor a legal determination of money owed, but the lien on land attaches only when the judgment, or an abstract of judgment derived from it, is recorded in the deed records of the county where the debtor owns property [-HFzhDO6v-A @ 00:00:41]. Dave at titlesearch.com describes two attachment routes. One is statutory attachment once the judgment is recorded in the county court records. The other is specific recording, where the creditor carries the judgment paperwork over to the land records [gyWC9jWNaXQ @ 00:00:19]. Simple Explain adds the state split: some states create the real estate lien automatically on entry, while in most the creditor must record it through a county or state filing [cZtaubxDzMA @ 00:00:34].

The second point of agreement is geography: the lien is county-scoped and blankets everything the debtor holds there. Tiffany Weber of The Real Estate Lawyer explains that a judgment docketed in a county attaches to every piece of property the debtor owns in their own name in that county. Reaching land in other counties requires the creditor to docket the judgment in each of those counties as well [RFKcDlW0gQU @ 00:01:25]. Bob Benjy runs the same play in California with abstracts. The lien attaches even where the debtor is only a joint co-owner; Benjy's example is Jane Roe holding Orange County real estate with five other people. An abstract recorded where the debtor owns nothing will 'sit and wait' and attach the moment property is acquired [hJmfkC9gu4c @ 00:04:43]. Nicholas Kralick confirms the after-acquired angle in Pennsylvania. Kralick names inheritance, a divorce settlement, or a later purchase as the ways a debtor picks up land after judgment, and tells creditors to index the judgment in the county where that new real estate sits [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:03:32]. PrepAgent states the result: once filed, a judgment lien is a general, involuntary lien against all property of the defendant [dccNDC1WYF4 @ 00:01:28].

Practitioners also agree that a recorded judgment does not go away on its own; it surfaces at sale or refinance and gets paid out of the proceeds. Weber warns that sellers who hide judgments get caught because the title search reveals them. Weber has seen closing statements where the payoff wiped out the seller's proceeds, so the seller had to bring money to closing [RFKcDlW0gQU @ 00:02:51]. Maggie's priority walkthrough shows where the judgment sits in line. Take a 2012 first mortgage, a 2014 judgment lien, a 2020 second mortgage, and a 2022 tax lien: the tax lien is paid first despite being recorded last, then the first mortgage, then the judgment, then the second mortgage [SFR_S8YmDSU @ 00:03:20]. The exemptions are equally consistent. Kralick says there is no judgment lien on tenants-by-the-entirety property when the judgment is against only one spouse [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:01:45]. The In Rem Podcast attorney says Arizona and most states have a homestead exemption. Under it, judgments either never attach to the debtor's personal residence, or the owner can sell and pocket a certain amount without paying the judgment [pHI86CVm1RQ @ 00:02:47].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Bob Benjy runs a Los Angeles litigation boutique, calls post-judgment collection their area of passion, and lays out the California abstract play step by step. After entry, the creditor asks the clerk of the court that entered the judgment to issue an abstract of judgment. It is a one-page Judicial Council form that asks for the entry date, the debtor's full name, their California driver's license number and the last four digits of their Social Security number if known, the last known address, and the principal balance awarded [hJmfkC9gu4c @ 00:00:57]. The creditor can submit several at once, one for each target county. If the form is correct and the name is spelled right, the clerk performs the ministerial task of issuing originals bearing the court seal and the clerk's signature [hJmfkC9gu4c @ 00:01:43]. In Benjy's example, three originals from the LA County Superior Court go to the Orange, San Francisco, and Contra Costa County Recorders through an attorney service [hJmfkC9gu4c @ 00:03:15]. SpeakLegal defines what the recorded instrument carries: the amount owed, the interest rate, court costs, and any specific orders the debtor must obey. It is acknowledged and stamped for recording, and if the debtor does not pay, the creditor can force a sheriff sale [EAME7zlaLqU @ 00:00:01].

On the hunting side, Hidden Power Files gives the most detailed search protocol. Judgment liens sit in the grantor-grantee index under the debtor's name, mixed in with deeds, mortgages, and releases. The instrument-type field may read judgment lien, abstract of judgment, judgment, or certified judgment depending on how the county indexes it, so a search that filters for deed-type entries 'will scan right past it' [-HFzhDO6v-A @ 00:01:42]. Once an entry turns up, the operator pulls the full instrument, which names the creditor and debtor, states the amount, references the court case, and shows the recording date. Some counties keep a separate judgment lien index or judgment docket in a different office, some post judgments on an online court portal, and some states run a statewide registry, so every one of these sources has to be checked [-HFzhDO6v-A @ 00:03:01]. A renewal filing extends the lien. A release or satisfaction recorded after the entry confirms payoff, and an entry with no later release may still be active [-HFzhDO6v-A @ 00:04:35]. Dave at AFX Title adds that judgment liens are 'usually not in the land records' yet hit the property as hard as a mortgage, so a title order must specifically call for a judgment search [a5bLikprQ4E @ 00:01:35].

For assets other than land, the practitioners agree the judgment does nothing until the creditor acts. Kralick says a Pennsylvania judgment is not a lien on personal property. The sheriff's levy on equipment, vehicles, furniture, or business inventory creates that lien, so creditors should not be put off by prior judgments and should 'beat the prior judgment creditors to the punch' with a levy [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:04:42]. The In Rem Podcast attorney describes two writs. A writ of garnishment served on the bank freezes non-exempt funds, so the bank cannot release them or pay checks until a later court proceeding turns the money over. A writ of execution after judgment lets the sheriff tow a non-exempt vehicle for sale by the court [pHI86CVm1RQ @ 00:03:42]. On real estate, Kralick dismisses the claim that a judgment is only good for five years. Five years is the window to start an action reviving the lien so it keeps its priority. A late revival still works, but the creditor drops to the end of the line [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:02:56]. David Babel shows why surviving liens are valuable: in New York a docketed judgment accrues interest at nine percent per year, and by the time the owner sells or refinances the number 'can be humongous' [q6PjSqG-sF4 @ 00:01:50].

1. Map the Debtor's Real Estate by County
   - Before requesting any abstracts, identify every county where the debtor is on title.

2. Request Sealed Abstracts From the Entering Court
   - File the one-page Judicial Council abstract form with the clerk, one copy per target county.

3. Record the Originals With Each County Recorder
   - Send the sealed originals through an attorney service to every target County Recorder.

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first fault line is whether a judgment is already a lien on real estate the moment it is entered. Nicholas Kralick of the Five Minute Legal Master Series says that in Pennsylvania a judgment automatically acts as a lien against real estate the debtor owns in the county where it is recorded, and that lien priority against that real estate is established just by virtue of having the judgment recorded [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:01:26]. Hidden Power Files takes the opposite position: the judgment itself does not automatically create a lien on real property, and the creditor has to record the judgment, or an abstract of judgment in some states, in the deed records of the county where the debtor owns land [-HFzhDO6v-A @ 00:00:41]. The In Rem Podcast host goes further, calling an unrecorded judgment just a number on a piece of paper or a number in a computer system until it is taken to the county recorder's office where the debtor owns real estate [pHI86CVm1RQ @ 00:02:08]. Dave at AFX Videos splits the difference from the title desk: in some states and counties the judgment attaches automatically, yet the holder can still carry it over to the land records and record it as a lien even though that might not be necessary [zYr0eoJPSpU @ 00:01:10].

Priority is the second split, and it decides whether a recorded lien ever turns into cash. The Real Estate Classroom instructor teaches that specific liens have higher priority than judgment liens, so even when the judgment lien was filed first, a tax lien or IRS lien automatically jumps above it [dwH7f7RGYC4 @ 00:09:48]. Maggie of Just Call Maggie runs a first-in-time model with carve-outs for property taxes, assessments and sometimes HOA liens: in the worked example, a 2012 mortgage, a 2014 judgment lien, a 2020 second mortgage and a 2022 tax lien pay out tax first, then the first mortgage, then the judgment, and only then the second mortgage [SFR_S8YmDSU @ 00:03:40]. Bob Benjie of Benjie Law Corporation treats a recorded abstract like a deed of trust paid through escrow in the order it was recorded, with senior deeds of trust ahead but any equity left flowing to the creditor [hJmfkC9gu4c @ 00:05:19]. PrepAgent flags a lien class that cuts in line: mechanics liens take priority, so in foreclosure the mechanics lien is paid off before the lender, which can impair the owner's ability to get a loan [dccNDC1WYF4 @ 00:01:43]. Where Maggie and the Classroom agree is the top slot: property taxes and government assessments override recording date.

The third split is what a judgment lien can reach and whether it survives the debtor's exit routes. Kralick says that where a married debtor owns real estate with a spouse as tenants by the entirety, there is no judgment lien against that property if the creditor only holds a judgment against one spouse [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:01:45]. Bob Benjie describes the California abstract reaching harder: it attaches to the equity of any property the debtor is on title for, even as a joint co-owner with five other people in Orange County [hJmfkC9gu4c @ 00:03:50]. Bankruptcy draws the sharpest contrast. The Real Estate Exam Questions channel answers that judgment liens can be discharged in bankruptcy proceedings depending on the type of bankruptcy and the specific circumstances [KgBg-x2ZZ4w @ 00:07:29]. David Babel warns that many people, including some attorneys, do not know a judgment lien may survive a Chapter 7 when the debtor owns a house or condo [q6PjSqG-sF4 @ 00:01:09]. In the Babel scenario the discharge forever ends the creditor's ability to sue on the debt, yet the lien docketed in the county stays on the property, so the debtor still cannot refinance or sell without paying it off. For a collector, the lien outlives the debt.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most common firsthand failure is simply not finding the lien. Hidden Power Files calls the judgment lien one of the most commonly missed instruments in a title search, because it sits in the grantor-grantee index under the debtor's name mixed in with deeds, mortgages and releases, and a search that only looks for deed-type entries scans right past it [-HFzhDO6v-A @ 00:02:02]. Dave at AFX Videos adds that judgment liens are usually not in the land records at all, yet may affect the property just as much as a mortgage, so anyone ordering a title search should specify that judgment liens are wanted [a5bLikprQ4E @ 00:01:35]. The reverse trap is a false hit. Realtor Dylan Raquel had a seller, Kristian Fuentes, in escrow when the preliminary title report showed a child-support lien against a Christian Fuentes; the seller had no kids, filled out a confidential statement of information, and the title insurance company found the Social Security numbers did not match and issued a supplement removing the lien [7NXn3JN88l8 @ 00:02:33]. The Raquel takeaway is that it is not a matter of whether the seller has an issue, but whether anyone with that name has one, and the prelim will reveal it.

The second trap is economics at the closing table. Tiffany Weber, a real estate attorney in Mooresville, North Carolina, sees sellers conceal judgments hoping the closing attorney will not catch them, only for the payoff to surface on the closing statement with not enough proceeds left, so the seller has to bring money to closing [RFKcDlW0gQU @ 00:03:04]. That payoff compounds: David Babel says a judgment docketed in the Bronx Supreme Court attaches to the debtor's property automatically and blocks any refinance or sale until it is paid, including interest accumulating at nine percent per year, which over the years can be humongous [q6PjSqG-sF4 @ 00:01:31]. On the creditor's side, the failure is attaching to nothing. Kralick warns that a debtor may own real estate without sufficient equity for the lien to attach, because the property is worth less than what is owed on it; the play then is to wait for improved finances or for the debtor to seek financing [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:02:02]. SpeakLegal lists the three standing problems with any abstract: finding the county where the debtor owns real estate, secured loans, tax liens and other judgments that come ahead of the judgment lien, and the debtor going bankrupt to avoid paying [EAME7zlaLqU @ 00:00:57].

The third cluster is timing failures. Kralick says to disregard stories that a judgment is only valid for five years; five years is the statute of limitations for starting an action to revive the judgment lien on real property, and a late revival still works but loses priority against intervening liens and goes to the end of the line [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:03:14]. Hidden Power Files reads the same clock from the record side: a renewal shows up as a new instrument in the deed books, while a judgment lien entry with no subsequent release may still be active [-HFzhDO6v-A @ 00:04:35]. For debtors, the Babel clock is ninety days: the lien can be avoided on both a primary residence and investment properties if it was filed within 90 days of the bankruptcy filing, it may also be knocked out where it impairs the homestead exemption, and attacking it after the case concludes is a lot more complicated, expensive and may not be permissible [q6PjSqG-sF4 @ 00:02:54]. On personal property, Kralick says the judgment is no lien at all; the sheriff's levy creates it, so a creditor sitting behind prior judgments just has to beat them to the punch by getting the sheriff out first [V5NrY_biljg @ 00:04:42].
