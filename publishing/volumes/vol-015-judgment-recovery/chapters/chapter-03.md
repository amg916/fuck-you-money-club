## 1. Build the cohort ledger before valuing the judgment inventory  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with one annual intake cohort and keep four denominators separate: screened matters, accepted engagements, paying resolutions, and recognized operator fees. The illustrative Conservative cohort accepts 60 engagements and produces 20 paying resolutions; Base accepts 200 and produces 80; Scaled accepts 300 and produces 125. Their respective paying-case rates are 33.33%, 40%, and 41.67%. Average client recoveries of $11,000, $14,000, and $16,000 generate $220,000, $1,120,000, and $2,000,000 of underlying recoveries. Applying illustrative contracted commissions of 30%, 27.5%, and 27.5% produces $66,000, $308,000, and $550,000 of provisional fee calculations. These are settlement-workpaper amounts, not an instruction to invoice before an enforceable fee entitlement exists. Scrubs and reversals reduce those calculations to the canonical $60,000, $280,000, and $500,000 recognized revenue. Net recognized fees per paying resolution therefore equal $3,000, $3,500, and $4,000. A larger judgment balance does not improve that yield unless reachable assets, priority, exemptions, and collection timing improve with it. Store the original judgment, accrued-interest calculation, renewal date, assignment chain, and client distribution separately so neither principal nor pass-through recoveries accidentally enter the operator's sales account. Use distinct matter identifiers for affiliated debtors rather than merging their assets into one apparently collectible balance.

The waterfall below is an illustrative allocation that reconciles exactly to the supplied scorecard; it is not a claim that the scorecard specifies each expense account. Gross fee reductions equal $6,000, $28,000, and $50,000, or 9.09% of provisional fees in every scenario. Split those amounts evenly between eligibility or entitlement scrubs and expected fee reversals. A scrub removes a duplicate engagement, unsupported assignment, excessive commission calculation, or otherwise unearned fee before recognition. A reversal allowance addresses an already estimated fee exposed to repayment, disputed allocation, or collection failure. Apply the accounting treatment appropriate to the actual contract and facts; an invoice alone does not establish earned revenue. Contractor delivery labor covers investigator, document, and permitted outsourced casework charges, not an assumed share of an attorney's legal fees. Attorney engagement terms and professional fee-sharing restrictions must be resolved separately. Enforcement expense covers operator-borne disbursements only. If a client reimburses an advance, classify that transaction according to the principal-agent relationship and contract rather than adding both the reimbursement and the original client recovery to service revenue. Retain a document link supporting every deduction so cohort yield can be reconstructed after staff changes.

Acquisition spending is $9,000, $28,000, and $40,000 across the three cases. Dividing by accepted engagements gives intake CAC of $150, $140, and $133.33; dividing by paying resolutions gives outcome CAC of $450, $350, and $320. Report both because an apparently inexpensive lead channel can deliver insolvent debtors and consume enforcement capital without producing fees. Total recognized operating deductions are $48,000, $196,000, and $300,000, leaving the required owner profits. The administrative and employee-support line includes budgeted nonowner payroll, payroll burden, insurance, and administration; contractor charges remain separate. The owner supplies the first full-time role in this illustration, with residual profit serving as owner compensation before personal tax. The Scaled second full-time role is funded within its $44,000 support allowance, an assumption requiring local compensation validation before hiring. Contractor counts indicate available specialists, not four full-time salaries. This distinction explains how headcount and a lean cost base coexist without pretending labor is free. Maintain a separate replacement-cost view for the owner's work, but do not silently deduct that imputed wage from the canonical profit figures. Display owner hours beside profit whenever comparing this operation with a business requiring less personal case management.

## 2. Turn reversal exposure and enforcement decisions into priced controls  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Reserve architecture needs three separate accounts rather than one undifferentiated haircut. First, the pre-recognition scrub removes fee calculations that fail entitlement testing. Second, expected commission reversals reduce recognized fee estimates where appropriate. Third, an expense provision covers losses on operator-funded advances or other distinct obligations. The same failed matter must not generate both a full fee reversal and another expense for that identical lost fee. Record each exposure with matter identifier, exposure type, maximum amount, probability assumption, expected settlement date, and supporting document. Review cohorts by origination quarter and jurisdiction, because a single blended percentage can conceal concentrated assignment defects or an enforcement practice that stopped working. The model's expense provisions are $4,000, $14,000, and $20,000, separate from fee reductions. They are accounting estimates, not automatically funded bank balances. When a provision becomes an actual cash payment, update the cash forecast without charging the same expense twice. If cash losses recur steadily, include expected payments in the recurring cash-cost baseline rather than permanently treating the provision as noncash. Keep release authority with someone other than the caseworker whose production metric improves when reserves fall; reserve releases should follow evidence, not a monthly revenue target.

For each enforcement step, calculate incremental expected contribution using only value still available after superior claims and applicable exemptions. An illustrative $40,000 reachable commercial recovery at a 30% commission creates $12,000 of potential fees. A 35% collection probability produces $4,200 expected gross fees. A 10% fee-loss allowance reduces that to $3,780; subtract $1,200 of incremental contractor work and $900 of operator-borne enforcement expense to obtain $1,680 expected contribution before shared overhead and funding cost. If new lien evidence cuts collection probability to 15%, expected fees after the allowance fall to $1,620 and contribution becomes negative $480. The correct response is to pause that step, renegotiate who funds it, or pursue a cheaper lawful route. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 69 generally directs federal money-judgment execution through the procedure of the forum state unless a federal statute applies. It does not make every asset attachable or authorize an unlicensed operator to practice law. Counsel should select writs, discovery, and local process after reviewing ownership, priority, exemptions, service requirements, and the available evidence. Record why the selected remedy beats the next cheapest lawful alternative.

A bankruptcy filing can destroy the timing assumptions without immediately resolving the ultimate claim value. Under 11 U.S.C. § 362, the automatic stay generally restricts collection and enforcement against the debtor and estate property, subject to statutory scope and exceptions. Stop affected activity promptly, route the matter to counsel, and move expected receipts out of near-term available cash until a supported collection path exists. Do not infer permission to continue from an old writ, a judgment assignment, or a creditor's instruction. Separately, distinguish legal collectibility from economic collectibility: a valid judgment against an insolvent entity may have almost no realizable value. Before approving an advance, retain a current docket check, asset-ownership evidence, priority analysis, and a written recovery budget. PACER can support federal docket review, while state court portals and official UCC filing offices supply different pieces of the record; none alone proves net reachable assets. Lawful data access must be documented for every investigative source. A consumer report requires a permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b, and availability through a vendor does not itself establish that purpose. Recheck material facts before disbursing additional enforcement money, not merely at original intake.

## 3. Fund the DSO and payout-lock gap without borrowing client money  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Define DSO here as days from supported fee recognition to collection of the corresponding cash, then define payout lock as additional days before collected operator fees become unrestricted and transferable. These are sequential intervals in this illustration; if a processor's reported settlement interval already includes its hold, do not add the same days again. Conservative assumes 60 days of DSO plus 15 locked days, Base 75 plus 30, and Scaled 90 plus 45. Exclude the initially noncash expense provisions from annual operating deductions to obtain $44,000, $182,000, and $280,000 of cash-cost proxies. Divide by 365 and multiply by the combined delay. The resulting steady-state float requirements are approximately $9,041, $52,356, and $103,562. Add separately identified liquidity buffers of $10,000, $15,000, and $25,000 to obtain planning targets of $19,041, $67,356, and $128,562. Those buffers cover timing dispersion and emerging cash losses; they are not additional expenses in the profit waterfall. This simplified calculation assumes reasonably even cash spending and receipts. It is a screening calculation rather than a substitute for a weekly forecast when recoveries arrive in a few large settlements or disbursements precede recognition by several litigation cycles.

The supplied startup capital range of $10,000–$75,000 does not automatically fund a mature Scaled operation. Its illustrative $128,562 liquidity target requires retained earnings, documented financing, client-funded disbursements where permitted, or slower intake. Do not make that gap disappear by presenting client collections as working capital. Equally, the stated $2,000–$12,000 monthly operating range should not be mistaken for a ceiling on every variable delivery and enforcement payment at scale. This chapter treats it as an early operating-envelope assumption; Scaled cash costs average about $23,333 monthly before irregular loss payments. The 6–24 month time to first deal introduces another funding layer because the steady-state DSO calculation starts only after fee recognition. At a deliberately lean $2,000 monthly pre-revenue burn, six months requires $12,000 and twenty-four months requires $48,000 before setup costs or a larger enforcement campaign. At $12,000 monthly, the corresponding burn becomes $72,000–$288,000. Build a dated ramp forecast rather than adding every maximum mechanically: some startup expenditure overlaps the later operating cycle, while other advances occur much earlier. Restrict commitments when the forecast minimum cash balance breaches the chosen buffer, even if the annual income statement still shows attractive profit.

Use a thirteen-week cash forecast with separate columns for client funds, restricted operator fees, unrestricted operator cash, contractor payables, enforcement commitments, and probable refunds. Reconcile the matter subledger to bank activity and the general ledger weekly. QuickBooks Online or Xero can hold the operating ledger, while an appropriately configured trust-account system should handle funds subject to trust rules; software selection does not determine the legal classification. Import payment references into the matter record, match partial receipts against approved distributions, and prevent automatic transfers when allocation remains disputed. Court deadline tracking should generate a named owner's task and escalation date rather than merely an email reminder. Payment reconciliation should flag unmatched deposits, duplicate fees, stale checks, and changes to beneficiary instructions. Require independent verification of changed bank details through a previously established contact channel. Financing costs are absent from the canonical waterfall unless absorbed within existing expense allowances. For example, a continuously drawn $50,000 facility at 12% annual interest costs $6,000 before fees and would reduce Base owner profit from $84,000 to $78,000 if incremental. Show that adjustment explicitly instead of preserving the headline margin by hiding funding expense inside client distributions or unreconciled clearing accounts.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Close the matter-level fee worksheet
   - Match each recovery to entitlement evidence and approved distributions.
   - Separate client principal, operator fees, reimbursable advances, scrubs, and reversal estimates before posting revenue.
2. Schedule unrestricted availability
   - Assign expected collection dates and additional release dates.
   - Record DSO and payout locks as nonoverlapping intervals; exclude stayed, disputed, and client-owned amounts from available operating cash.
3. Rebuild the thirteen-week funding forecast
   - Load contractor invoices, enforcement commitments, payroll, and probable refunds.
   - Compare the lowest weekly unrestricted balance with the liquidity buffer and defer unfunded commitments before ordering work.
4. Reconcile and investigate exceptions
   - Tie bank balances, matter subledgers, and general-ledger accounts.
   - Investigate unmatched receipts, stale disbursements, reserve releases, and changed payment instructions before releasing operator distributions.

## 4. Stress the canonical cases and ring-fence adjacent recovery plays  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Stress revenue, delivery cost, and timing independently before combining them. With operating deductions held fixed, a 10% reduction in recognized fees lowers Conservative owner profit to $6,000, Base to $56,000, and Scaled to $150,000. A separate 25% contractor cost increase lowers profits to $7,500, $66,500, and $172,500. Applying both shocks produces $1,500, $38,500, and $122,500. These are sensitivity outputs, not replacements for the canonical scorecard. Break-even recognized fee yield, holding total deductions and paying-case counts constant, is $2,400 per paying case in Conservative, $2,450 in Base, and $2,400 in Scaled. Compare those thresholds with the modeled $3,000, $3,500, and $4,000 yields. If slower cases also require additional attorney work, the fixed-cost assumption understates damage; enter that expense separately. A thirty-day cash delay alone does not reduce accrual profit, but it increases funding requirements by approximately $3,616, $14,959, and $23,014. Any borrowing interest then reduces profit. Set intake limits from the combined downside forecast rather than from the best single margin percentage. A business can remain profitable on paper while failing to fund the contractor invoices necessary to collect the next recovery.

For b2b_class_action_contingency_recovery, track court-approved commercial settlement funds involving payment-card interchange, freight, auto parts, commercial insurance, and SaaS through official notices and administrator instructions. Some funds can be multibillion-dollar programs, but fund size is not operator revenue or an individual claimant's entitlement. Treat the supplied 85–90% small-business nonfiling assertion as an unverified sourcing hypothesis until program-specific evidence supports it. An illustrative ten verified businesses receiving $10,000 each generate $100,000 client payouts. A permitted 25% contingency produces $25,000 provisional fees; a 20% payout or entitlement haircut leaves $20,000 recognized fees. Subtract $7,000 contractor labor, $2,000 acquisition, $3,000 document and administration expense, and $2,000 distinct operating-loss provision to leave $6,000 contribution before shared overhead. At a permitted 33% fee with otherwise identical assumptions, contribution becomes $12,400. These examples remain outside the canonical totals unless explicitly allocated into their existing revenue and cost budgets. Verify operating-business identity and primary processor or vendor statements; follow court-approved third-party claim-filing riders where applicable. If a program prohibits the arrangement, do not substitute an informal authorization. Suppress unsupported claims and use only an administrator-permitted service structure with documented claimant authority and the actual filing deadline.

For out_of_statute_zombie_debt_portfolio_settlement, distinguish limitation periods, extinguishment rules, credit-reporting periods, and any separate judgment-enforcement period before buying or contacting accounts. This consumer-sensitive adjacency does not inherit the commercial judgment workflow. An illustrative $500,000 face-value portfolio bought for $5,000 is purchased principal, not $500,000 revenue. If lawful voluntary resolutions yield $20,000, subtract the $5,000 purchase outlay, $6,000 servicing expense, and $3,000 remediation cash allowance to obtain $6,000 cash contribution before shared overhead; accounting revenue recognition requires a separate purchased-receivable policy. Under a distinct service-only arrangement, $20,000 client collections at 25% produce just $5,000 operator fees, yielding negative $4,000 contribution against those same servicing and remediation amounts. Do not merge these two ownership models. Regulation F, 12 CFR § 1006.26(b), prohibits covered debt collectors from suing or threatening suit on time-barred debt. State law may impose additional restrictions or extinguish obligations. Credit reporting under 15 U.S.C. § 1681c follows separate limits; payment does not authorize re-aging delinquency information or promising deletion of accurate records. Exclude extinguished or otherwise uncollectible obligations. Suppress affected accounts, correct reporting, and suspend collection until counsel approves remediation; never fund the core recovery operation with assumed proceeds from legally defective inventory.
