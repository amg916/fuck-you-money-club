## 1. Identify the creditor buyer, debtor payer, and actual release authority  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin every file with three separate identities: the creditor purchasing recovery services, the judgment debtor whose property may fund payment, and the person authorized to settle or release the judgment. A controller may approve a contingency engagement while only general counsel can approve a discounted satisfaction. A debtor's bookkeeper can transmit a wire without authority to bind the company to settlement terms. Obtain the entered judgment, current creditor identity, assignment chain, accrued balance calculation, and written settlement delegation before quoting outcomes. The creditor's acute pain is usually a written-off receivable, an aging reserve, or repeated spending on searches that never become enforceable remedies. The debtor's pain is different: a lawful levy, documented lien interfering with a sale, or the expense of continuing a dispute. None creates cash where assets are exempt, encumbered, or absent. Ask the creditor which employee owns the recovery budget, which person can approve enforcement expenses, and which signature releases collateral. Record those answers in Clio or an equivalent matter system with supporting documents, not merely a contact list. A signed engagement from someone without delegated authority is an operational defect, even when the underlying judgment is valid. Resolve that defect before ordering an expensive asset investigation.

Sell a bounded decision process rather than the nominal judgment balance. A creditor holding a $200,000 judgment might rationally authorize a $2,000 investigation when an identified receivable could support a $40,000 settlement. At an illustrative 25% service fee, that recovery produces $10,000 of operator revenue and $30,000 of creditor proceeds before separately agreed expenses. If the creditor also reimburses $2,000 from those proceeds, their economic recovery becomes $28,000; the reimbursement requires separate accounting rather than automatic treatment as additional margin. Compare this result with a documented alternative, such as another year of monitoring at a fixed cost, instead of claiming the full face amount is collectible. For debtor negotiations, establish a lawful reason to act now: an actual closing date, an enforceable order, or a documented discount expiration. Do not manufacture deadlines, threaten unavailable remedies, or imply criminal consequences for ordinary nonpayment. Counsel determines which execution procedures fit the forum. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 69 generally points federal money-judgment enforcement toward state procedure unless a federal statute applies. The operating product is therefore a jurisdiction-specific collection plan backed by evidence, not a national script promising identical leverage against every debtor.

Counterparty screening must reach both sides of the engagement. Verify the creditor's existence through the relevant secretary of state, confirm the signer's authority, and resolve competing assignments before accepting instructions. Verify the debtor's exact legal identity, bankruptcy status, appeal posture, enforceability period, and ownership of the proposed payment source. PACER supports federal docket review; state court portals, county recording offices, and authorized UCC searches answer different questions and should not be treated as interchangeable asset databases. A UCC filing indicates a claimed security interest, not necessarily the current payoff or perfected priority against every asset. Obtain the relevant instruments and have counsel assess competing rights. Record whether the obligation arose from business activity or a consumer transaction; a judgment does not automatically remove consumer-debt protections. The FDCPA definition in 15 U.S.C. § 1692a concerns qualifying consumer obligations, while state collection laws may reach additional conduct or commercial claims. License coverage, lawful data access, and local representation rules belong in intake. A commercial focus does not authorize a nonlawyer to appear for another creditor or bypass restrictions through a paper assignment. When authority, identity, or coverage remains unresolved, restrict work to permitted screening and suspend enforcement spending.

## 2. Select a settlement rail that preserves ownership and clearing evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat payment instructions as an executable settlement schedule, not an email containing bank coordinates. Identify the payer, beneficiary, receiving institution, matter reference, permitted amount, due date, allocation rule, and conditions for releasing the judgment. Prefer a lawfully designated attorney trust account or appropriately authorized escrow arrangement when disputed ownership, multiple beneficiaries, or conditional releases require controlled disbursement. The account must belong to the actual authorized custodian; calling an ordinary operating account escrow changes nothing. Verify wiring instructions through a previously established telephone number and document the callback. Reject instructions that redirect funds to an unrelated personal account, substitute cryptocurrency without an agreed operational reason, or change beneficiary details immediately before settlement. A genuine closing deadline is not a reason to skip identity controls. Wire transfers provide useful bank references but can still involve fraud, recall requests, legal restraint, or erroneous beneficiary information. ACH entries add authorization and return exposure whose timing depends on transaction type and applicable rules. No single hold period makes every payment final. Use the receiving bank's actual settlement evidence and counsel-approved release conditions; do not file a satisfaction merely because a debtor supplies a screenshot marked sent or a payment processor displays a provisional balance.

The eight rails below solve different problems and should not be sold as interchangeable checkout options. A retainer funds authorized work; it does not prove the debtor has paid the judgment. A statutory disbursement routes funds through a sheriff, court officer, or bankruptcy administrator under governing procedure. Factoring transfers economic rights or finances receivables and therefore changes counterparty risk rather than simply accelerating the same collection. Milestone holds are a contractual control layered onto an account and payment method, not an independent banking network. Platform checkout can simplify small installments, but Stripe or another provider must expressly support the actual collection activity and account structure. Processor reserves, chargebacks, and acceptable-use restrictions can defeat apparently convenient economics. Do not disguise recovery services as ordinary retail sales to obtain processing. Compare an illustrative $20,000 settlement paid by wire with the same settlement paid through a hypothetical card channel charging 3%: processing alone consumes $600 before disputes or reserves. That rate is a comparison assumption, not a vendor quote. The agreement must specify who bears permitted payment costs and whether they reduce the fee base. Adding an unauthorized collection charge to the debtor's balance can create a separate violation rather than protect margin.

Build clearing around a matter-level ledger with separate columns for gross receipts, lawful interest allocation, reimbursable costs, earned operator fees, counsel charges, creditor proceeds, and unresolved holds. Where a statute prescribes allocation, that rule governs before internal preferences; California Code of Civil Procedure § 695.221 provides one jurisdiction-specific allocation rule for money received in satisfaction of a money judgment. For an illustrative $40,000 recovery with a contractually permitted 25% operator fee and no separate deductions, record $40,000 received, $10,000 earned service revenue, and $30,000 payable to the creditor. The $30,000 never becomes operator revenue merely because it passed through a controlled account. If $2,000 of authorized creditor expenses are also reimbursed from proceeds, creditor cash falls to $28,000 under that expressly agreed waterfall. Whether the reimbursement is presented gross or net in business accounts requires a consistent accounting assessment. Reconcile bank activity, custodian statements, and individual client ledgers before disbursement; QuickBooks may support operating accounts but does not replace required trust-account controls. Use a second reviewer for beneficiary changes and outgoing distributions. After release conditions are satisfied, complete the jurisdiction's satisfaction procedure promptly. In California, § 724.010 addresses satisfaction of judgment; do not retain a satisfied lien as informal leverage for an unrelated fee dispute.

**Eight Settlement Rails: Custody, Clearing Exposure, and Release Controls**

| Rail and appropriate operating use | Who controls the money movement | Principal clearing or economic failure mode | Required evidence before distribution or release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wire transfer for documented negotiated lump sum settlements | Verified debtor bank sends to the authorized beneficiary | Spoofed instructions or restrained funds defeat expected availability | Bank receipt reference plus independently verified beneficiary details |
| Escrow arrangement for conditional payoff and lien release | Authorized escrow holder follows signed transaction disbursement instructions | Conflicting release conditions strand funds despite apparent agreement | Executed instructions identifying beneficiaries and exact release conditions |
| ACH transfer for authorized recurring debtor settlement installments | Originator and receiving banks process properly authorized entries | Returns and authorization disputes reverse previously displayed balances | Entry authorization plus bank status and applicable risk review |
| Retainer funding for defined research and permitted recovery work | Client funds the designated account under engagement terms | Unearned advances become improperly treated as earned revenue | Scope documentation and lawful earned fee withdrawal calculation |
| Statutory disbursement following levy or court administered distribution | Authorized officer or administrator applies governing payment procedure | Priority contests and statutory processing delays reduce timing certainty | Official remittance record and reviewed allocation of proceeds |
| Platform checkout for expressly permitted small settlement payments | Approved processor controls settlement timing and reserve deductions | Chargebacks or prohibited activity restrictions freeze expected payouts | Provider acceptance and reconciled net settlement transaction report |
| Factoring arrangement for selling documented eligible payment rights | Purchaser pays the seller under a rights transfer agreement | Recourse obligations and defective assignments erase apparent liquidity | Verified transferable rights and written recourse pricing analysis |
| Milestone holds layered onto a valid custodial arrangement | Named custodian releases funds against objective documented milestones | Vague milestones permit strategic withholding or premature distributions | Signed milestone definitions and evidence of each completed condition |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock authority and settlement terms
   - Obtain signed payer, creditor, and custodian instructions.
   - Specify amounts, beneficiaries, expense allocation, fee base, release conditions, and the process for changing bank details.
2. Verify receipt without relying on screenshots
   - Match bank or custodian evidence to the matter ledger.
   - Confirm payer identity, payment reference, account ownership, transaction status, and rail-specific return or restraint exposure.
3. Reconcile the contractual and statutory waterfall
   - Have a second reviewer approve the distribution calculation.
   - Separate client proceeds, earned fees, permitted reimbursements, counsel charges, and disputed amounts; resolve differences first.
4. Disburse and complete the required satisfaction record
   - Send approved distributions and document the judgment update.
   - Retain confirmations and statements, meet applicable satisfaction deadlines, and close or revise monitoring only after verification.

## 3. Protect earned fees without confusing custody, legal fees, or revenue  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Fee protection starts in the creditor engagement, not with secret control over debtor money. Define the covered judgment, recoveries included in the fee base, treatment of interest and costs, fee earning event, direct-payment reporting duty, termination mechanics, and any lawful, narrowly defined post-termination entitlement. Specify whether a fee arises on cleared cash, property actually received, or another measurable event; avoid billing on a settlement promise that may never perform. Require the creditor to notify the operator promptly when a debtor pays directly, and provide reasonable statement access to reconcile covered receipts. An enforceable payment direction acknowledged by the authorized custodian can reduce diversion, but it cannot override statutory priorities, trust duties, or another party's superior rights. Do not invent a lien merely by naming one in an invoice. Counsel should assess any assignment, security arrangement, or contractual remedy under the applicable law. Keep attorney compensation separate from the operator's lawful recovery-service compensation: a commercial agreement cannot authorize prohibited sharing of legal fees with nonlawyers. If the licensed and ethical structure cannot support the proposed contingent arrangement, redesign the permitted service scope and pricing rather than routing payments through an intermediary to conceal the same economics.

Reconcile the scorecard using service revenue only. For an illustrative portfolio with a uniform, legally permitted 25% service fee, the Conservative case requires $240,000 of annual client recoveries to produce $60,000 of recognized operator revenue. Deduct $48,000 of modeled operating expenses to obtain $12,000 annual owner net profit, a 20% margin, with 1 FT + 3 C. The Base case requires $1,120,000 of recoveries to produce $280,000 revenue; $196,000 of expenses leaves $84,000 profit at 30.0%, again with 1 FT + 3 C. The Scaled case requires $2,000,000 of recoveries to produce $500,000 revenue; $300,000 of expenses leaves $200,000 profit at 40%, with 2 FT + 4 C. These collection volumes explain the fee bridge, not additional scorecard revenue. The fee assumption must be replaced wherever statutory caps or negotiated terms differ. Expense totals include the full modeled cost burden; contractor headcounts do not establish full-time compensation for every contractor. The stated $2,000–$12,000 monthly operating range is an initial planning reference, not a ceiling overriding the annual scenario arithmetic. Base and Scaled annual expenses require explicit capacity and variable-cost budgets beyond a simplistic twelve-month multiplication of that reference range.

Underwrite each enforcement increment before committing portfolio capacity. Suppose a documented $50,000 reachable asset has a 40% probability of producing a full recovery after priority review, and the operator would earn 25% on collected cash. Expected service revenue is $5,000: $50,000 multiplied by 40% multiplied by 25%. If incremental operator-borne investigation and enforcement costs are $3,000, expected contribution is $2,000 before overhead, delay, and adverse outcomes. That estimate supports a bounded experiment, not an assertion that the $50,000 belongs in revenue. If a senior lien leaves only $10,000 reachable, the same calculation yields $1,000 expected fees and a $2,000 expected loss against those costs. Stop or obtain a genuinely different cost allocation instead of chasing sunk expenses. Require written authorization for third-party charges and clarify whether the creditor reimburses them even if recovery fails. Maintain separate aging for unpaid service invoices and uncollected debtor obligations; they have different counterparties and remedies. With $10,000–$75,000 startup capital and a stated 6–24 months to first deal, liquidity discipline matters as much as nominal margin. Reserve enough cash for already authorized work, and never borrow another client's proceeds to bridge a slow-paying matter.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make diversion difficult without taking unauthorized control**
> 
> Veteran operators protect fees with evidence, delegated authority, and reconciled receipts rather than aggressive language unsupported by the engagement or applicable law.
> - Attach a sample waterfall to the engagement using a $40,000 recovery; have the creditor confirm the fee base and every expense deduction.
> - Use one matter reference across settlement instructions, bank records, invoices, and satisfaction documents so direct payments remain traceable.
> - Obtain current senior lien payoff information before buying another asset search; the priority subtraction can eliminate the entire recovery thesis.
> - Recheck bankruptcy immediately before a levy, settlement demand, or distribution decision, and route any newly discovered filing to counsel.

## 4. Price distressed recoveries and bankruptcy claims against verified distributions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

For distressed_asset_recovery, the judgment-recovery variant is a dormant commercial judgment whose creditor lacks current asset intelligence or an economical enforcement partner. Suppose a $100,000 judgment supports a verified $30,000 settlement and a lawful 25% contingent service fee. Operator revenue is $7,500, creditor proceeds are $22,500 before separately agreed costs, and $1,500 of operator-borne incremental work leaves $6,000 contribution before overhead. The judgment's face amount is neither revenue nor a collectible asset estimate. Screen title to the claim, renewal requirements, debtor ownership, exemptions, senior liens, and likely enforcement expense before treating the opportunity as mispriced. Other variants include tax-sale overages, unclaimed state funds, and government surplus assets, but their ownership rules, solicitation restrictions, fee caps, and acquisition mechanics differ. Do not import the illustrative 25% rate into a statutory recovery program without checking its requirements. An asset acquisition spread also belongs in a separate investment model rather than the canonical service-fee scorecard. The specified fallback is diversification across multi-state dockets and attorney-backed filing partnerships where required. Diversification must preserve state-specific licensing and procedure; it does not mean mass filing against unverified debtors. Stop an uneconomic file before using another jurisdiction merely to repeat the same unsupported asset assumption.

For chapter11_trade_claim_convenience_class_rollup, the economic payer is the estate or plan disbursing entity, not an operator pressuring the debtor outside bankruptcy. Review Schedule E/F, the claims register, proof of claim, underlying invoices, defenses, plan, disclosure statement, and distribution notices together. A scheduled trade balance is a lead, not proof that an undisputed allowed claim will receive the advertised convenience-class percentage. Consider a documented $10,000 claim with a supported 20% expected general-unsecured distribution. Purchasing it for $1,200 and spending $200 on diligence and transfer produces an illustrative $600 expected spread before overhead and time value: $2,000 expected proceeds less $1,400 committed cash. A downside distribution of 5% returns $500 and loses $900 against that same cash commitment. These proprietary purchase economics are separate from recognized recovery-service fees. Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 3001(e) governs relevant claim-transfer procedures, with different treatment depending on transfer timing and purpose. Check plan-specific aggregation, claim-splitting, election, and related-holder provisions before assuming that several purchased claims retain separate convenience-class benefits. If that thesis fails, stop affected purchases, enforce documented seller recourse where available, and price only supported general-unsecured recoveries. Seller recourse has value only when enforceable against a seller capable of paying.

Bankruptcy changes the operating path immediately. Under 11 U.S.C. § 362, the automatic stay generally restricts covered collection activity against the debtor and estate property, subject to statutory exceptions and court orders. A public docket alert should pause affected workflows for counsel review, not trigger a faster demand before competitors react. Section 502 addresses claim allowance, § 507 establishes specified priorities, and § 1122 governs classification subject to its terms; none establishes a universal convenience-class payout. Model the actual plan waterfall and timing. Outside bankruptcy, refresh credit evidence around meaningful events: a business sale, missed installment, new secured borrowing, returned payment, or proposed release. Separate observable facts from estimates in the matter record. A bank receipt proves a payment event; it does not prove future installment capacity. A property record identifies recorded interests; it does not establish realizable net equity. Automate docket deadlines and payment reconciliation, but require human review when ownership, priority, or authority changes. Close the loop with a monthly cohort report showing recovered cash, earned fees, unreimbursed expenses, collection duration, and stopped files. That report reveals whether the enterprise is earning the canonical margins or simply accumulating large judgments and contingent expectations that never clear into distributable money.
