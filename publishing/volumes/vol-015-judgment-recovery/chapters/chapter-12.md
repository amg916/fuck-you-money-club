## 1. Days 1–7: Build the Authority, Evidence, and Spending System  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one state, one commercial judgment segment, and one documented enforcement route. The first deliverable is an operating boundary, not a debtor contact list. Record the originating obligation, judgment court, creditor identity, debtor entity, judgment date, balance method, and counsel responsible for interpreting local procedure. Commercial classification follows the transaction's purpose, not merely the presence of a business name; 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(5) defines covered consumer debt, while state collection rules may reach further. Have local counsel determine licensing, registration, bonding, assignment formalities, and which activities require an attorney before outreach or representation begins. An assignment does not itself authorize a nonlawyer to practice law. Separate creditor acquisition from debtor communications so marketing staff cannot drift into collection activity. Use PACER for federal records and the relevant state court portal for state judgments; retain source identifiers and retrieval dates rather than treating an aggregator summary as controlling evidence. Set up an encrypted case repository, role-based permissions, and a deadline ledger with a human reviewer. By Day 7, every proposed activity must have an owner, a permitted method, a spending limit, and a stop condition. Unresolved authority questions block the affected activity, not unrelated research.

Use the following chapter-level execution map to operationalize Candidate #8's fifteen mapped plays; these labels organize the sprint rather than assert an external taxonomy. P01 is segment selection; P02 is creditor sourcing; P03 is docket verification; P04 is judgment validity and deadline review; P05 is assignment or engagement authority; P06 is bankruptcy and discharge screening; P07 is lawful asset research; P08 is lien and exemption analysis; P09 is recovery underwriting; P10 is attorney-directed discovery; P11 is attorney-directed enforcement; P12 is authorized settlement coordination; P13 is payment reconciliation; P14 is creditor reporting; and P15 is portfolio triage and renewal tracking. One full-time lead owns sourcing, underwriting, client communication, and contractor coordination. Contractor A is local enforcement counsel; contractor B performs permitted records research; contractor C maintains bookkeeping and deadline controls. Counsel retains professional judgment and required client relationships. Initial weekly capacity is forty lead hours, four counsel hours, eight research hours, and three bookkeeping hours, purchased within written caps. This is a four-person pod, not four full-time salaries. Each case card identifies its current play, next evidence request, responsible person, and maximum authorized spend. An overdue task moves into the daily exception queue instead of silently aging.

Authorize no more than $6,000 of committed launch spending through Day 30: $1,800 for counsel and authority review, $900 for research support, $450 for bookkeeping setup, $350 for court records and permitted data, $250 for software, $650 for operating administration and insurance-related setup, $600 for approved case disbursements, and $1,000 contingency. Count purchase commitments when approved, not when invoices arrive, and do not count client money as available working capital. These allocations are planning limits, not vendor quotes; replace estimates with written prices before ordering. If mandatory licensing or insurance consumes the allowance, reduce activity or fund the difference before proceeding. The canonical startup range is $10,000–$75,000 and monthly operating expense range is $2,000–$12,000; neither authorizes spending beyond available cash. Preserve at least six months of measured recurring overhead outside the sprint budget. At a $2,000 monthly floor, completing the full $14,000 ninety-day plan therefore requires $26,000 of available capital before additional reserves. A $10,000 entrant should remain in lower-cost research and setup until funded for the chosen workload. Because first deals can take six to twenty-four months, six months of reserve is a minimum review threshold, not complete funding for every possible collection cycle.

## 2. Days 8–14: Convert Dockets into Qualified Recovery Files  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 8–14 turn public records into a narrow, auditable prospect cohort. Screen twenty unique judgments this week and forty cumulatively by Day 30; count a judgment once even when multiple creditors, guarantors, or court entries appear. A screened record must contain an actual judgment entry, identifiable parties, a transaction-purpose classification, and an initial enforceability check. A qualified file additionally needs documented creditor interest, a complete authority path, a plausible nonexempt asset or income source, preliminary priority analysis, and no unresolved stop condition. Target eight qualified files from forty screens, a twenty-percent qualification rate. This threshold tests sourcing quality, not the probability of collection. Approach creditors through accurately described services and verified business contact channels; do not imply court affiliation, guaranteed payment, or powers the operator lacks. Track every exclusion by reason: expired enforcement period, disputed ownership, apparent insolvency, consumer-debt complexity, bankruptcy restriction, or adverse priority. The exclusion ledger matters as much as the prospect list because it shows whether poor yield reflects a repairable sourcing filter or a fundamentally weak segment. Stop researching a rejected file once the decisive defect is established unless counsel identifies a specific, economical cure.

For each promising file, distinguish ownership, location, value, and legal reachability. A property record can identify an apparent owner without proving reachable equity; an entity registration can identify an officer without proving that officer owes the judgment. Use county recorder records, secretary-of-state filings, court documents, and appropriately authorized commercial databases to establish an evidence chain. Do not obtain consumer reports without a permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b, and do not use false pretenses to obtain bank customer information prohibited by 15 U.S.C. § 6821. Where applicable, counsel evaluates federal tax lien priority under 26 U.S.C. § 6323 and the selected state's enactment of UCC § 9-317 for secured-creditor and lien-creditor conflicts. Those rules do not replace examination of actual filing dates, collateral descriptions, perfection, exemptions, and senior balances. Calculate estimated reachable equity after supported senior claims, exemptions, sale costs, and ownership adjustments. Mark valuation dates and uncertainty explicitly in narrative fields. A social-media photograph or stale database hit is a research lead, never an asset certification. Require two independent supporting records before treating an asset lead as a basis for enforcement spending.

Underwrite fee potential separately from principal and client proceeds. For illustration, a $100,000 judgment with $40,000 of modeled recoveries and a contractually agreed thirty-percent service fee produces $12,000 of potential operator fees, not $100,000 of revenue. If the modeled collection outcome has a fifty-percent probability over twenty-four months, expected fees are $6,000 before timing adjustments. Against $2,000 of expected unreimbursed direct costs, the expected-fee-to-cost ratio is three; expected contribution is $4,000 before overhead, taxes, and owner compensation conventions. These underwriting figures are neither booked revenue nor the canonical annual profit cases. Record the probability rationale, collection horizon, fee trigger, cost payer, reimbursement ordering, and downside if no recovery occurs. Use conservative liquidation estimates rather than listing prices. To authorize a recovery plan, require expected fees at least three times expected unreimbursed direct costs and reachable equity at least 1.5 times the proposed enforcement disbursement budget. Both conditions must pass, and neither substitutes for lawful authority. If the direct-cost estimate is zero, investigate omitted work instead of claiming an infinite ratio. Counsel reviews any structure involving legal-fee sharing, claim purchases, or nonlawyer ownership restrictions before the economics are offered to creditors.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Ways Experienced Recovery Operators Avoid Expensive False Positives**
> 
> Buy evidence that changes a decision; avoid accumulating records that merely make a weak file look substantial.
> - Search satisfaction and assignment entries alongside the judgment; an impressive face amount can conceal payment or broken ownership.
> - Match the judgment debtor to the asset owner by supported identifiers; similar entity names are a frequent source of wasted research.
> - Request current senior payoff evidence when available through authorized channels; recorded original lien amounts do not establish remaining equity.
> - Ask counsel for the cheapest decisive next step and a written stop price before authorizing discovery or enforcement disbursements.

## 3. Days 15–21: Rehearse the Recovery Workflow Without Manufacturing Revenue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The third week converts qualified interest into executable instructions without assuming that a signature creates cash. Target two signed, authority-complete engagements or valid assignments by Day 30 and at least one counsel-reviewed recovery plan. A signed engagement is an intake milestone, not the canonical first paid deal. Agreements must identify the creditor, judgment, scope, authorized communications, fee calculation, expense responsibility, settlement authority, termination treatment, and reporting cadence. Require documentary support for the ownership chain and address any assignment filing required by the selected court. Counsel determines whether the operator acts as service provider, lawful assignee, or another permitted role; do not blend those roles opportunistically. For federal proceedings, Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 69 generally ties execution procedure to state practice, subject to applicable federal statutes, and permits judgment-related discovery within its terms. State proceedings require their own procedural analysis. The operator prepares records and instructions, while authorized counsel controls legal filings and appearances where required. Maintain one approved next action per file so researchers cannot create simultaneous, unbudgeted workstreams. Before each material action, refresh bankruptcy status, check recent payments, and confirm that authority and spending approvals still cover the proposed step.

Run a complete tabletop case through P10–P14 before accepting live collections. Counsel identifies an appropriate discovery or enforcement sequence, estimates service and filing expenses, and documents the response to a contested motion or failed service. The lead records authorized settlement parameters without promising acceptance beyond the creditor's instructions. Contractor C then processes a synthetic $10,000 receipt using a sample thirty-percent fee agreement: $3,000 allocated to operator fees and $7,000 to creditor proceeds when no separate expense deductions apply. Use test records clearly separated from production books. Actual funds must follow the applicable custody, trust-account, contract, and professional rules; do not assume an ordinary payment processor or general operating account is suitable. QuickBooks can record separate ledger categories, but software does not establish custody authority. Match the bank receipt, case identifier, contractual waterfall, creditor remittance, and earned fee entry. Investigate every difference before distribution. A disputed allocation remains in the exception queue under the applicable handling requirements. The test passes only when another person can reproduce the allocation from source documents and identify exactly which amount belongs to the operator rather than the creditor.

Install automation only where inputs and exceptions are understood. Court deadline tracking should capture the triggering event, governing rule, jurisdiction, computed date, human verification, and proof of completion. Use a shared Outlook calendar or an equivalent controlled system with reminders thirty, fourteen, and seven days before suitable long-horizon deadlines; short deadlines require tighter settings. Renewal and dormancy rules vary, so never apply a universal judgment lifespan. Payment reconciliation should suggest matches from bank references and case identifiers but require human review before allocating ambiguous receipts. A bankruptcy match is a stop signal, not an automation nuisance: 11 U.S.C. § 362 can stay covered enforcement, and 11 U.S.C. § 524 can restrict collection of discharged personal liability. Counsel evaluates scope, exceptions, and any relief before work resumes. Preserve notices and decisions in the file. Set service targets of one business day for reviewing stop alerts, two business days for reconciling received funds, and weekly review of every active deadline. These are internal management targets, subordinate to shorter legal obligations. Measure exceptions and rework hours alongside throughput; a pod processing more files with unresolved custody or deadline errors is degrading, not becoming more productive.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Validate the case packet
   - Attach judgment, ownership evidence, engagement terms, and a dated balance calculation.
   - The lead confirms completeness; counsel resolves enforceability and representation questions before affected activity proceeds.
2. Refresh stops and economics
   - Recheck bankruptcy restrictions, recent payments, priority evidence, and the approved expense ceiling.
   - Stop the proposed action if legal authority or either underwriting ratio no longer passes.
3. Execute the authorized next step
   - Assign one accountable person and record the instruction, due date, and committed cost.
   - Counsel performs reserved legal work; the operator coordinates records, logistics, and permitted communications.
4. Reconcile and report the outcome
   - Match receipts and invoices to the case, then update the creditor and next-action ledger.
   - Keep client proceeds separate from earned service fees and investigate every unexplained allocation difference.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Reviews at Day 60/90: Kill, Fix, or Scale the Evidence  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Days 22–30 complete the forty-screen cohort, audit eight qualified files, seek two authority-complete agreements, and finish one executable recovery plan. Reaching these targets supports a controlled next sprint, not a forecast of immediate fees. Keep cumulative committed spending within $6,000 at Day 30, $10,000 at Day 60, and $14,000 at Day 90; later limits include earlier spending rather than adding to it. Before each extension, recalculate six months of reserve using actual recurring overhead and committed case obligations. Stop new commitments if reserves fail, even when the pipeline passes. Review timesheets against the initial forty lead hours and fifteen combined contractor hours per week. At eighty-percent planned utilization, investigate bottlenecks before adding files; at sustained overload, reduce intake. Changes to hours require revised prices and an affordable commitment ledger. A lead should not hide free evening work to make the model appear efficient. Weekly reporting includes screened files, qualified files, signed authority, executable plans, committed spending, expected unreimbursed costs, and exception counts. Actual collections and earned fees appear separately, including zero when that is the observed result. Never fill an empty collections column with judgment face value or probability-weighted pipeline.

Apply decisions in this order: stop-condition review, Kill thresholds, Scale thresholds, then Fix for the remaining cases. Kill means stop new acquisition or retire the failed segment while preserving existing duties; it does not mean abandon clients or deadlines. Any unresolved material authority defect, restricted enforcement attempt, client-fund shortage, or insufficient reserve stops the affected work immediately. Commercial Kill thresholds are fewer than four qualified files after forty screens at Day 30, fewer than eight after eighty screens at Day 60, or fewer than twelve after 120 screens at Day 90. Do not infer market failure from an undersized sample: classify insufficient throughput as Fix unless another stop applies. Fix permits one fourteen-day repair experiment with a named owner, measurable target, and at most $1,000 reallocated inside the existing ceiling. Scale requires all cumulative funnel targets, passing underwriting on every executable plan, complete evidence fields, zero unresolved material control exceptions, and sufficient reserve. Increase screening capacity by no more than twenty-five percent for the next sprint. This is process expansion inside the existing pod, not permission to hire the scaled team or represent uncollected fees as recurring revenue.

At Day 60, require eighty screens, sixteen qualified files, four authority-complete agreements, and three executable plans for Scale. At Day 90, require 120 screens, twenty-four qualified files, six agreements, and five executable plans. Absence of collected revenue alone does not trigger Kill during this period because the canonical first-deal horizon is six to twenty-four months. Instead, compare observed milestones against case-specific dates and revisit probability assumptions when service fails, assets disappear, or senior claims increase. Preserve the annual scorecard exactly: $60,000 recognized fees at twenty-percent net margin produces $12,000 owner net profit; $280,000 at thirty-percent produces $84,000; $500,000 at forty-percent produces $200,000. Corresponding total annual costs are $48,000, $196,000, and $300,000. Launch budgets are cash-control envelopes, not substitutes for those full-year cost structures. Conservative and Base retain one full-time operator plus three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time people plus four contractors. Do not move to six people solely because Day 90 process targets pass. Require a separate hiring model supported by collected-fee history, case concentration analysis, paid workload, and reserves sufficient for the new fixed cost. The launch succeeds when evidence improves faster than irreversible spending.
