## 1. Qualify the borrower, classify the product and route an evidence-backed package  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin with an engagement record, not a lender blast. Assign a durable deal identifier and record the borrowing entity, beneficial ownership evidence, operating states, requested product, use of proceeds, requested principal, existing liens and authorized contact. Obtain permission for each intended document collection and lender submission; a general website inquiry does not authorize unrestricted distribution of bank statements. In HubSpot or Salesforce, keep workflow metadata separate from encrypted source documents in an access-controlled repository. Store document hashes and repository references rather than tax returns in CRM notes. One full-time originator owns borrower communication; the second owns packaging, lender responses and closing evidence. Two contractors provide bounded underwriting and accounting support without unrestricted export privileges. Before quoting compensation, determine which state licensing and commercial-financing disclosure rules attach to the activity, product and borrower location. California Financial Code section 22100 is a concrete licensing starting point, while sections 22800 through 22805 require a separate disclosure analysis for covered transactions. Establish whether the broker acts for the borrower, lender or a disclosed referral partner. Record the actual fee payer and payment trigger before spending underwriting time; an unsigned percentage assumption is neither a receivable nor a dependable acquisition budget.

Build the lender matrix around rejection reasons that an underwriter can verify. Equipment placements need vendor identity, asset description, invoice, age, residual-value assumptions and the proposed lien position. Receivables facilities need invoice-level aging, dilution, customer concentration, disputes, contra accounts and assignment restrictions; aggregate revenue alone cannot establish eligible collateral. SBA candidates need program-specific eligibility, ownership information, use-of-proceeds support and compensation documentation under 13 CFR 103.5 where applicable. Bridge requests need an independently supportable repayment event, current debt, maturity dates and downside collateral analysis. Store each lender's current written criteria, effective date, geographic coverage, maximum exposure and response service level. Rank only eligible destinations, then obtain borrower authorization for the selected submission sequence. A numerical score prioritizes analyst work; it does not approve credit or replace the lender's decision. Preserve business-credit application and disposition records consistent with the applicable provisions of Regulation B, including 12 CFR 1002.9 and 1002.12. Do not silently label a lender decline as a borrower withdrawal. Track the deciding institution, reason received, notice responsibility and next permitted action. Keep a second qualified lender available for every important product so concentration cannot turn one policy change into a stalled production month.

The mapped play fmcsa_dot_inspection_trigger_arbitrage belongs in sourcing, not automated credit judgment. Do not assume every SMS field or carrier-level result is publicly available. Reconcile USDOT identifiers, authority status, insurance filings and event dates before contacting a fleet. The proposed mandatory 21-day insurance-filing window must not become a hard-coded eligibility rule: verify the actual authority-processing timetable and insurance requirements for the carrier and activity. A new authority or maintenance citation is a conversation trigger, not proof of financing demand or financial distress. Illustratively, 200 verified records producing 20 authorized conversations, four packages and one paid placement yield $10,000 retained fees at the chapter's reference ticket. ## 2. Install six executable modules that serialize transitions and isolate fee accounting  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use the six reference modules below as a deliberately bounded SQLite implementation for whole-fee invoices and whole-invoice receipts. Save each named artifact in one directory, initialize a fresh local database with schema.sql, and run qa.py against its temporary database before adapting integrations. This reference has not been executed here. The design uses integer cents, immutable intake payloads, unique command identifiers, explicit state transitions and BEGIN IMMEDIATE transactions. SQLite serializes writers; uniqueness constraints reject duplicate business events even when separate workers submit different command keys. Every journal row contains equal debit and credit amounts by construction. Borrower principal is informational metadata outside this fee ledger and never enters fee income. Database files belong on local storage supported by SQLite locking, not a shared network filesystem with uncertain lock behavior. Restrict direct database writes to the application identity and keep encrypted, tested backups. These conditions remove the identified local read-check-write races; they do not make an arbitrary external service transactional. Partial receipts, fee amendments, refunds, taxes and multi-currency postings require additional schemas and tests before use. Do not force those cases through the whole-invoice demonstration by editing historical rows or changing a previously accepted fee.

The workflow separates commercial progress from accounting evidence. RECEIVED means the authorized intake exists; VERIFIED means an accountable reviewer accepted its evidence; ESCROW means a documented third-party escrow arrangement applies, not that the broker holds client money. Ordinary loan placements may move directly from VERIFIED to FUNDED when lender funding evidence supports that transition. INVOICED requires a contractually earned fee and creates accounts receivable against fee income. PAID requires a matched bank receipt and clears that receivable. Evidence references must resolve to retained artifacts, while reviewer identities must come from authenticated application sessions rather than borrower-supplied fields. The sample function checks their presence; the surrounding service must enforce their authenticity and access permissions. A cancellation is available before invoicing, while post-invoice disputes belong in an adjustment workflow not implemented here. Enqueue a nonfinancial notification inside the same transaction as each transition. Dispatch outside the database transaction so a slow email or CRM endpoint cannot hold the writer lock. The adapter must use a provider-supported idempotency key and stable payload. Without that capability, concurrent delivery and crash recovery cannot promise single external delivery, even though ledger postings remain singular.

Treat the reconciliation view as an exception detector rather than a substitute for the bank statement. It shows each deal's earned fees, receipts and residual receivable, while the operational ledger preserves the command, reviewer and evidence supporting each movement. For every cash posting, match the bank's immutable transaction reference, amount, date and paying entity to the engagement record. The reference requires one unique evidence reference per journal entry, preventing reuse of the same receipt identifier for another whole-fee payment. Accountants must still verify that the identifier corresponds to genuine settled cash. Do not mark a deal paid from a remittance email or a pending transfer screenshot. Run the QA module after schema changes; it exercises concurrent duplicate intake, concurrent transition retries, conflicting command payloads, invalid state order and receivable clearance. Add integration tests for provider timeouts and restored backups before enabling notifications. Read daily exception queues for funded but uninvoiced deals, aging receivables, delivery retries and unmatched bank activity. A reviewer signs the weekly reconciliation using a separate identity from the originator whenever staffing permits. Export the resulting accounting evidence to the general ledger; retain the brokerage database as the deal-level operational subledger, not an undocumented replacement for the complete company books.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Initialize and test the bounded reference
   - Save the six artifacts using their named filenames and run python qa.py.
   - The test uses an isolated temporary database; initialize production separately and do not treat unexecuted sample code as verified deployment.
2. Attach authenticated evidence review
   - Resolve evidence references through an access-controlled document service before invoking advance.
   - Derive reviewer identity from the signed-in session, enforce product-specific permissions and preserve the reviewed artifact version.
3. Prove external retry behavior
   - Test notification timeout, concurrent retry and crash-after-send behavior against the selected provider.
   - Require atomic idempotency across the complete retry horizon; otherwise leave automated delivery disabled and preserve the pending outbox.
4. Reconcile before releasing operating reports
   - Match settled fee receipts to bank evidence and compare the subledger with the general ledger.
   - Investigate unmatched cash, fee disputes and aging receivables; never repair discrepancies by deleting immutable journal evidence.

## 3. Execute and reconcile the worked 90-day lead cohort  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

This is an established-production quarter, not a promise that a new brokerage closes inside ninety days. The canonical time to first deal remains three to nine months. Assume the desk enters day one with functioning lender relationships, signed fee agreements, trained staff and $50,000 operating cash, but no opening receivables. Each of three monthly cohorts contains thirty authorized leads. By day ninety, the first cohort has eighteen scrubs, seven subsequent losses and five funded placements; the second has seventeen scrubs, eight losses and five placements; the third has eighteen scrubs, eight losses and four placements. Thus ninety leads resolve into fifty-three scrubs, twenty-three losses and fourteen funded deals, without counting a rejected application twice. A scrub means insufficient eligibility or documentation before lender submission. A loss means an otherwise packaged opportunity failed to close, including lender decline or borrower withdrawal recorded under the correct disposition. Each funded deal earns a fully retained $10,000 fee, illustratively two percent of $500,000 principal. Fourteen placements therefore represent $7,000,000 borrower principal and $140,000 operator revenue. Only the latter belongs in the scorecard. These ticket and conversion assumptions explain the scenario; they are not additional canonical forecasts.

Timing makes the quarter operationally useful. Month one invoices three first-cohort placements for $30,000 and collects two fees, or $20,000. Month two invoices the remaining two first-cohort placements and three second-cohort placements, producing $50,000 revenue. Its $40,000 receipts comprise three first-cohort fees and one second-cohort fee. Month three invoices two remaining second-cohort placements and four third-cohort placements, producing $60,000 revenue. Its $60,000 receipts comprise four second-cohort fees and two third-cohort fees. Ending receivables are therefore $10,000, $20,000 and $20,000 respectively, and all day-ninety receivables belong to the third cohort. The cohort table separates intake origin from closing month so the desk cannot attribute every late receipt to current marketing. Cash expenses are $30,000, $30,000 and $31,000, paid in their respective months with no closing payable balance in this illustration. Operating cash changes are negative $10,000, positive $10,000 and positive $29,000. The initial $50,000 becomes $40,000, $50,000 and $79,000. No principal passes through the broker's operating account, and no escrow balance funds payroll. If a receipt moves beyond day ninety, reduce cash rather than rewriting the earned-fee date.

The financial bridge is exact: quarterly retained fees of $140,000 less $91,000 total expenses produce $49,000 owner net profit, a 35.0 percent margin. Annualizing that quarterly production yields $560,000 revenue, $364,000 expenses and $196,000 owner profit, matching Base with two full-time staff and two contractors. Ending receivables explain why quarterly operating cash generation is $29,000 rather than $49,000. Do not multiply the first quarter's collection shortfall indefinitely: repeat production includes collection of prior cohorts, and steady receivables change the cash bridge. The supplied $4,000 to $18,000 monthly opex range cannot represent the Base case's complete expense load, which averages about $30,333 monthly. This example explicitly budgets $12,000 monthly core overhead plus $18,000, $18,000 and $19,000 of other production, staffing and acquisition costs; validate those categories without double counting. Conservative remains $120,000 revenue less $90,000 expenses, producing $30,000 profit at 25 percent. Scaled remains $1,000,000 revenue less $550,000 expenses, producing $450,000 profit at 45 percent with three full-time staff and three contractors. Model commission clawbacks separately from prospect losses: this worked quarter assumes no earned-fee reversals, while actual disputes require documented adjustments and a revised cash forecast.
