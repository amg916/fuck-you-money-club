## 1. Identify the economic buyer, the obligor, and the person releasing cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A specialty finance mandate has three distinct decision makers: the executive experiencing the cash constraint, the person authorized to bind the business, and the treasury operator authorized to release payment. In an equipment transaction, an operations leader wants a machine installed, the CFO approves the financing economics, and accounts payable follows the closing statement. In receivables finance, payroll timing creates urgency while the controller must prove eligible invoices, dilution, and customer concentration. For SBA financing, the owner values a complete package and a credible timetable, but neither urgency nor eligibility assures lender approval. Interview each role before quoting: what payment date creates the shortage, what cash source repays the facility, and which person can authorize a fee deduction? Record answers in Salesforce or HubSpot with a signed mandate and an authority document. A $500,000 equipment placement at a retained 2% fee produces $10,000 of brokerage revenue, not $500,000. The buyer compares that fee with avoided downtime and alternative financing costs. Do not manufacture a statutory deadline to create urgency; identify the actual tax payment, payroll date, purchase obligation, or contract milestone driving the decision.

The lender may write the commission check even when the borrower selected the broker. Separate lender-paid origination compensation, borrower-paid placement fees, and third-party referral splits in the mandate. Name the obligor for each amount; an introduction does not make every participant liable. Ask the lender's broker desk who approves commissions, which document establishes the rate, whether funding or a later payment triggers payment, and which events permit a clawback. Ask the borrower whether a board resolution, operating agreement, or treasury policy limits signing authority. A signed proposal from a plant manager cannot replace the company's required authorization. For a $20,000 gross commission with a $5,000 referral allocation, this playbook's retained-fee basis is $15,000; do not count both portions as retained revenue. Establish accounting presentation separately with the bookkeeper. Where both lender and borrower pay, disclose the arrangement and confirm product-specific restrictions before collecting either fee. SBA agent compensation requires particular attention to 13 CFR 103.5 and applicable SBA Form 159 instructions. Budget ownership is useful evidence of intent; an accepted payment obligation and a workable disbursement instruction are evidence that the fee can actually clear.

The mapped play b2b_class_action_contingency_recovery changes the payer mechanics: a verified operating business hires a recovery service, while a court-supervised administrator distributes eligible settlement proceeds. Payment-card interchange, freight, auto-parts, commercial-insurance, and SaaS settlements warrant monitoring, but fund size does not establish client eligibility or a collectible award. Treat claims that 85–90% of eligible businesses never file as an unverified sourcing hypothesis unless a specific administrator supplies supporting data. At an expressly permitted 30% contingency, a $100,000 verified cash recovery yields a $30,000 service fee and $70,000 to the claimant before other authorized deductions. A claimed loss, submitted claim, or preliminary estimate is not cash revenue. Obtain primary processor or vendor statements, reconcile the business identity, and check existing assignments and duplicate submissions. Use court-approved third-party claim-filing riders where available and required; if the administrator prohibits the contemplated arrangement, do not submit under it. The fallback is documented claimant-controlled filing or withdrawal, not an invented transaction history. These recovery-service fees remain separate from specialty finance brokerage scorecard revenue unless a distinct, lawful financing referral independently earns a retained fee. Settlement delays also require a collections runway that does not depend on the next distribution arriving on schedule.

## 2. Choose the payment rail by control, finality, and reversal exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The clean funds path normally runs from lender to borrower, equipment vendor, or authorized settlement agent, with the broker receiving only the documented fee. Do not route principal through the brokerage operating account merely to secure payment. Receiving or controlling client funds can create additional licensing, trust-account, contractual, and operational duties. Before closing, build a funds-flow sheet naming the sender, beneficiary, account owner, fee amount, release condition, and person verifying receipt. Fedwire is useful for time-sensitive domestic business payments, but a screenshot of an initiated transfer is not proof of receipt. Verify new or changed beneficiary instructions through a previously established telephone contact, not a telephone number supplied in the change email. Match the account title to the contracting payee and resolve any third-party beneficiary before release. UCC Article 4A, including the security-procedure framework in section 4A-202, is relevant to many commercial payment orders; the controlling state enactment and bank agreement matter. Use a separate bank-confirmed receipt field in the CRM so a closer cannot convert an emailed wire confirmation into a collected commission. Dual review should cover the payment instruction as well as the final amount.

ACH suits repeat commission payments when the payer and bank support the arrangement, but distinguish a credit pushed by the payer from a debit pulled under authorization. Return exposure, cutoff times, and available funds depend on the transaction and bank terms; never apply consumer debit assumptions indiscriminately to commercial accounts. Stripe or another platform checkout can collect a small permitted engagement fee, but provider restrictions, processing costs, reserves, and chargebacks can make it unsuitable for finance-related compensation. Check the provider's actual acceptance terms before implementation. A retainer is a commercial payment structure rather than a bank network; state what work earns it, what remains refundable, and whether it credits the success fee. If a $2,000 earned retainer credits a $10,000 total fee, only $8,000 remains payable at closing and total fee revenue is $10,000. Escrow and milestone holds likewise add release controls rather than creating new money. Have the authorized holder accept objective instructions before the transaction becomes urgent. An unsigned request to deduct the commission offers little protection when the borrower later contests performance. Record custody and bank-transfer mechanics separately so the operating team knows who can actually move funds.

Court-directed or statutory distributions deserve their own workflow because the administrator controls eligibility, permitted payees, and distribution timing. A private service agreement cannot compel an administrator to recognize an assignment or split an award. If direct fee remittance is prohibited, the claimant receives the recovery and pays an ordinary invoice under a permitted agreement; size credit exposure accordingly. Factoring is different again: the factor purchases or finances receivables and may remit an advance while retaining a reserve for dilution, disputes, and charges. On $200,000 of eligible invoices with an 85% advance, initial liquidity is $170,000 and the starting reserve is $30,000 before contractual adjustments. A separately agreed 1% retained referral fee calculated on that advance is $1,700; the agreement must establish that basis. Neither the invoice face value nor the advance becomes brokerage revenue. For milestone holds, specify whether delivery, installation, customer acceptance, or cleared funding releases the fee. Prevent circular instructions in which the borrower must pay the broker before receiving financing needed to pay them. The eight-rail comparison below deliberately separates transfer networks from custody and payment structures while preserving their practical closing differences.

**Eight Payment and Settlement Rails: Release Control and Failure Recovery**

| Rail or commercial settlement structure | Best fit and actual release authority | Primary failure mode affecting the broker | Required evidence and operational recovery path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wire transfer through a verified business banking relationship | Treasury releases a closing commission after authorized funding | Changed beneficiary instructions redirect funds before detection occurs | Verify instructions independently and confirm bank receipt directly |
| Escrow custody with separately accepted written release instructions | Authorized escrow holder disburses against objective closing conditions | Holder rejects commission deductions absent accepted party instructions | Obtain written acceptance before closing and resolve disputed deductions |
| ACH credit or expressly authorized commercial ACH debit | Lender accounts payable remits recurring documented broker commissions | Return exposure or mismatched account details delay collection | Retain authorization evidence and reconcile bank settlement and returns |
| Retainer funded through an approved underlying bank rail | Borrower pays defined preparation work under signed engagement | Ambiguous earning terms create refund and double-billing disputes | Define earned work and credit amounts against closing compensation |
| Statutory or court-directed disbursement through an authorized administrator | Administrator distributes only after eligibility and distribution conditions | Private assignment conflicts with controlling settlement distribution instructions | Follow governing documents and invoice claimant when splits prohibited |
| Platform checkout using a provider-approved finance-related business account | Authorized client payer submits a permitted engagement payment | Provider restrictions or chargebacks interrupt expected cash availability | Confirm acceptance terms and retain signed service delivery evidence |
| Factoring advance with contractual reserve and fee accounting | Factor funds verified receivables and separately calculates referral compensation | Dilution reduces availability while disputed invoices delay reserve release | Reconcile advance basis and exclude receivable principal from revenue |
| Milestone hold released through an accepted settlement mechanism | Designated holder releases fees after documented objective performance | Subjective acceptance language lets parties postpone payment indefinitely | Define evidence deadlines and contractually agreed dispute handling procedures |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the fee obligation
   - Execute the mandate and lender compensation schedule before transmitting a complete application.
   - Record the obligor, fee basis, retained share, earning event, payment deadline, and every permitted clawback trigger.
2. Map the movement of funds
   - Prepare a transaction-specific funds-flow sheet and obtain acceptance from the authorized releasing party.
   - Separate loan principal, vendor payments, referral allocations, brokerage compensation, and any escrow-controlled balances.
3. Authenticate the release instruction
   - Independently verify beneficiary details and match the fee to the approved closing statement.
   - Use established contact information, preserve the verification record, and stop payment when account ownership does not reconcile.
4. Reconcile receipt and continuing exposure
   - Match bank receipt to the invoice and track return, reserve, and clawback obligations separately.
   - Update the collected-fee ledger only from bank evidence; maintain contract-specific follow-up until continuing exposure expires.

## 3. Protect attribution while screening borrower, lender, and collection risk  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Fee protection begins with a defensible service record, not an aggressive noncircumvention paragraph. Define the borrower group, financing products, introduced counterparties, excluded existing relationships, and a reasonable negotiated tail for attributable transactions. Record each introduction in a shared deal log with delivery evidence and an acknowledgment where practical. Specify whether a renewal, affiliate facility, incremental advance, or replacement lender generates another fee; silence creates avoidable disputes. Tie any tail to demonstrable work and applicable law rather than claiming every future financing. The payment clause should identify funding as a measurable event, establish invoice timing, and distinguish a commission dispute from the borrower's separate dispute with the lender. Obtain closing instructions before the final scramble, but never represent that a contract can eliminate licensing requirements or override lender program rules. California Financial Code section 22100 illustrates why commercial purpose does not automatically remove broker licensing questions. Assess each state, product, and activity before accepting compensation. If a proposed debt placement becomes a securities transaction, pause the ordinary lending workflow and examine Exchange Act section 15(a), 15 U.S.C. 78o(a), rather than relabeling placement compensation as consulting revenue.

Screen the fee payer as carefully as the borrower. Verify legal existence and authority through state records, review ownership and bank-account consistency, obtain relevant financial statements, and check litigation, bankruptcy, sanctions exposure, and references using appropriate official sources. PACER, secretary-of-state searches, OFAC resources, and authorized commercial credit reports answer different questions; none independently proves payment capacity. A lender with a polished portal may still have unstable warehouse funding or a history of commission disputes. Request written commission terms and ask established brokers about actual payment behavior. Track lender concentration by retained fees and unpaid receivables, not merely submitted applications. If one lender represents $224,000 of the $560,000 Base revenue case, 40% of revenue depends on that relationship; prequalify alternatives before underwriting policy changes arrive. For borrowers, reconcile requested debt service against bank statements and operating cash flow rather than relying on headline sales. Apply consistent matching criteria and document exceptions. Regulation B reaches business credit, and certain referral activities can fall within its creditor definition for specified prohibitions. Protect applicant records with scoped access and secure document exchange, assessing FTC Safeguards Rule duties where the brokerage is a covered financial institution.

The mapped play aged_shelf_corp_tradeline_credit_stacking must remain a comparison of genuine entities and accurately disclosed financing options. A reinstated company's formation date does not establish operating history, revenue, repayment capacity, or beneficial ownership continuity. Verify reinstatement, tax standing, ownership changes, actual primary tradelines, and the issuer's treatment of promotional purchases versus transfers or cash advances. Never present purchased history as current management's performance or omit concurrent applications and liabilities when requested. A hypothetical $100,000 facility at 0% purchase APR for twelve months requires roughly $8,333.33 of monthly principal reduction, with a final rounding adjustment, to retire the balance before expiration. At a lawful, disclosed 2% retained placement fee, brokerage revenue is $2,000; issuer charges and any separately financed fee increase borrower costs. Availability is not revenue and a promotional rate does not create repayment capacity. Model the post-promotion APR and minimum-payment structure explicitly. If verified cash generation cannot support repayment, halt applications, correct disclosures, and use a transparent term facility or reduce financed spending. The fallback protects both the borrower and the brokerage from building a commission forecast around credit that cannot be honestly underwritten.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before releasing the closing package**
> 
> A file moves forward only when the responsible operator can produce the underlying evidence, not merely mark the CRM complete.
> - Verify required state licensing or a documented applicable exemption for the specific brokerage activity and financing product.
> - Confirm signed compensation terms identify every payer, retained amount, disclosure obligation, and applicable SBA compensation documentation.
> - Authenticate beneficiary ownership and obtain written acceptance from any escrow holder expected to deduct the fee.
> - Reconcile underwriting facts, concurrent liabilities, and authority documents; stop applications when material disclosures conflict with primary records.

## 4. Separate adjacent payment ecosystems from retained brokerage economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The mapped play section_340b_contract_pharmacy_spread_arbitrage is an adjacent healthcare-services model, not permission to book pharmacy reimbursement as brokerage income. HRSA-qualified covered entities include qualifying health centers, Ryan White organizations, and specified eligible hospitals; eligibility depends on the statutory category and requirements, not a marketing label. Section 340B, 42 U.S.C. 256b, establishes covered outpatient drug pricing and prohibits diversion and duplicate discounts. Drug-specific acquisition discounts of 25–50% or more may be a modeling assumption, but neither that range nor full commercial reimbursement is universal. A hypothetical eligible prescription with $6,000 acquisition cost and $10,000 collected reimbursement leaves $4,000 before dispensing, administration, inventory, reversals, and other costs. If those costs total $1,500, the illustrative remaining contribution is $2,500, belonging to the relevant pharmacy and covered-entity economics under their agreements. It is not automatically a distributable referral spread. Independently supported flat administrative or dispensing fees require actual services and appropriate pricing; calling compensation flat does not by itself satisfy the federal Anti-Kickback Statute. Tele-referral arrangements require particular care because clinical routing cannot become a disguised purchase of federally reimbursable business. Keep patient eligibility and clinical decisions outside the broker's incentive structure.

For a separately contracted TPA service, an illustrative $250 monthly administrative charge across 100 serviced locations produces $25,000 monthly service revenue, before service costs. That amount is neither a prescription-based entitlement nor specialty finance brokerage revenue. A genuine inventory facility arranged for the operating business might instead produce a separately permitted $12,000 retained financing fee on $600,000 funded at 2%; only that fee enters this chapter's brokerage model. Verify payer contracts, reimbursement lags, reversals, inventory ownership, and borrowing-base eligibility before representing collectible receivables to a lender. The operational fallback may include a properly licensed, entity-owned in-house specialty pharmacy alongside permitted contract pharmacies, but ownership alone does not resolve program eligibility or reimbursement restrictions. Automated 340B ESP submissions should follow applicable manufacturer policies and authorized data-use arrangements; uploads do not universally unlock restricted NDCs. Enforce EHR encounter checks, patient eligibility controls, and duplicate-discount reconciliation before inventory replenishment. If data or eligibility cannot be established, stop the affected replenishment and correct the record. A lender matrix should distinguish collectible operating cash flow from disputed spread assumptions, especially when manufacturer restrictions or payer recoupments could materially reduce collateral value after funding.

The canonical scorecard remains retained origination or referral fees only. An illustrative Conservative mix of twelve collected $10,000 retained fees produces $120,000 annual revenue; $90,000 of total modeled costs leaves $30,000 owner net profit at 25%, supported by 2 FT + 2 C. A Base mix of twenty-eight $20,000 retained fees produces $560,000; $364,000 of costs leaves $196,000 at 35.0%, with 2 FT + 2 C. A Scaled mix of forty $25,000 retained fees produces $1,000,000; $550,000 of costs leaves $450,000 at 45%, with 3 FT + 3 C. These mixes illustrate the required totals rather than predict deal sizes or lending volume. Costs include all modeled delivery, staffing, acquisition, and overhead expenses; the $4,000–$18,000 monthly operating range is not a substitute for those scenario totals. Budget $20,000–$100,000 startup capital against a three-to-nine-month first-deal window and stress delayed commission collection. Track application completion, lender fit, closing conversion, payment aging, and renewal dates through automated checklists. Measure clawback exposure separately from booked wins and maintain liquidity against contractual recourse. The operating objective is collected, defensible compensation with controlled labor, not impressive principal volume accompanied by uncollectible invoices.
