## 1. Classify the transaction before quoting fees or contacting lenders  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Open every file with a transaction classification sheet, not a lender blast. Record borrower organization, principal business address, collateral location, proceeds purpose, guarantor involvement, broker activity location, and proposed capital source. A corporate borrower does not erase state commercial lending requirements, and an equipment lease label does not determine whether the arrangement is legally a loan. California Financial Code section 22100 requires licensing for covered finance lending and brokering; analyze exemptions and the actual lender relationship rather than assuming every bank referral fits. California commercial financing disclosures under sections 22800 through 22805 are a separate analysis, generally involving covered offers of $500,000 or less. New York Financial Services Law article 8 uses a different framework, including a $2.5 million transaction threshold and implementing rules in 23 NYCRR Part 600. Neither disclosure framework substitutes for a licensing review. Store the applicable statute, exemption rationale, reviewed activity, reviewer, and review date in Airtable or a controlled spreadsheet. Mark lender permissions separately from broker permissions: a lender's charter does not automatically authorize an independent intermediary. Recheck the sheet when a transaction changes from equipment financing to real-estate bridge debt, receivables purchase, or an investment offering. Each change can move the file into a different licensing and disclosure perimeter.

Treat fair lending as an intake and routing discipline. The Equal Credit Opportunity Act, 15 U.S.C. section 1691, and Regulation B apply to business credit, although particular notification and recordkeeping rules vary by applicant and transaction. Regulation B includes certain regular referral and creditor-selection activities within its creditor definition for anti-discrimination and discouragement provisions; do not assume that avoiding underwriting eliminates exposure. Build lender matching from documented factors such as equipment age, receivables dilution, industry eligibility, debt-service coverage, and lien position. Never steer by protected characteristics or use demographic proxies as convenient shortcuts. Assign responsibility for adverse-action notices explicitly, while recognizing that a contract cannot transfer away a statutory duty. Maintain the version of the credit criteria actually used and preserve the applicant's submitted facts. For personal guarantor reports, establish a permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. section 1681b before requesting consumer-report data; a generic marketing checkbox is inadequate evidence. Collect only the information needed for the requested placement and restrict report access. For small-business lending data collection, assess 12 CFR Part 1002, Subpart B against the lender's coverage and effective implementation schedule rather than hard-coding a universal start date. Document whether the brokerage merely transmits required fields or independently has covered obligations.

Give SBA, collateral, and securities work their own routing rules. For SBA transactions, identify each agent, service, payer, and compensation basis under 13 CFR Part 103; use SBA Form 159 when applicable and reconcile compensation across borrower, lender, and referral agreements. Do not bury an additional success fee inside a document-processing charge. For equipment and accounts-receivable placements, counsel should determine the governing state's UCC enactment, debtor location, filing office, collateral description, perfection method, and existing lien priorities. A UCC search is evidence of filed records, not proof that every competing interest has been discovered. Request payoff and release mechanics before representing that a lender can receive first priority. Real-estate bridge transactions require an additional mortgage-broker and property-law review; business purpose alone is not a nationwide exemption. Finally, separate ordinary commercial loan introductions from solicitation of notes, fund interests, or partnership equity that may constitute securities. Exchange Act section 15(a), 15 U.S.C. section 78o(a), can require broker-dealer registration. Transaction-based compensation is a major warning signal, not the only relevant fact. Do not rename a placement commission a consulting fee and continue solicitation. Route securities activity to appropriately registered intermediaries and document the permitted services before discussing investor allocations.

## 2. Make compensation, authority, and downside allocation executable  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use an engagement agreement that describes what the brokerage will actually do: package an application, compare eligible lenders, coordinate diligence, and transmit offers without promising approval. Exclude authority to bind the borrower, sign loan documents, hold proceeds, or change lender terms unless a separate authorized arrangement genuinely requires it. Identify the paying party and disclose lender-paid compensation and material placement conflicts as required. Define the success-fee trigger using observable evidence: cleared funding to the borrower, qualifying funded amount, agreed percentage, payment deadline, and any exclusions. A term sheet, commitment, signed note, and funded loan are different events. For a revolving facility, specify whether the fee uses commitment size, initial draw, or cumulative draws; never leave the base implicit. Use a worked example: a $1,000,000 equipment financing at a 2% retained fee produces $20,000 of brokerage revenue, not $1,000,000. If $5,000 must be remitted to an outside introducer, retained revenue for this scorecard becomes $15,000. Include the introducer's authority and permitted compensation analysis before agreeing to the split. Require written amendments when loan size, payer, or economics change. Archive the executed agreement and the corresponding lender compensation schedule together so accounting does not infer terms from sales messages.

Keep anti-circumvention narrow enough to administer. List introduced lenders, define qualifying transactions, exclude documented preexisting relationships, and establish a finite tail measured from a stated event. A twelve-month tail is an illustrative negotiating position, not a universal entitlement. Require a causal connection between the introduction and the funded transaction instead of claiming every future borrowing. Specify whether renewals, affiliates, refinancings, and replacement facilities count. Pair the clause with a lender introduction log and dated borrower acknowledgment. For clawbacks, negotiate enumerated triggers, a limited recovery period, notice requirements, supporting evidence, dispute rights, and restrictions on offsets against unrelated commissions. Distinguish borrower fraud, early payoff, first-payment default, and lender processing error; they should not silently share one unlimited repayment obligation. Liability caps should identify the measurement period and treatment of confidentiality, data incidents, fraud, and willful misconduct, subject to enforceability review. Match insurance to the negotiated exposure rather than assuming professional liability covers every cyber event. State governing law, forum or arbitration procedure, interim relief, notice methods, and cost allocation. Review dispute provisions alongside statutory rights and the counterparty's location. A signed agreement with an unenforceable restriction is weaker than a modest clause supported by clean evidence and a commercially realistic collection process.

Separate operating revenue from transaction volume in both contracts and management reporting. The canonical Conservative case is $120,000 retained fees less $90,000 operating costs, leaving $30,000 owner profit at 25%. Base is $560,000 less $364,000, leaving $196,000 at 35.0%. Scaled is $1,000,000 less $550,000, leaving $450,000 at 45%. Teams remain 2 FT plus 2 C for Conservative and Base, and 3 FT plus 3 C for Scaled. An illustrative reconciliation at $20,000 retained fees per closing requires six, twenty-eight, and fifty annual closings respectively; those counts explain the totals without adding loan principal to revenue. They imply $6 million, $28 million, and $50 million of funded volume only if every deal has the same $1 million size and 2% retained yield. Record signed commitments separately from funded, earned, invoiced, and collected fees. Failed closings generate no success fee under a funding-based trigger. Track disputed fees and contingent repayment exposure separately from unrestricted cash, with the accounting treatment reviewed for the applicable reporting basis. Set lender-concentration alerts by retained fees and pipeline dependence. If one lender pauses originations, reroute only with borrower authorization and updated economics; never conceal a replacement lender's different fee or collateral requirements.

**Six Contract Clauses with Evidence, Economics, and Collection Mechanics**

| Clause and specifically bounded contractual promise | Drafting instruction for the engagement agreement | Evidence retained with the executed agreement | Operational consequence when the clause fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope limits brokerage to identified placement coordination services | Exclude binding authority, custody, underwriting promises, and unauthorized representations | Retain service schedule, authorized contacts, and documented borrower instructions | Stop expanded services until authority and pricing are documented |
| Fee trigger distinguishes funded principal from retained compensation | Name payer, percentage, funding event, exclusions, and payment deadline | Retain funding confirmation, fee calculation, invoice, and payment record | Do not invoice unfunded commitments as earned success fees |
| Anti-circumvention protects documented introductions within a finite tail | List covered lenders, causal connection, exclusions, and qualifying transactions | Retain dated introductions and evidence of preexisting relationship exclusions | Limit collection demands to transactions supported by contractual evidence |
| Liability allocation specifies caps and negotiated exception categories | Define cap period, indemnity procedure, insurance alignment, and exclusions | Retain negotiated redlines and coverage analysis for actual services | Escalate uninsured exposure before accepting broader contractual risk |
| Dispute resolution establishes forum and practical notice mechanics | Specify governing law, arbitration scope, costs, and interim relief | Retain notice addresses, delivery receipts, and dispute escalation records | Follow contractual notice procedures before pursuing recovery remedies |
| IP and data terms preserve client records and proprietary methods | Allocate template ownership, permitted processing, export rights, and deletion | Retain access logs, processor agreements, retention schedules, and exports | End unnecessary access while preserving required records and holds |

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the fee before celebrating the closing**
> 
> Experienced specialty finance operators reconcile the lender's payment mechanics against the borrower's agreement while the transaction is still negotiable.
> - For equipment deals, compare vendor payment, borrower funding, and broker commission dates; they often occur on different days.
> - For receivables facilities, define whether fee calculations exclude reserves, ineligible invoices, and unused commitments before presenting pricing.
> - For SBA referrals, compare Form 159 treatment with every side agreement so an extra processing charge cannot hide duplicate compensation.
> - For lender-paid commissions, request the clawback schedule before submitting the first borrower and track exposure by lender cohort.

## 3. Fence medical claims, privacy support, and solar equity into separate mandates  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play hospital_lien_medical_receivables_arbitrage is claims-recovery work, not permission to trade patient obligations or assume a hospital lien exists. Partner with surgical centers, air or ground ambulance operators, and specialty clinics only after classifying payer, service, plan type, patient protections, and representative authority. Federal No Surprises Act payment disputes under 42 U.S.C. section 300gg-111 and 45 CFR section 149.510 have specific eligibility and timing rules; federal protections generally do not cover ground ambulance services. For eligible disputes, track the 30-business-day open-negotiation initiation window from initial payment or denial, the negotiation period, and the ordinarily four-business-day IDR initiation window after that period. Check current extensions and governing process instructions. An illustrative $200,000 incremental recovery at a 25% contingency produces $50,000 gross recovery-service fees, within the mapped 20–35% range, before delivery costs and permitted splits. These are separate service economics, not additional canonical brokerage revenue. Use encrypted EDI transfers, minimum-necessary access, and signed BAAs where HIPAA requires them. If IDR timing has lapsed, evaluate available ERISA administrative appeals under 29 CFR section 2560.503-1, including applicable plan deadlines and authority to represent the claimant. An appeal is not an automatic substitute for a provider payment dispute, and some plans or claims fall outside ERISA entirely.

The mapped play cipa_vppa_wiretap_pixel_settlement_arbitrage supports counsel-led evaluation of genuine privacy claims; it is not a volume demand-letter machine. California Penal Code section 631 and the Video Privacy Protection Act, 18 U.S.C. section 2710, have distinct elements, defenses, and standing questions. Preserve the user's actual session, disclosures, permission state, relevant network requests, timestamps, and provenance under a written collection protocol. Do not create deceptive interactions, access another person's account, or represent that a tracking request alone proves liability. Counsel determines whether the facts support interception, protected video information, a covered defendant, cognizable injury, and a viable remedy. An illustrative authorized evidence-support engagement of twenty hours at $200 per hour yields $4,000 service revenue; a potential statutory damages figure is neither brokerage revenue nor an expected settlement. Avoid arrangements that improperly share legal fees, purchase clients, or reward solicitation; review the governing professional conduct and claim-transfer rules before any financing arrangement. The operator supplies technical evidence and remediation findings, not legal representation. Close unsupported matters with a documented reason and offer an authorized privacy-remediation audit instead. Keep investigation materials separate from outbound marketing systems. Do not promise privilege merely because a lawyer receives a report; counsel must structure the engagement and assess protection.

The mapped play solar_tax_equity_depreciation_strip_syndication concerns genuine equity ownership in commercial solar projects, not sale of depreciation as a freestanding credit. Partnership allocations require review under Internal Revenue Code section 704(b), related regulations, basis and risk limitations, and the transaction's actual economics. Section 7701(o) supplies an economic-substance framework where relevant; labels and spreadsheets do not replace ownership risk. Determine the applicable energy credit regime, construction and placed-in-service facts, current eligibility restrictions, recapture exposure, and any section 6418 transfer election separately. A transferable eligible credit and a depreciation deduction are different tax attributes. An illustrative $5 million equity commitment with a 2% placement charge yields $100,000 to the authorized placement intermediary under its agreement, not automatically to this brokerage. Only lawfully retained compensation could enter the brokerage scorecard, and the canonical totals remain unchanged. Suspend solicitation and closing if registration, offering, allocation, or ownership support is unresolved. Use appropriately licensed intermediaries and restructure only as independently reviewed genuine equity ownership. Demand project budgets, executed ownership documents, interconnection status, tax models, waterfall sensitivities, and conflict disclosures. Never describe a targeted tax benefit as assured cash yield, and never use an ordinary commercial loan engagement to authorize securities distribution.

## 4. Use permission-based scripts and preserve the evidence behind each statement  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Commercial outreach needs a channel-specific rule set. CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. section 7704, applies to covered commercial email, including business audiences. Use accurate sender information and subject lines, identify the commercial nature as required, provide a valid postal address and functional opt-out, and honor opt-outs within ten business days. For calling and texting, assess the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, 47 U.S.C. section 227, FCC rules, applicable state restrictions, number type, technology, purpose, and permission evidence. A business title does not make a personal mobile number unrestricted. Avoid purchased lists with unsupported permission claims and do not assume a website inquiry authorizes every affiliate's campaign. Use this email body after configuring the sender's real postal address and unsubscribe mechanism: 'This is a commercial financing services inquiry. We help equipment-intensive businesses compare eligible commercial lenders. We do not make credit decisions. If you are considering equipment financing, may I send our document checklist and explain our compensation? Please do not email bank statements or identification documents. Reply stop or use the unsubscribe link to end these messages.' Keep the message specific to the recipient's business need without inventing an existing relationship, lender endorsement, prequalification, or guaranteed funding timeline.

Run discovery before requesting documents. Say: 'What amount do you need, what will the proceeds fund, where is the business organized and operating, and what collateral is available? Are you seeking equipment debt, a receivables facility, SBA financing, or a bridge loan? Which lenders already know this request? We will explain our role, proposed compensation, and authorized recipients before submitting your information.' Ask only relevant follow-up questions and record the answers without filling gaps with assumptions. For fee disclosure, say: 'Our proposed success fee is 2% of the funded amount, payable at funding under the signed agreement. On $1 million funded, that is $20,000. We will identify the payer and any lender-paid compensation before you accept the engagement. A lender can decline or change its offer after diligence.' Use those numbers only when they match the actual agreement. For a medical provider, say: 'We first check payer type, service eligibility, representative authority, deadlines, and secure transfer arrangements. We cannot promise IDR access or recovery.' For solar investors, stop the ordinary broker script: 'This involves an equity offering. We will not discuss subscription terms or investor commitments until the authorized securities intermediary establishes the permitted process.'

Make the CRM a record of authorized actions rather than a warehouse of sensitive attachments. Assess whether the business is a covered financial institution under the FTC Safeguards Rule, 16 CFR Part 314, and implement the applicable written information-security program, risk assessment, access controls, encryption, service-provider oversight, and incident response requirements. GLBA coverage turns on actual activities and protected information, not merely the company's preferred industry label. For HIPAA business-associate work, address 45 CFR sections 164.308, 164.312, and 164.504(e), including subcontractor obligations. Configure Salesforce or HubSpot to store workflow status and secure document links; use a properly configured document platform with appropriate contracts rather than public sharing links. Keep medical files outside general sales workspaces. Apply role-based access, multifactor authentication, audit logs, and an evidence-based retention schedule that accommodates statutory duties and litigation holds. Review templates when statutes, lender terms, or channel rules change, and retain the version actually sent. Test application checklists, lender matching, and renewal reminders against synthetic records before enabling automation. The final pre-send review should verify recipient identity, permitted purpose, current fee language, suppression status, and attachment access. Automation should reduce missing documents and missed renewals, not multiply unsupported representations.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify authority and product
   - Complete the jurisdiction, product, lender, and activity record.
   - Attach applicable licensing analysis, exemptions, disclosure duties, and specialist routing decisions before quoting services.
2. Bind the economic and data terms
   - Execute the engagement and required information-handling agreements.
   - Reconcile payer, retained fee, funding trigger, permitted recipients, clawbacks, and required BAAs against lender-side documents.
3. Communicate through verified channels
   - Use the reviewed script and preserve its sent version.
   - Check channel restrictions, suppression records, recipient identity, actual fee numbers, and secure document access before transmission.
4. Reconcile funding and close access
   - Match funding evidence to earned fees and post-closing obligations.
   - Track collected compensation, clawback exposure, renewal authority, required retention, access removal, and outstanding disputes.
