## 1. Assign four accountable seats before expanding to six  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Structure Candidate #20 as four accountable people, not four departments. The first full-time seat is the owner-originator: they qualify commercial borrowers, control lender relationships, approve placement strategy, and own fee negotiations. The second full-time seat is the placement and operations lead: they maintain the lender matrix, inspect complete packages, track conditions, and reconcile closing evidence. Contractor one provides document assembly and borrower follow-up against a purchased deliverable; contractor two supplies specialist underwriting support and independent package review. Neither contractor promises credit approval, changes lender terms, or receives borrower funds. A HubSpot deal record identifies one accountable employee, while SharePoint stores versioned borrower evidence with access limited to the assigned file. Equipment, accounts-receivable, SBA, and bridge placements share this operating spine but retain separate document checklists and lender eligibility fields. Across the 29 mapped operator plays, this is a finance-intermediary model, not a balance-sheet lending business or a collection of 29 products. Two contractors remain two people even when their work spans several product lanes. Reject staffing plans that hide recurring processing labor inside unnamed vendors or assume the owner can originate continuously while checking every bank statement.

Buy contractor outputs with measurable acceptance criteria rather than rewarding submission volume. An illustrative document-package engagement pays $300 for an accepted assembly and $75 for a separately authorized material refresh; a specialist review pays $250 for a completed credit memorandum covering the agreed scope. These are procurement assumptions, not fee-market benchmarks or additions to canonical revenue. A package passes only when borrower identity, entity authority, requested amount, source documents, reconciled schedules, and lender-specific requirements match the assignment. Require acknowledgment within one business day, delivery within two business days after complete inputs, and correction of contractor-caused defects within one business day without a second fee. Pause the delivery clock for missing borrower evidence and record the dependency explicitly. The employee lead accepts every package and reviews all critical fields; weekly sampling tests the remaining checklist items. Do not convert independent deliverables into employee-style daily supervision merely by labeling workers contractors. Evaluate classification under applicable federal and state tests, including California Labor Code section 2775 where relevant and any applicable exception. If the actual relationship requires employment, change the staffing model rather than concealing the mismatch in a services agreement.

Expand to 3 FT + 3 C only after the existing pod demonstrates capacity pressure and positive collected contribution through at least two complete closing cohorts. Add a full-time relationship and renewal manager to remove routine borrower follow-up from the owner, then add one contractor for peak document intake and source-data reconciliation. The operations lead retains final package acceptance; the specialist contractor remains independent of the assembly they review. Track employee and contractor hours separately, because six named people do not imply six full-time equivalents. The canonical annual expense envelopes are $90,000 Conservative, $364,000 Base, and $550,000 Scaled: retained-fee revenue minus the specified owner profit. Those totals must contain actual payroll, contractor fees, systems, insurance, marketing, and other operating costs. The Conservative envelope is especially tight for two full-time seats; disclose owner compensation treatment and local wage requirements instead of assuming two market-rate salaries fit. Preserve the canonical $30,000 profit as the scenario target, while recording any actual budget shortfall separately. Before hiring, stress the thirteen-week cash schedule for a ninety-day closing delay and a contractually supportable clawback event. Capacity expansion follows cash resilience, not submitted loan principal.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy clean credit packages, not performative activity**
> 
> Protect the four-person pod by making evidence reusable while keeping lender-specific judgments explicit.
> - Require the AR specialist to reconcile the aging total to the general ledger and identify dilution, offsets, and concentrated account debtors before lender matching.
> - Store equipment quotes, serial identifiers, useful-life assumptions, and lien-search dates as separate fields so a changed asset does not silently reuse stale underwriting.
> - For bridge placements, distinguish sponsor liquidity from projected refinance proceeds and record the specific maturity exit assumption reviewed by the lender.
> - For SBA work, maintain the current agent-services scope, compensation evidence, and applicable SBA Form 159 documentation rather than copying conventional brokerage fee language.

## 2. Run a weekly cohort scorecard with explicit concentration limits  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run Monday's operator meeting from a frozen Friday extract, not a live dashboard that changes during discussion. Maintain one deal identifier across HubSpot, the document repository, and QuickBooks Online. The scorecard separates qualified mandates, complete packages, lender decisions, funded closings, earned retained fees, and collected fees. Define a qualified mandate as a borrower with verified business purpose, a documented financing request, a plausible repayment source, and authorization for the proposed brokerage work. A lender submission counts only when the lender receives an accepted package; sending another email does not create another opportunity. Measure package acceptance against all first deliveries, including rejected deliveries. Measure close rate by submission cohort and product lane after a stated observation window, reporting open cases separately instead of treating them as wins or losses. The operations lead owns data definitions, while the owner chooses one corrective action for each breached threshold. Contractor invoices reference accepted deliverable identifiers, preventing payment for duplicate packages. Every correction has an accountable person and a next review date. This turns weekly reporting into a control loop rather than a sales meeting dominated by the largest prospective principal amount.

Use the Base case to translate annual ambition into unit economics without changing the canonical scorecard. Forty funded placements at an average $14,000 retained fee produce $560,000 annual recognized revenue; $364,000 expenses leave $196,000 owner profit and a 35.0% margin. This is an illustrative fee mix, not a prediction that every equipment or SBA closing produces that fee. At a 40% mature-cohort funding rate, forty closings require one hundred complete submissions annually, approximately 1.9 per calendar week. A 50% qualified-mandate-to-submission rate would require two hundred qualified mandates, approximately 3.8 weekly. Display these denominators beside observed rates and report medians plus ninetieth-percentile cycle times; averages alone hide stalled bridge files and prolonged SBA conditions. Set initial internal service targets of at least 95% first-pass package acceptance and no unresolved critical evidence defects at submission. These are operating targets to calibrate against actual cohorts, not lender promises. Track fees per accepted package, review hours per closing, and collected contribution by product. A low-fee product can consume the same employee attention as a high-fee placement, so revenue growth without capacity economics can reduce owner profit.

Set a maximum 25% share for each economically connected borrower group and each lender group, measured separately against trailing-twelve-month retained-fee revenue. Consolidate affiliates; four lender brands sharing one credit platform do not automatically create four independent counterparties. Track referral-source concentration and expected pipeline fees with the same 25% operating limit, while keeping forecast exposure distinct from recognized revenue. At Base revenue, the annual ceiling is $140,000 per group; at Scaled revenue it is $250,000. For a new book, the first closing necessarily creates concentration above the limit. Label that startup condition nondiversified, do not describe the cap as achieved, and route growth toward independent borrowers, sources, and lenders. A breach stops additional discretionary exposure to the affected group while the owner addresses contracted obligations and develops alternatives. Never route an unsuitable borrower merely to improve a dashboard ratio. Maintain two credible placement alternatives for each active product lane, documenting geography, collateral, ticket size, and current appetite. Fair-lending controls also apply to routing: Regulation B covers certain referral participants for its antidiscrimination and discouragement provisions. Use documented credit criteria, not protected characteristics or unsupported geographic proxies.

## 3. Build a transferable fee ledger, evidence chain and rights package  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Make the retained-fee ledger explain every dollar without using loan principal as brokerage revenue. Each deal record holds signed fee terms, the service performance trigger, funding evidence, the retained amount, invoice date, cash receipt, referral obligations, and any contractual recovery period. Under the applicable accrual policy, recognize revenue when the brokerage's performance obligation is satisfied and the amount is supportable, not when a borrower accepts a preliminary term sheet. Evaluate variable consideration and reversal exposure under the chosen accounting framework; a cash reserve is not itself an accounting expense or a substitute for that analysis. Assess principal-versus-agent presentation for third-party amounts rather than mechanically netting every payment. The canonical operating scorecard remains retained origination or referral fees only, with a reconciliation to the general ledger if presentation differs. Separate expected clawback adjustments from credit-loss allowances on already earned receivables. QuickBooks Online can carry the accounting entries while a controlled deal subledger carries lender and cohort detail. Reconcile monthly opening receivables plus earned fees minus receipts, credits, and write-offs to closing receivables. Never let a dashboard silently overwrite previously reported cohorts after a failed closing.

Prepare transferability while the book is small. Every contractor agreement should identify pre-existing tools, assign specified newly created copyrightable deliverables through a signed writing, and grant necessary rights to embedded background materials. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a writing signed by the owner or their authorized agent. Do not rely on a work-made-for-hire label for every independent contractor spreadsheet or memorandum. Store the executed assignment beside the deliverable inventory and retain source versions of checklists, lender-matrix structures, training materials, and automations. An IP assignment does not transfer ownership of borrower records, override confidentiality terms, or authorize unrestricted data sale. Inventory lender agreements for assignment restrictions, change-of-control provisions, termination rights, and commission survival. Borrower engagements need their own transfer and information-sharing analysis. Maintain company-controlled accounts, multifactor authentication, role-based access, and a documented contractor offboarding routine. Where the business is a covered institution, implement the FTC Safeguards Rule under 16 CFR Part 314, including applicable service-provider oversight. Give prospective buyers aggregated cohort evidence first, then controlled file access only when confidentiality and data-sharing requirements support it.

Create a monthly close packet that a buyer can reproduce without interviewing the founder. Include the trial balance, deal-level revenue bridge, bank reconciliation, open commission aging, clawback schedule, contractor acceptance register, and concentration report. Reconcile Conservative revenue of $120,000 to $90,000 expenses and $30,000 profit; Base revenue of $560,000 to $364,000 expenses and $196,000 profit; and Scaled revenue of $1,000,000 to $550,000 expenses and $450,000 profit. Keep these scenarios distinct from actual results and identify the period covered by every actual statement. Track submission cohorts for operational conversion and funding cohorts for fee realization and subsequent recoveries. A lender switch should preserve the original deal identifier and decision history. Maintain a jurisdiction-and-product register covering the activity performed, licensing or exemption basis, required fee disclosures, and responsible employee. California Financing Law section 22100 requires licensing for covered finance-lender or broker activity unless an exemption applies; commercial purpose alone does not settle the analysis. SBA agent compensation has separate requirements under 13 CFR Part 103. Keep securities introductions outside the ordinary debt-placement workflow pending a separate broker-dealer registration analysis under 15 U.S.C. section 78o.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the monthly evidence population
   - Export all open, funded, declined, withdrawn, and adjusted deals using persistent identifiers.
   - Record the extraction timestamp and preserve source documents so later changes do not erase the original close packet.
2. Reconcile earned fees and subsequent cash
   - Match each recognized retained fee to signed terms, performance evidence, ledger entries, and bank receipts.
   - Separate timing differences, referral obligations, credit losses, and contractual recoveries instead of using one unexplained adjustment.
3. Test ownership and transfer restrictions
   - Verify signed contractor assignments and review lender and borrower agreement restrictions.
   - Flag missing rights, required counterparty permissions, commission survival issues, and unsupported data-sharing assumptions by contract identifier.
4. Publish an accountable exception register
   - Assign each unresolved financial, rights, or concentration issue to one employee with a dated corrective action.
   - Carry unresolved items into the next weekly scorecard and retain the original exception alongside evidence of resolution.

## 4. Price transferable earnings, then compare an exit with continued ownership  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A buyer pays for repeatable earnings they can operate, not for funded principal or projected renewals. Begin with the canonical owner-profit figures, then construct a separate earnings bridge for the contemplated buyer. Owner net profit, seller discretionary earnings, and EBITDA are different measures; identify owner wages, benefits, financing costs, taxes, and necessary replacement labor before choosing a multiple. For an owner-operator acquisition, an illustrative negotiation sensitivity of 2.0 to 3.0 times normalized discretionary earnings can frame a small, concentrated book. A genuinely manager-run, diversified operation might be tested at 3.0 to 4.5 times normalized EBITDA. These are underwriting assumptions, not observed transaction comparables or an automatic premium attached to finance brokerage. Thin retained-fee history, weak assignment rights, customer dependence, or a departing founder can push a transaction below those ranges or into an asset-only structure. Ask bidders to state the earnings definition, included working capital, assumed liabilities, and consideration form. A larger headline multiple with contingent payments may produce less certain value than a smaller cash offer. Candidate #20's score of 89/100 measures the operating opportunity across 29 mapped plays; it is not a valuation formula.

Model the Base transfer with explicit replacement-cost assumptions. If its $196,000 owner profit includes no separate owner wage expense, and a buyer must spend $100,000 annually to replace the owner's necessary work, transferable earnings before other adjustments fall to $96,000. At an illustrative 3.0 times that earnings measure, indicated enterprise value is $288,000. If equivalent replacement labor is already fully expensed, do not subtract it again. For the Scaled case, the same additional $100,000 assumption reduces $450,000 owner profit to $350,000; a 3.0 to 4.5 times sensitivity then indicates $1,050,000 to $1,575,000 enterprise value before debt, cash, working-capital, transaction-cost, and other adjustments. The Conservative $30,000 profit cannot absorb that replacement assumption, so a manager-run earnings valuation would be inappropriate without operating changes. None of these bridges revises canonical revenue or profit. They test transfer economics under a stated buyer structure. As a simple timing sensitivity, two years of unchanged Base owner profit totals $392,000 before personal taxes and reinvestment, but requires continued work and exposes the owner to closing and recovery risk. Compare risk-adjusted cash flows and workload rather than equating that sum with an immediately payable sale price.

Earn a stronger valuation by removing specific buyer objections before entering a process. Demonstrate twelve months of reproducible monthly closes, a second employee relationship on active accounts, documented renewal execution, and concentration within the 25% limits. Show that another employee can complete a placement using the lender matrix without the founder supplying undocumented criteria. Do not count uncommitted renewals as contracted recurring revenue; present historical renewal cohorts, fee realization, and borrower attrition instead. For contingent consideration, define the transferred customer cohort, collection window, retained-fee calculation, permitted deductions, and treatment of lender-imposed clawbacks. An earnout based on collected retained fees can be easier to audit than one based on buyer-controlled overhead, but still needs servicing obligations, reporting access, and dispute mechanics. Separate enterprise value from proceeds paid at closing and proceeds that depend on future performance. If bids do not compensate for giving up a durable cash stream, retain the business and apply the same transferability controls internally. The cash-cow version still needs contractor quality checks, lender redundancy, security maintenance, and replacement capacity. A brokerage becomes durable when its evidence and relationships survive a person's absence, not when its owner stops looking at the scorecard.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before hiring into scale or marketing the book**
> 
> Use evidence already produced by the pod. An unresolved condition becomes a dated operating exception, not a polished claim in a buyer presentation.
> - Verify recognized retained fees, cash receipts, clawback adjustments, and expenses reconcile by deal and month to the relevant canonical scenario comparison.
> - Verify every connected borrower and lender group is within the 25% trailing-revenue limit, and separately test referral-source and expected-pipeline concentration.
> - Verify signed contractor IP assignments, company-controlled credentials, and documented lender and borrower transfer restrictions before representing the book as transferable.
> - Verify replacement labor is counted exactly once and distinguish enterprise value, closing cash, deferred consideration, and continuing seller obligations in every bid comparison.
