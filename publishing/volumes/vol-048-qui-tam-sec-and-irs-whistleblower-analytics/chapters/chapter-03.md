## 1. Price accepted forensic cohorts, not allegations or government recoveries  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A monthly retainer can purchase several milestones, but the same delivered milestone cannot be counted again as a separate project sale. Use a contract identifier, service period, delivery identifier, and credit reason on every ledger entry. Recognize advance payments as contract liabilities until the promised service is performed; receiving cash does not itself establish earned revenue. Credit adjustments belong against their originating cohort.

Separate three economic objects before calculating any margin: the law firm's service purchase, the government's underlying recovery, and the eligible whistleblower's eventual award. Only the first supports the recurring forecast here. All three canonical scenarios assume zero realized award contribution, making their operating profitability independent of uncollected filings. If an eligible individual eventually receives an award, establish the actual recipient, contractual obligations, and accounting treatment before attributing anything to the analytics business. Do not presume an award is assignable, that a company is an eligible claimant under every program, or that a nonlawyer may share counsel's fees. Neither $2,000,000 nor $400,000 becomes analytics revenue automatically. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis applies to customer service arrangements; it does not automatically classify a statutory award. Confirm gross-versus-net presentation and award treatment with the accountant before combining separate income streams in management reporting.

## 2. Reconcile every deduction and distinguish reserves from trapped cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The expense waterfall deducts seven separately owned cost pools from recognized revenue. Delivery labor is $30,000, $125,000, and $180,000; data and infrastructure cost $6,000, $24,000, and $36,000; acquisition costs $8,000, $35,000, and $44,000. These allocations illustrate one reconciled cost structure, not additional canonical scorecard inputs. The first full-time position is the working owner; owner profit compensates that labor and invested capital rather than sitting above a second owner salary. Scaled delivery expense includes the second full-time employee's loaded cost and three contractors. Figures are before owner-level income taxes and discretionary distributions. If the owner requires a separate market salary, subtract it in an additional economic-profit view.

Express the same waterfall per cohort before promising capacity. At twenty, fifty, and eighty cohorts, allocated expenses are $3,230, $5,362.50, and $4,620 each, leaving $1,520, $4,387.50, and $6,380 owner profit per accepted unit. Acquisition assumes ten, twenty, and twenty-five newly acquired firm relationships, giving fully loaded CAC of $800, $1,750, and $1,760. Assign conference fees, outbound research, proposal labor, and founder selling time consistently; disclose imputed founder time separately when it is not a booked expense. Multiple cohorts per firm explain why customer counts differ from delivery counts. Avoid claiming lifetime value from one successful matter. Track repeat purchases, client concentration, and contribution after direct labor. A $10,000 Base invoice can look attractive while repeated free revisions consume the entire twenty-five-hour delivery allowance before acquisition and overhead are paid.

Maintain distinct treatments for price credits, evidentiary scrubs, bad debts, and liquidity buffers. Contractual credits reduce transaction price and are already deducted above revenue. The rework provision funds expected replacement analysis, failed source joins, and necessary correction work inside accepted service obligations; it is not a second deduction for the same credit. Collection-loss expense addresses earned receivables that may not be paid, using the applicable receivables impairment policy. A bank balance earmarked for delayed payments is neither an expense nor a second loss allowance. Maintain an issue code and originating cohort for each adjustment so a rejected CMS match cannot generate both an unsupported full refund and duplicate labor reserves. Evidence scrubbing should test National Provider Identifier changes, billing entity relationships, geographic denominators, Open Payments recipient matching, and import-manifest consignee normalization. Document why an outlier survives each check. When a client disputes acceptance, review whether revenue was earned at all before recording merely a credit loss. Reconcile opening provision, new provision, actual utilization, and closing balance monthly. Unused earmarked cash stays on the balance sheet; a released accounting estimate needs documented evidence rather than a convenient quarter-end margin target.

## 3. Finance receivables and refuse to finance sealed award expectations  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

That deliberately conservative bridge treats modeled provisions as funded spending capacity and assumes no supplier credit or customer advances. Do not add receivables and the cost bridge together; they describe different views of the same collection lag. Add a separate three-month fixed-cost safety buffer of $5,250, $20,250, and $28,000. The resulting minimum planning liquidity is approximately $13,214, $64,325, and $103,945, before setup expenditure, tax payments, owner draws, or litigation funding. Likewise, $1,500–$9,500 monthly opex is not the full expense burden at higher volume. Request a service deposit, milestone billing, and explicit acceptance dates rather than a promise of payment after litigation succeeds. Count deposit benefits in the cash calendar once, and do not also assume the original full-invoice DSO benefit. Negotiate contractor milestones that follow actual delivery without withholding payment for unrelated award outcomes.

Keep a separate award-duration schedule with two-, three-, five-, and longer-year scenarios and a permanent zero-recovery branch. Keep such optional funding outside the canonical operating model and obtain separate authorization before committing it. **SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Separate the ledgers
   - Create service, receivables, provision, cash, and contingent-award schedules.
   - Identify the legal payee and contractual revenue basis; leave uncollected awards outside recognized service revenue and available liquidity.
2. Reconcile each accepted cohort
   - Tie invoice face value, service credits, acceptance, and actual collections.
   - Match contractor invoices and correction hours to the same delivery identifier, preventing duplicated credits and reserve utilization.
3. Refresh the thirteen-week cash calendar

4. Authorize growth from collected service economics
   - Release additional delivery capacity only against funded backlog and sufficient liquidity.
   - Compare minimum cash with the safety buffer after owner draws and setup spending; defer optional case funding when service obligations are exposed.

## 4. Stress legal attrition, acquisition failure, and margin compression  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use an explicit rejection funnel instead of applying a single recovery percentage to public anomalies. An illustrative batch starts with one hundred flags; data scrubbing removes forty, public-disclosure analysis removes thirty more, representative-claim development removes fifteen, and counsel declines five of the remaining fifteen. Ten proceed to potential filings. At screening allocations of one-tenth hour for each initial flag, one-quarter hour for each of sixty survivors, one-half hour for each of thirty evidence candidates, and one hour for each of fifteen counsel packets, labor totals fifty-five hours, or $5,500 at $100 per hour. Charge that work within the existing delivery budget rather than deducting it again. Under 31 U.S.C. § 3730(e)(4), qualifying public disclosures can require dismissal unless the government opposes or an exception applies; original-source analysis matters. Public datasets do not automatically defeat every claim, but anomalies alone are insufficient. Rule 9(b) pleading requirements vary by circuit; counsel must identify the representative claims or other sufficiently particular facts required locally. Maintain claim identifiers, dates, submission mechanics, source provenance, and payment linkage where lawfully available. A statistically unusual provider is not automatically a provable false-claim defendant.

For the ten illustrative filings, assume four government interventions and six non-interventions. Assume one of those six is dismissed on the government's motion, leaving five non-intervened matters. If three intervened matters and one surviving non-intervened matter recover, four filings pay and six return zero. These are explicit stress assumptions: 60% non-intervention, 10% government dismissal across filings, 75% recovery among intervened matters, and 20% among surviving non-intervened matters. They are not reported DOJ or Granston dismissal rates. Replace them with dated, denominator-matched evidence before presenting a forecast as empirically calibrated. The historical Granston memorandum does not replace 31 U.S.C. § 3730(c)(2)(A), which provides notice and an opportunity for a hearing; applicable Supreme Court authority also governs government intervention and dismissal. Non-intervention is not itself dismissal or a finding that the allegations lack merit. FCA award ranges generally differ between intervened and non-intervened actions, while SEC and CFTC awards generally use 10%–30%, and qualifying IRS awards generally use 15%–30% under their respective rules. Do not apply one universal 15%–30% multiplier. Model program eligibility, collected proceeds, reductions, and recipient-specific deductions before estimating any eventual net award.

Stress service economics independently of that litigation tree. Holding expenses fixed, a 10% reduction in recognized service revenue cuts owner profit to $20,900, $170,625, and $422,400. A 20% delivery-labor overrun instead reduces profit to $24,400, $194,375, and $474,400. Doubling the rework provision reduces profit to $26,800, $204,375, and $492,400. An incremental bad-debt shock equal to 5% of recognized revenue, beyond the existing allowance, leaves $25,650, $195,000, and $466,400. Keep these isolated shocks separate; the combined revenue and labor shock yields $14,900, $145,625, and $386,400. A thirty-day DSO extension changes the liquidity requirement, not profit, unless financing costs or additional losses arise. Finally, zero statutory recoveries leave all three canonical service profits unchanged because none was included initially. Apply fallback actions to the relevant failure: reduce unpaid exploratory scope when CAC rises, replace fragile joins when scrub hours rise, and collect deposits when DSO lengthens. Do not compensate for weak collections by increasing hypothetical award probabilities. Counsel controls filing and seal decisions; the operator controls evidence quality, delivery pricing, collection discipline, and the amount of cash committed before a client pays.
