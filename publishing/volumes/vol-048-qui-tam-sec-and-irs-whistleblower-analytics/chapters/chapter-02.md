## 1. Sell a bounded evidence result to the person controlling firm cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The near-term customer is a plaintiff or relator law firm, usually represented commercially by a managing partner, practice leader, or case-budget partner. That person buys a reduction in expensive uncertainty: whether a suspicious reimbursement pattern supports a provable false claim, whether counsel can plead representative transactions, and whether the prospective recovery justifies attorney time. A research associate can champion the purchase but may lack spending authority. Ask who signs the engagement, who approves invoices, and whether payment comes from firm operating cash, a client-funded expense account, or an identified litigation budget. Record the firm's legal name and actual obligor before collecting sensitive evidence. A signed $3,750 monthly retainer should identify deliverable capacity, acceptance criteria, permitted recipients, and the payment date; it should not promise an enforcement result. Counsel's acute pain is wasted diligence, weak pleading, fragmented source records, and missed evidentiary connections. No statute requires counsel to buy analytics. The purchase becomes rational when a reproducible dossier costs less than the attorney and analyst hours it replaces. Compare a $3,750 package with thirty internal hours at an expressly assumed $175 loaded hourly cost: $5,250 of internal effort, before assessing quality or opportunity cost.

For this chapter, organize the 11 mapped plays into delivery workstreams without treating every workstream as an independent award pathway: public-record acquisition, CMS Part B analysis, CMS Part D analysis, Open Payments linkage, SAM.gov procurement linkage, import-manifest matching, FOIA corroboration, representative-claim assembly, counsel intake, seal tracking, and payment reconciliation. The supplied play identifier is public_record_api_intelligence. Its county dockets, probate registries, unclaimed-property ledgers, and public APIs can reveal entity relationships or commercial triggers; they do not establish whistleblower eligibility or authorize collection of another person's property. Price an illustrative monthly batch of 2,000 records at $0.15 acquisition cost each, plus twelve analyst hours at $60 and four quality-control hours at $90. Direct production cost is $1,380 against a $3,750 retainer, leaving $2,370 before overhead, sales, and owner compensation treatment. If APIs throttle or licensing changes, switch to scheduled bulk FOIA/public-record exports, official data feeds, and low-frequency cached queues. Specify a smaller refresh frequency rather than bypassing access controls. Use SQLite for reproducible joins and SHA-256 manifests for source integrity. Keep payer, provider, beneficiary, and corporate identifiers distinct so entity-resolution errors never become allegations merely because a dashboard looks persuasive.

Screen credit before screening a thousand additional leads. Verify the firm through its state bar directory, secretary-of-state record where applicable, independently obtained contact number, and the partner responsible for the expense. Obtain permission for trade references and examine public litigation or insolvency records relevant to payment capacity without spreading confidential case information. A prominent contingency practice can still have severe cash timing pressure. Ask whether vendor invoices remain payable if the client withdraws, DOJ declines intervention, or the court dismisses the complaint. Require the answer in the engagement, not in an optimistic email. Offer a funded monthly retainer and cap unpaid production at one explicitly authorized milestone. A $7,500 exposure ceiling equals two $3,750 invoices; it is a credit limit, not permission to continue indefinitely after default. Route legitimate billing disputes to the responsible partner, suspend new work prospectively under the contract, and preserve counsel's access to materials already paid for. Never threaten disclosure of a sealed matter to collect an invoice. The agency, defendant, and prospective relator are not substitutes for the firm obligor unless a separate, lawful engagement expressly makes one of them responsible for the particular service.

## 2. Select the payment rail after identifying the obligor and release authority  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A payment rail moves money; it does not establish who owes the invoice or who may release funds. For recurring analytics, use an invoiced advance service retainer paid through ACH credit or verified wire into the operator's business account. Distinguish that vendor arrangement from a lawyer's client trust deposit, whose ownership and withdrawal rules depend on the applicable professional rules and engagement. Counsel must determine whether a particular expense may be paid from client funds; the vendor cannot authorize that withdrawal. A $3,750 advance covering a defined monthly capacity should have a clear earned-fee schedule and treatment of unused capacity. Do not call every deposit nonrefundable or recognize every advance immediately as revenue. For a disputed one-time dossier, genuine third-party escrow can protect both parties if the agreement states objective release conditions, decision authority, fees, and dispute handling. Escrow is not created by labeling an ordinary bank account accordingly. Keep payment instructions separate from evidence delivery. Confirm new beneficiary details by calling a previously verified number; email-only changes are a familiar invoice-diversion failure mode. Store confirmation records alongside the vendor ledger, not inside a broadly shared litigation folder containing sensitive facts.

Compare all eight arrangements against reversal risk, settlement timing, confidentiality, and actual collection leverage. Wire is useful for larger funded milestones but demands beneficiary verification. ACH credit supports recurring firm payments; ACH debit requires proper authorization and attention to applicable return rules. Neither an initiation screenshot nor a processor dashboard substitutes for reconciliation to bank activity. Platform checkout through Stripe or PayPal may suit a small non-sensitive pilot, but card disputes, reserve policies, and processor data retention can make it unsuitable for sealed work. Transmit only a neutral service description and invoice reference. Factoring may accelerate an eligible, earned, undisputed B2B receivable, subject to contract restrictions and confidentiality controls; it should not be presented as routine financing against an unfiled tip or contingent federal award. Milestone holds trade collection certainty for acceptance leverage, so cap the held amount. Illustratively, a $10,000 project with $4,000 funded before acquisition, $4,000 after validated delivery, and $2,000 after documented acceptance leaves a defined final exposure. Escrow release, retainer earning, and statutory disbursement are commercial arrangements layered onto transfer mechanisms, not interchangeable bank products. Price their administrative cost into the engagement instead of discovering it during collection.

Fee protection starts with precise performance obligations, not an aggressive non-circumvention clause. Define the dataset, date range, allowed refreshes, output schema, source licensing, correction window, and acceptance reviewer. Specify that a validated negative finding can satisfy the research assignment; otherwise the firm can confuse an unfavorable case conclusion with defective work. State when invoices become due, how disputed line items are identified, and whether undisputed amounts remain payable. Have counsel assess enforceability of late charges and any narrow non-solicitation or introduction protection. Never charge for suppressing a report, sell protection against reporting, or condition cooperation with authorities on payment. Preserve lawful reporting rights, including protections reflected in 17 CFR 240.21F-17. Do not turn the analytics fee into a percentage of the law firm's legal fees without a jurisdiction-specific assessment of professional restrictions. A vendor cannot create a statutory award entitlement by contract with counsel. For confidentiality, restrict permitted use and recipients while preserving required disclosures and counsel-directed production. Deliver an acceptance manifest listing files and hashes, then obtain confirmation from the named reviewer. That evidence supports an ordinary service-invoice collection claim without requiring disclosure of substantive allegations to a processor.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the paying legal entity
   - Match the engagement, invoice account, and authorized firm signer.
   - Record the budget owner, payment approver, due date, credit ceiling, and permitted source of funds before production.
2. Verify the transfer instructions
   - Confirm beneficiary details through an independently established telephone channel.
   - Use dual review for beneficiary changes and keep sealed matter descriptions out of payment references.
3. Deliver against objective acceptance fields
   - Send the source manifest, validation report, and agreed output through counsel's secure workspace.
   - Capture the named reviewer's acceptance or specific correction request within the contractual review period.
4. Reconcile settlement and stop excess exposure
   - Match bank activity to invoices and enforce the contractual unpaid-work ceiling.
   - Record processor fees, returns, held amounts, and earned revenue separately; suspend new work without threatening disclosure.

## 3. Underwrite statutory eligibility before assigning any value to award upside  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The False Claims Act does not pay an analyst merely for detecting an outlier. Under 31 U.S.C. § 3730, an eligible relator may receive a statutory share of recovered proceeds, subject to the statute's conditions, reductions, and exclusions. The usual intervention range is 15%–25%; when the government does not proceed and the relator successfully conducts the action, the usual range is 25%–30%. Those percentages apply to qualifying recovery, not a dataset's estimated billing universe. Counsel must evaluate the public-disclosure bar in § 3730(e)(4), including whether substantially the same allegations or transactions appeared through enumerated channels and whether an original-source exception applies. Public accessibility alone does not answer that legal test. Original analysis is not automatically an original-source qualification. Rule 9(b) requires particularity for fraud allegations; representative-claim evidence requirements and permissible pleading inferences vary by circuit. Build claim identifiers, dates, amounts, submitting entities, source provenance, and the alleged falsity mechanism, then let counsel assess sufficiency. If records only show high utilization or manufacturer payments, report that limited finding. A lawful pattern can look statistically unusual. Redirect the assignment to paid corroboration or close the lead instead of manufacturing transaction details to support a preferred theory.

File FCA complaints exclusively through licensed relator counsel under 31 U.S.C. § 3730(b)(2), with the complaint under seal and the required disclosure to the government. The statutory initial seal period is at least sixty days; extensions can produce multi-year investigations. Model the contingent horizon as 2–5+ years, including extended FCA seal periods where ordered, rather than assuming every matter remains sealed for that entire duration. Counsel controls service, recipient access, court-order interpretation, and communications. A seal breach can create serious sanctions and dismissal risk; dismissal is not automatic for every breach. DOJ non-intervention is distinct from dismissal under § 3730(c)(2)(A), sometimes discussed through the Granston policy framework. Track separate rates with explicit denominators and dated primary sources. Without verified cohort data, use labeled sensitivities instead of invented national statistics. An illustrative hundred-lead stress test rejects thirty on disclosure or eligibility and twenty on evidentiary insufficiency. Of fifty filed matters, assume fifteen interventions and thirty-five non-interventions; five of the latter encounter government dismissal. If ten intervened and three non-intervened matters eventually recover, thirty-seven filed matters recover zero. These are planning assumptions, not observed DOJ rates, and none funds this year's payroll.

SEC, IRS, and CFTC awards have separate statutory triggers and procedures. SEC awards generally range from 10%–30% of monetary sanctions collected in qualifying actions exceeding $1 million, subject to original-information and other eligibility requirements under 15 U.S.C. § 78u-6 and implementing rules. CFTC uses its own 10%–30% framework under 7 U.S.C. § 26. IRS mandatory awards under 26 U.S.C. § 7623(b) generally range from 15%–30% of collected proceeds when statutory thresholds and conditions apply; the disputed amount must exceed $2 million, and an individual taxpayer generally must have gross income above $200,000 for a relevant year. Public-source and other limitations can reduce or eliminate awards. Route SEC Form TCR and IRS Form 211 through counsel, and identify the actual eligible claimant rather than naming an analytics company by commercial convenience. SEC and CFTC whistleblowers are individuals; program rules govern the relevant applicant elsewhere. A $4 million qualifying SEC collection at an assumed 15% award rate produces $600,000 for the eligible claimant before applicable deductions, not $4 million of operator revenue. The operator books no award merely because it supported the submission. Reconcile any legally payable operator interest with counsel and accounting advice before recognizing realized proceeds.

> 
> The commercial engagement and the statutory submission require separate verification records; success in one does not establish the other.
> - Confirm the law firm's signed payment obligation survives non-intervention, dismissal, and a zero-recovery outcome.
> - Obtain counsel's documented assessment of public-disclosure exposure, claimant eligibility, and circuit-specific Rule 9(b) needs.
> - Verify the current seal order, approved recipients, secure delivery location, and counsel's instructions before transmitting FCA materials.
> - Record the exact award program, eligible applicant, required form, collection trigger, and zero-dollar operating-budget treatment before assigning contingent value.

Keep four ledgers conceptually separate: invoiced service fees, earned service revenue, bank collections, and contingent award interests. A funded retainer can provide cash before the underlying service is earned; an accepted invoice can create earned revenue before cash arrives. Track both differences rather than treating the bank balance as annual profit. For presentation under ASC 606, evaluate principal-versus-agent status for service arrangements; statutory awards require their own accounting assessment and are not automatically customer-contract revenue. Where the operator acts as an agent, recognize only the retained contribution appropriate to that arrangement, not funds collected for another party. Do not invent a retained_share percentage absent a supported contractual allocation. If $20,000 passes through an account and $15,000 belongs to another principal, only the supported $5,000 retained amount is the candidate contribution in that illustration. Medicare billings, contract face values, tax deficiencies, recovered government proceeds, and a claimant's gross award are different bases. Never substitute one for operator revenue. When eligible award cash is actually realized, maintain a separate bridge showing recipient entitlement, deductions, operator ownership, and recognition treatment. Reconcile that bridge to the fixed scorecard totals or identify a separate actual-results variance rather than silently changing the model.

Clarify what the overhead range includes: it cannot automatically replace the scenario's full cost envelope, particularly contractor delivery labor and other direct costs. At $9,500 monthly overhead, $45,000 covers about 4.7 months before additional direct costs; at $1,500, $10,000 covers about 6.7 months. A twelve-month sales cycle therefore requires lower burn, other legitimate funding, or staged scope. For Base, the $268,125 annual cost envelope averages $22,343.75 monthly, and three months equals $67,031.25. This illustrative reserve exceeds the startup range and must be built or financed explicitly, not assumed available. Monitor funded backlog, accounts-receivable aging, customer concentration, correction hours, source refresh expense, and the share of production covered by collected advances. Stress one large firm's departure and two months of delayed collections while assigning every pending award zero current cash. If the business fails that test, reduce unfunded delivery, collect smaller milestones sooner, or reprice the retainer. Better anomaly detection does not cure an insolvent payment structure.
