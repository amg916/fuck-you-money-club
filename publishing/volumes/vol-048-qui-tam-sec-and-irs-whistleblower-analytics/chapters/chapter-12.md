## 1. Days 1–7: Bound the eleven plays, staff the pod, and cap exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a narrow buyer and one testable fraud theory, not a warehouse of accusations. Candidate #48 uses one full-time operator and two contractors: a data engineer and a forensic reviewer. The founder owns sales, collection, scope, and counsel coordination; the engineer owns reproducible acquisition and joins; the reviewer owns source interpretation and contradiction testing. For this launch, map the eleven plays as P1 CMS Part B utilization, P2 CMS Part D prescribing, P3 Open Payments relationships, P4 SAM.gov award context, P5 permitted import-manifest comparisons, P6 entity resolution, P7 FOIA corroboration, P8 representative-claim reconstruction, P9 counsel retainers, P10 counsel-routed FCA, SEC, and IRS submission support, and P11 restricted-matter tracking. These are working execution labels, not eleven simultaneous product launches. Activate P1, P3, and P6 against one specialty during week one; retain the other lanes as documented adapters. CMS aggregates can indicate a question but cannot independently establish a knowingly false claim. SAM.gov registration is not evidence of a procurement payment, and manifest inconsistencies require shipment-level explanations before escalation. Reserve contractor capacity explicitly: twelve engineering hours and eight reviewer hours weekly, with no case-specific production outside a written scope.

Adopt a $25,000 startup-capital assumption within the canonical $10,000–$45,000 range. Authorize no more than $10,000 of cumulative cash disbursements through Day 30: $6,000 for initial counsel consultation, access controls, equipment, and permitted source acquisition, plus $4,000 of operating expense. The monthly operating envelope allocates $1,800 to engineering, $1,200 to forensic review, $300 to storage and tools, $300 to buyer development, and $400 to contingency. Quoted contractor hours must fit those amounts; otherwise reduce scope before committing work. Maintain the same $4,000 monthly ceiling through Day 90, producing an $18,000 cumulative spend ceiling and $7,000 untouched reserve. These are launch controls, not the annual scorecard expense forecast. Founder living costs and tax reserves need separate funding; do not hide them inside supposed free labor. The stated monthly operating range is $1,500–$9,500, but its upper bound is inappropriate before retainers validate demand. Pay no source for stolen records and buy no access whose permitted uses exclude litigation support. On Day 2, record every recurring charge, cancellation date, contractor commitment, and maximum remaining liability in a cash register that the founder reconciles each Friday.

Build evidence controls before downloading production material. Use Python, SQLite, and SHA-256 manifests for acquisition and transformations, with encrypted storage, individual accounts, multifactor authentication, and a separate workspace per matter. Every extract needs publisher, retrieval timestamp, release version, permitted-use basis, query parameters, and digest. Keep original bytes immutable and log corrections as new versions. Use synthetic examples for sales demonstrations; never expose a prospective client's allegations to another buyer. Counsel must determine privilege arrangements, preservation duties, conflicts, and appropriate disclosure paths before sensitive intake. Under 31 U.S.C. § 3730(b)(2), an FCA complaint is filed in camera and remains under seal for at least sixty days, with government extensions possible; it is not served on the defendant until ordered. A matter may remain under seal for 2–5+ years, while award collection can take equally long or longer. That planning horizon does not describe SEC or IRS procedures as court-seal systems. Seal violations can imperil a case and its economics, although dismissal is not automatic for every violation. Require counsel's written distribution instructions, restrict matter identifiers, and disable outbound demonstrations using restricted evidence before the first substantive interview.

## 2. Days 8–14: Turn anomalies into reproducible, falsifiable dossiers  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Week two converts raw variation into an auditable shortlist. Run P1 and P2 only within comparable specialties, geographies, and reporting periods; payment totals without beneficiary and service denominators create misleading rankings. Join P3 Open Payments using provider identifiers and explicit reporting years, then test whether a relationship predates the relevant conduct. Use P4 procurement records to identify actual award identifiers and modifications rather than treating registration as a contract. P5 manifest comparisons require documented coverage limitations, consignee identity checks, and alternative explanations such as transshipment or classification changes. The engineer produces twenty ranked anomalies, but the reviewer must reject at least every unsupported causal inference rather than satisfy an arbitrary accusation quota. Check a stratified sample of twenty entity joins and require at least nineteen correct matches before producing client materials. Record source completeness separately from anomaly precision: a complete extract can still yield useless leads. Preserve negative controls, such as comparable providers without the flagged relationship, and publish the exact SQL and parameters behind each chart. An operator who cannot reproduce yesterday's ranking should repair the pipeline before contacting any law firm about its findings.

P7 and P8 supply the bridge between a pattern and a counsel-reviewable theory. Submit two narrowly framed FOIA requests for identifiable agency records, tracking request numbers, fee limits, and responses; do not assume statutory response periods mean records arrive during this sprint. For each of three candidate dossiers, identify the asserted representation, actor, relevant date, payment pathway, and primary record that supports or contradicts each element. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 9(b) requires particularity for fraud allegations; representative claims are a strong operating target, but circuit standards and reliable-indicia arguments differ. Counsel decides whether the evidence satisfies the applicable pleading standard. Separately inventory disclosures under 31 U.S.C. § 3730(e)(4), including relevant federal proceedings, federal reports and investigations, and news media. Publicly accessible material is not automatically a qualifying public disclosure, yet a clever recombination of public records does not automatically establish original-source status. Document independent knowledge, timing, and material additions for counsel's assessment. Mark a dossier unqualified when the public-disclosure issue remains unresolved; a high anomaly score cannot erase that defect. Treat corroboration spending as bounded discovery of facts, never as permission to reconstruct missing evidence.

Route each theory into the appropriate program rather than recycling one generic whistleblower packet. FCA relator shares generally run 15%–25% when the government proceeds and 25%–30% when it does not, subject to statutory qualifications and reductions. SEC awards generally range from 10%–30% of qualifying monetary sanctions collected in covered actions exceeding $1 million; original-information and submission rules matter. IRS section 7623(b) generally provides 15%–30% of collected proceeds when its statutory conditions apply, including the amount-in-dispute threshold and the individual taxpayer income condition. CFTC referrals need their own 7 U.S.C. § 26 analysis and are outside this sprint's active filing lanes. Thus the shelf's 15%–30% shorthand cannot substitute for program-specific calculations. P10 prepares counsel-routed Form TCR or Form 211 support, or FCA complaint exhibits; the analytics pod does not submit a qui tam complaint itself. Being a support vendor creates no automatic right to a statutory share. Only a legally eligible claimant's actual entitlement belongs in an award model, and counsel must assess any proposed assignment or compensation arrangement. Sell defined analytical services, not a percentage of a law firm's legal fees.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Preserve the source
   - Capture original bytes, publisher details, permissions, and SHA-256 digest.
   - Assign a source identifier before any transformation and retain retrieval parameters.
2. Reproduce the signal
   - Run versioned SQL with documented cohorts, denominators, and exclusion rules.
   - Have the second contractor reproduce the ranking and inspect twenty entity joins.
3. Attempt to disprove the theory
   - Test coding changes, identity errors, timing differences, and innocent explanations.
   - Attach contrary evidence and distinguish observed facts from analytical conclusions.
4. Route through counsel
   - Deliver a restricted dossier with disclosure chronology and program-specific questions.
   - Counsel determines pleading sufficiency, claimant eligibility, submission method, and authorized recipients.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four habits that make forensic work useful to relator counsel**
> 
> Counsel buys evidence that survives contradiction, provenance questions, and procedural scrutiny, not the largest anomaly count.
> - Save CMS release notes beside each extract; changed suppression or aggregation rules can explain an apparent year-over-year billing jump.
> - Maintain a separate public-disclosure chronology so counsel can evaluate source status without searching through the anomaly narrative.
> - Put the strongest innocent explanation on the first dossier page, then identify the exact record needed to test it.
> - Quote FOIA requests by agency record series, date range, and identifier; cap search fees and preserve every request acknowledgment.

These are illustrative prices, not canonical scorecard inputs or expected first-month receipts. Specify acceptance criteria, revision limits, permitted recipients, source costs, and invoice timing. A commercially qualified conversation includes a responsible buyer, recurring analytical need, procurement path, and budget discussion; an enthusiastic reply alone does not count. Demand advance payment for the pilot before case-specific delivery where the firm's process permits it. Do not buy leads containing confidential allegations or promise access to sealed matters. Measure time spent on proposals separately from delivery so apparent service margins do not conceal unpaid selling effort. The canonical first-deal window remains four to twelve months, so a thirty-day sprint can validate demand without producing a signed retainer or an invoice.

Keep the annual scorecard intact and the launch forecast separate. Conservative and Base use one full-time operator plus two contractors; Scaled uses two full-time operators plus three contractors. No supplied allocation divides those totals between retainers and awards, so do not invent one. The revenue label refers to operator revenue, not government recoveries, client damages, sanctions, tax deficiencies, or claimed overpayments. Maintain separate ledger accounts for earned support fees and realized operator-entitled award amounts. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis where relevant to support arrangements and retain only the operator's recognized share when acting as agent; counsel and the accountant determine appropriate treatment of award rights and receipts. Collected cash and recognized revenue need separate columns. A $3,000 advance for unfinished work improves liquidity but is not automatically earned revenue. Every runway calculation assigns zero dollars to uncollected awards and zero dollars to unsigned service proposals, regardless of their perceived probability.

Explicitly stress the award funnel without presenting invented national rates as observed facts. For an illustrative cohort of one hundred theories, assume 60% survive counsel's public-disclosure and source-status screen, and 50% of those satisfy the applicable particularity assessment. Of thirty remaining theories, assume 20% intervention and 80% non-intervention. Apply hypothetical government-dismissal rates of 10% to the intervention branch and 25% to the non-intervention branch, then recovery rates of 50% and 15% to their respective survivors. The result is 2.7 recovery equivalents in each branch, or 5.4 overall; the remaining 94.6 produce no recovery in this model. These conditional assumptions are sensitivity inputs, not current DOJ statistics, and fractional counts represent expected outcomes across repeated cohorts. Granston refers to a DOJ dismissal-policy framework, not a standalone statutory dismissal rate. Government dismissal authority comes from 31 U.S.C. § 3730(c)(2)(A), subject to applicable procedure; non-intervention itself does not terminate a case. Recalculate with zero recovery and a five-year collection delay before considering any award exposure. Only retainers fund operating commitments. Record source, period, denominator, and intervention definitions before replacing hypothetical rates with published DOJ observations.

## 4. Days 22–30: Run the acceptance test and schedule Day 60/90 decisions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use the final nine days to prove repeatability, not to manufacture a filing deadline. On Day 22, freeze a candidate dossier and ask the reviewer to reproduce it without the engineer's verbal assistance. Day 23 checks every material assertion against its source; Day 24 resolves counsel's distribution and program-routing questions. Day 25 presents the scoped service to qualified buyers, and Day 26 records proposal objections without exposing restricted material. Day 27 reconciles cash, contractor hours, and remaining liabilities. Day 28 tests backup restoration and a simulated misdirected-message response; Day 29 compiles the decision packet; Day 30 records Kill, Fix, or Scale with an owner and deadline. Define dossier completeness as the percentage of required fields containing a supported fact or an explicit, assigned research question; it does not measure legal merit. Require every material factual assertion to have a source regardless of the completeness score. Track reviewer rework as corrective hours divided by total delivery hours. Track join accuracy on a documented sample, not a convenient handpicked subset. A missing representative claim remains a substantive limitation even when every dashboard metric is green.

Decision precedence is simple: a hard stop overrides every commercial metric, and Scale requires every threshold in its row. Kill means terminate the affected acquisition route or business hypothesis while preserving records and honoring existing duties; it never means delete evidence. Stop immediately for unlawful sourcing, inability to protect restricted material, or spending beyond the authorized ceiling. Day-30 Scale means only authorize the next bounded experiment: three reproducible dossiers, 95% sampled join accuracy, 90% dossier completeness, four qualified buyer conversations, and spend within $10,000. With adequate controls but weaker demand, Fix one specialty, offer, or source for fourteen days rather than expanding all three. By Day 60, require eight cumulative qualified conversations, two written scoped proposals, and one buyer-confirmed evaluation timetable for continued commercial expansion. Zero proposals after eight qualified conversations kills the current offer, not necessarily the entire market. The cumulative spending limit becomes $14,000. No award count, filed complaint, or promising counsel reaction can replace those thresholds. Track buyer-confirmed timing in writing and distinguish procurement delay from an offer that solves no funded operational problem.

At Day 90, true delivery Scale requires two paying retainers totaling at least $8,000 monthly, two consecutive months with collected retainer cash covering cash operating expense by at least 1.25 times, and at least 50% delivery contribution margin. Calculate that margin after contractor labor, matter-specific tools, and founder delivery hours valued at $75 per hour; it is not the canonical annual net margin. Require rework of 10% or less and at least three months of unrestricted cash runway after proposed commitments. Under the full-spend, zero-receipt launch case, $7,000 remaining against $4,000 monthly expense gives only 1.75 months of runway and fails that test. If two buyers document viable evaluation dates within ninety more days but receipts lag, choose Fix: reduce monthly spending to $1,500 and reassess without hiring. The remaining $7,000 then covers about 4.67 months, subject to outstanding liabilities. If neither paid demand nor documented evaluation survives, pause the commercial hypothesis. Even when Scale passes, retain the three-person pod until delivery utilization and contracted cash justify expansion; the five-person Scaled scorecard is an annual operating case, not a Day-90 staffing entitlement.
