Candidate #40, ranked #08 of 42 with a 93/100 Money-to-Manpower Score, requires a controlled evidence sprint, not an immediate insurance placement. This chapter sequences 11 mapped plays through one full-time operator and three specialist contractors, from Form 5500 targeting through PBM reconciliation and Workers' Comp refund recovery. Days 1–30 establish licensed authority, secure intake, reproducible analyses and two decision-ready proposals under a $6,000 cash-disbursement ceiling. ## 1. Days 1–7: Build the Licensed Pod and Select One Entry Wedge  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one employer segment, one home jurisdiction and one accountable producer, rather than advertising three unrelated savings promises. Assign the full-time operator prospect selection, employer interviews, compensation disclosure, proposal ownership and cash control. Contractor C1 supplies benefits actuarial and stop-loss analysis; C2 handles PBM contract interpretation and prescription repricing; C3 handles Workers' Comp classification, experience-rating review and carrier correction packets. Verify actual qualifications before assigning work: an analyst designation does not confer producer authority, and a producer license does not authorize every TPA activity. Obtain narrowly scoped outside counsel when required, charged to the launch budget rather than represented as another permanent pod seat. Day 1 produces a state-and-service authority matrix covering individual and business-entity licensing, lines of authority, appointments and any administrator registration. Use NIPR and the relevant insurance department to verify records, not a contractor's email assertion. New York Insurance Law §2102 provides a concrete example of prohibited unlicensed producer activity; other states require separate checks. Until the necessary authority is active, restrict work to internal preparation and activities the applicable jurisdiction permits. Do not solicit, negotiate or accept transaction compensation on the assumption that a future license will cure today's conduct.

Use an explicit eleven-play work map so adjacent opportunities do not become eleven simultaneous engagements. P01 is Form 5500 renewal targeting; P02 is broker compensation and incumbent-contract reconstruction; P03 is self-funded plan feasibility; P04 is captive stop-loss suitability; P05 is specific and aggregate stop-loss restructuring; P06 is TPA fee and service procurement; P07 is NADAC-referenced PBM repricing; P08 is rebate and pass-through reconciliation; P09 is reference-based pricing feasibility with balance-bill defense; P10 is Workers' Comp payroll and classification correction; P11 is experience-modification review and contingent refund recovery. These are the chapter's operational labels for the eleven mapped plays, not eleven promised revenue streams. P01 and P02 support every account; select either the benefits branch or the Workers' Comp branch as the first paid wedge. Defer P04 and P09 unless employer size, funding tolerance, contractual protection and qualified specialists support them. On Days 2–4, screen forty employers with roughly 100–500 covered employees and a documented renewal or audit window. Use public DOL EFAST2 filings, employer websites and permitted business outreach. Treat Schedule A and Schedule C as historical clues: filing lag, reporting exemptions and plan structure can leave important commercial relationships outside either schedule.

Set the first-month disbursement ceiling at $6,000 against the published $8,000–$45,000 startup range. Allocate $1,400 to licensing, insurance and specialist contract review, $600 to secure systems, $2,400 to contractor analysis, $600 to targeted outreach and $1,000 to contingencies. These are planning envelopes, not vendor quotations; if required licensing or coverage costs more, reduce scope before committing funds. At the $8,000 capitalization floor, leave $2,000 untouched and sign no automatic contractor renewals. Create a weekly ledger separating cash paid, obligations already incurred, collectible invoices and speculative success fees. Preserve sufficient cash for the next thirty days of unavoidable obligations before releasing any new discretionary purchase. The launch ceiling includes setup purchases and does not replace the volume's $2,000–$11,000 monthly operating-expense range. By Day 5, configure Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace under appropriate contractual terms, MFA, restricted folders and named account access. Begin with aggregate census and contract documents; receive identifiable claims only after the applicable privacy role, business associate agreement and permitted purpose are established. Days 6–7 rehearse intake with synthetic records, test contractor access removal and produce the first scored prospect list. No employer evidence should enter personal email or an unapproved public model endpoint.

>

## 2. Days 8–14: Turn Outreach into Four Reconciled Evidence Packs  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run Days 8–10 as a measured discovery sequence, not a mass solicitation exercise. Contact the twelve priority employers individually with one specific observation from a public filing or a confirmed renewal calendar. Ask who can authorize contract review, when incumbent notice is due, which claims or payroll records can be released and whether procurement requires a separate broker-of-record process. Count a qualified conversation only when employer size, decision authority, timing and willingness to provide relevant evidence are documented. Target six such conversations by Day 14, then four authorized evidence packs by Day 21. A prospect who likes a savings headline but cannot produce its PBM agreement is not analysis-ready. Explain the engagement economics before requesting signatures: fixed diagnostic charges, retained advisory PEPM fees, stop-loss or TPA compensation where permitted, and contingent Workers' Comp recovery splits each require a defined payer and trigger. For covered group-health brokerage or consulting arrangements expecting at least $1,000 in direct or indirect compensation, apply ERISA §408(b)(2)(B), 29 U.S.C. §1108(b)(2)(B), with the required disclosures reasonably in advance of contracting. A prospect's request to skip compensation detail is a reason to repair the process, not an expedited path to a signed appointment.

Build distinct intake manifests rather than requesting every available employee record. The benefits manifest starts with plan documents, eligibility counts, paid-claims summaries, large-claim information at the permitted level, current stop-loss policy and TPA agreement. Record incurred and paid periods separately so a 12/12 contract is not compared casually with a 12/15 alternative. The pharmacy manifest requests the executed PBM agreement and amendments, claim extract, eligibility basis, rebate statements, specialty carve-out terms and invoice summaries. C2 creates a field dictionary for service date, NDC, quantity, days supply, ingredient cost, dispensing fee and member cost share, using identifiers only when the authorized analysis requires them. The Workers' Comp manifest requests policy declarations, endorsements, payroll by classification, final audits, loss runs and the applicable rating worksheet. C3 first confirms whether NCCI or a state rating bureau governs the account; interstate assumptions can invalidate the entire correction. Assign each file an employer, period, source and version. Under 45 CFR §§164.502 and 164.504, define permitted uses and applicable business associate responsibilities; apply the minimum-necessary standard where it governs. A protected folder alone does not establish authority to disclose or reuse the underlying records.

Days 11–14 establish whether the evidence can support an economically credible proposal. Reconcile pharmacy claim totals to invoices within 1% of dollars for the same population and period, with every residual explained before pricing recommendations leave the pod. Require at least 95% of included pharmacy spend to have the necessary pricing fields, and show the excluded remainder separately. NADAC is a reference for selected acquisition costs, not a complete substitute for a PBM contract: specialty distribution, dispensing costs, rebates and unavailable observations need separate treatment. For Workers' Comp, tie payroll exposure to audited totals within 1%, then identify each unresolved classification or loss-record difference. Do not label an open reserve a recoverable overcharge merely because an employer disputes it. For self-funding, distinguish historical paid claims from projected incurred liability and enumerate specific deductibles, aggregate corridors, lasers and run-out obligations. C1 owns that comparison; the operator owns whether the employer can fund the resulting timing exposure. Each contractor returns a one-page exception log with source references, impact range and next evidence request. Cap concurrent deep analyses at four accounts, preserving the four-person pod's capacity to correct exceptions rather than stacking unread documents into a growing pipeline.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Veteran Tactics That Protect the First Cohort**
> 
> The fastest credible launch removes predictable evidence disputes before the employer forwards a proposal to the incumbent broker.
> - Ask for the PBM amendment index before repricing; a rebate addendum or specialty exclusion can reverse an attractive ingredient-cost comparison.
> - Request both the current stop-loss policy and renewal proposal; a new individual laser can overwhelm apparent aggregate savings.
> - Preserve the original experience-rating worksheet and revision date; a later carrier correction must be compared against the actual billed period.
> - Record incumbent termination and broker-of-record notice terms on the first call; missing the available window can delay revenue beyond the entire sprint.

## 3. Days 15–21: Build Two Decision-Ready Proposals Without Inflating Revenue  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Days 15–17 convert the cleanest accounts into paired employer and operator economics. For the employer, compare total expected cost under a common population, coverage period and risk assumption. For the operator, show only earned retained fees and commissions, separately from employer savings. As an illustrative pricing bridge, 250 covered employees at $10 retained PEPM produce $2,500 monthly and $30,000 annual advisory revenue if the covered count remains constant for twelve months. If $15,000 of additional annual commission is actually retained, separately earned and not an offset against the advisory agreement, that account contributes $45,000 annual operator revenue. Do not add an already embedded commission again. A $40,000 carrier refund with a contractually earned 25% recovery split yields $10,000 operator fee revenue, not $40,000; employer claim funds and premiums remain excluded. When another licensed party receives part of a fee, calculate the operator's retained share before adding it to the scorecard. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent and variable-consideration analysis to the actual contracts with the accounting adviser. Keep forecast, signed contract, recognized revenue and collected cash in separate ledger columns. A signed contingent-recovery engagement does not establish that a refund or commission has been earned.

Anchor every scenario to the supplied Formula v2.0 scorecard rather than reverse-engineering new annual promises from a thirty-day funnel. The Conservative case is $120,000 annual retained-basis revenue less $81,600 total expense, leaving $38,400 owner net profit at 32%. The Base case is $535,000 less $294,250, leaving $240,750 at 45.0%. The Scaled case is $950,000 less $399,000, leaving $551,000 at 58%. Although the scorecard calls its top line gross revenue, its specified economic basis is net retained advisory, commission and recovery-fee revenue; gross employer premium is not an alternative denominator. Conservative and Base both use 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C. Expense totals are arithmetic reconciliations, not additional contractor quotations or a claim that monthly operating overhead explains every annual cost. For sprint decisions, estimate account contribution after directly attributable contractor time and delivery cost, then separately deduct acquisition spending and shared overhead. A 50% proposed account contribution threshold is therefore distinct from the 45.0% Base owner-profit margin. Do not extrapolate an unsigned pipeline into recognized revenue or apply another retention haircut to amounts already stated as retained. Track collected deposits separately when service obligations still prevent revenue recognition.

Days 18–21 produce proposals an employer can challenge line by line. Each proposal contains the baseline period, excluded evidence, implementation prerequisites, source-backed opportunity range, fee schedule and a named decision date. P03 through P06 require C1 to distinguish a quoted premium reduction from a shift in employer claim volatility, collateral needs or run-out liability. P07 and P08 require C2 to separate point-of-sale pricing from later rebates and to reconcile any guarantee definition with its exclusions. P10 and P11 require C3 to identify the correct rating rule, correction recipient and applicable objection deadline, without representing the bureau as having accepted an unfiled argument. P04 and P09 receive feasibility findings only unless the specialist evidence supports actual procurement. For reference-based pricing, name the balance-bill defense provider, scope of representation, settlement funding and exclusions before showing an employer a net savings number. Require two proposals from four authorized evidence packs by Day 21, with each baseline reconciled and each fee payer identified. Have a contractor who did not build the relevant calculation review its units and assumptions; the operator signs the final exception log. If source records cannot support a claim, narrow that claim rather than filling the gap with a market average.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the employer baseline
   - Version the source records and align population, period and coverage.
   - Keep claim liability, premiums, payroll and operator fees in distinct fields; record every exclusion before calculating differences.
2. Calculate only supported opportunities
   - Assign the selected play to its qualified contractor and reconcile inputs.
   - Use contract-specific pharmacy terms, actual stop-loss provisions or the applicable rating-bureau methodology instead of generic percentage reductions.
3. Bridge employer economics to retained fees
   - Identify the fee payer, earning event, sharing obligation and delivery cost.
   - Show recognized revenue and collected cash separately; avoid applying retained share twice to already netted scorecard amounts.
4. Challenge and release the proposal
   - Resolve material reviewer exceptions and obtain an employer decision date.
   - Retain the reviewed version, compensation disclosure evidence and signed scope before expanding work or requesting additional protected records.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Beyond: Apply Kill, Fix or Scale to Evidence, Not Enthusiasm  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use Days 22–24 to test whether the proposals survive buyer scrutiny. On Day 22, present the first proposal with its contractor available for technical questions; Day 23 does the same for the second. On Day 24, obtain written objections, incumbent responses and the employer's next procurement action. Days 25–27 close evidence gaps, confirm carrier or vendor submission requirements and recalculate contribution using actual contractor hours. Do not issue a broker-of-record letter or send employer records to an outside carrier without explicit employer authorization. Days 28–30 reconcile cash, inspect delivery capacity and publish the decision memo. The volume's first-deal expectation is two to five months; zero collected revenue on Day 30 is therefore not sufficient evidence to abandon a qualified launch. Instead, require the leading indicators built during the sprint: six qualified conversations, four authorized packs, two reconciled proposals and two dated next-step meetings. Count a meeting only when the employer accepts the calendar invitation and the decision owner is named. Apply a one-week correction window to clerical omissions, but never waive authority, privacy or funding defects. The operator records the decision and its evidence; contractors recommend technical corrections without committing the business to additional spending.

Define the thresholds before reviewing results. Kill means stop new discretionary spending on the affected channel or service, preserve records and satisfy existing obligations; it does not mean abandon an employer mid-engagement. Fix means one bounded ten-business-day experiment with one owner, one measurable defect and no new fixed overhead. Scale means the next limited acquisition cohort, not immediate movement to the six-person annual Scaled case. At Day 30, stop the acquisition channel if forty researched employers have received two permitted, personalized contact attempts each and produced fewer than three qualified conversations. If the outreach denominator was not completed, repair execution instead of declaring market failure. At Day 60, require at least one signed paid engagement or two written buyer-confirmed decisions due within thirty days to keep the current wedge alive. At Day 90, scale only with two signed paid engagements, at least $5,000 collected operator fees, 50% delivered contribution and no material unresolved evidence exceptions. Those are management thresholds, not scorecard outputs. Stop sooner for unauthorized regulated activity or inability to fund unavoidable obligations. A later procurement date can support Fix, but only with buyer confirmation and a cash-funded extension, not a salesperson's optimistic probability.

Authorize additional spending only through a cash-backed release. Day 30 Scale permits up to $2,000 of additional discretionary acquisition and analysis commitments for the next cohort; Day 60 Scale permits another $3,000; Day 90 Scale permits another $5,000. These releases are ceilings, not budgets automatically available from the initial $8,000 capitalization. After each proposed release, available cash must still cover all existing payables and the next thirty days of unavoidable operating obligations. If that test fails, the decision becomes Fix through narrower scope, additional committed capital or collected earned fees; projected refunds cannot fund the reserve. Keep the 1 FT + 3 C pod through the first repeatable delivery cycle, with no more than four active deep analyses and at least 90% of agreed milestones delivered on time. Consider the second full-time seat and fourth contractor only after three consecutive months of positive operating cash flow and eight weeks of signed backlog that exceeds current capacity. Even then, reconcile the hire to retained revenue and actual workload rather than assuming the $950,000 Scaled case has arrived. The final launch memo must state what was sold, what was earned, what was collected and what remains contingent. Maintain separate renewal and recovery calendars so slow carrier corrections do not conceal a deteriorating benefits pipeline.
