## 1. Mine renewal windows and executive events before buying broad lead lists  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the first account universe around employers with roughly 200–2,000 covered employees, an identifiable benefits decision maker, and enough time before renewal to evaluate alternatives. Treat this band as an initial targeting hypothesis, not a universal underwriting threshold. Download Department of Labor Form 5500 datasets through EFAST2 resources; join employer EIN, plan number, filing year, participant counts, and Schedule A contract periods into a dated account record. Separate health contracts from dental, vision, disability, and life coverage before assigning a renewal hypothesis. Schedule A insurance periods do not automatically establish a self-funded medical renewal, and Schedule C reporting is not a complete broker compensation ledger. Public filings can lag operating reality, amended filings can replace earlier values, and exemptions leave some employers outside this universe. Verify timing on the first conversation. An illustrative batch of 120 employers might yield 36 apparently relevant renewal windows and 12 verified dates; these are planning assumptions, not published conversion rates. Store source URL, retrieval date, evidence excerpt, and confidence separately from inferred need. Never turn a participant count into enrolled employees without checking the definition. The first sellable deliverable is a renewal evidence brief, not an unsolicited proposal claiming that a captive or self-funded plan will save money.

Implement sec_edgar_8k_vendor_trigger_arbitrage as a timing layer over that employer universe. Poll SEC EDGAR current-filing RSS feeds and documented JSON endpoints where available; resolve the accession, issuer identity, filing type, and event text rather than relying on keyword headlines. Form D reports exempt offerings, but the amount offered is not cash received; distinguish it from the reported amount sold. Route Form 8-K Item 5.02 departures or appointments, Item 1.01 material agreements, and Item 2.01 acquisition closings into separate queues. None proves benefit dissatisfaction. Require an employer match and a plausible operating consequence, such as acquired workforces or a new CFO inheriting renewal decisions. Use an identifying User-Agent, a token bucket capped at five requests per second across workers, deduplication by accession, and backoff on throttling. Each night, mirror available SEC bulk ZIP archives for historical lookbacks; use daily indexes and accession retrieval where no corresponding ZIP exists. Do not assume every feed has a nightly archive. A batch of 40 events producing eight relevant accounts and two conversations is an explicit test case. Aim for human-reviewed, sub-hour executive outreach after ingestion, before slower commercial databases refresh; measure actual latency rather than promising universal speed.

Map the chapter's 11 sourcing plays to distinct buying reasons: EDGAR executive triggers, Form 5500 renewal targeting, Schedule A/C compensation discovery, acquisition census reconciliation, NADAC benchmark repricing, PBM rebate reconciliation, stop-loss renewal comparison, TPA service unbundling, NCCI E-Mod correction, payroll classification review, and partner-led diagnostic demand. The matrix starts the first seven; the next section completes the map. Keep the outreach consequence specific. A new CFO receives a dated renewal-risk brief; an acquired business receives a census and contract inventory; an employer with a transparent PBM already receives no spread accusation. For compensation discovery, 600 covered employees at $4 PEPM creates $28,800 annual advisory fees only after enrollment, scope, and payment terms are confirmed. An acquisition census engagement priced at 800 employees times $3 PEPM also produces $28,800 annually, but do not stack both fees for identical work. A $500,000 stop-loss premium with a contractual 3% commission produces $15,000 gross commission before any contractual sharing. Neither premium nor alleged employer savings becomes operator revenue. Where evidence is weak, offer a fixed-scope inventory instead of an outcome fee. Preserve a written reason to abandon each play when the incumbent arrangement already meets the employer's needs.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Win the timing advantage without manufacturing a savings story**
> 
> Experienced operators sell a verifiable next step while preserving the employer's existing service continuity.
> - Compare amended Form 5500 filings before outreach; a revised Schedule A can invalidate the renewal date in a purchased lead list.

## 2. Qualify recoverable economics before requesting sensitive records  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a 100-point qualification scorecard with six explicit components: renewal or correction timing worth 20 points, verified economic exposure worth 25, access to a budget owner worth 15, accessible contract evidence worth 15, licensed delivery capacity worth 15, and workable payment terms worth 10. Define full, partial, and zero credit in the CRM rather than letting enthusiasm determine the score. Full timing credit means a confirmed review window and enough implementation time; a rumored renewal earns five points at most. Economic exposure requires a defensible fee basis after delivery cost, not a large premium alone. Work accounts scoring at least 75 immediately, nurture 60–74 with a dated document request, and close lower scores unless new evidence changes the inputs. Hard stops override the total: no authorized decision maker, missing required producer authority, or pressure to conceal compensation. Maintain separate health and workers' compensation tracks because the same employer can qualify for one and fail the other. An expiring health renewal may be unsuitable for transition while a documented payroll correction remains actionable. Ask initially for aggregate counts, renewal dates, contract summaries, and redacted worksheets. These establish fit without sending individual claim information through a prospecting platform.

Treat the PBM wedge as a reconciliation problem before treating it as a replacement sale. Normalize National Drug Codes, package units, quantities, dispense dates, retail versus mail channels, and specialty exclusions before comparing claims with CMS NADAC files. NADAC benchmarks pharmacy acquisition costs for covered survey populations; it is neither a universal contract price nor proof of unlawful spread. An illustrative 12,000 eligible fills with a $2 modeled difference creates $24,000 of employer exposure before dispensing fees, rebates, administrative charges, and implementation costs. A $4,000 diagnostic fee is operator revenue if earned; the $24,000 is not. For rebate reconciliation, a verified $100,000 recovery with a permitted 20% collection fee yields $20,000 only under the executed scope and recognition terms. Check licensing and conflicts before offering contingency pricing. Complete a business associate agreement when the service relationship requires one, restrict access by role, encrypt transfers, and define deletion dates. A confidentiality agreement alone does not authorize every disclosure. Use the minimum necessary information where that HIPAA standard applies. If usable records cannot be released, deliver a contract-definition audit using redacted examples and postpone claims repricing. Keep all patient-level information outside outbound CRM notes, demonstration spreadsheets, and email sequences.

Complete the remaining plays with independent evidence paths. TPA service unbundling might support 400 employees at $5 PEPM, or $24,000 annual advisory fees, only when the employer buys genuinely separate work and disclosed compensation avoids hidden overlap. NCCI E-Mod correction starts with the applicable worksheet, payroll periods, loss runs, valuation dates, and carrier reporting history; changes must follow the governing bureau's rules. A modeled $60,000 premium refund and 25% permitted collection split yields $15,000, but no receivable should be assumed from an analyst's estimate. Payroll classification review examines actual duties and state rules rather than searching for the cheapest code. A separate $40,000 collected correction at 20% produces $8,000; exclude amounts already counted in the E-Mod engagement. Partner-led demand comes from payroll firms, benefits counsel, accountants, and licensed brokers with noncompeting specialties. Ten partner conversations yielding three introductions and one paid mandate is a testable planning ratio. Do not pay insurance-related referral compensation before checking the relevant state's licensing and payment restrictions. Where contingency fees, referrals, or delegated administration create unresolved authority issues, use a properly licensed delivery partner or limit the engagement to an authorized fixed-fee analytical scope without negotiating coverage or handling claims.

## 3. Run a measured outreach cadence that sells the smallest useful mandate  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Design outbound around one observed event, one bounded hypothesis, and one low-friction request. For a verified acquisition, write: 'Your Item 2.01 filing describes the completed acquisition. If the acquired workforce has separate benefit renewal dates, a contract inventory can identify duplicated administration before renewal. Would a twenty-minute scope call be useful?' Do not claim knowledge of employee health, incumbent misconduct, or undisclosed contract terms. On day zero, send a short business email with the source link; on day two, place a manually dialed business call; on day five, offer a one-page evidence brief; on day nine, make a contextual LinkedIn connection request; on day fourteen, close the sequence with a clear opt-out. A sub-hour EDGAR alert deserves faster initial review, not more aggressive follow-ups. Apply CAN-SPAM identification, accurate headers, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out to commercial email. Honor suppression centrally across contractors. Avoid automated texts and prerecorded calls without reviewing the applicable TCPA requirements and state rules. Track reply quality rather than email opens, which are distorted by privacy features and scanners. One useful response is worth more than hundreds of unverified impressions. Keep the cadence at account level so three contacts do not receive simultaneous copies.

Use a cohort funnel to distinguish insufficient demand from poor execution. An illustrative first campaign begins with 300 raw employer records, qualifies 180 against evidence and timing, reaches 150 through valid contact paths, generates 30 positive replies, books 18 discovery calls, secures ten authorized diagnostics, and closes four paid mandates. That means 60% raw-to-qualified, 83.3% qualified-to-reached, 20% reached-to-positive, 60% positive-to-discovery, 55.6% discovery-to-diagnostic, and 40% diagnostic-to-mandate conversion. Overall raw-to-mandate conversion is 1.33%. These are starting hypotheses, not industry benchmarks. If half as many reached accounts respond positively and downstream ratios hold, expect roughly two mandates, subject to small-sample variation. Run weekly cohorts and display counts beside percentages. A signed mandate counts as a win only when scope, price, authority, and payment terms are executed; free calls and broker appointments without billable scope do not count. At $1,800 campaign cash cost and 36 owner hours valued internally at $100, fully loaded acquisition cost is $5,400 divided by four, or $1,350 per mandate. Four $4,000 diagnostics produce $16,000 revenue before delivery costs, not annual recurring revenue. Record both first-cash date and sales-cycle duration, because signed work with delayed collections can exhaust a small operator's working capital.

Support outbound with narrow inbound assets that reveal buying intent without collecting health records. Publish a Form 5500 renewal-date verification worksheet, a PBM rebate-definition checklist, and an E-Mod evidence-request guide. Each should offer a concrete diagnostic deliverable and identify the required source documents. Use a simple website, a scheduling tool, and HubSpot or a controlled spreadsheet for account tracking; no stack purchase compensates for an unclear offer. Host a twenty-minute session with a payroll firm on reconciling job duties to audit records, then invite employers to request a scoped review. Ten attendees requesting three follow-ups and one paid diagnostic provide a measurable local experiment. Keep referral economics separate from education and check state restrictions before sharing insurance-related compensation. Weeks one and two establish licenses, sources, assets, and the first account cohort; weeks three through six test messaging and deliver initial diagnostics; weeks seven through twelve convert evidence into paid mandates; weeks thirteen through twenty repeat only productive channels. The objective is three to ten signed wins within two to five months, with narrower scopes for the first engagements. If delivery capacity is constrained, delay new start dates openly instead of selling a savings implementation the team cannot support.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build and date the evidence queue
   - Load the first 300 employer records and deduplicate by employer identity.
   - Attach Form 5500 or EDGAR evidence, verify public-source dates, and assign a specific sourcing play.
2. Score before sequencing
   - Apply the six-part qualification score and separate immediate work from nurture.
   - Confirm licensing scope and contact suppression before releasing a human-reviewed message.
3. Sell a bounded evidence deliverable
   - Use the fourteen-day cadence to book discovery and propose a defined diagnostic.
   - Specify required records, access controls, price, completion date, and the employer's decision after delivery.
4. Convert and inspect the cohort
   - Execute paid mandates and review stage counts, delivery cost, and collections weekly.
   - Keep paid diagnostics, recurring advisory revenue, commissions, and contingent collections in separate ledger categories.

## 4. Resolve objections, protect the mandate, and reconcile retained revenue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Handle incumbent resistance by separating evidence collection from replacement authority. When the employer says the broker already performs this work, ask for the relevant deliverable and compare scope, timing, and compensation without attacking the incumbent. Offer a joint review or withdraw duplicated work. When the objection is disruption, propose a renewal inventory or retrospective reconciliation that leaves coverage unchanged. When the employer demands guaranteed savings, define a paid diagnostic with explicit assumptions and a decision threshold rather than promising a result before seeing contracts. For 'send your best rate,' explain that premiums alone omit attachment points, lasers, exclusions, contract basis, and run-out coverage. Stop-loss pricing can look attractive while transferring materially more risk to the employer. Reference-based pricing requires specific consideration of provider access, balance-bill support, and the contractual defense arrangement; it is not simply a lower reimbursement percentage. If the buyer refuses necessary evidence, narrow the deliverable to what can be supported. Never request a broker-of-record change merely to obtain a conversation. Document the employer's decision criteria before preparing alternatives so the proposal answers a real buying question. A preserved relationship with a useful future renewal date can be worth more than an unsupported immediate sale.

Make the first mandate small enough to deliver well and specific enough to enforce economically. Define the employer entities, covered period, records required, analytical method, exclusions, deliverables, fee basis, termination terms, and correction process. For recovery work, distinguish a modeled error, a carrier-approved adjustment, a posted credit, and cash actually received; define which event earns the fee and how reversals are handled. Do not let the operator receive premiums or claim funds merely because the engagement involves insurance. For covered group health brokerage or consulting arrangements reasonably expecting at least $1,000 in direct or indirect compensation, apply ERISA §408(b)(2)(B) disclosure requirements, including services, applicable fiduciary statements, compensation sources, and payment manner. Deliver the disclosures reasonably before entering, extending, or renewing the arrangement, subject to the statute's specific rules. Verify state requirements independently; an ERISA plan does not erase producer or TPA obligations. A $4,000 diagnostic with $1,200 contractor cost and eight owner hours budgeted at $100 has $2,000 contribution after those delivery inputs, before acquisition and overhead. If the fully loaded acquisition allocation is $1,350, only $650 remains on that initial engagement. Expand recurring work only when the client receives a distinct ongoing service.

Reconcile the acquisition plan to the supplied Formula v2.0 scorecard instead of inflating a small pipeline into an annual result. Conservative revenue of $120,000 less $81,600 total cost produces $38,400 owner net profit at 32%; Base revenue of $535,000 less $294,250 produces $240,750 at 45.0%; Scaled revenue of $950,000 less $399,000 produces $551,000 at 58%. Conservative and Base use one full-time operator plus three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time people plus four contractors. The scorecard's gross-revenue label refers here to retained operator fees and commissions, not underlying employer spending. Apply the ASC 606 principal-versus-agent assessment to each arrangement; do not mechanically net every subcontractor payment. Where the operator acts as agent and retains 70% of $100,000 fee billings, recognized retained revenue is $70,000, with the other $30,000 excluded from that revenue basis. Do not subtract the $30,000 again as an operating cost. Employer premiums, claim funds, and recovered principal remain outside operator revenue. The startup range is $8,000–$45,000 and monthly operating range is $2,000–$11,000; neither replaces the annual scenarios' all-in costs. Track actual delivery labor, acquisition expense, insurance, systems, and collection delays before increasing outbound volume.
