## 1. Build the intake queue around provable buying triggers  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an employer identity, an authorized contact, and a renewal window, not a speculative savings percentage. The public_record_api_intelligence play uses DOL EFAST2 Form 5500 filings, available Schedule A insurance information, and applicable Schedule C service-provider disclosures to identify benefit-plan changes worth investigating. Schedule availability depends on filing rules; an absent schedule does not establish missing compensation or misconduct. Normalize employer EIN, plan number, plan year, filing amendment, and source retrieval date before deduplicating. County dockets can expose an employer restructuring trigger, while state unclaimed-property records can support an expressly authorized recovery inquiry; neither creates a right to claim another party's money. Probate records usually add no value to this employer mandate and stay outside the queue. Across the illustrative cohort, 420 raw records lose 105 duplicates or unsuitable employers, leaving 315 usable targets. At six analyst minutes each, review consumes 31.5 hours; at $60 per hour, the labor allowance is $1,890. Illustrative cached retrieval expense of $0.03 per record adds $12.60. When interfaces fail, switch to scheduled bulk FOIA/public-record exports, official data feeds, and low-frequency cached queues rather than bypassing access restrictions.

Treat the volume's 11 mapped plays as eleven operational workstreams here: public-record intelligence, renewal timing, compensation discovery, self-funded feasibility, captive comparison, stop-loss placement, PBM repricing, rebate recovery, payroll classification review, experience-modification correction, and contingent refund collection. This is a coverage checklist, not eleven separate revenue streams. One employer can traverse several workstreams while paying one retained advisory fee. Request a signed engagement, current plan documents, census definitions, carrier authorizations, loss runs, payroll audits, PBM contracts, and a named employer decision-maker. Separate covered employees from covered members because a dependent count error can double a PEPM invoice. Under ERISA section 408(b)(2), including the group-health-plan brokerage and consulting provisions added by CAA 2021, applicable compensation disclosures must identify direct and indirect compensation before the arrangement begins. Florida Statutes section 626.112 illustrates producer licensing requirements; map actual activities and states before solicitation. Use licensed Life and Health and Property and Casualty producers for their respective lines. Pure analysis does not authorize insurance solicitation, and producer status does not automatically authorize TPA functions. Record every limitation in the engagement and route unsupported activities to an appropriately licensed partner under a disclosed agreement.

Assign the founder relationship ownership and final commercial review; use three contractors for actuarial or stop-loss review, PBM analytics, and workers' compensation auditing. Require written scopes, delivery dates, and access boundaries instead of giving every contractor the entire employer dataset. HIPAA business-associate obligations turn on the parties' roles and information handled. Execute applicable agreements under 45 CFR 164.504(e), document permitted uses, and implement minimum-necessary access under 45 CFR 164.514(d). Store clinical files separately from the prospect CRM and fee ledger; those systems need employer identifiers and evidence references, not diagnoses or prescriptions. A tokenized member identifier alone does not establish HIPAA de-identification. The reference intake score gives up to fifty points for covered lives and fifty for a sixty-to-one-hundred-eighty-day renewal window; it prioritizes work rather than estimating savings. An analyst must verify renewal timing against the current contract because Form 5500 filings lag operating reality. Reject prospects without employer authority, required records, or a feasible renewal timetable. Preserve rejection reasons and source hashes so the next campaign does not repeatedly purchase or review the same dead lead.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Win the evidence contest before the incumbent controls it**
> 
> Make the employer's source documents and executed contracts the authority for every proposal, invoice, and recovery.
> - Ask for the current stop-loss renewal and expiring contract together; a cheaper quote can hide a new laser, a changed contract basis, or a shortened terminal-liability period.
> - Reconcile PBM reversals and rebills before comparing ingredient costs; otherwise a single prescription can inflate both the baseline and the advertised recovery.
> - Order valuation-dated loss runs before challenging an E-Mod; a reserve reduction after the relevant valuation date may affect a later rating period rather than the current one.
> - Give the employer a compensation inventory early, including override and referral arrangements, so an incumbent cannot turn a late disclosure into a renewal derailment.

## 2. Implement an atomic evidence-to-ledger production line  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Deploy the six reference modules below into one controlled working directory. Save the SQL cells as schema.sql and reconciliation.sql, and the Python cells as intake.py, workflow.py, settlement.py, and qa.py. Initialize a local SQLite database with both SQL files before accepting work. Use Python 3.9 or later, integer cents, a dedicated operator fee account, and a single authoritative database on local storage; do not place a WAL database on a shared network filesystem. Every writer opens BEGIN IMMEDIATE before reading idempotency or state, so competing workers serialize their check-and-write sequence. Unique request keys prevent repeated commands, natural business keys block duplicate cases, and unique bank references block the same receipt under a different request key. A reused key with changed content raises an error instead of silently accepting altered instructions. The thirty-second lock timeout can reject a busy request; retry the identical durable key after rollback. This removes check-then-insert races within the supported local-database workflow, not every possible infrastructure failure. No module initiates a bank transfer or promises exactly-once behavior from an external payment service. Settlement records only already-observed bank movements.

The verification and escrow state machine is deliberately noncustodial: RECEIVED moves to VERIFIED, then HELD, EARNED, and SETTLED. HELD means the fee entitlement remains on a contractual release checklist; it does not mean this operator holds employer claims money in escrow. Verification requires document references for licensing, disclosures, and source reconciliation. Earning requires completed-service, authorized-fee, and release evidence reviewed by the accountable person. The program checks references and ordering, not the truth of the underlying documents. At EARNED, one balanced posting debits receivables and credits retained operator revenue. At SETTLED, a separately evidenced bank receipt debits cash and credits receivables. Fee candidates and refund estimates never enter revenue merely because intake succeeded. Reject unsupported cases before earning. This compact implementation accepts one exact receipt per earned case and rejects partial payments, overpayments, reversals, mixed remittances, and changed terms. Route those exceptions to a controlled accounting process rather than editing immutable rows. Expand the schema with reviewed allocation and reversing-entry journals before automating those cases. Keep original documents in a permissioned evidence store with checksums; the ledger carries references rather than sensitive source content.

Run intake with a durable request key and a JSON file containing source, external_id, covered_lives, renewal_days, case_id, lane, period, amount_cents, and evidence_ref. The source identity must remain stable across monthly fees; changed source facts require a reviewed revision process rather than a second employer identity. Invoke workflow with database, request key, case identifier, action, and evidence-file arguments. Settlement takes database, request key, and its documented JSON payload; fund and expense use a null case identifier. Apply a restrictive file-access policy because direct database writers could circumvent application transition rules. Keep backups through SQLite's backup API, test restoration, and retain append-only command evidence. The reconciliation view separates contract amounts, earned fees, receipts, and open receivables; summing contracts is not a revenue report. QA checks database integrity, foreign keys, balanced accounts, state consistency, and cash postings against an independently verified statement balance. A matching imported total alone cannot detect a transaction omitted from both imports and postings. Run a separate statement-line completeness review. These reference modules are supplied source, not an executed deployment or a substitute for adversarial concurrency and restore testing in the operator's environment.

## 3. Convert analytical differences into defensible earned fees  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

For self-funded transitions, build a monthly exposure model from paid and incurred claims, enrollment movements, large claimant detail obtained through authorized channels, and the employer's risk budget. Compare specific and aggregate stop-loss attachment points, aggregate accommodation, contract basis, exclusions, lasers, and terminal liability before comparing premiums. A 12/12 contract and a 12/15 contract do not provide interchangeable payment windows; read the actual policy definitions rather than relying on shorthand. Model an illustrative $250,000 specific attachment separately from a $500,000 claimant laser: the additional $250,000 remains employer exposure for that claimant if the contract so provides. Do not treat that retained risk as operator revenue or available cash. A captive proposal needs the same discipline plus collateral, assessment, exit, and claims-administration terms. Reference-based pricing requires a documented balance-bill defense arrangement and coordinated plan language, not an assumed universal provider acceptance rate. If underwriting deteriorates before binding, reprice, retain the incumbent arrangement, or postpone the transition. The fee ledger should recognize only the operator's completed advisory work and earned placement compensation under the applicable contracts, not projected employer savings.

For PBM work, normalize National Drug Codes, quantities, days supplied, fill dates, pharmacy channels, reversals, and contract definitions before comparing ingredient costs. CMS NADAC provides a useful acquisition-cost benchmark for covered retail observations, but it is not a universal payable price and does not resolve specialty, limited-distribution, dispensing-fee, or rebate economics by itself. An illustrative matched set of 10,000 fills with a $7 ingredient-cost gap creates a $70,000 investigation hypothesis, not an immediately collectible debt. Reconcile exclusions, fees, contract guarantees, rebate timing, and actual payments before alleging an underpayment. A negotiated 20% recovery fee on an accepted $40,000 rebate shortfall produces $8,000 of operator entitlement only when the engagement's earning conditions are satisfied; the employer's remaining $32,000 is not operator revenue. Review gag-clause restrictions under 29 U.S.C. 1185m when contracts impede permitted access to cost or quality information. If claim-level access remains blocked, pursue authorized contractual access and reconcile aggregate remittance evidence; do not fabricate precision from incomplete files. Keep any PBM recovery outside the cohort arithmetic below unless a distinct executed arrangement and receipt support adding that revenue.

For workers' compensation, reconcile audited payroll, classifications, experience periods, claim status, and valuation dates against the governing state's approved rating rules. NCCI methodology is not universal; independent rating bureaus require their own worksheets and dispute routes. A simple diagnostic uses $200,000 of illustrative premium before the experience factor: changing a supported modifier from 1.15 to 1.05 suggests a $20,000 difference before other rating elements. The carrier's corrected calculation, not that shortcut, establishes the actual adjustment. At a 25% contractual split, a collected $20,000 refund produces a $5,000 operator fee and $15,000 of employer benefit. Route the refund directly from carrier to employer wherever the arrangement allows, then invoice the earned fee. Check state producer, administrator, and fee-sharing requirements before signing a contingent arrangement. A class code challenge can also increase premium, so explain the complete corrected result rather than cherry-picking favorable categories. If the carrier disputes the worksheet or a correction window has closed, preserve the evidence, use the applicable review procedure, and leave the contested fee unearned. Separate future premium reductions from retroactive cash refunds throughout the proposal and ledger.
