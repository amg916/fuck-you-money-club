## 1. Sell documented contract correction, not ownership of employer claim dollars  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Self-funded health, PBM, and workers' compensation arbitrage is a licensed advisory and brokerage business that earns money by correcting contract opacity, procurement friction, and rating errors. The operator does not buy an employer's medical liabilities cheaply and resell them. An employer sponsors its health plan, a third-party administrator processes claims, a pharmacy benefit manager administers prescriptions, and a stop-loss insurer reimburses covered losses above negotiated attachment points. Separately, workers' compensation insurers price occupational injury coverage using payroll classifications, experience-rating inputs, and jurisdiction-specific rules. The operator earns disclosed advisory fees, placement compensation, or a contracted share of verified recoveries. A broker-of-record appointment changes representation under carrier rules; it neither transfers plan assets nor automatically creates commission rights. Candidate #40's edge is coordinating specialists around evidence that employers rarely assemble themselves. Its 93/100 score comprises revenue potential 25, profit-margin potential 24, team efficiency 19, startup accessibility 13, and automation potential 12. Those scores describe the opportunity's operating shape, not a client's expected savings. The practical product is a defensible decision package with executable contracts, identified counterparties, a measurement baseline, and clearly allocated responsibility for each implementation task and subsequent service failure.

The recognition boundary must survive a bank reconciliation. A client with 500 billed employees at $55 per employee per month produces $330,000 of annual advisory consideration when all twelve service months are delivered. Its several million dollars of claims and insurance premiums are neither operator sales nor a revenue multiple denominator. Count stop-loss or TPA commissions only under actual appointment, licensing, service, and carrier compensation terms. A $200,000 workers' compensation refund with a contracted 30% recovery fee creates $60,000 of potential operator consideration, not $200,000 of sales. Under ASC 606, assess each promised service and whether the operator controls it before transfer; principal-versus-agent presentation is not an elective shortcut. This scorecard uses net retained operator consideration, excluding amounts collected for counterparties. An operator acting as principal must separately reconcile its reporting presentation to that management measure. Recurring service fees generally follow service delivery; commissions follow the identified performance obligation and applicable reversal assessment. Recovery fees remain constrained while entitlement or amount could materially reverse. Cash receipt, a favorable worksheet, and a carrier promise are different events. Maintain separate schedules for earned consideration, receivables, deferred collections, partner allocations, and potential chargebacks.

Five archetypes keep the small team focused. The renewal advisor sources employers and evaluates bundled versus separately contracted arrangements. The pharmacy specialist reconstructs ingredient pricing, dispensing charges, rebates, and exclusions. The stop-loss specialist translates claim volatility into specific and aggregate protection. The experience-rating specialist traces payroll and losses into bureau calculations and carrier corrections. The purchasing specialist aggregates eligible indirect spend without pretending that every member qualifies for every supplier bracket. This chapter maps 11 plays across those archetypes; the comparison matrix identifies the two specified catalog plays and nine concrete operating extensions rather than inventing additional catalog IDs. Startup capital of $8,000–$45,000 covers licensing, errors and omissions coverage, secure systems, specialist contracting, and acquisition work. The $2,000–$11,000 monthly opex range is a planning envelope for recurring operating overhead, not an all-inclusive expense cap at every scale. Contract labor, acquisition costs, variable servicing, and other modeled expenses must also be reconciled. A first deal in two to five months depends on renewal timing and usable employer records. Begin with one repeatable archetype and named specialist coverage for the others; trying to administer claims, place insurance, litigate balance bills, and audit every payroll code personally destroys the manpower advantage.

## 2. Map eleven plays to measurable units and explicit fallback routes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The catalog play erisa_level_funded_transparent_pbm_bor_arbitrage begins with public Form 5500 information, not a purchased list of supposedly distressed employers. Use Schedule A insurance information and Schedule C service-provider information only where filed, relevant, and informative; filing exemptions and welfare-plan reporting rules leave real gaps. Request an employer-authorized CAA 2021 compensation review before recommending a broker-of-record change. At 200 contracted employees, $40–$65 PEPM supports $96,000–$156,000 annually for a genuinely specified advisory scope. Those fees are negotiated targets, not a statutory entitlement or universal market price. Compare the incumbent bundle against separate TPA, stop-loss, network, and PBM contracts, including implementation charges and replacement broker compensation. A level-funded arrangement commonly combines fixed payments with a self-funded claim component; its label alone does not resolve legal or economic classification. Suitable captive participation requires additional examination of collateral, assessments, withdrawal terms, and member risk. Retain or competitively rebid the incumbent structure if risk-adjusted economics deteriorate. Until contract conditions are satisfied, limit work to disclosed advisory services rather than promising a transition date.

Transparent pharmacy procurement requires a written pricing formula. CMS NADAC is a survey-based acquisition-cost benchmark for covered outpatient drugs, not a universal transaction price or a complete specialty formulary. Define NDC matching, quantity units, benchmark publication date, update frequency, dispensing fees, ingredient adjustments, and treatment of reversals. Explicitly price drugs without a usable NADAC record instead of silently substituting an optimistic benchmark. For 100,000 comparable fills, reducing net cost by an illustrated $3 per fill yields $300,000 of employer savings before transition charges and other changes; it does not create $300,000 of operator revenue. Separate manufacturer rebates, PBM guarantees, administrative credits, and pharmacy reimbursements so the same dollar cannot appear twice. Compare formulary changes and specialty access as well as price. A rebate recovery requires contractual entitlement, paid-claim eligibility, settlement periods, and audit access; an advertised pass-through percentage proves none of these. The adjacent workers' compensation plays are similarly evidence-bound. If a simplified premium component of $400,000 changes from a 1.20 modifier to 1.10, the indicated difference is $40,000 before other rating adjustments. Only carrier endorsements and billing reconciliation establish the actual refund. Hold contingent invoices when bureau acceptance, policy application, or recovery remains disputed.

The second catalog play, private_b2b_gpo_umbrella_rebate_arbitrage, is an adjacent employer-distribution opportunity rather than health-plan claim revenue. Pool 10,000–75,000+ eligible seats and indirect spend across hundreds of SMB members through authorized Una, OMNIA, or private vertical purchasing agreements. Verify whether the particular supplier and agreement accept private employers; public purchasing contracts do not automatically extend to them. Use authorized contract information and Vendr or Tropic procurement methods to challenge renewal price floors, without claiming access to proprietary benchmark feeds. A target 3%–12% supplier administration rebate on $1,000,000 of eligible settled spend would produce $30,000–$120,000 before member allocations, retention terms, and reversals. A separate 25%–35% Year-1 fee on $100,000 of verified incremental savings would produce $25,000–$35,000. These are conditional negotiation illustrations, not standard platform offers or additions to the canonical scorecard. Disclose dual compensation, agree whether supplier rebates reduce the savings-fee base, and separate price reductions from volume changes. If eligibility or the baseline fails, suspend disputed fees, reconcile with members and suppliers, refund unearned amounts, and move affected members to eligible agreements. ## 4. Build the moat around permissioned evidence, contractual authority, and recoverable errors  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Authority is activity-specific. Maintain active state Life & Health and Property & Casualty producer licenses for the insurance work performed, with required agency licenses and carrier appointments. TPA licensing is a separate inquiry when an operator administers coverage or claims; a producer license does not settle it. ERISA preemption does not erase state insurance requirements, and 29 U.S.C. §1144 preserves important insurance-law boundaries. Texas Insurance Code Chapter 4151 illustrates a distinct administrator regime. Under 29 U.S.C. §1108(b)(2)(B), covered group-health brokerage and consulting arrangements meeting the statutory compensation threshold require specified disclosures about services, fiduciary status where applicable, and direct and indirect compensation. Deliver the required information reasonably before entering, extending, or renewing the arrangement, and manage subsequent changes under the applicable timing rules. The employer's fiduciary process under 29 U.S.C. §1104 must evaluate reasonable compensation and prudent selection. A disclosure does not cure every conflict or turn unnecessary services into reasonable expenses. Avoid receiving employer claim funds merely to create a larger revenue narrative. Contracts should specify who holds funds, who pays claims, who owns records, and how the employer retrieves usable information after termination without disrupting member coverage or insurer reimbursement.

Claims access creates a security and workflow obligation. Where the operator acts as a business associate, execute appropriate agreements under 45 CFR §164.504(e), apply the minimum-necessary standard where required by §164.502(b), and implement the applicable Security Rule controls. Use role-based access, multifactor authentication, encryption, auditable exports, and a documented retention schedule rather than ordinary email attachments containing member-level claims. Separate public Form 5500 prospecting records from restricted claim and eligibility records. Automate public renewal targeting with filing-date checks and source attribution, NADAC repricing with versioned benchmark files and exception reports, and NCCI or payroll analysis with employer-authorized records and jurisdiction-specific rules. These automations recommend questions; they do not decide licensure, carrier underwriting, bureau corrections, or disputed contractual entitlement. Require an analyst to trace sample results back to original records before proposals leave the firm. Workers' compensation claim disclosures have distinct HIPAA treatment under §164.512(l); do not assume that exception makes all employer medical information freely shareable. The moat is a reproducible evidence chain that a new specialist can inspect, not an undocumented spreadsheet controlled by one founder. Preserve contract versions and dated approvals alongside each calculation so later audits can reproduce the decision.

Transition risk deserves the same attention as quoted savings. Specific stop-loss protects against individual claim severity, while aggregate protection addresses covered claim totals under its own definitions. A laser can impose a higher individual attachment or exclusion; a lower premium may therefore purchase materially less protection. Model incurred and paid windows, run-in exposure, run-out administration, reimbursement lag, and termination obligations across both old and new contracts. Reference-based pricing introduces provider collection and member balance-bill exposure; identify who supplies defense, funds settlements, and coordinates disputes. Federal surprise-billing protections, including 42 U.S.C. §300gg-111, cover defined circumstances rather than every out-of-network bill. Likewise, a corrected workers' compensation worksheet does not automatically reopen every historical policy. Check bureau rules, carrier endorsement procedures, and applicable dispute periods before offering a contingent engagement. Incumbent brokers will defend renewals with revised pricing, service promises, and commission changes; compare the revised written offer rather than treating retention as a lost sale. A disciplined operator can earn disclosed advisory income while recommending no change. Finish each engagement with a decision record, accepted implementation responsibilities, a billing reconciliation schedule, and an exit plan. These artifacts convert one-time analysis into defensible recurring service without requiring a large internal workforce.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish employer authority and a clean intake boundary
   - Secure the engagement, relevant authorizations, compensation disclosures, and required data agreements.
   - Record plan identifiers, actual renewal dates, carrier contacts, applicable states, permitted datasets, and who can approve contracts; public filings alone do not authorize claims access.
2. Normalize incumbent economics before requesting alternatives
   - Reconcile enrollment, fees, premiums, pharmacy settlements, payroll, and loss records to source documents.
   - Version NADAC inputs, identify missing pricing rules, separate claim funding from service costs, and reproduce experience-rating inputs under the applicable bureau methodology.
3. Compare executable contracts and preserve the incumbent fallback
   - Obtain written proposals and score total cost, member effects, risk transfer, and implementation responsibilities.
   - Resolve lasers, attachment points, incurred/paid periods, run-out needs, rebate rights, and balance-bill defense before recommending a change; retain or rebid the incumbent arrangement when the comparison fails.
4. Recognize earned consideration and monitor reversals
   - Invoice delivered advisory work, reconcile commission statements, and validate recovery-fee entitlement.
   - Maintain separate earned-revenue and cash schedules, document variable-consideration judgments, track carrier chargebacks, and credit employer invoices when enrollment or recovery amounts change.
