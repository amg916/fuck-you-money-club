## 1. Assign each service to its federal authority and state license boundary  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with the employer, plan type, employee locations, and the actual service being sold, not the label benefits consultant. ERISA §408(b)(2)(B), codified at 29 U.S.C. §1108(b)(2)(B), requires covered group health brokerage and consulting providers expecting at least $1,000 in direct or indirect compensation to disclose specified services, compensation, and relationships to the responsible plan fiduciary. Deliver the disclosure reasonably before the contract, extension, or renewal; identify PEPM charges, carrier commissions, overrides, referral payments, and contingent compensation with their payers and calculation methods. A broker-of-record letter is neither that disclosure nor a services agreement. Record the statutory deadlines for changes and corrections rather than assuming annual delivery is sufficient. ERISA §404 requires fiduciaries to evaluate prudence and loyalty; a contract cannot erase fiduciary status created by discretionary functions. Distinguish the employer's business decision to change benefits from fiduciary administration of the adopted plan. Governmental and qualifying church plans are generally outside ERISA Title I, so route those prospects through their applicable public-sector or other governing framework. One $2,000 monthly engagement already exceeds the disclosure threshold; dividing its invoices among affiliates does not justify omitting indirect compensation.

Hold active resident and necessary nonresident Life and Health and Property and Casualty producer licenses for the work performed; confirm each state's exact line names, business-entity requirements, designated responsible producer, and carrier appointments. California Insurance Code §§1621 and 1626 distinguish relevant producer capacities, while New York Insurance Law §2102 prohibits unlicensed insurance producer activity. Texas Insurance Code Chapter 4151 separately governs administrators, subject to its definitions and exemptions. Collecting premium, administering benefits, adjudicating claims, or representing an insured in a coverage settlement can cross additional TPA or adjuster boundaries. A payroll classification review does not automatically authorize negotiating an insurance claim. Stop-loss is insurance even when the employer health plan is self-funded; verify the state's applicable producer authority and stop-loss restrictions before quoting. ERISA preemption is not a universal exemption for insurers, MEWAs, or every PBM practice. NCCI rules also cannot be copied unchanged into California's WCIRB system, independent rating bureau states, or monopolistic state-fund arrangements. For a three-state employer, maintain three completed authority records before solicitation requires those permissions. If a state requires authority the firm lacks, engage a properly licensed specialist under a permitted compensation arrangement or exclude that activity expressly from scope.

Treat claims access as a separate workstream from prospecting. A public Form 5500 does not authorize receipt of identifiable medical or pharmacy claims. Determine whether the advisory firm is a business associate, execute the required business associate agreement, and apply 45 CFR §§164.502(e), 164.504(e), and 164.514(d) to permitted uses and minimum necessary access where applicable. Plan-sponsor access also requires attention to §164.504(f), including plan documents and separation from employment functions. Begin feasibility work with enrollment totals and appropriately de-identified summaries; removing names alone does not establish HIPAA de-identification. For workers' compensation, §164.512(l) permits specified disclosures authorized by applicable law, not unrestricted reuse for benefits sales. CAA gag-clause restrictions under 29 U.S.C. §1185m support specified price and quality information access, but do not override privacy safeguards. Request field definitions, permitted recipient terms, and a secure transfer path before requesting records. Use role-based SharePoint access or an appropriately configured secure portal with suitable contractual coverage, not ordinary outreach inboxes. At 800 covered employees, an 800-row eligibility file may answer a pricing question that would otherwise expose 80,000 claim lines. Escalate missing authorization to the plan administrator and pause identifiable data transfer until the necessary permissions are documented.

## 2. Write contracts around fee evidence, coverage continuity, and usable data  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Make the master services agreement describe deliverables, exclusions, named decision-makers, and evidence required for acceptance. A benefits advisory schedule can say, 'Monthly advisory fees equal eligible enrolled employees multiplied by the stated PEPM rate; the parties reconcile additions and terminations against the administrator's agreed enrollment report.' Specify whether covered lives, employees, or contracts are counted. For refunds, use, 'The recovery fee becomes payable only after the employer receives an attributable cash refund or an agreed usable premium credit, supported by the carrier's written adjustment.' Exclude projected savings, duplicate recoveries, unrelated audit credits, and carrier reversals. On a $120,000 collected recovery with a 25% fee, the operator earns $30,000; the other $90,000 remains employer value. A documented dispute over $20,000 should not silently produce a fee on the original estimate. Define whether the undisputed portion can be invoiced and how later reversals are refunded. Use narrowly defined anti-circumvention language covering identified introductions or documented findings for a reasonable period, not perpetual ownership of the employer relationship. State that producer compensation follows applicable licensing and commission-sharing rules. If the employer rejects contingency terms, offer a fixed-scope audit with an agreed price and no implied authority to bind coverage.

Attach a stop-loss comparison that shows specific and aggregate attachment points, eligible expense definitions, policy basis, reimbursement timing, disclosure obligations, and run-in or run-out treatment. A 12/12 policy and a 12/15 policy can produce different recoveries for the same service date because payment timing matters; confirm the carrier's definitions rather than relying on shorthand. With a $100,000 specific attachment and $180,000 of otherwise eligible paid claims for one person, the preliminary specific excess is $80,000. A $250,000 laser for that person can reduce that reimbursement to zero. Aggregate protection is calculated under its own terms and cannot be assumed to fill the difference. Require written laser information, terminal liability options, incurred-but-unpaid claim estimates, and transition responsibilities before an employer changes administrators or funding arrangements. Separate any advance-funding accommodation from the carrier's ultimate reimbursement obligation. For reference-based pricing, specify the pricing methodology, provider negotiation process, member communications, balance-bill defense provider, exclusions, and funding of settlements. The No Surprises Act, including 42 U.S.C. §300gg-111, protects specified situations rather than every out-of-network encounter. If defense or transition coverage is unresolved, compare a conventional network alternative and postpone the proposed transition instead of presenting an incomplete savings estimate.

Give the PBM schedule operational definitions for rebates, administrative fees, price concessions, spread, affiliated pharmacy payments, specialty classifications, and settlement timing. State which amounts pass through, what evidence supports reconciliation, and whether audit rights survive termination. NADAC is a useful acquisition-cost benchmark, not a complete employer net-cost measure; add dispensing fees, guarantees, exclusions, rebates, and claims eligibility before comparing alternatives. Require an accessible export specification rather than accepting a dashboard as the sole record. The employer or plan retains its contractual rights in supplied records; the operator retains pre-existing analytical code, subject to confidentiality and tightly bounded data-use permissions. Ownership language does not create a right to sell health information. Negotiate a liability cap proportionate to fees, insurance, and risk, with separately negotiated treatment of confidentiality breaches, fraud, and other excluded liabilities. Do not assume a cap can excuse ERISA fiduciary responsibility; 29 U.S.C. §1110 restricts exculpatory provisions. Specify notice, cure, mediation or litigation venue, collection of undisputed fees, and emergency remedies for data misuse. Require transition exports, retention periods, subcontractor controls, and incident reporting on termination. If a vendor refuses claim-level verification, price the engagement as a limited review and exclude unverifiable recovery promises from compensation calculations.

A captive assessment must include collateral, assessments, exit restrictions, and governing documents, not just a premium comparison. Remove open and click tracking, authenticate sender domains, and honor suppression across all accounts. Catch-all SMTP checks cannot verify every recipient and can encounter provider restrictions. ## 4. Use exact outreach scripts, qualify authority, and preserve the audit trail  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use an identifiable commercial email with accurate routing information and a truthful subject, then satisfy 15 U.S.C. §7704 requirements for advertising identification where required, a valid physical postal address, and a functioning opt-out. Populate the actual sender identity, employer, observed filing year, and address before sending; never invent those facts to complete a template. The following body is ready to use once the signature supplies those verified particulars: 'This is a commercial introduction about employer benefits advisory services. We review self-funded plan costs, PBM contract economics, and workers' compensation rating records. A public filing can help us identify a potential renewal conversation, but it does not establish your current funding arrangement or savings opportunity. Would a twenty-minute discussion with the person responsible for benefits purchasing be useful? We do not need employee medical information for that discussion. Reply stop to end our commercial outreach.' Honor opt-outs within ten business days and propagate suppression across every sender and vendor; retain the minimum suppression record needed to avoid recontact. Do not make suppression conditional on a fee or additional personal information. Public contact information and successful mailbox validation do not themselves establish permission or override service-provider restrictions.

For a manually dialed business discovery call, first check applicable federal and state calling restrictions, internal suppression, number type, and any recording requirements. Do not treat B2B labeling as a blanket exemption for automated calls, prerecorded voices, texts, or state rules. Use this opening: 'I am calling about your employer benefits purchasing process. Is this a suitable time for two questions, or should I end the call?' If invited, continue: 'Who controls the benefits services agreement, and when does the next renewal decision occur? Is the medical plan insured or self-funded? Which states have employees, and which administrator and PBM currently serve the plan? Please do not share diagnoses, names of high-cost claimants, or employee identifiers.' Then qualify the workers' compensation work: 'Which carrier and rating bureau issued the policy and experience worksheet? Has the carrier already adjusted the payroll audit or returned any premium for the periods you want reviewed?' Close with: 'The next step is an authorized document checklist and a written scope, not a carrier change. Our compensation sources will be disclosed before engagement.' If the recipient declines, record the preference immediately and end the sales discussion rather than moving the same person into another inbox sequence.

When the employer asks for pricing, say: 'Our proposal will separate monthly advisory fees, insurance commissions, and any recovery fee. Employer premiums and claim reserves are not our revenue. A contingency fee applies only to the recovery defined in the agreement, with an adjustment process for reversals. We will show the calculation before you sign.' When an incumbent requests patient files during the introduction, respond: 'We are not transferring identifiable claims through this email thread. The plan administrator must establish the authorized purpose, recipient, and secure delivery process first.' Preserve these exchanges alongside the proposal, compensation disclosure, signed scope, license checks, and delivery receipts. A practical sequence is public-source qualification, permissioned discovery, authorized limited review, and contracted execution; each stage has its own evidence requirements. For a 40-account pilot, track delivered messages, positive replies, qualified meetings, signed engagements, and collected fees separately. Four meetings do not equal four clients, and a signed contingency contract does not equal earned revenue. Review complaints, bounces, opt-outs, and licensing exceptions weekly. If any outsourced sender cannot demonstrate shared suppression and truthful identity, stop that vendor's campaign while preserving the records needed to investigate and prevent repeat contact.
