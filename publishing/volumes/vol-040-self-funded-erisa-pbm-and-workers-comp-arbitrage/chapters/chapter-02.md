## 1. Identify the economic buyer, the authorized payer and the loss they cannot ignore  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Sell first to the person accountable for the expense, then identify the person legally authorized to release money. At a mid-market employer, the CFO usually owns the health-benefit and workers' compensation budget; human resources owns employee disruption; treasury controls ACH enrollment; and the plan committee may control decisions involving plan assets. These roles overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A benefits director can sponsor an audit without authority to bind the company, direct a trust distribution, or replace a producer. Obtain the contracting entity's legal name, signatory authority, purchase-order requirements, invoice destination, and bank-verification procedure before accepting sensitive records. Ask whether fees will come from corporate operating cash or a plan account. ERISA fiduciary duties under 29 U.S.C. 1104 and prohibited-transaction rules under 29 U.S.C. 1106 make that distinction operationally important. Employer business expenses cannot simply be routed through plan assets because cash is available there. The immediate buying trigger is usually an unbudgeted renewal increase, an opaque pharmacy reconciliation, a disputed premium audit, or a stop-loss exclusion. Translate each trigger into an approved expense category and a documented payment obligation, not a promise that savings will eventually finance everything.

Organize the 11 mapped plays into buying problems rather than presenting eleven unrelated services. Self-funded feasibility, captive participation, specific stop-loss design, aggregate protection, and TPA fee review address renewal volatility. PBM spread repricing and rebate recovery address opaque pharmacy economics. Reference-based pricing support addresses provider-payment execution. Form 5500 renewal targeting creates qualified opportunities rather than independent client revenue. Historical workers' compensation recovery and separately authorized broker-of-record renewal work address premium leakage and future placement. For a 500-employee account, an illustrative $12 PEPM advisory agreement produces $6,000 monthly and $72,000 annually, not the employer's multimillion-dollar claim spend. A $2 reduction across 20,000 comparable prescriptions suggests $40,000 of potential purchasing improvement, but it does not establish collected rebates or earned contingent fees. The CFO wants defensible cash impact; human resources wants continuity; procurement wants contractual accountability. Show all three a baseline, implementation cost, owner, and fallback. If a stop-loss laser removes the expected advantage, retain the existing structure while renegotiating specific terms. If pharmacy repricing lacks usable claim-level records, sell a bounded data-and-contract review instead of projecting recovery. One client can buy several workstreams, but overlapping work must not create duplicate charges or duplicate savings claims.

Screen willingness and ability to pay separately. An employer can be profitable yet refuse an invoice because procurement never onboarded the supplier; another can approve the work while lacking liquidity at settlement. Review public bankruptcy records through PACER, secretary-of-state entity status, available trade references, and financial statements supplied with permission. For larger contingent exposures, inspect current liquidity, debt restrictions, related-party dependencies, and whether a parent actually guarantees payment. An entity search does not establish solvency, and a UCC filing is a financing signal rather than proof of distress. Request a named accounts-payable contact and a sample remittance process before kickoff. Use a modest corporate-funded retainer for research-heavy engagements, shorter billing intervals for thinly capitalized employers, and a documented stop-work right for delinquent advisory invoices. Never suspend participant claims administration or hold health records hostage to collect a consulting fee. For commission income, screen the carrier or intermediary separately: confirm appointment, commission schedule, producer-of-record status, cancellation treatment, and statement timing. A solvent employer does not cure an uncollectible commission promise from an unauthorized introducer. Record the actual obligor for every revenue line so treasury can pursue the right counterparty.

## 2. Select the payment arrangement without taking custody of employer money  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat payment method, payment condition, and custody as separate design choices. Wire and ACH move money; retainers and milestone holds define when it becomes payable; escrow appoints a custodian; factoring changes who purchases a receivable. Statutory disbursement is a constrained distribution process, not a universal payment network. Platform checkout is an interface over card or bank rails. The comparison below therefore evaluates eight commercial arrangements without pretending they provide identical protections. Default to corporate ACH for recurring advisory invoices and independently verified wire instructions for unusually large settlements. ACH authorization must identify the account holder, amount or calculation method, timing, and revocation procedure. Confirm business-account rules and bank deadlines rather than importing consumer protections into a corporate contract. Neither a screenshot nor a pending bank entry proves final collection. Reconcile the bank posting, invoice, and remittance reference, and handle returns or reversals under the actual bank agreement. For wires, verify changed instructions through an established telephone number, never the number included in the change request. A compromised email can redirect an otherwise legitimate payment faster than a contract clause can recover it. Keep initiation and bank-detail approval with different authorized people.

Keep claim funding and premium payments outside the advisory operating account. Employers fund the TPA or insurer under their existing agreements; carriers pay recognized producer commissions under appointment terms; PBMs remit rebates to the contractually entitled recipient; and carriers return premium overpayments to the insured or other entitled payee. The operator then invoices its earned fee against the documented event. A direction-to-pay letter can simplify administration only when the payer accepts it and the arrangement respects ownership, licensing, and applicable asset restrictions. It does not automatically assign funds or make the carrier responsible for the employer's consulting debt. For a $100,000 accepted premium refund at a 35% contingency, the employer receives $100,000 and owes $35,000 under the engagement; the operator does not book $100,000 and later expense $65,000. If a permitted independent escrow handles both distributions, require written release conditions, verified beneficiary details, segregated records, and a dispute mechanism. Do not call an ordinary operating account escrow. Where a recovery belongs to a plan or includes participant interests, establish ownership and permissible expense allocation before distribution. Employer convenience alone cannot convert plan money into unrestricted corporate cash.

Build the recurring payment schedule around observable work and the contingent schedule around observable collection. A $6,000 monthly advisory fee can be billed in advance against a defined service period, with termination and any unused-fee treatment stated explicitly. That billing timing does not itself determine revenue recognition. A recovery fee should distinguish cash refunds, applied credits, disputed credits, and future renewal reductions. If a carrier applies a $40,000 credit against a payable premium installment and the contract treats realized credits as recovery, a 35% fee equals $14,000 once the credit is actually applied and evidenced. A proposed worksheet adjustment alone does not trigger that result. Use milestone holds for a contested portion while releasing undisputed compensation; define the reviewer, evidence deadline, and escalation path. Factoring should be a last-mile liquidity decision for an assignable, earned receivable, not a device for financing speculative savings. A 3% purchase discount on a $20,000 invoice costs $600 before other charges; recourse can leave collection risk with the operator. Platform checkout suits small corporate retainers, but processor fees, card disputes, and transaction limits can make it unsuitable for large contingent settlements.

2. Define the earned event and evidence

## 3. Protect the fee with evidence, scoped authority and defensible recovery math  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The play workers_comp_ncci_emod_retroactive_recovery begins with a signed historical-audit scope, not a blanket claim that every high experience modifier is wrong. Audit three to four historical policy years only where carrier, state, and rating-bureau correction rules permit. Compare actual job duties with applicable classifications, reconcile payroll to audited exposures, test eligible overtime adjustments, and check officer exclusions or limits against the governing state rules and policy elections. Reconcile NCCI experience-rating worksheets, or the applicable independent bureau's equivalent, to accurate carrier loss reports and valuation dates. An open reserve is not an error merely because the ultimate claim later costs less. Build an issue register showing policy, disputed item, governing rule, source document, proposed correction, and possible adverse adjustment. A documented $200,000 collected premium overpayment at 35% produces a $70,000 operator fee and leaves $130,000 with the employer before any separately agreed expenses. The permitted commercial range here is a negotiated 30%–40%, subject to the actual engagement and applicable state requirements. Withdraw unsupported claims, disclose additional premium exposure, use the bureau's documented dispute process for defensible items, and improve prospective payroll and claims reporting when historical correction is unavailable.

Protect payment by defining attribution narrowly enough to survive a real dispute. Attach the initial issue register and identify preexisting carrier disputes so the operator cannot claim credit for someone else's completed work. State whether the fee applies to cash, realized premium credits, or both; describe netting for offsetting additional premiums arising from the same accepted correction. For example, a $60,000 refund accompanied by a related $10,000 additional premium produces a $50,000 net recovery and a $17,500 fee at 35% if that is the agreed basis. Give the client a reporting duty for carrier correspondence and settlements, reasonable access to supporting statements, and a limited post-termination tail covering identified work already delivered. Avoid a perpetual claim over every future premium reduction. A targeted noncircumvention clause should preserve payment for documented work without preventing the employer from speaking to its carrier. Separate recovery authority from broker-of-record authority: obtaining records does not appoint a producer, and a renewal appointment does not create historical contingency rights. When the client disputes attribution, collect the undisputed portion and use the agreed review process rather than interfering with coverage or threatening essential benefits operations.

Licensing and disclosure determine whether an otherwise attractive fee can be retained. Verify active state Life and Health authority for applicable benefits sales and Property and Casualty authority for workers' compensation placement, including entity licenses, appointments, and nonresident requirements where relevant. Determine separately whether specific administration functions require TPA authority; a consulting label does not change the activity performed. Texas Insurance Code Sections 4001.051 and 4151.051 illustrate separate agent and administrator frameworks, subject to their definitions and exceptions. For covered group-health brokerage or consulting arrangements expecting at least $1,000 in direct or indirect compensation, apply ERISA Section 408(b)(2)(B), codified at 29 U.S.C. 1108(b)(2)(B), including required advance disclosures and applicable update obligations. Explain commissions, overrides, referral payments, and termination-related compensation in the actual arrangement. Disclosure does not replace the requirement that compensation be reasonable. Before receiving protected health information as a business associate, execute the required agreement and implement role-based access. For PBM records, confirm audit rights and applicable gag-clause restrictions rather than assuming a broker appointment unlocks every field. Keep stop-loss run-out terms, reference-based pricing defense arrangements, and carrier reimbursement conditions visible because collection economics deteriorate when promised implementation cannot actually be delivered.

## 4. Reconcile the retained-fee ledger to all three canonical operating cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the scorecard's annual revenue totals as retained operator revenue, even though the scenario metadata calls them gross revenue. They are not gross employer billings, claim disbursements, premium volume, or rebate principal. Under the stated retained-share basis, amounts belonging to other parties remain outside the operator's revenue bridge. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis to the actual contracts rather than assuming every commission or pass-through arrangement has identical accounting. The following unit bridges are illustrative compositions of the supplied totals, not additional scorecard outputs. Conservative uses 1,000 average billed employees at $8 PEPM for twelve months, producing $96,000, plus $12,000 retained commissions and $12,000 recovery fees from $40,000 recovered at 30%. Total revenue is exactly $120,000. At 32% net margin, owner net profit is $38,400 and total modeled costs are $81,600. Staffing remains 1 FT + 3 C. Count only billable employee months actually covered by the agreement; dependents are not additional employees unless the contract explicitly uses another billing unit. A partial-year launch cannot claim twelve months of PEPM revenue merely because the signed client census reaches the illustrative year-end headcount. Reconcile monthly census changes before invoicing.

Base uses 2,500 average billed employees at $12 PEPM for twelve months, producing $360,000, plus $105,000 retained commissions and $70,000 recovery fees from $200,000 recovered at 35%. Total revenue is $535,000; the 45.0% margin produces $240,750 owner net profit and $294,250 total modeled costs with 1 FT + 3 C. Scaled uses 4,000 average billed employees at $15 PEPM for twelve months, producing $720,000, plus $125,000 retained commissions and $105,000 recovery fees from $300,000 recovered at 35%. Total revenue is $950,000; the 58% margin produces $551,000 owner net profit and $399,000 total modeled costs with 2 FT + 4 C. These bridges assume the fees and commissions are separately earned, disclosed where required, and not contractually offset against each other. If an advisory agreement credits commissions against PEPM fees, subtract that credit instead of counting both amounts. The rising margins are canonical scenario assumptions, not results established by these unit examples. Validate delivery capacity, contractor expense, acquisition cost, insurance, software, and collection losses against the full cost envelope. Do not treat the narrower monthly overhead range as the entire annual expense model.

Run a settlement ledger beside the revenue ledger because earned fees and available cash diverge. Each recovery entry should identify the client, policy period, accepted correction, gross refund, related offset, realized credit, contingency rate, invoice date, due date, and collected fee. Each commission entry needs carrier statement references, applicable revenue-sharing deductions, cancellation exposure, and any client fee credit. For recurring advisory work, tie invoices to the agreed employee-month census and service period. A 30-day payment delay on $60,000 of valid invoices creates a $60,000 working-capital gap without automatically changing recognized revenue; a $6,000 unrecoverable balance reduces economic profit unless already included in the cost assumptions. Maintain an aging review that separates administrative delays, disputed scope, counterparty distress, and confirmed bank returns. Escalate administrative delays to accounts payable, scope disputes to the named reviewer, and deteriorating credit to revised terms for future work. Keep recovered employer money out of operator cash forecasts except for the earned retained fee. Close each month by reconciling contracts, source statements, invoices, bank receipts, and outstanding disputes. This discipline makes the small-team model collectible without turning the advisory business into an unlicensed custodian or an involuntary lender.
