## 1. Assign eleven plays to four accountable seats before adding capacity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat Candidate #40's eleven mapped plays as one renewal production system, not eleven disconnected businesses. This chapter's execution map is P1 Form 5500 renewal targeting; P2 renewal and compensation diagnostics; P3 self-funded transition design; P4 captive placement analysis; P5 specific and aggregate stop-loss optimization; P6 TPA selection and implementation; P7 NADAC-based PBM repricing; P8 rebate and spread recovery; P9 reference-based pricing support and balance-bill defense coordination; P10 workers' compensation payroll and classification correction; and P11 NCCI or applicable bureau E-Mod correction and refund collection. These labels organize the stated opportunity rather than assert that every employer should buy every service. Connect each play to an employer, renewal cohort, engagement authority, deliverable, and collection event. The full-time principal owns sales, licensed recommendations, disclosures, and final client decisions. Contractor one supplies health-plan, captive, and stop-loss analysis; contractor two supplies PBM economics and reproducible data work; contractor three supplies licensed workers' compensation analysis and carrier follow-through. Keep beneficiary decisions and claim adjudication with the parties actually contracted and authorized to perform them. The four-person pod produces advice and recoveries; it does not quietly become a risk-bearing insurer, medical administrator, law firm, or custodian of employer claim funds.

Write a seat charter before recruiting a recognizable specialist. The principal must hold the applicable Life and Health and Property and Casualty producer authorities, including required business-entity licenses and appointments. A contractor performing regulated solicitation, negotiation, or sale needs their own applicable authority; the principal's license is not an umbrella permission. Contractor one delivers attachment-point comparisons, exclusions, lasers, terminal liability, and run-out analysis with source policy language. Contractor two delivers an NDC-level pricing bridge, rebate entitlement schedule, and documented treatment of specialty drugs, dispensing fees, and exclusions. Contractor three ties payroll classifications and loss runs to bureau worksheets, carrier endorsements, and employer receipts. Require an independent second look before client delivery: the principal checks commercial conclusions while the relevant specialist resolves technical exceptions. A principal unable to evaluate a specialty needs a qualified reviewer, not a signature ritual. Build this limited review purchase into engagement economics rather than silently creating another permanent seat. Contracts specify outputs, confidentiality, acceptance criteria, and delivery windows while preserving genuine independent-contractor discretion. Classification depends on actual working arrangements and applicable law, not the label printed above the signature. Reassess it whenever ongoing supervision starts resembling employee management.

Expand to two full-time employees and four contractors only after the existing pod demonstrates repeatable throughput. The second full-time seat owns account operations, renewal calendars, document completeness, reconciliation, and service recovery; obtain producer licensing before assigning regulated recommendations to that seat. The fourth contractor handles health implementation and vendor coordination, including P6 and P9 handoffs, without substituting for qualified balance-bill defense counsel. Retained outside counsel, tax preparation, and software subscriptions remain purchased services rather than hidden dedicated pod seats; budget them explicitly. Trigger expansion when eight consecutive weekly scorecards show accepted backlog beyond the principal's documented capacity, renewal deadlines remain protected, and contracted recurring contribution covers the added fixed cost under a realistic churn stress. An illustrative $90,000 annual account-operations cost requires $120,000 of incremental recurring revenue at a 75% incremental contribution rate before providing any buffer. That calculation tests a hiring decision, not a revision to the canonical scenarios. Transfer routine employer meetings using shared agendas and documented escalation rights. The principal should retain pricing and relationship accountability while another authorized person can retrieve every material commitment, explain its economics, and keep the renewal moving during a two-week founder absence without relying on personal messages.

## 2. Buy accepted outputs and run a weekly exception scorecard  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Pay contractors for accepted technical outputs rather than vague availability or projected employer savings. A workable illustrative starting schedule pays $1,200 for a complete stop-loss comparison, $900 for a reproducible PBM repricing package, and $750 for a workers' compensation correction packet. Define complexity bands and scope limits before work starts; large specialty files and multistate payroll disputes need separate quotes. Pay 80% upon documented acceptance and the remaining 20% after a ten-business-day defect window, subject to the contract and applicable payment rules. A contractor who performed the agreed analysis should not finance a carrier's unrelated payment delay. Licensed commission participation, where permitted, belongs in a separate written schedule with required disclosures; unlicensed analytics vendors should not receive disguised placement commissions. For budgeting illustrations, reserve $36,000 annually for the three contractors in Conservative, $96,000 in Base, and $168,000 for four contractors in Scaled. These are allocations inside canonical expenses, not incremental deductions from the published owner profits. Quote each engagement against its available contribution and stop accepting work that requires uncompensated expert rescue. Require correction of attributable defects without charging the client twice, but distinguish changed scope from poor original workmanship.

Use a Friday scorecard that exposes denominators and unresolved exceptions, not an attractive collection of percentages. Track qualified employer conversations, authorized data packages, proposals delivered, bound or signed engagements, and first cash receipts as separate funnel stages. Define a qualified employer as an identified decision-maker with a relevant renewal date, workable group profile, and permission to discuss the engagement. For production, target acknowledgment within one business day, completeness review within two, and technical delivery within the agreed window after complete inputs arrive. Record client-caused pauses separately rather than resetting clocks invisibly. An illustrative service objective is at least 95% on-time accepted deliverables and at least 98% first-pass accuracy on explicitly defined critical checks; disclose counts because one defect in five jobs is not comparable with one in fifty. Track stop-loss coverage exceptions, disputed PBM assumptions, and rejected E-Mod submissions by age and owner. Escalate any missing effective-date protection immediately, regardless of a green average. Weekly meetings should end with named corrective actions, promised dates, and the financial exposure of each open item. Keep contractor performance review focused on agreed results and service obligations rather than prescribing unnecessary daily supervision that undermines the intended independent relationship.

Concentration belongs on the same weekly page as delivery because a seemingly efficient book can hide one fragile dependency. Set a maximum of 25% of trailing-twelve-month recognized revenue from any single employer controlled group and 25% from any economically distinct commission-paying counterparty group. Aggregate affiliates rather than treating separate carrier entities as independent risks. Measure the next-twelve-month contracted revenue forecast and receivables alongside those historical ratios. Also cap any single carrier, TPA, or PBM dependency at 25% of the recurring revenue that its withdrawal would interrupt, even when employers pay the operator directly. These are portfolio objectives, not reasons to place an employer into an inferior arrangement. A startup with one employer necessarily exceeds the limit; show the breach openly, prohibit discretionary expansion of that exposure, and build a dated diversification plan. Do not call the book exit-ready until the limits actually hold. Recalculate revenue retention by renewal cohort, separating employer departures, employee-count changes, pricing changes, and new recovery wins. Stress the loss of the largest customer and the largest counterparty independently and together where exposures overlap. Fund fixed commitments from stressed recurring contribution, not from this month's unusually large contingent refund receipt.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly evidence set
   - Export CRM stages, engagement tickets, recognized revenue, receivables, and contractor acceptance records each Friday.
   - Use consistent cutoffs and engagement identifiers; retain the underlying export so later corrections remain traceable.
2. Reconcile metrics to source events
   - Have the principal or account-operations owner tie every scorecard numerator and denominator to the frozen records.
   - Separate contingent estimates from earned fees, document paused work, and aggregate affiliated customers and counterparties.
3. Resolve exceptions by exposure
   - Prioritize effective-date protection, material calculation errors, overdue collections, and concentration breaches before ordinary backlog.
   - Assign one owner and one dated next action to each exception; preserve the original commitment and explain any revision.
4. Close the loop before accepting more work
   - Confirm corrections, release accepted contractor payments, and update capacity commitments for the following week.
   - Compare actual engagement contribution with quoted contribution and suspend repeatable loss-making scope until pricing or production changes.

## 3. Reconcile retained revenue, accruals, and contractor ownership monthly  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Preserve the scorecard's economic boundary before discussing a multiple. Conservative revenue of $120,000 less $81,600 of expenses produces $38,400 owner profit at 32%; Base revenue of $535,000 less $294,250 produces $240,750 at 45.0%; Scaled revenue of $950,000 less $399,000 produces $551,000 at 58%. The first two cases use one full-time principal and three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time seats and four contractors. Although the source scenarios label their top line gross revenue, the defined basis is agency revenue from net retained advisory fees, stop-loss and TPA commissions, and contingent E-Mod refund splits. Employer premiums, claim deposits, pharmacy spending, and refund principal never become operator revenue merely because a dashboard displays them. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis to each contractual promise and document the retained-share bridge wherever collections include amounts belonging to another party. Do not apply an invented second haircut to the canonical revenue. Under the illustrative contractor budgets above, remaining expense allowances are $45,600, $198,250, and $231,000 respectively. Those amounts must cover all other included operating costs; they are not additional owner distributions. Reconcile actual ledger classifications before comparing owner profit with buyer-adjusted earnings, because the scorecard alone does not specify every compensation or accounting convention.

Maintain accrual schedules by employer, policy year, renewal cohort, revenue stream, and counterparty rather than relying on deposits in QuickBooks Online or Xero. A PEPM advisory engagement earns revenue as the contracted service is delivered using reconciled eligible lives and the agreed billing definition. For example, 500 covered employees at $6 retained PEPM produce $3,000 for a fully earned service month, not $36,000 on signature day. Commission recognition follows the actual contract, performance obligations, cancellation provisions, and applicable variable consideration assessment, with clawback exposure separately supported. For a $100,000 employer refund carrying a 20% contractual recovery fee, the operator's fee opportunity is $20,000; the employer's $100,000 recovery is not agency sales. Recognize contingent fees only when the performance obligation and variable-consideration requirements are satisfied, including the assessment of significant reversal risk. A promising bureau recalculation is not automatically an earned, collectible carrier refund. Reconcile opening receivables plus recognized fees less cash, credits, and write-offs to closing receivables monthly. Separate unbilled earned amounts from speculative pipeline. Accrue accepted contractor services in the same close rather than inflating a strong month by postponing invoices. Preserve cohort-level acquisition costs and service hours so a buyer can distinguish durable renewal contribution from founder-subsidized delivery.

Transferability also requires ownership of the machinery that generates the numbers. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a transfer of copyright ownership generally requires a signed writing by the owner or their authorized agent. Obtain assignments covering commissioned scripts, calculation templates, documentation, and other copyrightable deliverables; do not assume that paying an independent contractor creates ownership or that every commission qualifies as work made for hire. Schedule preexisting contractor tools separately and secure an adequate continuing license where assignment is unavailable. Copyright assignments do not transfer employer records, PBM confidential material, bureau data rights, or third-party software licenses. Establish separate permissions for each. Keep source repositories, administrative accounts, and recovery evidence under organization-controlled identities with least-privilege access and tested offboarding. Where the operator or contractor acts as a HIPAA business associate or subcontractor, execute the applicable agreements and implement required safeguards before sharing protected health information. A buyer data room starts with aggregated operating metrics and redacted contracts, not identifiable claims files. Document assignment restrictions, carrier appointments, producer registrations, and business-entity change requirements in a transfer register. An asset sale does not automatically transfer every license, appointment, client authorization, or vendor contract. Price unresolved transfer work before entering exclusivity.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four transfer conditions before promising a buyer continuity**
> 
> Treat each condition as an evidence requirement. Unresolved items affect transaction structure, timing, and price rather than disappearing into an optimistic earnings multiple.
> - Verify active producer and business-entity authorities, required appointments, applicable TPA requirements, and ERISA section 408(b)(2) compensation disclosures for the actual services performed.
> - Verify that each material engagement and vendor agreement identifies assignment restrictions, change-of-control provisions, termination rights, and the party responsible for obtaining required permissions.
> - Verify signed contractor IP assignments or sufficient continuing licenses, organization-controlled repositories, and written treatment of preexisting tools and restricted source materials.
> - Verify reconciled trailing-twelve-month revenue, contingent-fee support, concentration at or below 25% for each defined exposure, and a normalized earnings bridge that includes founder replacement costs.

## 4. Underwrite an exit multiple against transferable earnings and retained cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Value the transferable business, not the founder's ability to work indefinitely without replacement. A small owner-operated advisory book may be discussed using seller's discretionary earnings, while a larger professionally serviced book is more naturally assessed on normalized EBITDA or retained recurring revenue. Those denominators are not interchangeable. For preliminary negotiation modeling, use illustrative ranges of 2.0 to 3.5 times normalized SDE for a founder-dependent microbusiness and 3.5 to 5.0 times normalized EBITDA for a more transferable small specialist operation. These are underwriting assumptions, not observed transaction comparables or an assurance that a buyer will bid. Obtain current comparable transactions with matching size, revenue quality, retention, and deal structure before setting an asking price. Pure recurring books can also be discussed using revenue multiples, but that shortcut becomes dangerous when contingent refund fees dominate or service intensity varies. A strategic buyer may credit distribution synergies, yet the seller should not capitalize savings the buyer has neither validated nor agreed to share. The defensible premium comes from documented renewal retention, clean rights, diversified counterparties, accountable service ownership, and low founder dependence. A high annual margin achieved by skipping coverage review or contractor accruals should reduce confidence rather than increase the multiple.

Build the earnings bridge explicitly before multiplying anything. For an illustration only, assume canonical owner profit is measured before an additional market-rate replacement for the working principal and that interest, income taxes, depreciation, and amortization require no further bridge adjustments. Deduct $100,000 of fully loaded replacement cost from Base owner profit of $240,750 to obtain $140,750 of illustrative normalized earnings. Deduct $130,000 from Scaled owner profit of $551,000 to obtain $421,000. If actual expenses already include that replacement role, remove the duplicate deduction; if additional normalizations exist, rebuild the bridge. At 3.5 to 5.0 times, the illustrative enterprise values are $492,625 to $703,750 for Base and $1,473,500 to $2,105,000 for Scaled. These calculations do not change the canonical profit cases. Conservative's $38,400 cannot support the illustrative $100,000 replacement cost, making a self-operating EBITDA valuation inappropriate without a different cost structure or buyer synergy. Bridge enterprise value to seller proceeds through actual debt, cash treatment, working-capital adjustments, fees, escrows, and contingent payments. Never present the top end of enterprise value as cash available at closing. Show buyer assumptions separately from the operator's verified historical results.

Use the same evidence package to choose between selling and retaining a durable cash-producing book. A buyer's headline price may contain a substantial earnout tied to renewals they will influence after closing. Define retained revenue, excluded cancellations, producer reassignment, pricing changes, reporting access, dispute resolution, and collection timing in the purchase agreement. Consider an illustrative $1,700,000 enterprise-value offer with 70% paid at closing and 30% contingent: the stated components are $1,190,000 and $510,000 before other adjustments, not $1,700,000 of immediate liquidity. Compare expected net proceeds with a discounted distribution model that includes founder replacement, maintenance spending, working capital, taxes, and losses from concentrated customers or counterparties. Do not compare a pretax sale headline with after-tax annual distributions. For the retained-book option, maintain a cash reserve calibrated to payroll, contractor commitments, clawbacks, and collection delays; test a minimum three-month operating-cost buffer against actual seasonality. Run the founder-absence exercise twice annually and refresh the transfer register after each renewal. Quarterly buyer readiness should improve service and liquidity even if no sale occurs. The pod has earned a premium only when another qualified operator can preserve its economics without recreating the founder's undocumented judgment or inheriting avoidable counterparty surprises.
