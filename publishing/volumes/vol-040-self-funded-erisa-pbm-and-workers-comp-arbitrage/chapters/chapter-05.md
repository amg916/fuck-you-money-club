## 1. Refuse the Coverage Gap Before Selling the Savings  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is treating an employer's premium budget as the brokerage's addressable revenue. A $4 million health-plan budget is an employer funding obligation, not $4 million of agency sales. Renewal intelligence from EFAST2 Form 5500 filings identifies potential conversations, but Schedule A and Schedule C are neither universal nor current contract inventories. A self-funded arrangement can have incomplete commission visibility in those schedules, and filing exemptions remove some prospects entirely. Confirm plan funding, renewal date, incumbent authority, and actual decision rights directly. For benefits advisory, 1,000 covered employees at $8 PEPM produces $96,000 annually only if the agreement bills employees rather than members and all twelve months remain collectible. A covered-life count of 1,800 does not justify billing 1,800 employees. Build a monthly census bridge covering additions, terminations, retroactive eligibility changes, and invoice adjustments. Stop automated invoicing when unexplained census differences exceed 1%; that is an internal investigation threshold, not an industry standard. Incumbent broker defense typically attacks timing, compensation ambiguity, and coverage continuity. Counter with signed authority and reconciled facts, never fabricated incumbent fees or unsupported savings claims. The safe fallback is a fixed-scope diagnostic while the incumbent coverage remains intact.

The second and third traps are buying a stop-loss headline and forgetting the calendar. A $100,000 specific attachment point does not protect a claimant carrying a $500,000 individual laser. If that claimant incurs $420,000 of otherwise eligible expenses, employer exposure can be $420,000 rather than $100,000, a $320,000 difference before other policy terms. An aggregate attachment is not automatically a second route around that exclusion; inspect how laser claims enter the aggregate calculation. Compare incurred and paid windows, terminal liability, actively-at-work language, disclosure duties, reimbursement deadlines, and advance-funding provisions against the plan document and TPA agreement. A 12/12 arrangement and a 12/15 arrangement allocate late-paid claims differently. Never describe a three-month extension as complete protection against every late submission. For self-funding transitions and stop-loss placement, require a claimant-specific exposure schedule and a monthly cash stress test. A $250,000 eligible claim reimbursed sixty days later still requires $250,000 of interim liquidity unless an enforceable advance mechanism applies. Pause placement when any material claimant falls outside the signed coverage comparison. The fallback is to retain existing coverage or select a fully insured alternative rather than finance the employer's shock claim from the operator's working capital.

The fourth trap is presenting captive participation as premium savings without contingent liabilities. A $75,000 contribution plus a possible $50,000 assessment creates a different exposure from a fixed $75,000 premium; collateral, exit accounting, and loss-development provisions determine the actual commitment. The fifth trap is outsourcing TPA selection to a commission grid. Reconcile eligibility feeds, claim-payment authority, stop-loss reporting, banking controls, and termination assistance before comparing administrative prices. A $2 PEPM discount across 1,000 employees saves $24,000 annually, but one unrecoverable $60,000 reporting defect overwhelms it. The sixth trap is selling reference-based pricing as a discount that providers must accept. If an illustrative $100,000 billed service yields a $30,000 plan payment and a $20,000 negotiated settlement, the relevant employer cost is $50,000 plus defense and administration, not the initial payment alone. Confirm provider agreements, participant communications, appeals, balance-bill defense, and federal surprise-billing rules where applicable. Escalate every collection notice immediately rather than waiting for a monthly dashboard. State producer authority and any required TPA authority must match the actual activities. When defense capacity or licensing is missing, use properly authorized partners and narrower services; do not convert an advice engagement into unauthorized claims administration or custody.

## 2. Rebuild PBM Savings from Adjudicated Claims and Collectible Rebates  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The seventh trap is repricing pharmacy claims against NADAC and calling the difference recoverable spread. NADAC is a survey-based acquisition-cost reference, not the employer's contract price or a universal benchmark for specialty, limited-distribution, or unavailable products. Normalize NDC format, package quantity, dispensing units, service dates, reversals, ingredient cost, dispensing fees, and member cost share before comparing transactions. Suppose 10,000 comparable paid prescriptions show $1,200,000 in employer and member payments against a $1,050,000 modeled alternative. The $150,000 difference is a screening result, not operator revenue and not automatically an enforceable refund. If implementation and network effects cost $40,000, estimated employer improvement falls to $110,000. A separately contracted 20% fee would equal $22,000 only on the agreed, validated measurement basis. Stop the repricing run when unmatched product or unit records exceed 2% of evaluated spend; inspect all high-dollar exceptions even below that threshold. Compare therapeutic and network disruption before promising savings. The fallback is a paid procurement comparison with clearly separated measured, modeled, and excluded populations, not a recovery demand built from unmatched benchmarks. Preserve the original extract and transformation log so a challenger can reproduce every included claim without receiving unnecessary patient identifiers.

The eighth trap is equating a pass-through label with ownership of every manufacturer dollar. Rebate definitions may exclude administrative fees, purchase discounts, price-protection payments, specialty arrangements, or amounts earned through affiliated entities. Read the economic definition, permitted deductions, reconciliation timing, audit rights, termination tail, and disputes process. At 1,000 eligible members, a $300 annual guarantee suggests $300,000, but a contract excluding 200 members from that guarantee produces $240,000 before other adjustments. A 15% recovery fee on a verified $60,000 incremental payment is $9,000; it is not 15% of all historical rebates. Establish one ledger for amounts contractually earned, another for invoiced receivables, and another for cash received. Flag an unpaid amount immediately after its contractual due date and suspend any discretionary distributions tied to that receivable. A rebate payable 180 days after quarter-end is not late at day ninety, although it still creates a working-capital requirement. Tie contingency recognition to the accountant-approved treatment of variable consideration and reversal risk. If the PBM refuses the evidence needed to establish an amount, pursue contractual audit or dispute channels and revert to an agreed fixed-fee analytical scope. Do not invent an accrual to preserve a monthly sales target.

The ninth trap combines hidden remuneration with careless health-information access. ERISA Section 408(b)(2)(B), codified at 29 U.S.C. 1108(b)(2)(B), imposes disclosure requirements on covered group-health brokerage and consulting arrangements expecting at least $1,000 in direct or indirect compensation. Disclose applicable commissions, referral compensation, and payment formulas; merely saying that carriers may pay compensation does not explain a known arrangement. Whether a particular person is a fiduciary depends on their functions, not their business card. Separate compensation analysis from claim-level processing permissions. Execute required business associate arrangements when performing covered functions involving protected health information, apply minimum-necessary access where required, and restrict employer access according to plan-administration rules. A pharmacy extract containing 40,000 rows does not justify forty thousand patient names in a sales workspace. Use purpose-limited identifiers and secure transfers, with named accounts and access expiration. Stop processing upon an authorization mismatch, a rejected credential, or an export outside the agreed purpose. Do not bypass a portal suspension with shared credentials, rotating accounts, or scraped session tokens. Those shortcuts can terminate contractual access and create additional liability. Preserve the failure evidence, contact the authorized administrator, and use an approved export while permissions are repaired.

## 3. Separate Bureau Corrections from Tax Credits and Unsupported Recoveries  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The tenth trap is changing workers' compensation classifications because a cheaper code appears plausible. Actual operations, governing classification rules, state exceptions, payroll segregation, and supporting records control the result. NCCI does not administer every jurisdiction; California uses WCIRB, and other states have independent arrangements. Obtain the applicable bureau worksheet, policy audits, payroll detail, and carrier unit statistical reporting. A simple illustration uses $300,000 of mod-sensitive premium: changing the modifier from 1.20 to 1.10 suggests a $30,000 difference before other rating factors. At a permitted 25% recovery share, the operator fee is $7,500 only if the defined recovery is actually supported and the agreement's earning conditions are met. Do not apply that percentage to total payroll, claim reserves, or the entire premium invoice. Expense constants, discounts, state assessments, and policy-specific adjustments can change the final refund. Stop the demand whenever the governing classification or reporting period is unresolved. The fallback is a fixed-fee payroll and classification review with an appropriately licensed producer handling placement or premium negotiations. Never coach an employer to relabel hazardous work, conceal subcontractors, or alter time records; those actions create false applications rather than legitimate rating corrections.

The eleventh trap is assuming a closed claim automatically repairs an experience modifier. Rating uses defined experience periods, valuation dates, loss limitations, and reporting procedures. A $100,000 reserve reduction after a valuation date may affect a later calculation, an allowable revision, or neither immediate premium nor the current worksheet. Trace each proposed adjustment from carrier claim history through unit statistical reporting to the bureau's applicable revision rules. Track correction acceptance separately from refund issuance and client receipt. Flag a carrier acknowledgment missing after ten business days as an internal service escalation, while respecting the actual contractual and bureau deadlines. The twelfth trap is ignoring commission chargebacks when a corrected premium falls or a policy cancels. If a $20,000 commission has a contractually determined $6,000 reversal, available contribution falls by $6,000 even though the sales report still celebrates the placement. Maintain a policy-level chargeback schedule covering earning rules, cancellation provisions, audits, and producer splits. For recovery compensation, confirm state restrictions and avoid promising advocacy beyond the operator's authority. If a carrier disputes causation or timing, preserve the written decision and invoke the applicable review process. Do not net a disputed recovery against employer premiums or withhold claim funds to force payment.

The mapped play wotc_hourly_payroll_tax_credit_contingency_engine combines authorized WOTC screening with separately substantiated Section 41 work; it does not make employee hiring a research activity. Verify statutory program authorization for each hiring date before projecting WOTC. Do not assume a post-2025 start date qualifies merely because payroll software offers a screening form. For an authorized hiring period, complete Form 8850 pre-screening as required and submit the certification request to the designated state workforce agency within the applicable 28-day deadline. In an illustration involving ten certified employees in a qualifying category with a $6,000 wage limit, each reaching 400 hours and sufficient eligible wages, the 40% rate yields $2,400 each, or $24,000. A hypothetical permitted 15% service fee is $3,600, never $24,000 of operator revenue. Wage coordination can reduce the credit; maintain an employee-level allocation ledger. Section 41 requires its own activity analysis, qualified-expense support, funded-research review, and applicable base calculation. A separately validated $50,000 credit could imply a $5,000 fee at 10% only where professional and other applicable fee rules permit. Exclude unsupported claims and use fixed-fee payroll and research-documentation services under independent tax review when eligibility or fee authority fails.

## 4. Install Cash, Access, and Evidence Circuit Breakers Before Scaling  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep the canonical Formula v2.0 cases separate from every downside illustration. Conservative recognized operator revenue is $120,000; at 32% net margin, costs are $81,600 and annual owner profit is $38,400. Base revenue is $535,000; at 45.0%, costs are $294,250 and profit is $240,750. Scaled revenue is $950,000; at 58%, costs are $399,000 and profit is $551,000. Staffing stays 1 FT + 3 C for Conservative and Base, and 2 FT + 4 C for Scaled. One illustrative composition, not an additional scorecard assumption, reconciles Conservative as $96,000 PEPM fees plus $18,000 retained commissions plus $6,000 recovery fees. Base can reconcile as 3,000 employees times $10 times twelve months, plus $125,000 commissions and $50,000 recovery fees. Scaled can reconcile as 5,000 employees times $11 times twelve, plus $200,000 commissions and $90,000 recovery fees. These components must already reflect the operator's retained share where applicable; never deduct the same producer split twice. Determine gross-versus-net presentation under ASC 606 from the actual principal-agent relationship, not the scorecard's shorthand revenue label. Employer premiums, pharmacy spend, recovered tax credits, and claim funds stay outside these revenue totals.

The thirteenth trap is running an agency on accrued wins while paying contractors in cash. Track cash collected, revenue recognized, disputed receivables, expected commission reversals, and contractor commitments separately. The stated $2,000–$11,000 monthly operating range is a planning input, not the full expense denominator behind every canonical profit case; do not substitute that range for scorecard costs. At the upper planning level, three months requires $33,000 before claim disputes or owner withdrawals. Startup capital of $8,000–$45,000 and a two-to-five-month first-deal cycle make collection timing material. Use an internal stop on discretionary owner distributions when unrestricted cash falls below three months of committed outflows. Reserve commission exposure using contract-specific earning schedules rather than a convenient flat percentage. A Base downside illustration with $25,000 of lost recognized revenue and $15,000 of additional dispute expense reduces profit from $240,750 to $200,750, assuming other costs remain unchanged. That illustration does not revise the canonical Base case. Investigate receivables thirty days beyond their contractual due date and any client exceeding 25% of annual retained revenue. Concentration is an exposure signal, not an automatic reason to terminate a good client. Renegotiate deposits, milestones, or fixed fees before expanding unfunded work.

The fourteenth trap is automating a broken permission model. Public Form 5500 research, employer claim extracts, PBM portals, and bureau worksheets have different access rights; one employer signature does not authorize every downstream system action. Respect published access methods, service terms, rate limits, and the precise scope of delegated authority. Stop after an authorization rejection or account suspension; repeated evasion attempts can convert a repairable access issue into permanent termination. Detect bulk-export anomalies, new administrator accounts, unexpected destination changes, and expired client authority. For this chapter, the eleven operating lanes are renewal intelligence, benefits advisory, self-funding transitions, captive placement, stop-loss placement, TPA selection, PBM repricing, rebate recovery, reference-based pricing, E-Mod recovery, and the named WOTC/Section 41 engine; these labels organize work rather than assert additional catalog identifiers. Give each lane an evidence owner and a recoverable shutdown procedure. Preserve raw records, calculation versions, signed terms, and correction history under a documented retention schedule. When a defect appears, stop the affected calculation, quantify exposed revenue and client obligations, notify the authorized decision-maker, and resume only after correction is verified. A small team survives by narrowing unsupported work immediately, not by hiding exceptions until renewal.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the affected lane
   - Suspend the disputed calculation, submission, invoice line, or portal session without disabling unrelated authorized work.
   - Preserve original extracts, timestamps, contract versions, access events, and the current client-facing calculation.
2. Quantify the actual exposure
   - Separate employer obligations, operator retained revenue, repayable commissions, receivables, and incremental correction costs.
   - Calculate a bounded downside using specific contract terms; never count employer claim funds as available agency liquidity.
3. Assign the authorized correction
   - Route coverage defects to the licensed producer, reporting defects to the carrier or TPA, and tax questions to independent tax review.
   - Notify the responsible client decision-maker and record applicable response, appeal, filing, and contractual dispute deadlines.
4. Verify before resuming
   - Reperform the affected reconciliation against corrected evidence and obtain the required client or administrator authorization.
   - Resume only the repaired scope, update reserves and forecasts, and add the defect to the recurring exception checklist.
