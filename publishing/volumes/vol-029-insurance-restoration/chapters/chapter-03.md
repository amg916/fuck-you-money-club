## 1. Reconcile every restoration dollar before calling the job profitable  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with gross restoration billings, not the homeowner's policy limit, replacement-cost estimate, or total insurance settlement. The canonical Conservative, Base, and Scaled cases produce $500,000, $1,500,000, and $2,500,000 of annual operator revenue. Their respective owner profits are $40,000, $195,000, and $450,000, exactly 8%, 13%, and 18%. To make those totals inspectable, this model uses 50 completed jobs averaging $10,000, 100 averaging $15,000, and 125 averaging $20,000. These are illustrative cohort decompositions, not additional scorecard forecasts. Assign each job a unique identifier shared by Xactimate, Encircle, the dispatch calendar, and QuickBooks Online. Separate the signed original scope, executed change orders, billed amounts, cash received, and disputed amounts. A homeowner's $60,000 settlement does not become contractor revenue when the contractor performs $15,000 of work. Conversely, subcontractor and material expenditures remain costs inside this gross-billings business; they are not removed to present a misleading commission-only revenue denominator. Close cohorts by substantial completion month and preserve later adjustments against their originating jobs so collection losses cannot disappear into a newer, stronger production month.

The waterfall below is one explicit allocation that reconciles to the canonical results; the scorecard does not prescribe these individual cost buckets. Employee payroll includes employer taxes, workers' compensation, and allocated delivery benefits. Subcontractor delivery is separate and covers purchased trade packages rather than the same employee hours counted twice. Equipment includes rental, maintenance, depreciation, and consumables not already charged to materials. Acquisition contains advertising, call tracking, and acquisition-specific administration; general overhead excludes those amounts. The two-percent scrub and reversal provision is a planning expense for collection losses and supported billing corrections, not an automatic deduction from the published revenue headline. Actual credit memos and receivable allowances need separate ledger treatment, with no duplicate expense. Before acquisition, overhead, and financing, management contribution after delivery and the provision is $115,000 Conservative, $485,000 Base, and $975,000 Scaled. Those are management subtotals, not a substitute for the company's formal gross-profit presentation. Dividing final profit by completed jobs yields $800, $1,950, and $3,600 respectively, showing how little error the smallest case can absorb.

Keep the staffing denominator visible: Conservative and Base each use five full-time employees plus six contractors; Scaled uses six full-time employees plus seven contractors, thirteen people total. Contractor counts describe a delivery roster, not six or seven guaranteed full-year salaries. The illustrative payroll allocations of $200,000, $350,000, and $480,000 imply average annual loaded employee costs of $40,000, $70,000, and $80,000. Conservative payroll is particularly thin for specialized restoration work and must survive local wage, overtime, insurance, and benefit quotes. If five employees actually require $60,000 each, that case incurs $100,000 additional payroll and changes from $40,000 profit to a $60,000 loss without repricing or other verified savings. Do not repair this result by silently replacing full-time employees with occasional labor. Record any working owner's wages inside the appropriate labor bucket; owner distributions come after the modeled profit and do not reduce it again. The supplied startup capital range of $18,000 to $75,000 and monthly operating expense range of $4,000 to $20,000 are planning references, not evidence that total delivery costs fit a fixed monthly allowance.

## 2. Make five operator plays earn their place in the job margin  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The acquisition play, google_maps_gbp_lead_gen_network, must operate through genuine eligible businesses rather than fabricated city addresses. For insurance restoration, partner with licensed local operators whose real shops or yards can withstand live video verification; a physical address alone does not establish Google Business Profile eligibility. Follow Google's service-area and address-display rules, retain the business's control of its profile, and never suggest that renting a tracking number creates a branch. An illustrative $3,000 monthly campaign producing 60 calls, 30 qualified opportunities, and six completed jobs has $500 acquisition cost per completion. That fits the Conservative per-job allowance but supplies only part of a larger production plan. CallRail should distinguish flood emergencies, unrelated inquiries, duplicate callers, and callers outside licensed service territory. Measure cost against completed collectible work, not every answered call. If only three jobs complete, acquisition cost doubles to $1,000 and consumes another $500 per job. The fallback is to stop ineligible profile launches, retain only genuine partner-backed locations, and pair each legitimate listing with accurately branded organic city pages supporting the rank-and-rent arrangement.

The second play, evidence-led drying documentation, converts field discipline into defensible scope rather than a promise of insurance payment. Use IICRC S500 procedures appropriate to the assignment, record indoor and outdoor temperature and relative humidity, map material moisture, and identify equipment by serial number. IICRC standards are technical references, not statutes. Suppose a $15,000 Base job contains $1,800 of drying charges and a missing daily log causes a $600 negotiated reduction. That single error consumes almost 31% of the modeled $1,950 owner profit. The fallback is prompt reconstruction from genuine timestamped field records and a documented correction, never invented readings. The third play, controlled dispatch and equipment utilization, protects purchased capacity. Saving eight unnecessary rental days at $45 per day preserves $360 before any related collection effect. Conversely, leaving equipment after documented drying completion creates cost without dependable billability. Dispatch through Jobber or a comparable calendar, connect pickup tasks to measured completion criteria, and reserve emergency coverage without counting standby capacity as productive labor. When internal capacity fails, use a priced subcontract package instead of making an uncosted overtime promise.

The fourth play, supported Xactimate supplements, prices actual scope changes using photographs, measurements, trade quotations, and the applicable local price list. On a $15,000 job, a $2,000 change requiring $1,200 of incremental delivery leaves $800 before additional overhead, financing, and loss exposure. At a modeled 70% collection probability, expected receipts are $1,400 and expected contribution is only $200; this calculation supports a decision, not revenue recognition by probability. Obtain an executed customer change order and identify the payment source before committing discretionary work. The fallback is to defer the optional addition or secure lawful customer funding, while performing necessary safety measures under the existing authorization. The fifth play, job-cost-led collections, pairs QuickBooks Online aging with signed completion evidence and mortgage-servicer release requirements. Moving a $15,000 receipt forward 30 days avoids approximately $148 of interest at a simple 12% annual borrowing rate. Contractors can explain their work and invoices without undertaking policy interpretation or negotiating coverage as an unlicensed public adjuster. Escalate coverage disputes through the customer's insurer contact or properly licensed representative, preserving the construction receivable as a separate contractual matter.

## 3. Finance the cash-conversion gap without spending tomorrow's payroll  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Profit and liquidity use different clocks. Days sales outstanding measures the time between invoicing and collection, but restoration also carries prebilling work, supplement negotiation, recoverable depreciation timing, and checks requiring multiple endorsements. For the simplified steady-state model here, use 75 days Conservative, 60 days Base, and 45 days Scaled. These are illustrative cash-conversion assumptions, not insurer payment commitments. Annual cash spending before owner distributions is $450,000, $1,275,000, and $2,000,000 after removing the respective $10,000, $30,000, and $50,000 noncash planning provisions from total modeled expenses. For this screening calculation, treat equipment depreciation as matched by cash replacement spending; a real forecast must separate both. Multiplying annual cash spending by collection days and dividing by 365 gives approximately $92,466, $209,589, and $246,575 of gross funding need. Receivables measured at invoice value are larger: approximately $102,740, $246,575, and $308,219. Do not finance the entire receivable at cost and then separately add its embedded profit. Neither calculation captures work performed before invoice issuance, so maintain a separate unbilled-work schedule for that exposure.

Credit only payment terms that suppliers have actually granted. If subcontractors and material vendors collectively provide 15 days of average payable support, eligible annual spending is $150,000 Conservative, $530,000 Base, and $845,000 Scaled. The resulting offsets are about $6,164, $21,781, and $34,726, leaving net steady-state operating float of $86,301, $187,808, and $211,849 using unrounded inputs. These balances exclude startup equipment purchases, deposits, taxes payable, seasonal surges, and a protected minimum cash balance. They also assume a stable production mix, which a storm event can destroy. The $75,000 low end of the startup capital range would not cover even the Conservative modeled float before equipment and reserves. Build a thirteen-week cash forecast with weekly payroll, vendor due dates, expected customer receipts, and available borrowing. A committed line is usable only to the extent its borrowing base accepts the receivables: disputed invoices, aged balances, and concentrated catastrophe exposure may be excluded. Customer deposits reduce funding need only when validly collected, contractually available, and not restricted by applicable home-improvement or trust-fund rules.

Model payout locks separately from ordinary aging. A $40,000 insurance check naming both homeowner and mortgage lender does not provide spendable cash merely because a claim portal says payment issued. If that job requires $28,000 of cash delivery before release, reserve the actual staged expenditures and obtain the lender's inspection, endorsement, and draw instructions. Never record the insurer's issue date as the contractor's bank receipt date. Across the whole portfolio, another 30 days of collection delay requires approximately $36,986 Conservative, $104,795 Base, and $164,384 Scaled before any additional payable relief. This is a liquidity shock, not automatically a profit loss; incremental interest and eventual credit losses are the profit effects. For perspective, financing the Base incremental balance for a full year at 12% costs about $12,575, whereas a shorter borrowing period costs proportionately less. Before authorizing owner distributions, reconcile the bank balance to restricted deposits, accrued payroll, vendor commitments, tax obligations, and the downside cash forecast. If that forecast breaches the protected balance, pause discretionary expansion and distributions rather than delaying wages or assuming an unsupported supplement will arrive next Friday.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly job-cost snapshot
   - Export open jobs, invoiced balances, unbilled work, committed purchase orders, and payroll obligations.
   - Tie every line to the shared job identifier and reconcile the receivable control account before forecasting collections.
2. Classify each receipt by actual release condition
   - Separate customer payments, insurer-issued checks, mortgage-held draws, and disputed supplements.
   - Use documented endorsement and inspection dates; an issued check without usable funds remains a collection dependency.
3. Build the thirteen-week downside cash schedule
   - Place cash expenditures on contractual due dates and delay modeled receipts by another thirty days.
   - Include borrowing-base exclusions, interest, tax payments, equipment purchases, and a protected payroll balance.
4. Release only genuinely available owner cash
   - Compare projected weekly balances with restricted funds and committed operating expenditures before distributions.
   - Reduce optional expansion or arrange verified funding when the downside schedule breaches the protected minimum.

## 4. Stress the reserve, subcontract pricing, and collection authority before scaling  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat scrub as a traceable taxonomy, not a convenient percentage hiding poor estimating. Separate unsupported quantities, duplicate equipment days, unauthorized scope, customer concessions, credit deterioration, warranty callbacks, and processor reversals where cards are accepted. A technical billing correction can require a credit memo; an unpaid valid receivable may require a credit-loss allowance; a callback usually belongs in delivery or warranty expense. The planning waterfall aggregates these risks for decision-making but the ledger must preserve their different accounting treatment. Review the originating cohort at 30, 60, 90, and 180 days after completion, including closed jobs that later reopen. The initial two-percent provision equals $200, $300, and $400 per modeled job. Raising expected loss to five percent consumes another $15,000, $45,000, and $75,000 annually, leaving owner profits of $25,000, $150,000, and $375,000. Do not release reserves solely because an adjuster verbally accepted a line item. Require documented settlement of the construction balance or observable collection history, and maintain a reserve roll-forward that connects opening balance, new expense, specific write-offs, recoveries, and closing balance.

Stress acquisition and subcontractor pricing independently before combining them. An added subcontract cost equal to five percentage points of gross revenue means $25,000, $75,000, and $125,000 of extra expense, reducing owner profit to $15,000, $120,000, and $325,000. This is a five-point revenue sensitivity, not a five-percent increase on the subcontractor bucket. Doubling the acquisition budgets adds $25,000, $75,000, and $100,000 and leaves $15,000, $120,000, and $350,000 respectively. Applying both shocks together with a reserve increase from two to five percent produces a $25,000 Conservative loss, zero Base profit, and $150,000 Scaled profit, before incremental financing costs. The apparent Scaled resilience depends on delivering its assumed job sizes and payroll productivity; it does not emerge automatically from adding a sixth employee. Track gross contribution per crew-day and per equipment-day alongside annual revenue. When storm demand raises subcontract bids, reprice future authorized scopes or restrict intake to capacity that meets the downside threshold. Do not recover a weak margin by manufacturing drying days, inflating quantities, or treating the customer's deductible as invisible.

Make statutory boundaries part of the cost model because violations create expenses that no estimating template can absorb. Florida Statutes section 626.854 restricts public-adjusting activity and defines the boundary relevant to contractors explaining their own bids; it does not authorize general policy negotiation by restoration staff. Texas Insurance Code section 707.002 requires payment of applicable property-insurance deductibles and restricts practices that help avoid them. Apply these rules in their jurisdictions rather than claiming a single national collection rule. For qualifying renovation work in pre-1978 target housing or child-occupied facilities, 40 CFR 745.85 establishes lead-safe work practice requirements, with specific treatment of emergency renovations. Price required containment, cleaning, and verification into the authorized scope instead of discovering them after mobilization. OSHA construction respiratory-protection requirements in 29 CFR 1926.103 incorporate 29 CFR 1910.134; medical evaluation, fit testing, and program administration belong in the applicable labor or overhead budget. Before entering a new market, verify contractor and remediation licenses, permitted collection communications, deductible practices, and safety costs against local requirements. A smaller, funded job with supported scope beats a larger disputed invoice that exhausts payroll cash.
