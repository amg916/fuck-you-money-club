## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Speed starts with search. EstimateTV teaches working from the Search tab rather than Macros. Type a term like paint and press return, and Xactimate returns the first 100 matches. The results include options such as one coat or two coats, a (V) marks a variable item, and a double-click adds the item to the room [sSaHviAzZ6w @ 00:01:33]. The faster route is the selector code box. Type a category code and press space, and every item in that category lists out. For example, type INS for insulation, then narrow with bat or foil. The codes to memorize are PNT for paint, DRY for drywall, RFG for roof and STG for siding [sSaHviAzZ6w @ 00:02:17]. The Restoration Advisors Group built a personal cheat sheet at scale. They selected all their estimates, exported them to PDF and combined two years of work into a single 15,000-page file. They then exported it to TXT, because Excel is not worth the bother, and use Ctrl-F on a term like Oak to surface every related item ever written [qQzYZWLmaSI @ 00:00:12]. That text later trained a chatbot on their website, which the speaker says is not going to replace estimate writing. Once a set of items works, save it. Adjuster TV notes that X1 desktop macros sync to mobile, so a macro built at the desk travels into the field [dtHSqRxkHLA @ 00:03:54].

The unit economics are concrete. Daughtry Construction Group's rule for drying equipment is that 'these pay per 24 hour period per air mover and per dehumidifier.' Two air movers running three days is therefore a quantity of 6, and setup, teardown and daily monitoring hours are charged on top [HLwvN9mF1R0 @ 00:01:05]. Debris belongs on all work, roofs included. It is measured in tons, by pickup load or by 12, 20, 30 and 40-yard dumpster, at Dallas prices that Daughtry says vary by location and by month [HLwvN9mF1R0 @ 00:01:56]. Project management is the line most often missed. Their Dallas rates run about $80 an hour commercial and $70 residential, and final cleaning comes in near 29 cents a square foot commercial and 37 cents residential [HLwvN9mF1R0 @ 00:04:43]. Canei Adjusting supplies the drywall arithmetic from the carrier side. A patch quantity of 32 equals one 4x8 sheet. Joint tape runs 8 feet, or 4 feet if the ceiling stain touches the wall. The board specified is 5/8 drywall [BQZjEuyEaq4 @ 00:03:24]. Because Canei writes from the adjuster seat, those quantities show exactly what a carrier-side estimator expects to see on a ceiling patch.

Finishing mechanics separate a draft from a deliverable, and three practitioners walk through the exact clicks. Claim Daddy reprices after dropping in any macro. They set depreciation at 7.5 years on the second floor and 5 on the first and let automatic labor minimums apply. The sample water loss lands at roughly a $30,000 claim carrying about $6,000 in depreciation, a file they say would take about an hour working solo [Qu-NqEwWWPk @ 02:40:15]. Crest LLC shows the bulk version: Ctrl-A, Global Changes, Depreciation, uncheck do not change age, and enter 15 years. Labor-only items are skipped automatically [ZY8afiQROiI @ 00:00:35]. Crest also shows what happens when a contractor's real number replaces the line items. The worked example is a roofer at $8,600 excluding tax and O&P, then a bid of $8,200. The existing lines are zeroed through Taxes, O&P and Repaired. An RFG BID item is added as tax exempt with the note 'per Joe's roofing invoice', and an exclude note closes the loop [ZY8afiQROiI @ 00:11:10]. Estimate Mastery's closing routine is to resequence line numbers so they run clean, print the Final Draft report, and lay out photos at two images per page with annotations [Vj-mEgtLqUw @ 00:19:28].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is between the contractor who writes high and the field adjuster who writes lean. Chad Michael of The Practitioner says carriers are 'gonna try to push me down at all times,' so he opens 'from the highest position so that i can end up at the highest position' [Lh586v6HcYw @ 00:14:28]. To him, a 100% approval is a warning sign that means 'what did I miss,' not a win [Lh586v6HcYw @ 00:14:10]. Canei Adjusting builds the opposite default from the carrier chair. Swap the floor protection cloth for a cheaper option because insurers prefer it, write standard insulation, and let the contractor submit a receipt to collect the upgrade difference [Z7kbWUDeX6A @ 00:02:43]. On ceilings, the same adjuster names plastic film as 'what most insurance companies will go for' and notes that contractors reach for costlier protection [BQZjEuyEaq4 @ 00:01:40]. Daughtry Construction Group lands closer to Chad. Anything necessary on the job can be charged, and on O&P for mitigation-only jobs, 'I'd rather ask and be turned down than to not ask at all' [HLwvN9mF1R0 @ 00:06:37]. The operator read is that carriers anchor low by default. A contractor estimate that mirrors the adjuster macro gives up the negotiation before the first email goes out, and it leaves nothing to trade back when the reviewer starts cutting.

Paint coats and O&P show the same rift line by line. Daughtry Construction Group writes 2 coats on new items and 1 coat on existing painted items being matched, and always charges mask and prep as separate lines [HLwvN9mF1R0 @ 00:04:07]. Canei Adjusting reports that carriers allow 1 coat for white ceilings and 2 for dark, while public adjusters and contractors push for 2 coats across the board [BQZjEuyEaq4 @ 00:10:29]. On O&P, Daughtry sets 10% overhead and 10% profit when three or more trades are involved. Daughtry calls it a fight on roofs and admits it is usually rejected on mitigation-only work [HLwvN9mF1R0 @ 00:06:18]. Estimate Mastery hard-codes O&P at 10/10 in the estimate parameters and loads an opening statement quoting the O&P white paper, so the justification rides on page one before any adjuster reads a line item [Vj-mEgtLqUw @ 00:10:56]. Canei Adjusting's claim-information walkthrough treats O&P as earned only when the job requires trade coordination [Sk9blhK0GiU @ 00:13:19]. In practice, Daughtry and Estimate Mastery put the argument inside the estimate itself, while the adjuster side waits to see whether coordination exists before releasing the 20%. Arm the file before the adjuster asks, because the roof and mitigation-only fights Daughtry describes are the default, not the exception.

The practitioners also split on how to move an adjuster. Chad Michael first tries to educate gently, even showing codes on a phone. If that fails, he pushes as far as possible, accepts the payment, and then goes back again [Lh586v6HcYw @ 00:01:18]. Claim Daddy warns from inside the carrier workflow that reviewers 'don't want you kind of trying to think you're educated or smart,' so you will have a much easier time just replacing drywall [Qu-NqEwWWPk @ 00:18:55]. On countertops, Daughtry Construction Group writes detach-and-reset first because insurers want it, then supplements for replacement if the top breaks [HLwvN9mF1R0 @ 00:03:15]. Claim Daddy counters that a contractor landing $5k over a $32k estimate in a 30-40k fee tier means no T&E for the adjuster, and that writing it right the first time is the fix. Graham of EstimateHub describes the middle path. When the contractor is 'a little bit more,' the parties reconcile by email or phone, and 'sometimes it does take a long time' [KeJzOY4eZHU @ 00:04:57].
