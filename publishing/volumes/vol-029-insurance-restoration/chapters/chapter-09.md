## 1. Assign Five Plays to Licensed Roles Before Accepting the Loss  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Organize Candidate #15 around five explicit plays: emergency water mitigation; fire, soot, and contents restoration; mold remediation; storm reconstruction; and evidence-led estimating, supplements, and collections. The fifth play supports completed or authorized restoration work; it is not a separate business selling claim representation. Assign the five full-time positions to an owner and operations lead, a project manager and estimator, a dispatch and receivables coordinator, and two field technicians. Use six contracted resources for licensed trades, specialty remediation, contents capacity, and surge labor, with assignments based on actual credentials rather than a universal subcontractor label. Contractor status also depends on applicable employment tests, not the wording of an agreement. Maintain a jurisdiction register showing contractor classifications, qualifying individuals, municipal registrations, permits, environmental credentials, renewal dates, and permitted activities. The dispatcher checks the property address and work category before offering a mobilization window. An IICRC credential demonstrates training but does not replace a state contractor license, mold license, or required trade license. For each play, define an accountable employee, a qualified backup, an evidence package, and a hard boundary on what the team may promise. This prevents emergency demand from silently expanding the firm's licensed scope during a regional catastrophe.

Separate repair expertise from insurance representation at the sentence level. A contractor can describe observed damage, proposed drying methods, quantities, equipment duration, and the price of their own services, subject to local restrictions. Interpreting coverage for the policyholder, negotiating their settlement, or advertising that the contractor will handle the claim can cross public-adjuster boundaries. Texas Insurance Code sections 4102.051 and 4102.163 are especially important: licensing governs public adjusting, and a contractor cannot act as public adjuster on property where the contractor provides or may provide contracting services. A separate license therefore does not automatically cure a dual-role conflict. Florida Statutes section 489.147 likewise restricts contractor conduct involving insurance claims and deductible practices. Train the estimator to say, 'Our estimate explains our repair scope and pricing; your insurer determines coverage, and you decide whether to engage an authorized representative.' Xactimate line items do not confer settlement authority. When an adjuster disputes a dehumidifier day, answer with moisture readings, equipment records, and the service rationale, not an interpretation of policy duties. Escalate coverage disputes to the owner for referral, and preserve the distinction in email subjects, engagement documents, call notes, and website copy.

Build state variations into dispatch rather than relying on one national form. Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1958 and Florida Statutes Part XVI of Chapter 468 regulate mold services, with different licensing, exemptions, and separation requirements. Check whether assessment and remediation must be independent before combining those services. California Business and Professions Code section 7159 imposes detailed requirements on covered home-improvement contracts, while other jurisdictions use different deposit, notice, and cancellation rules. Add federal overlays by task and building age. EPA's renovation rules in 40 CFR Part 745 apply to covered work disturbing painted surfaces in pre-1978 target housing and child-occupied facilities. Emergency provisions in section 745.82 narrow particular requirements only to the extent specified; they do not erase every cleaning, verification, or recordkeeping duty. OSHA asbestos, lead, and respiratory rules require an exposure-based work plan, not a checkbox attached to every loss. Commercial occupancy introduces tenant coordination, shutdown permissions, and potentially hazardous inventories that residential intake will miss. Require the project manager to classify occupancy, construction age, suspected materials, and access limitations before destructive work. The six contracted resources must supply applicable licenses and evidence of task-specific capability; a certificate of insurance alone does not establish either.

## 2. Contract for Measurable Work, Lawful Payment, and Preserved Customer Rights  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a document stack that follows the job's changing uncertainty. Start with an emergency service authorization identifying the property, contracting parties, immediate stabilization tasks, access permissions, equipment rates, minimum charges, spending ceiling, and the method for approving additional work. Then issue a reconstruction agreement only after the repair scope and required permits are sufficiently defined. Record whether pricing is fixed, unit-based, or time-and-materials; an insurer's estimate is evidence for discussion, not a substitute for customer agreement. Define the fee trigger as authorized services performed or a valid contractual milestone, subject to applicable deposit and consumer-contract rules. State when invoices become payable and distinguish customer payment obligations from insurer reimbursement decisions without inventing a universal pay-now clause. A change order should name the discovery, added quantity, revised price, schedule impact, and approving person. For drying, specify how daily monitoring, equipment removal, inaccessible areas, and interrupted power affect performance. For contents, attach the inventory, handling permissions, storage pricing, and release procedure. Never let a technician improvise a disposal authorization after salvageable property has already left the premises. Give the customer completed copies immediately, and preserve the signed version separately from editable estimating files for every subsequent billing review.

Apply cancellation and deductible rules to the actual sale, not to assumptions about disaster urgency. The FTC Cooling-Off Rule, 16 CFR Part 429, covers qualifying sales at homes and certain other locations, with thresholds, exclusions, required notices, and a three-business-day cancellation period. Its emergency exclusion requires specified conditions, including a buyer-initiated request and a separate handwritten emergency statement; a preprinted waiver tucked into an authorization is not equivalent. State rights may be broader, so use the jurisdiction-specific version and record the sale location and solicitation history. Texas Insurance Code Chapter 707 makes deductible payment a concrete operating issue for covered property claims. Do not inflate an invoice, hide a rebate, or describe a referral payment as a way to eliminate the customer's deductible. Record actual discounts transparently and assess their effect under the applicable rule before offering them. Distinguish a direction to pay from an assignment of benefits: labels do not control legal effect. Florida section 627.7152(13) prohibits covered post-loss benefit assignments under property policies issued on or after January 1, 2023. Verify policy issuance and statutory scope rather than deploying a legacy assignment template. Direct payment instructions must also respect mortgagee interests and check endorsements.

Write the protective clauses narrowly enough that operations can honor them. An anti-circumvention provision should address deliberate diversion of a specifically introduced subcontractor during an active project, if enforceable, rather than punish customers for contacting their insurer or selecting another contractor. Avoid liquidated charges unrelated to a defensible anticipated loss. Define liability allocation around controllable risks, insurance requirements, and expressly identified exclusions; do not assume a liability cap can excuse gross negligence, statutory duties, personal injury, or defective workmanship. Have local counsel evaluate enforceability and mandatory wording before release. A dispute clause should sequence project-level review, management escalation, mediation, and the selected forum without obscuring mandatory consumer remedies or lien deadlines. For data, distinguish ownership of project deliverables from the operator's estimating templates and methods. Give the customer a usable license to final reports and define access to photographs, psychrometric logs, inventories, and invoices. Obtain separate permission before using loss images in marketing. Limit vendor access and establish retention rules tied to contract, litigation, insurance, and statutory needs. Finally, align termination, safe demobilization, equipment retrieval, and undisputed payment provisions so the crew can exit without abandoning hazards or using customer property as improvised collection leverage during a contested final invoice.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Contract Details Experienced Restoration Operators Protect**
> 
> Write for the wet, occupied property and the disputed invoice, not just the clean estimate.
> - Make the equipment schedule identify serial number, room, deployment time, removal time, and the monitoring record supporting continued use.
> - Attach a customer power-interruption and access log so drying delays can be explained without inventing equipment utilization.
> - Keep emergency authorization, reconstruction approval, and marketing-image permission separate so one signature is not stretched across unrelated purposes.
> - Save the original Xactimate estimate and each revision with a change explanation; never overwrite the version the customer actually approved.

## 3. Sell Restoration Services Without Selling Coverage or Unlicensed Representation  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Make outreach channel-specific before making it persuasive. Commercial email must satisfy CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. section 7704, including accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, required identification, a valid postal address, and an effective opt-out process. Business recipients are not a blanket exemption. Calling and texting require a separate analysis under 47 U.S.C. section 227, FCC rules in 47 CFR section 64.1200, the FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule where applicable, and relevant state laws. Evaluate the number type, calling technology, message purpose, applicable consent, do-not-call status, and local time restrictions. Artificial or prerecorded voices introduce requirements that a manually dialed live call does not automatically share. Purchased storm leads are not proof that the person authorized this company and this channel. Keep the permission record, acquisition source, disclosure text, timestamp, and revocation history together. The coordinator should maintain a company-specific suppression list and process stop requests promptly under applicable deadlines. Use HubSpot or a comparable CRM to enforce suppression, not merely store a note that sales staff can overlook. Do not upload customer loss photographs or contact lists into advertising audiences without an established permission and data-use basis. Public disaster maps identify affected areas, not permission to contact individual occupants through every available channel.

Use a permission-based live introduction: 'Hello, this is Jordan with Harbor Restoration. We provide water mitigation and repair estimates in your area. Is this a suitable time to discuss your service request? We price our work; we do not determine your insurance coverage.' Substitute the real business identity and an accurate reason for contact; never imply a request exists when it does not. For inbound discovery, say: 'Are you the owner or authorized property representative? What happened, when did you first notice it, and is water or smoke exposure continuing? Is anyone facing an electrical, structural, or medical emergency? What is the building's approximate construction year, and has anyone tested suspect materials?' Stop commercial discovery when immediate emergency services are needed. Continue with access, occupancy, utilities, prior work, and photographed conditions rather than leading the customer toward a preferred cause of loss. For a property-manager introduction, use: 'We can review your emergency vendor requirements, response territory, and documentation standards. Would a short capability review help?' The owner handles account relationships while the coordinator qualifies dispatch. This keeps two field technicians from spending billable hours negotiating promises they lack authority to make. Record answers as customer statements, not independently verified findings, until the field inspection establishes the facts.

Prepare scripts for the moments most likely to create a claim dispute. For price and coverage, say: 'Our proposed authorization covers the services and rates shown here. Your insurer decides coverage under your policy. Please ask your insurer about your deductible and reimbursement; we will explain our scope and supporting measurements.' For a supplement, say to the adjuster: 'Attached are the room measurements, dated photographs, drying logs, and revised quantities supporting our repair estimate. Please identify any disputed service or quantity so we can address the factual difference.' Do not add demands about what the policy legally owes the insured. For collection, say: 'Invoice 1042 shows the authorized work, payments received, and remaining balance. Which charge or payment allocation would you like us to review? We can explain available payment arrangements without changing the invoice to conceal a deductible.' Ordinary collection of the contractor's own receivable generally differs from third-party debt collection, but state collection laws and the federal definition still require review, especially when using another name or outside collectors. For opt-out, say: 'I have recorded your request to stop marketing contact.' Separate necessary project communications from advertising, and never turn a safety update into a disguised promotional message.

## 4. Turn Documentation, Collections, and Safety Controls Into Preserved Margin  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the evidence package as a production deliverable for all five plays. Water mitigation requires initial conditions, moisture maps, temperature and relative humidity readings, relevant psychrometric calculations, equipment placement, daily progress, and a defensible drying endpoint. IICRC S500 supplies a professional framework, but project facts must support the actual method and duration. Fire and contents work needs condition photographs, item identification, custody records, and documented cleaning or disposal decisions. Mold work needs the applicable assessment, work protocol, containment records, and required verification without presenting a conflicted self-check as independent clearance. Storm reconstruction needs measurements, material specifications, permits, hidden-condition photographs, and signed changes. The fifth play assembles these records into Xactimate explanations and accurate receivables follow-up. Use CompanyCam or Encircle for organized field capture, then export essential evidence into controlled storage with role-based access and backups. Record corrections as new entries rather than rewriting dates or measurements. The project manager reviews exceptions daily; the owner samples closed files weekly. Missing logs should trigger an honest explanation and process correction, not reconstructed readings presented as contemporaneous evidence. This discipline protects factual credibility when a carrier, customer, expert, or court later examines why a specific service was necessary.

Reconcile contract discipline to the canonical economics without treating the insurance claim as operator revenue. Conservative gross restoration billings are $500,000 at an 8% net margin, producing $40,000 annual owner net profit; Base is $1,500,000 at 13.0%, producing $195,000; Scaled is $2,500,000 at 18%, producing $450,000. Subcontractor and material costs are already reflected in those margins. The first two cases use 2 FT + 2 C; Scaled uses 3 FT + 3 C, thirteen people. The implied annual expense envelopes are $460,000, $1,305,000, and $2,050,000 respectively. Track earned billings, collected cash, disputed amounts, subcontractor commitments, and customer deposits separately. An approved insurance payment is neither automatically earned revenue nor cash available to the restoration firm. As an illustrative timing calculation, Base billings average $125,000 monthly; a thirty-day collection delay can leave roughly that amount additionally tied up when billing is even and other timing is unchanged. That illustration does not revise canonical profit. Use a thirteen-week cash forecast to distinguish collection delays from actual margin losses. Do not stack the stated monthly operating range onto scenario expenses without checking cost classifications, or materials and subcontractors may be counted twice.

Run disputes through a controlled evidence and deadline workflow. The coordinator classifies each objection as authorization, quantity, workmanship, coverage, payment allocation, or customer hardship; the project manager owns factual scope responses, and the owner handles escalation. Preserve preliminary notice, lien, bond-claim, and contract deadlines in a separate state-specific calendar because informal negotiation does not reliably extend them. Do not threaten liens without checking eligibility, required notices, amounts, and filing rules. Refer coverage advocacy outside the contractor role, and use qualified local support for contested collection proceedings. Review safety incidents with the same evidence discipline: isolate the hazard, protect workers and occupants, preserve records, notify appropriate parties, and correct the work method before restarting affected tasks. Evaluate required OSHA reporting and recordkeeping rather than assuming the subcontractor absorbs every duty. Before adding another state or catastrophe zone, test license coverage, contract readiness, safe staffing, vendor capacity, and cash runway together. Dispatch automation, photo documentation, and job-cost tracking should reduce missed handoffs, not manufacture extra field capacity. The four-person pod remains constrained by supervision and specialist availability. Expansion makes sense when the existing five plays close with defensible records, predictable collections, and manageable rework, not merely when lead volume rises after a storm.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify the Property and Permitted Role
   - Dispatch identifies jurisdiction, occupancy, service play, customer authority, and immediate hazards.
   - The owner confirms required credentials and prohibits claim-representation promises before the coordinator issues the correct contract package.
2. Authorize and Document the Work
   - Obtain the applicable signed authorization, required notices, pricing terms, and spending ceiling.
   - Technicians capture baseline conditions; the project manager approves the method, hazardous-material precautions, and required specialty resources.
3. Support Changes and Invoice Actual Performance
   - Link each invoice and estimate revision to authorized quantities, field evidence, and actual services.
   - The estimator addresses repair facts without negotiating policy coverage; the coordinator records transparent discounts and payment allocation.
4. Resolve Exceptions and Preserve the File
   - Classify disputes, calendar statutory deadlines, reconcile receipts, and export the final evidence package.
   - The owner reviews unresolved exposure and cash timing while the project manager verifies completion, safe demobilization, and outstanding corrective work.
