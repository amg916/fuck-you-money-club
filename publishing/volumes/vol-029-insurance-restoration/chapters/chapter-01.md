## 1. Define the contractor business before accepting the claim  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Insurance restoration is a field-service and construction business that stabilizes damaged buildings, removes affected materials, cleans or restores recoverable property, and reconstructs authorized assemblies. Its demand often follows water escape, fire, microbial growth, or wind damage, but insurance is a possible payment source rather than the product sold. The customer buys measured work: extraction, containment, equipment deployment, cleaning, demolition, drying, and rebuilding. A homeowner authorization, commercial work order, or properly structured program agreement establishes who ordered that work and who owes payment. An insurer's adjuster evaluates the claim; the contractor records conditions, estimates its services, and substantiates its invoices. Those functions overlap in the job file without becoming interchangeable. Begin every intake by identifying the property owner, authorized signer, affected spaces, immediate hazards, payer expectations, and access constraints. A $900,000 house with a $120,000 insurance claim might generate only a $24,000 mitigation contract for this operator. Neither the building value nor the entire claim is contractor revenue. Temporary housing, customer-owned contents, lender-held funds, and another contractor's reconstruction proceeds remain outside that contract. This boundary determines useful sales metrics, credit exposure, estimating responsibilities, and whether the business has actually earned its reported production.

The five mapped plays in this chapter are water mitigation, fire and contents recovery, mold remediation, storm reconstruction, and rank_and_rent_pay_per_call_routing. The first four are restoration delivery archetypes; the fifth is a distinct customer-acquisition or lead-selling model, not restoration production disguised as software. A water operator turns emergency availability and drying competence into authorized equipment and labor charges. A fire specialist earns through cleaning decisions, contents custody, odor treatment, and coordinated repair. A mold specialist sells controlled remediation against a defined scope, with assessment and verification separated where required. A storm rebuilder manages temporary protection, roof or envelope repair, subcontractors, and permit-dependent reconstruction. The routing operator sells qualified introductions and has no automatic entitlement to the resulting repair contract. Choose one delivery wedge before combining these operations. Each requires different equipment, training, scheduling, and working capital. IICRC S500, S520, and S700 provide relevant professional frameworks for water, mold, and fire work; they are not substitutes for jurisdiction-specific licensing or adopted requirements. A technician credential does not license a construction company, authorize public adjusting, or establish coverage. Durable positioning states exactly which deliverables the team can complete safely, document consistently, and collect without improvising contractual authority.

Treat gross restoration billings as the scorecard's revenue basis, while recognizing that sending an invoice does not alone establish earned revenue. Under an applicable accrual policy, recognize consideration for satisfied performance obligations, using an appropriate progress measure when over-time criteria are met. Deposits for unperformed work remain liabilities; unapproved scope additions and disputed supplements need support before entering recognized revenue. If the operator controls the promised restoration service as principal, subcontractor and material expenses remain costs beneath gross revenue rather than being silently netted against sales. If an arrangement is genuinely only an agency service, assess that arrangement separately instead of counting another firm's contract value. For example, a completed $20,000 principal-contractor job with $7,000 subcontract labor, $3,000 materials, and $4,000 direct crew and equipment cost produces $6,000 before allocated overhead and other expenses. It does not produce a $20,000 owner distribution. Conversely, a $200 referral fee on that job is $200 of lead-business revenue, not $20,000. Build separate ledger fields for earned contract value, invoices, customer deposits, receivables, cash receipts, direct costs, and disputed amounts. That separation exposes businesses that appear profitable only because customer advances or unpaid vendor bills temporarily inflate the bank balance.

## 2. Select a sub-vertical using evidence burden and failure exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Water mitigation rewards speed only when speed preserves a defensible drying plan. Record source conditions, affected materials, initial moisture mapping, ambient temperature, relative humidity, and equipment placement before the site changes. Use psychrometric calculations to interpret humidity ratio and drying conditions; surface readings and room humidity answer different questions. Establish material-specific drying goals against suitable unaffected comparisons rather than declaring every assembly dry at one universal percentage. Equipment placement, containment, and removal decisions should follow the evolving conditions, not a fixed desire to bill more days. In an illustrative month, ten completed $8,000 jobs produce $80,000 gross revenue. If direct delivery consumes $48,000, the remaining $32,000 must still absorb dispatch, vehicles, rent, insurance, administration, and other expenses. It is contribution before those expenses, not the chapter's annual owner profit. A denied extension of equipment days can erase that cushion quickly. The fallback is a shorter, evidence-supported drying sequence, timely equipment reassignment, and early communication about materials that cannot be economically dried. Use Encircle for field records and Xactimate for scoped estimating, but audit the underlying observations. Software can make a weak moisture record attractive without making it persuasive, accurate, or technically useful.

Fire, mold, and storm projects each concentrate a different tail risk. Fire work can lose money through contents custody failures, repeated odor treatment, or an optimistic cleaning-versus-replacement decision. Four completed $25,000 projects create $100,000 gross revenue; $70,000 of direct production leaves $30,000 before overhead, with one extensive re-clean threatening the entire contribution. Test representative materials and secure specific contents authorizations before committing warehouse capacity. Mold remediation is not simply water extraction with more plastic: containment, pressure relationships, worker protection, removal methods, and an appropriate verification endpoint must fit the assessed conditions. Six $10,000 jobs yield $60,000 revenue; if direct work costs $42,000, $18,000 remains before overhead. When concealed conditions expand, stop the affected activity, preserve containment, and obtain an authorized scope revision. Storm reconstruction carries weather exposure, permit sequencing, subcontractor coordination, and material-price risk. Five $40,000 completed contracts yield $200,000 revenue; $150,000 direct cost leaves $50,000 before overhead. Stage purchases and labor commitments around access, permits, and agreed payment milestones rather than an adjuster's verbal optimism. These examples explain unit economics, not additional annual scenarios. Their contributions cannot be added to the canonical scorecard without also adding the associated capacity, overhead, and collection burden.

The sub-vertical matrix distinguishes services that share a customer from services that share production economics. A commercial water loss may offer larger invoices but require overnight work, security coordination, tenant communication, and more equipment than a residential crew can responsibly deploy. A contents pack-out may look labor-light in the estimate while generating months of storage custody and repeated handling. Roofing may produce attractive contract values while exposing the operator to fall hazards, weather-sensitive scheduling, and lien-release administration. Evaluate each lane through accepted scope, measurable production units, evidence requirements, callback probability, and days to collectible cash. The platforms named below support measurements, records, or routing; they do not confer insurer acceptance, technical competence, or preferred-provider status. Fee descriptions are operator contract structures, not vendor subscription quotes or universal carrier allowances. Separate customer-requested upgrades from loss-related work and obtain distinct authorization before installation. Track denied line items and callbacks by cause rather than writing them off as general claim friction. A recurring omission in photos calls for field coaching; recurring unsupported scope calls for estimating discipline. Enter an adjacent lane only after the team can explain its failure triggers and fund a realistic correction without using deposits from unrelated unfinished jobs.

## 3. Reconcile owner profit with capacity, collections and call economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical Conservative case is $500,000 gross restoration revenue at an 8% net margin, producing $40,000 annual owner net profit with 5 full-time staff and 6 contractors. Base is $1,500,000 at 13.0%, producing $195,000 with the same headcounts. Scaled is $2,500,000 at 18%, producing $450,000 with 6 full-time staff and 7 contractors, or 13 people. The implied annual total costs are therefore $460,000, $1,305,000, and $2,050,000. These costs already include subcontractor and material expense within the margin calculation. Do not deduct them twice or present gross contract value as subcontractor-free revenue. Using an illustrative $20,000 average earned contract, the cases require 25, 75, and 125 completed contract equivalents annually. Actual mixes will contain smaller mitigation jobs and larger rebuilds. Contractor headcount does not describe hours purchased, and the Conservative staffing burden requires an explicit wage, utilization, and benefits budget before hiring. Preserve the scorecard numbers while testing whether local costs can support them. The $18,000–$75,000 startup range, $4,000–$20,000 monthly operating-expense range, and 1–5 months to first deal are planning inputs, not proof of funded capacity. Reconcile the monthly expense definition against total annual costs rather than treating its upper endpoint as a ceiling on all production spending.

Cash conversion can break a profitable operator because crews and suppliers demand payment before the final claim dispute is resolved. At the Base revenue run rate, 60 days of receivables represents approximately $246,575 using $1,500,000 divided by 365 and multiplied by 60. That is an illustrative gross receivable balance, not the exact cash reserve required: deposits, payment timing, taxes, vendor terms, and production costs change the reserve calculation. Build a weekly thirteen-week cash forecast using expected receipts by invoice and committed disbursements by job. Separate undisputed balances from contested supplements so a plausible collection date does not conceal a coverage dispute. A lender's endorsement requirement or recoverable-depreciation process can delay customer access to funds even when work is complete. Ask about those mechanics during contracting instead of discovering them after payroll. Invoice authorized completed work promptly, attach the evidence packet, and maintain a clear customer balance that reflects actual payments and lawful deductible obligations. Do not fund growth by assuming every supplement will be accepted at face value. Track disputed receivables by age, reason, and responsible next action. Where work can be safely staged, pause unfunded discretionary phases under the contract rather than extending involuntary credit across an expanding reconstruction scope.

The TRACK_2 play rank_and_rent_pay_per_call_routing builds local service hubs and routes calls through Ringba or Twilio to participating operators. Its specified commercial unit is $75–$300 for an inbound call lasting over 90 seconds, but duration alone should not define acceptance: document geography, requested service, duplicates, spam exclusions, operating hours, and buyer availability. Forty accepted calls at $150 produce $6,000 lead revenue. With illustrative monthly traffic, routing, and support costs of $2,400, contribution is $3,600 before remaining overhead and taxes. That result belongs to the lead operation and is not an extra profit line inside the restoration scorecard. If an affiliated restoration company buys the calls, eliminate intercompany charges when consolidating results. Anchor acquisition in organic city and calculator pages so a Google Business Profile suspension does not automatically remove that traffic source; organic rankings still face algorithm and indexing risk. Use Maps profiles only for eligible real businesses with verified partner locations, never fabricated offices. Enforce prepaid auto-refill wallets, low-balance alerts, spending limits, and automatic routing pauses before balances run out. For disputed calls, preserve attribution and permissible qualification evidence, issue contractual credits where justified, and disable buyers who repeatedly challenge clearly accepted units.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the job-level revenue boundary
   - Record the contracting entity, customer, authorized scope, payment terms, and deposit balance.
   - Keep total claim value and property value in reference fields that cannot feed recognized revenue.
2. Match evidence to earned production
   - Link daily work records, measured quantities, approved changes, and completion milestones to billing lines.
   - Evaluate disputed supplements separately instead of automatically recognizing every estimate revision.
3. Close costs before reporting margin
   - Accrue crew time, subcontract invoices, materials, equipment costs, disposal, and allocated operating expenses.
   - Compare the resulting annual totals with the canonical 8%, 13.0%, and 18% scenario margins.
4. Refresh the thirteen-week cash forecast
   - Update invoice-level receipt dates, disputed balances, deposits, payroll, and committed supplier payments weekly.
   - Assign a named owner and next action to every overdue balance without assuming insurer payment.

## 4. Build defensible operating moats without becoming an unlicensed adjuster  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

UPPA means the unauthorized practice of public adjusting, and the operational boundary is narrower than many sales scripts suggest. A restoration contractor can describe observed damage, explain its methods, support its own pricing, and seek payment for authorized work within applicable law. Acting for the insured to interpret coverage or negotiate settlement can require a separate public-adjuster license, and some jurisdictions restrict combining adjusting with repair interests. Texas Insurance Code §4102.163 specifically addresses the contractor conflict; Florida Statutes §489.147 restricts contractor insurance-claim conduct. Train estimators to say what the evidence supports: affected area, necessary access, documented labor, material quantities, and the reason for a changed scope. Do not promise that a carrier will pay, offer unlawful deductible inducements, or present a contractor's assignment document as universal authority to control the claim. Texas Insurance Code §707.002 requires payment of applicable property-policy deductibles. Collections should distinguish the customer's contract obligation from unresolved insurer decisions and should escalate policy interpretation to the insured's appropriately authorized representative. Preserve delivery receipts and notices, but verify local lien eligibility and deadlines before relying on lien rights. A factual supplement is a construction record; advocacy over coverage is a different service with different authority.

Safety is a production requirement because a single uncontrolled exposure can overwhelm several profitable jobs. The employer duties in 29 U.S.C. §654 sit alongside task-specific OSHA standards, including construction asbestos requirements in 29 CFR 1926.1101 and fall protection in 29 CFR 1926.501. Respiratory protection must follow the applicable program requirements; issuing disposable masks is not a substitute for hazard assessment, medical evaluation, and fit testing where required. Before destructive work, identify materials, building age, occupancy, utilities, structural conditions, and sampling needs. The EPA renovation work-practice requirements in 40 CFR 745.85 apply to covered renovation activities in target housing and child-occupied facilities, with specific applicability and emergency provisions that require review. Suspected asbestos or lead hazards should change the work sequence before demolition exposes occupants and crews. Mold licensing and assessment-remediation separation also vary; Florida Statutes §468.8419 supplies concrete restrictions for regulated mold services. Build qualified specialist relationships so the fallback is controlled isolation and an orderly handoff, not improvised removal. Stop the affected activity when conditions exceed training or authorization, document why, and preserve safe site control. This protects the customer and prevents a low-margin job from becoming an open-ended remediation liability.

The durable small-team moat is an operating system that converts field observations into accurate decisions, collectible invoices, and fewer callbacks. Dispatch automation should route the right crew to the right hazard and geography, not merely shorten response time. Photo documentation should enforce location, timestamp, and scope linkage rather than reward hundreds of unlabeled images. Job-cost tracking should expose labor variance and missing subcontract commitments before the final invoice. Connect these three scorecard automation levers through a stable job identifier across Encircle, Xactimate, scheduling, and accounting records, using supported exports or integrations rather than assuming every product synchronizes automatically. Audit a sample of closed jobs monthly for authorization completeness, moisture evidence, equipment duration, scope changes, gross margin, collection age, and repeat visits. Compare sales sources by collected contribution after callbacks and lead costs, not by booked claim value. Fragile beginner hype celebrates catastrophe demand, oversized estimates, and nominal contractor networks without checking who can actually supervise the work. A resilient operator declines unsafe volume, reserves cash for disputed balances, and expands only after existing crews produce reproducible results. With a 87/100 score and last-place pipeline rank, this is a disciplined execution business, not a low-labor arbitrage opportunity.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify authority, safety, evidence and cash before dispatch**
> 
> Release the crew only when the job has a documented operating boundary. Emergency conditions may change sequencing, but they do not create unlimited scope or payment authority.
> - Verify the signer can authorize property work, the contracting entity holds required credentials, and the scope does not assign unlicensed adjusting duties.

> - Verify baseline photos, moisture mapping where relevant, equipment identifiers, and the signed scope can be retrieved under one job identifier.
> - Verify deposit handling, deductible responsibility, available working cash, and a collection plan that does not depend on an unsupported coverage promise.
