## 1. Assign the four-person pod before accepting the loss  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with five mapped plays, not five independent departments: water mitigation, fire and smoke restoration, mold remediation, storm reconstruction, and documented supplements with collections. These are this chapter's operating labels for Candidate #15's domain, not additional revenue streams layered onto its scorecard. The Base pod contains an owner-estimator, a dispatcher who also owns receivables, a field lead, and two restoration technicians. Six recurring contractors supply electrical, plumbing, roofing, drywall, painting, and qualified remediation capacity; their fees remain job costs. Assign each subcontracted scope to a named business, verified license where required, insurance record, purchase order, and scheduled inspection. The three-person internal field crew cannot simultaneously run three complex containment projects simply because a dashboard shows available leads. The dispatcher reserves labor hours, equipment days, and contractor windows before promising arrival. The owner controls estimating and unusual scope decisions; the field lead controls physical release of work areas. Collection responsibility never migrates informally to whichever technician answers the phone. This division supports ten completed jobs monthly in the worked example only if actual scope and measured capacity support that throughput; it is a planning assumption, not an industry productivity benchmark.

Intake begins with property address, responsible customer, loss date, observed hazard, source status, access authority, and referral origin. Create one lead identifier before dispatch and retain the original call record under a limited access policy. A severity score ranks response urgency; it does not decide insurance coverage or replace a safety assessment. An active electrical hazard, structural instability, suspected asbestos disturbance, or uncontrolled water source changes the deployment plan immediately. Document who isolated utilities and which specialist authorized the relevant condition before technicians enter. For water losses, collect unaffected comparison readings, material moisture measurements, temperature, relative humidity, and equipment placement. Record meter model, measurement location, timestamp, and the technician responsible. IICRC S500 and S520 inform technical practice, but neither is a universal statutory license or an insurer payment commitment. Fire work adds residue classification, odor pathways, contents custody, and cleaning tests. Mold work adds containment design and jurisdiction-specific assessment requirements. Storm work adds temporary weatherproofing, roof access controls, and permit sequencing. Each branch rejoins the same signed scope, documented production, invoice, and collection workflow rather than creating a disconnected spreadsheet.

Contract with the property customer for described restoration services, payment terms, change orders, and access; do not sell a promised claim outcome. Florida provides a useful concrete boundary: section 626.854 regulates public adjusting and distinguishes explaining a contractor's bid from prohibited claim representation. Section 489.147 restricts specified contractor insurance practices, including deductible-waiver inducements. Apply the actual property's jurisdiction before using any script. The estimator can explain measured quantities, drying duration, labor categories, and omitted construction work without interpreting coverage or negotiating the insured's policy rights. Refer policy disputes to the customer's appropriately licensed representative. Before disturbing painted surfaces in covered pre-1978 housing, evaluate EPA renovation requirements under 40 CFR 745.85 and applicable exceptions; emergency work does not erase every subsequent obligation. Evaluate asbestos construction requirements under 29 CFR 1926.1101 before demolition, and respiratory protection duties under 29 CFR 1910.134 when respirator use requires them. These checks determine crew selection, containment, scheduling, and cost. They belong in the production plan, not in an improvised footer after the customer has already authorized demolition.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Register and triage
   - Create a stable lead key and assign one primary operator play.
   - Capture authority and hazards before ranking urgency; reject out-of-area or unqualified work with a recorded reason.
2. Verify and mobilize
   - Link immutable authorization, baseline, and safety evidence before production.
   - The field lead confirms crew capacity and specialty requirements, then advances the versioned job state.
3. Produce and substantiate
   - Capture daily technical records and customer-authorized scope changes.
   - Separate equipment deployment evidence from drying necessity and explain every supplement through measured work.
4. Bill and reconcile
   - Post earned invoices, verified bank receipts, and paid operating costs.
   - Track lender holds separately, resolve unmatched receipts, and close only fully reconciled jobs with released holds.

## 2. Build a serialized evidence ledger with explicit replay semantics  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use the six modules below as one copy-pasteable reference program: concatenate the code cells in numeric order into restoration_ledger.py and execute it with Python 3.9 or later. Imports remain entirely within the standard library. The program creates an isolated test database, exercises the cohort, and prints its reconciled totals only when its assertions pass. It is a narrow operational subledger, not a complete accounting package. Each modeled job receives one earned final invoice; no customer advance, tax collection, retainage liability, credit memo, or unpaid supplier balance enters this example. Add separately balanced accounts and corresponding tests before extending those boundaries. The amount helper requires positive integer cents, avoiding binary floating point in postings. Invoice totals represent gross restoration billings, including the subcontracted production recovered through customer prices. Equipment purchase funding, owner distributions, and borrowing belong outside the example's operating cash bridge. Artifact hashes point to separately retained evidence; a digest alone proves neither customer authority nor technical adequacy. The reviewer must examine the underlying document before recording its digest. Store those documents in access-controlled project storage, not in unrestricted ledger exports.

Every mutation acquires SQLite's write reservation with BEGIN IMMEDIATE before reading business state. A unique request key stores a hash of the complete command and its committed response in the same transaction as the posting. An identical retry receives that response; reuse with changed arguments fails. Unique lead, invoice, bank, and expense references provide a second defense against duplicate economic events submitted under different request keys. Version checks reject stale state transitions instead of silently overwriting another worker's decision. Receipt limits are checked inside the same serialized transaction that inserts cash, preventing concurrent workers from overcollecting against the modeled invoice. These properties eliminate write races within this reference's single authoritative database and prescribed functions; they do not make unrelated email, bank, or field systems transactional. Keep the database on a local filesystem with one authoritative host, restrict direct writes, and use SQLite-aware backups. Do not deploy this WAL design on a shared network drive. A busy timeout becomes a visible failed attempt; the caller may retry the identical command after contention clears. No network request or physical dispatch occurs inside a ledger transaction.

Verification and escrow tracking remain different concepts. The workflow moves from intake to verified, working, invoiced, and closed; authorization, baseline, and safety hashes are required before verification. A separate hold state tracks clear, held, and released lender-controlled proceeds. The contractor does not become an escrow custodian merely by recording that state. The conservative reference blocks receipts while a job is held and blocks closure until release; actual mixed held and unrestricted payments require an instrument-level extension. A release letter changes status, while a bank-confirmed receipt changes cash. Never post the same amount for both events. Monthly views calculate revenue, paid costs, profit, receivables, and operating cash movement from source postings. The cohort tests check those identities and exercise duplicate delivery, changed payload rejection, stale-version rejection, and held-payment rejection. They are executable checks supplied for the operator to run, not a claim that this chapter has executed them. Before connecting dispatch notifications, add a transactional outbox and a recipient-side deduplication contract; without both, a crash between sending and acknowledging can repeat an external action.

## 3. Turn technical production into collectible, reviewable scope  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The field lead runs a daily production review by exception, not by counting uploaded photographs. For a water job, compare successive material readings at the same locations, ambient conditions, equipment configuration, and the documented drying objective. A photograph of an air mover establishes placement only when its location and date are clear; it does not independently establish necessary runtime. If readings plateau, examine source control, concealed assemblies, vapor barriers, air movement, and dehumidifier performance before adding days. Fire work requires representative cleaning tests and a defensible restore-versus-replace decision for each affected material class. Mold work requires containment and remediation records consistent with the assigned assessment and local licensing boundaries. Storm reconstruction requires measurements, material selections, permit events, concealed-condition photographs, and inspection results before covering work. The dispatcher reviews missing records before the crew leaves the property, when a correction remains cheap. One missing cavity photograph can later force an expensive return visit or make a supplement unprovable. Preserve originals and revisions rather than editing a historical report until it agrees with an estimate. Evidence quality reduces collection friction without manufacturing a different physical history.

Build the estimate from observed work and the customer-authorized scope, then map it into Xactimate line items with the appropriate locality and price-list date. Record unit definitions, dimensions, waste assumptions, equipment duration, and trade dependencies. A supplement packet should explain the original condition, newly discovered fact, incremental work, customer authorization, and supporting quantity. It should not consist of a larger total accompanied by an accusation that an insurer underpaid. Review tax treatment, permit charges, overhead, profit, and subcontractor inclusions consistently; avoid recovering the same cost through both a separate line and an embedded allowance. The owner's weekly review compares sold scope, purchase commitments, labor hours, and likely completion cost. Margin erosion first appears as unpriced additional work, unscheduled return trips, idle equipment, or a trade invoice without a matching purchase order. Job-cost tracking should expose those differences before invoicing. In the reference model, the final invoice is posted only after the modeled work is earned and its amount established. Real progress billing needs an explicit earned-revenue policy and separate advance handling; an insurer's reserve or preliminary estimate is not operator revenue.

Collections use an invoice-centered script: identify the service supplied, the contractual balance, the payment instrument, the customer's required action, and the next dated follow-up. When a check includes a mortgage lender, obtain the lender's actual endorsement and inspection process instead of predicting its release date. Keep held proceeds in the status register until funds reach the contractor's bank and can be allocated to the invoice. A claim discussion must stay within the contractor's permitted role; explaining measured construction scope differs from representing the insured on coverage or settlement. Do not disguise a deductible waiver as a marketing credit. The dispatcher reviews aging twice weekly, separating missing documents, customer disputes, lender holds, and unapplied bank receipts. The owner handles scope disagreements and authorizes economically justified corrections through a documented process. Never delete a disputed invoice or silently shorten equipment days simply to make the aging report cleaner. The supplied ledger intentionally lacks credit and write-off commands; extend it with immutable adjusting entries before processing either. A zero bad-debt assumption in the cohort means collections remain collectible in the example, not that restoration receivables carry no loss risk.

> 
> Experienced operators make evidence easy to retrieve while the crew still remembers the work.
> - Photograph the meter reading and measurement location together, then reuse the same location identifier on every drying visit.
> - Capture concealed-condition dimensions before closure and attach them directly to the affected Xactimate line-item explanation.

> - Separate equipment placement, continued drying necessity, and removal evidence so a reviewer can trace every billed equipment day.
