## The Practitioner Consensus

Every practitioner on the roofing side starts from the same premise: the first carrier estimate is short, and the gap is systematic rather than accidental. Becca Switzer of Roof Sales Mastery frames it as carrier math, a State Farm saving of $1,000 on each of 500,000 claims, and tells contractors to supplement 100% of claims because a file never comes back with everything paid [jj-GWZEtOc0 @ 00:02:33]. The same channel's sales call runs the math at 100,000 claims times $1,000 for $100M, while the contractor-side version is 100 claims times $10,000 for $1M [q63afBPc6eY @ 00:02:12]. Adjusters are described as paid to mitigate loss, with one Liberty Mutual adjuster's card literally reading "loss mitigation," so objections are expected as part of the job rather than taken personally [sICOGLUjsao @ 00:13:18]. Chad Michael adds the operational layer: start the dumpster, wet drywall removal, materials, and permits to "plant our flag" and keep the claim moving instead of waiting on the carrier [4dhIe6HGIs0 @ 00:16:20]. Even the most accuracy-focused shop, American Roof Supplements, opens its sample file with a carrier estimate of about $12,000 carrying "10 smaller things" wrong, not one dramatic miss, which is exactly the kind of leakage the volume-driven channels build their entire pitch around [HpoMhPz1KfU @ 00:00:52].

The second point of agreement is that a supplement is built line by line against the carrier's own estimate, with evidence attached to every difference. American Roof Supplements lays the two estimates side by side, lists existing quantity, proposed quantity, and evidence for each line, labels photos by roof location, strips out unsupported upgrades, then runs a "Show me why" test on the whole file [HpoMhPz1KfU @ 00:08:20]. Its beginner workflow runs seven steps: get the carrier estimate, gather evidence, compare line by line, identify legitimate differences, build or revise with notes, organize documentation, and submit and respond [TkCEjbO6vQs @ 00:04:30]. Chad Michael works the same direction, reconciling from the top of the carrier estimate downward, backed by a PDF of codes and photos [4dhIe6HGIs0 @ 00:02:16]. American Roof Supplements reduces every roof component to four questions, namely what it is, where it is, how it is measured, and what documents it, and uses that drill to keep eave footage separate from rake footage, count pipe boots that aerials hide behind dormers, and avoid confusing the ridge cap with the vent underneath it [DR03Zlogfe0 @ 00:00:48]. The shared rule under all four voices is blunt: measurements drive quantity changes, photos drive damage items, cited requirements drive code items, and anything discovered at tear-off gets photographed before it is covered back up.

Practitioners also agree on how the file should look and how hard to push. Estimate Mastery bolds every changed line and adds a note explaining why its line items differ from the original statement of loss, so the reviewer sees the argument without hunting for it [q6R8Iom5Uz8 @ 00:13:44]. Nobody recommends trench warfare over small items. Chad Michael concedes roughly $100 items on purpose to build rapport and keeps phone calls with adjusters friendly rather than combative [bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:28:20]. Becca Switzer is "not teaching you guys to argue for fifteen days over a turtle vent" and calls $5,800 received against a $6,700 ask good enough to close [jj-GWZEtOc0 @ 00:18:01]. The homeowner has to be briefed before any of this starts. Adam Bensman of The STRONG Roofer explains the supplement at color selection at the latest, reframes price as an objective assessment, walks through Xactimate as the third-party platform insurers use, and pitches the evidence packet as protection from surprise costs that "doesn't change a darn thing for you" [zxGa72oAf0w @ 00:02:17]. The warning attached is sharp: homeowners left without a narrative invent one, decide the contractor is a "slime ball" chasing free money, and the job ends with uncollected money and bad reviews instead of referrals.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The build itself runs on macros. Alina Wilson of Estimate Mastery keeps over 120 macros and says they are the only way to write a full roof and siding estimate in 10 to 15 minutes, because the search bar is slow and extra characters pull up the wrong item [fdW0W_-MR-s @ 00:01:00]. The rules are specific: one trade per macro, double-click to load it into the item or quick-entry screen, fill calculator fields from the roof report including waste, and treat the loaded macro as a checklist, deleting lines like a gable cornice return only after checking photos, field notes, and the measurement report. Wilson used to leave off chimney flashing while running a third-party supplementing business until it went on the macro. Roof Sales Mastery puts a hand-built estimate at 30 to 35 minutes and says macros cut that in half [sICOGLUjsao @ 00:00:39]. Dustin Taylor structures the Texas file with bold F9 headings for carrier approved line items and supplement line items, mirrors the carrier's order, and runs a macro drop with "skip duplicates" turned on, which caught 2 items on that job [gtsj3GY7D8c @ 00:20:19]. Chad Michael orders the estimate as a sandwich that follows the job: removes first, then accessory R&R, then replaces [bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:26:28].

The money lives in specific line items. Estimate Mastery's sample file started at $21,027 RCV on a May 2024 price list, was repriced to April 2025 on the argument that nobody can buy gas at 2024 prices, switched three-tab to architectural, added starter on rakes and hips at a unit price of 8.57, then layered fascia, vents, rain caps, code-cited ice-and-water, hand-load labor, tarp and contents labor, project management hours, and permit to reach $47,192 RCV with O&P [q6R8Iom5Uz8 @ 00:19:04]. Dustin Taylor corrected a carrier bid of 29-gauge metal to the 22 gauge actually on the roof, roughly 5.40 versus 9 per LF, about $4 per LF more, and used the 24-gauge line because no 22-gauge item exists in Xactimate [gtsj3GY7D8c @ 00:03:20]. Becca Switzer's fascia substitution swaps roughly $3 per foot prefab for $10 per foot custom-bent, turning $400 into $1,200, and the master list math says asking for 18 items and getting half approved yields 9, triple the old result [jj-GWZEtOc0 @ 00:10:15]. Chad Michael caught a missing ridge cap of about 240 to 250 LF and argued waste should be 15% rather than the adjuster's 10% once ridge, starter, and valley are counted inside it [bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:03:52].

Routing and packaging are treated as mechanics, not afterthoughts. Chad Michael never sends the supplement to the field adjuster, because that invites them back out and prolongs the claim, and instead submits over everybody's head to ASI corporate claims, before the build, within 2 days, setting a 2-week expectation so a one-week answer looks like over-delivery [bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:19:04]. At State Farm, the move is getting past the robot and asking for Team 53, whose inside adjusters carry more authority than the field adjuster. Becca Switzer packages everything into one PDF rather than 62 attachments, opens with a non-aggressive "as you requested" paragraph, and gives each item its why, documentation, and a photo, adding an O&P cover sheet [jj-GWZEtOc0 @ 00:20:34]. Objection handling runs on "if this then that" scripts with 2 to 5 rounds expected, such as asking $5k and settling at $4k, while roughly 1 in 20 adjusters simply approve [sICOGLUjsao @ 00:14:46]. Homeowner letters are the escalation "dynamite": the sales call claims O&P lands 75 to 80% of the time once the letter goes in, drawn from 25 to 30 templates covering partial approvals, discontinued materials, stonewalling, preferred contractors, and multiple bids [q63afBPc6eY @ 00:07:50].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture the roof before it disappears
   - Pull a measurement report, four elevation photos, and tear-off documentation the next morning.
   - Shoot wall flashing, decking, valleys, and pipe boots before and during tear-off because aerials miss them, and separate what was observed from what is only suspected [American Roof Supplements — DR03Zlogfe0 @ 00:09:02].
2. Load macros and reconcile against the carrier estimate
   - Drop a one-trade macro, fill calculator fields from the roof report, and delete only what evidence rules out.
   - Use the macro as a checklist for low-slope double coverage, 30 lb versus 15 lb felt, and supervisory hours, then run skip duplicates before finalizing [Estimate Mastery — fdW0W_-MR-s @ 00:03:03].
3. Write the supplement with bolded changes and code notes
   - Bold every changed line, add an F9 note explaining the difference, and footnote code items.
   - Notes explain why line items differ from the original statement of loss [Estimate Mastery — q6R8Iom5Uz8 @ 00:13:44]; Chad Michael adds 2012 IRC footnotes, with drip edge code on page 12 [Chad Michael - The Practitioner — bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:40:21].
4. Route above the field adjuster and work objections
   - Submit one PDF before the build, then answer objections with if-this-then-that scripts.
   - Never send to the field adjuster, since it invites them back and prolongs the claim [Chad Michael - The Practitioner — bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:00:30]; expect 2 to 5 rounds, such as asking $5k and settling at $4k [Roof Sales Mastery — sICOGLUjsao @ 00:14:46].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is philosophy. American Roof Supplements sets the goal as 'the most accurate, supportable estimate for the legitimate covered work,' rejects aiming for the biggest possible estimate, and warns that 'habits are not line items': anything added out of routine rather than because the roof shows it has no place on the file [TkCEjbO6vQs @ 00:04:15] [HpoMhPz1KfU @ 00:10:06]. Roof Sales Mastery runs the opposite play. Ridge, starter, O&P and drip edge are the 'basic BS' every adjuster has a scripted rebuttal for, so the money lives in a master list of 100-plus items: cornice returns, generators, harnesses, OSHA supervision hours, ladder jacks, a single-axle dump truck, hand loading, window cleaning. They wave off the 'fluffing' charge because the adjuster decides what gets paid [q63afBPc6eY @ 00:04:41]. Chad Michael sits closer to Roof Sales Mastery on the opening position: start every item from the highest position, detach-and-reset instead of mask, and when a Florida carrier cut $8K to $4K, the counter went out at $16K [4dhIe6HGIs0 @ 00:09:28]. The operator choice is whether the file reads as an audit trail of one specific roof or as an opening anchor built off a master checklist, and the two camps price their services and train their people around those opposite answers.

Routing and posture split next. Chad Michael never sends the supplement to the field adjuster, because that invites them back out and prolongs the claim. The file goes 'over everybody's head' to corporate claims, and it goes in before the build, so the contractor learns which items the carrier will resist while there is still time to photograph them. That is the 'two bites at the apple' play, with a target of submission within two days and a response inside a week [bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:00:30] [bVX2IRINNSM @ 00:18:44]. Roof Sales Mastery's Becca Switzer is softer on tone and longer on persistence: open with a non-aggressive 'as you requested' paragraph, attach the why, the documentation and a photo to every line, work if-this-then-that objection scripts, and take the compromise, because $6,700 asked against $5,800 received is good enough [jj-GWZEtOc0 @ 00:18:01]. Estimate Mastery adds a third posture: play strictly by Xactimate's inclusion rules whether they help or hurt, which is why pipe jack and exhaust cap removal stay folded into shingle removal while RNR drip edge earns its own line [q6R8Iom5Uz8 @ 00:04:08]. The tension is speed versus rapport: going over heads compresses the timeline, while the conciliatory email keeps the adjuster in the deal across two to five rounds.

O&P and decking are where the playbooks diverge on proof. Roof Sales Mastery calls the three-trade requirement a 'smoke screen objection' and builds an entire module around maxing out roof-only claims on brick homes that carry nothing but three-tab shingles and felt, with no gutters to add [Jvj3So17gtA @ 00:00:35]. Chad Michael counters with history: the three-trade rule shifted around 2011 to 'complexity and/or coordination,' started by State Farm, and there is no law behind it, so the fight is over logged coordination rather than trade count [4dhIe6HGIs0 @ 00:24:47]. On decking, Dustin Taylor wrote a full redeck on a Texas file off attic photos, with no tape-measure proof of the spaced-deck gap, explicitly 'to be in the conversation' with the carrier [gtsj3GY7D8c @ 00:18:16]. American Roof Supplements draws the hard line against that move: do not assume decking damage, capture a wide photo, a close photo, measurements and location, and remember that 'necessary replacement does not automatically mean covered replacement' [HpoMhPz1KfU @ 00:06:18]. Operators running the Taylor play are betting the carrier negotiates off whatever scope is written down; operators running the American Roof Supplements play are betting that a clean evidence chain wins the line on the first pass without a second round of argument.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most repeated failure is documenting too late or trusting the aerial. American Roof Supplements names five core mistakes: supplementing from memory, adding items without evidence, assuming every roof uses the same items, assuming Xactimate pricing settles every cost dispute, and documenting too late. Tear-off discoveries get photographed before they are covered, or they are gone [TkCEjbO6vQs @ 00:08:30]. The same shop warns that aerial images cannot see wall flashing, that pipe boots hide behind dormers on measurement reports so the count has to come off the actual roof, and that valleys must never be guessed from satellite [HpoMhPz1KfU @ 00:05:03] [DR03Zlogfe0 @ 00:06:47]. Estimate Mastery's Alina Wilson, who previously owned a third-party supplementing business, admits to constantly leaving off chimney flashing until it went onto a macro that forces a decision every time: deal with the line or delete it [fdW0W_-MR-s @ 00:03:48]. Wilson also flags the Xactimate search bar as a trap in its own right, because it is slow and a few extra characters pull up the wrong line item. The fix the three converge on is a checklist loaded before the estimate is written, then checked against photos, field notes and the measurement report, rather than memory applied after the fact.

The second failure mode sits on the homeowner side. Adam Bensman of The STRONG Roofer says that when the supplement is explained badly, homeowners assume the contractor is chasing their insurer for 'free money,' worry about the deductible, and the contractor ends up 'not collecting money and bad reviews.' Without a narrative for what happens next, homeowners invent one and cast the roofer as a 'slime ball' [zxGa72oAf0w @ 00:01:05] [zxGa72oAf0w @ 00:08:27]. Bensman's fix is timing, explaining it at color selection or the deposit check at the latest, and framing it as protection from financial surprises. American Roof Supplements adds the mirror trap: never promise the homeowner payment, because construction scope and insurance coverage are different questions [HpoMhPz1KfU @ 00:07:44]. Inside the company, the same channel reports owners who tell their sales force 'supplements don't work,' pay reps commission on the original amount, and collect the supplement money themselves on the back end; their fishing analogy says the best bait is the one you believe in [wi2YpyR1was @ 00:03:00]. Roof Sales Mastery's counterpart warning: when reps supplement their own jobs, results go inconsistent, rep number two pulls about half of rep number one, and a lazy rep simply lets items go.

The economics checks cut against the marketing. Chad Michael warns that 10 and 10 O&P is not your margin, puts the target at 50 to 70 percent profit, and refuses to spend an hour arguing over 10 feet of drip edge [4dhIe6HGIs0 @ 00:42:38]. Roof Sales Mastery's own figures need auditing before anyone builds a forecast on them. One sales call advertises a $4,500 average across 165 jobs for $742,500, cites Brad at $45,400 across five supplements while speaking the average as '$980,' and then resets the realistic expectation at $6,000 to $7,000 [q63afBPc6eY @ 00:10:27]. On a separate call the same channel advertises a $2,500 average, calls the 'real' figure $4,000 to $4,500, and runs the floor case: 100 claims at just $250 each is still $25,000 [sICOGLUjsao @ 00:20:09]. Becca Switzer's carrier-level reality check is blunter: Allstate and Safeco may pay 2 of 11 items, and the stated expectation is a $2,500-plus average increase per claim [jj-GWZEtOc0 @ 00:18:50]. Time is the other cost line. Estimate Mastery will not chase about $100 in repricing, and Roof Sales Mastery says untrained supplementing takes three times as long, which is the per-hour test behind the drip-edge rule.
