## 1. Stop Mistaking Approved Scope and Gross Billings for Spendable Margin  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The first fatal trap is underwriting payroll against a carrier estimate rather than an enforceable customer obligation and a realistic collection schedule. Insurance proceeds, replacement-cost holdbacks, mortgage-company endorsements, and contractor invoices follow different workflows. A $30,000 estimate does not put $30,000 in the operating account, and recoverable depreciation is not automatically available before qualifying work and documentation are complete. Build a job ledger separating signed scope, billed work, disputed supplements, customer deposits, carrier-issued checks, cleared receipts, and remaining cost to complete. Never count an unaccepted supplement as available liquidity. The canonical Conservative case is $500,000 gross restoration revenue less $460,000 total modeled costs, leaving $40,000 owner net profit. Base is $1,500,000 less $1,305,000, leaving $195,000. Scaled is $2,500,000 less $2,050,000, leaving $450,000. Those totals already incorporate subcontractor and material costs; subtracting them again corrupts the model. Conversely, calling the full insurance settlement operator revenue inflates it when proceeds include unrelated property, contents, or business-interruption payments. Reconcile each deposit to a specific invoice and customer balance. A check made jointly payable to a mortgagee remains a collection task, not cleared cash, until endorsements and bank availability are resolved.

The second trap is buying catastrophe volume without pricing its funding duration. Use a thirteen-week cash forecast with weekly payroll, supplier terms, equipment rentals, taxes, and committed subcontract balances. At Base revenue, daily billings average $1,500,000 divided by 365, or approximately $4,110. A simplified thirty-day increase in collection time absorbs about $123,288 in additional receivables if billings remain level and deposits do not offset the delay. That is roughly 63% of Base annual owner profit, although the cash absorption is not itself an expense. Compare the exposure with the stated $75,000–$350,000 startup range and $10,000–$45,000 monthly operating range; neither range establishes that a particular crew plan is funded. Five full-time staff plus six contractors must fit the Conservative case's entire $460,000 cost envelope, so validate wages, utilization, and contractor availability locally. Contractor headcount is not eleven salaried employees, nor permission to misclassify workers. Trigger an expansion pause when projected unrestricted cash falls below six weeks of committed payroll and noncancelable obligations. Continue emergency stabilization only through a documented, funded plan; do not strand an occupied property because an internal dashboard changed color.

The third and fourth traps are invisible channel deductions and subcontractor defects priced as someone else's problem. A hypothetical $20,000 reconstruction job with $13,000 direct cost produces $7,000 contribution before overhead. If its signed network agreement charges 6% of gross billings, subtract $1,200; add $1,000 of unrecoverable rework and contribution falls to $4,800, or 24%. This is an illustrative job calculation, not a replacement for the canonical net margins. A later $2,000 valid recoupment reduces it to $2,800. Identify whether each deduction is a price concession, service fee, refund, or offset, and have bookkeeping apply the corresponding treatment without double counting. Match subcontract payments to measured progress, required inspections, and properly timed conditional releases rather than photographs of delivered materials. Maintain a jurisdiction-specific lien calendar: a dispute does not automatically extend notice deadlines. Freeze new assignments from a channel if unexplained deductions exceed 2% of its trailing sixty-day billings, pending reconciliation. That threshold is an internal diagnostic, not an industry benchmark. Preserve written objections within contractual deadlines while paying undisputed amounts when due and protecting existing customer commitments.

## 2. Make Water, Fire, and Mold Evidence Survive an Adversarial File Review  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Evidence-led water mitigation fails when technicians substitute a room full of machines for a documented drying strategy. IICRC S500 is a technical standard, not a universal statute or an automatic carrier payment obligation. Record the source, affected materials, contamination assessment, measured dimensions, ambient temperature, relative humidity, material readings, meter type, and an appropriate drying reference. Establish drying goals and revisit them as conditions change. For an illustrative mitigation play, four dehumidifiers billed at a contracted $100 daily for four documented days produce $1,600 of equipment billings; twelve air movers at $30 daily for the same period add $1,440. The $3,040 total is gross equipment billing, before labor, transport, power constraints, rentals, and overhead. If one unnecessary day is removed across that fleet, $760 disappears from billings. Investigate every equipment day lacking installation or progress evidence and every unchanged reading pattern across two visits. Never manufacture readings to repair a weak file. The fallback is supervisor reassessment, corrected equipment deployment, and removal of unsupported charges, with safe drying continuity. Copying yesterday's humidity values can conceal a failed dehumidifier, inaccessible moisture reservoir, or ongoing leak that makes the entire mitigation ineffective.

Documented fire and contents recovery fails through custody gaps, duplicate charges, and promises to restore items that cannot safely return to use. Use a barcode inventory with room origin, item condition, photographs, cleaning method, customer disposition instructions, and custody transfers. Separate pack-out, cleaning, storage, and return delivery rather than burying all four in an unexplained allowance. An illustrative batch of 200 approved items at $25 each produces $5,000 gross cleaning billings. If testing shows 40 items are unsuitable before cleaning and the authorization permits billing only completed cleaning, eligible billings become $4,000. Any authorized assessment or disposal charges require their own contract basis; do not relabel failed cleaning to preserve revenue. At $2,800 actual direct cost, the remaining $1,200 is contribution before overhead, not owner net profit. Quarantine disputed contents and preserve customer access arrangements instead of using possessions as improvised collection leverage. Investigate a missing barcode immediately and repeated odor complaints before returning the next batch. The fallback is a documented test-clean decision, specialist referral where needed, and written disposition approval. Ozone, thermal treatments, and chemical products require appropriate exposure controls, manufacturer instructions, and an occupancy plan rather than a sales promise.

Licensed mold remediation fails when demolition starts before the operator understands state licensing boundaries, independent assessment requirements, and hidden hazardous materials. Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1958 establishes mold-related licensing and assessment-remediation boundaries with specified exceptions; determine applicability before assigning roles. An illustrative $12,000 remediation contract with $7,800 direct cost leaves $4,200 contribution before overhead. A $2,500 uninsured corrective visit reduces that amount to $1,700, even before management time or collection delay. Confirm the written protocol, containment plan, waste route, worker protection, and applicable clearance process before destructive work. For covered renovation in pre-1978 target housing or child-occupied facilities, EPA lead renovation requirements under 40 CFR Part 745 can change personnel qualifications and work practices. Asbestos obligations depend on material, activity, and applicable worker-protection and environmental rules; building age alone does not clear a project. An OSHA respirator requirement brings program, medical evaluation, fit testing, and training obligations, not merely a box of masks. Stop affected destructive work when suspect materials or containment failures appear. Preserve safe isolation and obtain qualified assessment. The fallback is controlled stabilization and a properly qualified remediation team, not a customer signature purporting to erase worker-safety duties.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the initial evidence packet
   - Capture authorization, room maps, conditions, photographs, and immediate safety findings.
   - Preserve originals with timestamps and assign a unique job identifier before equipment deployment.
2. Reconcile production at every visit
   - Match measurements, crew time, equipment movements, and subcontract progress to the current scope.
   - Flag missing evidence during the shift so a supervisor can inspect conditions while they remain observable.
3. Review exceptions before billing
   - Remove unsupported equipment days, duplicate line items, and work lacking contractual authorization.
   - Record each correction as a dated revision rather than replacing or backdating the original file.
4. Close the evidence-to-cash loop
   - Match the final invoice, customer acceptance, settlement deductions, and cleared receipts.
   - Keep disputed balances visible and retain records under applicable contractual, statutory, and insurer requirements.

## 3. Separate Contractor Scope from Public Adjusting, Appraisal, and Coverage  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play public_adjuster_appraisal_clause_storm_arbitrage, Commercial Hail-Swath Public Adjuster & Appraisal-Clause Storm Arbitrage, belongs to TRACK_1. Its defensible mechanism is matching verified commercial damage with properly licensed public-adjuster capacity and the actual policy's appraisal process, not selling a guaranteed settlement increase. Distinguish damage inspection, construction estimating, licensed claim representation, valuation appraisal, and legal interpretation in separate engagement documents. Consider an illustrative $400,000 disputed valuation and a hypothetical lawful adjuster agreement charging 8% of a defined $250,000 qualifying recovery: the adjuster's gross fee would be $20,000, subject to applicable restrictions and the agreement's actual fee base. That fee is not this restoration operator's revenue. If the operator separately performs $180,000 of authorized reconstruction with $135,000 direct cost, its gross restoration billings are $180,000 and contribution before overhead is $45,000. Do not add the $250,000 recovery, $400,000 disputed valuation, or adjuster fee to those billings. Appraisal availability, procedure, appraiser qualifications, and treatment of causation vary by policy and jurisdiction. Verify the clause and relevant law rather than assuming appraisal decides coverage. Neither the construction contract nor a referral establishes authority to negotiate policy rights for the insured.

The next fatal trap is unauthorized public adjusting hidden inside helpful customer service. A contractor can explain its measurements, methods, prices, and performed work without assuming authority to represent the insured on coverage or settlement. State definitions and exemptions control the boundary. In Texas, Insurance Code Section 4102.051 addresses public-adjuster licensing, and Section 4102.163 restricts a public adjuster's contractor role and participation in restoration of property within the claim they adjust. Separate companies or job titles do not automatically cure prohibited conduct. Screen ownership, referral compensation, solicitation scripts, representation agreements, and who actually communicates settlement demands. Customer-directed scope documentation is not permission to interpret exclusions or threaten coverage litigation. If a $60,000 supplement contains $35,000 supported quantity and pricing differences plus $25,000 dependent on disputed coverage, preserve those categories instead of presenting a single undifferentiated entitlement. The contractor supports its construction facts; licensed adjusters handle authorized valuation representation within their permitted role. The fallback for the storm play is explicit: pause solicitation and refer coverage disputes to qualified counsel while licensed adjusters handle supported valuations. Preserve policy deadlines and evidence during the handoff; a referral does not suspend contractual suit limits or statutory filing periods.

Deductible rebates, inflated invoices, and false completion certificates are asymmetric shortcuts: a small sales conversion can create repayment, license, civil, and criminal exposure. Texas Insurance Code Section 707.002 prohibits specified deductible-waiver and related inducement conduct. Do not advertise a free roof by disguising an unpaid deductible as a marketing allowance while invoicing the carrier for a different economic transaction. On a $25,000 repair with a $5,000 deductible, a carrier-funded portion of $20,000 does not establish that the contractor earned or collected $25,000. Record actual prices, lawful discounts, payments, and balances consistently across customer and insurer documents. Similarly, depreciation-release paperwork must describe actual completion rather than scheduled work or materials sitting in a warehouse. Screen assignment documents and lien threats against state-specific rules before using a downloaded template. Collections should request payment under the construction agreement, explain invoices, and document disputes without purporting to adjust the insurance claim. Trigger immediate review of any salesperson promise about coverage, deductible elimination, or guaranteed appraisal results. Freeze the affected script and correct customer communications before further solicitations. Keep compensation incentives tied to collectible, documented work rather than raw signed contract value that rewards misrepresentation.

## 4. Build a Loss Radar for Clawbacks, Channel Removal, and Catastrophic Defects  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Platform removal usually follows breached trust, failed service requirements, or contractual violations rather than one isolated pricing disagreement. Third-party administrator networks can impose documentation rules, response targets, audit rights, fees, and offset mechanisms; the signed agreement determines which apply. Xactimate is estimating software, not a universal job-awarding platform, and an estimate accepted by one reviewer does not immunize it from audit. Never share licensed credentials improperly, reuse another property's photographs, submit duplicate emergency charges, fabricate moisture logs, or buy reviews represented as genuine customer experiences. These shortcuts can cause account suspension or permanent exclusion where contractual terms permit, alongside repayment and broader liability. Archive channel rules and revisions with effective dates, and assign one owner to each network relationship. Track exceptions per job rather than rewarding technicians solely for upload completion. An uploaded but irrelevant photograph is still defective evidence. A credible falsification allegation triggers immediate preservation and suspension of the affected submission process, not deletion of embarrassing records. Contest unsupported deductions through the specified process with original evidence and deadlines. Do not evade removal by creating replacement identities; diversify legitimate acquisition channels and rebuild documented operating quality.

Subcontracted storm reconstruction fails when the operator treats warranty exposure as transferred simply because a subcontractor signed an indemnity. Verify license scope, insurance terms, exclusions, actual crew qualifications, permits, manufacturer installation requirements, and inspection milestones. A certificate of insurance summarizes information; it does not rewrite the policy or establish coverage for every activity. For an illustrative ten-job batch averaging $30,000, gross billings equal $300,000. With $210,000 direct cost, contribution before overhead is $90,000. Two $12,000 unrecovered water-intrusion repairs reduce it to $66,000. A subcontractor's promise to reimburse does not restore cash until recovery occurs. Require concealed-work photographs and inspections before covering critical flashing, penetrations, or moisture-sensitive assemblies. The fallback is fewer concurrent roofs, verified specialist crews, and staged acceptance rather than accelerating uninspected work. OSHA construction fall-protection requirements under 29 CFR 1926.501 depend on the activity and work conditions; document the applicable protection system before exposure. A serious injury can create losses far beyond a single job's contribution and cannot be modeled as an ordinary percentage discount. Stop unsafe activity immediately, secure the site, and activate emergency response rather than waiting for the weekly profitability meeting.

Run the loss radar every Monday with operations, estimating, bookkeeping, and the owner using the same job identifiers. Initial thresholds are management hypotheses: tune them against actual contractual terms, job mix, and historical outcomes. Investigate receivables older than sixty days, supplements unanswered for fourteen days, any unsupported equipment day, and weekly direct-cost forecasts rising more than five percentage points of contract value. Measure rework at actual labor, material, rental, and subcontract cost, not at retail price. Reserve cash for documented unresolved refund and defect exposure without claiming that a cash reserve determines accounting recognition. At Base, a $25,000 unrecovered loss consumes approximately 12.8% of the $195,000 annual owner profit. At Conservative, the same loss consumes 62.5% of $40,000; at Scaled, approximately 5.6% of $450,000. These are downside sensitivities, not revised scorecard scenarios. Every alert needs a named owner, next action, due date, and explicit release condition. Close an alert only after evidence or cleared cash resolves it, not after someone changes its status label. The final circuit breaker is refusing additional unfunded or unsafe work while fulfilling existing duties through a documented stabilization, funding, or qualified handoff plan.
