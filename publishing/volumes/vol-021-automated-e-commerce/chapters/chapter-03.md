## 1. Reconcile checkout billings to spendable owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with the revenue definition before connecting Shopify, Amazon Seller Central, Stripe, or a warehouse ledger. The scorecard's gross revenue label represents retail sales net of refunds; it is not marketplace merchandise volume, sales tax collected, or processor deposits. For the illustrative Base cohort, 12,500 orders billed at $100 produce $1,250,000 of merchandise billings. Refunds and credits totaling $125,000 leave $1,125,000 of recognized sales. The resulting $90 per original order is a revenue normalization, not a claim that every customer keeps exactly ninety percent of a purchase. Exclude collected sales taxes from both billings and revenue in this model, and hold their cash in a separate liability account. Record gift-card receipts as liabilities until recognition is appropriate. Match each order identifier to capture, shipment, refund, processor fee, and warehouse invoice. A settlement deposit combines several accounting movements and cannot replace this bridge. Keep the original sales cohort attached to later credits so January acquisition does not look profitable merely because February absorbed its returns. The Conservative and Scaled columns apply the same ten-percent illustrative gross-billings deduction, with cent rounding confined to the displayed bridge.

Build the margin waterfall with mutually exclusive cost buckets. Landed goods include supplier price, inbound freight, duty, brokerage, and nonrecoverable inbound damage; fulfillment includes outbound postage, pick-and-pack, packaging, and warehouse handling. Processing is modeled as a blended three percent of recognized sales, not a quotation from Stripe or Shopify Payments: actual fees can attach to original charges, include fixed transaction components, and remain after refunds. Replace that allowance with contract-level calculations before buying traffic. Return handling covers inspection, return labels, and inventory write-downs after any recovered stock value, while customer refunds already reduced revenue. Fraud losses cover incremental unrecovered dispute principal and fees; any reversal already classified as a sales reduction must be removed from this expense bucket. The Base arithmetic is $1,125,000 less $472,500 goods, $112,500 fulfillment, $33,750 processing, $157,500 acquisition, $11,250 retention, $22,500 return handling, $5,625 fraud, $45,000 contractors, and $135,000 fixed overhead. The residual is exactly $129,375. Owner distributions consume that residual rather than becoming a second operating expense. Inventory purchases and loan principal payments belong in the cash schedule, not duplicate deductions from this profit waterfall.

Do not mistake the canonical staffing labels for evidence that payroll is affordable. Conservative retains 2 FT plus 3 contractors, Base retains 2 FT plus 3 contractors, and Scaled retains 3 FT plus 4 contractors. Contractor pools are $10,000, $45,000, and $80,000 respectively; headcount describes participating people, not purchased full-time equivalents. At an illustrative loaded contractor rate of $30 per hour, those pools buy approximately 333, 1,500, and 2,667 hours annually across customer support, catalog corrections, and exception handling. Fixed overhead is only $27,500 Conservative, $135,000 Base, and $250,000 Scaled. An illustrative split reserving $9,500, $35,000, and $55,000 for software, insurance, accounting, and other overhead leaves employee compensation envelopes of $18,000, $100,000, and $195,000. The Conservative envelope cannot support two market-rate full-time employees; preserve its published $12,500 result while explicitly rejecting it as a fully funded hiring plan. Track working-owner hours and calculate a separate replacement-labor adjustment. Do not quietly improve the margin by assigning unpaid labor to automation. These annual owner-profit figures are the specified operating residuals, before personal owner taxes, with no additional financing or entity-tax charges assumed in the illustrative allocation.

## 2. Underwrite acquisition using retained cohort contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use blended and new-customer acquisition measures together. The Base budget of $157,500 divided by 9,000 independently deduplicated new customers yields $17.50 CAC; its 12,500 original orders imply $12.60 acquisition expense per order. These denominators answer different questions. New customers can place repeat orders, and platform-attributed conversions can overlap across Google Ads, Meta, Klaviyo, and affiliate reports. At $90 recognized revenue per original Base order, landed goods consume $37.80, fulfillment $9.00, processing $2.70, retention $0.90, reverse logistics $1.80, and fraud $0.45. The remaining $37.35 precedes paid acquisition, contractor labor, and fixed overhead. Subtract $12.60 acquisition, $3.60 contractors, and $10.80 fixed overhead to reach $10.35 owner profit per original order. Do not call $37.35 an allowable CAC without preserving coverage for those remaining obligations. Track first-order contribution separately from ninety-day repeat contribution, and exclude unobserved repeat purchases from cash-payback claims. Conservative assumes 2,000 acquired customers at $20 CAC; Scaled assumes 16,000 at $15 CAC. These customer counts are explicit modeling assumptions, not additional scorecard facts. Test whether each acquisition channel can deliver them without lowering realized prices or worsening returns.

The mapped play identity_graph_pixel_arbitrage contains a commercially tempting but unsuitable premise: resolving anonymous visitors into private contact and household records through cross-party identity matching. Do not build IP-to-person enrichment, reverse hashed consumer directories, or buy presumed identities for unsuspecting visitors. A CNAME changes a hostname, not the visitor's permission, and SHA-256 hashing does not make identifiable customer data anonymous. Treat the advertised 35–55% match range as an unsupported vendor claim rather than forecastable demand. Substitute voluntary account creation, permissioned email capture, contextual search advertising, and authorized customer-list audiences. For an illustrative test, 10,000 eligible visits with eight-percent voluntary signup produce 800 subscribers. A six-percent incremental purchase rate produces 48 orders and $4,320 recognized sales at the Base $90 rate. Their pre-acquisition contribution is $1,792.80 at $37.35 per order; a $1,200 incremental campaign leaves $592.80 before incremental labor and fixed costs. Use a randomized holdout to establish incrementality instead of crediting every subscriber purchase. Honor applicable deletion and sale-or-sharing opt-outs before audience uploads. US-only traffic restrictions do not remove state privacy duties, and platform permission to upload a list does not establish the underlying right to use it.

Physical mail is another measured acquisition channel, not a permission loophole. CAN-SPAM regulates commercial electronic mail under 15 U.S.C. 7704; postal campaigns instead face their own data-use, deceptive marketing, and suppression requirements. Use addresses customers provided for an authorized purpose, assess that purpose before marketing, and suppress objections. Mailing 1,000 eligible households at $0.85 delivered costs $850. Twenty-five incremental orders generate $933.75 of Base pre-acquisition contribution, leaving only $83.75 before campaign labor. Thirty orders leave $270.50; twenty orders lose $103 before labor. The campaign therefore needs at least 23 incremental orders just to cover postage and production, because $850 divided by $37.35 is approximately 22.76. Compare this test with permissioned email and contextual ads using the same retained-sales window. Product liability and delivery failures can reverse apparently excellent retention economics: recalled inventory is not a remarketing opportunity, and a delayed shipment is not revenue earned by a tracking notification. Under 16 CFR 435.2, build shipment-promise controls and the required delay-consent or refund workflow. Keep customer service empowered to stop sequences when a dispute, safety complaint, or delivery exception makes another automated sales message commercially destructive.

## 3. Fund inventory, settlement delays, and reversal reserves separately  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Retail customers usually pay before shipment, but the operator can still face a material collection delay. Measure DSO here as elapsed days between recognized sale and unrestricted processor cash; do not confuse card capture with money available to pay suppliers. The Base planning case uses 45 inventory days, seven settlement days, and 15 payable days. Using 365 days, inventory funding is $472,500 divided by 365 times 45, or $58,253.42. Settlement receivables are $1,125,000 divided by 365 times seven, or $21,575.34. Supplier credit offsets $472,500 divided by 365 times 15, or $19,417.81. Net operating working capital is $60,410.96 before processor rolling reserves and liquidity buffers. This simplified revenue-based settlement estimate excludes tax cash and assumes refunds are already reflected in the revenue run rate; the treasury forecast must instead use actual gross settlements, refund debits, and fees. Prepaid suppliers provide zero payable days, increasing the Base need by $19,417.81. Inventory days must include stock economically owned in transit and at the 3PL, not just sale-ready units. A seven-day dashboard payout average can conceal a much longer tail for new accounts, disputed orders, or concentrated marketplace exposure.

Layer processor holds and return liquidity on top without recording them as new expenses. Assume, illustratively, a processor withholds five percent of the Base $1,250,000 annual gross merchandise billings for 90 days. The steady-state restricted-cash balance is $15,410.96, calculated as billings divided by 365 times ninety times five percent. Actual contracts can use different bases, caps, release tests, or indefinite security rights; obtain the signed schedule. Separately allocate one month of modeled customer refunds, $125,000 divided by twelve or $10,416.67, as accessible refund liquidity. That amount is not a second refund expense and is not freely distributable profit. A fraud reserve forecast likewise distinguishes the expected loss already in the income statement from cash potentially withdrawn during dispute handling. Add one month of contractor and fixed overhead, $180,000 divided by twelve or $15,000, for a defined operating buffer. These components produce a Base planning target of approximately $101,238.58. This is a specified liquidity scenario, not a complete launch budget: deposits, initial tooling, abnormal demand ramps, taxes, and lender covenants require separate lines. Reconcile the target against unrestricted cash, undrawn committed facilities, and the next thirteen weeks of dated obligations.

Model the cash failure before increasing the reorder quantity. A fourteen-day extension to the Base settlement delay traps another $43,150.68 of revenue-based cash. Fifteen additional inventory days require $19,417.81. Together they add $62,568.49, raising the illustrative Base liquidity target to $163,807.07 without changing recognized revenue or accounting profit. That distinction explains why profitable shops miss payroll after promotional growth. The Conservative planning case uses 60 inventory days, ten settlement days, and zero supplier credit; Scaled uses 35, five, and 30 respectively. Apply the same five-percent, ninety-day gross-billings reserve assumption, one month of refunds, and one month of contractor plus fixed overhead to compare their funding needs. Do not assume better supplier terms merely because the Scaled column needs them: get written credit limits and confirm whether freight, duty, and deposits actually qualify for those terms. Route inventory alerts through reorder rules that use sell-through and cash availability together. Retain an alternate fulfillment route, but never move identical inventory obligations between dashboards to make exposure disappear. If a payout lock exceeds funded capacity, pause discretionary acquisition and new purchase commitments while preserving refunds, essential service, and already-promised customer shipments.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Close the retained-sales bridge
   - Join order, shipment, refund, dispute, and processor balance exports.
   - Reconcile gross merchandise billings to recognized sales and bank settlements separately; retain original cohort identifiers.
2. Rebuild dated inventory obligations
   - Schedule supplier deposits, balances, freight, duties, and 3PL invoices.
   - Use contractual due dates and actual ownership transfer; separate credit-eligible purchases from prepaid charges.
3. Stress the thirteen-week cash schedule
   - Add fourteen settlement days and fifteen inventory days.
   - Include restricted processor balances, tax liabilities, refund debits, and committed purchase orders without duplicating costs.
4. Release spending against funded capacity
   - Authorize only purchase and advertising commitments the stressed forecast supports.
   - Document the minimum unrestricted cash balance and assign an owner to any forecast breach before increasing spend.

## 4. Stress tariff, acquisition, and operating assumptions before scaling  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The mapped play section_321_de_minimis_bonded_tariff_arbitrage must not be underwritten using its historical exemption claims. The broad US duty-free de minimis suspension effective August 29, 2025 invalidated the premise that qualifying low-value parcels could generally enter duty-free merely because their origin was Vietnam, India, the EU, Bangladesh, or Cambodia. Verify current CBP implementation and any subsequent changes before quoting landed cost; this model assumes no Section 321 savings. Neither Mexican IMMEX processing nor Canadian bonded storage independently changes origin or creates US duty relief. Splitting shipments does not manufacture eligibility under 19 U.S.C. 1321. For historical sensitivity only, a $40 customs value carrying a 25% duty creates a $10 duty line; removing it would save $10, but only an actually available legal treatment can justify that removal. If a valid program saves $4 per unit while border handling adds $2.50, net savings are $1.50, not $4. A service provider charging forty percent of that net saving would earn $0.60 per unit before its own costs. Such service fees are a different business model and are not added to this retail scorecard. The retail operator recognizes its own merchandise sales and actual landed-cost reduction.

The practical fallback is documented customs classification, actual origin, ordinary duty-paid fulfillment, and valuation supported by the transaction record. First-sale valuation is not an automatic discount: under 19 U.S.C. 1401a and applicable customs precedent, the importer must substantiate a qualifying sale, destination, valuation elements, and the supporting chain of transactions. Have the customs broker identify the evidence required for the specific merchandise and commercial structure; a cheaper upstream invoice alone does not establish eligibility. Authorized-dealer agreements also need review for territory, channel, warranty, and returns economics. A product that loses manufacturer warranty support across a border can destroy more margin than duty planning saves. Keep product-safety traceability by lot and supplier, and escalate reportable hazards under 15 U.S.C. 2064 rather than allowing automated replenishment to continue. For each SKU, compare a domestic duty-paid route against any substantiated cross-border alternative using freight, duty, brokerage, delivery time, returns recovery, and replacement-stock funding. Reject an alternative when its savings require undocumented origin changes or unavailable parcel treatment. A ten-percent increase in Base landed goods cost is $47,250, cutting annual profit from $129,375 to $82,125 if prices, volume, and all other costs remain unchanged.

Use explicit sensitivities rather than optimistic blended forecasts. A twenty-percent increase in acquisition spending at unchanged customer volume subtracts $8,000 Conservative, $31,500 Base, and $48,000 Scaled, leaving $4,500, $97,875, and $312,000 respectively. Adding two percentage points of recognized revenue to fulfillment expense subtracts $5,000, $22,500, and $40,000. An incremental loss equal to two percent of current recognized sales, with no cost recovery, produces the same dollar reductions; if the event instead reduces revenue, recompute the margin denominator rather than reporting the old percentage. Stack acquisition inflation, fulfillment inflation, and that loss to produce owner residuals of negative $5,500, $52,875, and $232,000. These are downside tests, not replacements for the canonical three cases. Then overlay settlement and inventory stresses because losses and cash locks can occur together. Startup capital of $30,000–$250,000 and monthly operating expense of $4,000–$25,000 are screening ranges, not substitutes for this expense taxonomy or dated cash schedule. Order routing, inventory alerts, and returns tracking earn their place only when exception rates, labor consumption, and retained contribution improve. Expand after observing those improvements through a complete return cycle, not after an advertising dashboard posts a flattering revenue multiple.
