## 1. Define the retailer, the customer promise, and the five operating archetypes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Automated e-commerce is online retail in which software executes repeatable decisions while named people own purchasing, customer promises, cash exposure, and exceptions. The lean version sells a deliberately narrow catalog through Shopify, BigCommerce, or selected marketplaces, buys from documented suppliers, and sends executable orders to a third-party logistics provider. The retailer remains responsible for its offer, delivery representations, refunds, and product selection even when another company stores and ships every unit. Order routing, inventory alerts, and returns tracking remove repetitive handling; they do not transfer commercial accountability. An authorized dealer obtains written resale rights, channel permissions, warranty coverage, and current product identifiers before publishing listings. A stocked merchant owns inventory exposure, while a supplier-fulfilled merchant relies more heavily on supplier availability and shipping evidence. Neither arrangement becomes passive merely because a webhook submits the order. Define the operating boundary on one page: who controls the goods before transfer, who sets the selling price, who handles claims, and which party absorbs refunds. That boundary determines the revenue model and exposes dependencies that attractive storefront screenshots hide. A complete definition also identifies the person authorized to suspend sales when inventory, safety, or delivery evidence stops supporting the advertised promise.

Use five archetypes to select an execution model rather than treating every product category as the same opportunity. Stocked authorized dealers win through availability and trustworthy provenance; replenishment specialists win through predictable consumption; compatibility specialists win through precise fitment information; bundle merchants win through a useful assembled solution; and supplier-fulfilled dealers win through selection without owning every unit. Each can use a 3PL, but each needs a different exception queue. Replenishment requires cancellation and address-change handling, compatibility requires pre-sale qualification, and bundles require component-level availability. Compare ShipBob, ShipMonk, and Amazon Multi-Channel Fulfillment using the same carton dimensions, order destinations, monthly order distribution, return assumptions, and receiving schedule. Request separate quotes for receiving, storage, pick-and-pack, additional items, packaging, postage, returns, and aged stock. A low headline fulfillment price means little if oversized cartons, remote delivery areas, or fragmented inventory create extra charges. Run a physical test order before connecting paid acquisition. Photograph the delivered package, inspect inserts and serial labels, and reconcile the actual invoice to the quote. The first defensible moat is knowing precisely what the customer receives and what that promise costs under ordinary operating conditions, including a damaged shipment and a customer-initiated return.

Durable small-team advantages come from assets competitors cannot reproduce by copying a theme: supplier access, accurate compatibility data, a replenishment history, reliable delivery performance, and a permissioned customer relationship. A merchant selling replacement filters can record equipment model, installation date, and observed replacement interval, then send a relevant reminder rather than a blanket discount. Klaviyo or Shopify Email can execute that sequence, but the underlying data quality creates its value. Measure incremental repeat contribution against a holdout group; attributed email sales alone can include purchases that would have happened anyway. Fragile beginner models reverse this logic: they copy public products, depend on one advertising account, conceal long delivery times, and mistake supplier photographs for quality evidence. Their apparent automation often moves work into angry support tickets. Build an exception register covering negative stock, missed carrier scans, repeat defects, supplier substitutions, and unusual refund clusters. Assign each category an owner and a response deadline. If the business cannot explain why customers should buy again without another expensive advertisement, it has a campaign rather than a durable retail engine. Retention is strongest when the next order solves a known operational need, not when a discount merely accelerates an otherwise unprofitable purchase.

## 2. Reconcile recognized revenue, contribution, and the three owner-profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical revenue basis is retail sales net of refunds, with goods, fulfillment, and advertising included in margin. Although the scorecard labels its scenario column gross revenue, use that stated net-of-refunds basis consistently throughout the operating model. Recognize merchandise revenue when the applicable control transfer conditions are satisfied, not when an abandoned checkout appears or a payment authorization arrives. Discounts reduce transaction consideration, and expected returns require an appropriate estimate rather than waiting indefinitely for cash refunds. Sales taxes collected for taxing authorities are liabilities, not merchant earnings. Customer deposits and unredeemed gift-card balances generally remain obligations until the applicable recognition conditions are met. Processor payouts are also not revenue: they combine collections, timing differences, fees, refunds, reserves, and disputes. Reconcile the order ledger to processor activity and the general ledger separately. A retailer that controls merchandise before customer transfer generally records the merchandise sale and related costs. An agent arranging another merchant's sale records its earned commission instead. Shipping from a supplier does not alone resolve that principal-versus-agent assessment. Client store GMV belongs outside the operator's retail revenue unless the operator actually occupies the relevant principal role; access to a dashboard does not create ownership of its sales.

The three annual cases are fixed planning anchors, not interchangeable screenshots of marketplace volume. Conservative revenue of $250,000 at 5% produces $12,500 owner net profit and allows $237,500 of total costs. Base revenue of $1,125,000 at 11.5% produces $129,375 and allows $995,625 of costs. Scaled revenue of $2,000,000 at 18% produces $360,000 and allows $1,640,000 of costs. The stipulated teams are respectively two full-time employees plus three contractors, the same Base team, and three full-time employees plus four contractors. Those headcounts do not establish that prevailing wages fit the cost envelopes, especially in the Conservative case. Validate loaded payroll and contractor commitments before treating any case as executable; never rescue the calculation by silently removing labor. One illustrative Base allocation is $450,000 goods, $135,000 fulfillment, $202,500 advertising, and $208,125 remaining operating costs, including people, payment processing, software, and other expenses. Those categories total $995,625 exactly. Startup capital of $30,000–$250,000 funds launch needs and working capital; it is not annual profit. The stated $4,000–$25,000 monthly operating range is a planning input, not permission to exclude additional variable costs from the annual reconciliation.

Underwrite acquisition at the order and cohort levels before trusting annual percentages. For an illustrative $90 order after discounts and expected refunds, subtract $36 landed merchandise cost, $12 fulfillment and shipping, $3 payment cost, and $4 incremental service and return handling. That leaves $35 before advertising and $15 after a $20 acquisition cost, before fixed overhead. Do not subtract refunded merchandise value twice: the $4 allowance here covers operational handling and related costs outside the revenue adjustment. Break-even first-order acquisition cost is $35 before overhead, so bidding at that amount leaves no money for staff or owner profit. Set a lower target and stress it against a 20% advertising-cost increase, which reduces the example's post-ad contribution from $15 to $11. A second-order assumption belongs in the model only after observed cohorts support it. Separately track stock purchasing, supplier deposits, processor reserves, and tax remittances because profitable accrual results can coexist with a cash shortage. Illustratively, 300 units at $36 tie up $10,800 before sales arrive. Require a thirteen-week cash view alongside the income statement, and reduce replenishment commitments when slower sell-through extends the inventory cash cycle.

## 4. Build an exception-driven control system before increasing volume  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the operating stack around a small set of authoritative records. Shopify or BigCommerce owns the customer order, the warehouse system owns physical inventory movements, and the accounting ledger owns posted results; define explicit reconciliation rules instead of allowing every application to overwrite every field. Shopify Flow, Make, or a narrowly scoped integration can route paid, screened orders and create exception tickets in Gorgias or Zendesk. Use order identifiers and idempotency keys so a retried webhook cannot create a second shipment. Record acknowledgment timestamps, warehouse acceptance, carrier handoff, delivery evidence, and refund state separately. Label creation is not proof that a parcel entered the carrier network. Publish available-to-promise inventory after subtracting committed orders, quarantined units, and a documented buffer. Illustratively, 12 units daily demand across ten replenishment days plus 30 safety units gives a 150-unit reorder point, before adjusting for lead-time variability. Alert on feed staleness as well as low quantity because a stale feed can display comfortable stock that does not exist. Send refunds through controlled payment workflows with role-limited access, and reconcile return receipt against refund decisions. Automation should expose exceptions earlier and preserve evidence, not turn uncertain upstream data into irreversible downstream actions.

Translate statutory duties into catalog and fulfillment controls. The FTC Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule, 16 CFR Part 435, requires a reasonable basis for the advertised shipping time; absent a stated time, its default generally is thirty days. When timely shipment fails, follow the applicable delay-consent and prompt-refund requirements rather than silently rolling the delivery estimate forward. Product safety needs an incident escalation path under 15 U.S.C. 2064(b), which imposes reporting duties on covered manufacturers, distributors, and retailers when specified hazard information becomes available. Do not wait for a statistical certainty threshold before assessing that duty. Imports require classification, valuation, and entry information supported by reasonable care under 19 U.S.C. 1484; a supplier's quoted delivered price does not establish who bears importer responsibilities. Review sales-tax obligations by actual nexus, inventory locations, and applicable marketplace collection rules. Commercial retention email must meet 15 U.S.C. 7704 requirements, including accurate routing information and a functioning opt-out mechanism. Minimize stored customer data, restrict exports, and document applicable state privacy duties before expanding collection. These controls belong in ordinary operating records, with owners and evidence, rather than in a policy document nobody uses.

Launch with a bounded pilot and explicit stop conditions. Choose one archetype, a handful of authorized products, one warehouse arrangement, and one acquisition channel; the expected two-to-six-month time to first deal allows supplier qualification and shipping tests rather than promising immediate income. Before increasing spend, verify a complete order cycle that includes a return and a failed-delivery exception. Review contribution by SKU, channel, and customer cohort weekly, then reconcile actual owner profit monthly against the canonical case. Assign separate accountability for merchandising and purchasing, growth and retention, and fulfillment and customer service, even when two people cover several functions. Contractors should receive limited access and explicit deliverables rather than unrestricted administrator credentials. If acquisition cost rises above the contribution budget, cut the losing segment before testing creative variations with additional cash. If stockouts recur, lower advertised availability and investigate lead times before expanding the catalog. If product-liability signals emerge, isolate inventory, preserve complaint records, and initiate the relevant reporting and recall assessment. Scale only the workflows that have survived observed exceptions. The goal is a business whose ordinary decisions are repeatable and whose unusual decisions reach an accountable person before losses compound.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Document the commercial boundary
   - Identify the seller, supplier, inventory controller, importer where applicable, and party responsible for customer refunds.
   - Retain resale agreements, channel permissions, product evidence, recognition policies, and approved customer promises in one controlled launch record.
2. Underwrite one complete order
   - Calculate contribution after refunds, landed goods, fulfillment, payment costs, advertising, and incremental service expenses.
   - Reconcile the annual plan to the chosen canonical case and reject staffing assumptions that exceed its total cost envelope.
3. Exercise the exception paths
   - Run a paid test purchase, duplicate-event replay, inventory interruption, delayed shipment, and customer return.
   - Record ownership, timestamps, customer communications, inventory changes, and ledger outcomes before enabling unattended routine routing.
4. Expand only observed contribution
   - Review weekly SKU cohorts and monthly reconciled profit before increasing advertising or purchase commitments.
   - Preserve stop conditions for acquisition inflation, stale inventory feeds, supplier failures, product defects, and cash coverage deterioration.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before funding the next inventory or advertising cycle**
> 
> Treat expansion as a documented operating decision supported by current evidence rather than a reaction to yesterday's sales chart.
> - Verify written supplier authorization, product identifiers, applicable safety evidence, and channel permissions for every product being promoted.
> - Verify delivered unit economics using the actual 3PL invoice, processor deductions, advertising costs, refunds, and loaded staffing commitments.
> - Verify routing retries, stock buffers, customer delay handling, cancellation processing, and refund reconciliation with recorded test cases.
> - Verify the thirteen-week cash forecast funds replenishment, taxes, refunds, reserves, and payroll without counting client GMV or pending affiliate bounties.
