## 1. Map eighteen bounded plays before connecting an LLM to operations  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an event ledger, not an autonomous shopping agent. For an authorized-dealer store using Shopify and a contracted 3PL such as ShipBob, the commerce platform records orders, the warehouse records physical movements, and the accounting ledger records recognized sales and expenses. A Python receiver verifies each provider's documented webhook signature against the original request bytes, durably stores the event, and returns promptly. A separate worker retrieves current order state before proposing a transition. Persist provider, event identifier, resource identifier, received time, schema version, and processing outcome. Use a database uniqueness constraint for duplicate delivery and an order-level lock for conflicting updates; webhook arrival order is not business chronology. One employee owns merchandising and contribution, while the other owns operations and customer remedies. Three contractors cover integration maintenance, creative production, and scheduled support. Contractors receive narrowly scoped accounts rather than shared administrator credentials. Establish named backup coverage for absences and weekend delivery incidents. This arrangement makes five people accountable for an observable operating system, rather than expecting automation to compensate for missing ownership or unclear warehouse responsibilities. Document provider outages and escalation contacts alongside the event schema before processing live orders.

Map eighteen plays to that ledger as bounded workflows, not eighteen independent agents. Plays one through six are supplier-document intake, dealer-authorization checking, landed-cost extraction, catalog normalization, claim-evidence retrieval, and contribution screening. Plays seven through twelve are paid-order validation, warehouse routing, inventory reservation, replenishment alerts, shipment reconciliation, and return-status tracking. Plays thirteen through eighteen are the named pinterest_flipboard_visual_serp_arbitrage workflow, permissioned lifecycle email, customer-referral distribution, supplier and publisher outreach, creative-variant drafting, and cohort payback reporting. These are chapter-level workflow mappings; only the supplied visual-distribution play carries its external play identifier. An LLM may extract a supplier's minimum order quantity, but arithmetic code must calculate landed unit cost and a person must validate dealer rights. A retrieved document can support a product claim only when its SKU, revision, and issuer match the listing. Treat supplier PDFs, reviews, and inbound messages as untrusted data, never instructions for tool execution. Each play receives an input contract, output schema, review condition, and reversible fallback. The orchestration layer rejects extra fields and never accepts model-generated credentials, payment destinations, or executable commands. Keep the matrix below in the operations runbook and assign every workflow a primary owner.

Measure the automation target against work actually performed. In an illustrative weekly baseline, 1,000 repetitive tasks require six minutes each, or 6,000 minutes. Suppose deterministic checks complete 740 tasks without intervention and route 260 tasks to people. Review a ten-percent sample of the completed population: 74 checks at two minutes consume 148 additional minutes. If exception work still averages six minutes, total human effort becomes 1,560 plus 148, or 1,708 minutes. The observed reduction is 71.53%, while straight-through completion is 74%; report both measures because they answer different questions. Include reopened tickets, contractor debugging, and reconciliation effort in the next measurement rather than hiding them outside the denominator. Stratify samples by supplier, SKU risk, warehouse, and new versus repeat customer. Review every adverse product-safety signal and every proposed change to a consequential product claim regardless of sampling rate. A system that saves keystrokes but increases refunds has failed the business test. Publish weekly minutes saved beside exception age, duplicate shipments, inventory divergence, and contribution per retained order. Expand automation only when these measures remain stable across normal demand and at least one deliberately simulated integration failure.

## 2. Engineer order, inventory, and return state machines with recovery paths  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Implement order routing as a state machine with explicit transitions: payment verified, stock reserved, warehouse submission pending, warehouse accepted, shipped, and reconciled. Separate cancellation and partial-shipment branches instead of encoding them as free-text notes. Python can validate webhook payloads with json, verify compatible HMAC schemes with hmac and hashlib, and use decimal for monetary calculations. Follow each provider's exact signing specification rather than assuming one universal signature format. Persist an outbound command before sending it, attach the provider-supported idempotency key, and record the response identifier. Where an endpoint lacks idempotency support, query remote state before retrying an ambiguous submission. A timeout after transmission is an uncertain outcome, not permission to ship again. Retry transient failures with capped exponential backoff and jitter; route exhausted attempts to a durable exception queue. Reconcile warehouse acceptances against open commerce orders on a scheduled basis so missed webhooks cannot strand purchases indefinitely. Use SQLite only for a suitably constrained single-worker deployment; concurrent reservations need a transactional datastore designed for that workload. Never log complete customer addresses or payment details merely to make debugging convenient. Retain correlation identifiers and redact payload fields according to operational need.

Inventory alerts must distinguish physical stock, available stock, reservations, quarantined returns, and inbound purchases. For an illustrative SKU selling twelve units daily with a twenty-day supplier lead time and sixty safety units, the reorder point is 300 units: twelve multiplied by twenty, plus sixty. An inventory position of 260 therefore triggers investigation, not an automatic purchase commitment. Inventory position includes credible inbound quantities and subtracts backorders; storefront availability excludes unreceived goods unless the store deliberately offers an accurately described preorder. If daily demand rises to eighteen units, the same assumptions produce a 420-unit reorder point. Recompute after promotions and supplier delays rather than treating historical averages as permanent. Reserve units atomically when an order becomes eligible, release reservations through explicit cancellation transitions, and give stale reservations a reconciliation process. A feed older than its documented freshness limit should reduce offered availability or pause affected listings. Keep a buffer for multi-channel contention when warehouse updates lag. If Shopify and the 3PL disagree, the operations employee resolves the physical count before replenishment software increases advertising. The fallback is a smaller honest assortment, not a queue of paid orders that the warehouse cannot fulfill.

Returns tracking needs its own event history: requested, authorized, label issued, carrier scanned, warehouse received, inspected, remedy authorized, and ledger posted. A carrier scan establishes movement, not resale condition. Route refunds through explicit amount limits and approved policy rules; require human review for disputed liability, high-value exceptions, repeated serial-number mismatches, and any product-safety allegation. An LLM can summarize a customer's account and retrieve policy text, but it cannot decide whether an injured customer deserves a remedy or whether a defect requires reporting. Under 15 U.S.C. 2064(b), covered manufacturers, importers, distributors, and retailers must immediately inform the CPSC when the statutory reporting criteria are met, subject to the statute's conditions. Preserve incident evidence and escalate promptly to the responsible human; do not wait for a weekly dashboard. Separately, 16 CFR Part 435 governs shipment representations and required delay or refund procedures for covered orders. Automate deadline alerts and customer communication drafts, while keeping responsibility for accurate promises explicit. Quarantine returned units until inspection supports restocking. Reconcile refunds to both the payment processor and the sales ledger, keeping restocking losses, reverse freight, and recovery value visible instead of burying them inside an aggregate refund count.

**Webhook and Warehouse Failure-Recovery Matrix**

| Failure observed in production | Deterministic detection and immediate containment | Human resolution and evidence required | Safe replay or fallback procedure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duplicate paid-order webhook arrives after successful warehouse submission | Unique event constraint rejects duplicate processing before command creation | Operations checks existing warehouse acceptance only when states disagree | Replay reconciliation without issuing another physical fulfillment command |
| Warehouse submission times out before remote acceptance becomes visible | Mark command outcome uncertain and suppress immediate blind retries | Operations retrieves remote order using stable merchant order reference | Resubmit only after remote absence is established through documented procedure |
| Inventory feed exceeds its documented freshness and reconciliation interval | Reduce sellable quantities and pause traffic to affected products | Operations confirms physical availability with warehouse contact and timestamp | Restore listing quantities after reconciled stock passes reservation checks |
| Supplier delay pushes promised shipment date beyond current capability | Create deadline alert and draft accurate delay communication immediately | Operations applies required customer options under applicable shipping rules | Record customer response and execute shipment or refund accordingly |
| Returned item carries damage or a reported safety concern | Quarantine inventory and preserve customer report with restricted access | Responsible employee evaluates reporting duties and customer remedy promptly | Keep item unavailable until documented disposition authorizes further handling |
| Refund exists at processor but sales ledger lacks matching entry | Reconciliation flags unmatched amount using currency and transaction identifiers | Bookkeeping owner verifies settlement timing and original retained sales | Post missing ledger adjustment without issuing a second refund |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture and authenticate the event
   - Verify the documented signature, persist the event, and acknowledge durable receipt.
   - Record a deduplication key and correlation identifier; reject invalid signatures without exposing payload data in error messages.
2. Evaluate the current business state
   - Fetch current order, payment, reservation, and warehouse status before constructing a command.
   - Apply deterministic money and inventory rules; route conflicting states to the operations employee rather than asking a model to guess.
3. Execute one bounded transition
   - Persist the outbound command and send it with provider-supported duplicate protection.
   - Treat timeouts as uncertain outcomes, check remote state, and require human review for consequential exceptions.
4. Reconcile and close the evidence trail
   - Match remote acceptance, shipment, refund, or stock movement to the local ledger.
   - Close the task only after the expected business effect is confirmed; retain unresolved work in an owned, timestamped queue.

## 3. Turn visual discovery into stocked-product and owned-audience loops  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Pinterest search and Flipboard discovery can keep useful assets circulating over a six-to-eighteen-month testing horizon, but longevity varies and neither platform guarantees indexing or reach. Underwrite the loop with explicit assumptions. Suppose forty monthly visual assets cost $300 in creative review and production, and the associated guides attract 5,000 incremental sessions. At a 1.2% purchase rate, sixty retained orders generate $6,000 of net sales at $100 each. An illustrative order contributes $42 before acquisition: $100 less $38 goods, $12 fulfillment, $3 payment costs, and $5 expected reverse-logistics and inventory-loss expense. The last allowance is an operating cost, not another subtraction of already excluded sales refunds. Sixty orders therefore provide $2,520 before acquisition; subtracting $300 leaves $2,220 before shared overhead and other channel costs. This is a test calculation, not additional canonical profit. A 3% to 5% email capture rate yields 150 to 250 subscribers from those sessions, provided the offer and permission flow genuinely support that result. Attribute subsequent purchases by cohort and avoid counting the same order as both visual-channel and email-channel incremental revenue. If traffic halves, retain the cost base in the calculation and reassess payback. If stock becomes constrained, route guides toward genuinely available alternatives or a clearly described restock digest rather than advertising an immediately purchasable item.

Complete the flywheel with lifecycle messages, referrals, and selective outreach that reuse verified information rather than multiplying unsupported claims. After delivery, send product-specific setup instructions; after a realistic usage interval, request an honest review without conditioning rewards on positive sentiment. Offer referral benefits with clear terms and model their cost before activation. Paid endorsements and material connections require appropriate disclosure under the FTC framework, while 16 CFR Part 465 prohibits specified deceptive review and testimonial practices. For commercial email, implement accurate headers, required sender information, working opt-outs, and suppression handling under 15 U.S.C. 7704. Treat outreach to relevant publishers or authorized suppliers as a small researched queue, not a scraped bulk-email operation. An LLM drafts messages from approved facts; a person reviews initial campaigns, recipient relevance, and consequential claims. Use customer data only for documented purposes and honor applicable privacy requests, including California requirements when the business falls within their scope. Maintain deletion and suppression workflows across the commerce platform, email provider, and analytics exports. A weekly holdout cohort helps distinguish genuine incremental retention from purchases that would have occurred anyway. Stop distributing a creative when returns or support contacts reveal that its promise misrepresents the product.

## 4. Price every automated run and scale only reconciled contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Cost each automation at the run level before celebrating low token prices. Consider an illustrative document-classification run using 1,200 input tokens and 200 output tokens. At assumed planning rates of $0.50 per million input tokens and $2.00 per million output tokens, model usage costs $0.001. Add $0.0008 for execution and queue handling and $0.0007 for storage and logging: the machine cost is $0.0025 per run, or $25 for 10,000 runs. These are budget assumptions, not a vendor quotation. If one percent of runs require three minutes of review at $25 per hour, one hundred reviews cost $125. The combined variable cost becomes $150, or $0.015 per run, before fixed subscriptions and engineering maintenance. Measure retries and rejected outputs because they still consume resources. Cache approved supplier facts by document revision, shorten prompts to relevant excerpts, and use deterministic parsing wherever formats are stable. Send only necessary customer information to a model provider under appropriate data handling terms. Compare this classifier's review rate with its own task mix; it does not replace the earlier whole-operation labor baseline. Purchase model capacity only after measuring recovered staff time and defect costs.

Reconcile automation economics to the canonical cases without manufacturing a fourth scenario. Conservative retail sales are $250,000 at a 5% net margin, producing $12,500 annual owner net profit with two full-time employees and three contractors. Base sales are $1,125,000 at 11.5%, producing $129,375 with the same headcount. Scaled sales are $2,000,000 at 18%, producing $360,000 with three employees and four contractors. The corresponding all-in expense envelopes are $237,500, $995,625, and $1,640,000. Treat the scorecard's revenue figures as retail sales net of refunds, not payment-processor throughput, underlying marketplace volume, or collected sales tax. Goods, fulfillment, advertising, labor, software, and other operating expenses must fit inside those envelopes. Do not add modeled time savings to profit unless they produce an actual expense reduction or supported incremental contribution. At the illustrative $100 retained order value, the cases imply 2,500, 11,250, and 20,000 retained annual orders; these are workload translations, not revised revenue forecasts. The startup capital range of $30,000 to $250,000 and monthly operating range of $4,000 to $25,000 do not replace the complete annual expense model. Inventory purchases and settlement delays also require a separate cash schedule even when reported profit rises.

Scale by constraint, not by dashboard enthusiasm. If an illustrative $100 order produces $42 before acquisition and paid acquisition costs $24, it leaves $18 before shared overhead. A twenty-five-percent increase in acquisition cost raises that charge to $30 and reduces the remaining contribution to $12, a one-third decline. An inventory shortage can erase more value through canceled orders, expedited freight, and support time, so link campaign ceilings to dependable stock coverage. Establish per-workflow kill switches that stop new commands while allowing status reads, reconciliation, and customer support to continue. Keep a last-known-good configuration, versioned prompts, reproducible test payloads, and documented manual queues. Run a monthly recovery exercise covering duplicate webhooks, warehouse downtime, an incorrect return classification, and an overstated creative claim. Inspect both technical recovery time and affected customer outcomes. The scaled seven-person team should absorb verified exception volume and channel complexity, not conceal automation fragility through overtime. Review the contribution ledger, unresolved incidents, and cash commitments before raising spend. If evidence deteriorates, freeze the affected workflow, preserve its audit trail, and restore the narrower reliable process. Sustainable leverage comes from repeatable decisions whose costs and consequences remain visible after the original builder stops watching.
