## 1. Qualify inventory, underwrite contribution, and release only executable orders  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an authorized-dealer packet, not an advertising campaign. The operator records supplier identity, resale authorization, permitted channels, warranty responsibility, landed unit cost, carton dimensions, lead time, and the exact product identifiers accepted by the warehouse. Match the purchase order, supplier invoice, and receiving report before making stock available. Product images and descriptions require usable rights; a distributor invoice alone does not establish every marketing permission. For imported goods, assign importer-of-record responsibility and classify duties before underwriting margin under 19 U.S.C. 1484. For consumer products, identify applicable testing, certification, traceability, and reporting duties rather than assuming the supplier absorbs them. Section 15(b) of the Consumer Product Safety Act, 15 U.S.C. 2064(b), creates reporting obligations when the statutory conditions are met. Quarantine a suspect lot, preserve customer and shipment identifiers, and give one named employee authority to stop sales. This chapter organizes sixteen retail workstreams plus the two specified adjacent plays into eighteen operating lanes; it does not manufacture additional catalog identifiers. The storefront owns retail revenue only when it sells goods as principal; marketplace volume or a partner merchant's turnover is not operator revenue.

Underwrite each SKU through a contribution worksheet that reconciles to the ledger. In the Base illustration, 1,000 fulfilled orders at $100 create $100,000 before refunds. Refunds of $6,250 leave $93,750 recognized retail revenue. Product cost is $37,500, fulfillment is $7,500, advertising is $18,750, and processor expense is $3,000, leaving $27,000 before $16,218.75 fixed operating expense. That produces $10,781.25 monthly owner profit, exactly 11.5% of revenue. The $18.75 advertising allowance per fulfilled order is a blended operating assumption, not permission to buy every incremental order at that price. Segment new-customer acquisition from repeat orders and evaluate refunds by original acquisition cohort. At the stated mix, pre-advertising contribution is $45.75 per fulfilled order; additional acquisition spend consumes that contribution dollar for dollar before overhead changes. Shopify or WooCommerce can capture orders, while a 3PL performs physical execution, but neither dashboard replaces this calculation. If a carrier surcharge or advertising auction destroys the SKU's allowance, suppress that SKU's campaign, revise its offer, or stop replenishment. Do not disguise an operating loss with projected repeat purchases that have not occurred.

Translate the worksheet into explicit release criteria. Intake checks price version, available stock, destination serviceability, dealer restrictions, and payment evidence before any warehouse instruction. Use integer cents and stable storefront order identifiers; a repeated webhook must retrieve the existing order rather than reserve another unit. Separate available inventory from quarantine, damaged stock, and inbound purchase orders. A reorder alert should compare usable stock with lead-time demand plus safety stock, not merely announce that a shelf is nearly empty. At thirty daily units, twenty days of replenishment lead time, and ten days of buffer, the reorder point is 900 usable units. Demand volatility, supplier reliability, and carton economics determine whether that buffer is adequate. The 3PL must return accepted quantities, shipment identifiers, and delivery exceptions that can be joined to the original order. Under 16 CFR 435.2, shipping representations require an appropriate basis, and delays require the prescribed customer options and refund handling. A stockout therefore triggers customer communication and a recorded disposition, not an indefinite payment hold. The matrix assigns paired workstreams so the two-person core team can delegate specialist work without surrendering exception ownership.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Receive and qualify
   - Create the supplier, SKU, cost, and inventory evidence packet.
   - Separate usable inventory from quarantine and reconcile the receiving report to the supplier invoice before storefront availability changes.
2. Accept and verify
   - Reserve stock atomically and authenticate the captured-payment event.
   - Compare integer cents, merchant account, order reference, and capture identifier before creating the warehouse outbox record.
3. Route and fulfill
   - Submit the immutable shipment request with its persistent idempotency key.
   - Store warehouse acceptance, then recognize sales and product cost at the documented shipment or delivery recognition point.
4. Refund and reconcile
   - Post verified refund, fee, and deposit events before reviewing cohort performance.
   - Resolve clearing differences, update return disposition, and compare realized contribution against the original acquisition allowance.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before paid acquisition**
> 
> The operator records evidence for each check before making the offer purchasable or increasing its acquisition budget.
> - Verify dealer permissions, applicable product evidence, landed costs, and lot traceability against the exact sellable SKU.
> - Run a duplicate-order test and a competing-order stock test; neither may create a second reservation for the same accepted order.
> - Match a captured payment, warehouse acceptance, refund, processor settlement, and bank deposit using persistent identifiers.
> - Confirm shipping promises, delay handling, subscription terms where applicable, keyboard checkout, and customer-support ownership using recorded test cases.

## 2. Implement the six-module ledger and an idempotent order workflow  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The reference below is a deliberately narrow, executable single-SKU order kernel, not an entire storefront connector. Save the SQL cells as schema.sql and reconcile.sql, then concatenate the Python cells in order into reference.py in the same directory. Run python reference.py to execute the temporary-database checks. All amounts are integer cents, every journal row contains equal debit and credit amounts, and every external event has a unique operation key. SQLite BEGIN IMMEDIATE serializes competing writers before stock reservation or state changes; conditional updates enforce availability within that same transaction. Reusing an order key with different commercial terms raises an error instead of silently changing history. The schema assumes a single merchant ledger and a durable local filesystem suitable for SQLite locking. Use PostgreSQL with equivalent uniqueness and row-locking behavior when deployment requires multiple database hosts; do not put this SQLite file on an unreliable shared drive. Back up the database and test restoration before accepting live orders. Application access is the supported write path, and direct database edits are prohibited operationally. These boundaries matter: database constraints cannot authenticate an untrusted processor message or prove that a physical shipment actually arrived.

Verification means matching authenticated provider evidence to the order, not trusting a browser redirect. The payment adapter must verify the provider signature, merchant account, currency, captured status, amount, and order reference before calling verify. The internal held state is an inventory reservation; it is not escrow and does not authorize custody services. A captured payment debits processor clearing and credits deferred sales until fulfillment meets the documented recognition policy. This example recognizes at confirmed delivery. Production orders need taxes, discounts, multiple lines, partial shipments, cancellations, and jurisdiction-specific handling added as explicit events, not hidden mutations. The outbox separates committed intent from external network execution. Concurrent workers can submit the same immutable request, but they must use the same provider key, and the provider must guarantee idempotent creation with stable response identity throughout the retry window. A local transaction alone cannot prevent duplicate remote shipments after a timeout. If an integration lacks that contract, disable automatic submission and use a controlled search-and-reconcile queue. Early delivery notifications remain in the adapter's durable retry inbox until warehouse acceptance has been recorded; they never force the order backward.

Reconciliation starts with separate asset, liability, revenue, and expense balances. Captures increase processor clearing; refunds, fees, and bank settlements reduce it. Delivery releases deferred sales and posts product cost against inventory. The order view therefore reports recognized sales separately from bank cash, while the trial-balance view includes inventory funding, advertising, fulfillment expense, overhead, and owner capital when those events are posted. A batch payout must be allocated from processor detail rather than posted as unexplained revenue. Sales taxes belong in a payable account, not in sales; the cohort excludes tax flows to keep its retail comparison clean. The reference rejects refund totals above delivered order value and rejects changed payloads under reused journal keys. Its concurrent tests exercise repeated intake, repeated capture, competing outbox workers, repeated delivery, and repeated refunds. Before deployment, add authenticated adapter tests, crash injection between provider response and local acknowledgment, backup recovery, and expired-provider-key behavior. Never describe these checks as proof against every infrastructure failure. Within the stated database and provider contracts, stock changes and local postings are serialized and remote retries preserve one shipment identity. A failed invariant stops routing while an operator resolves the evidence gap.

## 3. Walk three acquisition cohorts from accepted orders to reconciled cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The Worked 90-Day Cohort Walk models three identical monthly acquisition cohorts, not a claim that a new store reaches steady volume immediately. Each month starts with 5,000 qualified visits and 1,050 submitted orders. Fifty fail pre-capture checks for address, inventory, payment, or duplicate-order reasons, leaving 1,000 captured and delivered orders at $100 each. The model assumes delivery and refund recognition occur within the acquisition month. Fifty orders receive full $100 refunds and twenty-five different orders receive $50 partial refunds, producing $6,250 total refunds. There are 950 orders retaining some revenue, including the twenty-five partially refunded orders, and $93,750 net retail sales. Refunded products have zero recovery value in this example, so product cost remains $37,500 for all 1,000 shipped units. No separate chargeback loss is assumed; adding chargebacks requires reducing profit or offsetting another documented cost. The fifty pre-capture scrubs create neither sales nor processor receipts. Acquisition spending covers the whole traffic cohort, including failed submissions and subsequent refunds. These definitions prevent a common reporting error: dividing advertising by only successful retained customers while quietly removing the acquisition costs of everyone else.

Processor cash follows a different clock from revenue. After $6,250 refunds and $3,000 processor expense, each cohort yields $90,750 net settlement entitlement. Assume 80% reaches the bank during its acquisition month and 20% reaches the bank the following month. This is a stress assumption for settlement timing, not a statement about any provider's standard schedule. Current-month cash is therefore $72,600, with $18,150 carried as a processor receivable. Nonprocessor cash spending is $79,968.75 each month: $37,500 inventory replacement, $7,500 fulfillment, $18,750 advertising, and $16,218.75 fixed overhead. The first month consumes $7,368.75 cash despite earning $10,781.25. Months two and three each receive $90,750 because the prior cohort's deferred settlement joins current receipts, producing $10,781.25 monthly operating cash. The operator funds $90,000 before day one: $60,000 bank cash and $30,000 opening inventory. Frequent replenishment keeps inventory at $30,000 while monthly purchases equal recognized product cost.
