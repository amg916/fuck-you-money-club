## Executive Summary

Practitioners agree that sales tax is now a growth-triggered liability: since the 2018 Wayfair ruling, most states key economic nexus to $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions, and FBA or 3PL inventory creates physical presence on its own. The working cycle is nexus, register, calculate, handle exemptions, then file and remit, and Avalara and LedgerGurus both insist you never collect before registering. Amazon, Walmart, and Etsy collect as marketplace facilitators; Shopify does not. Street numbers run from TaxJar at about $20 a month with AutoFile near $30 to $35 per filing up to Avalara engines at $10k to $30k plus $40 to $50 per return. The fights are over tooling: Avalara's 10-out-of-10 engine versus its price, TaxJar's March 2026 repricing and backfiling gaps, and Numeral, Taxify, and TaxCloud pitched as leaner challengers.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator starts from the same map: nexus is the switch that turns sales tax on, and it now flips two ways. Cherry Bekaert lists the physical triggers as employees, 1099 workers, sales reps, offices, and inventory sitting in Amazon or 3PL warehouses, then stacks economic nexus on top since June 2018, with most states keyed to $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions while California, New York, and Texas run higher bars, and what counts toward the bar shifts between retail and wholesale, marketplace and direct, taxable and non-taxable sales [rUvJ4UwGqiA @ 00:01:00]. Avalara's webinar presenters hammer the FBA angle hardest: parking inventory in a fulfillment center creates presence in that state whether or not you meant to sell there [EtT3YoVfP0c @ 00:17:05]. Arthur Glinkin adds the street nuance that physical triggers are not always obvious; California told them a passive partner does not create nexus, so their standing move is to phone the state department of revenue and get the answer straight from the source [1Cr_iHc8p4s @ 00:04:05]. LedgerGurus and Avalara close the loop on timing: liability begins only from the moment the threshold is crossed, which makes the crossing date the single most important number an operator tracks [pqXEldKeguY @ 00:30:16].

The second point of agreement is who actually holds the bag. Avalara's Tax 101 explainer states that Amazon, Walmart, and Etsy must collect under marketplace facilitator laws, meaning a major marketplace 'is already taking care of sales tax for you,' while Shopify is not a facilitator and leaves the seller fully responsible [QiT660QtcXA @ 00:01:18]. Arthur Glinkin runs the same split with Amazon, eBay, and Etsy and adds the operator payoff: those facilitated sales are excluded from threshold counts, so a seller's own storefront volume is what drives the math [1Cr_iHc8p4s @ 00:06:00]. Ryan McKenzie strips away the small-seller comfort zone entirely, warning that 'the size of your business does not grant you anything' and that the state will collect every last penny, with the brand owner on the hook to organize, file, and pay [jmsERJHMp_0 @ 00:01:27]. Avalara's webinar team frames it as tax 'triggered by growth, not intent,' pointing to a sweat-suit brand that scaled to $10M a month and an M&A deal in which $1,000,000 was withheld from the sale price [EtT3YoVfP0c @ 00:32:53]. The consensus is blunt: volume, not paperwork, decides when a seller is exposed, and the exposure follows the business all the way into its exit.

Process consensus is just as tight. Avalara's webinar lays out five steps in strict order: figure out where you owe, register, calculate, track, then file and remit, with the flat warning to never collect before registering [EtT3YoVfP0c @ 00:15:15]. The LedgerGurus session with Avalara repeats the sequence as nexus, register, calculate, exemptions, file and remit, and lands the same rule: 'please don't collect unless you're registered' [pqXEldKeguY @ 00:31:09]. Nobody argues for doing it by hand. Sal of SAL Accounting CPA says Shopify Tax 'does half the work,' covering more than 11,000 jurisdictions and free for the first $100,000 in sales but never filing or paying, and tells sellers not to fall back to manual work because of penalties [HbiniE5ODzI @ 00:02:41]. Avalara's SAP Commerce Cloud demo shows why: two buyers in the same zip code with identical carts pay different tax because one sits inside a special City Improvement District that zip-code calculation would most certainly have overlooked, while AvaTax validates the address and pushes the rate to the cart in milliseconds [A7FAFArV6JM @ 00:00:39]. The baseline prerequisite every speaker assumes is an automated engine plus a filing layer, wired in before the first taxable order ships.

   - Get an EIN, apply for each state permit through the department of revenue, and only then switch on collection.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive traps sit between collecting and remitting. Avalara's growing-sellers webinar warns never to collect before registering, because officers are personally liable, with exposure framed as jail and 'unjust enrichment' [EtT3YoVfP0c @ 00:22:52]; the LedgerGurus Sales Tax 101 session repeats it bluntly: 'So please don't collect unless you're registered' [pqXEldKeguY @ 00:31:09]. LedgerGurus then flags a TaxJar-specific leak: it remits what it calculates rather than what you actually collected, which produces either illegal retention of tax or overpayment out of your own margin [-DhtTN5_Rh4 @ 00:17:26]. Tyler's Shopify walkthrough shows how confusion creeps in, stating remittance goes 'to the IRS' when every other operator routes it to the state department of revenue, and confirming Shopify does not remit at all, so the filing report must be exported to an accountant [SgxDnkPR36U @ 00:04:15]. The fix pattern is consistent: get the permit first, reconcile collected tax against filed tax every period, and treat Shopify's collection toggle as the start of liability, not the end of the job. Registration itself is mostly free, though Wisconsin and South Carolina can charge $30 to $50.

Calendar and data-hygiene failures are the next layer. LedgerGurus says Avalara must be approved by the 11th at 5 PM ET or it auto-files, TaxJar's approval deadline is the 5th and Taxify's the 12th, with the tools setting deadlines one to two weeks ahead of the states [-DhtTN5_Rh4 @ 00:12:28]; the 2020 LedgerGurus breakdown instead lists Avalara at the 10th and TaxJar at the 6th, and requires product code mapping, exemption certificates and every location loaded up front [1L7s6llWlRo @ 00:08:32]. LedgerGurus calls Avalara setup long and 'garbage in, garbage out,' and warns Taxify has no exemption-certificate tracking plus a broken Shopify connection that forces manual imports [-DhtTN5_Rh4 @ 00:25:17]. Certificates also die on their own: Florida resale certificates expire December 31st every year, Louisiana does not exempt nonprofits, and a certificate issued to an old company name was disallowed [EtT3YoVfP0c @ 00:46:28]. The same Avalara webinar has sellers audit payroll and warehouses a couple of times a year, because tax is 'triggered by growth, not intent' [EtT3YoVfP0c @ 00:32:53]. The Sales Tax 101 session adds that you need one certificate per state per customer, with shared SST and MTC forms available [pqXEldKeguY @ 00:39:42].

Economics checks separate survivable mistakes from fatal ones. Sales Tax at Scale reports one customer hit with a 2,000% Avalara price increase and another paying $30k on TaxCloud versus $150k on Avalara, with Reddit users dropping from $100k to $150k a year down to $25k to $30k after switching [5Dtw2cworM0 @ 00:08:07]. Avalara's growing-sellers webinar describes one M&A deal where $1,000,000 was withheld from the sale price, Florida penalties running 10% per month up to 50%, and Texas voluntary prepayment earning a 1.25% discount [EtT3YoVfP0c @ 00:34:38]. The LedgerGurus Sales Tax 101 webinar recounts The Mattress Queen being shut down after a Washington audit, and lays out the VDA escape hatch: Texas lookback is unlimited, but a VDA caps it at 5 years, penalties are waived and interest usually is not [pqXEldKeguY @ 00:26:07]. Cherry Bekaert's remedy sequence matches: get a permit, then file prior-period returns or a VDA for large liabilities [rUvJ4UwGqiA @ 00:02:09]. Liability begins only from the moment you cross the threshold, so the first spreadsheet to build is the date each state was crossed, then price backfiling against a VDA before a state finds you first.
