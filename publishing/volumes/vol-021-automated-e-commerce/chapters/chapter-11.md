## 1. Assign eighteen plays to five seats before adding capacity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build one operating pod, not five loosely coordinated freelancers. For Candidate #28, the Conservative and Base configurations both contain two full-time seats and three contractor seats; an owner working daily must occupy a counted seat rather than become invisible labor. FT1 owns commercial performance, authorized-dealer relationships, purchasing economics, and the weekly close. FT2 owns order execution, inventory integrity, customer recovery, and the 3PL relationship. Contractor C1 delivers acquisition and creative production; C2 delivers retention and integration maintenance; C3 delivers bookkeeping and reconciliation. Contractors are bounded specialist capacity, not three additional full-time employees disguised by invoices. Record hours or deliverable capacity, replacement coverage, access scope, and acceptance criteria in each statement of work. At Scaled, add FT3 for customer experience and exception management, plus C4 for catalog and product-data quality. Transfer tasks explicitly instead of leaving the original employee accountable for everything. Shopify, Gorgias, Klaviyo, and the warehouse portal should each have a named internal owner and a documented recovery path. The outsourced warehouse remains a vendor organization outside the seven-person pod, but its fees and failures remain inside the economics. Use a shared exception queue so delegation changes visibility rather than concealing unfinished work.

Use the following eighteen-play operating map to make the assigned portfolio executable: P01 dealer authorization, P02 assortment underwriting, P03 landed-cost purchasing, P04 catalog publishing, P05 demand forecasting, P06 replenishment alerts, P07 order routing, P08 warehouse reconciliation, P09 delivery exceptions, P10 returns tracking, P11 refund control, P12 paid acquisition, P13 creative testing, P14 lifecycle retention, P15 cohort analysis, P16 accrual close, P17 product-safety evidence, and P18 transferability documentation. These labels are this chapter's working allocation of the eighteen mapped plays, not additional revenue streams. FT1 remains accountable for P01 through P03 and P12 through P18; FT2 remains accountable for P04 through P11, with specialist execution assigned in the seat matrix. C3 prepares accounting evidence but never releases its own vendor payments. C2 can repair a routing integration but cannot independently change payout destinations. Every play needs one primary owner, one trained backup, one measurable output, and one linked procedure. Rehearse absence coverage monthly by having the backup resolve a real exception from the documented queue. If the founder must interpret undocumented supplier promises or rescue every inventory mismatch, the pod has automated clicks rather than transferred operating knowledge. Correct that dependency before hiring another specialist to absorb its symptoms.

Buy contractor outcomes with retainers plus capped, auditable variable fees. An illustrative C1 agreement pays $1,200 monthly for eight accepted creative variants, campaign maintenance, and one contribution review; reserve any incentive for incremental contribution after media, refunds, and fulfillment, using a fixed baseline and attribution window. C2 might receive $900 monthly for monitored lifecycle flows and a bounded integration backlog; C3 might receive $600 for reconciliations and a documented monthly close. These example retainers total $32,400 annually before variable fees and are planning inputs, not additions to canonical profit. A critical routing incident requires acknowledgment within one covered business hour, a documented containment plan within four, and a next-business-day root-cause note. State coverage windows and emergency escalation separately; a small retainer does not purchase continuous staffing. Require written acceptance of creative rights, test orders, reconciliations, and change logs before paying disputed deliverables. Contracts do not determine worker classification by themselves: evaluate actual behavioral control, financial control, and the working relationship, plus applicable state tests. Preserve contractor discretion over methods where appropriate while retaining strict requirements for security and results. Budget employee payroll, taxes, benefits, and supervision explicitly before accepting the five-seat model as economically feasible.

## 2. Run a weekly scorecard that exposes contribution and concentration  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Hold a forty-five-minute weekly operating review using one frozen reporting cutoff, one metric dictionary, and links to underlying transactions. FT1 publishes net sales, contribution dollars, acquisition spend, cash availability, and supplier concentration; FT2 publishes routing success, overdue orders, available-to-promise accuracy, returns aging, and customer-contact backlog. C1 explains campaign economics, C2 explains lifecycle exceptions, and C3 reconciles material differences between commerce reports and the ledger. Define contribution consistently as recognized sales after refunds less product cost, outbound fulfillment, payment fees, variable return costs, and attributable advertising. Do not subtract a refund twice when the revenue number already excludes it. Separate contribution before advertising when diagnosing product economics, but label it explicitly. Revenue per person is secondary because warehouse labor is purchased externally and contractor capacity varies. Report contribution per internal operating hour alongside total outsourced spend. Each red metric must produce a named action, a deadline, and a quantified exposure, such as orders awaiting a carrier scan or dollars trapped in unreconciled returns. Close prior actions before discussing new experiments. The meeting succeeds when it changes an operating decision, not when every chart has an attractive trend. Archive the packet so a buyer can trace historical decisions.

Set initial service thresholds from the actual selling promise, then tighten them with evidence. A useful starting design targets at least 99.5% successful automated routing, at least 98% shipment within the promised window, and at least 99% agreement between sampled physical units and the inventory ledger. These are internal operating targets, not claims about existing performance. Define routing success as an eligible paid order acknowledged by the correct warehouse without manual repair; exclude documented fraud holds from both numerator and denominator. Track stockout exposure as forecast contribution lost, not merely the number of empty SKUs. For returns, report requests awaiting labels, parcels received but uninspected, refunds due, and resalable units awaiting restock separately. A warehouse receipt does not prove a refund was issued. Under 16 CFR 435.2, shipping promises need a reasonable basis, and qualifying delays require the prescribed customer options and refund handling. Configure alerts around those obligations rather than an arbitrary warehouse dashboard color. Test whether Shopify Flow or middleware retries create duplicate fulfillments and whether failed webhooks enter a visible dead-letter queue. Resolve root causes before rewarding contractors for increasing order volume through a visibly unstable fulfillment process.

Adopt a maximum 25% exposure policy for any single customer and each material commercial counterparty, with separate denominators rather than one misleading blended percentage. Measure customers by trailing twelve-month net sales, suppliers by trailing purchases, and warehouses by shipped orders; also track concentrated contribution and inventory replacement lead times. A consumer retailer may have negligible customer concentration while one distributor supplies most profitable products. Show both trailing twelve-month and latest-quarter views to catch rapid deterioration. One primary 3PL, processor, or storefront platform will often exceed the cap in a lean operation. Report that breach plainly; a backup account does not reduce actual concentration until volume moves. Maintain a dated remediation plan with tested export procedures, alternate receiving capacity, inventory ownership records, and estimated switching cash. Do not create four nominal suppliers controlled by the same parent and call the risk diversified. Aggregate related counterparties and shared failure points. Expansion from five to seven people requires both service pressure and durable contribution coverage after additional loaded cost. Adding seats because gross sales rose can lock the business into a larger payroll just as advertising inflation or supplier withdrawal erases the margin supporting it.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze and reconcile the reporting population
   - C3 snapshots the prior week's orders, refunds, ad spend, and warehouse charges.
   - Use one timezone and cutoff; reconcile missing events and preserve the exported source files.
2. Assign exceptions before the operating review
   - FT2 attaches an owner, customer exposure, and next action to every material service exception.
   - Separate broken integrations from warehouse delays and customer-requested holds so fixes address the actual cause.
3. Approve bounded commercial changes
   - FT1 sets campaign limits, purchase commitments, and contractor priorities from reconciled contribution.
   - Record the expected dollar effect and prohibit the preparer from independently releasing related payments.
4. Verify closure and preserve the decision trail
   - The responsible backup checks completed actions against transaction-level evidence.
   - Carry unresolved actions into the next packet with original dates, updated exposure, and explicit escalation.

## 3. Reconcile canonical earnings to accrual books and transferable rights  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Anchor every plan to the canonical scorecard before discussing optimization. Conservative recognized retail revenue is $250,000 at 5%, producing $12,500 annual owner net profit and $237,500 total modeled costs. Base is $1,125,000 at 11.5%, producing $129,375 profit and $995,625 costs. Scaled is $2,000,000 at 18%, producing $360,000 profit and $1,640,000 costs. All three revenue figures represent retail sales net of refunds, despite the scorecard's gross revenue label; they are not marketplace GMV, advertising budgets, or payment-processor deposits. Goods, fulfillment, and advertising already sit inside the margin calculation. The stated $4,000 to $25,000 monthly opex range cannot substitute for the complete cost envelope, which also includes inventory economics and other costs represented in those margins. Build a detailed bridge rather than assuming that range funds every operating requirement. Annual profit divided by twelve gives average monthly earnings of $1,041.67, $10,781.25, and $30,000 respectively, not a promise of monthly distributable cash. Seasonal buying, processor reserves, taxes, and debt service can absorb cash before distributions. The Conservative case is especially fragile for five seats; identify founder labor and fractional contractor capacity explicitly instead of implying that $12,500 comfortably supports additional unbudgeted compensation.

Close monthly on an accrual basis with transaction-level links among Shopify, processor settlements, warehouse movements, purchasing records, and QuickBooks Online or Xero. Establish the revenue-recognition point from the actual customer contract and shipping terms, then apply it consistently. Record inventory purchases as inventory until sold or otherwise expensed; processor payout timing does not determine revenue. Capitalize appropriate freight and duties into landed inventory cost, then release cost through a consistent costing method. Record collected sales tax as a liability where the seller is responsible, and reconcile marketplace-facilitator collections separately. Maintain reserves or estimates for expected returns, damaged goods, chargebacks, and obsolete inventory under the chosen accounting policy, with supporting history and subsequent true-ups. Cohort reports should group customers by first purchase month and channel, then measure realized contribution through a defined observation window. Do not compare a mature twelve-month cohort with a newly acquired thirty-day cohort and call the difference deteriorating retention. Klaviyo attribution is a marketing view, not independent evidence of incremental earnings. Reconcile its claimed sales to actual orders and use holdouts where feasible. Preserve source exports and versioned calculation logic so another operator can reproduce the reported margins without the founder's spreadsheet memory.

Build the transferability file while relationships are healthy. Obtain signed copyright assignments from creative and integration contractors covering the identified deliverables; 17 U.S.C. section 204(a) generally requires a signed writing for copyright ownership transfers. Payment alone does not establish ownership, and a broad work-for-hire label does not solve every contractor arrangement. Separate newly assigned work from licensed fonts, stock photography, open-source components, and preexisting contractor tools, documenting the buyer's continuing usage rights. Store dealer authorizations, territory restrictions, channel permissions, assignment provisions, and change-of-control terms alongside each supplier account. A profitable listing loses value if the buyer cannot continue selling it. Maintain product traceability, supplier certificates where applicable, insurance schedules, incident logs, and recall procedures. Evaluate reporting duties under 15 U.S.C. section 2064(b) when information reasonably supports a reportable product hazard; do not wait for ordinary customer-support thresholds. For imported goods, document who is importer of record and preserve classification, valuation, and duty evidence supporting reasonable care under 19 U.S.C. section 1484. Inventory every privileged account and service credential by business owner, renewal date, and transfer procedure. Transferable books combine reproducible earnings with enforceable operating rights, not merely a tidy income statement.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four evidence packages before scaling or circulating buyer numbers**
> 
> Stop the affected expansion or valuation claim when its supporting evidence fails; continue unrelated operating work with a named owner.
> - Reconcile all three canonical scenarios and identify every payroll, contractor, warehouse, advertising, and inventory cost category inside the modeled margin.
> - Demonstrate the latest monthly close from source orders through settlements, inventory movements, return estimates, and the general ledger.
> - Verify signed contractor IP assignments and supplier transfer provisions; list third-party rights that require separate permission or replacement.
> - Measure customer and counterparty exposures against the 25% cap, disclose every breach, and test the recovery procedure for each critical dependency.

## 4. Underwrite an exit multiple against transferable earnings and cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Value the business from a defined earnings base, not from the largest revenue number on a broker slide. For a small owner-operated retailer, an illustrative screening range of two to four times normalized annual seller's discretionary earnings can frame discussion; it is a modeling assumption, not a verified transaction-comparable range. Establish current comparables and buyer financing constraints before quoting an asking price. SDE commonly adds back one working owner's compensation and documented discretionary or nonrecurring items, while an absentee buyer must price the replacement labor required to operate the business. Canonical owner net profit is neither automatically SDE nor EBITDA. Reconcile interest, taxes, depreciation, owner compensation, and recurring operating costs before selecting the metric. Advertising required to sustain sales, recurring integration repairs, and ordinary product testing are not discretionary merely because the seller dislikes paying for them. A premium requires repeatable contribution, low unresolved concentration, transferable dealer rights, clean inventory, and evidence that the pod operates without founder rescue. Buyers may discount businesses with heavy marketplace dependence even when revenue is growing. Present trailing monthly earnings and cohort evidence alongside the latest annual total so seasonality and recent margin erosion remain visible throughout underwriting.

Keep illustrative arithmetic separate from the canonical operating cases. Multiplying Base owner profit of $129,375 by two and four produces $258,750 and $517,500; multiplying Scaled profit of $360,000 produces $720,000 and $1,440,000. Those are mechanical earnings-proxy values, not concluded enterprise values, because normalization and deal terms remain unresolved. The same exercise produces only $25,000 to $50,000 for Conservative profit of $12,500, which can be overwhelmed by transaction costs or replacement labor. Suppose a buyer identifies $60,000 of recurring replacement labor absent from Base expenses. Transferable annual earnings would fall to $69,375, and a three-times illustration becomes $208,125. A separate Scaled example with $90,000 of missing recurring replacement cost yields $270,000 and $810,000 at three times. Apply those deductions only when the costs are actually absent; subtracting already included payroll would distort valuation. Neither sensitivity changes the published scorecard. Bridge enterprise value to seller proceeds through debt, cash treatment, transaction expenses, working-capital adjustments, holdbacks, and contingent consideration. Define whether saleable inventory is included in normalized working capital or purchased separately. Counting inventory in both places manufactures proceeds that the purchase agreement will not deliver.

Run a ninety-day transferability rehearsal before seeking offers. During the first month, complete the rights register, reconcile inventory, document contractor capacity, and close unresolved supplier permissions. During the second, have backups perform the weekly review and monthly close while the founder handles only documented escalations. During the third, operate a planned absence period and measure service, contribution, and exception aging against the prior baseline. Record failures honestly and repair the procedures rather than editing the evidence pack. Give prospective buyers staged, access-controlled financial and operational evidence; minimize customer data and follow applicable privacy obligations when preparing diligence exports. Provide aggregate cohort reporting before identifiable records are genuinely required. Keep the cash-cow alternative equally rigorous: fund replenishment, tax liabilities, expected returns, debt commitments, and a measured operating reserve before owner distributions. A reserve based on supplier lead times and downside contribution is more useful than an arbitrary percentage of revenue. Revisit whether the additional scaled seats still protect enough contribution to justify their loaded cost. The best exit preparation also improves continued ownership because each documented right, reconciled balance, and independently executed play reduces the founder's dependence on constant intervention without pretending retail risk has disappeared.
