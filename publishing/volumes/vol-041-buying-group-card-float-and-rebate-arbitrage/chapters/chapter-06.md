## 1. Mine committed demand before mining discounted inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin with a demand ledger, not a shopping cart. Create one record per buyer, destination, exact SKU, quantity ceiling, delivered price, commitment expiry, receipt standard, and promised settlement interval. Review the account-accessible deal feeds of BuyForMeRetail, Pointsmaker, and MaxOutDeals each business morning, using permitted exports or manual entry rather than bypassing access controls. A listed price is an invitation to investigate; only a current, written commitment supports procurement. Preserve the commitment identifier and its timestamp beside the retailer order confirmation. Ask whether substitutions, split shipments, serial numbers, damaged cartons, and late arrivals change acceptance. Warehouse addresses in Delaware, New Hampshire, or Oregon do not themselves create a resale exemption or eliminate other tax obligations. Verify delivery jurisdiction, purchaser identity, and any required resale documentation before checkout. Your strongest trigger is a repeated, profitable shortage that the same buyer confirms twice, not a dramatic discount screenshot. For the first month, concentrate on two receiving partners and a narrow SKU family whose packaging and carrier handling you can inspect. This reduces reconciliation work while exposing whether quoted Net-3 or Net-5 settlement actually begins at delivery, inspection, or acceptance.

Extend demand mining through public evidence that changes a buyer's purchasing need. Manufacturer discontinuation notices, retailer assortment changes, public liquidation catalogs, and distributor overstock announcements can identify inventory imbalances. Secretary of State records help verify entity names and status; UCC searches help investigate liens where their scope matters, but neither proves solvency or unrestricted title to particular goods. Confirm claimed distributor authorization directly through the manufacturer or an authenticated channel contact. Build a watchlist with the source URL, publication date, affected SKU, hypothesized buyer, and the fact still requiring confirmation. Approach regional wholesalers with exact inventory questions rather than a request for every closeout list. For TJX, Costco, or Burlington, use published vendor channels and established wholesale intermediaries; a buyer name or submission receipt is not a purchase order. Amazon wholesale demand also requires actual account eligibility, accepted invoices, and category access. Publish a plain supplier intake page requesting SKU, available units, location, lot age, packaging photographs, and invoice provenance. Route every inbound submission through the same evidence checks as outbound leads. Reject unsolicited files that require credentials or executable downloads to reveal inventory.

Treat authorized_distributor_grey_market_diversion_arbitrage as an adjacent wholesale sourcing lane, not as permission to disregard distribution or import restrictions. Its regional-price thesis targets quoted differences of 35–60%; verify each comparison on identical specifications, currency dates, quantities, and delivery terms. A foreign corporate purchasing entity must have genuine purchasing authority and accurate customs records; it does not erase contractual restrictions. In an illustrative universal OEM consumables lot, a $100 domestic reference price versus a $60 foreign purchase price creates a 40% headline difference. Add $5 freight, $4 assumed duty and brokerage, and $3 inspection and loss reserve: landed cost becomes $72. A signed $85 wholesale sale leaves $13 before overhead, not $40. Obtain the actual tariff classification before replacing that assumption with a quote. Kirtsaeng interprets copyright first sale under 17 U.S.C. § 109; trademark restrictions, materially different goods, and FDA requirements remain separate. Reject medical consumables without product-specific admissibility and labeling evidence. The fallback is globally identical universal-packaging goods with documented authenticity and permitted importation, sold against direct B2B purchase orders. Start with samples and a small commercial lot before contemplating containers.

## 2. Qualify each opportunity against proof, cash, and operational capacity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a weighted qualification score only after applying hard exclusions. Reject unsupported provenance, prohibited product handling, unclear buyer identity, negative stressed contribution, or repayment dependent on an unconfirmed receipt. Score the survivors on committed demand, margin evidence, liquidity, counterparty authority, traceability, and execution capacity, using the weights below. Require at least 85 points for a paid pilot, with no hard exclusion overridden by a high aggregate score. Give full demand credit only to a commitment specifying quantity, price, acceptance, and expiry; a chat expressing interest earns less. Margin credit requires a landed-cost worksheet and the removal of unvested rewards from the downside case. Liquidity credit requires cash that remains available after payroll, operating expenses, existing card balances, and other obligations. Verify the payment recipient through an established contact before sending funds after any bank-detail change. For a supplier relationship, examine title warranties and conflicting claims under applicable UCC Article 2 provisions rather than treating an invoice as conclusive proof. Keep the source documents attached to the scored record so another operator can reproduce the decision without trusting the salesperson's memory.

Build the cash calendar before negotiating a larger allocation. Record purchase authorization, statement close, contractual payment due date, carrier delivery, buyer inspection, expected ACH initiation, and cleared cash availability as distinct events. Advertised statement float approaching 55 days depends on purchase timing and the actual card agreement; it is not a standard maturity available on every order. Treat every card purchase as a short-term repayment liability from day one. Suppose a $10,000 pilot expects cleared reimbursement on day 12, while its card payment is due on day 15. A ten-day buyer delay moves receipt to day 22 and creates a seven-day funding gap. The operator must already hold enough available cash to pay on day 15; a pending ACH entry does not satisfy that requirement. Illustratively, a $10,000 balance charged 24% annual interest for seven days costs approximately $46.03 before any fee, and a $35 fee raises that burden to $81.03. Actual agreements may impose different interest mechanics or remove a grace period. The fallback is smaller purchasing, earlier payment from reserves, or declining the deal, never repeated consumer card-cycling.

Keep treasury qualification independent from product selection. A bank bonus can reward an eligible balance without creating customer demand, and a T-bill position cannot cure an uneconomic wholesale purchase. For a proposed $250 account bonus requiring $10,000 for sixty days, assume $25 of unavoidable fees and no deposit interest. Against a 3% alternative annual yield, foregone interest is $49.32, leaving $175.68 of incremental pretax benefit if every eligibility condition is satisfied. Verify whether the offer applies to the entity, prior account history, qualifying deposits, and the planned source of funds. Do not represent internal transfers as payroll or other qualifying activity when the terms distinguish them. For bills, calculate return from actual purchase price and maturity proceeds; the chapter's annual yield calculations are screening approximations. Maturity must precede the required cash date with an operational buffer, and early sale introduces price and settlement risk. FDIC insurance generally covers eligible deposits within applicable ownership-category limits, not Treasury securities. Count interest and earned bonuses separately from merchandise settlements, and never count the invested or borrowed principal as revenue, contribution, or owner profit.

## 3. Run a focused 90-day outreach and first-pilot conversion system  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

During days one through fourteen, build eighty researched lead records across buying-group demand contacts, regional wholesalers, documented suppliers, and legitimate invoice-paying businesses. Separate these audiences: a wholesale buyer needs availability and delivery certainty, while a business evaluating payment routing needs a fee comparison and authority over its own invoices. Never market fabricated savings or handle another business's funds without an appropriate, reviewed operating arrangement. Your first wholesale email should identify the specific SKU family, available documentation, receiving geography, and small pilot size, then ask for current buying criteria. A useful opening is: 'Can your purchasing team quote ten factory-sealed units of this exact model, with acceptance terms and payment timing in writing?' For a supplier, request an itemized availability file rather than their entire customer list. Use an accurate sender identity, nondeceptive subject, valid postal address, and working opt-out for commercial email under 15 U.S.C. § 7704. Maintain a suppression list across every operator and sending tool. Publicly listed contact information supports research, not unrestricted permission for automated calls or texts. Prefer relevant manual outreach over indiscriminate message volume.

Use a six-touch cadence only while the recipient remains relevant and has not opted out. Send the specific opening on day one, a short evidence packet on day three, and a manual call to a published business number on day six when applicable calling rules permit it. On day ten, share one usable landed-cost comparison; on day sixteen, propose a tightly capped pilot; on day twenty-four, close the loop and pause unanswered outreach. Do not manufacture urgency by claiming inventory reservations that do not exist. Handle 'your spread is too small' by showing contribution after shipping and reward reversals, then decline if the buyer needs a price below that floor. Handle 'we pay after inspection' by requesting the inspection clock and deduction procedure, not by pretending inspection creates immediate cash. Handle 'send everything first' with a sample or small paid lot and written acceptance terms. For 'the portal should cover the fee,' show the zero-portal downside case. Each reply must produce one next action, an accountable person, and a date. Store unanswered questions beside the opportunity rather than burying them in chat.

Define a win as a completed economic cycle: accepted goods or authorized service delivery, settled payment, reconciled deductions, and measured contribution. A signed purchase order is a milestone, not a settled win. Use a planning funnel of eighty researched leads, forty eligible conversations or qualified responses, twenty documented opportunities, ten accepted pilots, and six settled wins within ninety days. These imply 50%, 50%, 50%, and 60% stage conversions, or 7.5% from raw lead to settled win; they are workload assumptions, not measured industry benchmarks. Track observed results weekly and replace the assumptions after the first cohort. During days fifteen through forty-five, run two or three small pilots without overlapping more capital than the reserve supports. During days forty-six through ninety, repeat only the routes whose receipts and deductions matched the worksheet. Publish anonymized process evidence, such as packing standards and average observed settlement time, to attract better inbound inquiries; obtain permission before identifying counterparties. Record rejection reasons by stage so weak demand, poor pricing, restricted eligibility, and late payment remain distinguishable. Three clean repeatable wins are more valuable than ten orders still awaiting reimbursement.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Assemble the evidence-backed lead cohort
   - Build eighty segmented records during the first fourteen days.
   - Attach a public or authorized source, exact demand hypothesis, responsible contact, and unresolved qualification questions.
2. Convert interest into priced written terms
   - Run the relevant outreach cadence and score each documented opportunity.
   - Target twenty qualified opportunities from forty eligible responses; require at least 85 points and resolve every hard exclusion.
3. Execute capped and cash-covered pilots
   - Seek ten accepted pilots while limiting simultaneous exposure to available reserves.
   - Archive commitments, verify carrier coverage, capture serial evidence, and schedule payment before the contractual card deadline.
4. Count only settled and reconciled wins
   - Aim for six completed cycles within ninety days and revise assumptions weekly.
   - Compare realized contribution, rejection rates, and actual cash timing before increasing quantities or adding counterparties.

## 4. Reconcile first-deal economics before scaling the sourcing engine  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep three separate unit ledgers even when one purchase touches multiple rails. In the wholesale ledger, a $1,000 merchandise cost and $1,025 buying-group reimbursement create $25 of merchandise spread. In the reward ledger, an eligible 2% commercial card reward adds $20 and an eligible 1% portal payout adds $10. Shipping of $8, insurance of $2, and a $5 loss reserve leave $40 before fixed overhead when both rewards become final. Without either reward, the same transaction contributes only $10. These are illustrative terms, not current offers from any named provider. Keep expected rewards out of available repayment cash until received. The treasury ledger records only interest, earned bonuses, direct charges, and relevant opportunity costs; it does not record a second sale of the same goods. Allocate each shared cost once and preserve the transaction identifier across all three ledgers. If a rebate is properly treated as a purchase-price adjustment, maintain the associated inventory or expense basis rather than automatically classifying it as separate sales revenue. Reconcile actual reporting forms to the underlying transactions instead of treating their gross totals as profit.

Payment-routing proposals need especially disciplined subtraction. On a legitimate $10,000 supplier invoice, an assumed 3% processing fee costs $300 while a permitted 2% card reward earns $200, leaving a $100 deficit. Forty days of return on $10,000 at an assumed 4% annual yield adds approximately $43.84, so even that illustrative timing benefit leaves a $56.16 deficit before other costs. This comparison does not authorize investing money needed for repayment or assume that processor charges earn rewards. Check the actual eligible charge base, merchant coding, card treatment, and fee quote. A genuine supplier discount can change the result, but it must appear in the invoice terms and must not duplicate a discount already included in the purchase price. Never create circular invoices, self-payments disguised as supplier purchases, or transactions whose only purpose is to manufacture reward volume. When an invoice route fails, direct ACH is often the correct answer. When a portal fails, submit the permitted claim with order evidence, keep the disputed amount outside cash forecasts, and stop routing similar orders until the attribution failure has an identified cause.

Reconcile the annual scorecard without pretending these sample spreads produce its retained share automatically. Formula v2.0 uses a 35% retained contribution assumption: $250,000, $1,025,000, and $1,800,000 of gross scorecard basis become $87,500, $358,750, and $630,000. The corresponding landed merchandise deductions are $162,500, $666,250, and $1,170,000. Additional operating costs of $67,500, $215,250, and $270,000 then reconcile exactly to annual owner profits of $20,000, $143,500, and $360,000. Those profits equal 8%, 14.0%, and 20% of gross basis, or 22.86%, 40.0%, and 57.14% of retained contribution. These are canonical portfolio scenarios, not measured results or evidence that ordinary rewards yield 35%. The monthly opex range does not explain every residual cost; budget labor, losses, and other operating deductions explicitly. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent presentation requires a contract-specific control assessment; a retained-share parameter cannot determine recognition. Keep gross merchandise flows, retained contribution, recognized revenue, and owner profit separately labeled. Within the $10,000–$55,000 startup envelope, scale only after cleared pilot results support the intended mix. If observed margins remain lower, revise the operating forecast rather than relabeling card principal as earnings.
