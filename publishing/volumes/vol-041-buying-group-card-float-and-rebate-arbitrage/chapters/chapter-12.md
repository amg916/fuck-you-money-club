## 1. Days 1–7: Establish Eleven Play IDs and Protect Repayment Cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a written operating charter, not a shopping cart. Candidate #41 has eleven mapped plays; this chapter gives them explicit execution IDs so every assignment, transaction, and exception has one owner. The wholesale lane contains P01 counterparty qualification, P02 written deal commitments, P03 destination and resale documentation, P04 insured fulfillment, and P05 warehouse-to-ACH reconciliation. The commercial reward lane contains P06 business-card eligibility and statement scheduling, P07 Rakuten or TopCashback routing, P08 card-offer verification, and P09 Plastiq or Melio invoice economics. The treasury lane contains P10 unencumbered-cash T-bill sweeps and P11 bank-bonus qualification. These are operational labels for the eleven-play remit, not eleven independent profit claims. Assign the full-time owner treasury, purchasing authority, and exceptions; assign contractor A commitments, serial numbers, and shipment evidence; assign contractor B settlements, rewards evidence, and ledger matching. Cap each contractor at five hours weekly during the pilot. Neither contractor receives bank-transfer authority or shared card credentials. Use a restricted Google Sheet or Airtable register for commitments, QuickBooks for accounting, and a protected document folder for vendor terms. The owner signs the daily liability snapshot before authorizing another order. This assignment structure keeps purchasing decisions separate from evidence preparation without pretending a three-person pod has enterprise staffing.

Set the cash envelope before requesting any limit increase. At the $10,000 minimum startup case, earmark $6,000 as protected repayment cash, $2,000 as working-capital capacity, $1,500 as operating expense capacity, and $500 as loss capacity. Earmarks describe cash allocation; they do not create additional assets. Limit aggregate unsettled merchandise commitments to $2,000, including card-funded purchases and cash deposits without counting the same purchase twice. The first-month nonrecoverable cash-burn ceiling is $1,500, including contractor invoices, software, shipping losses, payment fees, interest, and unrecovered claim costs. An illustrative allocation is $700 for contractors, $150 for software, $150 for routine overhead, and $500 for exceptions. Owner draws are zero during this test. Merchandise principal stays outside the expense ceiling but inside the exposure limit. Keep unrestricted cash at least 1.05 times all posted and authorized card principal, associated fees, and a $250 shipment-loss stress. Exclude pending ACH, disputed receivables, rewards, and available card limits from that numerator. At larger startup balances, preserve the same ratios rather than spending the $55,000 upper range automatically. If actual contractor quotes exceed this envelope, reduce transaction volume or use owner hours; do not quietly expand the authorized burn ceiling.

Qualify BuyForMeRetail, Pointsmaker, and MaxOutDeals independently; a familiar name is not evidence of today's payment performance. Record the current legal counterparty, warehouse address, accepted SKU, quantity, serial-number rules, commitment expiry, rejection procedure, and settlement trigger. A Net-3 or Net-5 label must specify calendar or business days and whether counting begins at delivery, inspection, or acceptance. Validate the DE, NH, or OR destination and the actual transaction's sales-tax treatment. Those destinations do not transform every purchase into an exempt resale. Where a seller requires a resale document, supply the valid state-specific certificate or permit and retain the invoice; Washington RCW 82.04.470 illustrates the wholesaler documentation requirement. Do not misstate resale status to overcome a retailer restriction. Obtain issuer terms for business purchases, reward exclusions, payment posting, and repayment timing. Business credit is generally outside Regulation Z under 12 CFR 1026.3(a), so do not assume consumer timing protections apply. Obtain carrier insurance terms covering the actual electronics and route. Day 7 ends with verified permissions, a cash snapshot, and one executable small commitment. If onboarding takes longer, preserve the cash and continue documentation; the canonical first-deal window is one to three months.

## 2. Days 8–14: Run Small Tests Without Mixing the Three Economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Execute the wholesale test only after the commitment survives a final price and quantity check. For illustration, a $1,000 merchandise purchase has a written $1,010 reimbursement, $20 of eligible card rewards, $10 of conditional portal rewards, and $12 of shipping. The expected contribution is $28 before overhead, but the conservative order case excludes the conditional portal amount and equals $18. Neither figure includes the $1,000 borrowed principal as earnings. A further $25 unrecovered return cost makes the conservative result negative $7. Record this downside before dispatch, not after an exception appears. Use one eligible portal per transaction rather than assuming two portals can both attribute the same checkout. Record each reward once in its own lane, then link it to the merchandise lot for combined profitability analysis. The lot view must eliminate that linkage on consolidation to prevent duplicate revenue. A claimed uncapped 2%–4% stack is a hypothesis until the particular card, merchant, category, and portal terms support it. Keep screenshots and timestamps, but recognize that screenshots cannot override exclusions. This pilot's thin merchandise spread is not the scorecard's 35% retained-share assumption; it is a transaction-level test that may reject the candidate's economics.

Test the invoice-payment model separately, initially on paper. For a permitted $10,000 business invoice, an illustrative 2.9% processing fee costs $290 while a 2% reward on the invoice principal earns $200. The immediate spread is negative $90 before other costs. At an assumed 4% simple annual yield, thirty days of interest on $10,000 equals $32.88, leaving negative $57.12 before tax and any transfer friction. These are demonstration inputs, not current Plastiq or Melio quotes. Check the live fee, funding-card network, permitted recipient category, delivery timing, and issuer reward treatment for each proposed payment. Do not send a payment to yourself, recycle consumer credit, manufacture an invoice, or treat a credit limit as earned capital. A valid vendor invoice and permitted funding route are prerequisites. The launch pod should reject this example instead of attempting to repair it with an unearned bonus. A $10,000 demonstration also exceeds the pilot exposure cap and therefore cannot become a live test without a separately authorized budget change. Capture the rejected calculation as useful output: screening out negative unit economics is a successful launch task, not a reason to force transaction volume.

Keep treasury testing independent of both reimbursement and card rewards. P10 measures interest earned on genuinely surplus cash; P11 measures a bank bonus only after its balance, transaction, eligibility, and retention conditions are satisfied. At a hypothetical 4% simple annual yield, $5,000 held for thirty days earns $16.44 before tax. That does not support an expensive bank-account chase. Treasury bills purchased at a discount require their actual price and maturity proceeds in the ledger; the simple-yield example is only a screening approximation. Do not assume every purchase receives fifty-five days of statement float. Calculate the actual days from authorization and expected posting to the specific due date, and schedule repayment five business days early. The minimum-capital pilot may have no surplus eligible for a sweep; a documented zero allocation is the correct deliverable. Prefer instruments maturing before the repayment buffer starts, without relying on secondary market liquidation or a new transfer limit. Bank-deposit insurance and Treasury securities have different protections; aggregate covered business deposits by ownership category under 12 CFR 330.11. Record interest under IRC section 61 and document bonus tax treatment separately. Borrowed principal remains a liability throughout the holding period.

## 3. Days 15–21: Reconcile Cleared Cash and Stress the Repayment Calendar  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The third week is a collections exercise, not a sourcing contest. Reconcile four independent events for each lot: retailer charge, carrier delivery, warehouse acceptance, and cleared ACH. Match the reimbursement to accepted units and the written price rather than assuming the bank deposit proves full payment. Record shortages as disputes with an owner, amount, evidence link, and next contact date. A payout notification is not cleared cash; use the bank's available-balance status and retain awareness that an ACH credit can still be reversed in applicable circumstances. Measure settlement punctuality against the contractual due date, not a universal three-day assumption. For example, a five-business-day term beginning at acceptance must account for weekends and bank holidays. Freeze fresh commitments with a counterparty once its payment is five business days late, even when another counterparty continues paying. Contractor B prepares the match report; contractor A resolves quantity and acceptance discrepancies; the owner decides cash movement. Track open exposure by counterparty, issuer, warehouse, and carrier. This reveals correlated failure that a single total-profit cell hides. Keep the initial concentration unavoidable only while exposure is tiny; later expansion requires actual settlement history from multiple counterparties, not multiple untested account registrations.

Run the repayment stress in a dated cash calendar every morning. Move every pending buying-group ACH thirty calendar days later, remove all unpaid rewards, charge the applicable processor fees, and add the $250 shipment-loss event. Preserve every card due date and the five-business-day repayment buffer. The test passes only if unrestricted cash remains nonnegative on every date and reserve coverage stays at least 1.05. Existing Treasury holdings count only when proceeds will be available before the relevant payment deadline; projected liquidation proceeds do not repair a cash shortfall. Record statement closing dates because posting immediately before a close can sharply shorten the practical repayment interval. Do not make repeated repayments to push purchase volume beyond approved limits; operate within issuer terms and stop when an issuer requests documentation or restricts the account. Preserve invoices and business-purpose evidence for a financial review. An issuer shutdown is a liquidity event even if all underlying lots are profitable. Maintain a bank-funded repayment method that does not require obtaining another card. The owner's daily signature confirms the cash calculation, not optimism about tomorrow's reimbursement. Failed stress means halt new commitments and collect existing receivables before any further sourcing.

Reconcile the pilot dashboard to Formula v2.0 without forcing observed transactions to match a favorable annual assumption. The canonical gross revenue cases are $250,000, $1,025,000, and $1,800,000. Applying the specified 35% retained share gives $87,500, $358,750, and $630,000. Subtracting owner profits of $20,000, $143,500, and $360,000 leaves $67,500, $215,250, and $270,000 of other modeled costs. These imply retained margins of 22.86%, 40.0%, and 57.14%, respectively. Those cost residuals are not monthly overhead quotations: they may include variable operating costs beyond the stated $1,500–$9,000 monthly opex range. Keep the scorecard's retained-contribution bridge separate from the entity's actual ASC 606 principal-versus-agent presentation; a fixed percentage alone cannot determine accounting treatment. Underlying merchant sales and invoice principal are never additional operator revenue. Inventory costing under IRC section 471 and purchase-related price adjustments require consistent records; distinguish them from bank interest and service income. Reconcile any Form 1099-K under IRC section 6050W to transaction records rather than equating reported gross payments with profit. The Day 21 deliverable is a reproducible bridge from bank movement to lane contribution and consolidated owner result.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Extract the complete obligation set
   - Export authorized and posted card charges, bank balances, fees, and accepted wholesale lots.
   - Contractor B timestamps the extract; the owner adds pending authorizations missing from posted statements.
2. Match evidence before recognizing completion
   - Join purchase, serial, acceptance, reimbursement, and bank-clearance records by lot identifier.
   - A completed lot requires accepted delivery, matched cleared reimbursement, and a recorded reserve for unresolved return exposure.
3. Recalculate reserves under delayed collections
   - Delay all pending wholesale ACH thirty days, exclude unpaid rewards, and include fees plus the shipment-loss stress.
   - Test every payment date; use the larger actual expected shipment loss when it exceeds the $250 minimum stress.
4. Authorize only the next bounded commitment
   - The owner signs reserve coverage, remaining burn capacity, and counterparty exposure before purchasing.
   - A failed test blocks new purchases while collections, documented returns, claims, and scheduled card repayment continue.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Beyond: Apply Ordered Kill, Fix, or Scale Decisions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Spend the final nine days turning transaction history into an auditable decision, not a presentation built from projected rewards. Define realized contribution as cleared wholesale spread plus received eligible rewards and earned, settled treasury income, less merchandise cost, shipping, processor fees, interest, realized losses, and reserves for unresolved claims. When wholesale spread is already net of merchandise cost, do not subtract that cost again. Report each lane separately before consolidation. Define contribution yield as realized contribution divided by merchandise purchase principal plus external invoice principal, with each funded transaction counted once. Treasury-only performance uses average eligible cash and actual holding days instead; it cannot inflate the purchase-lane yield. Report owner result after contractor and overhead costs, and report contribution per owner hour separately. Completed-lot samples concern wholesale lots only; genuine invoice transactions and treasury placements have their own evidence counts. A count of five lots does not mean five products within one unresolved shipment. Use trailing thirty-day observations for Day 60 and Day 90 profitability tests and cumulative counts for settled lots. Keep rejected proposals in the funnel log, but never include them in revenue, completed volume, or settlement success statistics.

Apply decisions in strict order: Kill first, Scale second, and Fix otherwise. Kill means stop new exposure in the affected lane, preserve evidence, collect receivables, complete documented recoveries, and repay cards; it does not mean abandoning obligations. A shared liquidity failure stops all purchasing lanes. Kill immediately for reserve coverage below 1.05, a projected missed repayment, prohibited transaction mechanics, an issuer prohibition, or cumulative nonrecoverable burn above $1,500 at Day 30, $3,000 at Day 60, or $4,500 at Day 90. These ceilings require renewed owner authorization for each subsequent month, rather than automatic spending permission. After at least ten completed lots, negative realized purchase-lane contribution also kills that route. Day 30 Scale requires five completed lots, at least 1.5% contribution yield, at least 95% on-time settlements, and no unresolved repayment or claim exception. With five observations, the punctuality test effectively requires all five to be on time. Scale means increase the unsettled exposure cap by at most 25%, not annualize one promising week. With zero completed deals but sound controls, choose Fix and keep the original exposure ceiling; the one-to-three-month first-deal window makes incomplete Day 30 evidence plausible.

Day 60 Scale requires fifteen cumulative completed lots across at least two paying counterparties, trailing contribution yield of at least 2%, at least 95% on-time settlements, nonnegative trailing owner result, and at least $40 contribution per owner hour. No counterparty may hold more than 60% of unsettled wholesale exposure. Day 90 raises the sample to thirty completed lots, punctuality to 98%, and contribution per owner hour to $60; require at least $1,500 trailing owner result and maintain the other Day 60 tests. Neither threshold claims the Base annual case is proven. Continue with one full-time owner and two contractors, each limited to five weekly pilot hours, unless observed workload and cash earnings support a deliberate staffing change. The Scaled canonical case uses two full-time people and three contractors, not the launch pod disguised as five available workers. Fix decisions receive a fourteen-day repair plan with one measurable cause, such as delayed acceptance matching or excessive shipping cost. Maintain or reduce exposure during repair. If the same economic defect survives two repair cycles and the lane still produces negative realized contribution, retire that route. Increase only the lane that passes; a treasury bonus cannot rescue loss-making wholesale fulfillment.
