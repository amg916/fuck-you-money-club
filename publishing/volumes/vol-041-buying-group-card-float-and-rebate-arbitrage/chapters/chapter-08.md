## Executive Summary

Practitioners treat the no-fee Ink Cash and Ink Unlimited as the entry rung of a Chase business stack: sole proprietors apply on an SSN, balances stay off personal reports, and 0% APR covers the first 12 months. Both cards were pushed to an all-time-high 100,000-point bonus on $8,000 spend in 4 months, and speaker after speaker routes the points into a Sapphire or Ink Preferred to unlock Hyatt, Aeroplan and Air France transfers. Street mechanics center on the Ink Cash 5x bucket: phone and internet autopay, office-supply gift cards bought fee-free, and a 125,000-point annual ceiling. Disagreement runs on which card comes first, with John Liang and Credit4CEO's split on Unlimited simplicity versus Cash categories, while Spencer Johnson logs roughly 5,000 points a year after the bonus and The Rewards Master reports the Ink Train being shut down.

## The Practitioner Consensus

The entry ticket is lower than most operators assume. John Liang says a formal LLC or C-Corp is unnecessary: anyone who has flipped items on eBay, driven for Uber or run any side hustle generating revenue qualifies as a sole proprietor and fills their Social Security number into the EIN field twice, with a free IRS-issued EIN optional for anyone who wants to hand vendors a separate number [SkbED_I8KzM @ 00:00:41]. Cards and Points pushes the floor further, stating that a freelancer, independent contractor or gig worker needs no EIN, no separate bank account and no revenue yet, and can list a personal phone number and home address on the application [z9qhRLe7frA @ 00:01:15]. Credit4CEO's frames it as intent: if you intend to make money you are a business, and their own first business approval, the Ink Unlimited, landed before they had a company or any income, helped by existing Chase checking, savings and personal cards [uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:07:34]. The payoff every camp leans on is reporting. Ish of Just Money Ish says the personal guarantee pulls personal credit at approval, but the account never reports to the personal file unless you default, so a $5,000, $10,000 or $20,000-plus business balance can sit beside a strong personal score as long as no payment is missed, keeping the door open for further personal and business applications [04bDgAQcsYs @ 00:02:57].

Gatekeeping runs through Chase's 5/24 rule, and the speakers agree on how to sequence around it. Just Money Ish describes it as an unofficial rule that blocks a new Chase card if you opened five or more cards in the last 24 months, warns that authorized-user accounts eat a slot, and notes the business card needs that last open slot at application but does not consume it afterward because it never lands on the personal report [04bDgAQcsYs @ 00:01:32]. Rachel of Rachel | Points & Miles confirms the rule counts cards from every issuer and lays out the sequencing play: with four personal cards opened in two years, a Sapphire Reserve for Business approval does not push the count to five, because Chase business cards drop into a separate pool, so an Ink Business Unlimited application can follow while still under 5/24 [59DiBbciIn8 @ 00:12:56]. The second gate is bonus eligibility. CardsChooser says Chase may block the bonus if you ever held a no-fee Chase business card, so pick either the Ink Cash or the Ink Unlimited, and match the spend window to when your normal bills hit [pie5mBLwycU @ 00:00:27]. Miles with Mary states flatly that Chase lets you earn the welcome bonus on only one of the two no-fee Inks, so the second card gets added later purely for its everyday earning structure [z45WdojPWE8 @ 00:07:21].

The core value mechanic is identical across playbooks: the no-fee Inks earn Ultimate Rewards points marketed as cash back, and the real money comes from moving them. John Liang explains the Ink Cash and Ink Unlimited cannot transfer to airline or hotel partners directly; you combine the points into a Sapphire product or the Ink Preferred and transfer from there, pointing to Air France business class to Paris for 60,000 points against a $3,000 to $5,000 fare, and Hyatt nights at 30,000 to 45,000 points instead of $700 to $1,000 cash [SkbED_I8KzM @ 00:06:37]. Daily Drop calls it the cashback-to-points conversion that explains why they hold seven Chase business cards: paired with a points-earning Chase card, the Ink Cash 5% becomes 5x Ultimate Rewards and a $750 bonus becomes 75,000 points from a no-fee card [61XiQ9ahJjA @ 00:03:51]. Miles with Mary adds that the move happens in a few taps inside the Chase app, and that left standalone the points are worth one cent each [E07WIJs8BE0 @ 00:05:15]. The financing leg is equally agreed: Ish calls the 12-month 0% purchase APR, stacked with no personal reporting, one of the craziest plays in the credit game for funding a new business interest-free for a full year while the personal score stays intact [04bDgAQcsYs @ 00:04:02].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The Ink Cash 5x bucket is where the unit economics live. Miles with Mary runs the ceiling: maxing the $25,000 cap at 5x yields 125,000 points a year from office supply and telecom spend alone, enough in their framing for a roundtrip business class flight to Europe or three to five luxury Hyatt nights [E07WIJs8BE0 @ 00:02:58]. In the head-to-head, Mary turns that into a hurdle the Ink Unlimited cannot clear cheaply: the flat 1.5x card would need over $83,000 of total spend to match those 125,000 points, so if spending lines up with the Ink Cash categories it wins on pure math [z45WdojPWE8 @ 00:04:34]. Ask Sebby Business models the most common scaled use, commercial internet. Low-end business internet runs $500 a month and fiber $1,300 to $1,500; at $1,500 times 12 months, $18,000 of spend earns 90,000 bonus points, worth $900 at one cent or $1,800 at their two-cent target. Sebby is blunt that nobody should buy extra internet for fun; the play is capturing 5x on bills already being paid [oH1xNl2vB28 @ 00:04:30]. Daily Drop runs the small-operator version, a $250 monthly phone and internet bill producing $3,000 of annual spend and $150 back at 5%, and admits the number is not exciting until the points-conversion layer turns it into transferable Ultimate Rewards [61XiQ9ahJjA @ 00:03:36].

The street-level accelerator is office-supply gift cards run through the 5x category. Colleen of Leen on Travel says Staples and Office Depot each run fee-free Visa and Mastercard gift card windows twice a month; buying those with the Ink Cash earns 5x, and one month they logged 6,000 points by pushing $1,200 of grocery and utility spending through those cards [zK3uQ4upb_4 @ 00:00:43]. Their execution rules are tight: always add a soda or a pack of gum so the total never ends in 000, because round numbers look like manufactured spending; register a PIN on the card's website before use, since Aldi and most merchants require it; and at Office Depot buy two $200 cards rather than a $200 and a $100 card to capture the $15-off-$300 promo [zK3uQ4upb_4 @ 00:02:28]. The Rewards Master confirms the same sleeper angle, noting office supply stores often sell gift cards at a discounted rate so the 5x stacks on top, calling it what a lot of people do with this card even though they mostly use it for their phone bill [j9y58XP66lw @ 00:03:29]. Ask Sebby Business adds that Staples and Office Depot carry strong price-match guarantees and that a lot of people treat the category as a money printer and hit the $25,000 cap [oH1xNl2vB28 @ 00:04:02].

Application mechanics and limit engineering get specific. Credit4CEO's says the annual revenue field takes projected numbers: they entered $5,000 a month, about $60,000 a year, for an e-commerce store that did not exist yet, but they warn that a $20,000 monthly spend estimate against $60,000 of personal income and $60,000 of projected revenue raises red flags, so $2,000 to $3,000 a month is the realistic band, and trucking, real estate, cannabis and gambling categories read as high risk [uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:10:36]. Ish says Chase hands out $50,000 to $75,000 limits on this one card, but only through a relationship manager who takes the application in the branch and justifies the funding need to the underwriter; without one, expect $2,000, $10,000 or $12,000. Their checklist is a 700 score, no derogatories in two years, few hard inquiries in three to six months and one existing card at $5,000 or more [04bDgAQcsYs @ 00:06:39]. Spencer Johnson describes the bonus-reload loop: Ink bonuses become available again every two years, so you product change the current Ink Cash to a different Ink card and reapply fresh, or apply under a separate business, sole proprietorship on SSN versus an LLC, because Chase treats those as separate products and the welcome bonus can land multiple times [egM5zxfGtj0 @ 00:02:37].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Qualify and Sequence Under 5/24
   - Confirm you sit under five new cards in 24 months, keep personal utilization under 30% and stay below four Experian inquiries in 12 months, then land Chase business cards while the slot is open.
   - Approved Chase business cards drop into a separate pool and do not push you to five [Rachel | Points & Miles — 59DiBbciIn8 @ 00:12:56]; Credit4CEO's targets under 30% personal utilization and fewer than four Experian hard inquiries before applying [Credit4CEO's — uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:06:53].
2. Fill the Business Application Realistically
   - Select sole proprietor if applying on SSN, enter projected annual revenue, pick the closest NAICS category and set an estimated monthly spend that matches income and revenue.
   - Projected revenue of $5,000 a month, about $60,000 a year, was accepted for a pre-launch e-commerce store, while $2,000 to $3,000 monthly spend is the realistic band against $60,000 revenue; expect instant approval or a 7 to 10 business day review [Credit4CEO's — uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:10:36].
3. Clear the Minimum Spend and Load the 5x Bucket
   - Hit the $8,000-in-4-months threshold on the 100,000-point offer, then set phone, internet and cable bills to autopay and run office-store gift cards through the Ink Cash.
   - Both no-fee Inks offered 100,000 points after $8,000 in the first 4 months [John Liang — SkbED_I8KzM @ 00:02:47]; set the phone bill to the card and collect 5x every month [The Rewards Master — j9y58XP66lw @ 00:03:10]; one gift card month produced 6,000 points on $1,200 [Leen on Travel — zK3uQ4upb_4 @ 00:00:43].
4. Combine Points and Transfer Out
   - Move Ink points into a Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve or Ink Preferred inside the Chase app, then transfer to Hyatt, Aeroplan, United or Air France for outsized redemptions.
   - Points move card to card in the Chase app before transferring to partners [Miles with Mary - Points & Miles Travel — E07WIJs8BE0 @ 00:05:15]; a one-way Air France flight to Croatia priced at $2,762 cost 68,000 points plus $220, a 3.74 cents-per-point result [Spencer Johnson — egM5zxfGtj0 @ 00:09:49].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is which no-fee Ink to open first. John Liang, forced to pick one, leans Ink Unlimited purely for simplicity: 1.5% set-and-forget because a business owner has other things to worry about, reserving the Ink Cash for operators who know for a fact they spend at office supply stores or take clients out [SkbED_I8KzM @ 00:08:07]. Credit4CEO's normally ranks the Unlimited first for new businesses unsure where expenses will land, but flipped the order to Ink Cash first because its split offer paid $350 after $3,000 in three months plus $400 after $6,000 in six months, a lower spend bar than the Unlimited's $6,000 in three months [uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:12:47]. Miles with Mary sets a two-condition test: the Ink Cash wins only if real spend runs through office supplies, internet, cable and phone, and a flat-rate card like the Freedom Unlimited already covers everything else; maxing the $25,000 5x cap yields 125,000 points, which would take over $83,000 of spend on the Unlimited [z45WdojPWE8 @ 00:05:44]. The Rewards Master rejects the hedging entirely and calls the Ink Cash the best of the lineup, especially when paired with a Sapphire card for transfers, running a phone bill on it like clockwork every month [j9y58XP66lw @ 00:06:33].

Practitioners split hard on whether the Ink Cash keeps earning after the bonus lands. Spencer Johnson's one-year audit is the bear case: roughly $6,000 of the 2023 spend went to the welcome bonus and only about $800 to normal spending, generating 10,529 points beyond the 90,000-point bonus; the projection after year one is maybe 5,000 points a year, worth $50 in cash, $75 through the portal or $100 via transfer partners, and the verdict is that the card was mainly there for the welcome offer [egM5zxfGtj0 @ 00:11:36]. Ask Sebby Business runs the bull case on overhead: commercial internet at $1,500 a month is $18,000 a year, which at 5x is 90,000 points, $900 in cash or $1,800 at a 2-cent target, while office supply buyers lean on Staples and Office Depot price-match guarantees and treat the card as a money printer to hit the $25,000 cap [oH1xNl2vB28 @ 00:04:30]. Daily Drop lands in between, pricing a $250 monthly phone-and-internet bill at $150 a year and admitting it is not super exciting until the cash back converts to Ultimate Rewards points [61XiQ9ahJjA @ 00:03:36]. Leen on Travel pushes the ceiling with gift cards, earning 6,000 points in one month by routing $1,200 of groceries and utilities through fee-free Visa gift cards bought with the Ink Cash [zK3uQ4upb_4 @ 00:00:43].

Redemption philosophy splits the room as sharply as card choice. Hort frames cash back as a guaranteed win that stabilizes cash flow and points as a high-stakes game that demands learning transfer systems and checking award availability, which eats time for a busy business owner [udBIC2M_DhM @ 00:05:53]. Spencer Johnson shows why the points camp keeps playing: an Air France leg to Croatia with a cash value around $2,762 cost 68,000 points plus $220 in taxes, pricing out at 3.74 cents per point, while Southwest bookings never beat about 1.4 cents [egM5zxfGtj0 @ 00:09:49]. Miles with Mary runs a hybrid: book a $900 flight on another card earning 2x for 1,800 points, cash out 90,000 Ink Cash points as a $900 statement credit or mailed check, and pay the travel card off with it, so the points effectively beat 1 cent each [E07WIJs8BE0 @ 00:08:26]. On funding, Just Money Ish draws the hardest line, saying only get business cards with a 0% offer on purchases and stay far away otherwise, and that $50,000 to $75,000 limits take a relationship manager at a Chase branch who justifies the ask to the underwriter; without one, expect $2,000, $10,000 or $12,000 and that is it [04bDgAQcsYs @ 00:06:39].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The first wall is approval, and it trips people before they ever earn a point. Just Money Ish says Chase's unofficial 5/24 rule kills applications from anyone with five or more cards in 24 months, that authorized-user accounts eat a slot, and that the channel has seen many people denied because they opened too many cards; the pull hits Experian and sometimes TransUnion as well [04bDgAQcsYs @ 00:01:42]. Rachel | Points & Miles adds the counting nuance: the rule tallies personal cards from every issuer, but once approved, a Chase business card lands in a separate pool and does not push the count toward five, so the walkthrough stacks a Sapphire Reserve for Business and then an Ink Unlimited while staying under 5/24 [59DiBbciIn8 @ 00:12:56]. Credit4CEO's sets the soft thresholds: personal utilization under 30%, fewer than four Experian hard inquiries in 12 months, and a score of at least 680 [uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:06:53]. The same operator flags high-risk business types such as trucking, real estate, cannabis and gambling, and warns that a $20,000 monthly spend estimate against $60,000 of income raises red flags, so keep the number realistic [uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:10:36]. Pending reviews run 7 to 10 business days, and cards sometimes simply arrive in the mail.

Product-selection mistakes are the second trap. The Rewards Master knows someone who applied for the Ink Business Premier assuming the more expensive card was better, only to find its $195-fee rewards work as cash back or 1 cent per point and cannot be combined with other cards or transferred to partners [j9y58XP66lw @ 00:05:59]. Credit4CEO's adds the exit problem: an Ink Business Preferred that stops paying can be downgraded to a no-fee Ink, but the Premier has no downgrade path, so cancellation is the only way out [uvd7Aufi38A @ 00:16:03]. Even the right card decays. Spencer Johnson lost the internet and cable 5x once those bills moved into a rental portal, and points out the Ink Cash carries no cell phone protection, which is why that operator is willing to put phone bills on a lower-earning card that covers the phone rather than pay the carrier for protection [egM5zxfGtj0 @ 00:04:05]. Caps bite too: John Liang notes both the 5% and 2% buckets revert to 1% after $25,000 in a year, and the Ink Cash and Unlimited cannot transfer to airline or hotel partners without a Sapphire or Ink Preferred sitting in the ecosystem as an intermediary [SkbED_I8KzM @ 00:06:21].

The gift-card lane carries its own operational landmines, and Leen on Travel reports them firsthand: the fee-free Visa and Mastercard cards must be bought in store, branded cards can come from staples.com, but buying from the flagged office-store website codes as a third party rather than an office supply store; the cards fail at the gas pump, Costco takes only the Visa versions, and every transaction gets a soda or pack of gum added so the total never ends in 000 and looks like manufactured spend [zK3uQ4upb_4 @ 00:01:15]. The worst hit was a spoofed site that steals gift card numbers; typing the address fully avoids it, and the claim took about six weeks to produce a replacement, so receipts and front-and-back photos get saved [zK3uQ4upb_4 @ 00:02:59]. Bonus economics carry traps too. CardsChooser warns Chase may block the bonus if you ever had a no-fee Chase business card and that bonus clocks vary between 3 months, 180 days and 3 billing cycles [pie5mBLwycU @ 00:00:27], while The Rewards Master reports the Ink Train churn is being shut down because people were slamming it [j9y58XP66lw @ 00:02:00]. Leen's general rule still applies: category points have zero importance while a signup bonus is in progress.
