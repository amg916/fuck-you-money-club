## 1. Ring-fence liquidity before purchasing productive capacity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build three startup envelopes of $5,000, $15,000, and $30,000, not three excuses to buy different software. Allocate 50% to working-capital reserve, 10% to entity, banking, and insurance setup, 10% to contracts and accounting design, 8% to security and recovery, 10% to initial infrastructure, and 12% to prospect validation. The resulting reserves are $2,500, $7,500, and $15,000. Hold them in a separate business deposit account with a documented transfer rule; they are neither recognized revenue nor discretionary marketing cash. At the minimum $1,500 monthly recurring operating envelope, the smallest reserve provides only 1.67 months before collections. Since the first deal can take one to three months, a $5,000 launch requires deferred discretionary spending, separate living-expense coverage, or contracted early collections. Do not silently assume three months of runway. Quotes for insurance or contract review can exceed an envelope; reduce outreach purchases or increase committed capital rather than borrowing from the protected half. Spend setup money only against a receipt and a named operational dependency. An unused allocation remains cash, not permission to purchase another subscription. Reforecast available runway every Friday using settled balances, dated obligations, and collectible invoices rather than the CRM's probability-weighted pipeline.

Keep the scorecard and the cash budget on separate, reconciling schedules. Formula v2.0 specifies $100,000 revenue and $32,000 owner profit at 32%, $500,000 and $225,000 at 45.0%, and $900,000 and $522,000 at 58%. Their implied total annual costs are $68,000, $275,000, and $378,000. An illustrative fixed operating allocation of $1,500, $5,500, and $9,500 monthly consumes $18,000, $66,000, and $114,000 annually; the remaining cost allowances are $50,000, $209,000, and $264,000. Those allowances must absorb delivery labor, contractors, acquisition, applicable compensation, and other costs outside the fixed envelope without counting anything twice. They are reconciliation limits, not evidence that those costs have been quoted. Preserve teams of 1 FT + 3 C, 1 FT + 3 C, and 2 FT + 4 C respectively; contractor counts do not imply full-time utilization. Label the scorecard's gross revenue line as retained brokerage revenue in management reporting because customer software spend is excluded. Owner profit is the supplied model output, not a newly calculated personal after-tax distribution. If payroll, insurance, or acquisition quotes break these limits, change the delivery design or reject the operating plan rather than inflating revenue with pass-through subscription dollars.

Underwrite commitment exposure before activating annual Microsoft NCE seats. A customer ordering 100 seats at an illustrative $20 monthly wholesale cost creates $24,000 of annual supplier exposure before contract-specific cancellation rights. A $2,000 monthly debit does not prefund that obligation, and a $2,500 reserve cannot safely carry it. Require collected annual funding, explicit supplier-supported credit arrangements, or a genuinely shorter commitment permitted by the distributor; otherwise decline the order. Card authorization and ACH initiation are not settled cash, and settlement does not eliminate every return or dispute risk. Maintain separate ledgers for customer funding, supplier payables, earned fees, and reversal exposure. For an agency illustration, $1,000,000 of customer subscription spend generating a $100,000 commission pool with an 80% retained share produces $80,000 of operator revenue, not $1,000,000 or $100,000. The example does not assign an undocumented retained_share to the canonical scenarios. Apply the actual contract and ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis, then reconcile cash receipts to that treatment. A distributor's late rebate remains a receivable only when recognition criteria are satisfied; it cannot fund next week's supplier debit. Model a delayed payout and a customer default together before approving any material commitment.

## 2. Assemble twelve inexpensive layers around one auditable transaction  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one Google Workspace Business Starter identity at $7 monthly on annual commitment, one Bitwarden Teams seat at $4 monthly billed annually, one $5 Amazon Lightsail instance, and 20 GB of Amazon S3 Standard storage at $0.023 per GB-month in US East. That baseline is $16.46 monthly before requests, transfer, domain registration, payment fees, taxes, and any required seat minimums. It is deliberately much smaller than the $1,500 minimum operating envelope because software subscriptions are not the whole business. Increase identity and vault seats for actual workers, never shared logins; do not buy six paid CRM seats merely because the scaled team contains six people. Keep free-tier limits visible in the purchasing register. When a limit becomes binding, compare the paid upgrade against the hours it saves using a documented loaded labor rate. A $50 monthly upgrade needs more than one saved hour at $50 per hour to create positive direct value.

Implement saas_technographic_churn_pixel_scraping as a permission-aware public-signal collector, not a covert tracking deployment. Fetch public commercial pages and permitted DNS TXT/CNAME observations weekly, recording retrieval time, source, response status, and a content hash. Salesforce, HubSpot, Marketo, Segment, and NetSuite references can indicate technology use, but a script disappearance can also reflect a tag-manager change, caching, or blocked retrieval. Weekly polling identifies a seven-day observation interval, not the exact installation or removal day. Store last-seen and first-absent timestamps instead of inventing precision. For 1,000 domains and five observations per weekly cycle, budget 5,000 fetches weekly, or about 21,650 monthly using 4.33 weeks. At two seconds of serialized processing per fetch, that is roughly twelve processing hours monthly, mostly waiting rather than analyst labor. Rate-limit by host, honor access restrictions, avoid authenticated pages, and stop on sustained denial responses. Route only corroborated changes into the CRM; raw signal volume is not qualified pipeline.

At fifteen analyst minutes per change and $40 per hour, screening costs $400, or $100 per qualified conversation before other acquisition expenses. If the conversations do not produce enough retained contribution, reduce monitoring scope rather than accelerating outreach. Connect customer-approved opportunities to distributor workflows with stable customer, tenant, quote, and order identifiers. Pax8 and Ingram access, Adobe VIP Marketplace capabilities, and Telarus or Avant feeds depend on contracts and enabled interfaces; no generic integration promises universal CPOR or billing automation. Microsoft CPOR attribution does not itself transfer licenses or authorize tenant administration. Use supplier-supported exports until API permissions are confirmed. A nightly CSV reconciliation with exception ownership is safer than an untested write integration that duplicates seats, changes tenant assignments, or posts a rebate twice. Retain request identifiers so repeated jobs remain idempotent.

## 3. Make entity, treasury, and revenue evidence production-grade  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Form the operating entity in the jurisdiction justified by actual management and business activity, obtain its EIN directly from the IRS, and use the same legal name across banking, distributor applications, insurance, and customer contracts. A Delaware filing does not remove another state's registration or tax obligations. Document the responsible signatory and beneficial ownership information requested by the bank, then check current reporting requirements for the entity rather than assuming every domestic company has identical federal filing duties. Maintain operating, reserve, and customer-funding subledgers even if the bank offers only two physical accounts. FDIC coverage generally aggregates deposits by depositor, insured bank, and ownership category; multiple account labels do not automatically multiply protection. Require independent verification of changed supplier payment instructions through an established contact channel. A founder-only operation can use a delayed release and bank alerts until a second authorized reviewer exists, but should document that remaining concentration of control. Collect W-9 forms where appropriate, classify contractors from actual working conditions, and calendar state annual reports and applicable tax filings. These controls prevent entity confusion and payment diversion from consuming the same reserve that protects customer commitments.

Ask an insurance broker for technology errors-and-omissions and cyber quotations that describe brokerage, procurement recommendations, customer tenant access, and collection activity accurately. Review social-engineering, funds-transfer, contractual-liability, and professional-services exclusions instead of assuming a broad policy name covers supplier debt. Record deductibles and retention amounts in the liquidity model; insurance does not make an unfunded annual license commitment disappear. Customer agreements should state who buys licenses, who invoices whom, how savings are measured, which credits reduce fees, and what happens after cancellation or vendor repricing. For a $200,000 like-for-like renewal baseline reduced to $160,000, verified annual savings equal $40,000; a 25%–35% contingent fee yields $10,000–$14,000. At 30%, the invoice is $12,000, subject to the agreed earning and collection terms. Seat reductions, removed modules, different support levels, taxes, currency changes, and one-time credits need explicit normalization rules. Obtain written authority before requesting account details or submitting partner changes. For Adobe VIP Marketplace, Microsoft CSP, Zoom-related master-agent business, and private GPO membership, preserve the applicable supplier and member agreements. Never combine unrelated customers inside a pooled tenant to manufacture volume eligibility. A wholesale discount cannot cure an unauthorized license structure.

Make recognition policy traceable to each contract instead of hard-coding every receipt as commission income. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent guidance examines control before transfer; a reseller label alone does not decide presentation. For this candidate's scorecard, recognized operator revenue is the net retained fee basis, including eligible wholesale spreads, residuals, administrative rebates, and earned shared-savings fees, excluding customer subscription spend. Where actual contracts produce different accounting presentation, maintain an explicit bridge to the scorecard and have the accountant resolve statutory reporting treatment. Public-company revenue rules are not a universal filing mandate for a small private brokerage. Separately, outbound acquisition must reflect the channel used. Apply CAN-SPAM requirements to commercial email, including accurate headers, identification, address, and functioning opt-out handling. Maintain suppression records centrally rather than merely removing contacts from one campaign. Review 47 U.S.C. 227 and 47 CFR 64.1200 before automated calls or texts, and review 16 CFR Part 310 for applicable telemarketing restrictions, including business-to-business misrepresentation prohibitions. Do not treat a business phone number as blanket permission for automated outreach. Keep approved scripts and source records with the opportunity so complaints, disputes, and supplier reviews can be answered from evidence rather than recollection.

## 4. Prove readiness with a funded order and a reversible failure drill  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run one complete dry transaction before introducing live customer funds. Choose a supplier-supported test environment where available; otherwise use internal records and clearly separated synthetic orders that never submit purchases. Create a customer identifier, capture authority, attach a quote, calculate retained revenue, simulate collection, and reconcile the supplier statement. For a $10,000 customer invoice with $9,000 attributable supplier cost under an applicable agency arrangement, the spread is $1,000, not $10,000 of scorecard revenue. At the matrix's domestic card planning rate, collection costs $290.30, leaving $709.70 before other operating costs; capped ACH processing would cost $5 under the stated schedule, leaving $995. Actual authorization, settlement timing, return exposure, and account pricing still determine the collection design. Do not extrapolate those illustrative contribution figures into replacement annual scorecard margins. Then replay the same statement and webhook identifiers to prove that no duplicate invoice, seat order, or commission is created. An operator should be able to trace every amount from signed terms through earning evidence to the bank entry without joining records by customer display name. Preserve the dry-run artifacts as the template for first-deal review.

Exercise the failures that can overwhelm a small team: an ACH return after provisioning, a duplicate supplier statement, a compressed partner rebate, a lost administrator device, and a customer requesting an unsupported transfer. Assign each failure a person, a response deadline, and a bounded financial exposure. A $50,000 eligible monthly spend base at a 4% retained rebate produces $2,000; compression to 3% removes $500 monthly, or $6,000 annually if sustained. That loss must reduce forecast contribution unless contract terms or operating changes genuinely offset it. Test recovery by restoring evidence and the SQLite database into a clean environment, then checking record counts, hashes, and access restrictions. Set an initial recovery-point target of one business day and a recovery-time target of four business hours for internal records; do not advertise these as supplier-backed service commitments. Revoke a test worker's access and verify loss of vault, mailbox, CRM, and cloud permissions. Keep emergency credentials offline with controlled access. A backup that cannot be restored and an account that cannot be revoked are production defects, even when the subscription invoice is only a few dollars.

Release the first live order only after commercial authority, funding sufficiency, revenue mapping, and recovery evidence all pass the checks below. For the first thirty days, review supplier liabilities and settled collections daily, exceptions twice weekly, and acquisition economics weekly. Keep the founder responsible for supplier commitments; contractors can research public signals, reconcile approved statements, and prepare customer documentation within scoped permissions. Expand fixed spend only after recurring retained contribution covers the proposed recurring expense and the reserve remains intact under a payout delay. If a $300 monthly automation service saves six hours at $40 per hour, its $240 labor benefit does not justify the purchase on labor savings alone. Measure additional error reduction separately rather than asserting it. Keep a twelve-week cash forecast with dated receipts, contractual supplier debits, payroll, contractor milestones, and rebate-reversal scenarios. Reconcile the forecast to actual bank activity every week and record why each material variance occurred. A lean launch is complete when a funded, authorized transaction can be collected, fulfilled, reconciled, and recovered by the assigned team without improvisation. It is not complete merely because twelve vendor accounts exist or a dashboard shows a large pipeline.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the funding envelope
   - Separate at least half of startup capital into tracked reserve.
   - Document the remaining five allocations, dated obligations, and the twelve-week cash forecast before purchasing annual services.
2. Provision the minimum transaction path
   - Create individual identities and connect approved supplier records.
   - Record actual prices, permissions, customer identifiers, and evidence storage; begin with controlled exports where APIs are unavailable.
3. Rehearse collection and reconciliation
   - Run a synthetic order through fees, settlement, and statement matching.
   - Test duplicate events, partial credits, and payout delays without placing an unintended live purchase or charging a customer.
4. Release one funded live order
   - Approve the order only after all four readiness proofs pass.
   - Inspect the first settlement and supplier liability manually, retain the evidence, and resolve exceptions before increasing volume.

> - Reconcile a sample order to net retained revenue and replay its events without creating duplicate seats, invoices, or commissions.
