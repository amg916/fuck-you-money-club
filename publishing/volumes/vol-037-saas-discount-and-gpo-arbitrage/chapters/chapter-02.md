## Executive Summary

Pax8 and Microsoft operators converge on one playbook: get enrolled in Microsoft Commerce Incentives early, clear the $25K trailing-12-month revenue floor plus a designation or 25 skilling-driven capability points, and stop leaning on the core incentive, which FY27 strips from Modern Work and Business Apps. Money now sits in Tier 2 strategic SKUs at 7%, the growth accelerator jumping from 7.5% to 12.5%, and tiered Azure growth at 12/10/7%. Payout splits 60% rebate to 40% co-op, with first payment roughly 75 days out. CPOR claims double Modern Work deployment points without touching CSP rebates. The managed intelligence pivot layers fixed-fee assessments, scoped builds and workflow-priced management on Copilot and Agent 365, though David Shaffron expects only 5% of MSPs to go all-in while 90% bolt it onto existing services.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every incentive operator on the Pax8 and Microsoft side agrees the gate into Microsoft Commerce Incentives is a stack, not a single checkbox. You need CSP indirect reseller enrollment, MCI enrollment itself, $25K of trailing 12-month revenue per location ID, and then either a Solutions Partner designation, a specialization, or 25 capability points, and Pax8 says 20 to 25 of those points are reachable from skilling alone [tsL0gg_Jy9U @ 00:11:07]. The Inside Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program walkthrough confirms the same bar, adds that eligibility is checked monthly with a five-month lookback, and pushes operators to chase skilling points because scores refresh once a month rather than live [jcYTmMdEGPo @ 00:08:04]. Sherweb frames the drop from the old 70-point-plus-$5,000-fee route to the 25-point route as a net win, since the new path carries no fee and no internal-license requirement, while legacy Silver and Gold memberships die as a qualifier from October 1 [kD54qMCh58A @ 00:02:23]. Pax8's September update repeats the designation-or-25-points rule plus the $25K floor, which makes it the one number every speaker states identically [IXc8Kp4hrKo @ 00:11:49]. The shared instruction is blunt: enroll before you qualify so earnings start the month you cross the line instead of leaking for months.

The second point of agreement is that the FY27 rate card killed easy money on renewals. Pax8 reports the core incentive is gone for Modern Work, Security and Business Apps, and the upside moved into Tier 2 at 7%, which now carries E7, Agent 365 and Copilot Studio [tsL0gg_Jy9U @ 00:13:05]. Vandit's October refresher states the CSP growth accelerator climbs from 7.5% to 12.5%, Copilot Business and the business bundles drop into Tier 1, and Azure keeps a 3% consumption core with a tiered growth accelerator of 12% for Fabric, 10% for Foundry, Studio credits and Defender for Cloud, and 7% for every other workload [1RKZdigqOf4 @ 00:12:20]. The Microsoft enrollment walkthrough prices Tier 1 Modern Work at 2.5%, Tier 2 at 7%, global calling at 20%, and flags Dragon Copilot as a 7% strategic, 12.5% growth line paid 100% as rebate with no points required [jcYTmMdEGPo @ 00:02:44]. The September update fills in that the removed core had been worth 3 to 4% on Modern Work, Security and Business Applications [IXc8Kp4hrKo @ 00:13:06]. Read together, the consensus is that a partner who simply renews Business Premium seats now earns thin strategic accelerators, while net-new, expansion and premium-SKU motion is where the percentage points live.

On the managed intelligence side, the consensus sequence is security and governance first, then Copilot expands as each customer becomes ready, the same motion behind Sourcepass's single Copilot Studio agent for a logistics client spinning into three more engagements with the same customer [wMz3Phqj6cA @ 00:02:32]. Brittany Tremblay and Scott Fox frame the line in one sentence, managed service providers manage systems while managed intelligence providers manage outcomes, and stack Business Premium, Copilot Business, Purview and Defender for Business Premium, then Agent 365 as the control plane for agents [z4JbWkKJagI @ 00:06:26]. David Shaffron insists discover comes before build, because sometimes the answer is a process change, scripting or plain automation rather than AI [HLMRv3YJ-sI @ 00:17:57]. The Beyond EMEA panel lands in the same place from the field: VitSecure found roughly 400 automatable tickets inside its own shop, Gratopp IT stood up a dedicated data and AI department, and the panel's rule is that a process with no clear beginning and end should never be started [t4mdVMK1xl0 @ 00:20:35]. Across these operators, discovery work opens the account, the roadmap stays fixed when shiny tools appear, and recurring monthly management of outcomes is the revenue goal.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Enrollment itself is a 20-to-30-minute, five-step job that requires the Incentives Admin role in Partner Center [jcYTmMdEGPo @ 00:11:57]. If the program tile reads 'Invited,' click the program name rather than hunting for a button, enable third-party cookies before the forms load, and file a W-9 for US entities or a W-8BEN-E otherwise; bank transfer is the only payout method, and you must assign payment profiles separately for co-op, fee, indirect and rebate [jcYTmMdEGPo @ 00:18:34]. Validation can take up to 48 hours, earnings surface after about 45 days, and the first payment lands around day 75, with the walkthrough host telling partners to give it two to three days at most before coming back to validate. The money splits 60% rebate, meant as profit and paid monthly, to 40% co-op marketing funds that accrue for six months before use [jcYTmMdEGPo @ 00:01:58]. TBSC's Madison treats co-op as use-it-or-lose-it on a roughly six-month refresh, while rebates come back at 100% with no deadline pressure; claims run through the Incentives tile into Co-op management with invoices as proof, and Madison pushes partners to submit within 45 days of purchase and set the payment profile early to avoid delays [9veqF5Bgt4E @ 00:13:10].

CPOR is the points lever. Attribution runs per workload rather than per subscription, only one partner can hold a workload, and a partner can hold CPOR and CSP on the same customer [uHFljjNXztI @ 00:02:45]. On the Modern Work SMB scorecard a CPOR deployment scores 5 against CSP's 2.5, and usage growth scores 0.06 against 0.015, which is why the host says only Modern Work pays more points for the core association. Before filing, gather the customer contact name, title and email, the tenant ID, estimated seats, a start date inside 12 months, and an incentives admin or user role, then pick one proof-of-execution route: the digitally signed Microsoft template, or an existing SOW plus a customer acknowledgment email saved as .MSG with a two-to-three-sentence description per workload [uHFljjNXztI @ 00:10:11]. Synergy Advisors walks the click path: Partner Center, Incentives, Customer claims, modern work and security, Add customer, partner role 'use or consume,' then claim name, partner location ID, domain and tenant ID before uploading POE [KNnxTcJ4cEE @ 00:00:23]. Split uncontested and contested workloads onto separate claims because one denial rejects the whole claim, paste descriptions into comments since nothing autosaves, and start with one claim, then three, then five [uHFljjNXztI @ 00:23:37].

The managed intelligence offer is priced in three layers: a fixed-fee assessment, a scoped delivery project, and a monthly management fee priced on the workflow rather than hours, with credits and consumption billed separately [z4JbWkKJagI @ 00:24:47]. Tremblay and Fox cite a Gradoop assessment of three surveys over 11 days with eight staff that surfaced about 850 recoverable hours, roughly half an FTE, and a proposal workflow cut from 48 hours to 20 that freed 32 hours for 16 more proposals inside a 30-day pilot. One Beyond EMEA panelist landed a 'big number' proposal with a planned move to MRR, charging monthly after the first 12 months at a token and hosting cost described as almost nothing, and Marcus Gratopp earned money from the first month [t4mdVMK1xl0 @ 00:27:32]. On the licensing margin, Pax8 now runs an 80/20 partner-to-Pax8 split on E1, E3, Exchange Online, SharePoint Online, OneDrive storage and M365 Apps after Microsoft cut margin, and will not honor partner cost adjustments [IXc8Kp4hrKo @ 00:07:36]. Growth margin locks at transaction, already includes base margin rather than stacking on top, and qualifies through new-to-offer with no purchase in three years, mid-term seat expansion on a new subscription, or a strategic SKU-mix seat-ratio test [tsL0gg_Jy9U @ 00:07:16].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split is over how hard to pivot. Rex frames managed intelligence as S-curve “curve jumping” toward digital labor, but David Shaffron pushes back: roughly 5% of MSPs will go lock, stock and barrel into a new business model, 5% will do nothing and pretend it is not happening, and the remaining 90% will bolt managed intelligence onto the business they already run [HLMRv3YJ-sI @ 00:06:10]. The Pax8 Beyond EMEA panel leans toward urgency with a blunt “Don't wait” [t4mdVMK1xl0 @ 00:24:03], while Gert Jan of VitSecure turns that urgency into a low-risk entry point: test with your top five clients first. The second fork is where to start. Pax8 runs the customer-zero play, with its entire dev team and Pax8 Labs building AI solutions on Microsoft tools [wMz3Phqj6cA @ 00:00:59], and Microsoft backs internal use with up to 10,000 Copilot Studio credits a month [tsL0gg_Jy9U @ 00:23:43]. Ian Glofs of StarTech skipped customer zero entirely and started outside the business with a care-sector customer, while VitSecure looked inward and surfaced roughly 400 automatable tickets on its own desk. Both paths produced proof; the real decision is whether the first reference account is internal or paying.

Practitioners also split on whether AI is even the right tool. David Shaffron insists the discover play comes first because “sometimes the answer is as simple as let's change that business process,” and scripting or plain automation can beat an agent [HLMRv3YJ-sI @ 00:18:19]. The EMEA panel delivered the proof: one client employee wasted roughly 1,600 hours a year finding parts, a problem solvable through the ERP rather than AI [t4mdVMK1xl0 @ 00:26:20]. Brittany Tremblay and Scott Fox of Pax8 push it further, warning that automating a messy process just moves the mess faster and that partners should build only when the customer's work actually justifies it [z4JbWkKJagI @ 00:23:00]. On the opposite side, Austin Hampton of PIA sells 100+ ticket types at L0/L1 as packaged automation on usage-based pricing, and reports Restec replacing four L1 technicians with two lower-skill staff while closing 98 more tickets per week [IXc8Kp4hrKo @ 01:23:39]. The broker-versus-builder fork runs along the same line. The MIP assessment sorts partners into brokers, who buy from the Agent Store or partner with others, and builders, who also buy. The panel's own low light argues against going solo on the builder path: an owner coding alone left the entire team behind.

Monetization playbooks diverge just as sharply. Brittany Tremblay and Scott Fox price in layers: a fixed fee to assess, a scoped project to deliver, a monthly fee to manage that is priced on the workflow rather than hours, and usage credits or spend billed separately [z4JbWkKJagI @ 00:24:47]. The EMEA panel admitted it has no recurring model yet for its efficiency meetings, yet one member landed a “big number” proposal and now charges MRR after the first 12 months at a cost that is “almost nothing in token usage and hosting fees” [t4mdVMK1xl0 @ 00:27:32]. Rahul Bhavsar stays with classic services math: implementation at man-days times daily rate, migration as tooling plus effort, and managed support under an SLA [5WTYMwbF2fc @ 00:13:31]. Assessments divide the same panel, with one camp using them to “buy time” across about 50 customers and another logging over-focus on AI-readiness assessments as a low light. On incentive eligibility, Sherweb notes the 70-point designation route with a $5,000 USD enrollment fee still works, but the 25-point route carries no fee and no internal licenses [kD54qMCh58A @ 00:02:55]. Even payout terms conflict: Microsoft's walkthrough fixes a 60/40 rebate-to-co-op split, while TBSC's Madison says splits vary by partner.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The costliest silent failure is assuming MCI enrollment is finished. Inside Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program reports partners who believed they were enrolled and then saw no earnings for months; the fix is to open a ticket the moment any status is not green [jcYTmMdEGPo @ 00:24:16]. The mechanical snags stack up: a program stuck on “Invited” needs a click on the program name, third-party cookies must be enabled, bank transfer is the only payout method, scores refresh once a month against a 5-month lookback, earnings surface after about 45 days, and the first payment lands after about 75. TBSC's Madison flags the claim-side trap: only partner upfront or bulk purchases qualify, never end users buying through you, co-op is use-it-or-lose-it on a roughly 6-month refresh, and claims belong inside 45 days of purchase [9veqF5Bgt4E @ 00:02:29]. Margin math also shifted under partners' feet. The July price file showed 0% Copilot margin until Microsoft fixed it on August 1, with Pax8 committing to remediate July purchases [tsL0gg_Jy9U @ 00:06:01]. Growth margin is not a bonus layer either: “growth margin already includes base margin. It is not stacked on top” [tsL0gg_Jy9U @ 00:07:16]. Meanwhile the core incentive for modern work, security and business apps is gone.

CPOR is where sloppy paperwork kills points. The Inside Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program walkthrough lists the traps in order: start dates older than 12 months are likely rejected, a domain mismatch triggers an error, contact information locks after submission, and the comments field does not autosave, so workload descriptions must be pasted in deliberately [uHFljjNXztI @ 00:21:02]. The biggest trap is bundling. One denial rejects the whole claim, so unassociated workloads go on one claim and contested ones on another, because any workload already held by another partner goes to arbitration on proof of execution [uHFljjNXztI @ 00:23:37]. Reviewers are non-technical, partners get 30 days to dispute, points take about two months to surface, and the customer can opt out through Microsoft's email. Workload greed backfires as well: low-usage picks such as Viva, or Teams for a 12-user single-room customer, are flagged to avoid. CPOR now carries no incentives and does not touch CSP or rebates, so it is a points play, not a cash play. Synergy Advisors sets the proof bar at a customer-signed SOW plus the customer email saved as .MSG, after which Microsoft emails the customer for approval [KNnxTcJ4cEE @ 00:01:43]. As the walkthrough puts it, “Nothing about the Microsoft scorecard is quick” [uHFljjNXztI @ 00:31:04].

Delivery failures came from operators who lived them. The EMEA panel admits that pushing Copilot licences after a single session “doesn't work,” and warns that a process with no clear beginning and end should never be started [t4mdVMK1xl0 @ 00:17:17]. Brittany Tremblay and Scott Fox demand that every agent carry a named owner, least-privilege access, a registry entry and a credit limit, because “an agent nobody owns is a liability with a login,” and they warn that Agent 365 does not grant users E5 security and that Copilot is not a prerequisite for it [z4JbWkKJagI @ 00:30:34]. Billing exposure arrives on a fixed date: from December 1, Copilot usage-based billing is on by default, auto-creates an Azure plan and presets a limit of 4,000 credits per user per month, though partners can switch the feature off [1RKZdigqOf4 @ 00:08:36]. Mark Hammer of Acronis, a former MSP owner of 13 years, describes a 48-hour persistent session that bypassed MFA; the recovery was restoring an Entra ID backup from two weeks earlier, and the standing advice is to limit privileged accounts [tsL0gg_Jy9U @ 01:07:48]. The same session warns that an MSP relationship is tested like at no other time than during a recovery motion.
