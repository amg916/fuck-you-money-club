# Chapter 08: Contingency Cost Reduction and Telecom Expense Management Audits

*Contingency-fee expense consulting, consulting cost-cutting frameworks, telecom bill audits with credits, refunds, and forward savings, the role of a TEM specialist, mobile fleet TEM, UCaaS expense management for Zoom, Sage expense automation, and OMNIA spend visibility and private-equity group purchasing*

## Executive Summary

Contingency cost-reduction shops and telecom expense management (TEM) auditors converge on one pitch: nobody pays until savings land, vendors usually stay in place, and the money hides in auto-paid invoices nobody reads. Auditel pulled an $8,100 back-billing refund off a single $1,200-a-month account and a $165,000 credit on a go-behind audit; brightfin recouped roughly $56,000 from a switch left live in a closed office. Street mechanics run on a 12-month general ledger, rate benchmarking that cut some contract rates by more than 50%, disconnecting zero-usage lines, and policing carrier orders until they bill correctly. Calero MDSL extends the playbook to Zoom and Teams license reclamation. Practitioners split on outsourcing versus internal audits (15-40% versus 3-5% reductions), on fee models from contingency share to retainer and hourly, and on whether to swap suppliers or squeeze incumbents.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator here starts from the same diagnosis: the money leaks through invoices nobody reads. ExpenseSavvy's host watched loan applicants obsess over revenue while their vendors sat on auto pay, with zero transparency into what the business down the street pays the same vendor for the same account [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:01:28]. brightfin hit the textbook version when pulling a new client's telecom invoices: the company had moved out of an office, emailed the carrier to shut the switch off, the switch never got shut off, and finance kept paying because nobody told finance the office had closed; brightfin recouped roughly $56,000 of that erroneous billing [lxObl7Zpr2A @ 00:03:06]. Auditel reports the same pattern on forward-savings work, where customers closed smaller satellite offices and neglected to cancel services, which the Auditel presenter says happens more than you might think [F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:02:06]. RadiusPoint goes further and says flatly that every client it was hired by faced waste and overspending in telecom, most were overpaying carriers, and none could trace their device inventory to a number, a location, and an end user ID [mFoRTIncoOI @ 00:03:17]. The consensus prerequisite is therefore inventory and visibility before negotiation: you cannot cut what you cannot map back to a live user or site.

The commercial consensus is just as tight: the client pays nothing up front and the shop eats only from savings it actually lands. Expense Consulting says it costs nothing to get started, the firm simply shares the savings it finds, claims it has found savings for 100% of its clients, and says those clients now average savings in excess of 20% per year, with most saving within weeks [o2wyRpIYL0w @ 00:01:34]. Kirk Canol of DCI Solutions pitches the identical no-risk structure, getting paid only after the client gets savings, and backs it with hit rates: money recovered every time, savings 80% of the time in freight, telecom, and utilities, 70% of the time in insurance, and half the time in office and warehouse supplies or refundable sales tax and unclaimed tax credits [meAxKn8Td5k @ 00:00:16]. Auditel frames telecom audits as contingency-based, meaning the client pays nothing if nothing is found, a no-risk, no-lose alternative to continuing to overpay [YAHf-FXxBCM @ 00:01:52]. ExpenseSavvy runs the free analysis, charges nothing for the work, and takes a portion of savings only once the client starts saving [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:03:51]. The second shared rule is vendor neutrality: keep the incumbent, cut the price, a line DCI and Expense Consulting both repeat to clients.

The levers themselves line up across the generalist and specialist camps. Startup Akademia lays out four directions for any cost base: reduce usage, automate, optimize the process and cost level, and renegotiate contracts, with usage cuts running from fully eliminating an expense to changing the specification, using less of something or swapping in a cheaper substitute [T9lHBjJeZVI @ 00:00:14]. The TEM shops execute exactly those levers on telecom: killing dead lines, downgrading plans, and renegotiating rates. Mark from Calero MDSL, after 20 years in expense management, sequences it as visibility first, cost ownership second, and says real cost reductions show up once you foster that spirit of cost ownership down to department heads and individual employees who can reclaim and return their own licenses [bQwapQbcKSk @ 00:18:23]. OMNIA Partners' enterprise team adds the unglamorous prerequisite: the data has to be cleaned before it can be categorized, because the same supplier name can appear 20 times in one data file, and without GL coding nobody even knows what some suppliers do for the organization [qPpAutcFjBw @ 00:01:28]. Scope is broad by design; ExpenseSavvy says there are savings across the board in waste, telecom, and insurance, with hundreds of line items in play [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:04:49].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Intake is deliberately light, opening with a 10-minute call. ExpenseSavvy needs exactly one document, a 12-month general ledger exported from the client's accounting software; its partner's technology runs that ledger against millions of data points sorted by industry, employee count, and location, then returns a report along the lines of 'we think we can save you X dollars annually on these 10 line items,' pitching potential savings of up to 25% of operational expenses [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:02:29]. The ideal target, per ExpenseSavvy, is any business doing 500,000 to 200 million in revenue, and the firm does not attack all 100 vendors at once; it starts with three to five and negotiates new agreements with the existing vendors [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:03:32]. Auditel's benchmarking lever is the sharpest in the set: on a customer that simply was not paying attention when renewing its contract, rate benchmarking cut rates by a considerable margin, sometimes more than 50% [F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:01:49]. For portfolio-scale buyers, OMNIA Partners says it gathers data, cross-maps where its group purchasing programs fit, and typically starts identifying categories and implementing within two to three weeks, including one PE-level 'cube of cube' rolling up 30-plus portfolio companies [QYe90AHibvQ @ 00:03:11].

The audit itself splits into two revenue streams. The first is look-back money: Auditel found a $139-a-month overcharge on a $1,200-a-month account, corrected it going forward, then dug through historical billing and pulled an $8,100 refund on that one account alone [YAHf-FXxBCM @ 00:01:02]. On a client already audited twice by other firms, Auditel ran what it calls a go-behind audit, found multiple billing errors in data accounts, and obtained a $165,000 credit on a single account, with more credits beyond that across the engagement [YAHf-FXxBCM @ 00:01:19]. The second stream is forward savings: on a bill running $68,000 a month, Auditel killed months-dead lines, benchmarked rates, and cancelled services at closed satellite offices, landing the bill just under $36,000, a saving of a little under $33,000 a month on one account; two smaller accounts dropped from about $5,600 and $6,200 a month to a little over $900 each [F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:02:21]. Singlesource Telecom flags what the auditor actually needs to make those disputes stick: access to past and present tariffs, contracts, and rates, plus the staff hours to contact carriers and negotiate refunds, which can be the majority of total project time on an internal run [GcLqzAGpP2s @ 00:01:02].

Platform-driven TEM adds in-cycle optimization that a one-time audit cannot touch. brightfin staff manually review usage and, when someone has barely used a $100 plan, drop it before the invoice cycle closes so the bill lands at $10; when five phones out of a 30,000-device fleet eat all the data streaming Disney Plus, brightfin blocks Disney Plus on work phones [lxObl7Zpr2A @ 00:08:22]. The same shop flags the closet problem: 50 recouped phones at $100 a month is $5,000 buried in a $400,000 invoice, easy to miss month after month [lxObl7Zpr2A @ 00:07:47]. Calero MDSL's Zoom marketplace app pulls license and usage data straight from Zoom's APIs, automates moves, adds, and changes, downgrades Pro users who only join calls to Basic, and reclaims idle licenses for redeployment; it also catches stacking, where one heavy Zoom user holds licenses for one, two, or even three other similar products, and feeding HR data in lets it flag departed employees still holding active licenses and reclaim them automatically [bQwapQbcKSk @ 00:14:25]. RadiusPoint runs nine processes, closing the loop by disputing billing errors, recovering credits and refunds, and tracking vendor invoices that are not arriving on time so late fees and service interruptions are avoided [mFoRTIncoOI @ 00:02:27].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Pull the ledger and book the findings meeting
   - Collect a 12-month general ledger and run the free savings analysis.
   - ExpenseSavvy needs only a 12-month general ledger from the client's accounting software, runs the analysis, then meets again to walk through findings before signing the client [ExpenseSavvy — 2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:04:32].
2. Inventory every asset to a user
   - Map lines, services, data circuits, and mobile devices to end users and locations.
   - RadiusPoint's first processes are inventorying and auditing telecom assets and services, then assigning those assets to end users and tracking usage to detect abuse and device loss [RadiusPoint — mFoRTIncoOI @ 00:01:46].
3. Audit against contract and dispute the gap
   - Check every charge against contract terms and raise disputes on anything extra.
   - TEM teams audit each invoice to confirm charges match the contract and raise a dispute on any additional charge, because even a small amount recovered is savings for the client [Ancy Alphonse — RV9K4EXWxJs @ 00:02:42].
4. Place the orders and police them until they bill right
   - Present recommendations, place approved orders, and monitor them through correct billing.
   - Auditel places the orders with the provider, follows up, and confirms correct billing, because providers sometimes place orders incorrectly or not at all while the accounts keep billing [Auditel, Inc. Telecom Expense Management — F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:01:18].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The cleanest split on the street is how the auditor gets paid. Expense Consulting pitches pure share-of-savings: it costs nothing to get started, the firm simply shares the savings it finds, says it has found savings for 100% of its clients, and claims an average savings in excess of 20 percent per year, with most clients saving within weeks [o2wyRpIYL0w @ 00:01:34]. Auditel runs the same no-find, no-fee contingency on telecom audits, calling it a no risk, no lose alternative to continuing to overpay for telecom expense [YAHf-FXxBCM @ 00:01:52]. ExpenseSavvy layers a channel-partner model on top, paid only on actual savings and taking a portion once the client starts saving [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:03:51]. Single Source Telecom breaks ranks with the contingency purists: some auditing firms take a percentage of recovered revenues, while others charge a one-time fee, a retainer, or an hourly rate, and its own fees are set by the solution the client selects, from a simple audit all the way up to a comprehensive ongoing outsourced network and billing management system [GcLqzAGpP2s @ 00:03:18]. The operator takeaway: contingency wins the first meeting because the buyer never has to find room in the budget, but ongoing managed scope gets priced as a service rather than as a cut of recoveries.

Whether to keep the incumbent vendor is the second fault line. Kirk Conole of DCI Solutions sells the no-switch angle hard: savings require no changes to your vendors, so if you like your vendors keep them but lower the price, and one client saved $200,000 over three years on bank charges without changing banks [meAxKn8Td5k @ 00:01:52]. ExpenseSavvy runs the same play, negotiating new agreements with the existing vendor instead of replacing anyone, so the owner never has to change who they buy from [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:03:13]. Startup Akademia's consulting framework goes the other way: change the supplier through an auction among potential suppliers and pick the best offer, or change the form of use, moving from paying for something to renting it, so the producer is forced to optimize its own cost and therefore yours [T9lHBjJeZVI @ 00:02:29]. On who should run the work, Single Source Telecom quotes the sharpest split in the field: internally run audits typically land a 3 to 5% cost reduction, while outsourced auditing normally lands 15 to 40%, and most of the time outsourcing the auditing and expense management pays for itself at a fraction of the cost of an internal audit [GcLqzAGpP2s @ 00:02:19].

How the data gets in, and how wide the first sweep goes, splits the field further. ExpenseSavvy needs nothing but a 12-month general ledger from the accounting software, runs it against millions of data points keyed to industry, employee count and location, and deliberately starts with three to five vendors rather than all 100 [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:02:29]. Calero MDSL plugs straight into Zoom's APIs through its marketplace app, seeding licenses and spend for a turnkey implementation and onboarding UCaaS data in minutes instead of weeks or months [bQwapQbcKSk @ 00:09:53]. Brightfin argues the system of record matters most, storing mobile, fixed and cloud invoice data inside ServiceNow so spend lines up against tickets, departments and projects, while other TEMs keep invoices in a separate system that is extremely difficult to make talk to IT service data [lxObl7Zpr2A @ 00:11:04]. OMNIA Partners warns that before categorizing anything you must harmonize supplier names, since the same supplier can show up 20 times in one data file, then sequence the work in waves, starting with low-hanging commodity categories and pushing the rest to wave two or wave three [qPpAutcFjBw @ 00:01:28]. The quick-ledger route wins speed to a savings pitch; the API and ServiceNow routes win ongoing control over recurring spend.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure the TEM operators report is the disconnect that never happened. Brightfin onboarded a client that had moved out of an office long ago and emailed the carrier to shut the switch off; it never got shut off, finance kept paying the invoices because it did not know the office had closed, and Brightfin recouped roughly $56,000 in erroneous billing [lxObl7Zpr2A @ 00:03:06]. Auditel hit the same pattern in its forward-savings work: lines with no use for months still billing, plus closed satellite offices whose services were never canceled, which it says happens more than you might think [F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:02:06]. Auditel adds that the order cycle itself is a trap, because providers sometimes place disconnect orders incorrectly or do not place them at all, and the accounts keep billing the entire time, so orders must be monitored rigorously until the bill confirms the change [F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:01:18]. Ancy Alphonse describes the closed-site variant from the analyst chair: devices moved or ported to a different vendor, the building empty, yet charges persist on the invoice until someone works the vendor and discovers a modem still activated at the site [RV9K4EXWxJs @ 00:04:19].

Auto-pay is where leakage hides. ExpenseSavvy says owners put vendors on auto pay and forget what they are paying, with no transparency into what the business down the street pays the same vendor for the same account [2RDXap2GiBE @ 00:01:28]. Brightfin sizes the blind spot: 50 phones sitting in a closet at about $100 a month each is roughly $5,000 buried inside a $400,000 total charge, very easy to miss even a year after the employee left [lxObl7Zpr2A @ 00:07:47]. Prior audits are no proof of a clean bill either. Auditel ran a go-behind audit on a client that had been audited twice in the not-too-distant past, found multiple billing errors in data accounts overbilled for a very long time, and obtained a $165,000 credit on a single account [YAHf-FXxBCM @ 00:01:19]. The renewal desk is another leak: Auditel found a client that was not overcharged per se but was paying far more than it should after renewing without attention, and rate benchmarking cut rates, sometimes by more than 50% [F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:01:49]. The fixed-line results show the scale: one account fell from $68,000 to just under $36,000 a month, and two others dropped from about $5,600 and $6,200 to a little over $900 each.

In-house teams fail on bandwidth and memory. Single Source Telecom warns that staff must scour every bill, then contact carriers and negotiate refunds, which can be the majority of project time, and they still need access to past and present tariffs, contracts and rates, plus a procedure that survives turnover and retraining [GcLqzAGpP2s @ 00:01:02]. Calero MDSL sees the SaaS version of the leak: everyone got a license when work went remote during COVID, utilization is now low or zero, the same heavy Zoom user may carry one, two or three other similar products, and departed employees keep active licenses until off-boarding flags reclaim them [bQwapQbcKSk @ 00:12:00]. RadiusPoint reports that before engagement none of its clients could tie device inventory to a number, a location and an end-user ID, a number faced loss, theft and usage abuse, and invoices that do not arrive on time set up late fees and service interruptions [mFoRTIncoOI @ 00:03:17]. On labor economics, Brightfin cut Vitas Healthcare's IT setup work from 23 hours per new hire to one hour through automated workflows [lxObl7Zpr2A @ 00:09:41]. Every one of these leaks compounds month after month until someone with the tooling and the time goes looking.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Do Not Book a Saving Until the Invoice Proves It**
> 
> Every reported loss traces back to a change that was requested but never verified on the bill, an audit treated as final, or a license nobody owned. Clear these four checks before reporting savings to a client.
> - Track every disconnect and rate-change order until the next invoice confirms it, because providers place orders incorrectly or not at all while the account keeps billing [Auditel, Inc. Telecom Expense Management — F00NVfaC6Tk @ 00:01:33].
