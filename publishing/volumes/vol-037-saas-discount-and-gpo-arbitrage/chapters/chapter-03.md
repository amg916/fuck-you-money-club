## 1. Separate Customer Spend, Earned Fees, and Retained Revenue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with three ledgers: customer software spend, contractual operator compensation, and cash actually settled. A client paying $120,000 annually for subscriptions does not create $120,000 of brokerage revenue. If the authorized distribution arrangement leaves the operator a $12,000 spread, that spread is the starting compensation pool; a $2,000 referral allocation and $1,000 expected contractual reversal leave $9,000 retained revenue in this illustrative agency presentation. ASC 606 requires a principal-versus-agent assessment for each specified service, including whether the operator controls that service before transfer. Invoice routing, partner badges, and collecting the customer's money do not independently establish control. The canonical scorecard labels its top line gross revenue, but its stated revenue basis is retained operator compensation excluding pass-through subscriptions. Preserve that convention when importing the three scenarios. Define retained_share as the contractually retained fraction of an eligible compensation pool, never as permission to recognize the entire customer invoice. Record the assessment alongside contracts, distributor statements, and settlement identifiers so a bookkeeper can reproduce the treatment. Where a separate arrangement actually requires principal presentation, maintain its gross accounting ledger and bridge back to this chapter's retained economic basis rather than silently mixing incomparable revenue measures.

Build the Base revenue bridge from four illustrative retained streams: $200,000 of authorized Partner-of-Record wholesale spreads, $120,000 of master-agent residuals, $80,000 of private GPO administrative rebates, and $100,000 of shared-savings fees. These allocations explain the $500,000 total; they are modeling assumptions, not additional scorecard inputs. A 30% contingent fee requires approximately $333,333.33 of verified savings to produce $100,000 after rounding, before any separately agreed referral deduction. At the permitted 25% and 35% fee endpoints, the same fee target requires $400,000 and approximately $285,714.29 of qualifying savings. Establish a normalized baseline covering seats, term, support level, taxes, and currency before claiming a renewal improvement. Removing unused seats is different from lowering the price of identical seats; the agreement must say which savings qualify. For Telarus or Avant residuals, identify the actual supplier, commissionable products, customer payment condition, and termination rules. For GPO rebates, distinguish an earned administrative fee from money contractually owed back to members. Microsoft CPOR attribution, CSP resale, Adobe VIP Marketplace participation, and delegated administration establish different rights and payment mechanics. Reconcile each stream to its own evidence rather than assuming that partner attribution always creates a collectible commission.

Give every cohort a contract identifier, activation month, supplier program, billing frequency, earned-fee date, expected settlement date, and reversal expiration date. Use Pax8 or Ingram statements and the relevant master-agent portal as reconciliation evidence, not as substitutes for the signed compensation schedule. An illustrative Base pool of $550,000 before contractual allocations loses $30,000 to upstream or referral shares and $20,000 to estimated fee reductions, leaving $500,000 recognized operator revenue. The retention ratio is therefore 90.91% of the eligible fee pool, not 90.91% of customer subscription spend. Keep estimated variable-consideration reductions separate from receivable credit losses: a commission that was never earned and an earned invoice that cannot be collected require different explanations. Recognize contingent amounts only when the applicable performance and variable-consideration requirements support recognition; neither signature nor cash receipt alone decides the answer. Each month, compare estimated reductions with subsequent debits by supplier and vintage. Escalate cohorts whose cancellations, duplicate attribution, or member-credit adjustments exceed assumptions. Freeze owner distributions against disputed amounts while preserving the original journal trail. This discipline makes the volume's 12 mapped plays comparable without pretending their vendor rights, collection conditions, or reversal clocks are identical.

> - Request written treatment of GPO member rebates before quoting retained economics; a rebate owed to members is not an operator margin enhancement.

## 2. Reconcile Every Cohort Dollar to the Three Owner-Profit Cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use equivalent annual accounts to compare operating intensity without pretending every customer has identical software spend. At $5,000 of retained annual revenue per equivalent account, the Conservative, Base, and Scaled cases require 20, 100, and 180 account equivalents. Actual customer counts can differ because a large enterprise renewal can carry several equivalents. The illustrative eligible fee pools are $110,000, $550,000, and $1,000,000. Deduct partner allocations of $6,000, $30,000, and $60,000, then expected contractual reductions of $4,000, $20,000, and $40,000. That bridge produces exactly $100,000, $500,000, and $900,000 retained revenue. The associated fee-pool retention ratios are 90.91%, 90.91%, and 90.00%; the Scaled case deliberately tolerates slightly greater distribution leakage. These ratios are illustrative bridge outputs, not independently supplied Formula v2.0 parameters. Likewise, dividing recognized revenue by account equivalents is a workload normalization, not evidence of market pricing. A full-year model assumes full-year equivalent production; signing 100 accounts evenly through December does not automatically produce 100 account-years of residual income. Build monthly activation and recognition curves before converting these annual endpoints into a hiring plan. Keep annualized exit run rate separate from revenue actually earned during the year.

Allocate operating costs explicitly rather than backing into a flattering margin. Conservative contractor delivery consumes $24,000, acquisition consumes $12,000, employee payroll consumes $12,000, tools and administration consume $14,000, and credit-loss expense consumes $6,000. Total cost is $68,000, leaving $32,000. Base uses $100,000, $55,000, $72,000, $30,000, and $18,000 respectively: $275,000 total cost and $225,000 profit. Scaled uses $132,000, $72,000, $108,000, $40,000, and $26,000: $378,000 total cost and $522,000 profit. These illustrative allocations reconcile the canonical outcomes; they do not claim that the scorecard supplied salary schedules or contractor rates. The Conservative employee allowance assumes an owner-operated full-time role with limited booked compensation; it cannot support an additional full-time market-rate employee. Contractor counts represent engaged specialists, not three or four full-time salaries. Treat owner profit as the scorecard's operating residual after the modeled costs, without introducing personal tax assumptions. Do not subtract the earlier fee-reversal estimate again as an operating expense. The published $1,500–$9,500 monthly opex range is planning context, not a cap on all delivery, acquisition, payroll, and credit costs at scale; this explicit waterfall controls the scenario reconciliation.

Measure acquisition efficiency using newly won accounts, not the whole installed book. For illustration, $55,000 of Base acquisition expense supporting 25 new equivalent accounts implies $2,200 CAC. At $5,000 retained annual revenue less $1,000 delivery labor and $180 allocated credit expense per equivalent, contribution before acquisition and fixed costs is $3,820 annually, or approximately $318.33 monthly. Simple accrual CAC payback is therefore 6.91 months; settlement delays extend cash recovery and upfront onboarding can make the early curve worse. Do not call $55,000 divided by 100 installed equivalents CAC. Keep payroll and tools outside this contribution measure but inside owner profit. Apply the same transparent acquisition convention to Conservative: six new equivalents imply $2,000 CAC and approximately 6.86 months of contribution payback. Scaled acquisition of 36 equivalents implies $2,000 CAC and approximately 5.82 months. Those calculations use scenario-specific delivery and credit allocations while excluding fixed costs consistently. Retention, supplier clawbacks, and the first renewal still determine whether payback becomes durable cash. Require a cohort report showing acquisition source, labor hours, recognized fees, reversals, and collected contribution before raising bids or adding another contractor. Cheap leads are expensive when every renewal requires manual entitlement repair.

## 3. Fund Settlement Delays Without Financing Customer Defaults  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Model cash timing on an event calendar rather than a blended margin percentage. In the Base illustration, an earned fee waits 45 days for the relevant collection event and then another 30 days for a distributor or master-agent payout lock, creating 75 days from earning to available cash. Add those periods only when they are sequential; a hold running concurrently with customer collection does not create the same delay. Obtain actual statement and release dates for each contract instead of attributing these illustrative terms to Pax8, Ingram, Telarus, or Avant universally. At steady production, $500,000 divided by 365 and multiplied by 75 represents approximately $102,740 of retained revenue awaiting cash. This is a receivable exposure measure, not the required operating cash reserve. Using annual operating deductions as a conservative cash-outflow proxy, $275,000 divided by 365 and multiplied by 75 requires approximately $56,507 of operating runway. Because credit-loss expense itself is not a cash payment, replace that proxy with dated cash disbursements and collectible receipts in the live forecast. Never add the full receivable exposure and the operating-runway estimate as though both were independent funding uses. Identify the actual maximum cumulative cash deficit through a rolling thirteen-week forecast.

For planning, assign Conservative a 45-day total collection lag, Base 75 days, and Scaled 90 days. Their operating-runway proxies are approximately $8,384, $56,507, and $93,205. Add designated liquidity buffers of $4,000, $20,000, and $40,000, producing rounded funding targets of $12,384, $76,507, and $133,205 before supplier prepayments and usable customer advances. Those buffers are cash allocations, not additional expenses; matching an annual reversal estimate does not mean that every reversal will occur simultaneously. Validate them against the largest open clawback cluster and supplier concentration. The $5,000–$30,000 startup capital range can support a staged, low-inventory launch, but it does not automatically finance the mature Base or Scaled settlement book. Negotiate shorter payout cycles, collect earned audit fees promptly, and delay discretionary owner distributions before increasing volume. Customer advances reduce the funding requirement only where contracts permit their use and after the associated supplier liability is reserved. Exclude funds owed to members and refundable amounts needed for imminent obligations. A signed credit facility can cover timing, but its interest and fees must enter the operating model; borrowing principal is neither revenue nor profit. Reforecast when a supplier changes settlement frequency.

Annual Microsoft NCE commitments deserve a separate obligation ledger because monthly customer billing does not necessarily shorten the upstream commitment. For an illustrative $60,000 noncancelable supplier obligation, collecting only $6,000 before customer default leaves $54,000 of uncovered exposure before recoveries. A small brokerage spread cannot absorb that risk merely because an auto-debit authorization exists. Require cleared prefunding for the noncancelable exposure or another explicitly accepted funded protection before placing the order. Card payments and ACH collections can still reverse, so account for settlement status, return exposure, and contractual refund rights. Record the exact supplier cancellation window and permitted changes for the product ordered; do not generalize one subscription's rules across all Microsoft offers. A further 15-day Base payout delay adds approximately $11,301 of operating runway under the same cost proxy, while increasing retained receivables by approximately $20,548. Report both measures but fund the actual cash deficit only once. Reconcile each day the customer advance, upstream payable, settlement reserve, and unrestricted balance. If funding disappears, stop adding commitments and pursue the contractually permitted remedy rather than assuming that disabling a tenant cancels the supplier bill. Protect customer data and continuity while resolving the commercial exposure.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Export the obligation and settlement registers
   - Join customer invoices, supplier payables, and commission statements by contract and subscription identifier.
   - Capture earned dates, expected cash dates, upstream commitment balances, return exposure, and the earliest permitted cancellation date.
2. Build the thirteen-week cash schedule
   - Forecast dated collectible receipts and actual disbursements, separating unrestricted cash from customer-linked obligations.
   - Use the maximum cumulative deficit plus a documented stress buffer; do not sum duplicate receivable and runway measures.
3. Resolve unmatched debits and release dates
   - Investigate supplier reversals, delayed residuals, and duplicate partner allocations before updating revenue estimates.
   - Tie every adjustment to a settlement identifier and preserve the original estimate for vintage-level reserve testing.
4. Authorize volume against funded capacity
   - Compare each proposed annual commitment with cleared prefunding and the revised liquidity forecast.
   - Release owner distributions only after supplier obligations, member balances, and the modeled settlement stress remain funded.

## 4. Stress Discount Rights, Utilization, and Partner-Tier Compression  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The TRACK_2 play enterprise_excess_seat_commit_pooling_arbitrage targets 35%–65% discounts to retail only where the vendor explicitly permits assignment or resale. Consider 100 seats with a $40 monthly list price: annual list value is $48,000. An authorized 50% acquisition discount costs $24,000; selling those seats at $32 monthly yields $38,400 customer billings and a $14,400 economic spread. Subtract $2,400 provisioning and support labor, $1,200 expected contractual reductions, and $1,800 acquisition cost to obtain $9,000 contribution before fixed payroll and overhead. On the chapter's agency basis, customer billings are not the recognized top line; the spread and applicable reductions drive retained revenue. This standalone subcohort is not incremental income added to the canonical scenarios. If procurement requires $24,000 upfront while customers pay $3,200 monthly, supplier cash alone takes 7.5 months to recover before other costs. An advertised discount therefore creates both utilization risk and financing demand. Price the specific product, geography, term, support obligation, and assignment mechanism rather than buying a nominal seat count. Reject distressed startup credits or enterprise commitments whose original agreement does not expressly support the proposed transaction.

Program names do not make entitlements fungible. Microsoft SPLA usage rights, CSP subscription resale, GDAP delegated administration, Adobe multi-seat licensing, and AWS Marketplace CPPO private-offer authorization require separate eligibility and transaction checks. GDAP access alone grants no resale rights; CPPO authority does not automatically authorize transfer of unused cloud commitments. Provision isolated workspaces and customer-specific entitlements only after verifying the actual route. At 75 sold seats, the illustrative pool bills $28,800 annually against the unchanged $24,000 procurement cost. Holding the modeled $5,400 of delivery, reversal, and acquisition deductions constant leaves a $600 loss before fixed overhead. At full utilization, a 35% acquisition discount raises procurement to $31,200 and reduces contribution to $1,800; a 65% discount lowers procurement to $16,800 and raises contribution to $16,200. Do not budget the best discount until written authorization and executable pricing exist. If rights fail verification after provisioning, stop unauthorized provisioning, isolate affected access, and coordinate vendor-approved direct licenses or permitted migration while resolving audit liabilities transparently. Isolation must preserve required customer access and data-export arrangements rather than becoming an improvised deletion procedure. Document remediation costs in the affected cohort instead of burying them in future acquisition spending.

Run sensitivity tests as deviations from the canonical cases, never as replacements for their published results. A 10% reduction in retained revenue with operating costs unchanged reduces Conservative profit to $22,000, Base to $175,000, and Scaled to $432,000; stressed margins become approximately 24.44%, 38.89%, and 53.33%. A separate 20% increase in contractor delivery cost reduces profits to $27,200, $205,000, and $495,600. Another independent shock increasing contractual fee reductions by two percentage points of eligible fee pools costs $2,200, $11,000, and $20,000 respectively. Combine those shocks only when explicitly testing a joint downside; the same reversal cannot also be labeled lost revenue and bad debt. Audit acquisition assumptions against 15 U.S.C. 45 and the applicable B2B misrepresentation provisions of 16 CFR Part 310. Calling and messaging methods must satisfy applicable 47 U.S.C. 227 and 47 CFR 64.1200 requirements; B2B positioning does not create blanket immunity. Inflated savings claims can simultaneously increase refund exposure, destroy channel relationships, and corrupt CAC calculations. Protect the model by selling verified savings, using documented contact permissions where required, and retaining the quote and entitlement evidence behind every promised spread. Scale repeatable collected contribution, not the largest advertised discount.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Pre-Flight Checks Before Taking Commitment Risk**
> 
> Release a transaction only when entitlement rights, retained economics, cash coverage, and customer representations all match the actual contract.
> - Verify written assignment or resale rights for the exact SKU and customer route; do not substitute GDAP access, CPOR attribution, or a startup discount for authorization.
> - Recalculate contribution at 75% utilization and the executable acquisition discount, including supplier minimums, contractor labor, reversals, and acquisition expense.
> - Match cleared prefunding to the noncancelable upstream obligation and test payout delays against unrestricted cash before submitting the order.
> - Validate savings claims against the signed baseline and applicable calling rules, then document vendor-approved licensing or migration steps if rights fail verification.
