## Executive Summary

Distributor practitioners converge on one engine: the MSP buys Microsoft and third-party cloud through a marketplace like Pax8 or Ingram Micro, then earns its real margin on wrapped services, not on the licence. Pax8's co-founder cites IDC's $4.14 of services attached per $1 of Microsoft cloud, while the Pax8 profitability playbook demands at least 30% margin on security, RMM and non-Microsoft licensing and 60% on managed support. Street mechanics centre on reconciling the M365 tenant, billing engine and PSA, where one US client recovered $130,000 a year, plus per-client delta analysis from MSP CFO. Security bundles sold opt-out with a signed waiver push MDR adoption past 90%. Disagreement runs on whether VARs earn their slice: TC2 calls resale low margin and demands markup transparency, while a Tech Field Day panel splits on whether resellers are useless or time-saving brokers.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The baseline every distributor-side operator repeats is that software reaches small and mid-market buyers through the channel, not around it. The Pax8 co-founder learned this at MX Logic, which sold direct, entered the channel badly and only grew once it added channel partners; they now dismiss the beliefs that all customers will buy direct, that marketplaces will replace the channel, and that distribution is dead [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:04:02]. TC2's Joe Schmidt and Ben Fox put it bluntly from the procurement side: a company buying Cisco network equipment very rarely buys direct from Cisco, because OEMs sell through channel partners and VARs are close to unavoidable [6YNd6Xcyekw @ 00:01:16]. Ingram Micro's IBM alliance conversation extends the same logic to hyperscaler marketplaces, where IBM software such as HashiCorp and Apptio now transacts through AWS Marketplace via Ingram, and deals close about 27% quicker and land larger because customers themselves are pushing marketplace buying [ZoUkvQ-5m3c @ 00:02:21]. Bob Park frames the vendor side of the same funnel: one phone call to set up an account with a distributor such as Ingram Micro or Tech Data gives a manufacturer access to thousands of resellers, which beats funding a cold-calling squad that needs capital and resources before any revenue arrives [z-ZzUEITMGM @ 00:04:12].

Practitioners also agree on where the money actually sits: the licence is the entry ticket, and the service wrapped around it is the margin. Pax8's profitability playbook tells MSPs to hold at least 30% margin, not markup, on security, RMM and non-Microsoft licensing, and says best-in-class firms clear at least 60% margin on managed support services [KcmWalqZBzk @ 00:01:54]. Because Microsoft 365 margins are capped and lower, the same playbook folds M365 support cost into the services line rather than hoping the licence pays for it [KcmWalqZBzk @ 00:02:35]. The Pax8 co-founder backs this with IDC's finding that successful MSPs attach $4.14 of services for every $1 of Microsoft cloud sold, and adds that MSPs with more than half their revenue from cloud see 2x revenue, 1.8x growth and 1.5x profit [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:13:15]. Dan Harris Mills of Highlander Computing Solutions lived the shift: before Azure their firm was a commoditized box-shifter, and after building the Azure go-to-market with Ingram Micro's pre-sales technical resources and marketing, customers started treating them as an advisor, a jump that began with the CSP move Highlander had put off for a long time [bGmD7XlTNZI @ 00:02:02].

The third point of agreement is that a distributor earns its slot by carrying operational load the MSP cannot staff itself. The Pax8 co-founder lists what they had to build: a partner console with a multi-customer view, monthly billing with proration and co-termination instead of anniversary billing, and provisioning, billing and consumption APIs wired into ConnectWise, Autotask and Kaseya, while warning that the real challenge is quote-to-cash, not just selling [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:28:13]. Phylip Morgan demos the result as a self-serve portal to provision, bill, manage and analyse, and calls instant provisioning a tagline that holds up in practice, roughly two minutes from click to the Office portal [NVSw5ZYgQzw @ 00:23:50]. Nick Heddy, Pax8's CRO and employee number eight, sells the human layer as wingman support: 24x7 US-based L2 and L3 support with no finger pointing, and Heddy tells doubters to check Reddit for raw partner feedback [hsCUDuKzpaw @ 00:06:16]. Ingram Micro pitches the same backbone alongside Cisco, reframing itself from traditional distributor to platform company through Xvantage and running a CARE program built to cut churn and raise lifetime value in recurring models, backed by a free Advanced Solutions Integration Lab for demos and attack simulations [ADZwWSQGFFI @ 00:04:33].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The core mechanic Pax8's demo team teaches is reconciling the holy trinity: the Microsoft 365 tenant, the billing engine such as Xero, and the PSA. Left unsynced, gaps between those three systems run into the tens of thousands, and one US client saved $130,000 a year once they were closed [NVSw5ZYgQzw @ 00:14:03]. Setup is quick: the PSA integration takes 10 to 15 minutes using an API user with a public and private key, with formal integrations for ConnectWise, Autotask, Syncro, Tigerpaw and RepairShopr plus open APIs; the operator then binds each company and product one-to-one and exports products with MSRP pre-populated [NVSw5ZYgQzw @ 00:16:35]. Billing sync is one-way, pushing quantity changes and recurring additions, with two-way sync reserved for Pax8 Pro. Ordering supports future effective dates, $0 line items for bundles and auto-proration, a seat change from 10 to 11 took about 30 seconds on screen, and Pax8 can generate PDF invoices for Xero batch import [NVSw5ZYgQzw @ 01:00:32]. The Pax8 co-founder lists the plumbing that matters more than the catalogue: monthly billing with proration and co-termination, plus provisioning, billing and consumption APIs, are what MSPs need to get through quote-to-cash [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:21:57].

Larry Cobran of MSP CFO supplies the per-client math. Cobran's portfolio method treats clients like stores: five stores averaging 18% move to 20% just by lifting the one 6% outlier to 16%, and outliers hide across clients, agreements, engineers and ticket types. The delta compares the labor portion of an agreement against shadow billable value: a $3,000 labor portion against 20 hours at $200, or $4,000, is a negative delta meaning the client is overserved or underpriced [TxvzhnvDnxA @ 00:07:18]. One real 2024 client was only $21,000 off in aggregate, yet the bottom five clients made up 12.3% of managed-service revenue. The data only works if techs log 100% of hours against at least an eight-hour day, for example 6.7 hours of client work plus 1.3 hours admin, and never mark agreement-covered work as non-billable [TxvzhnvDnxA @ 00:16:57]. Root cause then runs through revenue per endpoint, billable per hour, hours per ticket and tickets per endpoint, against an agreement-efficiency target of 1.2 to 1.3 where the example sat at 0.75 [TxvzhnvDnxA @ 00:18:22]. Pax8's profitability playbook closes the loop: inspect margins consistently through PSA and MSP CFO dashboards and raise rates gradually on unprofitable clients [KcmWalqZBzk @ 00:01:18].

The packaging mechanic that turns a thin licence into a service is the opt-out bundle. Josh, who worked Pax8's go-to-market team before joining Usecure, found UK MSPs already owned the tools but bolted them on as a two-quid-a-month add-on with no service wrap; the fix is good, better, best tiers, with a waiver for any client who declines the recommended tier [oQIMe7F_kaw @ 00:05:23]. Sham of Blackpoint Europe runs the same play for MDR and SOC: the customer must sign a waiver to decline, and opt-in adoption runs over 90%; Blackpoint's account management team builds the waiver and pre-renewal conversations, and fence-sitters get a 180-day historical scan showing prior compromise such as wire-fraud prerequisites [fM8cQVJ3oh8 @ 00:08:39]. Pax8 arms the upsell with data: Pax8 Pro uses Microsoft APIs to flag clients buying a la carte instead of the M365 Business Premium bundle, idle resource groups and unused E5 security features, then generates SOW templates to pull through professional services [hsCUDuKzpaw @ 00:03:10]. The white-label MCAT report runs in one to two minutes, self-deletes after 30 days, and in the demo surfaced a 241-a-month saving framed to the client as two grand over 12 months [NVSw5ZYgQzw @ 00:57:26].

## 3. Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split is whether the middleman earns its cut at all. Tech Field Day Plus opened its roundtable on the premise that 'resellers are useless,' with critics favoring a direct sales model and faulting the account manager who takes 'a thin slice off the top' [oB21xupXerM @ 00:01:14]. The VAR-side panelists concede that 'some resellers are useless' when the buyer only wants a box. They then counter with the insurance-broker play: a VAR tracks discount programs and incentives that a customer buying every three to four years cannot follow, and earns its keep when the customer is unfamiliar with the technology [oB21xupXerM @ 00:05:14]. The Pax8 co-founder speaking to the Cloud Software Association attacks the direct thesis from the vendor side. The co-founder disputes that all customers will buy direct, that marketplaces will replace the channel, or that distribution is dead, and warns that MX Logic sold direct, entered the channel badly, and only grew once it added the channel [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:04:02]. TC2's Joe Schmidt and Ben Fox treat the question as settled by structure rather than ideology. OEMs sell through channel partners and buying Cisco equipment direct is 'very unusual,' so the live choice is between resale specialists such as SHI and WWT and carriers or systems integrators that offer resale as a complement [6YNd6Xcyekw @ 00:01:46].

Stack architecture splits the Pax8 camp itself. The co-founder's lesson list calls for a partner management console with a multi-customer view, so the MSP runs every customer from one place [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:21:02]. Phylip Morgan, demoing for Tubblog, rejects that framing outright: 'single pane of glass is anathema.' Morgan's reasoning is that an MSP carries five roles (sales and marketing, operations, finance, technical, and the CEO), and each one needs its own dashboard [NVSw5ZYgQzw @ 00:07:15]. On vendor count, Nick Heddy describes the typical MSP as running four to five vendors with Microsoft as the core. Heddy pitches Pax8 Stacks to surface cross-sell gaps across names like SentinelOne, Proofpoint, Azure migrations, and RingCentral [hsCUDuKzpaw @ 00:04:45]. Josh, who worked mostly with SMB MSPs on the Pax8 go-to-market team before joining usecure, pulls the opposite direction. In Josh's view, tool sprawl and inherited tooling are the problem, so the answer is to consolidate and build the stack around the human, starting at the bottom with email security, M365 or Google Workspace backup, web filtering, VPN, and ITDR [oQIMe7F_kaw @ 00:07:41]. The contrast for an operator is expansion driven by marketplace gap analysis versus consolidation driven by the people delivering the service. Both playbooks come out of the same distributor's orbit, and both are taught to the same partner base.

Fixing an underpriced book of clients produces two different playbooks. The Pax8 profitability brief says to inspect per-client profitability and raise rates gradually on unprofitable existing clients. It also says to hold at least 30% margin, not markup, on security, RMM, and non-Microsoft licensing [KcmWalqZBzk @ 00:01:18]. Larry Cobran of MSP CFO pushes price to the back of the line: 'firing a client is like your 10th decision,' so start with fixing the client. In Cobran's law-firm case, revenue per endpoint was normal but ticket counts ran high, and the fix was lunch-and-learns rather than a price increase [TxvzhnvDnxA @ 00:26:05]. Security packaging splits the same way. Josh at usecure found UK MSPs bolting tools on as a 'two quid a month' add-on. Josh's motion names 'fear selling' and lands on good, better, best three-tier packaging, with a waiver for clients who decline the recommended tier [oQIMe7F_kaw @ 00:05:23]. Sham at Blackpoint goes further with an opt-out campaign: the customer must sign a waiver to decline MDR and SOC, and Sham reports the opt-in adoption rate at over 90% [fM8cQVJ3oh8 @ 00:08:39]. Tiered choice keeps a cheaper rung on the table. Opt-out removes the decision entirely unless the client signs away cover in writing.

## 4. Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failures are timing failures. The Pax8 co-founder warns that MX Logic sold direct, entered the channel badly, and only grew after adding it. The fix is the 18 month rule: commit long-term, because unassisted 'Bluebirds' only start landing around the eighth assisted deal [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:18:08]. The same talk flags an internal sabotage loop in which reps earned more by avoiding channel margin, which is why channel sales need positive comp. It closes on the warning that the real challenge is quote-to-cash, not just selling [phXe8eJoVeo @ 00:28:13]. Dan Harris Mills of Highlander Computing Solutions admits the firm 'put off for a long time' the CSP move Microsoft wanted. Highlander then leaned on Ingram Micro Cloud for pre-planning, customer conversations, pre-sales technical resources, and Azure marketing. Only after that did it stop being a 'commoditized' box-shifter and start being treated by customers as an advisor [bGmD7XlTNZI @ 00:01:16]. Bob Park describes the capital trap on the manufacturer side of the VAR model: a cold-calling squad needs capital and resources before any revenue arrives. Park's phase one is a single phone call to open an account with a distributor such as Ingram Micro or Tech Data, which gives access to thousands of VARs [z-ZzUEITMGM @ 00:03:49]. In each case, months pass before the channel pays back.

Margin leaks quietly through billing and time data. Phylip Morgan describes the holy trinity of the M365 tenant, the Xero billing engine, and the PSA drifting apart. Reconciliation gaps reach '10s of 1000s,' and one US client saved $130,000 a year once the three were synced [NVSw5ZYgQzw @ 00:14:03]. Larry Cobran's MSP CFO data shows how an aggregate number hides the damage. A real 2024 client was only $21K off overall, yet its bottom five clients were 12.3% of managed-service revenue [TxvzhnvDnxA @ 00:09:33]. Cobran's warning on the input side is blunt: do not mark agreement-covered work as non-billable, and log 100% of hours on at least an 8-hour day. The shadow-billable delta depends on that data, and in the worked example agreement efficiency sat at 0.75 against a 1.2 to 1.3 target [TxvzhnvDnxA @ 00:17:30]. The Pax8 profitability brief adds the structural squeeze: Microsoft 365 margins are capped and lower. That means M365 support costs have to be folded into services pricing, then inspected consistently through the PSA and MSP CFO dashboards [KcmWalqZBzk @ 00:02:35]. An MSP that skips any of these checks prices from a blended average that masks its worst accounts.

Commercial traps sit in the fine print and the comp plan. Josh at usecure saw UK MSPs who held the right tools but pushed them as a 'two quid a month' add-on with no service wrap. Their problem was getting those tools into their customers in a form the client understood [oQIMe7F_kaw @ 00:04:03]. On hardware and software resale, TC2 warns that VAR resale is good for revenue but a low-margin business. A quote should carry only shipping and sales tax; import duties, customs, broker, FX-hedging, logistics, and management fees are the padding to strike out [6YNd6Xcyekw @ 00:05:38]. The same pair found that logistics and tax variance can exceed markup variance. Their answer is multiple quotes per transaction, itemised 'taxes,' and direct OEM relationships kept alive for price negotiation [6YNd6Xcyekw @ 00:08:02]. The Tech Field Day panel explains why VAR reps push the wrong box. Compensation is tied to quarterly numbers, publicly traded VARs face investor scrutiny every 90 days, and a missed number means a 'pink slip.' That pressure is how a customer ends up pitched a four-spatial-stream mGig flagship access point it never needed [oB21xupXerM @ 00:12:33]. The panel's remedy is to escalate to the rep's boss, and it notes that VARs can fire customers too.
