## 1. Assign twelve plays to four accountable seats before adding headcount  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the initial pod around one full-time principal and three deliverable-based contractors, not four miniature departments. The principal owns commercial authority, vendor relationships, cash exposure, and final pricing. Contractor A owns research and qualified renewal opportunities; Contractor B owns authorized provisioning evidence and renewal administration; Contractor C owns savings calculations, statement matching, and accrual support. For this chapter, organize the twelve mapped operator plays into an explicit execution map: P01 DNS/MX/SPF account signals; P02 renewal-cliff discovery; P03 Microsoft CSP margin capture; P04 eligible Microsoft CPOR attribution; P05 Adobe VIP Marketplace brokerage; P06 authorized distributor billing reconciliation; P07 Telarus Zoom residuals; P08 Avant UCaaS residuals; P09 GPO membership aggregation; P10 GPO rebate reconciliation; P11 benchmark renewal negotiation; and P12 contingent shared-savings audits. These are work packages, not twelve promises that every vendor pays every fee. CPOR attribution alone neither transfers a subscription nor establishes reseller authority. Each opportunity must identify its actual supplier agreement, customer authorization, eligible product, and compensation schedule before entering a forecast. One customer can traverse several packages, but the ledger must prevent duplicate revenue attribution. Keep responsibility visible in a shared register so a contractor absence never turns an annual commitment into an orphaned obligation.

Use a single deal record in HubSpot or an equivalent CRM, linked to an evidence folder and accounting customer identifier. Contractor A delivers a dated renewal source, procurement contact, estimated seat count, and qualification notes; a DNS signal is a hypothesis, not proof of a paid tenant. Contractor B verifies the permitted Microsoft CSP transaction route, Adobe VIP Marketplace reseller process, or master-agent order path before touching subscriptions. Use Pax8 and Ingram Micro statement exports or authorized APIs where available; never assume access to one distributor grants access to another. Contractor C independently matches contractual rates to billed seats, earned residuals, and cash receipts. The principal approves exceptions and executes agreements but cannot approve their own reconciliation adjustments without a documented second review. Use named accounts, least-privilege permissions, and separate credentials for each customer environment. Prohibit unauthorized multi-tenant seat pooling even when aggregate pricing looks attractive. Record P03 and P05 authorizations separately from P09 membership evidence because a purchasing-group agreement does not rewrite license terms. An evidence checklist, rather than informal chat history, determines whether a package is complete and whether a contractor can invoice for accepted work.

The scaled pod adds one full-time operations lead and one contractor, bringing the total to six people rather than building a sales hierarchy. The operations lead takes daily renewal control, exception routing, and first-pass quality review from the principal. Contractor D takes renewal outreach and member administration; Contractor A retains sourcing, Contractor B retains transaction execution, and Contractor C retains financial evidence. Trigger expansion from an eight-week workload record: accepted packages consume more than eighty percent of purchased capacity, renewal exceptions repeatedly breach the agreed response window, and collected contribution covers the incremental seat under the downside forecast. Capacity is purchased output, not required contractor attendance. Define customer response windows and acceptance deadlines while leaving independent contractors control over working methods consistent with applicable classification rules. IRS common-law employee analysis under 26 U.S.C. section 3121(d)(2) and California Labor Code section 2775 require fact-specific classification; a contractor label does not settle the issue. Use employment where operational control requires it. Every seat needs a documented backup, but backup coverage must fit the same four-person or six-person roster. The principal's absence test is five business days without missed renewal decisions, unreviewed exposure increases, or inaccessible records.

## 2. Buy accepted deliverables and run one weekly exception scorecard  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Price contractor work against retained revenue rather than customer subscription volume. An illustrative launch schedule pays Contractor A $500 monthly plus $75 per accepted renewal opportunity, Contractor B $650 monthly plus $100 per completed authorized transaction package, and Contractor C $600 monthly plus $125 per completed reconciliation or savings package. At four accepted packages per contractor each month, total contractor expense is $2,950 monthly, or $35,400 annually. These purchased deliverables are examples, not assumed market rates or additional scorecard inputs. The Conservative case permits $68,000 of total annual expense because $100,000 recognized revenue less $32,000 owner profit equals $68,000; this contractor schedule leaves $32,600 for all other modeled costs. The Base and Scaled cases permit $275,000 and $378,000 respectively. The stated $1,500 to $9,500 monthly operating range is a planning envelope, not a substitute for those complete annual expense bridges; document which fixed costs it includes and separately reconcile variable delivery costs. Do not assume owner profit already contains a market replacement salary. Set a written monthly package cap, require acceptance evidence, and approve additional scope before work starts. Budget rejected rework without paying twice for the same defective deliverable.

Write quality standards as objective outcomes. A research package needs a source dated within thirty days, a usable renewal window, and a valid procurement route; reject scraped contact lists without account evidence. A provisioning package needs the correct tenant or customer identifier, documented authorization, committed term, cancellation window, and supplier acceptance. A reconciliation package needs a complete invoice-to-statement bridge and a reviewer who can reproduce the calculation. Set one-business-day acknowledgment for ordinary exceptions and same-business-day escalation for unauthorized changes, threatened cancellation deadlines, or unfunded annual commitments. The principal owns actual customer protection while the contractor agreement defines delivery deadlines and purchased coverage. Sample at least ten percent of routine packages and review every new supplier route, material savings invoice, and credit exposure increase. Track first-pass acceptance with a launch target of ninety-five percent, but never dilute the denominator by removing rejected packages. Any optional success fee should attach to collected eligible retained revenue under written attribution and reversal terms, not total client spend. Do not reclaim compensation arbitrarily: distinguish contractual correction obligations from wage deductions and settle classification before deploying an incentive design.

Run a thirty-minute weekly review from a frozen Monday snapshot, using Friday's accounting cutoff. Review only exceptions, decisions, and accountable next actions; the dashboard already carries routine activity. Track qualified renewals against capacity, accepted packages against delivered packages, signed orders against authorized orders, and earned retained revenue against collected retained revenue. Measure partner receivable aging separately from customer receivables because a delayed master-agent statement is not the same failure as an unpaid NCE commitment. Show thirty-, sixty-, and ninety-day renewal cohorts, unresolved statement variances, and uncovered supplier obligations. Attribute each exception to P01 through P12 so attractive sourcing volume cannot hide weak rebate collection. Require an owner, a dated resolution, and a measurable cash consequence for every red item. For outbound activity, preserve contact source, suppression status, and channel permissions. Review 47 U.S.C. section 227 and 47 CFR section 64.1200 for calling and messaging practices; business-to-business positioning does not erase restrictions involving mobile numbers, prerecorded calls, or protected recipients. The FTC's 16 CFR Part 310 also contains applicable business-to-business misrepresentation prohibitions. Reward verified opportunities and realized collections, never raw dial counts that encourage unreliable claims or unwanted automated outreach.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly evidence set
   - Export CRM opportunities, distributor statements, cash activity, and renewal deadlines.
   - Use one accounting cutoff and preserve source identifiers so all twelve plays reconcile to the same reporting period.
2. Validate denominators and exceptions
   - Recompute acceptance, collection aging, concentration, and uncovered commitments.
   - Retain rejected packages and unpaid earned revenue in the relevant populations; document exclusions with a reviewer.
3. Assign cash-linked corrective actions
   - Give each exception one accountable seat, deadline, and quantified exposure.
   - The principal decides capital and contract issues; contractors receive bounded correction packages within purchased capacity.
4. Close with independently checked evidence
   - Attach corrected records and obtain review before marking an exception resolved.
   - Carry unresolved items into the next snapshot without resetting their original detection dates or concealing aging.

## 3. Reconcile retained revenue, renewal cohorts, and correlated concentration  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the scorecard's revenue labels as the supplied retained operator revenue basis, not permission to book underlying software spend. The canonical cases remain $100,000 at 32 percent, $500,000 at 45.0 percent, and $900,000 at 58 percent, producing $32,000, $225,000, and $522,000 annual owner profit. Although the scorecard calls these gross revenue, they exclude pass-through subscription spend and represent retained spreads, residuals, administrative rebates, and eligible shared-savings fees. Illustratively, $100,000 of client subscription billings with a ten-percent retained share produces $10,000 operator revenue on that modeled basis, not $100,000. Do not deduct the retained share again after entering the net amount. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent assessment requires analyzing control of each specified service; contract wording or billing presentation alone cannot determine gross reporting. Document the accounting conclusion by revenue stream and reconcile any statutory presentation difference back to the scorecard's retained contribution basis. Customer cash collected before an earned fee is not automatically revenue. Likewise, an expected partner rebate is not earned simply because a salesperson put it in the CRM. Keep performance conditions, payment timing, and collectibility evidence distinct throughout the monthly close.

Maintain a cohort subledger keyed by customer, supplier group, agreement, order identifier, play code, service month, renewal date, and termination terms. Contractor C closes earned residuals to statements and cash, tracks unbilled amounts separately, and records adjustments with the original cohort rather than hiding them in current sales. For GPO rebates, retain the member roster, eligible purchase base, bracket calculation, supplier confirmation, and contractual allocation between members and operator. For shared savings, define a like-for-like baseline before negotiation: seats, product tier, service period, currency, and excluded migration costs. A $200,000 baseline reduced to $160,000 produces $40,000 eligible savings; a thirty-percent fee is $12,000 operator revenue only when contractually earned and appropriately recognized. Separate this one-time amount from evergreen residual revenue in buyer materials. Reconcile the bank, customer ledger, supplier obligations, and deferred balances monthly. For annual Microsoft NCE commitments, require funding before commitment under the agreed credit policy; a stored card or scheduled ACH debit is not settled cash. Consider return, dispute, and reversal exposure before releasing reserves. Annual supplier liability can survive customer nonpayment, and monthly customer billing does not transform that liability into a cancelable monthly obligation.

Set both customer-group and counterparty-group caps at twenty-five percent or less of trailing-twelve-month recognized retained revenue, using twenty percent as the internal warning level. At Base, the hard revenue cap is $125,000 per group; at Scaled it is $225,000. Combine commonly controlled customers rather than splitting subsidiaries into cosmetic accounts. Measure payer concentration and underlying vendor dependency separately: Microsoft exposure routed through two distributors remains correlated Microsoft exposure. Apply the same twenty-five-percent ceiling separately to positive partner receivables and outstanding supplier commitments by counterparty group, reporting zero-total populations as having no outstanding exposure. Early books will often exceed the revenue cap; label the actual breach, stop optional expansion of the dominant exposure, and build unrelated cohorts rather than claiming diversification prematurely. Map P03 through P06 to software-vendor dependency, P07 and P08 to both master-agent and underlying carrier dependency, and P09 through P12 to actual payers and contractual savings sources. Stress a lost rebate tier, a delayed residual statement, and one annual-commit customer default independently. A transfer-ready book needs documented reserve responses and replacement acquisition capacity, not merely a spreadsheet showing many customer names.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before accepting or transferring a cohort**
> 
> An attractive spread does not compensate for missing authority, unfunded commitments, unearned revenue, or concentrated exposure.
> - Verify the customer authorization, permitted CSP or VIP transaction route, and applicable supplier agreement against the specific order.
> - Verify settled prefunding and reserves against the full noncancelable annual liability, including payment-return and dispute exposure.
> - Verify earned retained revenue, rebate allocation, and the shared-savings baseline through an independently reproducible reconciliation.
> - Verify customer and counterparty concentration against the twenty-five-percent caps; disclose existing breaches and document reduction actions.

## 4. Build a transferable book and value replacement-adjusted earnings  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Prepare the transfer package while the pod is small. Create an agreement register covering assignment, change of control, termination, post-termination residuals, customer solicitation restrictions, and supplier notification duties. A Telarus or Avant relationship is not automatically saleable merely because residuals recur; the executed agreement controls payment survival and transfer rights. Likewise, customer authorization does not replace a supplier's required transfer process. Keep renewal calendars, compensation exhibits, GPO member agreements, and reconciliation procedures under company-controlled access. Obtain written intellectual-property assignments from contractors for original scripts, templates, benchmark databases, and documentation where rights are transferable. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a signed writing; paying an invoice does not itself establish ownership. Do not rely solely on a work-made-for-hire label for commissioned software. Identify preexisting contractor tools and third-party materials, securing the licenses needed for continued operation and permitted transfer instead of claiming ownership of everything delivered. Maintain provenance for pricing benchmarks and customer information. A buyer should receive usable rights and permitted records through controlled diligence access, not an unrestricted export of customer credentials. Contractor departure must trigger access revocation and verified handover without interrupting the next renewal cycle.

Use valuation multiples as explicit sensitivities, not assertions that every brokerage commands a premium. An owner-operated microbusiness might be evaluated using an illustrative two-to-three-times normalized seller's discretionary earnings range; a genuinely managed, transferable book might be modeled at three-to-five-times normalized EBITDA. These are negotiation scenarios, not observed transaction comparables. Canonical owner profit is neither automatically SDE nor EBITDA. Build a bridge for owner compensation, interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, defensible addbacks, and replacement labor before applying either denominator. If Base owner profit of $225,000 equals normalized SDE after that bridge, two-to-three times implies $450,000 to $675,000 of illustrative enterprise value. In a separate simplified managed-buyer sensitivity, assume the supplied owner profit excludes founder replacement pay and all other EBITDA adjustments net to zero. Deducting $120,000 replacement cost leaves $105,000 Base EBITDA and $402,000 Scaled EBITDA. Three-to-five times then implies $315,000 to $525,000 and $1,206,000 to $2,010,000 respectively. If replacement pay is already expensed, do not deduct it twice. Conservative profit cannot support that replacement assumption, so avoid forcing a positive managed-earnings valuation.

Explain the premium through evidence a buyer can test: durable collected residuals, low customer churn, clean transfer rights, compliant license routes, diversified counterparties, and operations that survive founder absence. Segment P03 through P10 recurring economics from P11 and P12 project fees; classify each actual contract by its terms rather than assuming its play code guarantees recurrence. Show cohort retention on retained revenue, not underlying subscription spend, and distinguish seat expansion from vendor price increases. Provide twenty-four months of monthly bridges when available; a younger book should show its actual history without manufactured continuity. Reconcile enterprise value to seller proceeds through cash, debt, debt-like exposures, working capital adjustments, fees, escrow, and contingent consideration. Negotiate any earnout around identifiable collected cohorts and defined buyer servicing obligations, avoiding metrics the buyer can change unilaterally. Keep the cash-cow alternative equally disciplined: replacement coverage, reserves, and recurring audit work still matter when no sale is planned. Compare realistic after-cost sale proceeds with sustainable distributions under rebate compression and customer default stresses. The objective is optionality: the same four-person or six-person pod should support continued ownership, a partial transition, or an orderly full transfer without requiring the principal to remain the undocumented operating system.
