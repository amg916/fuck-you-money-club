## 1. Days 1–7: Establish Authority, Twelve Plays, and a Funded Pod  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Launch Candidate #44 as a recovery coordinator, not an unlicensed customs filing desk. The initial pod contains one full-time owner and three contractors: a licensed customs broker, a document-reconciliation analyst, and a settlement-claims specialist. Trade counsel is a separately budgeted specialist escalation, not a concealed fifth standing seat. The owner controls acquisition, client instructions, cash, and exception triage; the broker controls regulated customs work under 19 CFR Part 111 and valid Customs Powers of Attorney. The analyst prepares evidence within the broker's defined workflow rather than independently advising importers on customs treatment. The settlement specialist checks class definitions, administrator instructions, and claimant signatures. Budget 35 owner hours and at most 15 combined contractor hours weekly during the sprint, with fixed-scope quotes that fit the cash ceiling. If actual broker pricing cannot support that allocation, reduce concurrent files rather than dilute licensed review. Create one owner-visible case register with lane, entity, source-document identifiers, legal deadline, reviewer, collectible recovery estimate, contracted fee, and next action. Store evidence in a client-specific encrypted repository; use role-scoped access, multifactor authentication, and a documented deletion schedule. Bank credentials and unrestricted ACE credentials never belong in the sales system.

Use twelve numbered plays as the sprint's explicit operating map; these are workflow labels for the supplied opportunity, not twelve independent statutory entitlements. Drawback plays are P01 direct-identification unused merchandise, P02 substitution unused merchandise, P03 manufacturing drawback, and P04 export-trace remediation. Tariff-refund plays are P05 pre-liquidation Post-Summary Corrections, P06 classification protests, P07 valuation or origin protests, and P08 remedy-specific refund screening. Settlement plays are P09 interchange class screening, P10 other settled B2B antitrust pool screening, P11 accounts-payable ledger reconstruction, and P12 authorized submission plus distribution reconciliation. P04 and P11 improve evidence; neither creates a second recovery on the same transaction. Map every prospect to one primary play and allow secondary plays only after checking duplicate recovery restrictions. ImportGenius and Panjiva can identify commercial patterns, but manifests do not establish paid duty, importer rights, or class membership. Request ACE entry data, CBP Form 7501, commercial invoices, export bills of lading, and inventory records from the authorized client. For settlements, request purchase or processing records for the exact class period and legal entity. Acquire twenty named target accounts by Day 4; finish eight substantive qualification conversations by Day 14. A conversation counts only when the owner records authority, evidence availability, timing, and a plausible lane.

Set the first-month cash ceiling before signing subscriptions: $5,500 of launch spending plus $4,500 of recurring operating spending, for $10,000 maximum cumulative cash outlay by Day 30. Launch spending covers scoped broker and counsel setup, contract drafting, security configuration, insurance placement, and initial document ingestion; obtain quotes rather than treating these categories as fixed market prices. Within recurring spending, cap contractor purchases at $3,000 and all other recurring purchases at $1,500. Require prepaid scope reductions if those ceilings cannot buy the planned capacity. Maintain $19,000 of committed launch liquidity to cover the full ninety-day no-receipt case: $10,000 through Day 30, $14,500 through Day 60, and $19,000 through Day 90. This sits inside the stated $8,000–$35,000 startup range; an $8,000 operator must launch a narrower single-lane pilot instead. Never fund importer duty principal, client settlement advances, or bond collateral from this budget. A fee contract specifies the 25%–35% rate, the actual recovery base, payment timing, reversals, and whether outside charges reduce the fee base. No first-month cash assumption depends on a claim paying early. The stated one-to-three-month first-deal interval measures engagement acquisition, not a promise of government or administrator payment.

> 
> A commercially attractive recovery does not enter production until authority, eligibility, evidence, and liquidity are independently recorded.
> - Verify the claimant entity, signer authority, broker engagement, and valid Customs Power of Attorney before regulated customs work proceeds.

## 2. Days 8–14: Prove Three Different Collectible Bases  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The drawback lane starts with 19 U.S.C. § 1313 and the applicable provision in 19 CFR Part 190, not a spreadsheet multiplying all tariffs by 99%. Eligible drawback generally returns up to 99% of qualifying duties, taxes, and fees, with the precise base controlled by the selected provision. Exclude antidumping and countervailing duties under 19 CFR § 190.3 and separately test trade-remedy restrictions; Section 232 treatment cannot be copied from a Section 301 analysis. For P01, prove identity and unused status. For P02, validate the applicable eight-digit HTS substitution rule, statutory restrictions, quantity matching, and the lesser-duty calculation; sharing a code alone does not establish eligibility. For P03, obtain manufacturing records and the required ruling framework before quoting recovery. The broker verifies the relevant five-year periods for export or destruction and complete claim filing rather than advertising an unlimited historical lookback. Budget an illustrative $600 documentation package for a clean pilot, excluding separately quoted filings and exceptional remediation. Accelerated payment requires applicable privileges and sufficient drawback bond coverage; ordinary processing does not carry that same accelerated-payment condition. Underwrite six-to-eighteen months for ordinary cash planning, treating this as a reserve assumption rather than a CBP service commitment. Export-trace failure stops that file immediately.

The tariff-refund lane separates a correction before liquidation from a protest after a protestable decision. A standard ACE Post-Summary Correction generally must be filed within 300 days of entry and at least fifteen days before scheduled liquidation, subject to the applicable program requirements and exceptions. The broker checks actual entry eligibility and current CBP instructions instead of applying that shorthand blindly. Under 19 U.S.C. § 1514, protests generally have a 180-day filing period measured from liquidation or the relevant decision, as applicable; a sales conversation never tolls the period. P06 and P07 require a defensible classification, valuation, or origin position, not merely a lower-rate alternative. P08 checks the specific exclusion, remedy instrument, and entry dates before accepting a refund theory. Collectible base equals the supported excess duty or other recoverable amount, not shipment value and not the entire entry's paid duty. Budget an illustrative $450 clean-file documentation package before separately quoted technical opinions. These filings do not ordinarily require a dedicated drawback accelerated-payment bond, although underlying import bonds remain relevant. Use three-to-twelve months for correction-related cash planning and six-to-twenty-four months for protests, with contested matters potentially longer. Record both the statutory deadline and the payment assumption so a slow payment never obscures a fast-expiring filing right.

The settlement lane begins with an actual court-authorized distribution process, not a headline settlement amount or a presumed share of treble damages. P09 and P10 identify an open, applicable class and covered purchases; P11 reconciles accounts-payable or merchant-processing records to legal entities and class periods; P12 submits only under the claimant authority and third-party filer terms that process permits. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23 governs federal class-action settlements, while the specific order and plan of allocation determine eligibility, deadlines, acceptable evidence, and payment calculations. Apply court-required disclosures and clearly state that eligible claimants can file directly at zero cost. Do not imply official administrator status or legal representation. The collectible base is the claimant's eventual distribution, not purchases, processing volume, alleged damages, or the gross settlement fund. Until an administrator provides a reliable allocation basis, track purchase evidence without converting it into a dollar recovery forecast. Budget an illustrative $300 clean-file documentation package and six-to-twenty-four months from submission to payment, with appeals or supplemental distributions capable of extending that interval. No CBP surety bond applies to this lane. By Day 14, retain two written engagements and complete one quantified, source-supported opportunity in a chosen primary lane; maintain separate registers for the other lanes rather than pooling weak estimates.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Keep the Evidence Advantage Without Buying Bad Files**
> 
> Experienced recovery operators spend early effort on documentary bottlenecks that determine whether a fee can ever become cash.
> - Ask for a small ACE entry extract and one complete export chain before requesting a five-year data dump; missing linkage appears faster in a controlled sample.
> - Store liquidation events as dated evidence, not a manually typed deadline alone; broker-confirmed event changes must recalculate the work queue.
> - Keep a duty-component exclusion column beside each recovery calculation so barred trade-remedy duties cannot reappear during spreadsheet aggregation.
> - Obtain the administrator's exact entity and purchase-period rules before normalizing an AP ledger; subsidiaries and acquisitions frequently break otherwise clean matches.

## 3. Days 15–21: Package Claims and Reconcile Fees to the Scorecard  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build one evidence manifest per claim, with source filename, row identifier, normalized quantity, currency, date basis, reviewer, and exception disposition. Python csv and decimal can normalize client exports without importing floating-point rounding errors into duty calculations; retain raw files and hash them with hashlib so transformations remain reproducible. The analyst links records, but the broker determines customs eligibility and approves the actual customs submission. For settlement work, the specialist follows the administrator's required format and obtains client attestations without inventing missing purchases. Reconcile every included transaction to source evidence; a 90% portfolio completeness target never authorizes filing the unsupported remainder. Count a package as ready only when all required fields and attestations for its included claim scope are complete. Preserve rejected rows in an exception file with a reason and remediation owner. The owner may monitor claim status but must not represent internal preparation as government acceptance. Record separate timestamps for preparation, licensed or authorized submission, receipt acknowledgment, substantive acceptance, payment, and fee collection. By Day 21, produce two independently checked packages and submit eligible work where the relevant filing window is open. If the window is closed, remove that opportunity from the launch cohort instead of manufacturing a production milestone.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the source set
   - Create a dated manifest of client-authorized source documents.
   - Retain raw exports, record file hashes, identify entity ownership, and prohibit edits to the preserved source copies.
2. Reconcile the collectible base
   - Calculate recoverable amounts separately for each legal lane.
   - The broker validates customs duty treatment; the settlement specialist applies the actual court-authorized allocation and evidence requirements.
3. Authorize and record submission
   - Route complete packages to the licensed or otherwise authorized filer.
   - Preserve authority, attestations, deadline calculations, submission receipts, and rejected-row explanations in the same case record.
4. Reconcile recovery and operator cash
   - Match actual client payment to the contracted contingency calculation.
   - Separate principal from fees, capture delivery costs and reversals, and report invoice issuance and fee collection as different events.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Day 60/90: Decide Kill, Fix, or Scale From Evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Close the sprint with a cohort dashboard rather than a celebratory pipeline total. Define a qualified conversation as one with a documented authorized entity, eligible lane hypothesis, accessible source records, and viable deadline. Define a signed engagement as executed contingency terms plus the authority needed for its next work stage. Define evidence-supported fees as contracted fees calculated on a broker-validated customs recovery estimate or a settlement allocation supported by official instructions and claimant records. Exclude unpriced settlement claims from this dollar metric while preserving their documented count. Set Day 30 targets at twelve qualified conversations, three signed engagements, two complete packages, at least one actual submission, and $15,000 of evidence-supported fees. Measure 90% evidence completeness across required items in the active cohort, 100% completeness for submitted scope, and zero missed filing deadlines. Claims lacking valid authority never enter the production denominator. Audit the dashboard on Days 27 and 29, with a contractor checking a file they did not prepare wherever role qualifications permit. Protect the distinction between a government delay and an operator defect. A delayed distribution can remain a viable asset; an invented allocation or expired filing right cannot. Day 30 evaluates a controllable delivery system, not an artificial requirement that CBP pay within one month.

Apply decision rules in order: hard-stop conditions first, Scale requirements second, and Fix for the remaining recoverable gaps. Kill means stop new spending on the affected lane or acquisition channel, not abandon existing client obligations. Missing authority, fabricated evidence, or an unrepairable expired claim kills the affected file immediately. At Day 30, kill the acquisition hypothesis if twelve qualified conversations produce zero signed engagements, or three signed files produce zero legally eligible packages. Fix a repairable shortfall with a named owner, a fourteen-day deadline, and at most $1,500 redirected from the existing monthly budget. Scale only when all dashboard targets, the 60% direct-contribution floor, the 20% acquisition ceiling, and funded runway are satisfied. Scaling initially means adding no more than two engaged clients in the next fortnight within the same pod and spending caps. At Day 60, require five engagements, four complete packages, three submissions, and $30,000 evidence-supported fees. At Day 90, require eight engagements, six packages, five submissions, and $50,000 evidence-supported fees. These are cumulative cohort thresholds, not monthly revenue targets. If contractor workload exceeds 80% of purchased capacity for two consecutive weeks, stop intake until throughput improves or a separately funded capacity change is approved by the owner.

Cash collection changes the decision but does not replace evidence quality. By Day 60, every submitted case needs a verified external status and a recorded next checkpoint; by Day 90, obtain either at least $5,000 in collected operator fees or documented continuing external processing plus ninety more days of funded runway before expanding. With no receipts, another quarter at the $4,500 monthly ceiling requires $13,500 beyond the initial $19,000, bringing total committed capital to $32,500. That remains within the stated startup range but leaves little room for bond collateral or exceptional litigation, which remain outside this model. Two missed remediation deadlines without new supporting evidence trigger channel shutdown; return or transfer records under the engagement terms and preserve required retention. Cash spending above the cumulative ceiling freezes discretionary purchases immediately. Existing claims continue through their authorized filer, with clients informed of status and responsibility. Expansion toward 2 FT plus 4 C requires collected fee contribution and a forward workload plan that funds added labor, not simply a larger nominal recovery pool. Publish one weekly reconciliation covering spend, authority, deadlines, package defects, submission status, client recoveries, and operator cash. The launch succeeds when the owner can explain every dollar and every next action without confusing eligibility, filing, acceptance, and payment.
