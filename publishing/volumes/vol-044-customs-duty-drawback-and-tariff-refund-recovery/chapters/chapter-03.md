## 1. Underwrite three collectible bases before pricing a recovery  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start every opportunity with three separate amounts: the client's economic activity, the legally supportable recovery, and the operator's earned fee. Only the third enters this chapter's revenue line. A merchant's card sales, an importer's customs value, and a defendant's announced settlement fund are sourcing signals rather than revenue. Underwrite drawback under 19 U.S.C. § 1313 using the applicable statutory method, documented duties, export or destruction evidence, and entry-level exclusions. For an illustrative unused-merchandise cohort, $100,000 of otherwise eligible paid duties becomes $99,000 at a 99% recovery ceiling; an 80% documentation pass rate leaves $79,200 expected collectible principal. A 30% contingency produces $23,760 of potential fees, against $6,000 of delivery labor, before acquisition and shared costs. The resulting $17,760 delivery contribution is not owner profit. The documentation filter is a pre-revenue eligibility haircut, not another charge against fees already earned. Require a licensed Part 111 broker or trade counsel, the appropriate Customs Power of Attorney, and a Part 190 evidence file. ImportGenius and Panjiva identify trade patterns; neither replaces ACE entry records or proves drawback entitlement.

Tariff refunds require a different denominator and clock. Model a $100,000 reviewed overpayment population with $60,000 supported by classification, valuation, origin, or other applicable legal grounds. At 30%, the potential fee is $18,000; $4,500 of broker and analyst delivery produces $13,500 before acquisition and overhead. Do not apply the drawback 99% ceiling mechanically to a refund of excess duties. A Post-Summary Correction addresses an eligible unliquidated entry under CBP's ACE procedures; a protest challenges a protestable decision under 19 U.S.C. § 1514. The ordinary protest window is 180 days from the relevant liquidation or decision, not five years from importation. Check the PSC submission deadline, entry status, and scheduled liquidation separately; operationally, the ordinary PSC cutoff is the earlier of 300 days after entry or 15 days before scheduled liquidation, subject to applicable program rules. Missing either route does not create a new refund right. Price exceptional litigation separately rather than burying it in a standard contingency. Ordinary correction or protest work does not itself require an accelerated-drawback bond; existing import bonds and litigation security are separate exposures. Route customs filings through the authorized professional and preserve the legal basis alongside the entry-level calculation.

For b2b_class_action_contingency_recovery, begin with the actual settlement definition, eligible purchase period, administrator instructions, and distribution formula. An illustrative $100,000 preliminary allocation becomes $60,000 after a 60% evidence pass rate, then $45,000 after a 75% distribution realization assumption. A 30% fee is $13,500, and $3,000 of claim preparation leaves $10,500 before acquisition and shared costs. This assumption is not a prediction for any named settlement. Monitor payment-card interchange, freight, auto parts, commercial insurance, and SaaS matters, but verify that each identified fund actually covers the client's purchases. Treat the supplied 85–90% small-business nonfiling thesis as an unverified sourcing hypothesis, not a collectible-revenue input. The fallback is verified operating businesses with primary processor or vendor statements, authenticated authority, and the court-approved third-party filing rider where required. Disclose that claimants may file directly at zero cost. Use 25–33% pricing for this play only where the governing order and contract permit it. Court schedules, appeals, deficiency rounds, and distribution adjustments control timing. No CBP surety bond attaches to this lane; any separate security requirement must come from the actual settlement instrument.

## 3. Fund the payout lock separately from invoice DSO  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Working capital starts when acquisition or evidence expenditure leaves the bank, not when a fee invoice appears. Track four dates for every claim: first paid work, completed submission, client recovery release, and operator fee collection. The submission-to-release interval is a payout lock; invoice-to-cash is DSO. Combining them into one DSO metric conceals the largest funding burden. For planning, assume drawback release 180 days after submission, tariff refunds 270 days, and settlement distributions 540 days, then add 15, 30, and 30 days respectively for fee collection. These are underwriting assumptions, not agency or court service commitments. Pre-submission work adds further exposure. An illustrative 50/30/20 fee mix has a weighted 279-day release interval and 22.5-day DSO, or 301.5 days after submission. Cost-weighted funding duration can differ because labor is staged and mature cohorts overlap. Consequently, the shorter scenario funding gaps below require documented release acceleration, established collections, or later vendor payment dates. Do not apply them to an unseasoned book automatically. Accelerated drawback can shorten timing only when the claimant qualifies and required security is available; an audit hold can remove that timing advantage without reducing the operator's immediate payroll obligations.

Calculate a first-pass operating float using annual cash expenditure divided by 360, multiplied by the unfunded gap in days. This model uses a 30-day planning month and excludes the noncash reserve charge while retaining financing expense. Annual cash expenditure is therefore $71,500, $267,800, and $358,800. At illustrative funding gaps of 180, 150, and 120 days, float is $35,750, $111,583.33, and $119,600. Ring-fence cash equal to the annual reserve allowance as an additional liquidity policy: $3,300, $15,450, and $27,600. Combined planning liquidity becomes $39,050, $127,033.33, and $147,200 before collateral, taxes, or unbudgeted client-support advances. This ring-fence is not a second income-statement expense. The $8,000–$35,000 startup range can fund setup and a narrow pilot, but it does not fully finance even the Conservative full-year run-rate under these assumptions. A first engagement in one to three months is not a first cash receipt. Stage contractor work, limit simultaneous unpaid cohorts, and negotiate documented milestone payments where commercially available. Do not count client recovery principal as available operating cash, even when a payment arrangement routes it through an intermediary before remittance.

Build the actual treasury model by week rather than relying solely on an annual ratio. Begin with unrestricted cash, subtract committed professional invoices and acquisition spend, then add fee receipts only on probability-adjusted dates supported by the claim's current status. Maintain separate buckets for submitted claims, accepted claims, authorized distributions, and issued payments. A settlement administrator's acknowledgment is not a payment authorization. For surety underwriting, obtain the premium, bond limit, collateral demand, indemnity terms, and treatment of outstanding accelerated payments. A hypothetical $20,000 collateral requirement increases Base liquidity from $127,033.33 to $147,033.33 without changing its modeled profit. If a lender will not advance against contingent claims, exclude that proposed facility entirely until there is a binding commitment and satisfied borrowing conditions. Funding cost depends on drawn balances, rates, fees, and duration; reconcile those mechanics to the annual budget. Extend the forecast through the latest material expected settlement payment, not merely thirteen weeks, while using a thirteen-week view for near-term payments. Stop new paid intake when the downside cash trough exceeds available unrestricted cash and committed borrowing capacity. Profitable backlog does not pay a contractor whose invoice matures this Friday.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open a lane-specific cohort ledger
   - Record the legal route, client authority, fee basis, and first expenditure date.
   - Keep claimant principal, potential fees, earned fees, and collected fees in separate fields.
2. Schedule evidence and professional payments
   - Allocate contractor milestones and acquisition costs to actual expected payment weeks.
   - Include failed intake expenditure and any restricted surety collateral without expensing collateral twice.
3. Forecast release and collection separately
   - Assign evidence-based payout dates and then add contractual fee-collection timing.
   - Compare mature cohorts with new cohorts and remove unsupported acceleration assumptions.
4. Authorize intake against downside liquidity
   - Admit another cohort only when unrestricted funds cover its stressed cash trough.
   - Count borrowing only after commitment and borrowing conditions are satisfied; preserve client principal separately.

## 4. Stress the reserve, deadline failures, and labor before adding volume  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Separate scrub, reversal, and repayment exposure because they belong at different points in the economics. Scrub removes unsupported recovery amounts before fee recognition, as in the three lane examples. A reversal reduces a previously recognized fee or creates a collection loss, depending on the facts and accounting treatment. The 3% allowance here is a management planning deduction; financial reporting must classify variable consideration, revenue reductions, and credit losses appropriately rather than putting everything into one expense account. Accelerated drawback repayment exposure can concern client principal many times larger than the operator's fee. A $3,000 fee reserve does not fund a $100,000 claimant repayment obligation. Read the bond indemnity and engagement allocation before accepting any exposure to that amount. Require broker escalation for unsupported export links, duplicate claims, quantity mismatches, and questionable eight-digit HTS substitution matches. Matching codes alone does not establish statutory eligibility. For settlements, compare entity names, tax identifiers, purchase periods, and prior submissions before packaging a claim. Correct deficiencies through the administrator's specified process; fabricated invoices or opportunistic duplicate submissions destroy the collectible base rather than improve conversion. Keep original evidence and the rationale for every material adjustment.

Run the same shocks across all three scenarios so scale does not disguise fragility. A 20% increase in contractor delivery reduces annual profit by $6,000, $22,000, and $30,000, leaving $29,200, $209,750, and $503,600. Raising the reserve allowance from 3% to 8% reduces profit by another five percentage points of revenue: $5,500, $25,750, and $46,000. A 25% CAC-budget overrun costs $3,500, $11,250, and $16,250. Each standalone stress holds every other budget constant. For a collectible-fee shock, reduce revenue 10%, reduce the 3% proportional allowance accordingly, and hold other costs fixed. Profit becomes $24,530, $181,795, and $444,360. The loss is 9.7% of original revenue because the allowance falls with revenue. Do not claim that 10% fewer recoveries automatically cuts historical acquisition or completed delivery work. For a combined stress, use 90% revenue, an 8% allowance on that reduced revenue, 120% delivery cost, and 125% acquisition cost. The resulting owner profits are $10,080, $125,370, and $356,710. These are downside operating cases, not revisions to the canonical scorecard. They reveal the cash and margin consequences of weak evidence, rework, and expensive replacement acquisition.

Convert the stresses into specific intake decisions. A drawback prospect with incomplete export traces gets a bounded evidence-retrieval assignment, not a full-price filing commitment. A tariff prospect near liquidation gets immediate professional deadline triage before more sourcing expense. A settlement prospect without primary statements gets a retrieval request and a spending cap, not an allocation estimate based on total sales. Review cohort economics monthly using realized fee per paying account, delivery hours per accepted claim, acquisition spend per collected account, deficiency frequency, and days from first expenditure to collected fee. Compare those measures by lane and vintage because a recently submitted claim and an issued payment have different risk profiles. Maintain Part 190 records for the required period and preserve settlement evidence according to the governing order and engagement obligations. Price new engagements within the authorized 25–35% customs range or 25–33% settlement range only after estimating documentation difficulty and funding duration. Reject work whose legal route or authority cannot be established. The small-team advantage comes from reusable reconciliation and controlled exceptions, not from treating every imported shipment or business purchase as recoverable. Expand only when observed collections support both the fee margin and the required liquidity.
