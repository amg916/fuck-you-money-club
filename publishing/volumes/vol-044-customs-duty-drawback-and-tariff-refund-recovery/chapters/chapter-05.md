## 1. Kill Ineligible Claims Before They Become Expensive Inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with three separate entitlement ledgers, not one recovery pipeline. Drawback under 19 U.S.C. § 1313 requires a qualifying import, export or destruction, an applicable statutory provision, and a defensible duty calculation. A tariff correction concerns the treatment of an import entry; a settlement claim concerns membership in a court-defined class. Neither inherits drawback eligibility. The chapter's four drawback plays are unused-merchandise tracing, manufacturing recovery, substitution matching, and accelerated-payment management. Unused-merchandise tracing with $100,000 of eligible paid duties has a maximum illustrative recovery of $99,000 under an applicable 99% provision; a 30% fee is $29,700, before documentation expense. Manufacturing recovery with $40,000 of eligible allocated duties produces at most $39,600 and an $11,880 fee on the same assumptions. Require production records, bills of material, yields, and applicable manufacturing authorization rather than treating exports of finished goods as proof that every imported input qualifies. Exclude barred duty components before calculating fees; Section 301 duties are not automatically excluded, while particular Section 232 measures can expressly prohibit drawback. Inspect the governing measure and entry date rather than generalizing from a tariff headline. The circuit-breaker is zero unresolved statutory exclusions in any submitted amount. Quarantine the affected line, preserve eligible lines, and obtain broker or trade-counsel analysis before restoring it.

The four tariff-refund plays are Post-Summary Correction, liquidation protest, classification correction, and first-sale valuation review. They are workflows within one lane, not four recoveries available against the same duty dollar. An ACE Post-Summary Correction generally must satisfy the applicable filing window, commonly 300 days after entry or 15 days before scheduled liquidation, whichever occurs first, plus program eligibility restrictions. A protest under 19 U.S.C. § 1514 generally has a 180-day clock tied to the relevant liquidation or protestable decision. Do not start either clock from discovery of an error or receipt of a consultant's report. For an illustrative $50,000 collectible correction, a 30% fee yields $15,000; $3,000 of broker and evidence expense leaves $12,000 before overhead. A separate $30,000 protest yields $9,000 of fees, but a missed filing deadline can reduce that opportunity to zero. Classification correction on $200,000 of entered value, from an assumed 12% rate to 8%, saves $8,000 and supports only $2,400 of fees. First-sale review of a $100,000 supportable valuation reduction at 10% duty yields $10,000 and $3,000 of fees, not a percentage of merchandise value. Use licensed customs-broker or trade-counsel filing arrangements with valid authority. Escalate approaching deadlines immediately; an internal timer cannot create a remedy after the statutory window closes.

The twelfth mapped play, section_321_de_minimis_bonded_tariff_arbitrage, is a high-risk adjacency rather than an assumed recovery entitlement. Its proposed Tijuana IMMEX or Canadian bonded-warehouse routing cannot be priced on nationality, parcel size, or Entry Type 86 availability alone. Broad United States de minimis suspensions introduced in 2025 mean the earlier blanket exemption premise is unusable without checking operative orders, entry procedures, origin restrictions, and effective dates. Vietnam, India, EU, Bangladesh, or Cambodia origin is not an automatic fallback, and non-301 status does not establish eligibility. For historical sensitivity only, $100,000 of otherwise dutiable value at 20% implies $20,000 of potential duty avoidance; a 40% service share would be $8,000 if the exemption genuinely applied. If it does not, savings-based revenue is zero while warehousing and transport costs continue. Never split one commercial order artificially, falsify consignee identities, or disguise origin to satisfy a threshold. IMMEX authorization and Canadian customs-bonded status also impose their own inventory and movement obligations; neither automatically creates a United States exemption. The fallback is ordinary dutiable entry, a documented first-sale valuation analysis under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a where supportable, or abandoning the route. Keep fulfillment fees, actual costs, and import-duty savings in separate ledgers. Do not count this prospective service revenue inside the canonical recovery scorecard.

## 2. Make Every Claimed Dollar Survive a Document-Level Replay  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Substitution matching is where a convincing spreadsheet can conceal an invalid entitlement. ImportGenius and Panjiva are prospecting and shipment-discovery tools, not substitutes for ACE entry records, CBP Form 7501, paid-duty detail, or export evidence. An eight-digit HTS match is a screening condition under relevant substitution provisions, not universal permission to interchange merchandise. Apply the chosen statutory subsection's classification, description, value, quantity, and other restrictions, including limitations associated with certain residual descriptions. If a hypothetical substitution calculation compares $12,000 of import-side duties with an applicable $8,000 export-side limitation, a 99% factor produces $7,920, not $11,880; the 30% fee is $2,376. The exact comparison depends on the provision and transaction facts. Build matching around immutable source identifiers, explicit unit conversions, and a retained explanation of each limitation. Reconcile kilograms, pieces, and commercial packs before any quantity allocation. A tenfold conversion error can create an overclaim even when both invoices appear authentic. Flag every allocation exceeding an available imported or exported quantity and block submission until resolved. Use a batch-level defect trigger of more than 2% unmatched lines for additional sampling, while requiring complete support for every submitted line. Fallback to a narrower supported claim or direct identification where independently eligible; never force a substitution match to preserve a quoted fee.

The settlement lane has three mapped plays: class-eligibility screening, AP-ledger packaging, and distribution reconciliation. Start with the operative settlement agreement, court orders, administrator instructions, covered purchase period, claimant definition, exclusions, and assignment provisions. A supplier name or card-processing expense does not establish class membership. For an illustrative claimant with $500,000 of eligible purchases, a hypothetical 2% distribution would be $10,000, supporting a $3,000 fee at 30%; that percentage is a scenario input, not an administrator commitment. AP-ledger packaging with 2,000 records at $0.75 processing cost consumes $1,500 before exception handling. If an additional 20 hours at $60 costs $1,200, only $300 remains from that fee before overhead. Reject low-value files early or batch them efficiently without fabricating evidence. Match legal entities, subsidiaries, purchase dates, credits, returns, and administrator-specific identifiers. Deduplicate using the administrator's claim structure rather than assuming one tax identifier always equals one permitted claim. Provide court-required third-party filer disclosures, including that claimants may file directly at zero cost. Obtain valid claimant authorization and do not imply affiliation with the court or administrator. When a client has already filed, coordinate amendment or withdrawal through the administrator's permitted process. A second submission is not incremental revenue and can delay both submissions or cause rejection.

Every lane needs a replayable evidence package that survives the departure of its original preparer. Store source documents separately from normalized tables, preserve hashes and extraction versions, and link every claimed amount to its calculation and authority. For drawback, index import entries, duty payment evidence, transfers of rights where applicable, export bills of lading, inventory movements, and any required notices. For tariff refunds, preserve the original treatment, proposed treatment, technical support, filing receipt, and liquidation history. For settlements, preserve purchase evidence, authorization, disclosures, submission confirmation, deficiency correspondence, and distribution records. Do not overwrite original PDFs when correcting optical character recognition. A changed extraction should create a new version with a reviewer and reason. Internal defect metrics should distinguish missing evidence from contradictory evidence: one unresolved contradiction about quantity, ownership, or date blocks the affected claim even when document completeness exceeds 99%. Set a two-business-day internal target for classifying administrator deficiencies, then work backward from the actual response deadline. Monitor evidence cost per eligible dollar, not pages processed. Spending $2,000 to establish $5,000 of recovery produces only $1,500 of fees at 30%, before overhead. Stop that file unless scope reduction, shared retrieval costs, or a separately agreed fee structure restores the economics. Configure retention by the applicable records rule and preserve longer for active disputes or litigation holds.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Evidence Habits That Protect Small-Team Margins**
> 
> Reduce rework before optimizing extraction speed; the expensive defects are usually entitlement defects.
> - Ask for one complete import-to-export chain before accepting a drawback portfolio; use the pilot to price exception handling and rights-transfer work.
> - Maintain separate HTS-code, commercial-unit, and legal-entity dictionaries with effective dates so a corrected mapping does not silently rewrite old claims.
> - Request prior settlement submission confirmations during onboarding, not after a duplicate notice arrives from the administrator.
> - Save the governing settlement instructions and tariff measure with each claim version so later changes cannot obscure the basis of the original calculation.

## 3. Separate Earned Fees from Advances, Bond Exposure, and Recovery Principal  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical Formula v2.0 scenarios measure earned contingency fees, not importer duties, merchandise GMV, loan principal, or total settlement pools. Conservative revenue of $110,000 at 32% yields $35,200 owner net profit and implies $74,800 of total costs. Base revenue of $515,000 at 45.0% yields $231,750 and implies $283,250 of costs. Scaled revenue of $920,000 at 58% yields $533,600 and implies $386,400 of costs. Staffing remains 1 FT + 3 C for Conservative and Base, and 2 FT + 4 C for Scaled. These are complete annual scenarios; do not subtract the advertised monthly operating range again from already net owner profit. At a uniform illustrative 30% fee, their associated client recoveries would be approximately $366,666.67, $1,716,666.67, and $3,066,666.67. Those recovery amounts are not additional operator revenue. Actual portfolios may use 25%–35% fees and mixed recovery sizes. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent and variable-consideration analysis to actual contracts; the scorecard supplies earned-fee revenue, not a separate retained-share multiplier. Do not invent one or net the same broker expense twice. An internal 15% unreconciled-receivables trigger should initiate a contract-to-cash review, not a revenue adjustment by reflex. Reconcile earned status, collection rights, remittances, reversals, and presentation before changing the books.

Accelerated-payment management is the fourth drawback play and the largest asymmetric cash trap. Under the applicable Part 190 process, qualifying claimants may receive payment before final liquidation, typically with bond support and required privileges or approvals. Payment does not extinguish examination risk or make a deficient claim valid. A $100,000 accelerated recovery can create a $30,000 fee opportunity at 30%, but the claimant's repayment exposure may be materially larger than the operator's fee. Determine exactly who is principal, surety, indemnitor, and contractual reimbursement obligor. Do not assume a customs broker's bond insures the recovery firm against its own promises. Premium, collateral, aggregate capacity, claim concentration, and indemnity wording are separate variables. In an illustrative stress test, a 20% disallowance creates $20,000 of recovery repayment and a $6,000 fee reversal if the contract makes that fee refundable proportionally, before possible interest and other costs. A 10% internal reserve on the $30,000 fee is only $3,000 and therefore fails that stress. Set reserves from exposure and contract terms rather than an attractive fixed percentage. Stop new accelerated-payment commitments when modeled adverse cash requirements exceed available liquidity or documented bond headroom. The fallback is ordinary-payment processing with explicit client timing expectations, not moving the claim through another entity to conceal concentration.

Use lane-specific cash calendars rather than the advertised 1–3 months to first deal as a cash-receipt promise. For planning stress tests, model ordinary drawback collections at 12–24 months, tariff-refund collections at 6–18 months, and settlement distributions at 12–36 months; actual timing can be shorter or substantially longer. Accelerated drawback timing depends on eligibility, bond arrangements, processing, and holds, so substitute the broker's documented assumptions rather than a universal forecast. PSC and protest refunds do not use the drawback accelerated-payment bond merely because both involve CBP. Settlement filings ordinarily have no customs surety requirement, but administrator reviews, appeals, allocation changes, and returned payments can delay cash. Distribution reconciliation, the eleventh mapped play, converts an actual $20,000 settlement receipt into a $6,000 fee at 30%; a later $2,000 distribution reversal may require a $600 fee reversal under the contract. Keep client funds segregated from operating cash under the applicable custody arrangement and reconcile every remittance. For planning, six months of $7,000 monthly cash burn requires $42,000, exceeding the stated $35,000 startup-range ceiling before reserves or collateral. Reduce burn, secure committed capital, or stagger onboarding before taking that exposure. Trigger weekly review when an expected receipt slips 30 days, but never pressure staff to recognize an unearned fee to repair runway.

## 4. Stop Access Abuse and Convert Warning Signals into Containment  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Portal access is an operational dependency, not an entitlement purchased with a contingency agreement. A client authorization does not permit sharing a broker's ACE credentials, submitting outside the authorized relationship, impersonating a claimant, or bypassing an administrator's access controls. Use individually assigned accounts, documented roles, and supported interfaces. For customs business requiring a license, route formal work through the licensed broker or trade counsel with valid authority; an extraction contractor does not acquire that authority by preparing a spreadsheet. The classic shortcuts are fabricated export bills, altered invoices, borrowed importer identities, synthetic purchase histories, concealed third-party filing, and automated resubmission after rejection. These can produce claim denial, credential suspension, permanent account exclusion under provider terms, repayment demands, and civil or criminal exposure depending on the conduct. Neither a successful upload nor an administrator receipt validates the underlying entitlement. Under 19 U.S.C. § 1592, materially false statements or omissions connected to merchandise entry can create serious customs exposure; fraudulent claims against the United States may implicate additional statutes when their elements are met. Monitor rejected authentication attempts, permission changes, unsigned authorizations, and duplicate submission receipts daily. One unexpected privileged-account change or unexplained filing triggers immediate containment. Preserve logs, revoke the affected access through supported controls, and have the authorized filer assess required corrections before any replacement submission.

Build telemetry around economic damage rather than dashboard activity. Count eligible dollars supported, submitted dollars accepted, realized recoveries, earned fees, collected fees, direct labor hours, and contingent repayment exposure separately. A high document-processing rate can coexist with negative contribution when every extracted line needs specialist repair. Set an internal yellow threshold when direct processing cost exceeds 40% of expected fees and a stop-and-reprice threshold at 60%, unless a documented portfolio rationale covers the difference. At $10,000 expected fees, $4,000 of direct effort warrants intervention; $6,000 leaves only $4,000 for overhead, risk, and owner return. Measure deadline slack from the actual legal deadline and include retrieval, broker review, correction, and transmission time. A 30-day warning and 10-day escalation are internal defaults, not substitutes for earlier filing when complexity requires it. Track claim concentration using exposure, not merely count: one importer generating 45% of projected fees deserves a collection-delay stress test even if hundreds of small claims exist. Track portal rejection reasons and cohort-level defect recurrence; two repeated material defects from the same source trigger source quarantine and expanded review. Do not silently average a defective cohort into a healthy portfolio. Assign each alarm an owner, response deadline, and explicit release condition so monitoring changes behavior.

An incident becomes survivable when containment precedes sales reassurance. Freeze only the affected submission population initially, then widen the freeze if shared mappings, source integrity, authority, or calculation defects connect other claims. Preserve the original files, account logs, formulas, correspondence, and signed authorizations. Recalculate exposure in three columns: client principal potentially repayable, operator fees potentially reversible, and incremental remediation expense. For example, a portfolio with $200,000 of questioned recovery, $60,000 of associated 30% fees, and $8,000 of estimated remediation cannot be summarized as an $8,000 support ticket. The operator's actual liability depends on contracts and applicable rules, but liquidity planning must show each possible obligation explicitly. Have the authorized broker, counsel, or settlement administrator determine the correct amendment, withdrawal, repayment, or response process. Do not destroy files, create replacement identities, or resubmit corrected amounts without preserving the original trail. Notify affected clients with confirmed facts, unresolved questions, and the next decision date rather than a promised recovery. Resume the cohort only after the root cause is corrected, calculations are independently replayed, and required external responses are completed. Then update onboarding pricing, reserve assumptions, and source acceptance rules. A small team wins by reducing repeatable defects, not by accepting every available claim or treating administrative silence as final acceptance.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Contain the affected claim population
   - Stop new submissions and fee distributions tied to the identified defect.
   - Define the cohort by source, mapping version, claimant, entry period, or account; preserve all original evidence and expand containment when shared defects appear.
2. Reconstruct entitlement and exposure
   - Replay calculations from source documents and separate principal, fee reversal, and remediation amounts.
   - Record statutory deadlines, actual collection status, bond obligations, indemnities, client authorizations, and contract-specific reimbursement duties without assuming they are identical.
3. Use the authorized correction channel
   - Have the licensed filer, trade counsel, or settlement administrator direct the applicable response.
   - Preserve submission history and receipts; complete required amendments, withdrawals, repayments, or deficiency responses through supported procedures rather than duplicate accounts.
4. Release only independently verified work
   - Require a second reviewer to confirm the repaired calculation and completed external responses.
   - Update pricing, reserve assumptions, mapping controls, and source acceptance criteria; monitor the next comparable cohort for recurrence before restoring normal throughput.
