## 1. Mine Twelve Evidence Signals Before Building the Prospect List  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with three separate account universes: importers that also export, importers with potentially correctable entry treatment, and businesses that purchased products covered by an actual settlement. ImportGenius and Panjiva identify shipment patterns, counterparties, ports, and product descriptions; neither establishes duties paid or refund rights. Match names to corporate websites, state entity registers, and SEC EDGAR subsidiary exhibits before contacting a controller or trade manager. Ocean manifests miss substantial air, land, and confidential shipment activity, so absence is not a rejection criterion. Record the source date, suspected importing entity, export signal, probable decision maker, and evidence needed next. One importer with recurring returns to an overseas supplier can outrank twenty high-volume importers whose goods never leave domestic commerce. A useful first batch contains forty import-export accounts, thirty entry-correction accounts, and thirty settlement-purchase accounts. These are sourcing allocations, not claims that every account qualifies. Give each account one primary lane and one evidence hypothesis. Deduplicate legal entities and existing client relationships before enrichment. Never describe a manifest estimate as an ACE entry balance, an approved drawback amount, or money already held for the prospect. Public signals earn a conversation; authorized transaction records establish the recoverable base.

For drawback, search distributor export catalogs, foreign warranty return instructions, warehouse job postings, and announcements of international fulfillment operations. Ask whether the importer can connect entry lines to exported merchandise through SKU, lot, inventory, or legally valid substitution evidence. Eight-digit HTS matching is a screening aid, not a universal eligibility rule; particular subheadings and statutory provisions impose additional restrictions. For tariff correction, monitor published CBP rulings, relevant court decisions, supplier changes, and procurement notices suggesting classification, origin, or valuation changes. Obtain the actual entry and liquidation status before proposing either a Post-Summary Correction or protest. For antitrust recovery, begin with the court docket, settlement agreement, certified class definition, and administrator website. Map covered products, purchase dates, geography, and direct or indirect purchaser status to an authorized accounts payable export. A named supplier appearing in a ledger does not establish qualifying purchases. Twelve plays appear below as practical sourcing routes, not twelve interchangeable legal entitlements. Each route needs a collectible base, a documentation budget, and a failure fallback. Prioritize records that already exist in NetSuite, SAP, QuickBooks, or the broker's reporting system over evidence that someone promises to reconstruct later.

Use the parcel play as a separately contracted adjacent service: parcel_carrier_refund_tariff_audit_contingency. Request authorized FedEx Billing Online, UPS Billing Center, and DHL invoice exports, shipment events, measured dimensions, and effective service agreements. Screen late-delivery guarantees, dimensional-weight discrepancies, and duplicate charges separately. Exclude suspended guarantees, negotiated waivers, expired submission windows, and charges supported by the contract. At a negotiated 40% fee, $10,000 of realized eligible refunds generates $4,000 of operator revenue and leaves $6,000 with the customer before any separately agreed expenses. Do not add that illustration to the canonical customs and settlement scorecard. A minimum-net-charge provision is not an overcharge merely because it reduces the customer's discount. Negotiate prospective caps or floors only where offered, then measure savings against an agreed shipment-level baseline controlling for service, zone, weight, and surcharges. Keep prospective savings compensation separate from historical refund fees. If access changes or a submission integration breaks, pause the affected automation, use authorized exports and supported manual channels, and forecast only confirmed eligible services with open windows. Parcel evidence can win a controller's trust quickly, but it never supplies missing customs eligibility or validates a settlement claim.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Source the Missing Evidence, Not the Largest Shipping Footprint**
> 
> The best small-team accounts combine meaningful eligible recovery with records that can be produced without extensive reconstruction.
> - Ask the incumbent broker for an authorized entry-line report before purchasing a second manifest database; duties paid and entry identifiers determine whether deeper work is justified.
> - Request one complete import-to-export chain first, including commercial documents and export support; a thousand incomplete rows do not compensate for one broken evidentiary link.
> - Use settlement administrator instructions to design the ledger request, including covered products and entity names, instead of requesting every accounts payable field.
> - Obtain the carrier contract effective on each shipment date; a current discount schedule cannot establish whether an older charge was erroneous.

## 2. Qualify Three Recovery Lanes with Separate Clocks and Cost Models  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a qualification score out of one hundred points: twenty-five for documentary completeness, twenty-five for identifiable eligible recovery, twenty for deadline runway, fifteen for authority and ownership, and fifteen for expected contribution after delivery costs. This is an internal prioritization model, not the candidate's 95/100 Money-to-Manpower Score. Require seventy-five points and no fatal defect before issuing a priced engagement. A missing export trace, expired protest deadline, excluded class member, or unauthorized signatory overrides the numerical score. Keep a separate remediation queue for repairable gaps. Ask for a tightly scoped sample: ten entry lines, ten export transactions, or twenty covered purchase lines, depending on the lane. Request records through a client-approved portal with restricted access, not an unsolicited email attachment. Record the entity owning the claim, the individual authorized to engage providers, any prior filing, and whether another recovery firm has an exclusive mandate. Estimate review hours before promising zero upfront pricing. A $10,000 collectible recovery at 25% supports $2,500 of fees; six hours at $150 plus $400 of broker or specialist cost leaves $1,200 before acquisition expense and overhead. A small eligible claim can therefore be a poor acquisition target.

For drawback, 19 U.S.C. § 1313 supplies the statutory framework, while 19 CFR Part 190 governs the modern filing and evidence mechanics. Many qualifying recoveries reach 99% of eligible duties, taxes, and fees, but neither every duty component nor every exported item qualifies. Apply the particular provision, time limits, and substitution restrictions to each transaction. The general five-year filing horizon runs from importation; it is not permission to wait five years after discovering an export. Separate antidumping and countervailing duties and other barred components from the recoverable base. Retain licensed broker or trade counsel support for formal customs filings under valid authority, including the required customs power of attorney for broker representation. Budget a sample $1,200 documentation review for a prospective $100,000 drawback recovery; replace that allowance with a written provider quote before signing. Accelerated payment can require approval and suitable bond coverage, with surety premium, collateral, and capacity affecting economics. Ordinary processing is not equivalent to accelerated payment and should not inherit an assumed accelerated-payment bond cost. Plan for collection over months or longer, with audit holds possible; never fund a guaranteed ninety-day payout from a sourcing spreadsheet.

Tariff corrections require a different clock. The standard ACE Post-Summary Correction window generally closes at the earlier of 300 days after entry or fifteen days before scheduled liquidation, subject to transaction eligibility and current CBP requirements. A protest under 19 U.S.C. § 1514 generally has a 180-day deadline following liquidation or another protestable decision. Special refund provisions can impose different procedures, so capture the actual legal route rather than labeling every adjustment a protest. Budget an illustrative $900 review for a $40,000 correction; contested matters require separate counsel pricing. There is no universal new recovery bond for every protest, although underlying customs obligations remain. Antitrust distributions instead follow the court-approved class definition, claims process, and allocation plan. Budget $600 for a $20,000 distribution illustration, without presenting that distribution as predictable from purchase volume alone. No customs surety bond belongs in this lane. Court proceedings, appeals, deficiency review, and distribution scheduling can delay payment substantially. Provide required third-party filer disclosures and clearly explain that eligible claimants can file directly at zero cost. Stop when the court or administrator restricts representation, prohibits the proposed arrangement, or requires authority the client cannot provide.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Accepting a Recovery Mandate**
> 
> A high qualification score cannot repair a defective claimant, closed filing window, prohibited recovery component, or unsupported fee basis.
> - Verify the claimant's legal identity, recovery ownership, authorized signatory, prior filings, and any exclusive provider agreement against actual documents.
> - Have the licensed broker or trade counsel confirm the customs route, applicable deadline, required power of attorney, and transaction-level eligibility before formal filing.
> - Reconcile duties, export evidence, or covered purchases; remove barred components and unresolved duplicates from both the claim population and commercial forecast.
> - Confirm written fee scope, specialist costs, applicable surety needs, direct-filing disclosures, and the event that makes a contingency fee earned and collectible.

## 3. Run a Measurable Multi-Channel Cadence That Converts Evidence into Mandates  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Launch with a named-account batch rather than a mass address list. The working funnel is 300 researched accounts, 180 validated decision maker contacts, 36 substantive replies, 18 discovery calls, ten authorized sample packages, six qualified proposals, and three signed mandates. Those assumptions imply 60% contact coverage, 20% reply conversion from validated contacts, 50% reply-to-call conversion, 56% call-to-sample conversion, 60% sample-to-proposal conversion, and 50% proposal-to-signature conversion. They are planning benchmarks, not observed industry averages. At the same 1% account-to-mandate yield, ten mandates require approximately 1,000 researched accounts or better conversion from trusted introductions. A solo principal should not promise that larger volume without contractor research capacity and sufficient broker throughput. Track introductions from customs brokers, freight forwarders, fractional controllers, and acquisition operating partners as separate cohorts. Many brokers already offer drawback services; propose a complementary evidence-reconstruction role instead of claiming to replace their customs expertise. Discuss referral compensation only after checking the applicable professional and contractual restrictions. Attribute each opportunity to its first meaningful source, record acquisition hours, and compare fee contribution per reviewed sample rather than celebrating reply rates that produce no usable records.

Use a five-touch, fifteen-business-day cadence with a single evidence request. On day one, send a short email naming the observable trigger: recurring exports, a documented entry issue, or purchases potentially covered by a specific settlement. Say what the signal does and does not establish. On day three, call the published business line and ask who controls the relevant records. On day six, send a one-page evidence checklist and a redacted sample reconciliation created from synthetic transactions. On day ten, offer a fifteen-minute joint call with the broker or settlement specialist. On day fifteen, close the sequence with a clear choice to provide a sample, defer, or stop contact. Honor opt-outs immediately and maintain a suppression list. Commercial email must follow 15 U.S.C. § 7704, including truthful headers, required identification, postal address, and functioning opt-out handling. Avoid automated calling or texting without separately confirming applicable requirements. LinkedIn provides professional context, not permission to scrape restricted records. Publish inbound pages around evidence questions, such as whether export records can support drawback, rather than invented refund calculators. Route every inbound request through the same ownership, eligibility, and deadline checks used for outbound prospects.

Handle objections by reducing evidentiary uncertainty rather than discounting indiscriminately. When the controller says the broker already handles refunds, ask for permission to compare the broker's covered scope with one missed-export or correction hypothesis. Offer a written no-duplication boundary and include the broker in the review. When they say the company can file a settlement claim itself, agree, identify the official administrator, and explain the optional paid work: entity reconciliation, historical invoice normalization, deficiency responses, and tracking. When they resist sharing records, request fewer fields through their approved portal and demonstrate the exact matching procedure. When they demand a payout date, distinguish filing readiness, agency or court processing, and actual collection. Quote 25%–35% of realized eligible recoveries for the core lanes, with expenses and specialist participation explicitly allocated. Describe parcel recovery's separate 35%–50% range only when that service is actually proposed. The close is a signed mandate plus a named records owner, scheduled sample delivery, and authorized professional engagement. A verbal expression of interest is not a win. If the prospect cannot supply records or has no eligible route, close the opportunity cleanly instead of manufacturing urgency around a speculative deadline.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build and deduplicate the evidence queue
   - Research the first one hundred accounts across three distinct recovery lanes.
   - Record source links, claimant entity, observable trigger, decision maker, and the single document sample needed to test the hypothesis.
2. Secure authorized samples and professional review
   - Collect narrow transaction samples and score eligibility, authority, deadline runway, and delivery contribution.
   - Have the broker or trade counsel validate customs pathways; use current court and administrator documents for settlement pathways.
3. Execute the fifteen-business-day acquisition cadence
   - Combine specific email, business-line outreach, evidence checklists, and specialist discovery.
   - Track stage conversions by source cohort, honor opt-outs, and replace unsupported urgency with actual filing dates.
4. Close an evidence-ready mandate and hand it over
   - Execute scope, authority, fee terms, record delivery dates, and required representative documents.
   - Assign the delivery owner, preserve the evidence chain, and keep signing milestones separate from recovery and fee collection.

## 4. Land the First Three to Ten Wins Without Confusing Mandates with Cash  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Organize the first ninety days around evidence throughput. During weeks one and two, retain a licensed broker relationship, confirm trade counsel escalation capacity, establish the client portal, and build the initial one hundred accounts. During weeks three and four, complete the first ten discovery conversations and obtain enough samples to expose recurring documentation failures. During weeks five through eight, complete six qualified proposals and target the first three signed, evidence-ready mandates. During weeks nine through twelve, expand the best-performing source cohort and pursue additional mandates only within review capacity. Three to ten wins means executed engagements with usable records and a viable route, not approvals or paid distributions. Review deadlines daily, pipeline stages twice weekly, and source economics weekly. Keep a separate aging report for samples awaiting clients, technical questions awaiting professionals, submitted matters awaiting decisions, and earned invoices awaiting payment. With startup capital of $8,000–$35,000 and monthly operating expenses of $1,500–$9,500, select a spend plan that survives delayed collections. Reserve actual provider commitments first. Do not spend the upper monthly range merely because a large settlement pool appears in a prospecting presentation; the operator owns neither the pool nor its distribution schedule.

Reconcile revenue to the canonical Formula v2.0 scenarios using earned contingency fees only. One transparent bridge assumes a 25% operator fee with no additional retained-share haircut: $440,000 of realized client recoveries produces $110,000 Conservative revenue; $2,060,000 produces $515,000 Base revenue; and $3,680,000 produces $920,000 Scaled revenue. These recovery amounts illustrate the fee bridge rather than a prescribed mix of claims. They exclude parcel fees and never count importer duty principal or settlement pool size as operator revenue. Annual costs reconcile to $74,800, $283,250, and $386,400 respectively, leaving $35,200 at 32%, $231,750 at 45.0%, and $533,600 at 58%. The teams remain 1 FT + 3 C, 1 FT + 3 C, and 2 FT + 4 C. Monthly operating expense ranges are not the whole annual cost stack; include delivery labor, contractor payments, professional services, acquisition, and other scenario expenses. Where a broker or partner owns part of the customer fee, evaluate principal-agent presentation under ASC 606 and model the operator's retained entitlement explicitly. Never count the same partner share both as excluded revenue and again as an expense.

Make capacity and cash conversion visible before scaling sourcing. Use an illustrative twenty-hour monthly sample-review budget: ten two-hour samples fit, while forty do not without additional qualified labor. Measure median sample preparation time, percentage accepted without rework, specialist turnaround, and signed-mandate contribution after acquisition expense. A $25,000 fee opportunity with $5,000 expected delivery cost and $1,000 acquisition cost offers $19,000 before shared overhead, but its long collection interval still requires working capital. Probability-weighted pipeline values are planning estimates, not earned revenue. Record a recovery only against the appropriate payment evidence and recognize the fee when the contractual earning conditions and applicable accounting criteria are satisfied. For drawbacks, preserve import, export, matching, and professional submission records under the applicable retention requirements. For tariff corrections, retain the original treatment, supporting analysis, filing evidence, and decision. For settlements, retain eligibility support, claimant authorization, submission confirmation, deficiency correspondence, and distribution records. The repeatable asset is an auditable evidence pipeline with narrow handoffs, not a large contact database. Scale the cohort that produces clean files and collected fees; suspend the cohort generating attractive estimates but repeated deadline defects, ownership disputes, or unrecoverable documentation expense.
