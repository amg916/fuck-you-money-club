## 1. Sell recovered cash to the person who can authorize the fee  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The importer or eligible settlement claimant buys the service; CBP and settlement administrators ordinarily pay the claimant, not your operating company. An importer CFO values a documented receivable because duties have already consumed working capital, while the controller fears reopening historical entries without a defensible audit trail. The trade director controls classification evidence and broker relationships but may lack authority to approve a contingency contract. Procurement can approve a supplier yet still leave treasury unable to release payment. Identify all four functions before promising a start date: economic sponsor, records owner, contract signatory, and treasury approver. For a $200,000 realized recovery with a 30% fee and no deductible adjustments, the commercial decision is whether retaining $140,000 justifies releasing $60,000 for work the internal team cannot prioritize. Do not describe the entire $200,000 as your revenue. Ask the sponsor to explain the purchase internally using expired-rights exposure, evidence workload, and incremental cash rather than a speculative savings multiple. A signed engagement from a purchasing analyst is insufficient when the entity's delegation schedule reserves contingent liabilities to the CFO. Verify authority against an actual delegation record or written confirmation from an authorized officer.

Acute pain differs across the three lanes, so discovery must establish the actual deadline before discussing upside. Drawback rights generally require completion within the applicable five-year framework under 19 U.S.C. § 1313; the particular subsection controls qualifying exports, destruction, substitutions, and filing requirements. A classification refund may instead turn on an unliquidated entry's PSC availability, the generally 180-day protest period under § 1514, or an agreement-specific § 1520(d) claim generally due within one year of importation. Settlement recovery follows the court-approved notice, class definition, exclusions, and submission date, not customs deadlines. Translate each into a dated decision memo identifying the last actionable event and who owns the missing evidence. An importer with $80,000 of supportable refunds has a different buying problem from a distributor with $8 million of settlement-class purchases: the latter figure is purchase volume, not an expected distribution. Present the buyer with separate eligibility, valuation, and timing conclusions. Never use a filing deadline to imply that government approval is certain. When records are incomplete, quote a bounded evidence-retrieval task and preserve independently supportable claims through the authorized professional rather than inflating the recovery forecast.

The twelve mapped operating applications below use the two supplied play identifiers exactly; the other ten labels describe this chapter's recovery workflows rather than asserting additional catalog identifiers. The first six establish commercial demand and customs evidence; the second six develop claims, authority, and collection protection. For high_velocity_dm_outbound_systems, use a prospect's comment, reaction, or inbound question as the trigger, then route genuine hand-raisers to staff-owned inboxes within platform permissions. An illustrative funnel of 100 triggers, 30 substantive replies, 12 qualified importer calls, and three signed engagements costs $1,500 at $500 per engagement; none of those signatures is collected fee revenue. Do not respond to PeerFloodError or silent Message Request delivery by rotating accounts or increasing unsolicited volume. Fallback to user-initiated conversations and send external links only after a reply. For each resulting lead, screen the claimant's legal identity, operating status, accounts-payable process, and competing recovery engagements before accessing sensitive records. Request W-9 and entity documentation through a controlled channel, not public messages. Keep prospecting claims narrow: identify a reviewable transaction pattern and ask permission to investigate, rather than announcing an entitlement before a broker, counsel, or administrator has assessed the evidence.

## 2. Separate eligibility, collectible principal, and conversion time across three lanes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Drawback starts with eligible paid duties, taxes, and fees, not sales value or gross imported merchandise value. Section 1313 commonly permits recovery of 99% of qualifying amounts, subject to the specific provision, exclusions, quantity limits, and evidence requirements. Build the ledger from ACE entry data and CBP Form 7501 records, then reconcile exports using bills of lading, commercial invoices, inventory movements, and applicable substitution support. ImportGenius or Panjiva can surface trading patterns, but neither replaces claimant records. An eight-digit HTS match is a screening signal, not a universal substitution entitlement. For illustration, $1,000,000 of eligible duties yields $990,000 before $20,000 of offsets and $10,000 of agreed professional and documentation deductions; the net fee base is $960,000. A 30% fee is $288,000, leaving $672,000 after those deductions and the fee. Screen each tariff component for statutory or proclamation-based exclusions rather than assuming every additional duty qualifies. Accelerated payment requires applicable approval and bond arrangements; ordinary drawback does not automatically require that accelerated-payment bond. Budget an illustrative six-to-eighteen-month ordinary collection interval, with longer cases possible, and replace that planning assumption with the broker's case-specific assessment before forecasting liquidity.

The hts_cross_ruling_reclassification_and_psc_recovery play begins with historical entry lines, product composition and function, HTSUS text, explanatory analysis, CROSS rulings, and agreement-specific origin support. Another importer's published ruling is persuasive only where facts and legal reasoning fit; it does not itself bind CBP for this importer. Licensed brokers and trade counsel choose among an eligible unliquidated-entry PSC, a timely § 1514 protest of a protestable decision, and a qualifying § 1520(d) post-importation preference claim, including covered USMCA claims. A PSC is not a protest, and filing one does not create an unlimited extension of other deadlines. Check current ACE business rules and scheduled liquidation rather than relying on a generic calendar. A supported $120,000 historical overpayment, reduced by $10,000 offsets and $10,000 professional and documentation costs, produces a $100,000 collectible fee base and $30,000 fee at 30%. Keep projected future landed-cost reductions outside that calculation. Allow an illustrative three-to-twelve-month or longer conversion interval depending on route and dispute complexity; no separate drawback accelerated-payment bond attaches merely because this is a refund engagement. If technical support fails, withdraw the unsupported position, preserve valid timely claims, and seek additional evidence or a prospective binding ruling.

Antitrust recovery begins with the settlement's class definition, covered products, purchase channels, dates, exclusions, and claims protocol. An AP ledger establishes purchases only when invoices, vendor normalization, credits, and entity history support it; $2,000,000 of purchases does not imply a $2,000,000 award. Use a purely illustrative 2% distribution assumption to test economics: a $40,000 actual distribution less $2,000 agreed documentation costs leaves a $38,000 fee base, an $11,400 fee at 30%, and $26,600 to the claimant. The administrator's eventual allocation can differ materially because accepted claims, deductions, appeals, and distribution orders change the denominator and timing. Planning may require six-to-twenty-four months after submission or longer; verify the case docket and administrator updates rather than representing this interval as a service commitment. There is no CBP surety requirement in this lane, although the settlement may impose its own authorization and verification conditions. Provide every court-required third-party filer disclosure and plainly explain that eligible claimants can file directly at zero cost. Do not impersonate class counsel or the administrator. Screen acquisitions, dissolved entities, prior assignments, and duplicate filings before submission; contested ownership goes to counsel, while uncontested eligible records proceed separately where the protocol permits.

## 3. Choose a payment rail that preserves claimant ownership and invoice enforceability  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate the recovery rail from the service-fee rail. CBP refund procedures and settlement distribution instructions determine how principal reaches the authorized claimant; your engagement determines how an earned invoice is paid afterward. A bank wire cannot expand claimant standing, an escrow account cannot override an administrator's payee rules, and a checkout link cannot establish ownership of a federal claim. Record beneficiary legal name, bank verification method, payer reference, claim identifier, and the reason every deduction is allowed. For a $100,000 fee base received by the claimant, an ordinary arrangement is a $30,000 invoice paid by authorized ACH or wire after treasury matches cleared funds to the claim. If escrow is considered, first verify that the payer permits the proposed payee and that the escrow provider can legally hold these particular funds. Use a written waterfall allocating approved costs, claimant proceeds, earned fees, and disputed amounts. Do not operate an informal pooled client-money account simply because it is convenient. Holding or transmitting other parties' funds can trigger licensing, fiduciary, and safeguarding obligations; the practical default is direct claimant receipt and a separately authorized fee payment through the claimant's existing treasury controls.

The eight mechanisms in the matrix solve different problems; several are commercial structures rather than payment networks. Wire provides traceable high-value settlement but limited practical recovery after misdirection. ACH supports repeatable collections but requires authorization and remains subject to applicable return rules. Escrow segregates disputed allocations only when the provider, instructions, and underlying payer permit it. Retainers finance work but change the zero-upfront offer, so they belong only in a separately accepted scope rather than a hidden condition. Statutory disbursement identifies the government's prescribed payment process, not a shortcut around entitlement. Platform checkout can collect an already-earned invoice, while factoring advances cash against an eligible receivable and reduces proceeds through financing charges. Milestone holds defer release until objective evidence arrives, without turning submission into a realized refund. On a $30,000 earned fee, an illustrative 3% checkout charge consumes $900; a flat $25 transfer charge consumes far less, although availability and risk differ. Compare actual provider quotes and contract terms rather than assuming those illustrative prices. For federal claims, counsel must assess 31 U.S.C. § 3727 before any purported assignment; directing payment is not automatically a valid transfer of the claim.

Payment instructions deserve a separate control path from claim preparation. A compromised mailbox can deliver a plausible beneficiary change immediately before a large refund or fee transfer. Require a callback to a previously verified number, a second approver, and independent confirmation of account ownership for every beneficiary amendment. Do not accept the telephone number printed in the suspicious change request as verification. For ACH fee collection, retain the business authorization and invoice schedule, and reconcile returns rather than treating an initiated debit as cash. For settlement checks, track issuance, custody, deposit, and clearance; an administrator portal status alone is not bank evidence. Escrow release instructions should distinguish an undisputed $24,000 fee from a contested $6,000 increment so a narrow dispute does not freeze the entire transaction. Specify who pays bank fees and whether foreign-currency amounts are converted using the bank's realized rate or another agreed method. Preserve remittance documents alongside the claim ledger and restrict editing permissions. When payer instructions prohibit split payments, accept claimant receipt followed by ordinary invoicing; do not reroute funds through an affiliate or misstate the beneficiary to force an arrangement the payer does not support.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Verify the claimant and the beneficiary
   - Match entity records, engagement authority, payer requirements, and bank ownership.
   - Use a previously verified callback channel and a second approver for new accounts or changed instructions.
2. Reconcile principal before calculating compensation
   - Match the payer remittance to cleared funds, claim identifiers, offsets, and approved deductions.
   - Keep import value, purchase volume, refundable principal, claimant proceeds, and operator fees in separate ledger fields.
3. Issue the evidence-linked earned invoice
   - Apply the signed contingency percentage to the defined net fee base and attach the calculation.
   - Identify the receipt date, cost treatment, adjustment terms, due date, and authorized ACH or wire instructions.
4. Close the collection and retain adjustment evidence
   - Confirm fee clearance, resolve returns, and update reserves for any later recovery adjustment.
   - Archive the complete packet under the applicable customs, settlement, contract, and accounting retention requirements.

## 4. Protect the earned fee without confusing collections with operator revenue  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Fee protection starts before analysis creates an identifiable recovery, not after the claimant receives money. Define covered entities, entries or purchases, recovery lanes, excluded existing claims, contingency percentage, approved deductions, and the event that earns compensation. A zero-upfront 25%–35% offer needs an equally explicit provision for outside professional expenses: identify who authorizes them, who pays them, and whether they reduce the fee base. Use a claim schedule and documented work history to support a reasonable, legally reviewed post-termination fee provision for recoveries attributable to completed work. Avoid blanket ownership of every future refund or settlement the customer might receive. Require prompt notice of relevant receipts and proportionate access to remittance evidence, with confidentiality controls and a defined invoice-dispute period. Do not condition return of customer records or deadline-critical cooperation on payment of a contested fee. Engage licensed customs brokers and trade counsel directly for formal customs work under valid authority. A data-services contract does not authorize unlicensed customs business or prohibited brokerage fee arrangements under 19 CFR Part 111. Keep professional compensation, recovery-operations compensation, and any referral arrangement separately documented and reviewed for the rules governing the actual participants.

Credit screening matters even when the government or settlement fund ultimately supplies the cash, because the customer may spend the recovery before paying your invoice. Check Secretary of State records, authorized signatories, bankruptcy filings through PACER, material litigation, payment references, and the customer's existing AP terms. UCC searches can reveal competing liens but do not establish a complete priority opinion; counsel should assess any proposed security interest under the applicable state's Article 9. A distressed importer with $300,000 expected recovery and $90,000 prospective fees can still be a poor engagement if lender controls, insolvency proceedings, or government offsets absorb the proceeds. Request permission to verify treasury arrangements rather than contacting banks under a misleading pretext. Use shorter evidence-linked invoicing intervals after each partial receipt instead of waiting for the whole portfolio to close. If a customer disputes $5,000 of a $45,000 invoice, preserve an agreed route to collect the undisputed $40,000 without prejudging the disputed portion. When ownership or authority is genuinely contested, pause the affected work and seek a permitted resolution. Do not solve weak credit by secretly diverting claimant principal, manufacturing an assignment, or describing an unearned estimate as an existing receivable.

This is a reconciliation, not an additional forecast of government awards. Under ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis, assess the promised services and contractual control before presenting professional pass-through amounts gross; claimant refund principal never becomes operator revenue merely because a payment account briefly receives it. Do not apply an invented retained-share haircut to the canonical earned-fee figures. Maintain a cash calendar separate from recognized revenue because filing, approval, disbursement, invoice issuance, and fee clearance occur on different dates. The one-to-three-month first-deal target measures signed business, not refund collection; working capital must cover the longer conversion cycle and any subsequent adjustment obligations.
