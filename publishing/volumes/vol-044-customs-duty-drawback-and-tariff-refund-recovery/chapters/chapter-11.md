## 1. Assign every recovery play to a named seat and an independent release  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the four-person pod around one full-time owner who owns engagements, capacity, collections, and the weekly exception meeting. Contractor one is a licensed customs broker responsible for customs eligibility, authorized submissions, and regulatory communications. Contractor two is an evidence engineer who normalizes ACE entry exports, CBP Form 7501 records, export bills of lading, and accounts payable ledgers. Contractor three is a recovery analyst who assembles settlement packets, checks supporting schedules, and maintains deadline calendars. These are three contractor seats, not three unlimited service departments. Retain trade counsel for exceptional disputes outside the routine production pod; record that engagement as an incremental cost and disclose any additional staffing it creates. Only the broker or appropriately authorized counsel performs formal customs filings. Unlicensed personnel can prepare factual records but cannot independently perform reserved customs business under 19 CFR Part 111. The owner checks commercial release conditions without overruling professional eligibility decisions. Establish separate permissions for source uploads, working calculations, signed authorities, and submission receipts. ImportGenius and Panjiva identify prospects and shipment patterns; neither replaces importer-controlled entry records. Every case receives one accountable case owner, one qualified reviewer, and one escalation route before evidence production begins. This prevents attractive prospects from becoming owner-only obligations.

Make the 12-play mapping explicit in the operating register rather than treating twelve plays as twelve independent businesses. This chapter uses four operational subplays per lane. Drawback covers D1 direct-identification unused merchandise, D2 substitution unused merchandise, D3 direct-identification manufacturing, and D4 substitution manufacturing. Each requires its own statutory and regulatory analysis; an eight-digit HTS match alone does not establish substitution eligibility. Tariff recovery covers T1 eligible pre-liquidation classification corrections, T2 eligible pre-liquidation origin or preference corrections, T3 timely classification protests, and T4 timely origin, preference, or rate protests. Settlement recovery covers A1 interchange merchant evidence, A2 direct-purchaser purchase evidence, A3 indirect-purchaser evidence where the settlement permits it, and A4 affiliate or successor claim reconciliation. These are workflow mappings, not representations that every transaction qualifies or that every settlement remains open. The broker releases D and T cases; the recovery analyst assembles A cases under the specific court-approved plan and administrator instructions. The evidence engineer supports all twelve through reusable connectors, but each lane keeps distinct eligibility fields and acceptance checklists. The owner approves workload and engagement economics only after those requirements are understood. Do not sell an A4 reconciliation service as permission to submit duplicate claims for the same purchases.

Move to two full-time employees and four contractors by adding an employee delivery lead and a contractor quality specialist. The delivery lead absorbs scheduling, evidence acceptance, and routine client follow-up; the fourth contractor performs sampled reperformance and absence coverage. Qualified customs judgments still require a qualified customs professional. Scale on measured hours, not signed contingency face value. For eight consecutive weeks, track required production hours, available contracted hours, rework, deadline exposure, and cash available for committed labor. An illustrative hiring trigger is sustained utilization above 80% with a funded twelve-week staffing reserve and no overdue statutory tasks. Confirm that the added seats fit the canonical Scaled cost envelope rather than assuming higher revenue absorbs every new expense. Pay the evidence engineer per accepted source batch, the recovery analyst per accepted packet, and the broker through an agreed availability retainer plus case-specific fees. Define acceptance before work starts, including source coverage, reconciliation tolerances, and required reviewer credentials. Avoid paying solely for asserted refunds, which rewards inflated eligibility estimates. Apply employment classification rules to actual working arrangements, including 26 U.S.C. section 3121(d); a contractor label does not settle status. Maintain backup access and documentation without turning independent specialists into unbudgeted full-time employees.

## 2. Separate statutory eligibility, bond exposure, and cash clocks by lane  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Drawback begins with eligible duties, taxes, and fees actually paid on qualifying imports, not merchandise value or a prospecting database estimate. Under 19 U.S.C. section 1313, many qualifying claims recover 99% of eligible amounts, subject to the applicable subsection and limitations. The broker must verify import and export timing, claimant rights, merchandise treatment, substitution conditions, and prohibited charges. Section 1313(r) generally requires a complete claim within five years after importation; unused-merchandise pathways also impose their own export or destruction timing conditions. Exclude antidumping and countervailing duties under 19 CFR section 190.3, and check the controlling authority for additional tariffs rather than assuming all special duties qualify. Documentation cost includes entry reconstruction, export tracing, inventory reconciliation, manufacturing support where applicable, broker work, and record retention under Part 190. Ordinary processing and accelerated payment are different cash paths. Accelerated payment under section 190.92 requires approval and adequate bond arrangements; a surety quote is not available capacity. Model six to twenty-four months from accepted engagement to receipt as an illustrative planning range, then replace it with actual broker and cohort evidence. Retain repayment exposure in the case register after accelerated cash arrives; payment alone does not remove later examination or liquidation risk.

Tariff refunds require a separate clock and authority map. A Post-Summary Correction is a pre-liquidation ACE correction mechanism, not a protest under 19 U.S.C. section 1514. For eligible entries, the standard PSC operating window is generally within 300 days after entry and at least fifteen days before scheduled liquidation, subject to current CBP rules and entry eligibility. A protest ordinarily must be filed within 180 days after the relevant liquidation or other protestable decision. Record the actual triggering event and qualified review; never substitute an estimated calendar date. The collectible base is the supported overpayment attributable to a defensible correction or protest, excluding unrelated duties and merchandise principal. Documentation cost includes classification analysis, product specifications, origin support, entry reconstruction, and broker or counsel preparation. A refund request generally does not require a separate drawback accelerated-payment bond; existing import bonds and case-specific obligations still need review. Use three to eighteen months after submission as an illustrative ordinary refund planning range, with disputes potentially extending materially longer. Do not describe a denied protest as routine receivables collection. Judicial escalation creates separate standing, payment, timing, and litigation requirements. Price that work separately and prevent routine contractor budgets from silently absorbing contested proceedings.

Settled B2B antitrust recovery follows the court-approved settlement definition, allocation plan, claim form, and deadlines, not customs eligibility. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(e) governs approval of class settlements; the particular order determines qualifying purchasers, products, periods, exclusions, and filing procedures. The collectible base is the claimant's actual allowed distribution, not total purchases, alleged damages, or the settlement fund. Documentation cost includes purchase-ledger extraction, merchant identifiers, affiliate reconciliation, duplicate checks, and administrator responses. No CBP surety bond applies to this lane, although engagement terms or a particular order can impose other requirements. Use six to thirty-six months or longer as an illustrative distribution horizon where allocation, objections, or appeals remain unresolved. Provide required third-party filer disclosures, accurately identify the claimant and authorized representative, and explain that the claimant may file directly at zero cost. Across all lanes, quote the agreed 25%–35% contingency against defined realized recovery. At 30%, a $100,000 eligible distribution produces a $30,000 operator fee and $70,000 for the client before any separately agreed charges. Keep principal outside operator revenue. Prefer direct client receipt and separately invoiced fees; where funds pass through another authorized party, reconcile custody, remittance, and fee deductions explicitly.

>

> - Verify claimant identity, signed engagement, valid Customs Power of Attorney where required, and the licensed filer assigned to each customs submission.

> - Confirm bond capacity where accelerated drawback payment is requested, document custody arrangements, and obtain a qualified reviewer release with a saved submission receipt.

## 3. Enforce weekly scorecards and reconcile earnings to realized fee cohorts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run one forty-five-minute weekly meeting from a frozen scorecard exported before discussion. Start with statutory deadlines and evidence defects, then capacity, collections, and concentration. Use stable denominators: first-pass acceptance equals accepted packets without material rework divided by reviewed packets; source coverage equals supported claim lines divided by proposed claim lines. Set an initial 95% first-pass target, require complete source support for released amounts, and require zero unassigned deadlines. These are internal operating targets, not published industry benchmarks. Report both counts and dollars so one large defective claim cannot disappear inside a high packet acceptance percentage. Contractor service terms should require acknowledgment within one business day, triage of a complete intake within two, and ordinary defect correction within three. Shorter statutory windows take priority through an explicit escalation. Pause the service clock only for documented missing inputs, not because a contractor is busy. Sample at least ten percent of routine packets and independently inspect every high-value, novel, or exception case before release. Internal sampling does not replace the broker's professional review. Log root causes against the responsible transformation, checklist, or source system; a corrected spreadsheet without a corrected process simply moves the same defect into next week's batch.

Maintain three linked ledgers: claimant recovery principal, operator fee accounting, and contingent pipeline. The principal ledger follows money owed to clients and never feeds operator sales. The fee ledger records the contractual fee base, rate, performance status, invoice, receipt, and any reversal exposure. The pipeline ledger holds estimates that have not earned recognition. Under ASC 606, evaluate performance obligations and constrain variable consideration until a significant reversal is sufficiently unlikely; signed engagements and submitted claims do not automatically create earned revenue. Apply a documented policy with the accountant, and distinguish recognized revenue, receivables, and bank collections. For this volume, the canonical revenue basis is earned contingency fees on realized recoveries, not gross recovery billings. If another party retains part of the fee, assess principal-versus-agent presentation rather than inventing a retained-share percentage. Accrue contractor costs when services are received even if the claim remains unpaid. Group cases by lane, engagement quarter, submission quarter, and receipt quarter. Reconcile opening receivables plus earned fees less receipts and credits to closing receivables. Report median and seventy-fifth-percentile cash conversion alongside unresolved cohorts; excluding unfinished cases makes slow recovery look artificially efficient. Keep accelerated drawback repayment exposure visible rather than treating every receipt as permanently settled.

Reconcile the operating model to the canonical scorecard without changing its economics. Conservative fee revenue of $110,000 less $74,800 of implied total costs produces $35,200 owner profit at 32%. Base revenue of $515,000 less $283,250 produces $231,750 at 45.0%. Scaled revenue of $920,000 less $386,400 produces $533,600 at 58%. These implied annual costs include the complete modeled cost envelope; the stated $1,500–$9,500 monthly operating range is not a substitute for contractor delivery costs and every other modeled expense. At an illustrative uniform 30% fee, the three revenue levels correspond to approximately $366,666.67, $1,716,666.67, and $3,066,666.67 of realized client recoveries, respectively. Those are supporting unit checks, not new scenarios. Cap each customer group and each commercial counterparty at 25% of trailing-twelve-month earned fees and separately of the probability-weighted twelve-month fee pipeline. Combine related entities and referral-controlled accounts. Treat each settlement pool or administrator dependency as a separate risk concentration, even where clients differ. CBP is an unavoidable sovereign dependency for both customs lanes; disclose that structural concentration rather than claiming importer diversification eliminates it. A breached commercial cap stops incremental exposure to that group while existing obligations continue to be serviced.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze evidence and denominators
   - Export the case register, deadline calendar, contractor log, and fee subledger before the weekly meeting.
   - Save a dated read-only snapshot; retain unresolved cases in cash-conversion reporting and identify missing source records explicitly.
2. Release only qualified work
   - Review deadline exposure, unsupported amounts, professional approvals, and service exceptions before discussing new sales.
   - Assign one named owner and a dated correction task to each defect; retain both original and corrected packet versions.
3. Bridge fees, costs, and concentration
   - Reconcile recognized fees and collections, accrue received contractor services, and recompute both 25% concentration tests.
   - Keep recovery principal outside revenue; show settlement-pool dependencies and structural CBP exposure as separate risk measures.
4. Publish commitments and verify closure
   - Issue a one-page action register with owners, due dates, capacity changes, and any restricted new exposure.
   - At the next meeting, verify closure using receipts, corrected evidence, accounting entries, or signed documents rather than verbal assurances.

## 4. Build transferable rights and price earnings rather than pending claims  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the exit file while operating, not after a buyer requests diligence. Each contractor agreement should identify deliverables, confidentiality duties, security obligations, permitted subcontracting, return requirements, and ownership of scripts, schemas, checklists, and original documentation. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a signed writing from the owner or authorized agent. Do not assume paying an invoice transfers copyright, or that every contractor deliverable qualifies as work made for hire. Use an express present assignment for covered deliverables and identify pre-existing components with an adequate transferable license. Distinguish company-owned automation from client records, commercial database licenses, and broker-controlled access. A signed IP assignment does not transfer an ImportGenius subscription, a Panjiva data license, a customs broker license, or a client's authorization. Maintain an asset schedule showing ownership, license restrictions, assignment terms, and renewal dates. Review engagement change-of-control provisions and obtain required consents through the proper process. Confirm whether Customs Powers of Attorney and administrator representative authorizations remain effective after the proposed transaction; re-execute them where necessary. Give prospective buyers redacted cohort evidence first, then controlled access to permitted records. A clean transaction cannot depend on casually publishing confidential importer or claimant data.

Treat valuation multiples as negotiation models, not observed comparables supplied by this scorecard. An owner-operated book might be tested at 2.0–3.0 times verified seller discretionary earnings, while a manager-run, diversified operation might be tested at 3.0–5.0 times normalized EBITDA. Actual bids can fall outside either range, especially when collections are episodic or rights cannot transfer. Canonical owner profit is neither automatically seller discretionary earnings nor EBITDA. Require a bridge identifying owner compensation, replacement labor, interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, unusual expenses, and recurring rework. For a clearly labeled illustration, assume Scaled owner profit excludes replacement management and requires no other EBITDA adjustments. Deducting $125,000 of annual replacement cost from $533,600 leaves $408,600 of normalized earnings. Applying 3.0–5.0 times produces illustrative enterprise value of $1,225,800–$2,043,000. If replacement labor is already included, do not deduct it again. These figures do not alter the canonical operating scenarios. Enterprise value is not seller cash proceeds: debt, cash treatment, working-capital adjustments, transaction costs, escrows, and earn-outs change the final amount. A buyer paying for management independence will test whether the delivery lead actually runs the weekly system and whether client relationships survive the owner's absence.

Support a premium with evidence that earnings recur and delivery survives transfer. Present twenty-four to thirty-six months of lane-level cohorts where available, separating new recoveries, repeat importer work, settlement windfalls, reversals, and unpaid invoices. Explain shorter operating history directly rather than filling gaps with forecasted receipts. Pending claims belong in a probability-weighted schedule with explicit eligibility, timing, fee, cost-to-complete, and assignment assumptions. Do not capitalize a settlement distribution into recurring earnings and then charge the buyer again for the same pipeline. An earn-out can allocate uncertain collections, but define the covered cohorts, collection costs, settlement authority, reporting access, and dispute mechanism. Keep client principal outside the purchase-price calculation and working-capital target. Buyer diligence should reperform sample claims, inspect broker continuity, confirm signed IP transfers, and test customer and counterparty caps. For the durable cash-cow alternative, apply the same discipline: preserve a funded labor reserve, maintain backup specialists, limit owner-only knowledge, and distribute only cash remaining after obligations and recovery exposure are covered. The Conservative case's $35,200 owner profit leaves little room for absentee management; the Base case's $231,750 supports more flexibility but still requires a replacement-cost bridge. Scaled profit of $533,600 earns a higher-quality narrative only when six-person delivery demonstrably works without daily founder rescue.
