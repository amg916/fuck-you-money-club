## Executive Summary

A payable heir claim needs more than a plausible family tree. Build a cited proof argument, resolve conflicting identities, and calculate shares under the distribution rule actually governing the estate. This chapter separates genealogical evidence from legal entitlement, works a $180,000 residue through competing representation systems, and tests adoption, nonmarital parentage, and half-blood relationships. It also examines fractional heir-share acquisitions without confusing an estate expectancy with recorded real-property ownership. Use the exhibit, share schedule, and consensual buyout comparison to expose missing links before anyone prices or transfers an interest.

## Build the five-element proof exhibit  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an identity question, not a desired beneficiary: was the claimant's mother the same person identified as the decedent's daughter in the probate petition? The Genealogical Proof Standard requires reasonably exhaustive research, complete and accurate source citations, analysis and correlation, resolution of conflicting evidence, and a sound written conclusion. Reasonably exhaustive does not mean purchasing every database subscription. It means identifying the records capable of proving or disproving each relationship and explaining unavailable material. Build a research plan around birth registrations, marriage applications, divorce judgments, death certificates, probate files, census households, and adoption proceedings where accessible. Log unsuccessful searches with jurisdiction, date range, spelling variants, and collection coverage so another researcher can distinguish negative evidence from an incomplete search.

Assign each person a stable identifier and each source a separate identifier. Attach assertions to evidence rather than attaching a whole document to an unqualified family tree. A death certificate may accurately establish death while supplying secondhand parent names from an informant who never met the parents. Record the informant, creation date, record type, image location, and whether the entry is original, derivative, or transcribed. Correlate addresses, occupations, witnesses, siblings, and dates across records. Two people named John Martin in one county are not one person merely because a search engine merged them. Preserve both hypotheses until distinguishing evidence appears. A citation should let a reviewer retrieve the same page, including repository, collection, volume, page, certificate number, and access date.

Write a conflict register before writing the final narrative. If a census implies a 1908 birth and a contemporaneous registration gives 1906, identify the source of each date, assess informant knowledge, and explain why one carries greater weight. Do not silently choose the date that fits the proposed relationship. The written proof argument should state the question, summarize affirmative and negative evidence, resolve material conflicts, and disclose remaining uncertainty. Separate a research conclusion from a court's determination of heirship. Package the narrative with an indexed pedigree, numbered exhibits, missing-record log, and proposed share schedule. A fresh reviewer should be able to reproduce every parent-child edge without calling the original investigator, and should know exactly which unresolved edge prevents a claim from being priced.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Define
   - Name the disputed relationship.
   - Assign person IDs and alternative identity hypotheses.
2. Collect
   - Capture records with complete citations.
   - Keep originals, source IDs, access limits, and negative searches.
3. Correlate
   - Resolve contradictions explicitly.
   - Compare informants, chronology, and independent evidence.
4. Conclude
   - Deliver a reproducible proof argument.
   - Link every relationship and proposed share to its supporting exhibit.

## Calculate representation without switching algorithms  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

First determine what property enters the intestate calculation. California Probate Code section 6402 sets an order for property not passing to a surviving spouse under section 6401, or the entire intestate estate if there is no surviving spouse. Do not apply a descendant fraction to gross asset value while ignoring debts, administration expenses, spouse rights, or nonprobate transfers. In this worked example, the confirmed net residue is $180,000 and the decedent had children A, B, and C. A and B survive; C predeceased leaving surviving children C1 and C2. Assume every relationship and survival requirement is established and no other claimant changes the denominator. Three child branches receive one third each, with C's third divided equally between C1 and C2.

Strict per stirpes fixes the initial division at the child generation, preserving each represented child's branch. Modern per capita with representation generally starts at the nearest descendant generation containing a survivor, then distributes deceased members' shares through their branches. California section 240 starts at the nearest generation with living issue, counts living members and deceased members leaving living issue, and recursively allocates each deceased member's share. Per capita at each generation instead combines the shares of deceased members at the chosen generation and divides that pool equally among eligible descendants at the next generation, repeating as needed. Terminology varies across jurisdictions and instruments. Record the operative statutory or testamentary language, not merely a software menu label. The simple A, B, C example produces identical amounts under all three approaches.

Use a second pedigree to expose the difference. All three children are dead. A left living A1 and deceased A2, whose two children survive. B left living B1. C left deceased C1, whose one child survives. Strict per stirpes gives A1 $30,000, A2's children $15,000 each, B1 $60,000, and C1's child $60,000. Modern representation starts with four grandchild slots: A1, A2, B1, C1. A1 and B1 each take $45,000; A2's children split $45,000 and C1's child takes $45,000. Per capita at each generation gives A1 and B1 $45,000 each, then pools the remaining $90,000 for three great-grandchildren at $30,000 each. Check every schedule totals $180,000, retaining exact fractions until the final cent allocation and documenting any rounding adjustment.

**Primary example: A and B survive; C leaves C1 and C2**

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Verify legally relevant parentage and collateral relationships  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Nonmarital birth is not a reason to erase an otherwise qualifying child from a pedigree. It is a reason to identify the governing parentage rules and the evidence establishing the legal relationship. In California, read sections 6450 through 6453 alongside the applicable family-law provisions and the facts of the case. Obtain birth records, acknowledgments, parentage judgments, and other permitted evidence rather than treating a surname match as proof. A genetic match can support an investigative hypothesis while leaving legal parentage, admissibility, or inheritance conditions unresolved. Record which proposition each document establishes. Distinguish biological relationships from legally recognized parent-child relationships and from household membership. A person described as a son in an obituary may be a stepchild whose inheritance position needs a separate statutory analysis.

Adoption requires dates, orders, and jurisdiction-specific treatment of relationships to both adoptive and natural families. California section 6451 addresses circumstances in which adoption affects succession through a natural parent, including exceptions; a blanket rule that adoption always removes every biological inheritance link will produce errors. Restricted adoption files require an authorized access route, not impersonation or inference from a sealed index entry. Request accessible orders or other appropriate evidence through the representative or counsel. Keep legal names and former names connected with source references, while limiting unnecessary disclosure of sensitive records. Build a timeline showing birth, adoption, marriage, death, and any later adjudication. A timeline often reveals that a rule applied to the wrong event date is responsible for an apparently insoluble contradiction.

Half-blood relationships matter particularly when the search moves from descendants to parents, siblings, and their issue. California section 6406 generally treats relatives of the half blood like relatives of the whole blood in intestate succession, but do not export that rule into a different state's estate. Identify the shared parent and document each sibling's separate parentage rather than assuming a census household proves full siblings. Include predeceased siblings' descendants where the governing priority and representation provisions require them. Also check survivorship, disclaimers, and disqualification questions before freezing the share schedule. Present uncertain eligibility as an unresolved issue, not as a zero share. The reviewer should see the evidence needed, the affected branch, and how alternate outcomes change the denominator and proposed recovery economics.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Do not price an unresolved branch as zero**
> 
> An incomplete pedigree changes every remaining share.
> - Identify missing legal-parentage evidence.
> - Model both plausible denominators.
> - Separate research findings from adjudicated entitlement.

## Fractional Heir-Share Acquisition & Tenancy-in-Common Buyouts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Track 01 Operator Play fractional_heir_share_partition_forcing concerns fractional interests, but the practical transaction must begin by identifying what the seller actually owns. An expected estate distribution, an assignable beneficial interest, and a recorded tenancy-in-common interest are different assets. Trace the probate order, deed, legal description, encumbrances, and ownership fractions before drafting a purchase offer. A genealogical conclusion does not itself convey land. Price the interest against realistic holding costs, title work, taxes, occupancy, and possible sale expenses. If a house is worth $240,000 with $40,000 of debt and estimated $20,000 transaction costs, a one-quarter economic slice of the remaining $180,000 is $45,000 before interest-specific discounts or obligations. That calculation is an underwriting illustration, not proof of a realizable cash exit.

The Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act changes the expected partition route where enacted and applicable. Verify the state's definition of heirs property, effective provisions, ownership facts, and procedural deadlines. California's partition framework includes appraisal or other authorized valuation procedures, notice, opportunities for eligible cotenants to buy interests of cotenants requesting sale, and consideration of partition in kind before a court-ordered sale under applicable standards. Do not model a forced auction as an automatic outcome. Appraisal objections, buyout elections, funding periods, and the interests eligible for purchase must come from the governing statute and court orders. In California, also check the expanded application under Code of Civil Procedure section 874.313 rather than assuming the original model act's scope controls every tenancy-in-common proceeding.

The forensic question is whether the return comes from a sound consensual purchase or from an imagined ability to pressure relatives into a distressed sale. Document independent valuation, each owner's informed negotiated price, title defects, and payment mechanics. Prefer a consensual co-owner buyout with a title-reviewed deed and escrow instructions identifying releases, taxes, and recording sequence. If owners disagree, model the expense and uncertainty of the available partition process without promising removal of occupants or bypass of statutory buyout rights. Keep legal representation separate from genealogical research services. Stop a closing when the seller cannot establish the transferable interest or a necessary lien release is missing. A smaller negotiated return with a clean conveyance is materially different from a paper spread dependent on an invalid title assumption.

**Predatory-Partition Trap vs Consensual Co-Owner Buyout Fallback**

| Forensic test | Predatory-partition trap | Consensual fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset | Treat expected inheritance as recorded title | Confirm distribution and conveyance authority |
| Value | Assume a discounted forced auction | Obtain independent valuation and negotiate |
| Procedure | Ignore appraisal and cotenant election rights | Calendar statutory notices and buyout periods |
| Closing | Pay despite unresolved liens or missing signatures | Use title-reviewed escrow and specific releases |
