# Chapter 09: Ethical Outreach, Contracting & Jurisdictional Restrictions

*Separate probate assignments, unclaimed-property recovery, licensing, and legal representation.*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Heir outreach works when the recipient can independently verify the estate, the sender, and the proposed service. First classify the asset: a probate distribution assignment is not the same transaction as a state unclaimed-property recovery agreement. Then separate investigative licensing, contract enforceability, and legal representation. This chapter compares four jurisdictions, builds a low-pressure contact sequence, and dissects outbound shortcuts that create avoidable exposure. The operating objective is a documented introduction and a narrow, understandable agreement, not a signature obtained through confusion about government authority, deadlines, or the heir's ability to proceed independently.

## Classify the transaction before comparing percentages  `🏷️ STATUTORY_FACT`

Start every intake with four distinct fields: asset custodian, estate venue, recipient location, and proposed service. A county probate estate holding sale proceeds is not automatically a state unclaimed-property account merely because an heir is missing. Obtain the petition, docket number, personal representative's identity, and latest accounting before describing any available inheritance. If a state administrator holds the asset instead, record the property identifier and claim procedure. Keep the two records separate when an estate also owns abandoned funds. A contract drafted for locating a state-held account can fail when reused as an assignment of an expected probate distribution, even when both engagements involve the same family and the same researcher.

California Probate Code Section 11604.5 provides a specific court-review framework for transfers or agreements involving an heir hunter and a beneficiary's distribution. Treat court scrutiny, disclosure, and possible reduction of compensation as central economic variables, rather than assuming a signed assignment controls payment. Florida Section 717.135 addresses agreements to recover property under its unclaimed-property chapter, including prescribed disclosures and compensation restrictions; registration must also be checked under Section 717.1400. Its ordinary fee-and-cost limitation is 20 percent, with statutory alternative disclosure provisions requiring careful review. Do not transplant that percentage into a Florida probate retainer. Record the exact statutory route and form version selected by counsel before presenting a price to an owner.

Texas Property Code Section 74.507 limits covered recovery compensation to 10 percent of the recoverable property's value and imposes enforceability conditions, including a restriction tied to the period after delivery to the comptroller. A compliant percentage alone therefore does not establish a collectible contract. New York SCPA Section 2225 concerns proceedings involving unknown distributees and relevant proof; use it to frame the kinship investigation, not as a universal percentage schedule. Court review of compensation and assignments requires examination of additional authority, including SCPA Section 2112 where applicable. Across all four states, ask counsel to identify the controlling asset regime, timing restrictions, required signatures, review procedure, and fee base in a written file note.

**Cross-state classification: percentages are not interchangeable**

| Jurisdiction | Authority | Operational consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| California | Prob. Code 11604.5 | Probate heir-hunter agreement subject to court scrutiny. |
| Florida | 717.135; 717.1400 | Check recovery agreement route, fee restrictions, and registration separately. |
| Texas | Prop. Code 74.507 | 10% ceiling plus timing and enforceability conditions. |
| New York | SCPA 2225; 2112 | Distributee proof and applicable compensation review are distinct inquiries. |

## Separate investigation, contracting, and legal representation  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A genealogy label does not decide whether paid investigative work requires a license. California Business and Professions Code Section 7521 defines private-investigator activities broadly enough that locating people or investigating identity for compensation deserves a specific licensing analysis. Florida Chapter 493 likewise regulates private investigative services, subject to definitions and exemptions that must actually fit the engagement. Map the acts performed, where personnel work, where subjects are contacted, and which entity invoices the client. Do not infer reciprocity from holding one state's credential. If an exemption is asserted, retain its text and the factual explanation; a trade association membership or a database subscription is not a substitute for a statutory license or exemption.

The research team can assemble birth certificates, marriage records, death records, census extracts, and a source-linked family tree. It should not turn an unresolved relationship into a legal entitlement opinion. Counsel decides how adoption, nonmarital parentage, disclaimers, survivorship, and competing claims affect distribution in the actual venue. Researchers must not hold themselves out as the recipient's lawyer, choose pleadings for an unrepresented heir, or negotiate contested legal rights as though a recovery contract confers a law license. Establish a handoff record identifying the question, supporting documents, missing evidence, and intended attorney recipient. Keep client representation, estate representation, and the research firm's own contract advice distinct, because those interests can diverge sharply during a dispute.

Draft the commercial scope in plain language: what records will be obtained, what deliverables the heir receives, when payment becomes due, and which expenses are included. Distinguish a service contract from a transfer of distribution rights; do not hide an assignment inside a routine authorization. Specify the fee base using a worked dollar example and identify who bears unsuccessful research costs. Avoid blanket powers of attorney and promises that a clerk will release money immediately. Give the recipient a copy before signature, time to consult independent counsel, and a working cancellation or termination contact. Preserve versions and delivery dates so later review can reconstruct what was offered rather than relying on a salesperson's recollection.

> 
> An asset match does not establish investigative authority, an enforceable contract, or authority to practice law.
> - Classify investigative acts and license requirements.
> - Have venue counsel review the selected agreement.
> - Keep legal representation with an appropriately authorized attorney.

## Make first contact independently verifiable  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a short introductory letter that identifies the private firm, the research purpose, and an independent verification path. A usable script reads: We are a private genealogical research firm reviewing public records associated with an estate in this county. Those records suggest you may be a relative; we have not established your entitlement. You can verify the case through the court's independently published contact details. You do not need to send identification or sign an agreement to ask who we are. If you prefer no further contact, tell us through the enclosed contact options. Insert only verified facts, and avoid placing sensitive family relationships or an estimated payout on the outside of the envelope.

For a returned call, first confirm that the person intended to reach your firm without asking them to recite a Social Security number or bank credentials. Explain how the address was sourced and offer to send the public case reference in writing. State that the recipient can contact the personal representative or consult an attorney independently; do not imply your firm exclusively controls access to a public process. If identity documentation becomes necessary, explain the purpose, recipient, secure transfer method, and retention period before collecting it. A callback number printed in your letter verifies only that someone controls that number; it does not independently establish the estate, the research firm's identity, or the recipient's inheritance.

Use certified delivery when a documented delivery attempt is useful, not as a theatrical demand for action. A signature shows delivery activity, not understanding, consent, heirship, or acceptance of a contract. Maintain a contact ledger with source, date, channel, recipient response, and suppression status. A practical internal cadence is one introduction and one measured follow-up unless the recipient requests continued discussion; this is an operating limit, not a statutory safe harbor. Stop after refusal, investigate returned mail, and do not escalate to relatives or employers to pressure a response. Attorney corroboration should mean an accurately described attorney has reviewed the relevant facts or agreed to confirm a limited point, not an unauthorized endorsement pasted onto stationery.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Verify
   - Match the recipient and case.
   - Require independent records; distinguish a lead from proven kinship.
2. Prepare
   - Build a private-firm packet.
   - Include case reference, independent contact route, scope, and transparent pricing.
3. Release
   - Have a person review delivery.
   - Check suppression, channel rules, confidentiality, and factual wording.
4. Record
   - Log the response and honor refusal.
   - Keep delivery evidence separate from consent and contract execution.

## Track 02 Grey-Zone Outbound Forensic Teardown  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat aggressive outbound proposals as failure analyses, not growth recipes. A vendor offering thousands of cold texts cannot establish lawful outreach merely by saying that all numbers came from public records. The TCPA, 47 U.S.C. Section 227, can expose covered violations to actual loss or statutory damages of $500, with potential enhancement up to three times for knowing or willful violations. Applicability depends on the communication, technology, consent, and other facts; not every unsolicited message produces the same claim. A planning example of twenty separately actionable violations produces $10,000 at the statutory amount before possible enhancement, defense costs, or other claims. That exposure can exceed the entire expected fee from a modest estate lead.

Pretexting creates a different evidentiary failure: the firm obtains attention by misrepresenting its identity or purpose, then must defend a contract built on that interaction. Never impersonate court staff, an estate administrator, a bank, or a family member to obtain records or a signature. Government-lookalike mail is also a poor substitute for a verified source. Section 3001 of Title 39 addresses nonmailable matter and includes conditions concerning solicitations that imply a federal connection; related fraud and deceptive-practice rules may apply beyond that specific provision. Do not reduce the rule to a claim that every official-looking envelope violates one subsection. Analyze the actual representation while choosing plainly private stationery that does not borrow official authority.

The fallback is slower per contact but cleaner per completed matter: a narrowly addressed packet, a truthful explanation, an independently checkable case reference, and counsel corroboration limited to what counsel actually reviewed. Compare channels on collected contribution rather than reply rate. Track verified recipients, requested callbacks, signed agreements, approved compensation, paid fees, and complaints separately. A reply gained through confusion is not a qualified client. Before purchasing an outbound service, require sample messages and provenance records rather than accepting a conversion screenshot. Reject vendors that cannot explain recipient selection or honor suppression. Preserve the final approved wording with the case file so the outreach record and the eventual contract tell the same factual story.

**Naive outbound traps versus verified fallbacks**

| Trap | Failure mechanism | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naive cold-SMS blast | TCPA exposure; public number is not consent | Channel review and individually verified written introduction |
| Pretexting | False identity contaminates consent and evidence | Disclosed private-firm role and independent verification |
| Government-lookalike mailer | Misleading authority; evaluate 39 U.S.C. 3001 | Clearly private certified packet where appropriate |
| Invented attorney endorsement | Unverified legal authority | Attorney-corroborated statement with defined scope |
