# Chapter 04: Realistic Startup Requirements

*A bounded research pilot, professional review and enough cash to wait.*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

A $5,000 launch budget can test one research lane, but it cannot finance an open-ended portfolio of delayed probate recoveries. Reserve $1,500 for entity setup, contract review and insurance, $600 for a ninety-day research stack, $900 for certified records and postage, and $2,000 as business cash reserve. Start with defined jurisdictions, reviewed service boundaries and an encrypted case register. Expand only after the pilot supplies evidence about cost, entitlement quality and collection timing. Multi-case operations require a separate working-capital forecast, often modeled at $10,000 to $50,000, with explicit stop rules before spending outruns cash.

## Buy a bounded experiment, not a miniature national firm  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Define the pilot as ninety days in one recovery lane and a narrow jurisdiction set, with an initial research cohort of twenty leads rather than unlimited intake. The objective is to measure whether reliable entitlement evidence can be assembled within a predetermined cost, not to promise collections during the pilot. Use the $5,000 stack as four separate envelopes: $1,500 for entity formation, contract legal review and errors-and-omissions insurance; $600 for the ninety-day research stack; $900 for certified vital records and postage; and $2,000 of business cash reserve. The arithmetic leaves only $3,000 available for planned deployment. Household living costs, major equipment purchases and contested litigation are outside this example and need their own funding before the founder relies on the business for income.

Get written quotes before treating the $1,500 professional-services envelope as adequate. Entity filing charges, insurance premiums, deductibles and the scope of legal review vary; the allowance does not establish that coverage is available or suitable. Ask whether the proposed policy covers genealogy research, privacy incidents and the actual recovery services, and record exclusions rather than assuming a general policy covers everything. If the necessary review and coverage exceed the envelope, narrow the pilot or increase its funding before commitments. Do not quietly raid the $2,000 reserve to preserve an attractive launch headline. Keep counsel's contract review separate from any later representation of an estate or claimant, which may require a different engagement, client and fee arrangement.

The $600 research envelope equals $200 per month over ninety days, so subscribe only to sources that answer a defined question in the chosen lane. A county probate index, an official property listing and a genealogy database may be useful; five overlapping subscriptions with no measurable retrieval advantage are not. Track subscription renewal dates and export permitted working notes before cancellation. The $900 records envelope averages $45 across twenty leads, but screening should eliminate weak matches before certified orders, leaving more money for the few files that warrant proof. Quote record fees and delivery times individually because death certificates, marriage records and foreign documents differ substantially. Spend on a named evidence gap, not on collecting every available document about a surname.

## Confirm the service boundary before client acquisition  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Write a one-page service description covering jurisdictions, sources, outreach, deliverables and the party responsible for legal filings. California Business and Professions Code section 6125 generally restricts practicing law in California to active licensees of the State Bar. A researcher can gather and organize historical evidence without assuming authority to advise an heir about contested legal rights or represent them in court. Have counsel identify where the proposed workflow crosses from factual genealogy into legal advice, document preparation or representation. Do not present a researcher as an estate attorney, personal representative or government official. When a matter requires legal work, route it to appropriately qualified counsel under a clear engagement rather than describing the same work as administrative assistance.

Perform a private-investigator licensing pre-check in every jurisdiction where the actual services may trigger regulation. Business formation alone is not a professional license, and public availability of records does not establish a universal exemption. Describe the factual tasks to the relevant licensing authority or local counsel: locating living people, investigating identity, establishing relationships and performing compensated recovery assistance. Record the response, date, scope and any exemptions relied upon. Where licensing or supervision is required, budget for it or exclude the activity from the pilot. Do not treat a subcontractor's credential as automatically covering the business. Confirm the contracted party's permitted role, current status and insurance before sharing a live case or allowing outreach under the firm's name.

For California unclaimed-property work, review Code of Civil Procedure section 1582 before offering an agreement. Its restrictions concern covered property recovery agreements, including timing, compensation limits and required information; they are not a general permission slip for every inheritance engagement. Assemble the property report and transfer chronology needed to assess the restricted period, and have the reviewer specify which event makes contracting permissible in the actual case. A client signature cannot repair a prohibited agreement. For a probate lane, separately review the relevant probate rules and engagement structure rather than importing the unclaimed-property ceiling as universal. The pilot should produce a usable contract checklist tied to the selected lane, with unresolved questions visible before paid acquisition starts and before the first claimant is asked to engage.

## Build an encrypted register that can survive handoffs  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The minimum case register needs a stable case identifier, source URL or record reference, jurisdiction, property channel, decedent identifiers, potential claimant, relationship hypothesis, evidence status, next action, owner, committed costs and review date. Separate an unverified match from a documented kinship link; an identical name is not identity proof. Store source retrieval dates and document references so another researcher can reproduce the conclusion without repeating every search. Use a dedicated business account and an encrypted document store, with encryption at rest and in transit where supported. Full-disk encryption protects a lost locked device but does not prevent an already authenticated user from opening files. Pair it with access controls, automatic screen locking, updated software and a tested recovery process.

Limit each contractor to assigned case folders and only the documents needed for that task. Use individual accounts, multifactor authentication and prompt access removal when an engagement ends; shared passwords make attribution and revocation unreliable. Keep unnecessary identity numbers out of filenames, chat messages and task-board titles. A register entry can point to a restricted document instead of embedding an entire birth certificate or identity scan. Record who exported or shared a sensitive packet, the recipient and the reason. Before sending, verify the destination using a known contact route and use a restricted transfer mechanism rather than an unrestricted public link. Treat source access terms and record eligibility restrictions as part of the research workflow, especially when requesting certified vital records.

Create encrypted backups on a schedule matched to daily work and keep recovery credentials separate from the device being protected. Test restoration with a synthetic case before collecting sensitive client documents; a green backup icon is not evidence that recovery works. Retain originals and annotate copies so corrections do not erase the source evidence. Adopt a documented retention period based on engagement duties, disputes and applicable requirements rather than accumulating files forever. Log deletion decisions and suspend routine deletion when a relevant preservation obligation exists. If a device is lost or an account appears compromised, revoke access, preserve available logs and assess exposure before resuming sharing. The pilot needs a simple executable procedure, not an expensive security product that nobody has configured or practiced using.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open the record
   - Assign a stable case ID and source references.
   - Mark identity and kinship assertions as unverified until supported.
2. Restrict the evidence
   - Store sensitive documents in encrypted, access-controlled folders.
   - Use individual accounts and grant only the needed case access.
3. Prove recoverability
   - Restore a synthetic file from backup.
   - Record the result and keep recovery credentials separately.
4. Close access
   - Revoke contractor access and apply retention rules.
   - Preserve relevant evidence when a dispute or other duty requires it.

## Separate portfolio runway from the pilot and enforce stop rules  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A multi-case operation may require $10,000 to $50,000 of working capital, but that range is a scenario input rather than an assurance of sufficient funding. At $2,000 monthly operating expense, those balances cover five to twenty-five months before direct case spending. At $10,000 monthly expense, they cover only one to five months. An eighteen-month wait at the higher burn consumes $180,000 before incremental case costs, well beyond the range. Forecast monthly cash using actual payment dates, contractor commitments, software renewals and record orders, with no receipts until a defensible collection event. Compare six-, twelve- and eighteen-month collection scenarios and a no-collection pilot outcome. Keep owner household reserves separate and exclude client property from available business funds.

Set written stop rules before the first subscription purchase. In this pilot, pause new discretionary spending when deployed costs plus outstanding commitments would exceed $3,000, or when projected unrestricted business cash would fall below the protected $2,000 reserve. Stop adding leads after the initial twenty until the cohort review establishes evidence quality and remaining capacity. Stop paid work on a file when the next research step has no defined evidence question or when the remaining plausible lawful fee cannot cover incremental cost under a conservative outcome. An unresolved licensing issue or an impermissible contracting date stops that affected activity immediately, not unrelated permitted research. These are bounded pilot decisions; they do not cancel existing obligations or justify losing documents, missing agreed notices or abandoning a claimant without an orderly handoff.

Review the cohort at thirty, sixty and ninety days using intake counts, supported relationship chains, rejected matches, committed cost per viable file and documented next events. Do not require ninety-day cash collections from a lane deliberately modeled around longer administration periods, but do require a credible funded continuation plan for accepted cases. At day ninety, cancel nonessential renewals and choose closure, a narrower repeat pilot or a separately financed expansion. Expand only when service permissions are resolved, the evidence process is reproducible and the downside cash forecast remains funded. If the experiment fails, preserve necessary records, settle vendor commitments and explain the evidence-based reason for closure. A stopped $3,000 experiment with its reserve intact is more useful than an expanding pipeline whose apparent asset value never becomes collectible revenue.

| Available capital | Monthly opex | Simple runway | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| $50,000 | $2,000 | 25 months | Case spending reduces this runway |
| $10,000 | $10,000 | 1 month | Insufficient for a delayed portfolio |
| $50,000 | $10,000 | 5 months | Does not fund an eighteen-month wait |

## Action Checklist

- [ ] Bound the pilot to one lane, ninety days and twenty initial leads.
- [ ] Obtain quotes for review, entity costs and suitable E&O coverage.
- [ ] Protect the $2,000 reserve from planned deployment.
- [ ] Document PI licensing and legal-practice boundaries.
- [ ] Use encrypted storage, individual access and a tested backup.
- [ ] Track committed costs against the $3,000 deployment ceiling.
- [ ] Review at days thirty, sixty and ninety before expanding.
- [ ] Fund accepted-case obligations under delayed collection scenarios.
