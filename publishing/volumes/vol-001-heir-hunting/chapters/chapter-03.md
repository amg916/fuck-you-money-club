## Executive Summary

A contingency model earns compensation only on the contract's lawful trigger. The percentage, fee base, expenses, cancellation provisions and timing restrictions require local review. Measure contribution and cash flow separately from headline recoveries.

## Write the economic contract clearly  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Specify the services, authorized claimant, payment trigger and amount on which any percentage is calculated. Address unsuccessful cases, document charges and termination without implying that a signed agreement guarantees a valid fee.

A percentage below a nominal ceiling can still be unlawful if the agreement is premature, the service is regulated or mandatory disclosures are missing. Do not repackage prohibited compensation as expenses or obtain broad control of client assets to evade restrictions.

## Walk the fee down to contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Assume a $60,000 actual recovery and a lawful 10% fee: collected business revenue is $6,000. Subtract $1,200 in research and records, $800 in professional review and $1,000 allocated acquisition cost to obtain $3,000 contribution.

This is not owner net profit. Fixed overhead, owner compensation, taxes and financing costs remain. Allocate unsuccessful lead costs across the cohort; otherwise successful files appear more profitable than the business really is.

| Line | USD | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collected fee | 6,000 | Revenue |
| Research and records | -1,200 | Direct costs |
| Professional review | -800 | Direct costs |
| Acquisition allocation | -1,000 | Includes failed leads |
| Contribution | 3,000 | Before fixed costs, owner pay and tax |

## Cash timing can defeat paper margins  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

For a hypothetical 20-lead cohort with two paid cases at $6,000 each, revenue is $12,000. If all cohort research, review and acquisition spend totals $9,000, contribution is $3,000. One paid case at the same spending produces a $3,000 loss.

Track when money leaves as well as when it returns. A fee that arrives after a long administration cannot fund next month's expenses without reserves. Model no recovery, half the expected recoveries and twice the expected wait before expanding.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Track cohort-level spend, including dead leads.
2. Record actual collections rather than unsigned forecasts.
3. Calculate contribution before owner profit.
4. Stress-test both conversion and collection delay.
