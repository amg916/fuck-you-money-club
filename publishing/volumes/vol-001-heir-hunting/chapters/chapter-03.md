## Executive Summary

Heir hunting earns money when documented entitlement produces a recovery and a lawful agreement makes the research fee collectible. A listed estate balance is neither revenue nor an heir's distributable share. Price the correct recovery channel, verify contract timing and fee limits, and charge failed research against successful cases. The illustrative $60,000 recovery produces only $3,000 of contribution after specified direct costs and acquisition allocation. Annual scenarios must then absorb fixed overhead, capacity constraints and collection delays. Keep the official scorecard separate from cash forecasting: attractive annual profit cannot fund tomorrow's record requests without available working capital.

## Sell a defined recovery service, not a signature  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin the commercial file with the asset channel, not the percentage. A California Controller unclaimed-property claim and an inheritance distributed through probate involve different procedures, counterparties and contract restrictions. Record the holder, property identifier, decedent, proposed claimant, approximate recoverable interest and source date before quoting terms. California Code of Civil Procedure section 1582 regulates agreements to locate, deliver, recover or assist in recovering property reported under the unclaimed-property chapter, including a restricted contracting period and a ten-percent ceiling for covered agreements. That ceiling is not a universal probate finder rate, and a percentage below it does not cure defective timing or disclosures. Obtain channel-specific review using the actual agreement and record chronology rather than borrowing a competitor's form.

Define the earning event and the payment event separately. An illustrative agreement can make compensation contingent on the named claimant actually receiving the identified property, with payment due after documented receipt; whether that structure is permitted depends on the governing rules. A database match, signed authorization, filed petition or preliminary entitlement finding should not silently become an invoice trigger. Specify the fee base, treatment of partial distributions, excluded assets, termination and responsibility for approved third-party costs. Identify who receives the funds and avoid assuming the researcher may hold client money. Use a separate engagement where a licensed attorney is needed, and distinguish independent legal services from any prohibited sharing of legal fees with a nonlawyer.

Before execution, reconcile the proposed percentage with the maximum lawful compensation and every associated charge. Calling part of the compensation a document fee or marketing fee does not automatically remove it from a statutory ceiling. Preserve the reviewed version, signature dates, required disclosures and the event evidence supporting eligibility to contract. Explain the claimant's direct recovery route rather than implying a government affiliation or exclusive access to public records. A lawful contract still leaves credit risk: the claimant may dispute the invoice or receive less than the estimated share. Treat enforceability, recovery and collection as separate uncertainties in the case register. The operator's commercial advantage is accurate evidence and efficient service, not a contract that exaggerates entitlement or obscures alternatives.

> - Identify the governing jurisdiction and recovery channel.

## Reconcile the complete case waterfall  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The worked case starts with $60,000 actually recovered by the client, not the gross face value of an estate. Assume a lawful, enforceable ten-percent contingency agreement for this particular channel. The business receives $6,000 in fees; the other $54,000 is client value, not operating revenue. Deduct $1,200 for certified vital records and genealogy labor, then $800 for independent legal review borne by the business. Deduct another $1,000 allocated from the acquisition cohort, including searches that produced no valid heir, no collectible property or no signed engagement. The remaining $3,000 is contribution before fixed operating expenses and tax. It is neither the founder's take-home cash nor a promise that each completed file will support the same costs.

Use a cohort denominator that cannot improve merely because failed leads disappear from the dashboard. If twenty researched leads consume $4,000 of acquisition effort and only four eventually produce collected fees, allocate $1,000 to each collected case in that cohort. Preserve the original twenty identifiers and explain closures with reasons such as duplicate property, insufficient kinship evidence, superior claimant or uneconomic balance. Do not count the same genealogy hours both inside the $1,200 direct-cost bucket and inside acquisition allocation. Time sheets should distinguish lead screening from accepted-case proof work. If only two cases collect, acquisition burden becomes $2,000 per successful case, reducing the same waterfall contribution to $2,000 before overhead; the recovered principal has not changed, but the economics have.

Maintain a budget, committed-cost total and paid-cost total for every case. A certified certificate ordered today is a commitment even if the card statement has not arrived. Record rebates, refunds and unused retainers against their original cost category, rather than presenting them as new recovery revenue. Track owner research hours at an internal replacement rate so an unpaid founder does not make a labor-intensive file look artificially scalable. For financial reporting, reconcile that management estimate to actual booked expenses instead of deducting fictional payroll twice. Review contribution by recovery channel and acquisition source quarterly. A large payout from one exceptional estate can conceal several years of weak screening, so show both the individual case waterfall and the entire intake cohort's cumulative cash result.

The official Volume 001 scorecard is 87 out of 100, comprising 22, 23, 18, 12 and 12 points, with a plus-or-minus-three-point range and High confidence designation. Conservative assumes $80,000 of recognized fees and $20,000 owner net profit at a twenty-five-percent margin. Base Case assumes $340,000 and $127,500 at thirty-seven-and-a-half percent. Scaled Small Team assumes $600,000 and $300,000 at fifty percent. These are planning scenarios, not observed cohort results. The scorecard confidence label describes the assessment, not a statistical probability that a new operator will achieve those earnings. Use the exact scenarios for comparison while replacing their operating assumptions with actual evidence during the pilot.

The implied annual expense budgets are $60,000, $212,500 and $300,000 respectively: subtract owner net profit from gross recognized fees. Each budget must absorb direct delivery costs, unsuccessful acquisition, paid labor, software, insurance and other operating overhead without duplicate allocations. The people counts describe one full-time role plus three contractors in the first two cases and two full-time roles plus four contractors at scale. They do not represent four or six salaried full-time equivalents. Define contractor hours, rates and scopes before deciding whether the expense envelope can support the workload. For this planning presentation, owner net means business operating profit after modeled operating expenses and before the owner's personal income taxes; document owner compensation treatment separately when comparing it with a salaried job.

At an average collected fee of $6,000, the annual fee targets correspond to approximately 13.3, 56.7 and 100 fee-equivalent cases. Those fractions are a throughput comparison, not permission to book an incomplete claim as earned revenue. Real case counts depend on recovery size, lawful pricing and partial receipts. The worked waterfall's fifty-percent contribution rate leaves no fixed-overhead headroom at a fifty-percent annual net margin. Therefore the scaled scenario requires improved contribution economics, such as larger average fees or lower acquisition and delivery cost per dollar, rather than copying the example one hundred times and ignoring overhead. Test that bridge explicitly. Recognition policy also matters: signed contingencies and unresolved claims are pipeline, while recorded revenue must follow the business's documented accounting policy and remain reconcilable to collections.

## Stress the calendar before expanding intake  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build separate cash schedules for six, twelve and eighteen months between initial spending and collection. These are probate administration stress windows, not statutory completion deadlines; litigation, missing heirs or asset sales can extend them. California Probate Code section 11420 establishes an order for paying estate debts, including priority for administration expenses. It does not make a private heir-finder invoice a priority estate administration expense merely because the researcher found an heir. Debts, taxes, expenses and competing interests can reduce what is distributable before any contingency fee becomes payable. Review the inventory and appraisal, creditor claims, accountings and distribution orders relevant to the case. Never forecast the fee simply by applying a percentage to the largest asset figure appearing in a docket.

Suppose fixed business cash spending is $2,000 monthly and five accepted cases each require $2,000 of direct spending before collection. With no intervening receipts, required funding is $22,000 for six months, $34,000 for twelve months and $46,000 for eighteen months, before additional acquisition spending or contingency reserves. The $10,000 direct-cost component combines the worked example's records, labor and review costs, but does not include its cohort allocation. Show acquisition cash separately to avoid understating the funding requirement. If all five expected payments slip by six months, fixed spending adds another $12,000. A profitable expected outcome cannot pay vendors during that extension. Calculate the lowest projected cash balance each month rather than relying on an annual profit total that hides the trough.

Close the monthly review by comparing expected receipt dates with documented case events. An administrator's estimated timetable, an unsigned proposed distribution and an entered order are different evidence strengths. Update the forecast when creditor disputes, identity defects or missing certificates arise, and retain the previous estimate to expose repeated optimism. Stop new paid intake when committed costs plus the next operating period exceed available unrestricted cash and the protected reserve. Continue necessary existing obligations through a funded plan rather than abandoning files. Separate business reserves from household living expenses, and never count client funds as working capital. Expansion becomes rational when completed cohorts demonstrate positive contribution, collectible agreements and a cash cycle the business can finance even under the delayed scenario.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the fee basis
   - Record channel, recovery and reviewed terms.
   - Check restrictions before contracting; retain the source chronology.
2. Allocate all delivery costs
   - Reconcile direct and cohort costs.
   - Keep failed leads in the denominator and prevent double counting.
3. Stress collection timing
   - Model six, twelve and eighteen months.
   - Include committed spending, reserve protection and no-collection downside.
