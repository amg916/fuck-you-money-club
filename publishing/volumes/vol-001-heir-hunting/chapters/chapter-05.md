## Executive Summary

Experienced case screening asks what can reach the claimant after priority claims, administration costs and legal restrictions. Liens, Medicaid estate recovery and insolvency can change the economics completely. Treat operator advice as a hypothesis until official records and professional review support it.

## Lien priority comes before the exciting headline  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A property value or sale-surplus amount is only the beginning. Secured claims, taxes, administration expenses and competing rights can consume the amount that appeared available. Obtain the relevant title, docket and payment records rather than assuming the first person located receives the balance.

Priority rules differ between estate administration, trustee sales and tax sales. Record each known claim, its source, the unresolved amount and the legal question it raises. Have qualified counsel resolve priority disputes; a researcher should not turn an incomplete spreadsheet into a legal distribution opinion.

| Risk | Evidence to obtain | Triage response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secured claim | Recorded instrument and current payoff evidence | Recalculate plausible net value |
| Competing priority | Official claims and court orders | Seek legal allocation review |
| Uncertain estate solvency | Inventory, allowed debts and administration costs | Pause optimistic recovery assumptions |
| Stale or duplicate lead | Current docket and custodian confirmation | Close or merge with an audit trail |

## Medicaid estate recovery is not a universal clawback  `🏷️ STATUTORY_FACT`

Federal Medicaid law requires estate recovery for certain assistance and allows states choices within the statutory framework. For people receiving covered assistance at age 55 or older, specified long-term-care services are central to the mandatory recovery rules. States can differ in covered recoveries and in whether their estate definition reaches certain nonprobate interests.

Surviving-spouse and qualifying-child protections, deferral rules and hardship-waiver procedures can affect recovery. Do not assume that every Medicaid beneficiary's estate owes the same claim, or that every family qualifies for an exception. Obtain the state program's current notice and have counsel assess enforceability, timing and any applicable waiver before estimating distributions.

## Insolvent estates and dead-lead triage  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

An estate can contain valuable property and still have nothing distributable to heirs. If assets are insufficient after legally prior payments, locating additional relatives does not create a recovery. Record whether the problem is insolvency, missing authority, inadequate evidence, a closed deadline or an uneconomic research path.

Use staged research: inexpensive source confirmation first, a bounded evidence search second, and specialist escalation only after the file passes the earlier gates. Close a lead with a reason and a reopening condition rather than repeatedly rediscovering it. This preserves both cash and respectful treatment of families.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Confirm current asset and claim status.
2. List debts, liens and unresolved priority issues.
3. Check Medicaid recovery questions where relevant.
4. Estimate a conservative range of distributable value.
5. Close, pause or approve the next research stage in writing.
