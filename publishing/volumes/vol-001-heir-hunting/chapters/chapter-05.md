*The best early decision is often a documented no-go.*

## Executive Summary

Experienced heir hunters price the residue, not the headline estate. A valuable house can conceal a mortgage shortfall, unpaid taxes, administration costs, and a Medicaid recovery claim that leaves heirs nothing. This chapter separates statutory payment constraints from an operator's spending rules, then turns both into a staged research process. Spend $25 to establish a credible opening, $150 to test essential records, and $600 on specialist genealogy only when the economics survive. Record every rejection with evidence and a reopening condition so dead leads become reusable intelligence rather than recurring expenses.

## Value the distributable residue before valuing the family tree  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A probate inventory answers what the estate reports owning; it does not answer what an heir will receive. Start with the filed inventory and appraisal, the latest account, the creditor claims register, and recorded security instruments. Assign an evidence date to every number. A valuation from eighteen months ago is not a current sale estimate, and an original deed of trust is not a current payoff statement. Separate cash already held by the representative from property that must be sold. Mark disputed ownership and inaccessible balances as unresolved rather than silently counting them. The senior operator's first deliverable is a dated residue worksheet with traceable sources, not a polished family tree or an optimistic percentage fee calculation.

Consider an illustrative house appraised at $420,000, with $18,000 of estate cash. A current mortgage payoff of $350,000, $14,000 of property taxes, $29,000 of expected sale expenses, $24,000 of administration costs, and $30,000 of other payable claims produce negative $9,000: $438,000 minus $447,000. Do not present the $438,000 asset total as an heir opportunity. Also do not double count property taxes already included in a payoff or closing estimate. Reconcile each deduction against the underlying statement. A lien that attaches to the house and an unsecured claim against the estate require different treatment; grouping both into a generic debt percentage hides the very information that determines whether further research deserves funding.

Stress the worksheet before purchasing records. Reduce the expected sale price by ten percent, increase carrying costs for six additional months, and reserve for unresolved claims using explicit scenarios rather than invented precision. In the example, a $42,000 price decline alone destroys any thin surplus that a favorable negotiation might have created. An underwater property can still coexist with other distributable assets, so reject the economic thesis only after checking the complete estate rather than the parcel alone. Conversely, a solvent estate does not imply an attractive individual recovery: a $60,000 residue divided among twelve equal heirs leaves $5,000 per heir before any properly agreed service fee. Research effort follows the reachable share, not merely estate solvency.

## Read the payment order instead of assuming heirs come first  `🏷️ STATUTORY_FACT`

California Probate Code section 11420 supplies an order for payment of estate debts, beginning with expenses of administration and then obligations secured by a mortgage, deed of trust, or other lien, subject to the statute's treatment of the secured property. Funeral expenses, expenses of last illness, family allowance, wage claims, and general debts occupy subsequent listed classes. Read the entire provision and related rules before assigning a claim to a line. Section 11420 also preserves preferences established by United States or California law. Therefore, a spreadsheet that automatically ranks every tax claim below every ordinary expense is unsafe. The operative question is which enforceable priority applies to this obligation and this asset, not whether the claimant's name sounds important.

Taxes deserve their own evidence lane because a recorded property tax lien, a decedent's income tax obligation, and income tax generated during administration are not interchangeable. Obtain the available tax statements and relevant filed accounts; request clarification through the authorized representative when private information is necessary. Record whether an amount is assessed, estimated, disputed, secured, or already paid. A missing tax return is not evidence of a zero tax liability. Nor does the absence of a claim on an easily searched docket establish that all governmental claims are extinguished. Where payment priority is uncertain, give the representative or retained probate professional the actual documents and reserve the disputed amount until a supported treatment is available.

The practical wipeout often comes from several ordinary items rather than one dramatic creditor. A delayed sale creates insurance, utilities, property preservation, and additional professional work; a contested accounting can extend that delay. The operator should refresh the residue estimate when a material claim arrives, when an appraisal changes, and before committing the next research tranche. Do not treat a filed claim as conclusively allowed or a disputed claim as conclusively worthless. Track its procedural status and the evidence supporting any reserve. If expenses and superior obligations exhaust available assets, a perfectly documented heir still inherits no distributable residue from those assets. That is a commercial no-go, not a failure of genealogical skill, and the file should say so plainly.

## Treat Medicaid estate recovery as a specific statutory inquiry  `🏷️ STATUTORY_FACT`

Under 42 U.S.C. section 1396p(b), states must seek specified recovery for certain Medicaid benefits, subject to statutory protections. For individuals aged fifty five or older when assistance was received, the mandatory scope includes nursing facility services, home and community based services, and related hospital and prescription drug services. Federal law permits states to elect broader recovery for other covered assistance, with statutory exclusions, so the phrase all medical bills after fifty five is an unreliable shortcut. A separate recovery category concerns certain permanently institutionalized individuals described in subsection (a)(1)(B); do not collapse that category into the age threshold. Identify the state program, service dates, service category, and applicable state implementation before estimating an estate recovery exposure.

Section 1396p(b)(2) limits when recovery may occur: recovery is permitted only after the death of a surviving spouse, if any, and only when there is no surviving child under twenty one or surviving child who is blind or permanently and totally disabled under the statutory standard. These protections are not equivalent to a discretionary discount negotiated by an heir finder. Establish the relevant family circumstances through appropriate documents without collecting unnecessary medical details. Depending on the applicable state rules and facts, a protection may defer recovery rather than permanently erase every possible obligation. Avoid promising that a surviving spouse always cancels the claim. Certain home recoveries also involve qualifying sibling or caregiver child protections with specific residence requirements that must be checked separately.

The federal provision requires procedures for waiver where recovery would create undue hardship, but an operator cannot grant a waiver or assume one will succeed. Obtain the state's current notice, application instructions, submission deadline, and hardship criteria from its official recovery program. Record whether an application is pending and model both outcomes until a decision arrives. The federal estate definition includes the probate estate and permits states to adopt an expanded definition covering specified nonprobate interests; check what the relevant state actually adopted, including rules tied to death dates. Request an itemized claim or authorized clarification rather than estimating liability from a nursing home's advertised daily rate. Log the recovery issue as unresolved, protected, waived, payable, or disputed only when the documentary record supports that label.

## Buy answers in stages and preserve the reason for every stop  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use three incremental research ceilings: $25 for an initial screen, $150 for a vital record check, and $600 for specialist genealogy. These are internal planning amounts, not predictions of county charges or a universal market price. Count paid retrieval, contractor time, and internal labor at a consistent cost basis. The first stage should establish an open matter, a credible asset source, and no obvious residue wipeout. The second should test the weakest essential kinship link, such as whether the apparent sibling is actually the decedent's sibling. The specialist stage should have a written research question, jurisdictions, expected record types, and stopping conditions. Do not spend $600 merely because the preceding $175 now feels too large to abandon.

Suppose a supported $50,000 residue appears available to one reachable heir and an illustrative, locally permissible agreement would produce a $5,000 fee if recovery succeeds. A rough thirty percent joint probability of proving entitlement and collecting yields $1,500 of expected fee revenue before remaining fulfillment costs, overhead, and tax. Spending all $775 would leave only $725 before those further costs; a twenty percent success probability leaves $225. The example is a decision model, not a fee recommendation or measured conversion rate. Test whether a cheaper record can resolve the biggest uncertainty first. If lawful pricing, likely share, or expected work cannot support the next tranche, stop even when the genealogy is intellectually interesting and the contact seems enthusiastic.

Maintain a dead lead log keyed by court, case number, decedent identifier, and discovery date. Use reason codes such as CLOSED_DISTRIBUTED, NO_RESIDUE, REPRESENTED_HEIR, RECORD_PATH_BLOCKED, and CLAIM_UNRESOLVED, with a short factual explanation and a link or reference to the decisive record. Distinguish a permanent rejection from a temporary hold; a pending claim decision merits a dated review trigger, while a final distribution normally does not merit weekly rechecking. Preserve the minimum necessary personal information and keep sensitive supporting documents in restricted storage. Review rejection patterns monthly to improve source selection and screening questions. The objective is not to maximize the number of files opened; it is to avoid buying the same disproven assumption twice while preserving a defensible path to reopen genuinely changed cases.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Screen
   - Cap incremental spend at $25.
   - Capture open status, assets, claims, and the first residue estimate.
2. Test the weak link
   - Authorize up to $150 for vital records.
   - Specify the relationship being tested and the record that can resolve it.
3. Commission bounded research
   - Authorize up to $600 for specialist genealogy.
   - Require surviving economics, a defined question, and a stop condition.
4. Close or refresh
   - Log the decision and evidence.
   - Assign a reopening trigger only when a concrete unresolved event exists.
