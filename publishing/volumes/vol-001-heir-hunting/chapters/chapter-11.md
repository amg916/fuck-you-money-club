# Chapter 11: Small-Team Scaling: Running a High-Margin 2-to-4 Person Firm

*Design for disciplined contribution margins, not guaranteed profitability.*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Assign research, review, operations, and principal accountability explicitly. A small firm can combine roles, but high margin is a business objective rather than an assured outcome. Scale only after quality, collection, and cash controls work.

## Assign roles before adding headcount  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE ORGANIZATIONAL DESIGN; LICENSING REQUIREMENTS REMAIN INDEPENDENT.`

In a two-person firm, one person may lead research while the other handles operations and independent evidence review, provided both are qualified for their tasks. The principal owns intake, budget, and compliance escalation. Retain qualified outside counsel for legal work rather than treating a research reviewer as legal counsel.

At three or four people, separate research production, quality review, and outreach operations where workload supports it. Where internal review competence is insufficient, buy independent review instead of approving unsupported conclusions. Document who can approve contact, spending, data disclosure, and case closure.

| Function | Accountability | Release gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | Source collection and relationship analysis | Evidence packet ready for review |
| Quality review | Independent conflict and citation check | Material issues resolved or explicitly held |
| Operations | Scheduling, approved contact, and records | Consent and approved templates checked |
| Principal and outside counsel | Business approval and legal advice respectively | Legal and budget questions cleared by appropriate owner |

## Measure collected contribution and work in progress  `🏷️ MANAGEMENT METHOD; TARGETS SHOULD COME FROM OBSERVED RESULTS.`

Track cash collected, direct labor, record costs, approved external services, and allocated overhead separately. An estate's value is not revenue, and a signed agreement is not cash. Include failed searches and unrecovered cases when evaluating the portfolio rather than showing only successful files.

Set a work-in-progress limit using available research and review hours. For example, 40 weekly production hours divided by 10 expected hours per active case supports four case-equivalents of that week's work, not four guaranteed closures. Reserve capacity for corrections, court delays, and urgent record requests.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Estimate remaining effort
   - Update research, review, and external-dependency estimates for each open case.
2. Reconcile cash and costs
   - Compare actual collections and case costs with the approved model; separate overhead.
3. Gate new intake
   - Accept work only when budget, review capacity, and compliance approvals are available.

## Protect quality while delegating  `🏷️ INTERNAL CONTROL RECOMMENDATIONS, NOT A CERTIFICATION STANDARD.`

Use role-based access, documented onboarding, and a checklist for reviewer sign-off. Sample closed files for unsupported links, inconsistent calculations, missing consent, and retention errors. Record corrective actions with an owner and due date rather than quietly fixing the exported report alone.

Define suspension triggers for repeated evidence errors, unauthorized access, misleading outreach, or uncertain licensing. Revoke departing personnel's access promptly and preserve required records. Have counsel assess worker classification and vendor agreements; calling a worker a contractor does not determine their legal status.

> **CONTROL — Never scale an unresolved failure**
> 
> Pause the affected workflow before increasing volume when a material control fails.
> - Assign a corrective-action owner.
> - Review other potentially affected cases.
> - Document the evidence supporting restart.
