# Chapter 11: Small-Team Scaling: Running a High-Margin 2-to-4 Person Firm

*Design for disciplined contribution margins, not guaranteed profitability.*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Scale the work system before expanding the payroll. A lean heir-hunting practice needs separate responsibility for research, independent review, client communication, and legal decisions even when several roles are fractional. This chapter translates those responsibilities into weekly capacity limits, controlled access to sensitive records, and cohort accounting that separates signed opportunities from collected fees. The illustrative portfolio scenarios show why four contributors can still produce only modest owner income, and why the scaled six-person configuration is an expansion beyond the lean core rather than evidence that headcount automatically creates profit.

## Separate decisions even when people wear multiple hats  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the firm around five responsibilities, not five full salaries. The Principal or Case Lead chooses jurisdictions, owns the acquisition budget, resolves scheduling conflicts, and authorizes commercially sensible work. The Senior Genealogist constructs the lineage hypothesis, searches original records, and writes the proof argument. The Independent QC Reviewer tests that argument against conflicting evidence without being its author. The Client and Records Coordinator tracks certified copies, appointments, correspondence, and deadlines. Independent Outside Probate Counsel determines the legal route and handles reserved legal work under a separate engagement. A two-person core can combine principal and coordination duties while buying bounded research and independent review assignments; it cannot make independence real merely by changing an employee's title.

Create a responsibility sheet for every accepted case before ordering expensive records. Record the case lead, researcher, reviewer, coordinator, and counsel contact, plus who may change each deliverable. The researcher may correct a source transcription but cannot mark their own proof packet independently reviewed. The coordinator may send an approved factual status update but cannot improvise an interpretation of intestacy law. Counsel should receive the disputed relationship and governing facts, not a vague request to bless the entire business. When the principal also researches, assign another qualified person to QC and preserve the reviewer's unresolved objections. A revenue target must never silently convert an unresolved parentage conflict into a confirmed heir relationship.

Use a written handoff rather than a meeting as the unit of progress. A research handoff includes the source register, image references, identity timeline, negative searches, conflicting evidence, and proposed next action. QC returns a dated disposition of pass, correction required, or hold, with specific record references. The coordinator then knows whether outreach can proceed or whether another death certificate is needed. If an independent reviewer is unavailable, pause that transition while research continues on another permitted file. Outsourced counsel and reviewers need realistic turnaround expectations; buying ten hours of specialist time does not reserve unlimited availability. Maintain a second qualified supplier for continuity, with a separate confidentiality agreement and scoped access rather than shared passwords.

**Lean responsibility map**

| Role | Owns | Does not self-authorize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Principal / Case Lead | Budget and queue | Legal conclusions outside authority |
| Senior Genealogist | Source-backed kinship proof | Independent QC of own work |
| Independent QC Reviewer | Evidence challenge and disposition | Unlogged factual alterations |
| Client & Records Coordinator | Requests and approved correspondence | Distribution advice |
| Independent Outside Probate Counsel | Jurisdiction-specific legal analysis | Commercial return assumptions |

## Turn available hours into a defensible WIP limit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with forty protected research hours per week across the team, after subtracting intake, administration, training, and leave. If an active dossier consumes ten research hours each week, the deep-work limit is forty divided by ten, or four concurrent files. This is a concurrency limit, not a promise to finish four cases every week. A dossier requiring thirty total research hours may occupy a slot across three weeks. If all files had that same demand and records arrived instantly, theoretical throughput would be forty divided by thirty, approximately 1.33 research completions weekly. Real throughput is lower when dependencies, corrections, and uneven case difficulty intervene. Distinguish deep research from waiting on certified records so the board represents actual labor commitments.

Run the weekly board with explicit queues: screened intake, active research, external wait, QC, counsel query, outreach, claim pending, and closed. Every external-wait file carries its next follow-up date and an owner; otherwise apparent spare capacity hides abandoned work. Four active files already consume the full forty-hour research budget, leaving no research buffer. A prudent starting schedule assigns three full slots and retains ten hours for correction work or urgent documentary discoveries. If the reviewer has six hours available and each packet needs three, QC can accept two packets weekly, regardless of how quickly researchers draft them. Reduce upstream starts when finished packets accumulate; hiring another researcher does not repair a reviewer bottleneck.

Measure age and effort separately. A claim waiting sixty days for a court event might have consumed only twelve staff hours, while a five-day identity puzzle can consume twenty. Record both on the case board so an old case is not automatically labeled inefficient and a young case is not automatically cheap. Set a research spending checkpoint before the next record order: compare remaining evidence gaps, expected collectible fee, estimated additional labor, and the probability of a legally viable claim. A file that has exhausted its experiment budget returns to the principal for a documented continue, narrow, or close decision. Do not keep every weak lead alive merely to make the pipeline look full; stale WIP makes reliable promises impossible.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Budget hours
   - Subtract nonresearch obligations.
   - Record protected weekly hours by contributor; do not treat contractor headcount as full-time capacity.
2. Assign slots
   - Divide hours by expected weekly dossier effort.
   - At 40 / 10, cap deep files at four; reserve a slot when correction demand is uncertain.
3. Check downstream work
   - Compare QC and counsel availability with expected handoffs.
   - Delay starts when review queues exceed booked capacity; log the next review date.

## Classify workers by facts and restrict sensitive records  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Do not treat the letter C in a staffing model as a legal classification decision. Section 203 of Title 29 defines terms including employer, employee, and employ, with employ including suffer or permit to work; it does not make a contractor invoice dispositive. Actual worker status depends on applicable law and the working relationship, with state rules potentially adding different tests. Have the appropriate adviser evaluate control, economic dependence, scheduling, equipment, and the actual services before using a contractor arrangement. Maintain the resulting rationale and revisit it when an occasional researcher becomes a continuously supervised team member. Budget payroll taxes, insurance, and other employment costs when required rather than preserving a fictional margin through a label.

California Business and Professions Code section 7521 describes private-investigator activity broadly enough that compensated locating and investigative work requires a careful scope analysis. Read that provision with applicable exemptions and licensing requirements rather than assuming genealogical branding removes the issue. Send counsel the actual workflow: which persons are located, what records are gathered, who pays, and what communications are made. Counsel should identify which tasks may be performed, by whom, and under which authority in the selected jurisdiction. Outside probate counsel does not automatically supply the firm's separate investigative licensing authority. Keep professional legal work separate from factual research, and stop expansion into a new state until its relevant operating requirements have been evaluated.

Use individual accounts, multifactor authentication, and an encrypted case vault with access granted by case and role. The coordinator generally needs mailing details and request status, not unrestricted access to every identity document. A reviewer needs the evidence packet and source images but not unrelated client payment information. Give outside counsel a scoped transfer or folder rather than a reusable team login. Log exports, expire temporary access, and remove departing contributors promptly. Define retention periods by record type and applicable obligations, with documented exceptions for active disputes. Store redacted working copies where full identifiers are unnecessary; preserve originals in a restricted location. Test recovery from backup with a dummy case before depending on the vault for live work.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Access follows an assigned task**
> 
> No shared logins and no blanket contractor access.
> - Name the case and task before granting access.
> - Record who reviewed worker status and investigative scope.
> - Revoke access at assignment close and retain the audit trail.

## Scale collected contribution, not signed face value  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat the portfolio scenarios as annual planning illustrations, not observed outcomes or staffing prescriptions. Conservative uses one full-time contributor plus three contractors, four people in total, with eighty thousand dollars gross and twenty thousand owner net at twenty-five percent. Base keeps that four-person configuration and models three hundred forty thousand gross with one hundred twenty-seven thousand five hundred owner net at thirty-seven point five percent. Scaled uses two full-time contributors plus four contractors, six people, with six hundred thousand gross and three hundred thousand owner net at fifty percent. That six-person case extends beyond the lean two-to-four-person core. Contractor counts indicate contributors, not full-time equivalents, and each scenario needs its own hours and compensation assumptions.

Reconcile each scenario to its implied cost envelope before discussing growth. Conservative leaves sixty thousand dollars for modeled expenses, Base leaves two hundred twelve thousand five hundred, and Scaled leaves three hundred thousand. Those envelopes must cover the costs included in the model: labor, record procurement, specialist review, counsel paid by the firm, insurance, software, acquisition, and overhead. Define owner net consistently as modeled pre-personal-tax residual after listed business expenses, and disclose whether owner labor compensation is included or excluded. If excluded, track owner hours and an imputed market wage separately. A twenty-thousand-dollar residual supporting full-time owner effort is not evidence of a strong economic wage, even if the nominal percentage margin looks attractive.

Group files by intake month and legal lane, then track screened leads, accepted cases, labor hours, cash outlay, signed fee exposure, claims submitted, fees collected, refunds, and write-offs. Keep signed contingent fees separate from receipts; a large estate value is neither company revenue nor cash available for payroll. For an illustrative cohort collecting thirty thousand dollars against twelve thousand direct costs, contribution is eighteen thousand before overhead and owner labor adjustments. Allocate six thousand of overhead and the remaining twelve thousand is a cohort residual, not necessarily cash in the same month. Include failed files in the denominator. Expand only when matured cohorts support the additional fixed commitment and cash reserves can absorb the zero-collection downside while pending matters remain unresolved.

**Illustrative annual portfolio economics; FT = full-time, C = contractor contributor**

| Scenario | People | Gross | Margin | Owner net | Implied expenses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative | 1 FT + 3 C = 4 | $80,000 | 25% | $20,000 | $60,000 |
| Base | 1 FT + 3 C = 4 | $340,000 | 37.5% | $127,500 | $212,500 |
| Scaled | 2 FT + 4 C = 6 | $600,000 | 50% | $300,000 | $300,000 |
