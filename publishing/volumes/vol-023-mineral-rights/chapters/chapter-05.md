## 1. Underwrite the Conveyed Interest Before Pricing the Production  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is buying a spreadsheet description instead of the interest actually conveyed. In the NMA/NPRI title underwriting play, ten net mineral acres in a 640-acre unit at a 20% lease royalty produce an illustrative decimal of 10 / 640 x 0.20 = 0.003125. Against $100,000 of royalty-bearing proceeds before applicable adjustments, that decimal implies $312.50, not $2,000. This shortcut works only when the unit allocation, mineral ownership, and lease terms support it. A nonparticipating royalty interest may instead be fixed or floating, burden only particular tracts, and exclude executive rights, bonuses, and delay rentals. Never multiply an NPRI by the lease royalty until the instrument's language and controlling law establish that treatment. Have a landman assemble the recorded chain and counsel resolve ambiguous reservations. Require deed references, tract descriptions, depth limitations, and the exact fraction in the acquisition workbook. When the seller's decimal differs from the supported decimal by more than 1%, freeze pricing pending reconciliation; this is an internal threshold, not a title standard. The fallback is a smaller, verified acquisition or no purchase, rather than a speculative discount on ownership that may not exist.

The second cluster of traps hides between the courthouse index and the operator's pay deck: unprobated estates, omitted heirs, unreleased liens, marital-property claims, stale powers of attorney, and deeds that reserve the valuable depths. A division order is useful payment evidence but does not replace a title examination. Texas Natural Resources Code Section 91.402 addresses payment timing, qualifying withholding circumstances, and division orders; it does not make an operator's current decimal conclusive ownership proof. Pull certified instruments when the transaction requires them, search grantor and grantee variants, and reconcile every conveyance into the tract ledger. Use TexasFile or a county portal as a discovery aid, then preserve the underlying recorded document and retrieval date. Search applicable judgment, lien, and UCC records without assuming a UCC search establishes real property title. If a $40,000 purchase requires $6,000 of curative work, the economic basis starts at $46,000 before carrying costs; a hoped-for $50,000 exit leaves only $4,000 before selling expenses. Obtain executable curative deliverables before funding or use a negotiated escrow tied to specific releases. A seller's indemnity is only as useful as collectability and enforceability.

The third trap is treating every lease as the same royalty contract. Read the royalty valuation point, post-production cost language, affiliate-sale provisions, shut-in clauses, continuous development obligations, pooling authority, and depth or acreage release terms. A cost-free phrase does not automatically settle every deduction dispute; governing law and the entire instrument matter. Identify whether the operator can allocate compression, gathering, processing, or transportation charges and whether taxes are separately stated. An apparent $1,000 monthly gross entitlement with $180 of supported deductions and $70 of production taxes delivers $750, so valuing the asset at forty gross months rather than forty net months overstates that simple price anchor by $10,000. Those figures are illustrative underwriting inputs, not the portfolio scorecard. Obtain at least twelve production-month statements where available and flag any deduction ratio rising five percentage points above its trailing three-month median. That signal starts document review rather than proving misconduct. If lease language remains unresolved, model the disputed charge as payable, reserve investigation costs, and decline acquisitions whose margin depends on winning a future dispute. A cheap price cannot repair a defective valuation premise.

## 2. Separate Docket Optionality from Producing Cash and Exit Liquidity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Pooling-docket curve-jumping fails when an application is treated as a completed well. Oklahoma Corporation Commission proceedings under 52 O.S. Section 87.1 can establish important spacing and pooling consequences, but a filing, hearing, order, election, permit, spud, completion, and first sale are different events. Track each event separately in the authorized agency portal and retain the actual order, not a broker screenshot. A simple illustrative acquisition pays $24,000 for an interest expected to receive $600 monthly after first sale. At an analyst-selected 10% annual carrying hurdle, an additional nine-month delay adds $1,800 of economic carrying burden before actual operating costs. The hurdle is a valuation assumption, not an invoice or a scorecard expense. More seriously, the well may never be drilled. Do not infer participation elections or cost-bearing obligations from a royalty label; determine exactly which interest is subject to the order and calendar its deadlines. If development slips ninety days beyond the documented investment milestone, stop follow-on purchases in that thesis and refresh the probability-weighted valuation. The fallback is pricing existing production alone, with unproved future drilling assigned no purchase premium.

Production forecasting creates another asymmetric loss when newcomers annualize a flush-production check, borrow against it, and ignore depletion. For an illustrative stress test, 100 monthly cash units declining to 65 and then 50 cannot support a flat 1,200-unit annual forecast. Build a monthly model by well and product using production history from the Texas Railroad Commission or the relevant state agency, operator statements, and a documented price deck. Enverus can assist where licensed, but its estimates do not replace the seller's actual ownership and settlement data. Separate oil, gas, and natural gas liquids; distinguish sales month from production month and inspect curtailments, workovers, and changed purchasers. A greater-than-20% volume decline against the same-well forecast for two reported months triggers an engineering refresh, after allowing for reporting lag and revisions. Pair that signal with a 25% realized-price downside and a six-month cash-delay test. Do not treat these internal thresholds as geological laws. Where debt requires distributions that the stressed net receipts cannot support, reduce leverage before purchase. The practical fallback is an unlevered position sized to survive interruption, not a covenant waiver assumed in advance.

Institutional flips break when a nonbinding indication becomes the only exit plan. Build a buyer-ready package containing tract schedules, recorded instruments, title exceptions, lease abstracts, well identifiers, ownership decimals, and reconciled receipts. In the institutional-flip play, purchasing for $80,000 and selling for $100,000 produces a $20,000 realized trading gain before $5,000 of separately expensed diligence and selling costs, leaving $15,000 before other overhead. The $80,000 returned acquisition basis is not operator revenue. If the buyer's final supported decimal is 15% smaller and the bid falls proportionally to $85,000, those same costs consume the entire spread. Track retrades by cause: ownership, price deck, lease burden, missing documents, or buyer liquidity. Pause repeat buying from a source when two consecutive packages suffer ownership-related price reductions above 5%. Negotiate defect procedures, cure periods, and narrowly defined surviving obligations rather than promising unlimited future reimbursements. The fallback is a hold case supported by current net royalties and available reserves. If that hold case fails, a fast-close discount does not justify buying an asset that only one uncommitted counterparty might accept.

## 3. Reconcile Receipts Before Spending Cash That Can Be Recovered  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A deposited royalty check can contain money the owner cannot safely distribute. Operator corrections may reverse duplicate payments, revise product prices, change allocated volumes, or apply an earlier ownership decimal. A title dispute can move future payments into suspense, while a purchaser correction can affect several prior production months. In the royalty reconciliation play, compare a supported 0.003125 decimal against a paid 0.003500 decimal on $100,000 of eligible monthly proceeds: the apparent excess is $37.50 monthly, or $450 across twelve identical months. Actual exposure requires statement-level reconstruction, not multiplication across unequal months. Isolate the apparent excess in the cash forecast, notify the operator through a documented channel, and obtain the contractual and factual basis for any proposed recovery. Do not presume either that every demand is valid or that a payment's age defeats recovery. Maintain a correction ledger showing production month, original statement, revised statement, asserted reason, disputed amount, and resolution. Any unexplained negative adjustment above 5% of trailing three-month receipts triggers distribution restraint and review. The fallback is distributing only reconciled cash while the disputed amount remains liquid and traceable.

Build the control around a four-way reconciliation: recorded ownership, operator decimal, product settlement, and bank cash. Use authorized EnergyLink downloads where available, operator remittance files, and a controlled spreadsheet or SQLite ledger keyed by owner number, property number, product, and production month. Preserve gross values, taxes, each deduction category, adjustments, and net paid separately. Bank deposits prove receipt, not correct calculation. Investigate any supported ownership mismatch immediately; use a $50 or 2% statement-to-bank variance, whichever is larger, as an internal triage threshold after grouping payments correctly. Monitor missing statements against the operator's established cycle and escalate payments thirty days beyond that cycle, while separately applying the jurisdiction's actual payment rules. Texas Natural Resources Code Sections 91.402 and 91.403 address payment timing and interest with specific exceptions; do not substitute the internal alert for statutory analysis. Automation should propose exceptions, not silently write off small mismatches. Repeated $20 errors across fifty properties total $1,000 per cycle. Route changed bank instructions through independent verification, and never send account credentials to a supposed operator representative merely because their email contains accurate well names.

Keep the canonical operating ledger separate from acquisition capital and illustrative deal examples. Conservative revenue is $30,000 with $24,000 of implied aggregate costs, leaving $6,000 at 20%; Base revenue is $215,000 with $145,125 of costs, leaving $69,875 at 32.5%; Scaled revenue is $400,000 with $220,000 of costs, leaving $180,000 at 45%. Revenue includes royalty receipts and realized rights-trading gains, excluding returned basis. Contractor counts describe a delivery bench, not three or four full-time salaries: Conservative and Base use one full-time person plus three contractors, while Scaled uses two plus four. The $1,000-$12,000 monthly opex range is not a complete allocation of every scenario's aggregate costs; do not force the annual cost residual into that range. The $15,000-$75,000 startup capital range and six-to-twenty-four-month first-deal window demand a separate liquidity schedule. In an illustrative flat-cost sensitivity, a $10,000 revenue reversal would move Conservative profit to a $4,000 loss; this does not alter its canonical case. Maintain distinct cash reserves for documented recoupment exposure, carrying costs, and upcoming tax obligations. Never describe underlying well sales or returned purchase principal as portfolio revenue.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture immutable settlement evidence
   - Import authorized statements and bank records into a month-keyed reconciliation ledger.
   - Retain original files, retrieval dates, operator identifiers, and separate production and payment months; record revisions as new evidence.
2. Rebuild the supported entitlement
   - Compare ownership, valuation inputs, taxes, deductions, and adjustments against the controlling instruments.
   - Assign an owner to each discrepancy and distinguish a title question from a settlement calculation or timing difference.
3. Contain unresolved recovery exposure
   - Restrict distributions associated with apparent overpayments and pursue written operator clarification.
   - Document the amount, underlying production months, asserted recovery basis, reserve decision, and next review date without conceding unsupported demands.
4. Close and reconcile the exception
   - Verify corrected statements and actual cash settlement before releasing the associated reserve.
   - Update the forecast and source-quality score; reopen the issue if subsequent statements reverse the correction or introduce new periods.

## 4. Stop Outreach Abuse, Data Bans, and Pooled-Capital Liability  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play tcpa_litigator_scrub_honeypot_arbitrage is implemented here as consent-first screening, not a license to harass owners who lack litigation history. Lawful docket-derived risk signals may support review alongside mandatory applicable do-not-call checks, entity-specific suppression, revocation processing, and independently verified consent. Public mineral ownership does not establish telephone permission. Determine which federal and state calling rules apply to the actual campaign, technology, recipient, and purpose; a minerals purchase pitch cannot simply inherit another business's consent. The Telephone Consumer Protection Act, 47 U.S.C. Section 227, and 47 CFR Section 64.1200 govern relevant calling restrictions. Certain private actions under Section 227(b)(3) allow $500 per violation and discretionary trebling for willful or knowing violations; exposure depends on the specific facts and claim. Never treat that amount as a universal settlement price. An illustrative batch of 1,000 candidate records might leave 700 documented eligible contacts after all applicable checks; 300 are suppressed, not rerouted through fresh numbers. A $0.20 screening cost per candidate means $200 per batch, or about $0.286 per eligible record. This ancillary service illustration adds nothing to canonical mineral revenue.

A plaintiff-name match is a noisy risk signal, not a finding that someone is abusive or an authorization to contact everyone else. Common names, changed addresses, incomplete dockets, and mistaken party roles generate false matches. Use PACER or authorized court access within applicable terms, record provenance, minimize retained personal information, and restrict the service to documented campaign controls. Never create bait calls, impersonate a court, publish accusation lists, or suppress complaint handling because a recipient previously sued another caller. The fallback is explicit: disable uncertain enrichment and require independently verified consent and authoritative suppression checks. Any missing permission evidence blocks that record in this consent-first workflow; any failed suppression synchronization pauses the campaign. Twilio and other communications providers apply their own acceptable-use and messaging requirements, separate from statutory duties. Rotating accounts after suspension, spoofing identity, sharing purchased credentials, or bypassing court access controls can produce termination and additional liability. A warning or material complaint cluster triggers investigation before sending resumes. Preserve provider notices, timestamps, and permission records; appeal through the legitimate process. No provider can promise permanent account access, and a replacement account does not cure the underlying conduct.

The final trap is financing a slow, disputed asset with promises that create immediate obligations. When pooling outside money, selling passive participations, or receiving compensation for placing interests, assess the actual structure before soliciting funds. Securities Act Section 2(a)(1), codified at 15 U.S.C. Section 77b(a)(1), expressly addresses fractional undivided interests in oil, gas, or other mineral rights within its securities definition; other arrangements can also implicate investment-contract analysis. Section 5 registration requirements and any available exemption require transaction-specific work, while broker questions arise separately under Exchange Act Section 15(a). Calling investors club members or relabeling returns as royalty sharing does not decide the analysis. Require a documented offering path, accurate economics, proper custody arrangements, and clear authority over distributions before accepting outside capital. Track county mineral property taxes, reporting obligations, and applicable federal tax treatment separately from operator withholding. A $12,000 annual carrying budget requires $1,000 monthly reserving even during suspense. If fundraising authority, title, or liquidity remains unresolved, stop new commitments and preserve existing asset records. Survival comes from limiting irreversible obligations before chasing another apparently cheap acre.
