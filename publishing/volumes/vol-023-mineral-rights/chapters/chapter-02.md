## 1. Identify the Actual Payor, Their Pain, and Their Spending Authority  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate the economic beneficiary, the check writer, and the person authorized to release money. For producing interests, a purchaser of production or an operator's revenue department may remit royalties under the lease, division-order arrangements, and applicable payment statutes. The landman who discusses acreage usually cannot change the pay deck or authorize a suspense release. For an institutional acquisition, an acquisitions director sponsors the trade, an investment committee sets the ceiling, title counsel defines acceptable defects, and treasury funds the closing. For a family buyout, the sibling negotiating price might lack authority over an estate or trust. Obtain the relevant fiduciary instruments, entity resolutions, and signatory evidence before treating enthusiasm as buying power. Acute pain is concrete: a family wants estate liquidity, an institution needs contiguous exposure that fits its mandate, or a production payor must resolve documented payment obligations. None of these facts makes an unleased operator buy your minerals. Ask who owns the budget, which conditions release it, when treasury processes payments, and who can reject title. Record those answers separately from the broker's narrative. A transaction advances only when price, legal authority, and payment capacity converge on the same identified counterparty.

The mandatory TRACK_1 play, fractional_heir_share_partition_forcing, starts with a documented fractional estate, not a threat. Suppose an eighty-acre tract carries a verified one-quarter mineral interest: the acquisition covers twenty net mineral acres, before examining burdens and the precise estate conveyed. A $12,000 purchase followed by an $18,000 voluntary co-owner buyout produces $6,000 of gross realized gain before transaction expenses; the returned $12,000 is not revenue. The buyer pays for consolidation, reduced administration, and resolved ownership friction, not because a demand letter creates an unconditional duty to purchase. Inspect probate filings, deeds, reservations, marital interests, liens, and any existing lease before quoting that acreage. Texas Property Code Chapter 23A may alter partition procedure where the property qualifies as heirs' property; counsel must determine its application to the particular mineral estate and dispute. Appraisal, co-owner buyout opportunities, and partition preferences can defeat a simplistic forced-sale thesis. Budget the carrying period and litigation exposure before acquisition rather than using either as negotiating theater. The fallback is a renegotiated voluntary exit or continued ownership within the approved capital limit. Never threaten an unavailable remedy, conceal other owners' rights, or underwrite a compulsory buyout as certain cash.

Producing-royalty acquisition, the second operating mapping, purchases an existing payment entitlement rather than a collection service. Illustratively, $1,000,000 of monthly well-level sales value multiplied by a verified 0.001 royalty decimal yields $1,000 before permitted adjustments. That decimal must come from the lease, ownership chain, unit allocation, and applicable burdens; it cannot be inferred from net mineral acres alone. At a constant run rate, the example contributes $12,000 of annual gross entitlement, but underwriting must also model decline, commodity prices, downtime, and cash deductions. A nonparticipating royalty interest, or NPRI, does not automatically carry leasing authority or a share of bonuses. Read the granting and reservation language, including whether the fraction is fixed or floating. Texas Natural Resources Code Section 91.402 sets payment timing for covered proceeds and recognizes specified grounds for withholding; Section 91.403 addresses interest. These provisions do not turn disputed title into immediately collectible money. Screen the payor's financial condition and establish ownership in its records before forecasting a first deposit. If transfer processing stalls, retain seller cooperation, document the missing requirement, and distinguish recoverable suspense from a valuation loss caused by declining production.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the Payment Department Before Negotiating the Multiple**
> 
> Payment friction usually appears first in ownership records, authority documents, or statement detail.
> - Ask the seller for three consecutive royalty statements and the payor's ownership-transfer checklist; match owner numbers before submitting the deed.
> - For NPRIs, extract the complete reservation clause into the underwriting file and have title counsel resolve fixed-versus-floating language before assigning a decimal.
> - Call the institution's designated closing contact to confirm treasury cutoffs and funding prerequisites before promising a seller a same-day closing.
> - Price fractional heir interests using documented carrying costs and voluntary exit alternatives, rather than assuming a court will deliver a quick sale.

## 2. Match Each Obligation to a Settlement Rail That Can Actually Clear  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the eight payment choices as different control structures, not interchangeable buttons. Wire and ACH are transfer mechanisms; escrow and milestone holds are custody arrangements; retainers describe advance compensation; statutory disbursement describes an underlying obligation; platform checkout supplies an interface; factoring monetizes an eligible receivable. A mineral purchase commonly combines a wire with escrow, while recurring royalties commonly arrive by ACH under statutory and contractual rules. Choose the combination after identifying the asset, title conditions, actual payor, and remedy for failed performance. Use an escrow provider authorized and operationally equipped for the transaction, and confirm whether it handles severed minerals rather than assuming ordinary residential procedures fit. Written instructions should identify deposited documents, permitted disbursements, recording sequence, defect treatment, and the exact event allowing release. Verify bank instructions through an independently obtained telephone number and a second reviewer; an email thread alone cannot authenticate a last-minute account change. Do not let a seller's document-delivery obligation depend on an unverified screenshot of a pending transfer. Confirm receipt with the receiving institution or escrow holder under its actual settlement procedures. Recording and funding must follow a sequence both parties have expressly accepted.

For the pooling-docket acquisition mapping, public regulatory proceedings create an information schedule, not guaranteed production. In Oklahoma, review Oklahoma Corporation Commission filings, hearing materials, orders, and the actual election provisions under 52 O.S. Section 87.1. A proposed order, final order, and completed well are distinct events with different valuation consequences. An illustrative $30,000 acquisition resold for $45,000 yields $15,000 of gross realized gain before expenses, not $45,000 of operator revenue. Condition the purchase on verified ownership, the treatment of existing elections, and a precise description of rights conveyed. If a seller retains a pooling payment or an election has already been made, reflect that allocation in the agreement and price. The later institutional buyer pays for validated exposure and reduced research uncertainty; the commission does not fund your speculative resale. When an order is continued, challenged, or economically unattractive, the fallback is to retain the interest within a modeled carrying budget, renegotiate before a binding closing, or abandon an uncommitted acquisition. Do not promise a buyer an election option that has expired or assume a mineral purchase renews its deadline. Attach the operative order and deadline analysis to settlement instructions where either affects value.

Recurring disbursements need a different control file from asset purchases. For covered Texas proceeds, Section 91.402 generally starts with payment no later than 120 days after the end of the month of first sale; subsequent timing generally differs between oil and gas, subject to statutory provisions and permissible arrangements. Track the actual facts rather than applying one universal clock to every state and interest. Ownership disputes, minimum-payment provisions, and other specified conditions can change the collection analysis. Request the reason for withholding in writing and preserve the required notice history before pursuing remedies. ACH simplifies recurring receipts but remains subject to applicable return and reversal rules; do not describe it as unconditional finality. A platform receipt likewise proves only what the provider's records actually establish. Factoring requires a separate review of receivable existence, assignability, recourse, notification, perfection, and effective cost. If a factor advances $9,500 against a $10,000 eligible receivable and retains $500 as its fee, the fee consumes five percent of face value for that financing period, not five percent annually. Compare that cost against waiting, credit exposure, and contractual restrictions before assigning a royalty stream to solve a temporary cash shortage.

## 3. Protect the Gain, Screen the Buyer, and Reconcile the Scorecard  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Institutional aggregation flips, the fourth mapping, monetize a clean package rather than the gross dollars crossing escrow. For an illustrative Base bridge, purchase a package for $185,000 and sell it for $250,000: returned basis is $185,000 and gross realized gain is $65,000 before selling expenses. Combined with $150,000 of royalty receipts, recognized scorecard revenue is $215,000. Apply total modeled operating and transaction costs of $145,125 to reach the canonical $69,875 owner profit and 32.5% margin. The institution pays because verified acreage, burdens, ownership, and transfer files reduce its acquisition workload and fit its portfolio criteria. It does not pay extra merely because you compiled a large spreadsheet. Package pricing must expose parcel-level adjustments so a defect cannot silently destroy the margin on acceptable assets. Obtain a signed purchase commitment before incurring buyer-specific diligence that cannot be reused. If investment approval disappears, use staged acquisitions, alternative qualified buyers, or retention economics already supported by the royalty case. Do not buy an entire package with short-duration funding solely because an acquisitions employee expressed interest. The downside belongs in the capital plan before deeds are delivered, not in an optimistic resale narrative.

Keep all three annual scenarios tied to the supplied scorecard while treating internal receipt mixes as illustrations. Conservative revenue of $30,000 can comprise $24,000 of royalty receipts and $6,000 of realized trading gain; $24,000 of costs leaves $6,000 owner profit at 20%. Base remains $215,000 revenue, $145,125 costs, and $69,875 profit at 32.5%. Scaled revenue of $400,000 can comprise $280,000 of royalty receipts and $120,000 of realized gains; $220,000 of costs leaves $180,000 profit at 45%. Conservative and Base each use 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C, six people. These are annual operating cases, not acquisition capital recovery schedules or guaranteed staffing productivity. The supplied startup capital range is $15,000 to $75,000, monthly operating expense range is $1,000 to $12,000, and time to first deal is six to twenty-four months. Annual scenario costs are broader than a monthly overhead budget and must be reconciled through actual categories rather than forced into that range. Track purchase basis, transaction expenses, royalty deductions, financing charges, and owner distributions separately. A large closing wire can improve liquidity while adding only a modest amount to recognized revenue.

Fee protection begins by naming what is actually earned. An owner selling minerals earns a trading gain; an intermediary earns a separately contracted fee subject to applicable licensing and transaction rules. Do not invoice your own principal profit as a commission or assume a confidentiality agreement creates payment rights. Where lawful services are separately engaged, identify the fee obligor, specific introduced assets or counterparties, earning event, payment deadline, permitted tail, and dispute process. If payment is to come through closing, obtain written agreement from the responsible parties and escrow holder. A buyer's acknowledgment of an introduction is weaker than an executed payment obligation. Screen institutional counterparties using entity records, signatory authority, available financial statements, litigation and insolvency searches, and financing conditions; SEC EDGAR helps when the relevant entity reports publicly. Verify current funding evidence directly through an authorized channel rather than trusting a forwarded bank image. Review applicable lien records where receivables or assets secure credit. A special-purpose buyer with no assets requires funded consideration or negotiated credit support, not confidence borrowed from its sponsor's logo. Narrow noncircumvention terms to legitimate documented services; payment evidence and closing control provide stronger protection than expansive threats against everyone who sees the offering.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Pre-Flight Checks Before Releasing a Conveyance**
> 
> Each check produces a retained artifact that another reviewer can inspect.
> - Title check: reconcile legal description, fractional estate, NPRI burdens, probate authority, liens, and any applicable pooling election against counsel's written exception list.
> - Authority check: obtain buyer signing authority, fiduciary powers where relevant, and written identification of the treasury or escrow personnel controlling disbursement.
> - Funds check: independently authenticate account instructions, confirm the agreed funding status, and reject unsupported changes sent only through email.
> - Economics check: reconcile consideration, returned basis, recognized gain, expenses, holdbacks, and the scenario's owner-profit bridge before authorizing release.

## 4. Run the Receipt-to-Reconciliation Workflow and Escalate Real Exceptions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Purchased-interest suspense recovery, the fifth mapping, realizes rights that were acquired with documented entitlement to withheld proceeds. Buying minerals does not automatically transfer every historical receivable, so the agreement must allocate pre-effective-date and post-effective-date proceeds, adjustment rights, and seller cooperation. Illustratively, eight months of properly attributable $1,000 monthly payments creates an $8,000 gross suspense claim, not an $8,000 recovery fee. That amount can form part of the royalty-receipt bucket once collected; never add it again as trading gain. Verify whether the balance reflects title suspense, a minimum-payment threshold, returned mail, bank rejection, or an actual ownership dispute. Those categories demand different documents and produce different collection times. Ask the payor for its written requirements, submit a complete indexed package, and record acknowledgment. The fallback is a discounted carrying value, a contractual seller cure, or an appropriately supported claim through counsel, depending on the defect. Do not assume statutory interest applies to every withheld balance or that a new deed resolves an older probate gap. If the proceeds were remitted as unclaimed property, follow the responsible state's claim process rather than demanding that the payor send the same funds twice.

Build a receipt ledger that joins legal ownership to production months and bank deposits. Store the tract identifier, well identifier, payor owner number, interest type, effective date, decimal, product, volume, price, deductions, adjustments, and payment reference. Use EnergyLink statements where available, payor portals, and retained source documents; a spreadsheet can work initially if changes remain reviewable and source files are preserved. Railroad Commission of Texas production records and Oklahoma Corporation Commission records support monitoring, but public reports may use reporting units and timing that differ from revenue statements. Reconcile those differences before accusing a payor of underpayment. Automation should flag missing periods, changed decimals, unusual deductions, and deposits lacking statements; it should not automatically declare every variance collectible. Match a $1,000 expected gross entitlement to the actual statement's deductions and adjustments before comparing the net deposit. Document whether each adjustment is supported by lease language and applicable law. Separate an accounting correction from a commodity-price decline or depletion signal. This is where royalty reconciliation and production monitoring reduce labor without replacing title judgment. Assign one person to own each exception and require a dated next action so an unattended suspense item cannot masquerade as a healthy receivable.

Escalation should follow evidence and the controlling agreement rather than emotional pressure. Start with the relevant revenue or closing department, submit the exact exception with supporting records, and request a dated written response. Next, use the contractual notice process and any applicable statutory prerequisites. Texas Natural Resources Code Section 91.404 includes notice and response procedures relevant to an action for covered unpaid proceeds and interest; counsel should map those requirements to the particular claim. Preserve versions of division orders, transfer requests, correspondence, statements, and settlement instructions. Keep seller cooperation duties alive long enough to complete ownership transfers and address agreed historical adjustments. If capital comes from multiple investors, review the actual offering structure before solicitation: 15 U.S.C. Section 77b(a)(1) expressly includes fractional undivided interests in oil, gas, or other mineral rights within its securities definition. Registration, exemptions, disclosure, and intermediary rules depend on the arrangement and activity; a direct-asset label does not settle the analysis. Close each month with three separate views: royalty cash collected, trading gains realized, and capital still invested. That discipline makes buyer credit deterioration, depleted production, and unresolved title visible before a headline account balance persuades the team to fund another acquisition.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the entitlement and authority packet
   - Reconcile the mineral estate, payment entitlement, legal description, burdens, and authorized signatories.
   - Retain deeds, probate instruments, leases, applicable orders, seller allocations, and a dated list of unresolved title exceptions.
2. Authenticate settlement and release conditions
   - Confirm the responsible payor, escrow custody, funding prerequisites, and independently verified bank instructions.
   - Use a second reviewer for account changes and document the agreed sequence for funding, recording, document release, and holdback resolution.
3. Transfer ownership and match the first receipt
   - Submit the complete payor transfer package and reconcile the first statement to the actual bank deposit.
   - Check owner number, effective date, decimal, product month, deductions, suspense allocation, and any proceeds received by the seller.
4. Close the ledger and escalate documented exceptions
   - Reconcile royalty receipts, realized gains, returned basis, expenses, and open collection items each month.
   - Assign an owner and next-action date to every exception, preserve required notices, and compare results with the canonical annual scenario bridges.
