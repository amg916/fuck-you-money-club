## 1. Classify the Interest, Jurisdiction, and Actual Operator Role  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin every opportunity with an interest classification, not a price per acre. A fee mineral interest can carry executive, bonus, rental, and royalty rights, but prior reservations may separate those attributes. An NPRI normally lacks executive rights and participation in bonuses or rentals; its actual language determines whether the royalty is fixed or floats with the lease royalty. A deed conveying one sixteenth of production does not necessarily convey one sixteenth of the lessor's royalty. Have the title attorney resolve that distinction before the principal converts acres into cash flow. For this chapter, the five mapped plays are producing-mineral acquisitions, NPRI acquisitions, pooling-docket curve-jumping, royalty reconciliation, and institutional flips. Maintain a separate rights-and-authority sheet for each play, including tract description, recording references, ownership fraction, lease status, executive holder, and permitted activity. Texas Property Code Chapter 13 makes recording and notice relevant to competing claims; a county index search alone does not establish marketable title. Obtain the underlying instruments, probate documents, assignments, releases, and applicable judgments. The principal owns pricing; the contract landman assembles the chain; the attorney interprets legal effect; the tax accountant controls basis and reporting. That allocation keeps the canonical four-person pod from treating clerical extraction as a substitute for a title opinion.

Apply jurisdictional rules to the property and the activity separately. Texas Occupations Code Section 1101.005 contains an exemption concerning dealings in mineral leases or interests, but its text does not create a nationwide license or authorize unrelated surface brokerage. For Oklahoma transactions, have local counsel classify the exact service under that state's licensing statutes before promising a procurement fee. Oklahoma forced pooling under 52 O.S. Section 87.1 creates order-specific elections and deadlines; a hearing notice is neither a completed well nor an assured royalty payment. North Dakota's dormant mineral framework, Chapter 38-18.1, requires analysis of statutory use, notice, and preservation procedures. Louisiana mineral servitudes raise prescription questions under Mineral Code Article 27 and related provisions, rather than a universal perpetual-severance assumption. Federal and Indian leases introduce additional agency requirements: 30 CFR Part 1206 governs valuation for covered production, not every private royalty check. Record property location, owner location, contact location, and the operator's service location because each can matter differently. For docket curve-jumping, archive the signed order and subsequent amendments, then separate legal status from engineering probability. Never label a proposed unit as producing merely because Enverus displays nearby completed wells. The landman should reconcile vendor records against the commission docket and county instruments before the acquisition model changes.

Separate buying assets for the firm's own account from selling interests or arranging investments for others. Securities Act Section 2(a)(1), 15 U.S.C. Section 77b(a)(1), expressly includes fractional undivided interests in oil, gas, or other mineral rights within its securities definition. Entity interests and managed royalty pools can also raise investment-contract issues based on their actual structure. Before collecting investor commitments, have securities counsel identify the instrument, offering exemption, solicitation method, investor eligibility, state notices, and intermediary compensation. Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) are different operating paths: broad advertising does not fit the former, while the latter requires reasonable steps to verify accredited status and sales only to accredited investors. An exempt offering does not erase antifraud duties or resolve broker-registration questions under Exchange Act Section 15(a). A percentage of capital raised is a serious classification issue, not an administrative introduction fee. Keep institutional asset-sale outreach distinct from investor solicitation, and avoid publishing yield packages while assuming the transaction is merely a deed sale. For the four-person pod, the title attorney should identify when separate securities expertise is required and obtain it before marketing. If that expertise exceeds the approved contractor budget, remain an own-account buyer rather than improvising a pooled offering.

## 2. Write Contracts Around the Interest and the Payment Trigger  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use separate documents for acquisition, specialist services, and disposition. The purchase agreement should identify the exact estate, legal description, depth limitations, owned fraction, associated leases, and excluded rights. Attach a schedule rather than relying on a county name and a seller's spreadsheet. Specify whether the transaction covers mineral ownership, an NPRI, an overriding royalty, or another defined interest; those assets do not convey identical rights. State the price allocation, deposit handling, effective date, closing conditions, and treatment of receipts spanning that date. A per-NMA price needs an agreed net mineral acre definition and a title-adjustment mechanism; an NPRI purchase needs a separately defined economic fraction rather than an unexplained acre conversion. Include a title-defect notice period, defect valuation method, cure rights, minimum deliverable interest, and termination mechanics. Address probate, spousal signatures, entity authority, liens, and third-party consents where relevant. Texas Property Code Section 5.151 imposes a prescribed disclosure for covered mailed mineral purchase packages; use its current wording and format when the transaction falls within the statute. Do not substitute a friendly cover letter for required text. Record the final conveyance promptly and confirm indexing, because an executed deed sitting in email does not complete the recording work.

For the landman, define scope as specified counties, instruments, ownership questions, deliverables, and completion dates. For royalty reconciliation, define the properties, production months, source statements, exception thresholds, and allowed payor contacts. A service agreement should distinguish document collection from title interpretation and distinguish arithmetic exceptions from an asserted legal claim. Use fixed milestones or approved hourly budgets where suitable. If compensation depends on a closing or recovery, describe the exact trigger, calculation base, deductions, receipt date, reversals, and dispute process, then obtain the relevant licensing and professional-conduct analysis. Never pay on total sale proceeds when the agreed trigger is realized gain. Narrow anti-circumvention to identified counterparties, documented introductions, a reasonable term, and an express preexisting-relationship exception. It should not capture every mineral owner in a basin. Set a negotiated liability cap for appropriate service risks, with deliberate treatment of fraud, intentional misconduct, confidentiality breaches, and unauthorized transfers. Avoid assuming a cap can defeat nonwaivable statutory remedies. Define escalation, governing law, forum, and whether arbitration covers service disputes; local real-property proceedings may require different treatment. The principal approves budgets and business terms, while counsel drafts enforceable allocations rather than importing a software consulting form into a deed transaction.

Own the transaction evidence without claiming ownership of every underlying record. County instruments remain public records; vendor databases, imagery, and extracted datasets may carry contractual reuse restrictions. The service agreement should allocate ownership of newly prepared schedules and models, license necessary preexisting tools, identify permitted recipients, and require return or deletion of confidential seller materials when work ends. Check Enverus and county-access terms before placing exports in an institutional data room. For the flip, disclose the actual acquired interest, title exceptions, lease burdens, suspense status, and the provenance of production assumptions. A permit, drilling application, or pooling order cannot support a representation that reserves are proved. Match assignment language and warranty scope to what the seller can actually support. Obtain a closing statement separating consideration, transferred basis, expenses, and retained receivables. If purchase cost is $100,000 and gross disposition consideration is $130,000, the rights-trading gain before transaction costs is $30,000; the $100,000 returned basis is not operator revenue. Prevent duplicate recognition when accrued royalties are included in the purchase adjustment. Store the signed agreement, deed, recording receipt, funds confirmation, and accounting bridge together. The accountant should trace each recognized dollar to a royalty receipt or realized trading gain, not to a purchase price headline.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Required Verifications Before Signing or Sending Funds**
> 
> The principal records these checks separately; one completed check does not substitute for the others.
> - Counsel confirms the purchased estate, fraction, authority to convey, material title exceptions, and required state-specific offer language.
> - The principal matches price, deposit, effective date, title-adjustment rights, and payment instructions to the executed agreement.
> - The responsible specialist confirms the operator's licensing position and whether investor solicitation or transaction-based compensation requires separate analysis.
> - The accountant confirms basis treatment, royalty cutover, settlement adjustments, and the revenue bridge; independently verified payment instructions match the authorized payee.

## 3. Reconcile Statutory Payment Rules with the Four-Person Pod  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Royalty reconciliation begins with the lease and the payor's source data, not a blanket assumption that every deduction is improper. Obtain check detail, production month, sales volumes, realized prices, decimal interest, taxes, deductions, and prior-period adjustments. Texas Natural Resources Code Section 91.402 governs payment timing and specified withholding circumstances; Section 91.403 addresses interest. Oklahoma's Production Revenue Standards Act, including 52 O.S. Section 570.10, has its own payment and interest structure. Have counsel determine the applicable deadline, title condition, exceptions, and interest treatment before sending a demand. A division order helps administer payment but does not replace the underlying conveyance or lease; Texas Section 91.402 also restricts its effect on lease terms. Calculate a simple unit decimal only when the governing instruments support that method. Forty NMA in a 640-acre unit at a one-fifth lease royalty yields 0.0125 before applicable burdens or other allocation adjustments. Do not reuse that formula for a fixed NPRI, allocation well, or depth-limited interest without reviewing the operative documents. Flag volume discrepancies against state production records, recognizing reporting lags and differences between well production and sales. Escalate supported exceptions with a property identifier, production period, expected amount, observed amount, and documentary basis.

Keep taxes and management economics in separate but reconciled schedules. Internal Revenue Code Sections 611, 612, and 613A address depletion, basis, and oil-and-gas-specific limitations; eligibility depends on the taxpayer and interest, not a universal percentage applied to every deposit. The accountant should distinguish royalty income, acquisition basis, sale proceeds, realized gain, allowable deductions, and applicable state obligations. State severance taxes, local property taxes, and information reporting need their own calendars. A payor's tax deduction does not prove every owner filing has been completed. Preserve the canonical scorecard without replacing it with a transaction's headline proceeds: Conservative recognized revenue is $30,000, net margin is 20%, and owner net profit is $6,000. Base recognized revenue is $215,000, margin is 32.5%, and owner net profit is $69,875. Scaled recognized revenue is $400,000, margin is 45%, and owner net profit is $180,000. The corresponding differences between revenue and profit are $24,000, $145,125, and $220,000. These are scenario expense envelopes, not permission to expense returned acquisition principal as recurring overhead. Maintain a separate capital ledger for the $150,000 to $2,000,000 startup capital range. Explain timing and classification differences between cash movement, asset basis, and recognized results before comparing a quarter with the annual model.

Operate the Conservative and Base cases with one full-time principal and three contractors: landman, attorney, and tax accountant. The principal handles acquisition pricing, owner conversations, payment authorization, and institutional buyer relationships. The landman maintains tract-level evidence and monitors relevant commission dockets. The attorney reviews title, operative clauses, and jurisdictional questions within the retained scope. The accountant reconciles receipts, basis, and taxes. Use Airtable for tract and deadline tracking, QuickBooks for transaction accounting, and restricted document storage for source instruments; software does not decide title or waive contract deadlines. Automate royalty statement ingestion and production-change alerts, then route exceptions to the responsible person. For pooling-docket curve-jumping, require a second human check of order status and election dates before changing the bid. For NPRI acquisitions, require counsel's fraction interpretation before the model is released to a buyer. For institutional flips, assemble the buyer-ready evidence package before marketing. The Scaled case adds a second full-time operations analyst and a fourth contractor, such as a petroleum engineer, reaching six people rather than silently expanding the Base pod. With a six-to-twenty-four-month first-deal horizon and monthly operating expense range of $1,000 to $12,000, preserve capacity by rejecting unresolved low-value files early instead of multiplying speculative research assignments.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open the jurisdiction and rights file
   - Record tract, estate, fraction, lease, owner authority, and proposed operator role.
   - Assign the landman to source documents and the attorney to unresolved legal classifications; do not price an assumed interest.
2. Validate the legal and economic bridge
   - Connect operative instruments to decimals, payment rules, title conditions, and contract terms.
   - Keep proposed development upside separate from current receipts and retain the evidence supporting each modeled adjustment.
3. Execute and record through controlled instructions
   - Confirm signatures, closing conditions, independently verified payment details, and recording responsibility.
   - Retain the settlement statement, conveyance, recording confirmation, and payor ownership-change package in the same restricted file.
4. Reconcile receipts and monitor exceptions
   - Match incoming royalties and realized sale gains to accounting records and source statements.
   - Route title, payment, production, tax, and deadline exceptions to their assigned specialist; document resolution before closing each exception.

## 4. Use Verbatim Outreach That States Role, Limits, and Next Steps  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a plain initial owner-contact script: 'Hello, I am the principal of a business that buys mineral and royalty interests for its own account. Public records suggest you may own an interest in the tract described below. Those records can be incomplete, so I am asking whether you are the appropriate person to contact. We are interested in discussing a purchase, not collecting a fee from you. You do not need to send identification documents or bank details to have an initial conversation. If you prefer no further contact, reply stop and we will remove you from our outreach list.' Insert only verified sender and tract details. Do not imply affiliation with a producing operator, county office, or another owner. For commercial email, assess 15 U.S.C. Section 7704 and implement accurate headers, a truthful subject, required identification, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out with timely suppression. Maintain source, contact date, channel, and suppression status. If enclosing a deed and payment in a covered Texas mailing, apply Section 5.151 separately; this introductory script is not its prescribed disclosure. Never disguise a purchase solicitation as a royalty statement or an urgent government notice, and never suggest that silence transfers ownership.

For discovery, say: 'Before discussing price, may I confirm whether you hold minerals, a royalty reservation, or an inherited interest? Do you have a deed reference, lease, or recent statement you are comfortable sharing through our secure document channel? Please remove bank and tax identifiers from an initial copy. Are there other owners, estate proceedings, liens, or existing sale agreements we should account for? We will review the documents and explain the interest our offer covers, the proposed price, and any title conditions. You may have your own attorney review the documents before deciding.' Record answers as owner statements pending verification, not established title facts. For calls and texts, classify the actual campaign under 47 U.S.C. Section 227 and 47 CFR Section 64.1200, including technology, message purpose, number type, and applicable state restrictions. An offer to purchase property is not automatically treated identically to every sales solicitation; obtain a campaign-specific determination instead of assuming either universal exemption or universal coverage. Use manual, individually reviewed contact as the initial operating method, honor stop requests, and check relevant calling restrictions. Before recording, evaluate the laws implicated by both participants' locations; California Penal Code Section 632 illustrates why confidential calls need specific recording analysis. Do not record while that analysis remains unresolved.

For an institutional flip, use: 'We own the interest identified in the attached schedule and are evaluating a sale. The package separates recorded ownership, current lease terms, historical receipts, title exceptions, and development assumptions. Any pending pooling or drilling activity is labeled by its actual docket status and date. If this fits your acquisition criteria, we can provide a restricted data room and discuss your title requirements before agreeing on a closing schedule.' Use that ownership statement only after acquisition is complete; otherwise disclose the actual contractual position and transfer restrictions. For reconciliation, say: 'We are reviewing payments on an interest our firm owns. Please confirm the decimal and explain the listed adjustments for these production months. Our attached schedule identifies the source documents and arithmetic differences; please identify any title or account condition affecting payment.' These scripts support the five plays without adding outside service fees to the canonical revenue model. Log every material representation and retain the version of the package actually sent. The principal handles consequential conversations; contractors receive only their assigned documents. If a prospect asks to invest rather than sell an asset, stop the asset-buying script and route the proposal for securities review before accepting money or sending projected distributions. Suppression requests follow the contact across future lists.
