# Chapter 07: How Wells Get Drilled: Horizontal Technology and the Major Shale Plays

*Vertical, deviated, directional and horizontal wells, the annulus, horizontal drilling and frac stimulation, options for depleted wells, high build-rate Marcellus wells, gas EOR in the Permian, and the Utica, Marcellus and Permian Basin plays*

## Executive Summary

Operators across Expand Energy, Chesapeake, Range Resources and the British Columbia Energy Regulator describe the same drilling sequence: conductor, surface casing set below the deepest fresh water, a vertical section stopped a few hundred feet above the shale, a gradual build curve steered by an adjustable-bend motor or rotary steerable system, then a cemented production string and plug-and-perf stimulation. The numbers are specific: 21 to 28 rig days, laterals up to 28,000 ft, one pad replacing 32 vertical wells on 1,280 acres with 90% less surface disturbance. Play-level data shows Utica laterals doubling since 2017 and Marcellus pads costing up to $70 million. Disagreements surface over conductor depths, lateral lengths, mud versus air intervals, and whether geology or technology deserves credit for the shale boom.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator walk-through starts with the same steel telescope. Expand Energy drills a large-diameter hole for the first 90 to 110 ft before the rig even arrives, cements conductor casing, then air or freshwater drills until the bit clears the base of the freshwater zone, after which surface casing is cemented inside the conductor [zhTs0NrEVgE @ 00:01:15]. The Chesapeake version of the same sequence puts the Marcellus freshwater zone at roughly 800 ft and air drills an average of 100 to 200 ft below its base before setting surface casing [7tXxUXv528A @ 00:01:12]. Range Resources showed the physical hardware on a Marcellus pad: a 26-inch line for the first 40 ft, a 20-inch water string set to 401 ft at that location based on the nearby water table, a 13 3/8 mine string to isolate coal, a 9 5/8 intermediate, and 5 1/2 inch production casing to total depth [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:05:23]. The British Columbia Energy Regulator describes the same logic from the regulator's chair: surface casing isolates fresh water and serves as the foundation for the blowout preventer, with cement pumped out the shoe and forced up between casing and hole [qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:00:47]. Nobody skips a layer; the debate is only depth.

The second point of agreement is where and how the well turns. Expand stops the assembly a few hundred feet above the target shale, trips the string out, and swaps to either a rotary steerable system or a downhole motor with an adjustable bend to steer from vertical to horizontal [zhTs0NrEVgE @ 00:03:41]. The British Columbia Energy Regulator puts the kickoff point at approximately 500 feet above the planned horizontal leg, runs a downhole motor with measurement-while-drilling instruments, and says the curve from kickoff to horizontal takes just under a quarter of a mile [qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:01:26]. Range Resources hammers the same misconception every visitor brings: it is not an abrupt 90 degree bend but a slow, gradual build over several hundred feet, and the one-degree bend at the bottom of the motor lets the crew turn that bend any direction they want to steer [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:17:08]. Range also claims its crews could hit a basketball buried 2 miles deep and 2 miles out, using pulses sent through the drilling mud, the earth's magnetic pull, and formation calculations, all checked by human monitors [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:16:15]. The operating rule is simple: get vertical to the kickoff point cheap, then build the curve slow and controlled.

The business case is identical across every speaker: contact. Chesapeake's field script says horizontal wells touch more gas-producing shale, so fewer wells develop a field, and one multi-well pad can recover the same reserves from a 1,280-acre tract that would otherwise need as many as 32 vertical wells on their own pads, cutting surface disturbance by 90% [7tXxUXv528A @ 00:05:10]. Energy Vision Global frames it as the vertical well crossing the formation over a short interval while the horizontal stays inside the reservoir for a much longer distance, which is why it pays in thin reservoirs and developments where production efficiency is critical, at the cost of advanced directional tools and accurate real-time measurements [fihgFZ8xpQo @ 00:05:49]. Petro Learn adds that shale and tight oil sit in narrow layers, so the lateral exposes far more wellbore to oil-rich rock and significantly increases production rates [Xr_kSHJguTM @ 00:02:57]. The British Columbia Energy Regulator closes the loop: tight reservoirs lack natural fractures and cannot be produced economically without hydraulic fracturing, and multi-stage fracking is now the standard [qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:04:57]. Horizontal reach without stimulation is just an expensive hole drilled through rock that will not flow.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Range Resources splits the job across two rigs to save money. A conventional air rig, a Falcon Drilling unit built by Shram, drills the vertical sections and sets most of the casing strings on every well on the pad because it is more cost efficient than the larger horizontal rig; crews work 12-hour shifts and the rig moves on after a couple of weeks [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:11:39]. The horizontal rig then arrives; Range favors a super single only 90 ft tall that it calls nimbler, a lot cheaper, and more welcome to neighbors [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:18:18]. Bit selection follows the hole section: roller cone tricone and hammer bits in the vertical air-drilled portion, then synthetic-diamond PDC bits that shave the shale in the lateral, with mud jetted through the nozzles to cool the bit and carry cuttings [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:09:40]. The British Columbia Energy Regulator supplies the tubular math: 30-foot joints at roughly 495 pounds each, over 350 joints weighing nearly 87 tons for a 10,500-foot well, with the string pulled for bit and tool changes in a process called tripping pipe [qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:02:07]. Every trip is rig time, so the bit plan matters.

The mechanics that keep the lateral alive live in the annulus. Offshore Engineering Academy gives the driller's formula: hole diameter squared minus pipe diameter squared, divided by 1029.4, equals annular volume in barrels per foot, so a 12 1/4 inch hole around 5 inch pipe runs about 0.12 barrels per foot [YPFuHij-FYk @ 00:03:00]. That number is the first line of defense against a blowout: if returns do not match what was pumped, the crew is either losing fluid into the rock or taking a kick, and both are stop-everything emergencies [YPFuHij-FYk @ 00:03:48]. The same channel warns that bigger pipe in the same hole speeds annular velocity and cleans the hole, but extra friction pressure can fracture weak rock, so hole cleaning versus breaking the hole is a constant balancing act. Dennis Livingston, the Baker Hughes automated drilling systems technical advisor, describes the team with Consol Energy hitting two-mile-a-day Marcellus wells drilled under Pittsburgh International Airport, where the hard part was building a high dogleg curve into a long lateral at proper ROP, landing in target and staying there [40uE_5Fvn_A @ 00:01:09]. The stated lesson from that record run was three words repeated: plan, plan, plan, with operator, service company, office and rig personnel aligned on goals and communication holding through execution [40uE_5Fvn_A @ 00:01:45].

Once production casing is cemented, the rig leaves and the service crew takes over. The British Columbia Energy Regulator lays out the cycle: a perforating gun goes down on wireline, an electrical charge shoots holes through casing and cement into the shale, the stage is fracked with water, sand and additives at extremely high pressure, a temporary plug isolates it, and the sequence repeats until the lateral is covered, after which the plugs are drilled out [qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:03:41]. Range Resources showed the hardware: perf guns with angled charges blasting a few inches to a few feet into the Marcellus, a frac plug set in place, a frac ball dropped to control the next stage, and a small drilling unit grinding everything into material that looks like sawdust [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:31:51]. Range's fluid is 99.9% water and sand with three to four chemicals: an antimicrobial, a scale inhibitor and a friction reducer [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:30:13]. Water travels five to seven miles from centralized impoundments through temporary pipeline, 25 to 30% of it recycled, and fracking one well while prepping the next raised some efficiencies by a factor of four [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:21:40]. Each stage is controlled from the frac van, where crews can shut the whole spread down remotely.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Front-end science, permits and pad capital
   - Study the rock, topography and regulatory requirements before any surface work begins.
   - Range magnifies the shale tens of thousands of times to read organic content, files water sourcing, recycling and construction plans, and handles sound studies, traffic reports and road bonding, with planning beginning up to three years ahead on a pad worth upwards of $60 to $70 million [Range Resources Corporation — 8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:04:34]
2. Shallow hole and freshwater isolation
   - Set conductor, air drill below the freshwater base, then cement surface casing and install the BOP.
   - Chesapeake air drills an average of 100 to 200 ft below the base of a Marcellus freshwater zone that runs to approximately 800 ft, then cements surface casing inside the conductor before the blowout preventer goes on [Oil & Gas Industry VDA — 7tXxUXv528A @ 00:01:31]
3. Kickoff, build curve and lateral
   - Stop above the target, trip out, run a steerable assembly and build angle into the shale.
   - Drill to the kickoff point about 500 feet above the planned leg, run a downhole motor with measurement while drilling instruments, build the curve over just under a quarter mile, then drill the lateral to target distance [British Columbia Energy Regulator — qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:01:48]
4. Case, perf, frac and drill out
   - Cement production casing full length, then perforate and frac stage by stage behind plugs.
   - Perf and frac each stage, isolate it with a temporary plug, repeat across the entire horizontal distance, and drill out the plugs so gas can flow up the wellbore [British Columbia Energy Regulator — qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:05:17]

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Operators split on the hardware that turns the hole from vertical to lateral. Expand Energy stops the assembly a few hundred feet above the target shale, trips the string, and swaps to either a rotary steerable system or a downhole motor with an adjustable bend, then drills laterals up to 28,000 ft from the point of formation entry [zhTs0NrEVgE @ 00:03:57]. Range Resources runs the motor route: a one-degree bend at the bottom of the assembly that crews rotate to steer, building a slow, gradual curve over several hundred feet instead of an abrupt 90-degree turn [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:17:08]. Baker Hughes and Consol Energy took the other path beneath Pittsburgh International Airport, deploying high build-rate rotary steerable systems to land a high dogleg curve, stay in the target zone, and post two-mile-a-day wells in the Marcellus [40uE_5Fvn_A @ 00:01:45]. Even there, Denise Livingston names teamwork as the main key factor, with aligned goals between office staff, rig crews, operator, and service company, and reduces the lesson to one instruction: plan, plan, plan. The British Columbia Energy Regulator sequence sets the kickoff point roughly 500 ft above the planned horizontal leg and budgets just under a quarter mile from kickoff to horizontal using a downhole motor carrying measurement-while-drilling instruments [qSA8EoDzhKo @ 00:01:26].

Lateral length is the loudest split between the legacy playbook and the current one. Chesapeake's Marcellus method drills horizontally for more than 4,000 ft past formation entry, then cements production casing to roughly 2,500 ft above the producing shale or all the way to surface depending on regional geology [7tXxUXv528A @ 00:04:00]. Range Resources prices the payoff of that older geometry: its 4,000-ft laterals, each wellbore running down a mile and out about half a mile, total around 11 to 12 billion cubic feet equivalent, enough for one pad to fuel more than a quarter million homes over the next decade [8M30VzyjEpk @ 00:45:02]. Mark Roach of FutureWise Energy reports the Utica pushing far past that: average laterals roughly doubled since 2017, nearly nine of ten 2025 wells exceeded 12,500 ft, and Ascent Resources averaged just over 16,000 ft, putting more rock behind every set of fixed costs, though Roach notes they also demand serious planning for surface rights and infrastructure [nbj7-lPJ59w @ 00:11:46]. Expand Energy frames the same lever at the land level: a 1,280-acre tract that might need 32 vertical wells on 32 pads can be drained from one multi-well horizontal pad with 90 percent less surface disturbance [zhTs0NrEVgE @ 00:05:09].

Chevron's Permian team broke from the CO2 playbook on enhanced recovery. Lab studies and a Midland Basin field pilot proved CO2 huff-and-puff in horizontal shale wells improved long-term recovery, but high CO2 retention in the reservoir, the cost of the commodity, and pipelines missing from much of the basin pushed the team to compress and reinject its own produced hydrocarbon gas instead [r0aIro_ccSI @ 00:07:11]. Dvesh Kumar's group also rejects soak time, finding that adding a day or a week never changed the response materially, so Chevron treats soak time as optional rather than a value lever [r0aIro_ccSI @ 00:52:31]. Energy Window Insights draws a harder line inside the depleted-reservoir menu: single-phase MPD holds bottom-hole pressure nearly constant with surface back pressure, but back pressure only adds pressure and cannot remove it, and MPD is not a well control method; underbalanced drilling flows the reservoir on purpose to cut damage, losses, and differential sticking, but forfeits its benefits the moment underbalance is lost [aY0LJMEZzZo @ 00:01:18]. On target screening, the Premier sample library team in Midland found four New Mexico wells that looked fairly similar on open-hole logs, yet archived cuttings sorted them into two great, one marginal, and one poor [KynEhshipEg @ 00:07:34].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

Misreading the rock is the oldest and costliest trap in these plays. Range Resources' Renns No. 1 well in Mount Pleasant Township, Washington County was drilled as a structural test of the Westland Dome to 8,470 ft; millions spent, nothing commercial, site restored, the well slated to be plugged and abandoned as a write-off [oSSTf7vpnpU @ 00:02:51]. Geologist Bill Zagorski argued for one more attempt, and the October 2004 frac of the Marcellus and Skaneateles interval made about 300,000 cubic feet a day from a vertical hole with a small treatment. GeoScope adds the flip side: clay-rich shale deforms, absorbs the pumped energy, and closes when pressure drops, so an operator can spend a fortune and get nothing back [oSSTf7vpnpU @ 00:11:14]. GeoFins reports that Wolfberry wells drilled since the 1980s passed straight through the Wolfcamp, treating it as one ingredient in a stacked vertical completion rather than a target in its own right [xL3_8adbQ4Q @ 00:02:01]. The data trap compounds it. A Permian geologist on Hart Energy's Premier segment warns that collecting samples while drilling is the one time you can get actual rock data from a well you drill, and skipping sample capture means never having it [KynEhshipEg @ 00:05:06].

Inside the wellbore, the failures are quieter. Chevron's five-year Permian pilot found intermediate casing pressure climbing during injection even though that string takes no gas directly, driven by heat and conduction, and warns that unmonitored it can lead to casing failure [r0aIro_ccSI @ 00:18:31]. Compressor downtime was the other early bleed: every shutdown dropped reservoir pressure and burned project time rebuilding it, which is why runtime became a success metric [r0aIro_ccSI @ 00:19:46]. Without daily well tests in the first days of flowback, the oil spike that carries the uplift is easy to miss; Kumar calls high-frequency early testing incredibly important despite how basic it looks. Offshore Engineering Academy frames the drilling version: if returning mud does not match pumped volume, the hole is either losing expensive fluid to fractures or taking a kick of pressurized gas, both stop-everything emergencies, and annular volume math of hole diameter squared minus pipe diameter squared divided by 1029.4 is the first line of defense against a blowout [YPFuHij-FYk @ 00:03:48]. Swap to bigger pipe and annular velocity rises, cleaning the hole but raising friction pressure enough to fracture weak rock; widen the annulus and cuttings settle and pack off the well [YPFuHij-FYk @ 00:05:42].

The economics checks are blunt. Mark Roach traces Chesapeake from leasing well over a million net acres in eastern Ohio to a 2014-2016 crash in which oil fell from around $100 to the $20s and $30s, the US rig count dropped roughly 80 percent, and over-levered players came under real stress [nbj7-lPJ59w @ 00:06:28]. Chesapeake sold its Ohio Utica assets to Encino in 2018 for about $2 billion; EOG then paid $5.6 billion in 2025 to buy Encino itself, picking up roughly 675,000 net core acres. Roach flags the remaining ceilings: dry gas economics hinge on Henry Hub, ultra-long laterals require stitching surface rights across multiple parcels that slows permitting, and deep Utica wells at 10,000 to 12,000 ft are still high-cost appraisal [nbj7-lPJ59w @ 00:27:21]. Chevron's pilot shows depletion catching up with enhanced recovery: gas utility factor improved through year three as cycles increased, then ticked up in years four and five despite even more cycles, marking the point where the physics of a depleting reservoir takes over [r0aIro_ccSI @ 00:27:31]. And shutting in a well for flush production is not recovery at all; it only trades energy in time, while gas injection leaves a permanent wedge [r0aIro_ccSI @ 00:47:27].
