## 1. Build a source hierarchy that distinguishes ownership from operating signals  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with five explicit queues, not a universal parcel scraper. Severed-title acquisitions need county conveyances and tract interpretation; NPRI cash-flow purchases need the creating instrument, lease terms, and payment evidence; pooling-docket curve-jumping needs applications, hearing changes, and signed orders; owned-asset royalty recovery needs statements and production comparisons; institutional flips need transferable evidence and clean provenance. These are the chapter's five operating mappings, not five extra revenue streams. Assign the full-time principal to pricing, seller decisions, and capital allocation. Use three contractors for land/title research, petroleum and production analysis, and data/reconciliation work, with counsel engaged within the land/title scope when interpretation requires it. Contractor counts describe functional coverage, not three full-time salaries. Restrict the initial footprint to one basin and a manageable county set. A recorded instrument can establish a link in ownership but may omit earlier reservations; a tax roll can identify a mailing lead but cannot establish the mineral estate. An operator's division order can support payment reconciliation without curing title. Encode these distinctions in source types so a mailing address, production observation, and ownership conclusion never share the same confidence label. Every queued opportunity needs a named reviewer and a next evidence request.

Acquire evidence from the most authoritative source available for the question being answered. For Texas, county clerk records supply deeds and assignments, while Railroad Commission systems supply well, lease, completion, and reported production records. Texas production often arrives at lease level, so a lease total cannot automatically become one horizontal well's revenue forecast. Oklahoma Corporation Commission imaging and electronic case records support separate operating and pooling investigations; an application is neither a final order nor proof that the target interest participates. New Mexico OCD well files and hearing materials require the same distinction between requested relief and executed development. BLM MLRS adds federal land-status and case context, while ONRR public datasets provide aggregate revenue context rather than an individual's royalty ledger. Commercial products shorten the search path, but licensing, coverage, export rights, and historical depth determine usefulness. Ask each vendor for a trial using twenty already-researched tracts and a written description of correction handling. Measure missed wells, duplicated laterals, stale operator names, and title documents that cannot be reproduced. Prefer a narrower feed with traceable timestamps over broad coverage whose fields conceal their origin. Keep the trial findings alongside the purchase decision.

Treat the directory's latency descriptions as source characteristics and desk polling choices, not promised publication schedules. Before enabling a connector, record its actual access terms, permitted automation, advertised limits, export format, and responsible contact. Public search access does not imply unrestricted bulk retrieval, and a free portal does not make certified copies free. When a documented API exists, use the licensed endpoint and the provider's current authentication instructions; otherwise use an authorized export or a human download rather than reverse-engineering private application calls. Set one worker per host until documented limits justify more, honor Retry-After, apply bounded exponential backoff, and cache immutable instrument images by content digest. Poll active dockets each business day where permitted, but refresh monthly production after each observed reporting release and revisit revisions. Capture both the reporting period and retrieval time. A portal outage should create a visible stale-source flag, not silently extend yesterday's values into today's underwriting. Maintain a county clerk request path and a regulator records contact before depending on any feed. This modest discipline gives a four-person pod durable coverage without turning retrieval failures into speculative acquisition signals or spending contractor hours repairing avoidable duplicate downloads.

## 2. Turn mixed records into a tract-centered, auditable enrichment waterfall  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a tract-centered model with separate entities for instruments, parties, interests, wells, units, leases, docket events, and payments. Store the source's identifier unchanged alongside a normalized search key. API well identifiers must remain strings so leading zeroes survive; preserve sidetrack and completion suffixes where supplied instead of collapsing every record to one borehole. Texas district-and-lease identifiers belong in their own namespace. County instrument numbers require county and recording jurisdiction, because the same number can recur elsewhere. Represent an interest as a dated assertion linked to supporting instruments, not a permanent percentage attached to a person's name. Separate mineral ownership, executive rights, lease royalty, NPRI burden, depth limitations, and tract participation. For illustration, forty gross acres multiplied by a one-quarter mineral ownership fraction equals ten NMA; it does not determine the royalty decimal without lease and allocation terms. An NPRI can be fixed or floating depending on the instrument and governing interpretation, so a single generic royalty multiplier is unsafe. Keep geometry provenance and coordinate reference systems explicit. A wellbore crossing a polygon supports a spatial investigation, not a conclusion about unit allocation, effective pooling, or the seller's transferable interest. Require a reviewer to resolve those relationships.

Build the enrichment waterfall around increasing evidence cost. First normalize a seller-supplied legal description and search the county index for the creating and latest relevant instruments. Second connect the tract to regulator wells, leases, and units using identifiers and documented spatial relationships. Third retrieve docket materials for pending activity and distinguish application, continuance, hearing, and signed-order states. Fourth enrich with licensed production history and commercial interpretations only where those fields reduce a specific underwriting uncertainty. Fifth request seller statements, division orders, leases, and tax documents to reconcile actual ownership and cash receipts. Stop paid enrichment when a fatal reservation or excluded depth makes the target unbuyable. Maintain name variants, trust names, estates, and entity successors as candidate links until instruments support consolidation. An assessor mailing address is a contact clue, not proof that the addressee owns minerals. Use official entity records and seller confirmation before relying on a business-name match. Avoid broad personal-data purchases when a narrow county search answers the question. Log each match's basis, competing candidates, reviewer, and unresolved conflict. The principal should see the next cheapest decisive evidence request, not a decorative confidence percentage generated from loosely matched names.

Implement a small append-only evidence store before adding orchestration software. Python's urllib.request, csv, json, sqlite3, hashlib, decimal, and logging modules cover authorized HTTP retrieval, structured exports, local indexing, checksums, money arithmetic, and run records. Keep original files outside the working tables, store their SHA-256 digests, and record source URL, retrieval time, document date, reporting period, parser version, and access entitlement. PDF image extraction may require a separately installed desktop OCR product; its output remains a searchable derivative, never the controlling deed text. Save corrections as new observations with links to the superseded record. Make ingestion idempotent using source identity plus document digest or a documented observation key, not filename alone. Test fixtures should include duplicate downloads, revised production, missing pages, leading-zero identifiers, and one deliberately conflicting ownership chain. Export a narrow review workbook for the land contractor rather than requiring everyone to operate the database. Back up the evidence store and test restoration to a separate directory. The data contractor owns failed imports; the land contractor owns title exceptions; the production contractor owns allocation and decline questions. This division prevents software repairs from silently becoming substantive ownership decisions during a rushed bid.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Register the source and permitted retrieval route
   - Record access terms, cost, source owner, refresh behavior, and fallback contact before the first recurring import.
   - For a portal without documented automation rights, schedule a human export and retain its retrieval metadata.
2. Capture originals before parsing derivatives
   - Save the source file, compute its digest, and attach document and reporting dates before extracting fields.
   - Keep OCR text, normalized identifiers, and interpreted interests linked to the original rather than replacing it.
3. Route conflicts to the responsible specialist
   - Run identifier, geometry, ownership, volume, and payment checks; create a named exception for each material conflict.
   - The principal sees unresolved bid blockers separately from routine import repairs and harmless formatting differences.
4. Publish a dated decision packet
   - Export the interest calculation, source index, current event state, cash model, and unresolved conditions for review.
   - Freeze the packet used for each bid so later source revisions do not rewrite the original decision record.

## 3. Diagnose false signals before converting observations into bids or recovery claims  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The highest-value diagnostic is a contradiction queue. Compare new production against the prior reported vintage, not just against the previous month, because amended reports can create false growth. Flag zero-to-positive transitions, sharp volume changes, operator changes, and new completion filings for review, while distinguishing shut-ins, workovers, meter adjustments, and first sales. Keep oil, gas, and water units separate; do not add barrels to Mcf or treat a gas conversion convention as a contractual payment factor. A benchmark price from EIA is context, not the operator's realized sale price after quality, location, and contract effects. For pooling-docket curve-jumping, monitor the tract's relationship to requested spacing and pooling relief, applicant identity, notice evidence, hearing disposition, order terms, and subsequent development. Receiving a docket alert creates a research task, not proof of imminent cash flow. If a filed exhibit and a commercial map disagree, retain both and resolve the underlying survey or legal-description issue. Use the land contractor for ownership and order interpretation, the production contractor for timing and allocation, and the principal for the bid ceiling. That separation stops optimistic event narratives from flowing directly into price assumptions without a reviewed evidentiary bridge.

Royalty reconciliation starts with the asset's documented payment decimal and statement structure. In a simplified illustration with uniform unit treatment, ten NMA inside a 640-acre unit under a one-fifth lease royalty produces a 0.003125 decimal before other burdens or contractual adjustments. If the applicable sales volume is 10,000 barrels at $70 per barrel, multiplying $700,000 by that decimal yields $2,187.50 before applicable deductions and taxes. This is an interest-math illustration, not an addition to the canonical revenue cases. Actual allocation can differ for horizontal wells, tract factors, effective dates, and NPRI language. Match statement lines by property, product, production month, sale month, and adjustment references. Separate gross value, deductions, withholding, suspense releases, and prior-period corrections so a larger check does not masquerade as improved reservoir performance. Under Texas Natural Resources Code Section 91.402, payment timing and withholding conditions require fact-specific review; a delayed payment is not automatically wrongful. Prepare a recovery inquiry with statement extracts, supporting instruments, a decimal calculation, and the precise disputed period. Log the operator's response and any corrected payment. Count recovered receipts only once, when recognized under the desk's consistent accounting policy, rather than treating the demand amount as earned revenue.

Instrument and spatial diagnostics matter as much as payment arithmetic. Run duplicate-document checks, page-count comparisons, and OCR spot checks on fractions, exceptions, depth clauses, and exhibit references. A scanned one-eighth can be misread as one-third, and an omitted exhibit can remove the only precise tract description. QGIS can reveal invalid polygons, coordinate-system mismatches, or lateral paths that appear shifted after an incorrect transformation; it cannot interpret a reservation or resolve conflicting surveys. Validate intersections against recorded descriptions and appropriate survey evidence. For institutional flips, create a buyer-facing source index that identifies what the buyer can independently retrieve and what requires a separate license. Do not redistribute commercial datasets merely because the desk purchased access. Keep seller tax documents, bank details, and personal identifiers out of general review exports; use restricted storage and share only material needed for the transaction. Apply the same restraint to contractor access. Before outreach or data-room publication, have the principal confirm recipients and the intended disclosure scope. A clean packet should expose unresolved questions plainly, not bury them behind composite scores. The objective is a defensible interest and a reproducible cash explanation, not a visually persuasive map with unsupported ownership labels.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before a bid or buyer packet leaves the desk**
> 
> Require named reviewers and dated evidence for each check; unresolved material exceptions remain visible in the decision packet.
> - Verify the seller's transferable interest against the instrument chain, including reservations, depth limits, executive rights, and any probate or entity authority issue.
> - Verify the well-to-tract and unit relationship; document whether production is reported by well, completion, lease, or another aggregation level.
> - Verify payment arithmetic against actual statements and supported decimals; distinguish suspense releases and realized gains from recurring royalty receipts.
> - Verify source freshness, redistribution rights, recipient authorization, and the packet's treatment of personal identifiers before sending it externally.

## 4. Fund the stack from canonical economics and operate a four-person exception desk  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The scorecard is the operating boundary, not a target to rewrite with optimistic automation savings. Conservative revenue is $30,000 at a 20% net margin, producing $6,000 annual owner profit and implying $24,000 of total annual expenses. Base revenue is $215,000 at 32.5%, producing $69,875 and implying $145,125 of expenses. Scaled revenue is $400,000 at 45%, producing $180,000 and implying $220,000 of expenses. Conservative and Base use 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C. Do not subtract a new software budget from these profits and still claim the same canonical scenario. Instead allocate any proposed subscription inside its existing expense envelope or show a separately labeled sensitivity. For example, a $6,000 annual data package would consume one-quarter of the Conservative expense envelope before title work, administration, or other costs. A free-source pilot is therefore a sensible starting posture, not an assertion that diligence is free. The stated $1,000–$12,000 monthly opex range is a planning band, not a promise that every scenario's total annual expenses equal twelve times recurring overhead. Transaction-specific costs and accounting classifications still need explicit treatment in the desk ledger.

Keep capital deployment, cash proceeds, and recognized revenue in separate views. The stated $150,000–$2,000,000 startup capital range primarily describes the ability to buy and hold rights while research and payment cycles unfold; it is not operator revenue. In an illustrative flip, an interest purchased for $100,000 and sold for $130,000 produces $130,000 of sale proceeds, including $100,000 of returned basis and a $30,000 realized gain before separately modeled transaction costs. Only the gain belongs in the scorecard's rights-trading revenue component, with expense treatment kept consistent. Record royalty receipts separately and prevent a recovered payment from appearing in both ordinary receipts and a second recovery line. For institutional flips, preserve a basis schedule by acquired interest and track partial disposals so the remaining asset does not inherit an arbitrary zero basis. Never value the desk by the underlying operator's gross hydrocarbon sales or the buyer's total portfolio capital. The six-to-twenty-four-month first-deal window also means a promising research pipeline can coexist with limited near-term receipts. Track cash runway, committed purchase capital, research spend, and delayed payments alongside the profit model. Automation reduces clerical friction but cannot eliminate commodity exposure, depletion, or title disputes.

Run a weekly evidence meeting with one page per live opportunity and a strict exception agenda. The data contractor reports failed imports, stale sources, and revised records; the land contractor reports ownership breaks and docket changes; the production contractor reports volume, allocation, and decline concerns; the principal resolves spend and bid decisions. Measure instrument retrieval time, reviewed matches per paid hour, material exceptions caught before bidding, statement lines reconciled, and buyer questions answered from existing evidence. Avoid optimizing raw lead count when title research is the bottleneck. Promote a paid feed only after a basin-specific pilot demonstrates saved review hours or a documented reduction in costly misses, with both effects evaluated inside the canonical expense envelope. Recheck access terms and source coverage at renewal. If raising pooled capital, evaluate the proposed structure against securities definitions and applicable offering requirements before soliciting investors; fractional mineral interests and investment contracts require careful analysis under 15 U.S.C. Section 77b(a)(1). Texas recording priorities under Property Code Section 13.001 and federal communitization under 30 U.S.C. Section 226(m) likewise belong in targeted transaction review, not generic software labels. The winning small desk maintains a shorter distance between source evidence, specialist judgment, and an economically disciplined decision.
