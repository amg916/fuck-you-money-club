## 1. Ring-fence liquidity before pricing the first mineral deed  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with a capital ledger, not an acreage target. At $150,000, place $90,000 in a separately tracked working-capital reserve, allocate $30,000 to rights purchases, $7,500 to title and technical diligence, $5,000 to contingent options, $4,000 to entity and insurance setup, $3,500 to data and systems, and $10,000 to transaction contingencies. These amounts total $150,000; the reserve is 60% of opening capital. At $2,000,000, allocate $1,100,000 to reserve, $650,000 to rights, $80,000 to diligence, $40,000 to options, $30,000 to setup, $20,000 to systems, and $80,000 to contingencies. That reserve is 55%. Neither reserve is acquisition capacity, collateral for an option, or a second name for committed purchase money. Release acquisition funds only after recording all outstanding exercises, title invoices, and closing costs. At the stated overhead endpoints, twenty-four months consumes $24,000 or $288,000, leaving $66,000 or $812,000 of the original reserve before receipts. Those runway calculations exclude deal-specific expenses, which belong in their own allocations. If overhead rises, reduce the acquisition envelope before signing another commitment. The 6–24-month first-deal window makes liquidity an operating input, not idle money awaiting a persuasive seller.

Reconcile the scorecard before evaluating any software proposal. Conservative revenue of $30,000 less $24,000 of total expenses produces $6,000 of owner profit, exactly 20%. Base revenue of $215,000 less $145,125 produces $69,875, exactly 32.5%. Scaled revenue of $400,000 less $220,000 produces $180,000, exactly 45%. One illustrative expense bridge assigns monthly fixed overhead of $1,000, $4,000, and $12,000 respectively; annual fixed costs are $12,000, $48,000, and $144,000, leaving $12,000, $97,125, and $76,000 for all remaining recognized expenses. These are allocation constraints, not estimates of what every county or contractor will charge. Do not add acquisition principal to operating expense and then also deduct it from trading proceeds. Track asset basis, capitalized acquisition costs, depletion, disposition proceeds, and recurring expenses separately under the adopted accounting method. The teams remain 1 FT plus 3 contractors in Conservative and Base, and 2 FT plus 4 contractors in Scaled. Contractor counts describe scoped specialists, not four continuous salaries. Treat the owner as one full-time operator; any additional salary or benefit expense must fit the existing expense envelope. Personal income taxes and owner distributions require separate cash planning.

Build the ownership and payment structure before circulating offers. Use the entity's exact legal name consistently on purchase agreements, deeds, tax forms, bank instructions, and operator ownership-transfer packets. A state-filed limited liability company can simplify administration, but it does not repair a broken mineral chain or change an NPRI into executive rights. Obtain an EIN, adopt signing authority, maintain a distinct ledger, and reconcile the operating account monthly. Mercury's Standard account can serve as a low-fee interface; examine the actual partner-bank and sweep arrangements rather than treating the interface as the insured bank. Aggregate deposits by ownership category and underlying bank when reviewing FDIC coverage. Bind coverage for actual activities: general liability for field visits, cyber and social-engineering exposures where available, and professional coverage only where the business performs services that require it. Ask specifically whether mineral title, contract disputes, pollution, and wire fraud are excluded. Do not assume a surface title policy insures severed minerals. Obtain closing instructions through an independently verified telephone number, use transaction counsel or an appropriate escrow provider, and prohibit emailed bank-detail changes from becoming payment authority without a callback.

## 2. Assemble twelve inexpensive layers around an auditable tract ledger  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Make the tract ledger the system of record. Each asset needs a stable identifier, state and county, legal description, gross acres, acquired fraction, net mineral acres, interest type, lease references, unit allocation, operator, well identifiers, payment decimal, acquisition basis, and document links. Keep NMA distinct from royalty-adjusted acreage; a marketing convention using an eighth royalty must never overwrite the actual deed fraction. Store the original instrument alongside a structured extraction and a reviewer decision. Python and SQLite are sufficient for a small portfolio when backups, validation, and access restrictions are real. Use decimal arithmetic for money and ownership fractions, retain source precision, and round only at a documented reporting boundary. QGIS connects tract polygons to units, wells, surface constraints, and substations without an enterprise mapping contract. A spatial intersection is a research lead, not a title conclusion. County index gaps, duplicate names, ambiguous aliquot descriptions, and stale unit polygons require human resolution. Keep ownership assertions separate from machine-derived geometry so an attractive map cannot silently become an approved purchase premise. Export the ledger and its document manifest monthly so switching vendors never requires rebuilding the title record.

Use the twelve-layer matrix as a procurement baseline rather than a claim of live vendor quotations. The paid prices are explicit planning assumptions for named tiers, excluding taxes, promotions, optional services, and future changes; confirm vendor checkout terms before purchase. One Google Workspace Business Starter seat at $7 per month on an annual commitment and one DocuSign Personal subscription at $10 monthly equivalent imply $204 in annual subscription commitments. At the listed S3 Standard storage rate, twenty gigabytes adds $0.46 monthly before requests, transfer, or version accumulation. That makes the basic subscription-and-storage subtotal $17.46 per month, not the whole operating budget. County copies, counsel, landmen, insurance, bookkeeping labor, and technical review still consume the $1,000–$12,000 monthly envelope or separately budgeted deal costs. Avoid buying commercial production datasets until public records demonstrably fail a defined task. Test ten representative wells and three complete ownership files before upgrading. Measure missing months, mismatched identifiers, and reviewer hours, then compare the measured correction cost with an actual subscription proposal. A cheap dataset that creates expensive false positives is not a lean purchase.

Automate ingestion and exception detection, not ownership judgments. Download permitted public production files on a documented cadence, save retrieval dates and source hashes, and preserve the original royalty statement before normalizing its rows. Reconcile operator checks by production month, product, property, decimal, price, taxes, deductions, and suspense adjustments. Compare reported volumes with public records only after accounting for reporting delays, unit aggregation, gas measurement conventions, and corrections. Flag a decimal change immediately; flag a missing check against contractual timing and the jurisdiction's payment rules rather than an arbitrary calendar date. Texas Natural Resources Code Section 91.402 supplies specific payment timing and withholding provisions for covered proceeds. A dashboard should show the disputed amount, evidence, owner, and next action instead of merely coloring a cell red. Restrict bank access to the operator and authorized backup, require multifactor authentication, and keep tax identifiers out of source repositories. Encrypt offline backups, restrict document storage, and test restoration quarterly. Have the contract landman inspect title exceptions, the petroleum specialist inspect production assumptions, and the accountant inspect cash-to-ledger differences. One accountable owner closes each exception before the next acquisition relies on the same data.

## 3. Budget five operating plays without manufacturing revenue or secured power  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The first two mapped plays are producing-mineral purchases and NPRI purchases; these are descriptive workflows, not additional scorecard scenarios. For producing minerals, assume ten NMA uniformly participate in a 640-acre unit under a 20% lease royalty, with no allocation adjustments. The modeled revenue decimal is 10 divided by 640 times 0.20, or 0.003125. Applied to $80,000 of monthly unit sales value, that produces $250 before owner-level deductions and taxes. A $30,000 purchase therefore shows $3,000 annualized receipts and a 10% initial cash yield, not a 10% profit margin or a perpetual annuity. Stress price, volumes, deductions, and depletion separately. For an NPRI burdening an 80-acre tract within that same unit, a fixed 1/64 fraction of production produces $156.25 monthly, while a floating one-quarter of a 20% lease royalty produces $500. Those calculations assume uniform tract participation and expose why deed interpretation dominates spreadsheet precision. An $8,000 offer cannot rely on the larger result until title review supports it. If production economics fail, lower the price or pass; if NPRI meaning remains disputed, require curative evidence or withdraw rather than pricing ambiguity as assured upside.

The third play, pooling-docket curve-jumping, buys or options an interest because a public proceeding may improve buyer attention, not because a hearing guarantees drilling. Read the actual application, spacing description, requested relief, notice record, and subsequent orders. An illustrative transaction spends $2,000 on an option, $3,000 on eligible acquisition diligence, and $25,000 on exercise, producing $30,000 of assumed basis. A $40,000 sale with $2,000 of selling costs yields $8,000 of realized gain and returns $30,000 of basis; the $40,000 proceeds are not all operator revenue. If the proceeding slips or the title cannot close, let the option expire and cap the sunk exposure at the amounts actually committed. The fourth play, institutional rights flipping, needs a buyer-quality package before acquisition: indexed instruments, tract schedules, lease terms, payment history, and a reproducible ownership decimal. Purchasing rights for $100,000, adding $10,000 of eligible basis costs, and selling for $145,000 with $5,000 selling costs creates $30,000 of realized gain and returns $110,000 of basis. If the buyer disappears, retain only assets whose stressed royalty economics independently justify the purchase. Otherwise reduce exposure before closing, rather than treating an indicative bid as cash.

The fifth mapped play is ferc_grid_interconnection_queue_land_speculation, the TRACK_1 FERC / ISO Substation Interconnection Queue & Powered-Land Option Flipping workflow. Overlay the correct ISO or utility queue with substations, parcels, zoning, floodplain, access, and transmission constraints. Distinguish generator interconnection from the load-service process relevant to an industrial user. Queue position, study progress, and nearby infrastructure are signals; none conveys deliverable power, surface control, easements, or transferable interconnection rights. Assume a $5,000 assignable option on 45 acres with a $225,000 exercise price, plus $4,000 of studies. A permitted $15,000 option assignment produces an illustrative $6,000 gain if the $9,000 expenditure is properly included in basis and there are no further selling costs. The $225,000 exercise obligation remains outside that assignment calculation and must never be silently funded from protected reserves. Counsel must confirm assignability and the accounting treatment. Under 18 CFR 35.28, applicable tariffs and interconnection procedures govern relevant service processes; a land marketer cannot replace them with promotional language. If access, service feasibility, or assignment fails, let the contingent option lapse with $9,000 exposure, or reposition only for independently viable uses without claiming secured power.

## 4. Rehearse closing, collection, and failure before launching acquisition outreach  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a dry closing on one representative tract before scaling outreach. Start with the seller's claimed interest and trace every required conveyance, reservation, probate event, and entity succession through the chosen title standard. Texas Property Code Section 5.021 addresses written conveyances of covered estates; Section 13.001 addresses the effect of recording against specified subsequent parties. Neither a recorded deed nor a county search result independently proves that the seller owns the fraction advertised. Ask transaction counsel to identify exceptions, required curative instruments, acknowledgment requirements, and county recording practices. Compare the resulting ownership fraction with the lease, unit documents, division order, and most recent check detail. Confirm that any executive rights, bonus rights, delay rentals, and development rights are actually included rather than assumed from the word minerals. Assemble a closing packet containing the purchase agreement, deed, recording instructions, settlement statement, identity and authority evidence, tax documentation, and operator notification materials. Rehearse the failed-close branch as carefully as the successful branch: return unused escrow under the contract, terminate outstanding permissions, calendar option expiry, and preserve the reasons for rejection so the same defective interest does not reenter the pipeline under another seller.

Then rehearse the first ninety days after funding without assuming the first check arrives immediately. Record the instrument, retain the recording confirmation, submit the operator's transfer package, and track requests for additional documentation. Division-order processing can delay payments, while disputed ownership can create suspense balances that remain unavailable for overhead. Establish a property-level receivable log showing production periods, expected decimal, paid amounts, deductions, and unresolved differences. Use the applicable lease and state payment rules when escalating a missing payment. For Texas assets, evaluate the circumstances against Section 91.402 rather than promising one universal check date. Have the accountant maintain acquisition basis and depletion schedules under the applicable tax rules, including 26 U.S.C. 611 and 612, instead of applying an automatic deduction percentage to every receipt. Track state severance withholding, property assessments where applicable, and filing obligations by jurisdiction. Do not net an operator's entire sales statement into business revenue when most production belongs to other interests. Post only the owned interest's royalty receipts and realized rights gains on the scorecard basis. A reconciliation is complete only when the bank deposit, statement detail, and ledger entry agree or a documented exception has an accountable owner.

Finally, establish a release decision that can survive an absent founder and a bad market. Give each specialist a written scope: the landman assembles evidence, counsel resolves legal ownership questions, the technical reviewer tests decline and allocation assumptions, and the accountant verifies treatment and reconciliation. At smaller scale, these are scoped contractor assignments rather than permanent departments. Fund the actual scopes inside the canonical expense limits before representing the team as operational. Require a second-person check of outgoing wire instructions and ownership decimals; retain the review record with the deal. Test whether the portfolio survives six months without a new sale, a 30% commodity-price reduction, and a production decline that the technical reviewer considers plausible for the assets. These are sensitivity tests, not replacements for the three canonical profit cases. If raising pooled capital, resolve the offering structure before soliciting investors: 15 U.S.C. 77b includes specified fractional mineral interests in its securities definition, and Section 77e governs registration requirements subject to applicable exemptions. Keep subscriber money distinct from operator revenue. Launch only after restoring a backup, reconciling a sample check, completing the title packet, and demonstrating that every outstanding commitment fits outside the protected opening reserve.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the reserve and commitment register
   - Separate reserve cash from acquisition availability and enter every signed obligation.
   - Use the $150,000 or $2,000,000 allocation model as a starting envelope; retain at least half of opening capital as protected working-capital liquidity.
2. Complete one evidence-grade tract file
   - Resolve seller authority, ownership fractions, lease rights, unit allocation, and recording requirements.
   - Link original instruments to the tract ledger and obtain named specialist review rather than treating a geographic overlay as ownership evidence.
3. Rehearse payment and collection end to end
   - Verify wire instructions, simulate the settlement entry, and reconcile one sample royalty payment.
   - Show returned basis separately from realized gain and preserve statement-level deductions, production periods, tax withholding, and unresolved suspense amounts.
4. Release only the funded acquisition scope
   - Restore the backup, confirm specialist capacity, and sign the dated launch checklist.
   - Keep unresolved title, utility-service, offering-structure, and liquidity issues outside the launch scope until the responsible reviewer supplies the missing evidence.
