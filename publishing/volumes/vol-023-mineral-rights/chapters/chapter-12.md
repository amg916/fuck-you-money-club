## 1. Days 1–7: Define Five Plays, Fund the Pod, and Freeze the Denominators  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Launch one county and one producing formation, not a national mineral-buying campaign. For this chapter, the five mapped operator plays are P1, producing net mineral acre acquisitions; P2, nonparticipating royalty interest acquisitions; P3, pooling-docket curve-jumping; P4, reconciliation of royalties on owned interests; and P5, institutional flips. These are operating lanes within Candidate #7, not five independent businesses requiring separate staff. The full-time principal owns sourcing, cash authorization, underwriting version control, and buyer relationships. Three fractional contractors provide land and title research, reservoir or production analysis, and jurisdiction-specific legal review. Give each contractor a bounded deliverable rather than an unlimited research mandate. P1 and P2 establish what is being purchased; P3 changes the timing hypothesis; P4 tests whether ownership becomes collectible cash; P5 tests whether another buyer values the same evidence. Start with Oklahoma if pooling orders are central, using Oklahoma Corporation Commission records and the relevant county clerk. Do not transfer Oklahoma election mechanics into a Texas file. By Day 1, publish one responsibility sheet identifying the decision owner, authorized spend, required evidence, and escalation path for every lane. That sheet keeps a four-person pod from becoming four disconnected research queues.

On Days 2 and 3, separate the acquisition account from the operating account and impose a $6,000 first-month cash-burn ceiling. An illustrative allocation buys fifteen landman hours at $150, eight engineering hours at $175, four attorney hours at $250, $450 of records and data, $150 of outreach, and $750 of contingency: exactly $6,000. These are purchasing assumptions, not market quotations. The principal contributes the full-time seat; this launch budget excludes their draw and does not redefine annual owner profit. Four legal hours buy issue spotting and document scoping, not an unlimited title opinion. Any closing-specific work needs a separate written estimate before commitment. Candidate #7 requires $15,000–$75,000 of startup capital and has a stated $1,000–$12,000 monthly operating range; the sprint ceiling is a chosen control inside that range. After any purchase, retain six months of the approved operating budget in liquid cash, initially $36,000. Limit the first acquisition to 25% of committed equity, including its closing costs. Unfunded investor conversations do not count as committed equity. Maintain an additional asset-specific reserve for documented taxes, title work, and transaction obligations rather than spending the operating reserve twice.

Days 4 through 7 establish measurement before outreach creates a flattering pipeline. A screened tract is a unique legal description with a documented owner hypothesis, interest type, source date, and target formation. A verified file has every critical ownership, burden, acreage, and payment field supported, plus at least 90% completion of the remaining evidence checklist. Duplicate owners across mailing lists never create additional screened tracts. Separate gross acres, net mineral acres, royalty fractions, and decimal revenue interests in the database. An undivided 25% mineral interest across 160 gross acres equals 40 NMA. Under a simple uniform 640-acre unit with a 20% lease royalty, that interest would yield a 0.0125 revenue decimal: 40 divided by 640 multiplied by 0.20. Actual allocation can differ with tract participation, depth limitations, lease burdens, and horizontal allocation terms. An NPRI must be interpreted from its granting language rather than forced through this NMA calculation. Build these fields in Airtable or a controlled spreadsheet, attach instrument images, and assign immutable tract identifiers. End the week with twenty screened tracts, two contractor-reviewed examples, and a defect log that distinguishes missing evidence from evidence contradicting the seller.

## 2. Days 8–14: Turn County Evidence and Pooling Dockets into Priced Files  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Day 8 begins with the recorded chain, not a broker's acreage summary. Retrieve the mineral deed, reservations, relevant leases, probate instruments, assignments, and recorded releases needed to establish the proposed conveyance. The landman identifies gaps; the attorney determines the legal effect of ambiguous grants and state-specific curative requirements. Treat an NPRI described as a fraction of royalty differently from a fixed fraction of production, and document whether the holder has executive rights or other associated rights. On Day 9, request available division orders, check detail, tax statements, and lease amendments from an authorized seller. A payor's decimal is corroborating evidence, not a substitute for title. Under 52 O.S. § 570.10, Oklahoma production-proceeds timing and interest provisions make payment dates and title-related suspense material underwriting inputs. They do not make every suspended balance immediately collectible. Record the payor's stated suspense reason, the documents requested, and the person responsible for curing it. Files with unresolved critical ownership facts remain outside the verified count even when the seller supplies attractive check stubs. By Day 10, four files should support reproducible ownership calculations and a specific list of remaining defects.

Use Days 10 and 11 to test P3 without mistaking regulatory activity for cash flow. Oklahoma's forced-pooling framework under 52 O.S. § 87.1 makes the application, notice record, hearing, final order, election terms, and subsequent filings distinct evidence events. Track them separately in the docket register. The operative order determines the relevant election requirements; never substitute a remembered statewide deadline for the actual document. Match the applicant, section-township-range description, targeted formations, and respondents against the acquisition file. Then check operator history, nearby completions, production reporting, and the evidence for development timing. A hearing date is neither a drilling commitment nor proof that capital has been allocated. Curve-jumping means buying a correctly identified interest before the market fully incorporates documented development evidence, not claiming confidential knowledge of an operator's plans. Preserve the timestamp of each public observation so a later buyer can reconstruct the thesis. Ask the engineer to state what event would invalidate the production assumption: canceled development, inferior offset performance, changed spacing, or uneconomic commodity prices. If the interest works only with an immediate new well, classify it as development exposure and do not price it as an established producing royalty.

Days 12 through 14 produce an illustrative price discipline independent of the canonical business scorecard. Model existing production monthly, using lease-specific net pricing, reasonable decline, documented deductions, production taxes, and an explicit terminal assumption. For a training case, use $65 oil and $3 gas as reference prices, then stress $45 oil, $2 gas, a 20% volume reduction, and six months of payment delay. These are chosen sensitivities, not current quotations or forecasts. Discount stressed net asset receipts at 10% annually using monthly timing, and cap all-in acquisition cost at 80% of that present value. A $60,000 stressed present value therefore supports at most $48,000 of purchase price plus closing costs combined. Exclude speculative wells from this initial hurdle; display development upside separately. Also calculate portfolio operating coverage as stressed next-twelve-month royalty receipts divided by approved next-twelve-month operating cash costs. Require at least 1.25 times coverage before claiming the owned portfolio supports its operating platform. A pre-acquisition sprint instead relies on funded reserves and cannot claim that status. Finish the week with six verified files, two reproducible stressed models, and a ranked queue that explains why rejected interests failed.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Mineral-Desk Tactics That Save Real Diligence Dollars**
> 
> The best early advantage is preventing attractive-looking documents from becoming unsupported valuation assumptions.
> - Read NPRI granting language before using a broker's royalty-acre figure; fixed and floating fractions can create materially different receipts.
> - Save the actual pooling order beside its docket entry; calendar elections from the operative text and record who owns the response.
> - Compare production month with payment month before estimating decline; delayed checks can mimic falling production or exaggerate a rebound.
> - Ask institutional buyers for written county, depth, minimum-size, and title-package preferences before paying to assemble a portfolio they cannot purchase.

## 3. Days 15–21: Test Sellers, Reconcile Royalties, and Establish Buyer Depth  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Day 15, contact owners through a documented, narrowly targeted process using verified addresses and a plain description of the interest sought. Use a letter or individually reviewed email rather than buying scale before ownership data works. Commercial email must satisfy applicable requirements of 15 U.S.C. § 7704, including accurate routing information, required identification, a postal address, and an effective opt-out mechanism. Telephone campaigns introduce separate rules and should not be added casually to this sprint. Record delivered contacts, substantive responses, document permissions, and seller price expectations as separate fields. A response is substantive only when the owner confirms the interest, supplies relevant evidence, or discusses terms; automated replies do not count. On Day 16, compare asking prices with the stressed all-in ceiling before commissioning additional work. Any indication of interest should state the precise property scope, diligence conditions, cost assumptions, and expiration mechanics drafted for the transaction. Do not send an accidentally binding purchase commitment from a spreadsheet template. By Day 17, the attorney should categorize curative tasks by responsible party, expected expense, and dependency. Stop a file when the seller refuses necessary evidence or insists on an interest the recorded instruments do not support.

Days 18 and 19 test P4 with an owned interest or a seller-authorized diligence sample. The latter produces an underwriting finding, not operator revenue. Reconcile each payment line using property identifier, production month, product, reported sales volume, realized price, ownership decimal, deductions, taxes, and payment date. Maintain separate columns for gross well sales and the interest holder's receipts. For example, $80,000 of gross sales multiplied by a 0.0125 decimal produces $1,000 before attributable deductions and taxes; it does not produce $80,000 of operator revenue. Route differences exceeding the greater of $25 or 2% of expected net receipts into an exception queue. That threshold prioritizes research rather than waiving smaller contractual amounts. Categorize each difference as timing, decimal, pricing, deduction, tax, or missing production evidence. Use Python csv and decimal modules or a controlled spreadsheet to automate matching, while retaining original statements and human-reviewed exceptions. Production monitoring should alert on a 20% unexplained change across comparable months, not declare a reservoir problem from one late report. P4 earns its place by reducing leakage and improving asset knowledge; unresolved recovery claims stay outside receipts until actually collected under the chosen operating scorecard convention.

Days 20 and 21 test P5 before assuming an institutional exit. Obtain two written buyer responses specifying county, formation or depth, interest type, minimum package size, required title evidence, and valuation method. These responses establish buyer fit, not a committed bid. Share a redacted teaser first and release seller-authorized documents through a controlled data room only when their use is permitted. Calculate net sale economics after transaction expenses and distinguish returned basis from recognized gain. Buying an interest for $40,000, capitalizing $2,000 of acquisition costs, and selling for $50,000 with $3,000 of selling expenses produces $5,000 of realized gain under this simplified illustration. The $42,000 recovered investment is not revenue. Track selling expenses once rather than subtracting them again in the operating ledger. If investors will pool money, stop fundraising until counsel determines the instrument, offering path, intermediary obligations, and required filings. The security definition in 15 U.S.C. § 77b(a)(1) expressly addresses fractional undivided interests in oil, gas, or other mineral rights; an entity wrapper does not settle the analysis. This sprint assumes funded principal equity and direct asset acquisition, avoiding dependence on an unfinished offering to meet purchase or operating commitments.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the source packet
   - Save authorized instruments, statements, docket records, and production extracts under the immutable tract identifier.
   - Record retrieval dates, document versions, and permitted sharing scope before anyone changes the underwriting model.
2. Recompute the economic interest
   - Have the landman calculate ownership and the principal independently reproduce the revenue decimal.
   - Escalate conflicting grants, depth reservations, and fixed-versus-floating NPRI language to the attorney; unresolved critical fields block verified status.
3. Price only evidenced receipts
   - Apply documented deductions, decline, commodity sensitivities, and payment delays to monthly cash flows.
   - Require all-in cost at or below 80% of stressed present value and keep uncommitted development upside outside the initial purchase hurdle.
4. Record the next decision
   - Publish a dated Kill, Fix, or Scale determination with the responsible person and remaining cash authorization.
   - Archive rejected versions, keep one active model, and preserve the distinction between scaling research capacity and authorizing a purchase.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Days 60/90: Decide with Evidence, Cash, and Explicit Failure Thresholds  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Days 22 through 24 convert research into a decision packet: refresh title exceptions, rerun the stressed models, and verify buyer responses against the actual interests. Days 25 and 26 audit cash commitments and contractor output. On Day 27, rehearse a closing without sending money: check conveyance scope, recording sequence, payment instructions, and custody of executed documents. Independently confirm wire instructions using a previously verified contact channel. Days 28 and 29 challenge the strongest acquisition case and archive the evidence snapshot. Day 30 is a capital-allocation decision, not a mandatory transaction date. Candidate #7's expected first-deal interval is 3–9 months, so zero closings at Days 30, 60, or 90 is compatible with a successful launch. The Day-30 Scale threshold is forty screened tracts, ten verified files, three price-qualified interests, and two written buyer-fit responses, with operating burn no greater than $6,000. Price-qualified means documented seller terms fit the stressed all-in ceiling and critical title fields pass. Scaling at this stage authorizes another bounded research cycle, not larger offers or additional employees. Acquisition authorization still requires complete transaction-specific diligence and the separate concentration and reserve tests.

Use the matrix below as an illustrative operating policy, with the most severe applicable outcome controlling. Kill means stop spending on the affected tract, lane, or geography and document which scope has failed; it does not automatically mean liquidating good assets. Kill immediately for fabricated evidence, an incurable ownership defect, an operating-budget breach, or insufficient funded reserves. Kill the sourcing thesis at a checkpoint if at least ten verified files have been priced and none qualifies. Fix applies when no Kill condition exists but any Scale threshold is missed. Assign one measurable repair, seven calendar days, and at most $1,000 of incremental spending inside the existing monthly ceiling. Recheck once; a failed repair stops new spending on that lane pending a revised thesis. At Day 60, require seventy screened tracts, eighteen verified files, five qualified interests, and three buyer-fit responses, with cumulative burn at most $12,000. At Day 90, require one hundred, twenty-five, eight, and four respectively, with cumulative burn at most $18,000. Reconfirm six months of forward operating reserves at every checkpoint. If the ceiling is exhausted without passing evidence, pause rather than using acquisition capital to conceal an unproductive sourcing process.

Keep the canonical annual scorecard beside the sprint dashboard without turning either into the other. Conservative revenue is $30,000 with a 20% margin and $6,000 owner profit; Base revenue is $215,000 with a 32.5% margin and $69,875 owner profit; Scaled revenue is $400,000 with a 45% margin and $180,000 owner profit. These imply total annual expense envelopes of $24,000, $145,125, and $220,000 respectively, not three interchangeable monthly operating budgets. Revenue includes royalty receipts and realized rights-trading gains while excluding returned basis; underlying well sales, mineral purchase principal, and unrealized appreciation never fill a revenue shortfall. Conservative and Base use one full-time person plus three contractors. Scaled uses two full-time people plus four contractors, so a four-person launch pod cannot claim the six-person capacity merely by increasing its spreadsheet targets. Before adding staff, measure specialist hours per verified file, exception resolution time, and cash collected per owned interest. Require two consecutive monthly reviews with no unresolved critical ownership exceptions before expanding geography. For an owned portfolio, also require 1.25 times stressed operating coverage. A pipeline-only operation may expand research within funded reserves, but must continue reporting that portfolio cash support has not yet been established.
