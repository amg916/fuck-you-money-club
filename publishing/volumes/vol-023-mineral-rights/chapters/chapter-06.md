## 1. Map five acquisition plays to evidence, not acreage rumors  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one basin, two adjacent counties, and a written acquisition box that names formations, operators, interest types, check sizes, and maximum exposure to undeveloped acreage. The five mapped plays are operationally distinct: severed-title mineral acquisitions buy documented mineral ownership; NPRI purchases buy a defined nonparticipating royalty interest; producing-royalty acquisitions buy existing payment streams; pooling-docket curve-jumping buys before a public development signal becomes fully reflected in seller pricing; institutional flips aggregate or clarify interests for a documented downstream buyer. Net mineral acres, or NMA, measure ownership rather than identify a separate legal estate. An NPRI ordinarily lacks executive leasing rights, and its precise economic fraction depends on the instrument and applicable law. Assign each prospect one primary play and optional secondary tags so a producing tract near a new application does not become two pipeline opportunities. The owner runs sourcing and underwriting; a contract landman assembles ownership, an oil-and-gas attorney resolves instruments and closing terms, and a royalty accountant tests payment economics. That is the canonical 1 FT + 3 C pod, not four full-time salaries disguised as inexpensive automation. Concentration makes their limited hours useful.

Mine records in a sequence that preserves provenance. Begin with county clerk grantor-grantee indexes, recorded mineral deeds, reservations, probate filings, and assignments; use appraisal rolls only as discovery aids because assessed ownership does not establish the complete mineral chain. Connect legal descriptions to Railroad Commission of Texas GIS and drilling-permit records, or Oklahoma Corporation Commission imaging and case records when operating there. A permit, spacing application, pooling application, hearing notice, final order, spud report, completion report, and first production report are separate events with different certainty. Capture document date, retrieval date, docket identifier, applicant, formation, tract description, and current disposition in Airtable or a comparable searchable register. Link to the actual filing rather than a screenshot stripped of context. Enverus or another licensed data service can accelerate matching, but confirm material claims against primary records and license terms before redistribution. Reject acreage matches based solely on a common owner surname or a nearby well icon. The landman must connect the target estate to the correct tract and potentially relevant unit. A horizontal well crossing a map boundary does not establish that every nearby owner participates in its revenue.

Demand mining begins before an acquisition offer, not after closing. Interview ten credible mineral buyers and record their basin, county, formation, operator exclusions, minimum economic interest, producing-versus-undeveloped preference, documentation requirements, and typical review duration. Ask whether quoted prices assume clean title, current pay status, particular commodity prices, or a specified royalty burden. Request written acquisition criteria and refresh them monthly; a buyer's old purchase announcement is not current demand. Institutional flips work when the operator removes fragmentation, documentation friction, or lot-size mismatch that the buyer genuinely values. They fail when the operator merely adds a markup to the same incomplete package everyone else received. Build two plausible exits for each proposed flip, while underwriting the ability to hold the interest if neither buyer proceeds. Never describe an indicative bid as committed liquidity. Source direct owner purchases for the operator's account; compensated representation, solicitation for others, and certain assignments require a separate review of applicable licensing and transaction rules. For this chapter, a win means a completed acquisition or completed resale with reconciled settlement funds, not an introduction, unsigned expression of interest, or speculative buyer mandate.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Exploit documentation gaps, not unverifiable development stories**
> 
> Veteran operators preserve the link between ownership, unit participation, payment economics, and a buyer's actual acquisition box.
> - Read amended pooling exhibits against the original application; a removed tract can invalidate yesterday's sourcing thesis.
> - Ask owners for adjustment pages as well as statement summaries; a large check may contain retroactive corrections rather than recurring production.
> - Index reservations separately from conveyances so a surface transfer never silently becomes a mineral acquisition lead.
> - Have buyers price one standardized, redacted sample package before paying to assemble a larger institutional lot.

## 2. Qualify ownership, royalty economics, and downside before spending heavily  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a 100-point qualification score that rewards evidence rather than excitement: ownership traceability earns 25 points, verified economics 25, development evidence 15, seller fit 15, and executable exit or holding capacity 20. Define anchors before scoring. Ownership earns its full allocation only when the chain, reservations, succession, and signing authority are sufficiently resolved for the proposed transaction; a tax-roll match earns five. Producing economics receive full points only with reconciled statements and a defensible decline model. Nonproducing interests instead require explicit probability, timing, commodity-price, and zero-development cases; do not award them producing cash-flow points. A score of 75 advances to attorney review, 60–74 returns to targeted research, and below 60 leaves the active queue. These are internal screening rules, not observed market success rates. Suspected identity fraud, an unresolved material title defect, or inadequate acquisition funding blocks an offer regardless of total score. Seller fit measures willingness, authority, timing, and documentation access, never presumed vulnerability. The owner reviews twenty lightweight prospects weekly but releases only two or three for paid deep diligence. Contractor costs otherwise consume the economics before the team has made its first defensible bid.

Calculate the economic interest independently before accepting the seller's acreage shorthand. If an owner holds an undivided one-quarter mineral interest in 80 gross acres, the holding represents 20 NMA. Under a simple acreage-based allocation to a 640-acre unit with a one-fifth lease royalty, the illustrative revenue decimal is 20 divided by 640, multiplied by 0.20, or 0.00625. Actual participation can differ because of unit terms, allocation methods, retained interests, production intervals, and instrument language. Do not reuse this calculation for an NPRI until counsel determines whether its fraction applies to gross production or to the lessor's royalty. Collect twelve-to-twenty-four months of statements where available and compare production months rather than check dates. Identify suspense releases, prior-period adjustments, minimum-payment accumulation, and purchaser changes. Reconcile operator-reported volumes with public production records while allowing for reporting delays and aggregation differences. Examine taxes and post-production deductions under the actual lease and governing law. A higher recent check can reflect price recovery, not improved reservoir performance. Underwrite each well cohort separately when new completions temporarily hide the decline of older production, and preserve the raw statements behind every modeled annual receipt.

Translate the score into a maximum cash commitment, not a claim that the asset is worth one precise number. Model a producing-interest downside with lower realized prices, faster decline, delayed payments, and no new wells; model undeveloped acreage with a genuinely unproductive outcome rather than an indefinitely postponed success. Deduct title work, legal review, recording, taxes, transaction expenses, and required holding liquidity from the acquisition budget. The canonical startup range is $15,000–$75,000, with monthly operating expense capacity of $1,000–$12,000; neither range authorizes tying up every dollar in deeds. For a $150,000 starting pool, an illustrative allocation of $100,000 acquisition cash, $20,000 diligence and closing capacity, and $30,000 liquidity keeps the uses explicit. This allocation is not an annual earnings scenario. Reject a transaction if the downside holding period exhausts cash needed for existing obligations. Texas Property Code § 5.021 supplies a foundational written-conveyance requirement; Texas Natural Resources Code § 91.402 governs covered payment timing and specified withholding circumstances. Neither replaces instrument-level review. Use local counsel for probate, marital interests, liens, entity authority, and recording requirements before treating the seller's signature as transferable ownership.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before issuing an executable purchase offer**
> 
> An attractive screening score does not waive identity, ownership, economics, or capital verification.
> - Verify the seller's identity and signing capacity independently; match the exact conveyed estate to the traced ownership chain.
> - Have counsel interpret the lease, reservations, NPRI fraction, and material burdens; resolve defects affecting the interest being priced.
> - Recalculate the payment decimal and recurring receipts; retain a downside model that removes speculative wells and temporary adjustments.
> - Confirm acquisition funding, transaction expenses, holding liquidity, and closing instructions through independently verified counterparties.

## 3. Run a respectful outbound cadence and an evidence-rich inbound funnel  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build a rolling cohort of 400 raw record observations, not a purchased list labeled mineral owners. Deduplicate spouses, trusts, estates, entities, and repeated docket appearances into an illustrative 240 distinct owner opportunities. Research may produce 80 qualified opportunities, 60 with a usable contact route, 24 substantive owner conversations, 12 document packages, eight written offers, four signed agreements, and three completed purchases. These planning assumptions imply a 0.75% raw-to-close yield and a 37.5% offer-to-close yield; they are testable starting hypotheses, not published industry benchmarks. Record both stage-entry dates and reasons for rejection so improved conversion does not merely reflect quietly dropping difficult leads from the denominator. Run this cohort across the 6–24 month first-deal horizon rather than promising a monthly acquisition cadence. Ten completed wins would require roughly 1,334 raw observations at the same assumed yield, with no assurance that yield persists as the county list becomes thinner. Track purchases and resales separately, and never count the same asset's acquisition and resale as two new sourced-owner wins. Paid diligence begins after meaningful qualification, keeping three contractors focused on evidence likely to change an actual bid.

Use a six-touch sequence over approximately 45 days, stopping immediately after a refusal or communication restriction. On day one, send a plain mailed introduction identifying the actual purchasing entity, the county, and the interest you want to discuss. On day seven, make a manual call only after checking applicable federal and state calling requirements and suppression records. On day fourteen, send a second letter with a short explanation of mineral versus royalty ownership. On day twenty-one, use individualized email only through an appropriate, lawfully obtained address and with required commercial-email information. On day thirty, offer a document checklist or scheduled call through the owner's preferred channel. On day forty-five, close the loop and explain how to recontact the buyer later. Avoid automated calls and unsolicited texts as the default; 47 CFR § 64.1200 and applicable state rules require channel-specific analysis. The opening message should say, 'We purchase mineral and royalty interests in this county; would you consider reviewing an offer for your interest?' Never imply operator affiliation, government authority, a mandatory response, or an impending loss of ownership. Store opt-outs centrally so contractors cannot accidentally restart a stopped sequence.

Inbound should answer a narrow owner's question rather than attract broad commodity speculation. Publish county-specific pages explaining what documents support a mineral offer, why a royalty decimal differs from acreage, and how producing and undeveloped interests are evaluated. Show a redacted sample document checklist and a clear purchasing-entity identity. Collect only contact details, county, interest type, and permission to discuss the property initially; request sensitive statements through a restricted-access upload after identity and relevance checks. Attribute each inquiry to its page, referral, mail campaign, or professional introduction. Respond within two business days, but do not price an asset from a form submission alone. Handle objections by exposing assumptions: when an owner says a neighbor received more, compare interest type, royalty burden, production, and transaction date. When they expect another well, show the filed evidence and separate the producing-only offer from any development increment. When they prefer monthly income, acknowledge that a sale exchanges future receipts for current cash and stop pushing if that trade does not fit. Professional referrals can include estate attorneys and accountants, but review compensation restrictions before offering referral payments or representing another party.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the evidence-linked owner cohort
   - Deduplicate recorded ownership and activity signals into one opportunity per proposed transaction.
   - Attach source documents, tract identifiers, primary play, retrieval dates, and an accountable owner before any outreach begins.
2. Qualify before releasing contractor spend
   - Apply the weighted score and investigate blocking identity, title, and funding issues.
   - Restrict paid deep reviews to two or three strong opportunities weekly; record the specific question each contractor must answer.
3. Run the channel-specific owner sequence
   - Execute the six-touch cadence using verified contacts and a shared suppression register.
   - Log channel, date, response, requested follow-up, and document access; stop immediately when the owner declines further contact.
4. Price, contract, close, and reconcile
   - Issue evidence-supported terms, resolve closing conditions, and verify settlement and conveyance.
   - Keep signed agreements separate from completed wins, then update ownership records, payment-transfer requests, and realized transaction economics.

## 4. Close the first wins without confusing sale proceeds with operating revenue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Convert a successful conversation into a bounded purchase process with a written interest description, price methodology, diligence period, permitted access to records, closing conditions, and responsibility for identified expenses. Counsel should distinguish a preliminary indication from a binding purchase agreement and ensure the deed conveys only the intended estate. Do not send an owner a broad mineral deed when the negotiated purchase covers only a defined royalty interest. Resolve title exceptions through specific curative documents rather than a spreadsheet note saying they are probably harmless. Verify payment instructions using independently established contact information; email threads alone are inadequate protection against diverted closing funds. Arrange the agreed recording and settlement sequence with the closing professional, then submit the operator's required ownership-transfer package. Recording does not instantly update the operator's pay deck. Track delivery, acknowledgment, requested documents, and the first statement under the new owner. Review division orders against the acquired interest rather than assuming the proposed decimal is correct. Texas Natural Resources Code § 91.402 provides relevant payment and division-order rules for covered interests, but the transaction still needs fact-specific review. A closed acquisition is a sourcing win; verified payment transfer is a separate operational milestone.

Keep the canonical scorecard separate from individual transaction illustrations. Conservative annual revenue is $30,000, with a 20% net margin and $6,000 owner profit; the implied expense envelope is $24,000. Base revenue is $215,000, with a 32.5% margin, $69,875 profit, and $145,125 of expenses. Scaled revenue is $400,000, with a 45% margin, $180,000 profit, and $220,000 of expenses. The revenue basis is royalty receipts plus realized rights-trading gains, excluding returned basis. An illustrative Base bridge could contain $120,000 of royalty receipts and a $155,000 rights resale carrying $60,000 of recoverable basis: the $95,000 gain plus receipts equals $215,000, not $275,000. This simplified bridge assumes no additional basis adjustments and places selling expenses in the expense envelope without double counting. Have the accountant reconcile the management presentation to tax and financial records. Acquisition cash recovered at resale is liquidity returned, not new operating earnings. Never count the operator's underlying oil-and-gas sales as the mineral owner's revenue. The monthly operating expense range does not cap total annual scenario expenses, which can include transaction-linked costs. Nor do scenario revenues specify how many acquisitions must close to achieve them.

Operate a weekly dashboard around evidence quality, capital exposure, and completed outcomes. Track distinct qualified owners, reachable contacts, substantive conversations, complete packages, written offers, signed agreements, completed purchases, and realized exits. Measure sourcing cost per qualified opportunity and total diligence cost per closed transaction, including failed files. Keep acquisition basis outside those acquisition-marketing ratios and maintain a separate deployed-capital schedule. Royalty reconciliation and production monitoring are the two canonical automation levers: automate imports, variance flags, reporting-date reminders, and document version tracking, not instrument interpretation or investment judgment. The owner reviews exceptions; the landman, attorney, and accountant receive bounded work orders with source links. Commodity prices, depletion, and title disputes remain material even after a workflow becomes efficient. The Scaled case uses 2 FT + 4 C, six people, rather than silently loading twice the work onto the original pod. If outside investors will pool funds, analyze the actual structure before solicitation: 15 U.S.C. § 77b(a)(1) expressly includes fractional undivided interests in oil, gas, or other mineral rights within its securities definition. Do not assume that calling an arrangement a land purchase removes securities requirements. Expand only after completed files demonstrate repeatable sourcing, clean handoffs, and adequate holding liquidity.
