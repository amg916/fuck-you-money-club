## 1. The Practitioner Consensus: Regulatory Clocks, Bond Exposure, and the Permit as the Real Lever

Every operator-side witness in Santa Fe made the same complaint: regulatory clocks run shorter than real deals and real engineering. McGowan, an operator and reservoir engineer, told the Oil Conservation Commission that the proposed 30-day window is too short. He wants 90 days plus a grace period to cover complex projects, OCD data requests, and third-party consultants, and he summed it up as 'I like 90 days better than 30' [gfqoliQ78bk @ 00:04:45]. He went further on the 12-month marginal-well window. His own deal negotiation had already taken one year, with roughly 6 more months needed to raise capital. He said he would 'love' 5 years and cited transient flow, decline curves, and Fetkovich [gfqoliQ78bk @ 01:01:06]. NMOGA's Clayton Sporich, who co-founded Tap Rock and left as EVP Land and Legal, landed in the same place. He called 30 days inadequate and said the right number is 'closer to 90 days,' with a 5-year or 15-month presumption as fallbacks [gfqoliQ78bk @ 01:42:23]. In the Devon versus Perian special docket, Holiday flagged the 30-day proposal and 20-day notice periods before the deadlines were locked [Gyi9ijeMNgA @ 01:26:09]. In Texas, Commission Shift reported that producing regions sit 2 to 7 hours from Austin, which is why the group pushed the Railroad Commission to take remote public input over the conference phone [fqz-wQBuZ2A @ 00:01:29].

Both sides of the New Mexico bonding fight agree that the money question turns on marginal and inactive wells and on who ends up holding them. Sporich wants to keep the risk-based individual and tiered blanket bonds. He pointed out that the state reclamation fund held just over $66 million in April while federal grants were used instead, and argued that the fund should be the alternative to excessive bonding [gfqoliQ78bk @ 01:54:30]. McGowan put a number on the operator side. A 15% marginal trigger turns a 100-well operator carrying 15 marginal or inactive wells into '$15 million overnight,' and he warned that operators may simply 'hand you the keys and walk off' [gfqoliQ78bk @ 00:54:15]. During public comment, Krukauskas of a small family-owned Southeast New Mexico operator said cash bonds waste capital. He added that state approval of well transfers hurts small producers, and that the rule could 'run every small independent operator out of our state' [-7pNwWErBfk @ 00:04:29]. From the other side, Sheely of Gallup said about 95% of wells are plugged properly. Sheely estimated exposure at 5,000 at-risk wells times $163,000, or about $815 million, and said sellers dump marginal wells on 'poorly funded companies' [-7pNwWErBfk @ 00:13:27]. Operators and opponents disagree on the remedy. They agree that tired wells moving to thin balance sheets are where the plugging liability lands.

Regulators in three states treat the permit itself as the real enforcement weapon, and practitioners on every side organize around it. Greg Sovas spent 33 years at New York's DEC, the last 22 running the Division of Mineral Resources. He said his shop chose fixes over fines and withheld new permits from operators who did not clean up. He called that 'legal probably not' but 'effective very,' and summed it up as 'what do we care about a fine a penalty we got the environment cleaned up' [_C039ie8UHw @ 00:21:35]. In Texas, Commission Shift reported that the Railroad Commission revoked Blackbuck's 'True Blue' saltwater disposal permit in the North Culberson-Reeves seismic response area. The revocation followed quakes over magnitude 3, and a fault lay within a quarter mile; a lawsuit is likely [fqz-wQBuZ2A @ 00:03:32]. Alpha Seven Energy tells its roughly 200 investors to do their own due diligence by checking the permits and ownership on file at the Commission [gjm7ZmMazmc @ 00:00:28]. In Southfield, Michigan, the DEQ approved drilling at Word of Faith Church. The city still kept a temporary drilling moratorium running until late April and said it would request a temporary restraining order if necessary [Wvs9BxP1JfA @ 00:01:21]. Across all four venues, a state permit opens the fight rather than ending it.

## 2. Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers: Dockets, Permit Files, and Hard Permit Conditions

Hearings are won or lost in the filing layer before anyone testifies. The RRC CASES portal handles hearing requests, pre-hearing requests, appearance slips, and e-consent. Users can search by the last 4 digits of a docket number, the 8-digit case number, the operator, or the district. Dockets route to Legal Enforcement and Hearings, and the help line is 512-463-7158 [dgKvg8N8xd8 @ 00:00:41]. Missing a deadline is not always fatal. Fox moved to exclude Sporich's rebuttal because NMOGA filed it September 19 against a September 15 deadline and identified no direct testimony, yet the hearing officer declined to exclude it [gfqoliQ78bk @ 01:27:18]. Examiners do police scope hard. In the Devon versus Perian special docket, the examiner limited further Third Bone Spring testimony and denied a bid to recall Curry as 'not rebuttal.' The examiner then set a notice update for November 5, amended exhibits for the 12th to 14th, and closing arguments for the 25th [Gyi9ijeMNgA @ 01:29:20]. On appeal the clock tightens further. The Texas First Court of Appeals gave Oasis Pipeline and Penn Virginia 20 minutes each, with the appellant reserving 5 for rebuttal. The argument turned on whether 'seller's gas supply' covered frozen wellheads during Winter Storm Uri [oigm_h7P_sc @ 00:06:46].

Alpha Seven Energy's walk-through is the cleanest street-level permit audit taught. First, pull the W-1 drilling permit and its API number, then locate the well and its offsets on the RRC GIS viewer [gjm7ZmMazmc @ 00:04:22]. Their Roberts County design on the Mesa Vista Ranch kicks off around 2,800 feet, goes to about 8,500 feet, and runs a 5,000-foot lateral with a multi-stage frac. Alongside the permits, they pitch roughly 24,000 acres with room for up to 60 pads. Over 60 wells have been drilled and about 47 produce from the Granite Wash [gjm7ZmMazmc @ 00:08:39]. On the title side, Stump verified the Penrose-to-Perian chain of title during rebuttal. The landman witness warned that Lee County's online records run about 2 months behind, so recent conveyances may not show up in a remote search [Gyi9ijeMNgA @ 01:16:29]. For spacing exhibits, Enverus markets the spacing analytic inside Prism. It shows wells per section across the Lower Spraberry, Wolfcamp A, and Wolfcamp B, with a dynamic gun barrel viewer, and splits zone-specific spacing from multi-bench 'wine rack' spacing. It also bins type curves by boundedness and parent or child status, and its type curves show child wells are 'significantly impacted' [2rbY0Lt22CU @ 00:02:50].

The hard numbers sit in the permit conditions on injection and storage. After the Blackbuck revocation, Commission Shift reported that the Railroad Commission expanded the area of review from a quarter mile to a half mile plus a 2-mile review. It also cut maximum injection pressure where orphan or inactive wells sit nearby [fqz-wQBuZ2A @ 00:10:33]. In New Mexico's Class 6 primacy hearing, Dr. Helm Clark proposed a 10-mile radius and said the mean area of review in the permits reviewed was 8 miles. Clark also pushed for a 0.5-mile buffer around schools and hospitals, a cement temperature log run before the cement cures, through-casing resistivity logging, and at least one year of soil-gas baseline [_pyC_AisrQA @ 00:36:56]. Under questioning, Clark conceded that the director already has case-by-case authority to lower the pressure limit, as Ohio did on the Redbird #4 well. That undercuts a blanket cut from 90% to 80% [_pyC_AisrQA @ 00:31:02]. Sovas described New York's review as starting with casing and cementing, followed by an on-site visit. Aquifer wells need three strings, with DEC staff witnessing cement returns. The 2008 law set 330-foot setbacks and requires a 640-acre unit to be fully drilled within 3 years [_C039ie8UHw @ 00:19:40].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split in the New Mexico rulemaking is over who carries plugging risk and how fast the bill arrives. Operator witness McGowan ran the 15% trigger on a 100-well company with 15 marginal or inactive wells and got a blanket bond of "$15 million overnight," dismissed the 30% alternative as "speculation… just a guess," and pushed incremental, risk-adjusted bonds or a higher threshold instead [gfqoliQ78bk @ 00:54:15]. NMOGA's Clayton Sporich ran a different playbook: keep the existing risk-based individual and tiered blanket bonds, reject the CPI escalator, and treat the reclamation fund as "an alternative to excessive bonding" [gfqoliQ78bk @ 02:34:33]. Public commenters attacked from the opposite flank. Rossi of Moms Clean Air Force cited an LFC average plugging cost of $163,000, some wells above $700,000, more than $100 million already spent by taxpayers, and up to $1.6 billion unfunded, adding that nearly nine in ten voters back full cleanup [-7pNwWErBfk @ 00:01:06]. Dueweke of the Sierra Club turned the capital-lockup complaint around: unspent bond money is refunded, so "only irresponsible polluters will be harmed," and every plug the taxpayer funds is an industry subsidy [-7pNwWErBfk @ 00:28:41]. Same wells, same plugging math, opposite conclusions about who should front the cash.

Whether a permit actually ends the fight depends on the state. In Michigan, the DEQ approved drilling by Word of Faith Church on its own property in a residential area and said the project met all of its requirements; days later an Oakland County judge issued a temporary restraining order that stayed in effect until the Wednesday hearing, while about two dozen protesters lined Evergreen and vowed to return to the church every Sunday [K4HrSTGgbFs @ 00:00:43]. Southfield stacked a temporary drilling moratorium running until late April on top of that and told reporters it would request its own TRO if necessary, since the operator only has to notify the state five days before drilling [Wvs9BxP1JfA @ 00:01:21]. Former New York DMR director Greg Sovas described the opposite architecture: the 1981 law Sovas helped author replaced a bare "notice of intention" with permits and fees and superseded local rules except for taxation and roads [_C039ie8UHw @ 00:16:02]. In Texas, the procedural lever cuts toward the operator: after the RRC revoked Blackbuck's True Blue disposal permit, Commission Shift reported that simply filing a hearing request may allow injection to resume [fqz-wQBuZ2A @ 00:13:33]. The playbook is jurisdictional: local injunctions bite in Michigan, state preemption shields wells in New York, and hearing requests buy time in Texas.

Practitioners also split on how much downhole proof a regulator should demand before trusting a wellbore. McGowan opposed blanket caliper and casing-integrity log runs on older inactive wells, arguing that a well cemented to surface, or one completed with a DV-tool two-stage cement job, is already "bulletproof" [gfqoliQ78bk @ 00:16:54]. On the Class VI side, Dr. Helm Clark argued for more logging, not less: set the cement and run a temperature log before it cures, "because when cement sets, it's an exothermic reaction" [_pyC_AisrQA @ 01:14:25]. Yet Helm Clark retreated on monitoring wells that penetrate the injection zone, pointing to ADM, where the gauges at the perforations "all failed" and the monitoring wells were eventually pulled out of the injection zone [_pyC_AisrQA @ 00:17:16]. The 10-mile review radius followed the same arc. Helm Clark picked 10 miles as half the farthest 20-mile incident distance because 20 miles "just seemed too extreme," while the commission countered that the mean area of review in the permits reviewed already ran 8 miles, and Helm Clark ultimately conceded the extra radius is "unnecessary" if the area of review is that large [_pyC_AisrQA @ 00:40:32]. McGowan wants fewer blanket log runs; Helm Clark wants more logs but fewer blanket radii. Neither defends a one-size mandate.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure McGowan described is not a blowout; it is a balance-sheet event. Under the 15% trigger, an operator with 100 wells and 15 marginal or inactive ones sees the blanket bond become "$15 million overnight," and McGowan warned operators may simply "hand you the keys and walk off" rather than post it [gfqoliQ78bk @ 00:55:11]. Concentration compounds the problem: McGowan could not borrow against a single well, described "a million and a half barrel well," and put the exposure at "$50 million for one operator" [gfqoliQ78bk @ 00:57:44]. Public commenters documented where those keys end up. Sheely of Gallup said about 95% of wells are plugged properly but estimated 5,000 at-risk wells at $163,000 each, roughly $815 million against the LFC range of $700 million to $1.6 billion, and said sellers dump marginal wells on "poorly funded companies" [-7pNwWErBfk @ 00:13:27]. Jones of Citizens Caring for the Future pointed to the Carlsbad brine-well sinkhole: 14 years to fix, with the company bankrupt two years in [-7pNwWErBfk @ 00:09:23]. The economics check is blunt: a buyer picking up a marginal-heavy package inherits the bond step-up and the plugging tail at once, and the weakest buyers are exactly where commenters say those packages land.

Several witnesses lost ground on cross-examination through their own paperwork. Under questioning by applicants' counsel Tisdale, NMOGA's Clayton Sporich conceded that the trade group's redline used a five-year no-production presumption while Sporich's own direct testimony defended the existing 15-month standard, answering simply "they do conflict" [gfqoliQ78bk @ 03:21:28]. Minutes later Sporich admitted misreading the $200,000 bond figure, an admission that undercut the bonding critique built on it [gfqoliQ78bk @ 03:36:18]. In the Devon–Perian pooling fight, the Devon reservoir engineer had to walk the examiner through a tangle of facility costs: $3.15 million per well if only two wells are drilled, $2.21 million in the updated AFE, a $1.27 million figure that was a "clerical error" for the $1.3 million full credit back, and a $2.56 million average, insisting these were three values, not six [Gyi9ijeMNgA @ 00:25:33]. Later in the same docket, a request to recall Curry was denied outright as "not rebuttal," closing off a chance to repair the record [Gyi9ijeMNgA @ 01:22:04]. Every one of these slips came from numbers or positions already on file, and every one handed opposing counsel a clean impeachment line in front of the examiner or the commission.

Winter Storm Uri produced the costliest contract trap on the record. Oasis Pipeline told the First Court of Appeals that Penn Virginia shut in all 500 wells, and quoted PVA's own summary-judgment brief conceding "frozen equipment and frozen wellheads certainly were the central cause" of the non-delivery [oigm_h7P_sc @ 00:09:36]. Because the parties struck the NAESB safe-harbor language and added "other failure of production," Oasis argued the contract never required PVA to deliver "a single molecule" of gas, and that "the consequence under this contract is financial, not operational" [oigm_h7P_sc @ 00:51:19]. A force majeure declaration riding on transporter declarations is only as strong as the clause it sits in. Saltwater disposal carries its own reversal risk. Blackbuck's True Blue well in the North Culberson-Reeves seismic response area sat amid quakes over magnitude 3 with injection deeper than 9,000 feet; Blackbuck had relinquished six permits, injected 30,000 barrels a day in 2022, was suspended in January 2024, and lost the permit with a fault inside a quarter mile [fqz-wQBuZ2A @ 00:03:32]. Helm Clark's Ohio case showed the CO2 version: four undocumented 1930s wells led to brine damage in 28 wells after Redbird #4 began operating around 2017 [_pyC_AisrQA @ 01:36:32].
