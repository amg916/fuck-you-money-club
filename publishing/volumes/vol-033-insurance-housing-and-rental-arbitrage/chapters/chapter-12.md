## 1. Days 1-7: Establish Demand, Written Rights, and a Funded Exposure Limit  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the first week as a controlled underwriting exercise, not a shopping trip for apartments. Candidate #33 targets carrier-paid Additional Living Expense placements and corporate relocations lasting 30 to 270 days; the published time to first deal is one to three months. A signed placement during this sprint is therefore an acceleration, not the minimum evidence of progress. For this chapter, map the 12 operator plays into a working register: P1 referral-channel development, P2 displacement-demand qualification, P3 landlord sourcing, P4 written sublease authorization, P5 rate and duration underwriting, P6 furnishing and readiness, P7 payer and booking verification, P8 access and telemetry, P9 occupancy and service management, P10 extension and utility-cap billing, P11 collections and reconciliation, and P12 exit and redeployment. These labels are the sprint's operational decomposition, not additional revenue streams. Assign one full-time principal to sales, contracts, cash, and exception decisions. Use three contractors for sourcing and inspections, turns and maintenance coordination, and bookkeeping with administrative support. Record availability, response windows, backup contacts, and written spending authority before assigning any live household. Contractors provide defined services; their classification must fit the actual working relationship.

On Day 1, select one service area and define acceptable commute distances, bedroom counts, pet accommodation, accessibility requirements, and school-area constraints from actual displacement needs. On Day 2, build a named list of housing coordinators, restoration-company referral contacts, and corporate mobility buyers; verify whether ALE Solutions or CRS accepts supplier applications in that market. Portal registration is a channel setup task, not evidence of an awarded placement. On Day 3, conduct structured buyer interviews covering authorized daily rates, extension procedures, billing recipients, purchase-order requirements, and payment timing. Do not request claim narratives or medical details merely to qualify demand. On Day 4, inspect candidate properties with the sourcing contractor. On Day 5, obtain a landlord-signed addendum explicitly permitting corporate-housing subleases and the intended occupancy pattern. On Day 6, validate habitability, insurance terms, and property-specific occupancy restrictions. On Day 7, reconcile the evidence into a single opportunity register. A unit is not ready because a leasing agent verbally approves it; confirm the signer's authority and retain the executed lease, addendum, inspection record, and relevant association restrictions together.

Use an illustrative $24,000 launch treasury, inside the canonical $12,000 to $60,000 startup-capital range, rather than treating available credit as cash. Allocate at most $6,000 to setup and readiness, $6,000 to first-month operating disbursements, and preserve $12,000 as an uncommitted reserve. Refundable deposits consume treasury even when they are not expenses; track them separately from burn. The $6,000 operating ceiling is an illustrative sprint limit within the published $3,000 to $18,000 monthly opex range, not a replacement scorecard assumption. Include rent paid before collections when testing cash capacity, even if the accounting model treats that rent as a pass-through. Keep a 13-week cash schedule with weekly landlord payments, contractor invoices, furniture commitments, tax remittances, and payer receipts. Before signing, calculate the maximum contractual cash exposure through the earliest enforceable exit, including lease-break charges and restoration costs. Reject a commitment that would consume the reserve or push projected unrestricted cash below two months of unavoidable recurring obligations. An annual master lease supported only by a hopeful thirty-day booking fails this test unless separately funded exposure and enforceable termination rights close the mismatch.

## 2. Days 8-14: Price One Defensible Placement and Build Its Operating Packet  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

During the second week, convert P5 through P8 into one bookable operating packet. The principal owns pricing and payer verification; the sourcing contractor completes property documentation, the service contractor quotes turns, and the bookkeeping contractor constructs the invoice template. Under Formula v2.0, gross housing billings are not interchangeable with retained operator revenue. Apply the canonical 45% retained share to $180,000, $790,000, and $1,400,000 annual gross billings, producing $81,000, $355,500, and $630,000 retained revenue. The corresponding landlord-rent and utility pass-through amounts are $99,000, $434,500, and $770,000. After other modeled costs of $59,400, $205,400, and $266,000, annual owner profit reconciles exactly to $21,600, $150,100, and $364,000. Gross-basis net margins remain 12%, 19.0%, and 26%; retained-revenue margins are 26.67%, 42.22%, and 57.78%, rounded. Use these as three distinct annual operating cases, not a linear occupancy forecast or a first-month profit promise. The scorecard's ASC 606 retained-share presentation does not itself establish the principal-versus-agent accounting conclusion for every contract. Have the accountant document that conclusion from control, contractual responsibility, and the actual service arrangement while preserving this scorecard bridge.

For a concrete pilot, assume a buyer accepts $6,000 for thirty occupied nights and landlord rent plus utilities attributable to those nights equal $3,300. The retained spread is $2,700, exactly 45% of gross billings. If incremental cleaning, service labor, consumables, payment costs, and other booking-specific expenses total $600, placement contribution is $2,100 before shared overhead, owner compensation treatment, and idle-period carrying costs. It is not the canonical owner net profit. In this illustration, idle nights cost $110 each and earn nothing; ten uncovered nights consume $1,100 and reduce the placement-plus-gap contribution to $1,000. Run the same worksheet with a seven-night early closeout, a fifteen-day payment delay, and a utility overage that cannot be recovered. State whether the buyer owes a minimum booking amount or only occupied nights; never assume that insurance coverage makes the coordinator responsible for every lease obligation. Quote cancellation and extension economics before acceptance, including who signs an amendment. A carrier's coverage authorization, a household's housing preference, and an enforceable payment obligation are three different records. Require the appropriate record for each rather than merging them into one optimistic sales note.

On Day 8, finish the quote calculator and duration stress tests. On Day 9, complete the property packet with room dimensions, internet speed verification, parking, pet terms, accessibility facts, and dated photographs. On Day 10, complete supplier onboarding and confirm a working human escalation contact. On Day 11, inspect furniture, linens, smoke alarms, required carbon-monoxide protection, and essential appliances; buy only within the approved readiness allocation. On Day 12, test an authorized smart lock, an offline entry fallback, leak alerts, and non-recording noise-level monitoring where permitted. Explain devices to occupants and avoid interior audio or video surveillance. On Day 13, rehearse invoicing in QuickBooks or an equivalent ledger using a simulated booking with tax and utility-cap fields. On Day 14, send five fully priced, available-date proposals to qualified buyers. Treat ALE or CRS synchronization as an authorized integration or documented manual update process until a supported interface is confirmed. Do not scrape authenticated portals or describe a spreadsheet upload as real-time integration. The principal checks availability twice daily during live quoting, because a stale calendar can create two commitments against the same bedroom inventory.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Veteran Tactics That Protect the Spread Before Occupancy**
> 
> The best launch advantage is a smaller evidence gap between the quote, the lease, and the eventual collection.
> - Ask the housing coordinator for their invoice rejection checklist before sending the first quote; copy required claim references without collecting unnecessary claim narratives.
> - Negotiate a documented release window or priced termination option with the landlord before buying furniture; a cheaper monthly rent can hide a much larger exit liability.
> - Photograph utility meters at possession and departure, retain provider statements, and bill overages only under an accepted cap clause with supporting calculations.
> - Keep a clean, buyer-ready property packet and a live availability timestamp; fast accurate replies outperform broad inventory claims that collapse during verification.

## 3. Days 15-21: Rehearse Delivery, Secure Authorized Demand, and Measure the Pod  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Week three connects P7 through P11 without assuming that the first placement has already arrived. On Day 15, follow up every qualified proposal and classify objections as price, location, household fit, procurement friction, or timing. On Day 16, verify the prospective payer's identity through independently obtained business contact details and confirm who can authorize changes. On Day 17, rehearse arrival with the service contractor, including an inaccessible smart lock and a reported leak. On Day 18, test the extension workflow using a booking that expires in fourteen days. On Day 19, reconcile utility allowances and draft a sample supported overage invoice. On Day 20, rehearse collections against a missing purchase order and a disputed departure date. On Day 21, review response times and contractor load. If a real household moves in, substitute its actual records for the rehearsal and retain occupant acceptance of the condition report. If no booking exists, maintain an honest pipeline rather than inserting simulated occupancy into operating metrics. Track response speed using business-hour timestamps and separately measure urgent after-hours incidents; a polished daytime average can conceal an unsupported overnight housing failure.

Build the service register around named responsibilities rather than an assumption that three contractors equal three full-time employees. The principal retains payment, pricing, landlord negotiation, and serious incident decisions. The sourcing contractor maintains the backup-property list and verifies inspection evidence. The service contractor owns arrival readiness, maintenance dispatch, and departure turns within contracted availability. The bookkeeping contractor matches accepted booking terms, invoice lines, bank receipts, and open receivables. Give each person only the records needed for their work; cleaners do not need insurance claim identifiers or payer banking information. Use a restricted Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 folder structure and a shared task register with documented access removal. Measure principal workload, contractor hours, unresolved incidents, and invoice exceptions weekly. For the pilot, target no more than 45 principal hours per week and at least 95% completion of agreed service tasks by their deadlines. Emergency coverage must remain available through named backup providers, with their quoted charges included in underwriting. Do not add a second simultaneous placement while the first depends on unpaid overtime, undocumented favors, or one contractor answering every overnight call without a defined coverage agreement.

Property and tax verification remain operating tasks, not generic paperwork. Texas Tax Code Section 156.101 provides a state hotel occupancy tax exemption for a qualifying permanent resident, while 34 Texas Administrative Code Section 3.161 supplies important implementation detail. Confirm uninterrupted occupancy, payment responsibility, written commitment, and the applicable collection timing before applying that treatment. Local hotel taxes require their own analysis; neither a thirty-day marketing label nor a relocation purpose automatically resolves every jurisdiction. For California properties, Civil Code Section 1941.1 identifies basic tenantability characteristics that belong in inspection and remediation records. Federal Fair Housing Act requirements under 42 U.S.C. Section 3604 also matter when advertising, matching households, and handling disability-related accommodation requests. Apply objective property-fit criteria without substituting protected characteristics for actual requirements. A pet policy must not be mechanically applied to an assistance-animal request. Obtain property-specific confirmation of commercial liability coverage and relevant exclusions; a certificate alone does not rewrite a policy. Finally, record every early departure and extension in writing. A text from an occupant expressing a preference is not necessarily a payer-authorized amendment or a landlord-approved change to the underlying occupancy arrangement.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Validate the booking packet
   - Match household requirements, payer authority, property rights, dates, and accepted price.
   - The principal signs the internal booking record only after written payment responsibility and property authorization are attached.
2. Deliver and document possession
   - Complete condition photographs, safety checks, occupant instructions, and unique access credentials.
   - The service contractor records arrival acceptance and confirms that the backup entry method works before closing the task.
3. Control extensions and variable charges
   - Review expiry fourteen days ahead and obtain written authorization for changes.
   - The bookkeeper prepares utility evidence while the principal confirms payer acceptance and continuing landlord permission.
4. Reconcile cash and release inventory
   - Match invoices to receipts, document departure, and calculate placement-plus-gap contribution.
   - Separate refundable deposits, taxes, pass-throughs, retained revenue, and operating expenses before the principal approves another commitment.

## 4. Days 22-30 and Reviews at 60/90: Kill Exposure, Fix Bottlenecks, or Scale Evidence  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the final nine days to close the evidence loop and activate P12, exit and redeployment. Day 22 refreshes every buyer's dates and removes expired requests. Day 23 obtains current landlord availability and reconfirms quoted release terms. Day 24 audits the property packet against physical readiness. Day 25 reconciles all bank movements, deposits, invoices, and committed purchases. Day 26 reruns the thirteen-week forecast with receipts delayed fifteen days and ten uncovered nights for each exposed unit. Day 27 checks contractor invoices and principal workload against the delivery log. Day 28 requests written decisions from the most qualified buyers and records the actual reasons for deferral. Day 29 prepares a one-page decision memo with source documents linked beside each metric. Day 30 makes the decision and schedules the next review. Report cumulative outreach, substantive buyer conversations, qualified requests, proposals, signed bookings, occupied nights, collected dollars, and contractual exposure separately. A qualified request requires a named buyer, household fit, location, expected dates, budget, and a documented procurement path. Several requests concerning the same household count once; repeated coordinator forwarding must not inflate the apparent demand available to support a lease.

Apply the matrix in precedence order: Kill overrides Scale, and Fix is the remaining state when no Kill trigger applies but any Scale condition is missing. Kill means stop new commitments and execute a funded, orderly exit or suspension; it never means abandoning occupants or ignoring existing obligations. Common Kill triggers at every review are zero effective written sublease authorization for committed inventory, unresolved critical habitability defects before delivery, projected cash below the reserve floor, or negative contribution on a proposed placement under the ten-gap-night stress. A live negative placement triggers containment and renegotiation rather than improper cancellation. Day 30 Scale authorizes only the first pilot, because the normal first-deal window extends to three months. Day 60 Scale permits one additional unit after collection and delivery evidence; Day 90 Scale permits one further unit, followed by another thirty-day review. Occupancy equals occupied nights divided by all controlled rentable nights, including vacant and readiness-delay nights. Exclude units only after control legally ends. Collection performance equals dollars received against invoices due by review date divided by those due dollars; if nothing is due, report the actual due-dollar amount and keep expansion in Fix.

The thresholds below are launch policy assumptions, not outputs of Formula v2.0. Day 30 requires five qualified requests, five priced proposals, and one accepted booking before releasing pilot funding; absent a booking, continue demand validation without adding lease exposure. Day 60 requires at least thirty occupied nights, 70% cumulative controlled-night occupancy, 95% collection of due dollars, and a positive stress-tested contribution. Day 90 raises the sample to sixty occupied nights and occupancy to 80%, while retaining the collection and cash tests. At both expansion reviews, require at least one invoice to have become due and at least one cleared payer receipt. Require at least 95% on-time service-task completion, no unresolved critical incident, and principal workload at or below 45 weekly hours over the preceding fourteen days. Fix assignments receive one owner, a fourteen-day deadline, and a maximum $1,000 incremental remediation budget inside existing cash limits. These pilots preserve the 1 FT plus 3 C pod. The canonical Scaled case instead uses 2 FT plus 4 C; its $364,000 annual owner profit cannot be presented as a verified four-person outcome or inferred from one successful placement. Expand capacity only after separately funding the additional people and contract exposure.
