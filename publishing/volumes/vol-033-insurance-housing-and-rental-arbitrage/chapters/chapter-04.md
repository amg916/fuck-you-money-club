## 1. Ring-fence float before buying inventory or accepting fixed rent  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat startup capital as a liquidity allocation, not a shopping allowance. The three launch budgets below preserve 55% as working-capital reserve: $6,600 from $12,000, $19,800 from $36,000, and $33,000 from $60,000. Keep that reserve in a separate operating savings account with same-day transfer access; refundable landlord deposits and household security deposits do not count toward it. The smallest budget supports only a tightly negotiated pilot, preferably furnished inventory with limited setup work, rather than a portfolio of speculative leases. At an illustrative $2,400 monthly landlord rent plus $300 utilities, a forty-five-day collection delay consumes $4,050 per occupied unit before cleaning, payroll, or damage. Two simultaneous placements consume $8,100, already exceeding the smallest reserve. Require either advance collections, shorter payment terms, supplier timing relief, or fewer committed units. A reservation email is not collected cash, and a carrier's coverage decision is not automatically its payment obligation to your company. Model the contractual debtor, invoice acceptance date, disputed-charge process, and actual bank receipt separately. The operating decision is how much irrevocable rent you can service through a delayed receivable and an early departure, not how many listings you can publish. Never spend household deposits to bridge that timing gap.

Build a thirteen-week cash forecast with property-level rent dates, expected collections, utility due dates, refundable deposits, and exit costs. Maintain a second reserve test based on sixty days of unavoidable property commitments plus thirty days of central overhead and one emergency turnover. For one illustrative unit carrying $2,700 monthly property obligations, $3,000 monthly central overhead, and a $600 turnover allowance, that test requires $9,000: $5,400 plus $3,000 plus $600. The $12,000 launch budget therefore fails this particular configuration despite satisfying the 55% allocation rule. Reduce fixed obligations or secure advance payment before proceeding; do not quietly relabel deposits as reserves. Treat the stated $3,000–$18,000 monthly opex range as an operating envelope whose inclusions must be declared. For planning here, it covers recurring central overhead, while property pass-throughs and variable placement expenses remain separate forecast lines. Never add a rent invoice twice by including it in both retained-spread calculations and central overhead. Release furnishings money only against an identified unit, measured room dimensions, written installation access, and a delivery date preceding occupancy. Prefer transferable beds, desks, kitchen kits, and washable textiles over property-specific decoration that loses its value when a landlord terminates.

Reconcile every expansion proposal to the supplied Formula v2.0 scorecard rather than promoting a strong monthly booking into an annual forecast. Conservative gross billings of $180,000 less $99,000 of landlord and utility pass-throughs leave $81,000 retained revenue; $59,400 of remaining expenses leave $21,600 owner profit. Base billings of $790,000 less $434,500 leave $355,500 retained revenue; $205,400 of remaining expenses leave $150,100. Scaled billings of $1,400,000 less $770,000 leave $630,000 retained revenue; $266,000 of remaining expenses leave $364,000. Dividing profit by gross billings produces 12%, 19.0%, and 26%; dividing by retained revenue produces 26.67%, 42.22%, and 57.78%, rounded. Remaining expenses include both central overhead and variable costs, so the scaled monthly equivalent of $22,166.67 does not mean central overhead alone fits inside $18,000. Maintain this distinction in lender packets, dashboards, and acquisition models. The scorecard's ASC 606 retained-share presentation is the required economic basis here; actual principal-versus-agent presentation requires contract-specific assessment under ASC 606-10-55-36 through 55-40. Underlying property value, the policyholder's insured loss, and carrier claim limits are not operator revenue. Expansion must preserve these reconciliations rather than hiding vacancy inside a larger gross-billings headline.

## 3. Make entity, banking, property rights, and insurance reinforce each other  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Form the operating entity in the jurisdiction justified by actual operations, then confirm any foreign qualification, local business registration, and property-specific licensing requirements. Obtain the EIN directly through the IRS without paying an intermediary merely to submit the application. Open an operating account, reserve account, and any legally required deposit account using consistent entity names and documented signatory authority. The same contracting entity should appear on the master lease, housing vendor agreement, invoices, and insurance unless a documented structure explains the difference. Bank customer identification procedures under 31 CFR 1020.220 require identity verification; prepare formation documents, addresses, and requested identifying records before rent becomes due. Determine current beneficial-ownership reporting obligations from FinCEN's operative rules rather than an old incorporation checklist. For larger reserves, evaluate FDIC aggregation rules: 12 CFR 330.11 addresses qualifying business accounts, and separate account nicknames do not create separate insured ownership categories. Collect contractor tax information before payment, restrict bank access by role, and verify changed payment instructions using an independently sourced telephone number. A forged landlord remittance email can erase the entire pilot reserve faster than vacancy. Reconcile every account weekly and separate owner distributions from property operating payments.

Execute explicit written landlord corporate-housing sublease addenda on every property before advertising possession you cannot lawfully deliver. Address permitted occupants, corporate and insurer referrals, assignment, access devices, furnishings, utilities, inspections, insurance, deposit handling, extension mechanics, and termination economics. Confirm that condominium rules, recorded restrictions, and local occupancy rules do not defeat the landlord's permission. A thirty-day booking is not a universal tax exemption or a universal escape from lodging regulation. California Revenue and Taxation Code section 7280 authorizes specified local transient occupancy taxation tied to stays of thirty days or less; the actual ordinance and exemption documentation still control the property workflow. California Civil Code section 1941.1 identifies habitability conditions for residential dwellings, while section 1950.5 governs covered security deposits. Use those examples only for properties to which they apply, then obtain the corresponding rules for each operating location. For a $4,000 monthly placement with a hypothetical 10% local tax, an unsupported exemption creates $400 of exposure before penalties. Maintain an exemption evidence folder and recheck extensions, interrupted stays, payer changes, and required filings. Do not let a billing system's default thirty-night setting make the legal classification decision for the property.

Bind insurance for the activity actually performed: furnished corporate housing, authorized subletting, occupancy turnover, and any employees or contractors entering units. Ask the broker to explain commercial general liability, business personal property, loss-of-income limitations, cyber exposure, and workers' compensation requirements for the operating state. A certificate of insurance summarizes coverage; inspect endorsements, exclusions, deductibles, additional-insured wording, and effective dates. Landlord-required coverage does not automatically cover displaced occupants' belongings, nor does a homeowner policy necessarily cover your rental business. Separate the customer's ALE entitlement from your receivable. An insurer may reimburse its policyholder while a relocation company contracts with you; the invoice must name the party actually obligated to pay. Record approved dates, rate limits, pet charges, cleaning charges, utility caps, extension authority, and early-closeout treatment. Apply housing decisions consistently with 42 U.S.C. 3604, including applicable disability accommodation duties, rather than treating insurer funding as permission for arbitrary occupant selection. Keep only necessary household data, restrict loss documents, and exclude medical narratives from routine operations boards. Turn the insurance review into explicit incident instructions: whom to call, what evidence to preserve, which repairs need authorization, and when to notify the carrier or landlord.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four evidence tests before any household receives possession**
> 
> A failed test means repair the specific defect or replace the property; a high expected nightly rate does not cure missing rights or cash.
> - Property-rights test: retain the signed corporate-housing sublease addendum, verify the signer's authority, and resolve conflicting association or occupancy restrictions.
> - Liquidity test: preserve at least 50% of starting capital as float and independently satisfy the modeled rent-tail, collection-delay, and emergency-turnover requirement.
> - Payment test: identify the contractual debtor, approved dates and charges, invoice destination, acceptance evidence, and return or dispute handling.
> - Habitability and coverage test: document the completed property inspection, functioning access and safety devices, applicable occupancy classification, and effective insurance endorsements.

## 4. Run a complete placement rehearsal before increasing committed inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a synthetic placement through the entire system before handing over keys. Create a test household record without real sensitive information, attach a sample authorization, generate the occupancy agreement, issue a test invoice, and reconcile a sandbox payment event. For systems without test mode, use clearly identified internal records and prevent external delivery. Verify that one placement identifier survives every export and that cancelled tests cannot generate live charges. Rehearse a utility-cap adjustment using a $250 monthly allowance and a $310 documented bill: the potential excess is $60, collectible only under the agreed terms and any applicable allocation rules. Rehearse an extension from sixty to ninety days, making the authorization effective date distinct from the system update date. Test duplicate imports and confirm that one source invoice cannot become two customer charges. Send a controlled leak alert and time the human response; a sensor notification alone does not stop water damage. Confirm mechanical access during an internet outage and retain an emergency contact route outside the automation platform. Record defects with an owner and correction evidence, then rerun the failed segment before accepting an actual arrival.

Stress the unit economics against early claim closeout, vacancy, and a disputed receivable instead of testing occupancy alone. An illustrative $4,000 monthly placement yields $1,800 retained spread at the canonical 45% share, with $2,200 allocated to landlord rent and utility pass-throughs. If the carrier-supported stay ends after twenty days and the customer owes only prorated occupancy, billings fall to $2,666.67 at a thirty-day convention. If the full $2,200 property commitment remains payable, the residual spread drops to $466.67 before other expenses. This stressed unit is not a replacement for the canonical annual scenarios; it exposes the liquidity consequences of an unmatched contract. A negotiated notice payment may reduce the loss only when the correct counterparty actually owes it and collection is supportable. Keep a secondary corporate relocation channel, but never assume a replacement occupant arrives the next morning. Model a fourteen-day gap at $2,200 divided by thirty, producing $1,026.67 of property carrying cost before turnover. Reprice, shorten the landlord commitment, increase float, or reject the unit when the stressed cash position breaches your minimum. Automated demand scoring must not override this property-specific cash test.

Operate the first month as a controlled production pilot with daily exceptions review and weekly cash reconciliation. Track accepted invoices, cash received, receivable aging, authorization expiries, committed rent tails, vacancy days, service incidents, and reserve coverage. Distinguish the scenario teams precisely: Conservative and Base each use 1 FT plus 3 C, while Scaled uses 2 FT plus 4 C, six people in total. Contractor counts describe people available for defined work, not three or four full-time salaries hidden outside the model. Assign cleaning, maintenance, and bookkeeping responsibilities explicitly, with backups for urgent access and water events. Move from the one-to-three-month first-deal window into additional fixed leases only after demonstrating a complete collection cycle and the necessary liquidity under stress. Keep the monthly stack review merciless: cancel duplicate storage, unused customer-management seats, and integrations that require more supervision than their manual fallback. Retain exports before cancellation and test recovery from a local encrypted backup. Expansion should improve retained contribution per operator hour while preserving property service quality. A larger gross-billings dashboard is irrelevant if overdue receivables, undocumented extensions, or growing lease tails absorb the cash needed to meet next month's obligations.
