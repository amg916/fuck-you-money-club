## 1. Buy authorized housing capacity, then sell placement reliability  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Insurance housing and corporate rental arbitrage means contracting for housing capacity, furnishing and servicing that capacity, and charging an authorized payer for temporary occupancy. The operator does not buy the underlying home and does not earn the home's market value, the employer's relocation budget, or the household's insurance claim amount. The core product is a habitable, available dwelling with documented occupancy rights, predictable utilities, responsive maintenance, and a clean billing trail. Insurance Additional Living Expense, or ALE, funds eligible displacement costs under the relevant policy; it is not a universal housing allowance. Corporate housing instead relies on an employer, relocation manager, or occupant contract. Thirty-to-270-day stays are the operating focus, but a minimum stay does not erase local tenancy, zoning, licensing, or tax requirements. Before advertising a property, secure a landlord-signed addendum expressly permitting the intended corporate-housing and sublease activities, occupant substitutions, furnishings, pets where accepted, and the proposed channels. A generic residential lease plus verbal enthusiasm is inadequate inventory control. The practical arbitrage is between predictable wholesale occupancy commitments and a buyer's premium for immediate, suitable accommodation, not between two screenshots showing different nightly prices. Service execution determines whether that premium survives the placement.

The anchor play, insurance_ale_displaced_housing_master_lease, screens suitable three-to-five-bedroom single-family homes around a $2,500 monthly base-rent assumption. Seek placements through ALE Solutions, CRS Temporary Housing, or Sedgwick only through their actual supplier and authorization processes. A quoted $6,500-$9,500 monthly placement for four-to-nine months depends on geography, available inventory, household requirements, policy limits, and written authorization. At an illustrative $7,500 monthly charge, subtracting $2,500 rent and $650 utilities leaves $4,350 before furniture, vacancy, labor, cleaning, collections losses, and overhead. That property illustration does not replace the scorecard's portfolio-wide 45% retained assumption. Four occupied months generate $30,000 billings; nine generate $67,500, but neither duration should be assumed before authorization. Match school proximity, bedroom count, parking, pets, and accessibility before presenting a quote. A displaced family's need does not establish who owes the invoice. Identify the contracting payer and distinguish carrier reimbursement from a direct payment commitment. When repairs finish early, obtain a written extension if justified, activate landlord-approved alternative occupancy, or use reserves and negotiated termination rights. Never treat smart-lock access as permission to bypass tenant protections. Require a documented end date and escalation owner before collecting keys.

Five operator archetypes explain the shelf without pretending every business carries the same balance-sheet risk. The master-lease principal carries rent during vacancy; the placement agent earns an arranging fee without necessarily controlling accommodation; the corporate block operator matches employer demand to reserved units; the clinical specialist serves rotating assignments; and the accessibility or pet specialist solves hard-to-match household constraints. Their shared moat is verified fulfillment, not a secret listing website. Candidate #33's score components are 23 revenue potential, 24 profit-margin potential, 18 team efficiency, 13 startup accessibility, and 12 automation potential, totaling 90. The $12,000-$60,000 startup range must cover deposits, furnishings, insurance, setup, and working capital rather than just furniture photographs. At $2,500 rent plus $650 utilities, three vacant months consume $9,450 before other costs; that reserve alone nearly exhausts the lower startup case. First-deal timing of one-to-three months is a planning window, not booked demand. Start with one authorized property and a documented buyer requirement, then measure days vacant, acceptance rate, collection lag, and extension conversion. A beginner who signs five leases before testing payer acceptance has purchased five recurring liabilities, not five revenue streams. Expand only when those measurements support another fixed commitment.

## 2. Reconcile gross billings, retained spread, and actual owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Maintain three separate ledgers: customer billings, direct accommodation obligations, and residual operating expense. Formula v2.0 uses gross insurance relocation and corporate housing billings with retained_share=0.45 after master-lease landlord rent and utility pass-throughs. Therefore the modeled direct allocation is 55% and the retained operating pool is 45%. Do not describe the entire customer's claim, relocation allowance, property value, or employer payroll as operator revenue. Equally, do not assume that labeling a payment a pass-through makes net presentation correct. Under ASC 606's principal-versus-agent framework, evaluate whether the operator controls the specified service before transfer; contractual responsibility, inventory risk, and pricing discretion inform that assessment. A principal may report gross service revenue with housing costs separately, while an agent generally reports the fee or commission earned for arranging the service. Lease components can instead require ASC 842 analysis. The 45% retained figure is the scorecard's management bridge, not a substitute for contract-level accounting. Deposits remain liabilities until earned or validly applied, and advance collections do not automatically become current revenue. Document the performance period, cancellation terms, principal-agent conclusion, and treatment of taxes for each contract form. Have the accountant reconcile these decisions before dashboard totals become lender or investor representations.

Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast with lease due dates, approved invoice dates, expected remittances, deposits, and extension decisions. Stress cancellation at the earliest contractually permitted date, rather than assuming the repair contractor's original schedule holds. Track arrears by payer and dispute reason. A creditworthy carrier's involvement does not eliminate exposure when the actual agreement names a different debtor. Keep refundable deposits outside the spending pool and reconcile every receipt against its invoice.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the contract and payer
   - Record the landlord authorization, occupant agreement, placement authorization, and invoice debtor together.
   - Assign one placement identifier and distinguish the person occupying the dwelling from the party contractually responsible for payment.
2. Classify each cash movement
   - Separate earned housing charges, refundable deposits, collected taxes, rent obligations, utilities, and service expenses.
   - Record the service dates and document principal-agent or lease accounting treatment instead of treating every bank receipt as revenue.
3. Bridge the scorecard without changing it
   - Calculate gross billings times 45%, then subtract the scenario's residual expense to reproduce canonical owner profit.
   - Show actual property economics alongside the benchmark and explain variances rather than forcing every individual property to retain exactly 45%.
4. Test cash and expiry exposure weekly
   - Update the thirteen-week cash forecast and review authorization expiries, vacancy intervals, and receivable disputes.
   - Assign a named owner and next action to every threatened extension, payer discrepancy, or lease commitment lacking matched demand.
