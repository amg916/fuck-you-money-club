## 2. Price the placement cohort and reserve the charges that can disappear  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Create one placement record linking the landlord lease, corporate-housing sublease addendum, resident agreement, payer authorization, and invoice schedule. ALE placement does not automatically mean the insurer is the contractual debtor; identify whether payment comes from a carrier, a housing intermediary, an employer, or the resident. Record the authorized dates, daily ceiling, permitted occupants, pet charges, tax treatment, utilities, and extension procedure before arrival. For the Base ninety-day equivalent, $23,700 is collectible only if all three $7,900 billing periods satisfy those terms. A ten-day disallowed extension at an illustrative $263.33 daily rate removes approximately $2,633, while the landlord may still demand rent. That exceeds the placement's $474 reversal allowance by more than five times. Require documented payer authorization for extensions, but separately resolve resident rights and move-out procedures under applicable tenancy law. Do not use smart-lock access withdrawal as a collection device. California Civil Code section 1940 can govern residential occupancy, and section 1942.5 restricts specified retaliation. Written authorization addresses billing exposure; it does not replace habitability duties or lawful possession procedures. Set the lease-tail reserve using the landlord's enforceable termination terms rather than the insurer's estimated repair completion date.

Separate billing scrub, credit loss, damage, and vacancy even when management initially budgets them together. A scrub corrects an unsupported invoice before submission; a reversal removes a previously accepted charge; a credit loss concerns an otherwise valid receivable. These events can require different accounting treatments, so the waterfall's combined allowance is an operating forecast rather than a universal journal entry. Maintain reason codes and retain the underlying authorization, utility statement, arrival evidence, and departure confirmation. Base budgets $15,800 annually, or $158 per billed month. If actual deductions reach four percent of original gross billings, incremental leakage is $15,800 and owner profit falls to $134,300 with other costs fixed. A refundable resident deposit remains a liability until a lawful, documented deduction is established; it cannot automatically refill the operating reserve. California Civil Code section 1950.5 supplies residential security-deposit requirements where applicable. Utility overages need actual meter or supplier evidence and a contractual cap. Price a $250 monthly allowance separately from a $340 actual bill: the $90 difference is recoverable only when the agreement permits it. Charge disputes are resolved with evidence, not by relabeling them as administration.

Measure acquisition against completed, collectible stays rather than signed inquiries. A three-month equivalent carries CAC of $720 Conservative, $711 Base, and $525 Scaled under the allocations above. Base acquisition spending of $23,700 divided by $711 implies 33.33 three-month placement equivalents; that is a normalization device, not a fractional household. The supplied mapped play municipal_code_violation_trigger_arbitrage polls municipal building-department portals and county recorder dockets daily for failed structural or fire inspections, expired permits, code citations, and newly filed mechanic's liens. Route verified, exclusive leads to remediation contractors and construction attorneys, without treating a citation or lien as proof of displacement or ALE coverage. An illustrative contractor-only pilot spends $120 on permitted data access, $180 on verification, and $100 on outreach; eight accepted leads at $100 produce $800 billings and $400 contribution before overhead. Exclude that separate business from the canonical housing totals. ## 3. Fund the receivable clock, payout locks, and refundable deposits  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Define the cash clock explicitly. DSO runs from invoice issuance to payer remittance, while a separate payout lock covers the period from remittance to unrestricted bank availability. Do not add a lock when the measured DSO already ends at cleared cash. For this model, use forty-five days to remittance plus seven additional days to release, producing fifty-two days of prefunding. These are planning assumptions, not stated ALE or CRS payment terms. Verify each counterparty's contract and actual remittance history. Estimate annual cash operating outflow as gross billings minus owner profit minus the reversal allowance: $154,800, $624,100, and $1,022,000. Divide by 365 and multiply by fifty-two to obtain $22,053.70, $88,912.88, and $145,600 of expense-based bridge funding. This proxy treats furnishing consumption as recurring replacement cash and vacancy allocations as expected cash expenditures; replace both with dated payments in the treasury forecast. It does not fund owner distributions. The calculation approximates an initial collection gap and is not a full accounts-receivable borrowing-base model. Carrier receivables, security deposits, and disputed invoices have different liquidity and cannot be treated as interchangeable cash equivalents. Build the actual forecast by property and payment date.

Invoice timing can lengthen exposure before DSO even begins. Monthly billing in arrears creates approximately fifteen additional days of average service-to-invoice lag under uniform delivery, although landlord rent paid in advance can produce a larger peak. Base then needs approximately $114,560.82 for sixty-seven expense days rather than $88,912.88 for fifty-two. Add refundable landlord deposits and initial furnishing purchases separately. **SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the contractual cash clock
   - Record invoice timing, acceptance requirements, remittance terms, and bank-release delays for each payer.
   - Measure DSO to remittance only when adding a separate payout lock; retain dated evidence for both intervals.
2. Schedule every committed cash use
   - Load thirteen weeks of rent, utilities, delivery invoices, wages, deposits, and furnishing purchases.
   - Separate refundable assets, operating expenses, and capital purchases; eliminate duplicate furnishing and vacancy allocations.
3. Stress delayed and disputed collections
   - Shift receipts fifteen days later and remove contested extension charges until collectibility is supported.
   - Recalculate the lowest unrestricted cash balance without counting resident deposits or uncommitted financing.
4. Match inventory commitments to available liquidity
   - Approve additional leases only after documenting funding for the stressed cash trough and lease tail.
   - Reduce inventory growth or negotiate terms when committed resources cannot cover the modeled obligations.

## 4. Stress margin compression before adding another master lease  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Recompute actual spread after each stress. A fixed landlord obligation does not become a smaller payment because a carrier negotiates a lower nightly rate. This distinction is the central difference between a spreadsheet margin and an enforceable lease liability.

Automated extensions must stop when authorized dates expire; automation can prepare a request but cannot manufacture payer assent.
