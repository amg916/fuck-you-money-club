## 1. Separate approved placement revenue from unconditional lease exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is treating an adjuster's referral as a payment promise. Additional Living Expense coverage reimburses qualifying displacement costs under a policy; it does not automatically make the carrier your contracting customer. Identify the obligor before releasing possession: insurer, housing intermediary, employer, or displaced household. Record the purchase order, authorized nightly rate, approved dates, utility allowance, invoicing route, and termination terms. A claim number authenticates a workflow, not an unlimited receivable. In an illustrative placement, a $5,000 monthly housing invoice leaves $2,250 at the canonical 45% retained share after $2,750 of landlord rent and utility pass-throughs. Eight vacant nights priced at $160 represent $1,280 of foregone billings, not automatically $1,280 of net loss. Add a $1,200 unrecoverable lease-break charge and the combined exposure exceeds that month's retained spread before ordinary operating expenses. Model vacancy using actual fixed costs, avoided variable costs, and replacement collections. The circuit-breaker is contractual: match the placement's cancellation protection to the master lease, or reserve the uncovered tail before accepting the booking. Never substitute a carrier logo, portal status, or confident telephone assurance for an executed allocation of responsibility between named parties.

The second trap is confusing occupancy, approval, invoicing, and cash collection as one event. Build four separate dates into Airtable or a controlled spreadsheet: possession start, authorization expiration, invoice submission, and expected collection. A household can remain in lawful possession after carrier funding stops. A housing portal can display an active reservation while its purchase order has exhausted approved dollars. Query authorized days remaining every morning, flag seven days, and escalate at three days without written extension. These are internal operating thresholds, not carrier standards. Stop promising additional funded nights; do not disable locks or remove possessions. Send the resident and payer a documented funding-status notice, pursue an agreed extension, and follow applicable tenancy procedures if possession becomes disputed. A ten-night approval gap at $180 creates $1,800 of disputed billings; whether any becomes collectible depends on the signed contracts and actual debtor. Measure unapproved occupied nights separately from vacancy because the recovery paths differ. Reconcile ALE and CRS housing portal exports to the signed authorization ledger rather than allowing either portal to overwrite contract terms. Treat changed rates, duplicate extensions, and retroactive date edits as exceptions requiring human review before invoice release.

The third trap is celebrating gross volume while funding losses from deposits. Formula v2.0 produces $180,000, $790,000, and $1,400,000 of annual gross billings. At 45% retained share, contribution before remaining operating costs is $81,000, $355,500, and $630,000. Deduct $59,400, $205,400, and $266,000 respectively to reconcile owner profit of $21,600, $150,100, and $364,000. The corresponding gross-basis net margins are 12%, 19.0%, and 26%; retained-basis margins are 26.67%, 42.22%, and 57.78%. Keep these denominators visible on every dashboard. The scorecard's retained-share presentation does not independently settle ASC 606 principal-versus-agent classification; evaluate control of the promised service and contractual facts before deciding statutory revenue presentation. Refundable resident deposits remain liabilities, while prepaid landlord deposits are assets subject to recovery risk. Neither creates earned spread. Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast with separate buckets for unrestricted cash, restricted deposits, disputed receivables, taxes, and unavoidable property payments. Freeze new master leases when unrestricted cash cannot cover eight weeks of committed property costs plus documented near-term exit charges. Recompute that test weekly and after every large claim closeout, rather than averaging away concentrated obligations across otherwise profitable properties.

## 2. Price every clawback, utility overage, and exit defect into the unit ledger  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The fourth trap is selling an all-inclusive rate while buying uncapped inputs. A utility allowance becomes collectible only when the agreement defines the baseline, measurement period, allocation method, evidence, and responsible payer. Suppose actual electricity, water, and internet costs total $540 against a $300 contractual allowance. The $240 difference is a receivable only if the contract permits that charge and the supporting bills match the occupancy period. Otherwise it reduces contribution. Track utility cost per occupied night and compare like seasons, unit sizes, and climates; investigate a 20% rise against the property's trailing comparable baseline rather than assuming resident misuse. Smart thermostats and leak sensors can expose equipment faults, but cannot replace habitability obligations or authorize service restrictions. The fifth trap is amortizing furniture over an optimistic stay count. A $6,000 package allocated across twenty placements costs $300 each; ten realized placements double that allocation to $600. Keep cash purchase timing separate from depreciation and management allocations. Calculate recoverable resale value after delivery, storage, cleaning, and disposal. Stop furnishing additional speculative units when signed placement demand cannot support the existing inventory's cash carrying cost, even if listing inquiries appear strong.

The sixth trap is assuming a damage photograph guarantees recovery. Create a timestamped move-in inventory, resident acknowledgment, vendor condition report, and itemized move-out inspection. Distinguish ordinary wear from chargeable damage and avoid billing full replacement cost for an already depreciated item where the applicable rules prohibit that treatment. In California, Civil Code section 1950.5 controls residential security-deposit purposes, limits, and accounting, including the generally applicable twenty-one-day disposition deadline. Apply the current provisions and relevant exceptions to the actual tenancy rather than labeling every arrangement corporate. A $900 disputed repair plus $250 of additional cleaning consumes $1,150 before staff time; if only $400 is supported and recoverable, recognize the remaining $750 exposure promptly. The seventh trap is double charging a carrier and household for the same cleaning, pet, or replacement item. Use one charge identifier linking the incident, invoice, payer allocation, credit, and collection. For payment-card disputes, preserve the signed agreement, authorization evidence, delivery of accommodation, and refund communications. Processor acceptance is not proof that an insurer approved the expense. Credit duplicate charges immediately and reconcile the correction across the ledger, resident statement, and intermediary portal.

The eighth trap is growing into payer concentration while measuring only occupancy. Set an internal review trigger when one debtor exceeds 35% of open receivables or any invoice exceeds its contractual due date by fifteen days. Review does not mean assuming insolvency; it means obtaining a named collection owner, resolving missing documents, and limiting incremental unsecured exposure. The ninth trap is paying sales commissions on submitted invoices before cancellations and credits settle. Define commission eligibility on collected eligible spread, subject to lawful employment and contractor terms, rather than inventing retroactive wage deductions. An illustrative $20,000 invoice batch at 45% retained share produces $9,000 before remaining costs. A 10% commission on that spread is $900, not $2,000 on gross billings. If a later $4,000 credit reverses billings, recalculate actual property costs rather than automatically assuming 45% retention survives the cancellation. Finally, keep the canonical scenarios separate from downside overlays. A $7,500 incremental unrecovered loss would reduce Base owner profit from $150,100 to $142,600, all else equal; it does not redefine the Base scorecard. Run overlays for one delayed payer, two simultaneous closeouts, and a prolonged unit outage before adding fixed landlord commitments.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Preserve the placement evidence bundle
   - Export authorizations, contracts, occupancy events, invoices, and condition records.
   - Use a placement identifier and dated versions; retain originals and restrict editing so later corrections remain distinguishable from original evidence.
2. Rebuild the actual unit waterfall
   - Separate gross billings, credits, property pass-throughs, remaining expenses, and cash.
   - Reconcile each amount to source records and distinguish disputed receivables, deposit liabilities, and incurred costs from collected contribution.
3. Assign recovery and contain expansion
   - Give each exception a responsible person, contractual basis, deadline, and next action.
   - Pause new exposure to the affected property or payer when reserve coverage fails; continue essential resident services and existing contractual obligations.
4. Close only after ledger reconciliation
   - Record collections, supported credits, write-offs, and corrective contract changes.
   - Verify the resident statement, portal balance, accounting ledger, and bank activity agree before closing the incident and restoring normal acquisition limits.

## 3. Treat possession, occupancy taxes, and safety as property-specific constraints  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The tenth trap is signing a residential lease with a quiet assumption that furnished subletting is permitted. Obtain an explicit written corporate-housing addendum covering permitted occupants, placement durations, sublease rights, assignment limits, insurance, maintenance, access, and notice. Confirm that the signatory can bind the owner and that condominium rules, lender restrictions disclosed by the owner, and local use requirements do not defeat the arrangement. A master lease cannot legalize a prohibited land use. Nor does a thirty-day minimum universally remove licensing, registration, inspection, or tax obligations. The eleventh trap is projecting a tax exemption from advertised stay length instead of the actual jurisdiction's conditions. Texas Tax Code section 156.101 addresses permanent residents for state hotel occupancy tax; document the statutory entitlement and facts supporting the claimed treatment. Separately evaluate local taxes and whether the accommodation falls within the relevant taxable category. For an illustrative taxable $5,000 charge at a verified combined 9% rate, omitted tax creates $450 of exposure before penalties and interest. Nine percent is a scenario assumption, not a Texas rate quotation. Preserve exemption support and reassess early departures before issuing the final invoice.

The twelfth trap is treating a funding cutoff as permission to recover possession immediately. Determine tenancy status from local law and facts, not the booking platform's vocabulary. Residential notice, eviction, deposit, entry, and repair requirements may apply even when an insurer or employer pays. California Civil Code section 789.3 addresses specified utility interruptions and lockout conduct intended to terminate residential occupancy. A connected lock is therefore an access-management device, not a collection weapon. Maintain a resident access exception procedure, physical backup, and verified emergency contact. If bankruptcy affects a debtor or occupant, evaluate 11 U.S.C. section 362 and its applicable exceptions before continuing collection or possession actions. The thirteenth trap is using a blanket no-pets rule to reject an assistance-animal request. The Fair Housing Act, including 42 U.S.C. section 3604, and 24 CFR section 100.204 govern covered discrimination and reasonable-accommodation duties. Use an individualized process where required; avoid automatic pet fees for assistance animals entitled to accommodation. Route sensitive disability information separately from ordinary booking notes. A screening vendor's recommendation does not replace the operator's own responsibility for lawful rental decisions and consistent treatment.

The fourteenth trap is assuming an ordinary landlord or homeowners policy covers a corporate-housing operation. Give the broker the actual occupancy model, maximum stay range, subletting structure, furnishings ownership, contractor activities, and access technology. Obtain the policy forms and endorsements establishing relevant coverage; a certificate summarizes insurance but does not expand it. Review commercial liability, property contents, loss-of-income conditions, deductibles, exclusions, and any contractual additional insured requirements. Independently verify vendor coverage and licenses where the trade requires them. The fifteenth trap is collecting telemetry without an incident response. A water alert that sits overnight can turn a replaceable hose into an uninhabitable unit. Adopt internal targets of fifteen minutes for alert acknowledgment and immediate dispatch for confirmed active leakage, with local emergency services used when appropriate. These are staffing commitments, not universal statutory deadlines. Use disclosed, privacy-respecting noise-level sensors rather than covert recording. Any smoke, carbon-monoxide, electrical, or essential-service defect requiring immediate action takes the unit out of sale until qualified personnel verify remediation. Price the alternative accommodation and uncovered deductible into the incident reserve instead of assuming every safety event is reimbursable by a carrier.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight verifications before possession changes hands**
> 
> Complete these checks for each property and placement; a prior successful stay does not establish permission for a different unit, debtor, or occupancy pattern.

> - Verify the legal debtor, approved dates, authorized rate, cancellation allocation, and reserve coverage for the remaining master-lease tail.
> - Verify occupancy-tax treatment, deposit handling, resident notices, and accommodation procedures against the applicable jurisdiction and tenancy facts.
> - Verify coverage documents, tested resident access, working safety equipment, and a staffed escalation path for essential-service failures.

Google's eligibility rules exclude lead-generation agents or companies as such. A real partner's physical shop or yard, legitimate business activity, and successful live video verification support evidence; none alone guarantees eligibility. The partner should control its own listing and authorize manager access. Follow service-area address-display rules and never manufacture storefronts at mailboxes, vacant apartments, or unstaffed borrowed locations. Use call-tracking numbers only where they accurately reach the represented business and satisfy current platform rules. Illustratively, $600 of monthly content and call-routing cost produces twenty calls, twelve accepted qualified leads, and $90 per accepted lead: $1,080 revenue and $480 contribution before remaining overhead. This is a separate play illustration, not additional revenue embedded in the volume's canonical housing cases. Written lead acceptance criteria, duplicate handling, and refund terms prevent a high call count from disguising uncollectible placements or disputed referral invoices.

The sixteenth trap is using blackhat shortcuts to create apparent distribution. Fake reviews, review gating, paid positive sentiment, keyword-stuffed business names, fabricated addresses, and duplicate profiles can produce suspension, lost verification, or account restrictions that disable an entire acquisition channel. Request honest reviews consistently without conditioning requests on satisfaction. Never solicit a review that exposes a household's claim details or displacement circumstances. Retain partner permission, genuine business documents, signage evidence where relevant, and a change history for reinstatement submissions. The required fallback is to launch profiles only for eligible real partners with physical shops or yards capable of live verification, pairing each legitimate listing with organic city rank-and-rent pages. Those pages must truthfully identify the represented service and routing relationship, avoid fabricated local presence, and provide substantive local information instead of doorway-page spam. If eligibility fails, do not launch the profile; retain truthful organic acquisition and direct partner referrals. If suspension occurs, correct the underlying issue and use the official appeal process, not replacement accounts. At $480 illustrative monthly contribution, three lost lead batches worth $160 each erase the month's result. Diversify acquisition before relying on that contribution to fund fixed housing obligations.

The seventeenth trap is connecting automation to irreversible actions without validating source authority. Portal synchronization should create proposed updates when dates, prices, or debtor identity change; it should not silently rewrite executed agreements. Use stable placement identifiers, idempotent invoice keys, role-limited access, and a daily exception report. Test duplicate extension events, delayed cancellations, time-zone boundaries, sensor outages, and a household moving between units mid-stay. Configure an internal review trigger for any duplicate bill, any unauthorized date extension, utility costs 20% above the comparable baseline, or more than two unresolved access incidents within seven days. These thresholds initiate investigation rather than prove misconduct or authorize eviction. Track recovery time, cash at risk, and recurrence by root cause. With Base staffing of one full-time operator and three contractors, assign one accountable incident owner and named backup coverage; six people at Scaled means two full-time staff and four contractors, not unlimited response capacity. Reopen expansion only after evidence, ledger corrections, resident remedies, and reserve coverage are verified. The durable edge is fewer unpriced obligations per placement, not more listings, more sensors, or faster invoices disconnected from contractual authority and actual cash collection.
