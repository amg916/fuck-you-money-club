## 1. Establish property authority before quoting a placement  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an address-level permission file, not a citywide assumption that furnished rentals become unrestricted on day thirty. Obtain the executed master lease, an explicit written landlord corporate-housing sublease addendum, applicable association documents, and the current zoning and occupancy classification. The addendum should authorize successive displaced-policyholder and corporate occupants, permitted stay lengths, furnishings, named booking channels, utility administration, and the actual operator entity. A generic guest clause does not reliably authorize a commercial subleasing business. Confirm whether the municipality regulates transient lodging, residential rental registration, business operation, or each separately; a state tax exemption does not settle local land-use permission. California Civil Code section 1940 illustrates why a lodging label cannot automatically displace residential occupancy rules. Keep the written agency response or published code provision with its effective date and address-specific interpretation. For a proposed $3,000 monthly master lease, signing before permission is resolved creates $9,000 of exposure over three months before furnishings or utilities. Make execution conditional on the necessary permissions where commercially achievable. If the landlord declines express sublease authority, reject that property rather than substitute an informal email about guests. Preserve the declined-property record so sales cannot reintroduce the same unit through a different listing.

Treat occupancy taxes as a separate decision tree for each jurisdiction and each stay. Texas Tax Code section 156.101 addresses permanent-resident treatment for at least thirty consecutive days; the Comptroller's documentation rules distinguish a qualifying written advance commitment from a guest who only later reaches the threshold. Florida Statutes section 212.03 generally addresses transient rentals of six months or less, with longer continuous arrangements and other exceptions requiring their own analysis. Neither example supports a national thirty-day exemption. Record who occupies the unit, who contracts, the uninterrupted dates, whether unit transfers matter, and which state and local taxes apply. A carrier paying the invoice does not itself make the occupant exempt. At an illustrative 8% applicable lodging tax, a taxable $5,000 housing charge creates $400 of collected tax, not $400 of operator spread. Quote taxes separately and document the treatment of extensions, cancellations, cleaning charges, and utility charges under the local rules. Keep certificates and occupancy evidence for the jurisdiction's retention period. If exemption evidence is incomplete, use the legally required collection treatment and correct it through the authorized refund process. Never backdate a commitment merely to improve booking economics or overwrite the original invoice trail.

Build habitability and access rules into operations before connecting Smart-lock or leak telemetry. California Civil Code section 1941.1 supplies concrete habitability requirements, while section 1954 limits landlord entry and prescribes notice rules with exceptions. Other states use different repair, entry, deposit, and termination provisions; select the actual address's rules rather than copying a California form nationwide. Federal Fair Housing Act provisions, including 42 U.S.C. section 3604, also require attention to discriminatory advertising, selection, and disability-related accommodations in covered housing. Screen for a valid payer and lawful occupancy criteria, not the family's protected characteristics. Separate assistance-animal requests from ordinary pet pricing and route them through the applicable accommodation process. Ask the insurance producer to confirm commercial liability coverage, business personal property, subtenant occupancy, and any vacancy restrictions in writing. Insurance requirements may arise from the lease, carrier vendor contract, or local licensing rule; there is no universal federal policy limit for this model. A certificate alone does not amend exclusions. Disclose noise measurement without audio recording, minimize stored access logs, and prohibit interior surveillance. When repairs make a unit unsuitable, fund the documented relocation remedy rather than use a lock code change to force departure.

## 2. Separate housing supply from brokerage and insurance claims work  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Describe the business according to what it actually does. An operator leasing inventory as principal and supplying its own authorized housing differs from an intermediary arranging another owner's lease for compensation. California Business and Professions Code section 10131 includes compensated leasing activity for another within its broker definition; Texas Occupations Code section 1101.002 likewise defines regulated brokerage activities. Exemptions and entity treatment require jurisdiction-specific review, especially when staff solicit properties, negotiate third-party terms, or receive referral compensation. Calling a payment a coordination fee does not determine its licensing treatment. Put a transaction diagram in the operating file showing the landlord, master lessee, occupant, employer, housing coordinator, and actual payer. For a $5,000 monthly booking against $2,750 of landlord rent and utility pass-throughs, the modeled retained spread is $2,250; that arithmetic does not establish whether the conduct requires a license. If a prospect only wants introductions to unrelated landlords, route the engagement through an appropriately licensed brokerage arrangement or decline that service. Do not have an unlicensed contractor negotiate another owner's lease under a principal-inventory sales script. Reassess the boundary when entering each state, adding managed inventory, or changing who signs the resident agreement and receives compensation.

ALE means additional living expense coverage, not an unlimited carrier purchase order. The policyholder's policy, covered loss, applicable limits, reasonable expense standard, and authorized dates drive reimbursement. Supplying availability, invoices, and move-in records is different from interpreting coverage or negotiating the insured's claim. Texas Insurance Code section 4102.001 defines public insurance adjusting activities; other states have their own definitions, exemptions, and restrictions. Keep staff within factual housing coordination unless the business has the authorization and licensing required for broader work. Ask the adjuster or authorized coordinator to identify the funding window and billing process, but verify the actual contract obligor. A carrier email approving a rate may not make that carrier a party to the housing agreement. If ninety days at $5,000 per month are requested but only thirty days are funded, show $5,000 authorized and $10,000 contingent, not $15,000 collectible. Obtain a lawful, clearly accepted allocation of extension and cancellation liability. When authorization ends, pursue renewal or the contractual exit process without promising coverage, charging undisclosed balances to the household, or threatening immediate removal. Never submit fabricated occupancy dates or inflated utility expenses to bridge a funding shortfall.

Set privacy boundaries around the minimum facts needed to house the resident. An ALE referral often contains claim identifiers, displacement circumstances, employer details, and accessibility requests; none justifies collecting the full claim file by default. Use role-restricted folders, documented retention periods, encrypted transfer, and separate booking records from public directory profiles. Ask ALE Solutions or CRS which vendor portal functions and export permissions are actually available under the signed relationship; portal synchronization is an automation lever, not permission to scrape credentials or republish household data. If a consumer report influences housing eligibility, the Fair Credit Reporting Act's permissible-purpose rules under 15 U.S.C. section 1681b and adverse-action duties under section 1681m may apply. An employer or carrier paying does not automatically eliminate the resident's rights. For covered pre-1978 housing, evaluate lead-disclosure requirements under 42 U.S.C. section 4852d and applicable implementing exceptions before execution. Capture electronic signatures with attribution and accessible retained copies under the applicable E-SIGN and state rules, while checking documents requiring another delivery method. If a vendor requests excessive data, narrow the transfer to booking dates, approved occupants, contact details, and authorized charges. Document the rejected fields and contractual reason for exclusion.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Draw the transaction and licensing map
   - Identify every contracting entity, property interest, compensation stream, and negotiation task.
   - Separate principal inventory supply from third-party brokerage and insurance claim representation for each operating state.
2. Collect the booking authority packet
   - Obtain landlord permission, permitted-use evidence, insurance confirmation, and the proposed funding authorization.
   - Record the payer's legal name, authorized representative, funded dates, rate, tax treatment, and invoice submission route.
3. Execute matching resident and payer terms
   - Resolve any mismatch between landlord exposure, resident rights, and the payer's cancellation provisions.
   - Deliver applicable disclosures and signed copies before access, and record responsibility for extensions and disputed charges.
4. Reconcile funding throughout occupancy
   - Review approaching authorization expirations and unpaid invoices against the live occupancy ledger.
   - Request extensions early, preserve written decisions, and use lawful termination or relocation procedures when funding stops.

## 3. Draft clauses that survive extensions, closeouts, and collections  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a contract stack with aligned definitions: landlord master lease and sublease addendum, payer or coordinator booking agreement, resident occupancy agreement, and vendor work orders. Define the premises, furnished inventory, named occupants, permitted use, included utilities, service frequency, accessibility commitments, and maintenance response. Identify which document controls if terms conflict, without pretending the hierarchy overrides mandatory resident protections. The fee trigger should distinguish reservation acceptance, access delivery, actual occupancy, and a legally enforceable cancellation charge. Specify billing intervals, authorized taxes, deposits, invoice evidence, dispute deadlines, and whether charges are prepaid or invoiced after service. Avoid treating refundable deposits as earned revenue. A utility cap needs a measurable billing period, included allowance, meter or invoice evidence, allocation method, and lawful collection process. If the cap is $250 and eligible actual utilities are $330, the proposed overage is $80, not a discretionary convenience charge. Confirm whether applicable law allows that allocation and whether the payer authorized it. Keep photographs and a signed inventory schedule for damage disputes. Separate ordinary wear, resident-caused damage, contractor mistakes, and insured losses so each follows the correct remedy rather than an automatic deduction from any money available.

Match extension and early-closeout terms to the master-lease exposure before promising flexibility. A carrier's request for immediate cancellation does not automatically release the operator from landlord rent, and a resident's legal possession does not end merely because reimbursement stops. Define extension request timing, who can accept, the new rate, minimum increment, and written funding evidence. Use reminders fourteen and seven days before authorization expires, with a named human resolving exceptions. For a thirty-day illustrative booking at $5,000, an evenly prorated daily charge is $166.67 after rounding; cancellation after twenty days leaves approximately $1,666.67 of planned gross billings exposed. The 45% model implies about $750 of lost spread only if related pass-through costs fall proportionately. A fixed landlord commitment can make the actual loss larger. Draft cancellation charges and mitigation credits for enforceability under the governing law, avoiding a punitive amount unrelated to permitted recovery. State how replacement bookings reduce overlapping claims. Require documented notice and a lawful possession process; never substitute remote lock deactivation. If the counterparty demands day-to-day cancellation while the landlord requires fixed months, increase reserves, obtain matching flexibility, reprice the unit, or decline the booking rather than conceal the duration mismatch.

Keep anti-circumvention narrow: identify introduced counterparties, a reasonable protected period, excluded preexisting relationships, the covered transaction, and a lawful measure of loss. Do not prohibit a resident from seeking suitable housing or an insurer from performing claim duties. Allocate indemnity according to each party's conduct and control, then negotiate a liability cap with appropriate treatment of bodily injury, property damage, fraud, data incidents, and duties that cannot be waived. A cap based on one month's $5,000 fee may leave a severe mismatch with a much larger property claim; insurance limits and exclusions must be evaluated separately. Specify governing law, venue, notice addresses, escalation contacts, and mediation or arbitration mechanics only where enforceable for the parties and claim involved. Preserve mandatory resident remedies. IP and data clauses should distinguish operator templates and directory scoring methods from resident records, client confidential material, and vendor software. Grant only necessary service licenses; prohibit public reuse of claim narratives. Define export format, deletion timing, security incident notification, and subcontractor restrictions. Keep a versioned clause register showing who accepted changes. If a coordinator's procurement form overrides payment or liability terms, resolve the conflict before occupancy rather than rely on a sales email that procurement never incorporated.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the spread where experienced operators expect friction**
> 
> The strongest savings usually come from matched obligations and cleaner evidence, not more aggressive collections language.
> - Negotiate a landlord extension option before taking a booking whose repair timeline can move; an option is more useful than an informal promise of future availability.
> - Put authorization end dates beside lease commitment end dates in the same exception report, then assign one person to every unmatched interval.
> - Send utility overage evidence with the first invoice rather than waiting for a dispute; distinguish actual consumption from administrative fees.
> - Preserve an immutable version of each published directory ranking, while selling clearly separated sponsored inventory without editing the earned score.

Use permission-aware outreach that identifies the commercial purpose and keeps claims precise. Will you authorize corporate-housing subleases in a signed addendum, and which registration or association restrictions apply?' Log answers as qualification evidence, not as a binding contract unless the recipient has authority and actually accepts the terms.
