## 1. Reconcile gross settlements before declaring a retained margin  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Begin with the scorecard denominator: gross billable inbound-call and ping-post settlements, not a homeowner's repair invoice, an insurance premium, a legal recovery, or an advertiser's entire media budget. Formula v2.0 assigns 55% retained share after publisher payouts and click-to-call acquisition. Consequently, $200,000, $850,000, and $1,500,000 of annual gross billings produce $110,000, $467,500, and $825,000 of retained contribution. The accompanying waterfall allocates the other 45% to publisher payouts at 30% and paid media at 15%; that allocation is an illustrative operating assumption, not another supplied scorecard fact. These are blended portfolio costs: individual calls need not incur both a publisher commission and direct media expense. Keep the scorecard's ASC 606 retained-share convention separate from the statutory financial statements. ASC 606-10-55-36 through 55-40 requires a principal-versus-agent assessment based on control of the specified service. Purchased advertising does not automatically become a revenue deduction merely because the management model nets it against settlements. Maintain a bridge from gross buyer billing to recognized revenue, acquisition expense, and economic retained contribution. Otherwise, the same media invoice can disappear from costs while inflated settlements remain in reported revenue.

The remaining deductions must reconcile exactly, rather than being chosen to make the opportunity appear attractive. In the Base case, subtract $25,500 of expected scrubs and reversals, $96,000 of contractor delivery labor, $36,000 of platform and verification costs, $60,000 of staff and administration, $12,000 of funding costs, and $38,250 of other operating costs from $467,500. The result is precisely $199,750. Conservative deductions total $80,000; Scaled deductions total $345,000. Their resulting profits are $30,000 and $480,000. These expense categories are a reconciled illustrative budget, not claimed vendor quotations or independently observed operating results. The contractor line funds campaign operations, call-quality review, buyer reconciliation, and technical maintenance; contractor counts are people, not assumed full-time equivalents. The first full-time position is the working owner, whose compensation comes from modeled owner profit. The Scaled staff budget also supports the additional employee. Treat owner profit as business earnings before the owner's personal taxes and distributions. A separately salaried replacement operator would reduce those earnings. Keep that distinction visible when comparing an owner-operated exchange with a genuinely passive portfolio or a management-heavy call center.

Gross-basis profit margins remain exactly 15%, 23.5%, and 32%. Dividing the same profits by retained contribution instead produces 27.27%, 42.73%, and 58.18%, rounded to two decimals. Never present the larger retained margin beside gross billings without labeling both denominators. The model places a 3% gross-billing scrub provision below the retained subtotal so readers can see its economic effect. In the accounting ledger, expected contractual price concessions may instead reduce the transaction price, while genuine credit losses require separate treatment. Reclassify those items without charging them twice. Likewise, a buyer's recoverable holdback is an asset-timing issue, not another expense. The published monthly operating range of $2,500 to $14,000 must not be mistaken for the complete waterfall: it can describe a narrower recurring-overhead perimeter, but it cannot cover every modeled payout, contractor invoice, reserve, and funding charge. Base deductions after retained contribution average $22,312.50 monthly, including the scrub provision. Establish an explicit account map before using either range in a cash forecast. At month-end, reconcile auction exports, buyer statements, publisher payables, media invoices, and bank receipts into the same cohort identifiers.

## 2. Price accepted cohorts and isolate the two mapped acquisition plays  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Normalize the Base case to a 1,000-call billable cohort at an illustrative $100 average settlement. Gross billing is $100,000, publisher allocation is $30,000, and media allocation is $15,000, leaving $55,000 retained contribution. Applying the annual Base ratios adds $3,000 of expected reversals and $28,500 of remaining operating deductions, leaving $23,500 owner profit. The acquisition CAC is $15 per billable call for paid media alone, or $45 including the publisher allocation. These definitions must travel with every dashboard export. Suppose 1,250 connected calls generate 1,000 billable calls after an 80% qualification rate. A 90-second or 120-second buyer buffer is a contract term, not proof that every surviving connection will settle. Ringba or Retreaver records must preserve routing identity, transfer timestamps, actual talk time, duplicate checks, geographic eligibility, and buyer responses. Use connected talk time rather than queue time whenever the contract requires it. Reconcile the buyer's clock definition before publishing a duration-based bid ceiling. At the same $15,000 media spend, deterioration to 812.5 expected billable calls from those connections raises media CAC to $18.46; maintain fractional expectations in forecasts while settling only actual whole calls.

The mapped play social_to_search_ping_post_arbitrage replaces fragile $1.50 RSOC/AFD search-feed monetization with direct $45–$180 lead auctions, but cheap curiosity clicks are not qualified demand. Buy 10,000 clicks at $0.08 across Meta, TikTok, NewsBreak, or native networks for $800. A four-question bridge converting 4% yields 400 submissions; 40% qualification yields 160 eligible leads; selling 25% of those exclusively yields 40 settlements at $75, or $3,000 gross. Deduct $800 media, $200 verification and routing, $300 delivery labor, and a deliberately higher 5% reversal allowance of $150: contribution is $1,550 before shared overhead and funding. This lane-level example does not replace the portfolio's canonical 55% retained share. At $0.12 clicks, 2% submission, 40% qualification, 20% sale, and $45 settlement, gross falls to $720. With $1,200 media, the same $500 service costs, and $36 reversals, contribution becomes negative $1,016. Use entirely factual hooks, retain TrustedForm or Jornaya session evidence for every submission, and cryptographically hash the stored evidence bundle. Preserve vendor certificate identifiers and available signature verification without assuming every vendor artifact is digitally signed. Diversify across at least five direct buyers.

The second mapped play, agency_ad_spend_card_multiplier_rebate_cycling, is a treasury optimization on genuine client-authorized advertising, not a reason to create more spend. On $50,000 of eligible spend, a contract-permitted 2% card reward produces $1,000 and a 0.4% volume rebate adds $200. Deduct 0.8% processing costs of $400, a contractual client pass-through of $200, and 20 days of funding at 18% annual interest, or $493.15. Net incremental benefit is only $106.85. With unchanged terms, funding beyond roughly 24.3 days consumes the entire remaining benefit. Record reward eligibility, category exclusions, redemption value, clawbacks, and statement dates rather than counting advertised points at face value. The $50,000 client advertising flow is neither operator settlement revenue nor automatic profit. Track it in a separate client-funds and reimbursement ledger, subject to the actual contractual arrangement. Neither rewards nor volume rebates are needed to reach the canonical profit cases in this chapter. If permission, eligibility, or collections weaken, move immediately to client-direct billing or prefunded approved agency terms. Stop rewards-dependent spending, and never substitute circular charges, artificial transactions, or unapproved client credit exposure for an economically sound advertising campaign.

> - Store buyer quote expiry and destination capacity with every ping; an attractive expired bid has zero executable value.

## 3. Fund the collection gap without mistaking holdbacks for losses  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build working capital from contractual cash dates rather than a monthly profit percentage. Let annual gross billings be G and use 365 calendar days. Assume buyers pay 90% after 21 days and release the remaining 10% another 30 days later. Publishers receive their 30% allocation after seven days; media consumes its 15% allocation immediately. The acquisition bridge is therefore G divided by 365, multiplied by the sum of 0.30 times 14 and 0.15 times 21. This gives $4,027.40 Conservative, $17,116.44 Base, and $30,205.48 Scaled. The additional rolling buyer holdback consumes 10% of daily gross billings times 30 days, or $1,643.84, $6,986.30, and $12,328.77. This calculation assumes the holdback begins releasing predictably after its extra lock period; a discretionary or continually extended holdback requires a different forecast. Track age from the actual contractual invoice or acceptance event, not whichever timestamp makes collection speed look better. Keep a daily receivables schedule by buyer legal entity, because five destinations controlled by one debtor do not create five independent sources of liquidity. Measure cash received rather than dashboard balances marked payable.

Add a 30-day operating cash reserve for deductions other than the noncash scrub provision. The annual cash-cost bases are $74,000, $242,250, and $300,000; multiplying each by 30/365 gives $6,082.19, $19,910.96, and $24,657.53. Acquisition bridge, recoverable holdback, and operating reserve together require approximately $11,753, $44,014, and $67,192. These are planning requirements under the stated assumptions, not universal minimum balances; they exclude setup purchases, taxes, owner distributions, and an additional cash cushion for adverse disputes. The $8,000–$45,000 startup-capital range therefore supports only particular launch configurations. Base nearly exhausts its upper end before setup spending, while Scaled requires retained earnings, prepayment, improved settlement terms, or committed funding. The 3% expected reversal expense does not itself place cash in a reserve account. Conversely, moving cash into a designated bank subaccount does not create an additional profit deduction. Maintain one schedule for expected economic losses and another for restricted or intentionally protected cash. Allocate funding charges to the cohort that caused the borrowing, including statement interest and transfer fees. A campaign that appears profitable before its settlement delay can still destroy contribution after expensive financing.

Before increasing publisher caps, prove net-seven buyer collectability with actual bank receipts on several small, fully documented cohorts. A promised net-seven term is not the same as an observed seven-day DSO. If buyers really pay in seven days, publisher timing aligns with their receipts, and only seven days of immediately paid media require the initial acquisition bridge; holdbacks still need separate funding. Negotiate payout locks around the buyer's documented review window, with explicit release dates and dispute procedures, rather than silently extending publisher payment terms. Publish source-level credit caps, single-buyer exposure limits, and minimum cash thresholds in the routing configuration. When collections slip, reduce new traffic while honoring existing obligations. A 14-day collection delay adds gross receivables exposure of $7,671.23 Conservative, $32,602.74 Base, or $57,534.25 Scaled; these figures measure additional cash awaiting collection, not an automatic expense or write-off. Model that shock in the daily cash ledger alongside scheduled payroll and publisher releases. Do not count an uncommitted credit limit, an unsettled reward balance, or a buyer's disputed invoice as available cash. Pause expansion before liquidity pressure forces unplanned changes to valid publisher commitments.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the settlement cohort
   - Export call identifiers, buyer acceptances, publisher obligations, and media spend into a dated settlement ledger.
   - Preserve original source events and record later adjustments as separate entries instead of overwriting the first settlement view.
2. Classify every deduction
   - Separate contractual reversals, credit losses, vendor invoices, recoverable buyer holdbacks, and cash transfers.
   - Apply each economic deduction once, then bridge accounting classification to the scorecard's retained-contribution presentation.
3. Project cash by payment date
   - Schedule buyer receipts, holdback releases, publisher payments, contractor invoices, media charges, and funding costs daily.
   - Run both observed DSO and a fourteen-day delay while excluding uncommitted financing from available liquidity.
4. Release only funded traffic capacity
   - Set buyer and publisher caps from collected contribution, evidence quality, and available settlement funding.
   - Increase caps after repeated timely receipts; reduce new acquisition when the forecast breaches the operating cash floor.

## 4. Stress the spread and price evidence failures before scaling  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Sensitivity testing starts with the same canonical waterfall, not a more favorable alternate model. Holding billable volume constant, a 25% increase in the 15%-of-gross media budget costs another 3.75% of original gross billings. Annual owner profit falls to $22,500 Conservative, $167,875 Base, and $423,750 Scaled. Increasing the scrub rate from 3% to 8% independently reduces profits to $20,000, $157,250, and $405,000. Together, those shocks leave $12,500, $125,375, and $348,750. The apparently strong Scaled margin does not eliminate buyer concentration or liquidity risk. A separate 10% settlement-price decline, with call counts and all non-scrub costs fixed, reduces profit by 9.7% of original gross billings because the 3% scrub provision also falls with price. Resulting profits are $10,600, $117,300, and $334,500. These tests deliberately isolate assumptions; do not add their results mechanically when changes share the same underlying cause. Model conversion deterioration separately from CPC inflation, and recompute contractor workload when more rejected calls require review. Establish source-level stop rules using realized contribution and cash exposure rather than the campaign's original underwriting spread or the most recent winning bid.

Evidence requirements change both acceptance probability and delivery cost. Under 47 U.S.C. 227 and 47 CFR 64.1200, determine the requirements for the actual communication, technology, purpose, and recipient; a consumer-initiated inbound call does not automatically authorize later automated marketing texts or outbound calls. The FCC's 2023 federal one-to-one consent amendment was vacated in Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC in January 2025, so do not describe it as a current universal federal requirement. Seller-specific permission remains a useful operating standard, and Medicare TPMO data-sharing restrictions separately require particularized treatment under 42 CFR 422.2274 and 423.2274. For covered Medicare activity, apply the relevant recording, retention, and 48-hour scope-of-appointment rules, including their actual scope and exceptions; not every inbound inquiry requires the same waiting period. Budget secure recording storage, access controls, certificate retrieval, and human exception review. TrustedForm and Jornaya artifacts document events, not a blanket legal entitlement to contact anyone. Apply DNC screening where the outbound activity requires it; a commercial litigator list is neither a substitute for permission nor evidence that an excluded consumer lacks rights. Version the rule mapping with each offer.

Legal-intake and emergency-trade auctions need their own acceptance definitions rather than an insurance template with a different payout. California Business and Professions Code sections 6151 and 6152 address runners and cappers; a fee arrangement that depends on prohibited solicitation cannot be repaired by a longer duration buffer. Confirm buyer eligibility, permitted advertising arrangements, and source restrictions before enabling legal destinations. For home-service calls, record service radius, job category, opening hours, and duplicate windows so an answered emergency call does not become a predictable refund. Reserve rates should follow buyer and source vintages: track initial billing, day-seven disputes, day-thirty settlements, and late reversals. Retain evidence through the applicable dispute and statutory periods, with access limited to assigned reviewers. Require a reason code and supporting event for every buyer scrub, then measure recovery rates and review time. Use sub-second RTB routing to enforce capacity and economics, not to outrun missing evidence. When review costs exceed recoverable spread, narrow the source or buyer relationship. Scale only the cohorts whose collected economics survive adverse pricing, documented reversals, and realistic payment delays; automation accelerates both good underwriting and expensive mistakes.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four verifications before increasing a traffic cap**
> 
> Release additional acquisition capacity only when unit economics, evidence, collection performance, and funding agree.
> - Verify that every modeled settlement uses the signed buyer price, duration clock, geographic restrictions, duplicate window, and reversal deadline.
> - Verify applicable seller permission, Medicare recording and appointment requirements, and legal-intake source restrictions using versioned offer documentation.
> - Verify repeated buyer bank receipts against promised net-seven terms before relaxing publisher traffic caps or shortening payout locks.
> - Verify that available cash covers acquisition timing, recoverable holdbacks, operating payments, and the fourteen-day collection-delay scenario without relying on rewards.
