## The Practitioner Consensus

Insurance is the default starting vertical for buyers and sellers of calls alike. Dave Mamann of WeCall calls it one of the top paying verticals because nearly every American carries a policy, the companies are enormous, budgets tend to be huge, and demand is somewhat recession-proof [HxYmbtP1ydQ @ 00:01:00]. Carlos Corona, who scaled from $2,000 a week to six-figure days, tells new operators to pick large populations and start with insurance before financial, home services, and legal [7Q7WzSONnX4 @ 00:32:22]. On the agent side, the consensus mindset is that an inbound call is a conversation already bought. Tyler Glennon says to always answer inbound calls because each one is a free conversation, while warning that transfers suit agents with money rather than time, since a single conversation can run $50 to $100 [LvZ0uD7-Xvs @ 00:01:31]. Glennon's own shorthand is that you either have time or money in this business. The Integrity Connect training puts it bluntly to agents on the Ringba program: you treat it like you already paid for it [N3imbfBa8rI @ 00:36:38]. That framing drives everything downstream: fallback products for confused callers, callbacks on hang-ups, and refusing to burn a billable call on a rushed price quote.

Practitioners agree that revenue per call, or RPC, is the number that decides whether an agent keeps getting calls, and they explain the math from the publisher's chair. Trey and Shanti Berlin of Ringba lay it out: a $5 RPC forces publishers to produce calls for about $4, while a $20 RPC lets them spend about $17 per call, and ads stating $1 to $2 per day cost at least $20 per call [N3imbfBa8rI @ 00:14:24]. Adeline runs the same arithmetic on a $50 billable call: if an agent takes 1 in 5, the publisher earns $10 per call and has to generate calls for $6 to $8, but agents who take half get the $15 to $20 calls instead [-YRrdoKG1xs @ 00:12:11]. The billing trigger is the buffer. PRiMeCalls defines it as the time on a call before it becomes billable, and a caller who answers yes to all four qualifying questions is a qualified, billable call even if they hang up afterward [B5upgjqvbi4 @ 00:01:45]. The Integrity Connect team warns that rejecting good prospects trains the algorithm toward worse calls [N3imbfBa8rI @ 00:19:18]. The counterintuitive conclusion: taking more billable calls buys better traffic. As Adeline puts it, the more calls you take, the less calls you'll get of people thinking it's free.

Across six different trainers, the opening of an inbound or transferred call follows the same rules: take control with a question, hold price back, and qualify fast. Cody Askins says 99% of agents open with how can I help you, which hands control to the customer, and eighty seconds later the prospect hangs up; the rule is that the first couple minutes are key [lRuXMEO6hbI @ 00:24:31]. In the opener-to-closer workflow, Askins qualifies in the first 3 to 5 minutes on area, health, payment, and age, accepting checking or savings only and refusing direct bill, Direct Express, and money orders, because interest is already there and time is the most precious asset [E5sK0JQaXSY @ 00:05:08]. Adeline avoids giving a price in the first 90 seconds and says tonality matters more than the words [-YRrdoKG1xs @ 00:17:09]. Trey's breakdown of Melissa's calls makes the same point from the other direction: Melissa never rushed, while other agents pounce with a price to avoid the $55 billable charge, and prices given too early sound expensive [XaPPDdvtCAA @ 00:28:10]. Askins ends every single turn with a question, warning that one bad question surrenders all control and that more information only breeds analysis paralysis.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Buffer discipline is the first mechanic every buyer learns. PRiMeCalls publishes campaign-specific buffers: 20 seconds on final expense raw inbound, 180 seconds on final expense inbound transfer, 90 seconds each on FE Spanish, FEX straight inbound, and FEX AI inbound, and 60 seconds on CTV call inbound [B5upgjqvbi4 @ 00:00:36]. On a 20-second call the agent only verifies a live person and hangs up before 20 seconds if nobody greets them. On 60, 90, and 180-second calls the agent asks four questions inside the buffer: interested in a final expense policy, under 85, has a checking or savings account, and understands the plans are not free [B5upgjqvbi4 @ 00:01:08]. The hard rule is never to quote pricing during the buffer, because a price inside the buffer makes the call billable, and disputes on calls past the buffer are usually denied. Integrity Connect runs the same no-price rule inside 90 seconds [N3imbfBa8rI @ 00:17:51]. The billable charge on that program runs $55, which is exactly why Trey says other agents pounce with a price early, and why Trey held up Melissa's patient first 90 seconds, covering not free, most affordable payment, and spouse or kids on the line if needed, as the model [XaPPDdvtCAA @ 00:26:43].

The scripts that close are sequenced, not improvised. Melissa's order on Integrity Connect inbounds starts with a recorded-line opener and the line that price is going to be dependent on age, a few health questions, and a few discount questions, then moves through state, age, living independently, a written-down name and direct number with a digital business card by text, broker framing, a beneficiary story, and a $15,000 average burial cost [XaPPDdvtCAA @ 00:09:33]. The close lands on Americo at $10k for $50 a month or $15k for $75. Adeline kills the Social Security number objection by saying no medical exam early and again right before asking, and presents Transamerica FE Express at $10k for $59, $20k for $80, and $30k for $100 [-YRrdoKG1xs @ 00:27:27]. Alexander Strate frames the application as two minutes of health and two minutes of money, then counters a tight budget by pushing the draft date up to 30 days out [xdbb7zNo-kk @ 00:06:24]. Peter Roberts shops about 26 companies and, for a prospect with COPD, quotes Royal Neighbors of America at $7,000 for $88 a month, the only South Carolina option with no two-year wait, and a carrier that pays claims in 24 to 48 hours [6NKrG957hww @ 00:08:31].

The buying platforms and performance standards are where the unit economics get enforced. John Ventura's dashboard example shows $347 paid per enrollment against a $79 cost, with 700 calls turning into 180 policies, and the platform lets agents listen, barge, and whisper on live calls [UFWPlbDFPOg @ 00:04:29]. Ventura's traffic flow runs CMS-approved, benefit-led ads into a quiz funnel, reveals the phone number, then has an IVR re-ask the same questions to make sure the caller is the right person before connecting [UFWPlbDFPOg @ 00:16:27]. For agents building their own stack, JustCall's review prices Ringba at $127 a month with an IVR builder that screens each caller before an agent picks up and recording billed at one cent a minute [DOcBtaUIjpE @ 00:03:52]. On the Integrity Connect x Ringba program, the standard is a 50-call running total: an RPC under $5, possibly raised to $8, gets the agent turned off, and the stated target is taking three calls out of every 10 past 90 seconds [N3imbfBa8rI @ 00:26:18]. Penalties escalate from a temporary pause to a one-week pause to removal. Agencies on the program report a 4 to 5x return, with $500 spent yielding $2,000 to $2,500 issued, and reformed agents climb to a $26 to $27 RPC.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Configure and fund the buy
   - Set carriers, products, states, and wallet balance before the first call routes.
   - Ventura's call sync signup asks for carriers, lead sources, products, and states in under 60 seconds, captures a card with no charge, and has the agent load about $250 into the wallet; calls only arrive from the selected states [John Ventura — UFWPlbDFPOg @ 00:02:40].
2. Work the buffer clean
   - Run the four qualifiers inside the buffer and never quote price there.
   - Ask about interest in a final expense policy, under 85, a checking or savings account, and understanding the plans are not free; four yeses make the call billable, and a price quoted inside the buffer makes it billable too [PRiMeCalls — B5upgjqvbi4 @ 00:01:08].
3. Run the script spine
   - Open on a recorded line, defer price to age, health, and discounts, and anchor your direct number.
   - Use the opener that price is dependent on age, a few health questions, and a few discount questions, collect state and age, have the prospect write down your name and direct number, and text a digital business card [FFL Success Zone | Integrity Connect Inbounds — XaPPDdvtCAA @ 00:09:33].
4. Lock persistency after the sale
   - Stay in contact before the first draft so the policy sticks.
   - Adeline positions the agent as family, makes a reminder call the day before the draft, sends a thank-you card with a gift card, and shares a digital business card with photo and license number [FFL Success Zone | Integrity Connect Inbounds — -YRrdoKG1xs @ 00:32:04].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Carlos Corona, interviewed on Ryan Clogg's channel, gives a volume-dependent answer on how many buyers a call operation actually needs. The beginner rule is at least 10 buyers [7Q7WzSONnX4 @ 00:12:16], yet at high volume Corona concedes one huge buyer running 500 to 600 agents can probably get by, while still warning that buyers take lunch breaks and skip states [7Q7WzSONnX4 @ 00:48:40]. John Ventura's call-sync model flips the frame: the agent sits on the buy side of a single platform, selects states, loads about $250 into a wallet, and pays one flat price per call, with pricing tiers still pending [UFWPlbDFPOg @ 00:16:53]. Refund policy splits just as hard. Ventura records every call, logs IP and location, and lets agents submit dead-air calls for a refund, pitching that you are never stuck paying for bad calls. PRiMeCalls trains the opposite expectation: every campaign carries its own buffer, from 20 seconds on final expense raw inbound to 180 seconds on final expense inbound transfers, and disputes on calls that run past the buffer are usually denied [B5upgjqvbi4 @ 00:02:34]. Inside that buffer, PRiMe agents run four yes-or-no qualifiers and never quote price, since a quoted price makes the call billable on the spot.

The sharpest split is pacing. Cody Askins teaches that the third question is already the close, framed as a hypothetical, and holds up Derek Maybin running opener, commitment question, and 'Visa Mastercard or American Express' in about 4.5 minutes once interest is confirmed [lRuXMEO6hbI @ 00:15:28]. Askins also wants qualification on area, health, payment, and age inside the first three to five minutes so agents never burn 45 to 90 minutes on someone who cannot qualify [E5sK0JQaXSY @ 00:04:10]. The Integrity Connect camp coaches the reverse. Trey praised Melissa for never rushing, contrasting agents who pounce with a price to dodge the $55 billable charge, and warned that prices given too early sound expensive [XaPPDdvtCAA @ 00:26:43]. Melissa's sixteen-step order runs state, age, beneficiary story, burial math, discount questions, knockout health questions, and a budget check before any number appears. Openers diverge too. Tyler Glennon opens as the underwriting advisor and forces a choice between final expense programs or leaving a little extra for family [LvZ0uD7-Xvs @ 00:02:27], while Askins positions as the local field underwriter and Melissa opens on a recorded line stating price depends on age, health, and discount questions. Alexander Strate opens even more directly: 'help you get your life insurance set up today.'

Payment mechanics and follow-up draw open contradictions. Alexander Strate counters a tight budget by pushing the draft date up to 30 days out [xdbb7zNo-kk @ 00:11:48], while Adeline on the Integrity Connect side refuses to build draft dates into the close because prospects may call the number again and get rewritten [-YRrdoKG1xs @ 00:29:50]. On Direct Express, Cody Askins simply will not accept it, along with direct bill and money orders, whereas Melissa keeps the call alive by eliminating Americo and pivoting to Transamerica or Corebridge [XaPPDdvtCAA @ 00:33:25]. Willie Goldberg reports Direct Express prepaid cards now being screened out of the transfer pool. Follow-up splits the same way. Peter Roberts closed a Royal Neighbors of America policy on a callback after the prospect was in the hospital, calling it a live transfer that was not a one call close [6NKrG957hww @ 00:15:15]. The Integrity Connect session puts follow-up buyers at about 20 percent but warns you will go broke thinking that way, and Adeline's answer to think-about-it is submitting for approval first so the numbers are real. PRiMeCalls tells agents to call back qualified hang-ups who dropped from question fatigue, while Integrity Connect permanently bans agents caught hanging up and calling back.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The Integrity Connect inbound program punishes picky agents by the numbers. Trey and Shanti Berlin of Ringba explain that a $5 revenue per call forces publishers to produce calls for about $4, while a $20 RPC lets them spend about $17 per call, so cherry-picking directly degrades traffic [N3imbfBa8rI @ 00:14:24]. Rejecting good prospects trains the algorithm toward worse calls, and the AI flags a buyer after roughly 30 calls with no conversion. Over a 50-call running total, an RPC under $5, possibly raised to $8, gets the agent turned off, and penalties escalate from a temporary pause to a one-week pause to removal [N3imbfBa8rI @ 00:26:18]. Adeline puts the floor near $9 and runs about $18 RPC by taking roughly 40 percent of calls [-YRrdoKG1xs @ 00:07:47]. The math is explicit: at a $50 billable call with one in five taken, the publisher earns $10 per call and must generate calls for $6 to $8, while taking half unlocks the $15 to $20 calls [-YRrdoKG1xs @ 00:12:11]. Adeline's first week still produced 20-plus billable calls and only one sale before Adeline went back to Go Leads. The same training flags rudeness, profanity, pushing 'this isn't free,' and impersonating the Department of Insurance.

Lead and transfer quality fails in predictable ways. Willie Goldberg's two-week test of 49 transfers at $25 each produced 5 sales, roughly one in ten, putting cost per sale near $250 to $300, and the ACA pool was cut after a single term sale [A4zDgHX2VWI @ 00:01:15]. Goldberg's bigger warning is vendors reselling sold leads and recycling them within 30, 60, or 90 days, which means lower persistency and more replacement, plus ads that mislead with free, Social Security, or VA claims, which Goldberg calls a very shady practice [A4zDgHX2VWI @ 00:09:22]. Tyler Glennon hits the same mismatch from the agent chair: some ads promise accidental coverage at $500,000 for about $25 a month, so the agent must redirect to real coverage or sell accidental as a fallback because the lead is already paid for [LvZ0uD7-Xvs @ 00:05:08]. Carlos Corona's own scars are operational: a flights campaign built on Google showed hundreds of missed calls in Ringba three days later because the setup was incomplete, and Corona names poor distribution and call routing, not creative, as the biggest mistake [7Q7WzSONnX4 @ 00:04:54]. Dave Mamann of WeCall adds that the legal department often makes big-carrier IVRs very challenging, a roadblock usually overcome only by high-intent calls.

John Henson, the TCPA lawyer on Henson Legal, quantifies the downside for agents who buy or dial leads. The statute sets $500 per call, up to $1,500, so 10 calls means $5,000, and Mariah Gates cites one agent who settled for over $220,000, which Henson says is not the biggest number seen [8-P7UvS--tE @ 00:01:36]. Roughly 40 percent of TCPA cases come from repeat filers, one of whom admitted to over a thousand followers [8-P7UvS--tE @ 00:24:25]. Buying leads means betting on the vendor's compliance program: Henson flags $3 co-reg leads, insists consent name you and match the product, and says to seek indemnification while confirming the vendor can actually pay, noting Quote Wizard could not trace its leads [8-P7UvS--tE @ 00:29:51]. Dialer capability alone, such as RingCentral, can make a system an ATDS, AI voice counts as artificial voice, and verbal consent does not qualify for marketing. A mortgage company was sued over a fake 'calling you back, press one' AI call. On the recording side, JustCall notes Medicare sales calls must be kept for six years, as audio for at least the first three, and prices Ringba recording at one cent a minute on top of a $127 monthly plan [DOcBtaUIjpE @ 00:00:15].
