## Executive Summary

Ringba, TrackDrive, ExclusiveLiveCall, ceohec, and Decoding Ads all describe call RTB as ping-post for live phone calls. The platform pings buyers with call data, bids and durations come back in a couple hundred milliseconds, and the call routes to the best expected payout before the phone rings. The consensus prerequisites are unique tracking numbers per publisher, owning your own numbers, eligibility filters on geo, hours, caps, and concurrency, and failover so an unanswered winner never kills a paid call. The street mechanics include Ringba ring trees with a $29.01 minimum-revenue floor over static fallbacks, and RingbaX's 10-second dial and 3,000 ms ping timeouts. They also include ceohec's Retreaver build at about 4 cents a minute, with TTL 45, a 5-second timeout, and an 8-second billing buffer. Practitioners split on static versus RTB buyers, Ringba versus Retreaver, and highest-bid versus earnings-per-call routing.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every practitioner here treats real-time bidding as the ping-post model ported from lead gen to live calls. Ringba frames it plainly. The caller dials, and the platform pings every programmatic buyer with mapped metadata such as geography and call category. Buyers return a bid, a required duration, and sometimes a dynamic destination number. The call is then routed before the phone even rings, all inside a couple hundred milliseconds [yat7hRHHsKY @ 00:01:10]. ceohec, operating on Retreaver, uses the same vocabulary from the street side. Static means no ping post. RTB means the buyer's system is pinged to see if a buyer is available, and then the call is posted through. In ceohec's view, most operators getting into pay per call run RTB unless they have one buyer who can absorb volume [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:04:42]. TrackDrive lists ping post as a fifth routing mode alongside priority, weight, highest bid, and earnings per call. It describes the ping as a quick message asking whether the buyer wants the call and what they will pay [9uZila67hss @ 00:02:52]. ExclusiveLiveCall adds the coverage angle. With a ping integration you do not need the downstream buyer's destination number loaded into your platform, because the external network or marketplace returns both the bid and the routing instructions in real time [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:06:45].

The second point of agreement is that attribution gets set before any money moves. TrackDrive's rule is one tracking number per publisher and campaign, because two publishers sharing a number kills the ability to say who earned the call and breeds disputes. A sub ID scheme on top tells you which ad, page, or placement produced the call, which TrackDrive calls gold for diagnosing source performance [9uZila67hss @ 00:03:29]. ExclusiveLiveCall pushes the same logic down to the traffic source. It splits one roofing site across separate numbers for Google Ads, SEO, and each subpublisher, so origin follows the call through every routing decision [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:01:07]. Decoding Ads runs programmatic SEO sites with many numbers on the page. They advise against dropping a pay-per-call network's number directly onto the site. Instead, use your own number and route it yourself through Ringba, which also lets you stand up your own network by adding publishers [OFst3KoUCj0 @ 00:00:38]. Ringba's onboarding shows how cheap that first number is to get. It offers on-demand toll-free and local DID inventory in over 60 countries. The number is auto-assigned to you until publishers exist, and the campaign name carries through every report [8ojiGzzsrV0 @ 00:01:17].

Third, nobody lets a paid-for call die at a busy signal. Ringba's position is that if the winning ping buyer does not answer, drops, or returns busy, the platform reruns the process. It then routes to another ping buyer or a normal static buyer, so the ping tree inherits every standard routing rule [yat7hRHHsKY @ 00:04:41]. Ringba's Jordan Soto gives the buyer-side reason the model pays. Buyers can deny calls when the floor has no capacity or the caller is a bad fit, and they can bid harder on high-intent callers or when seats are empty. Soto says this yields higher payouts, fewer abandoned calls, and fatter margins [VTCVhKxmT1o @ 00:00:20]. ExclusiveLiveCall walks the eligibility funnel call by call. Start with ten buyers on a campaign. Strip out the one not covering the zip, the closed one, the one capped for the day, the one at max concurrency, and the one rejecting the traffic source, and maybe three remain [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:04:00]. Overflow then tries buyer B and C, because the publisher may already have paid Google $50 to $100 for that click [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:10:01]. TrackDrive keeps the two jobs separate when diagnosing problems. A call that went to the wrong place is a routing failure, and a call that should have been stopped is a filter failure [9uZila67hss @ 00:04:10].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Inside Ringba the build order is campaign, number, target, then payout. The account walkthrough starts with verifying billing documents, or service gets disrupted. A wizard then names the campaign, picks the country, issues a toll-free or local DID, and creates the first target. Use SIP whenever the target accepts it, because it connects faster, sounds better, and converts better. Phone-number targets must include the country code [8ojiGzzsrV0 @ 00:02:20]. The campaign training layers on the controls that protect margin. These include reporting duplicate calls, treating anonymous callers as duplicates, and paying out once per caller regardless of publisher. Recording is on by default, with an option to trim silence. Payouts can be fixed amounts triggered on call length, connection, inbound call, or conversion, or a revenue share with limits [BOFMLupLrSM @ 00:01:12]. On each target, the connection timeout defaults to 30 seconds before the call reroutes, and priority 1 is the highest. Two targets with equal weight split traffic 50/50. Caps run on conversions, connections, or revenue at overall, monthly, daily, or hourly levels, with a separate concurrency ceiling [BOFMLupLrSM @ 00:06:12]. The targets training adds tag routing filters with AND and OR rules. It also adds an RPC setting that controls how estimated revenue per call is calculated for each destination [2p-PtocR9pE @ 00:02:29].

Ring trees are where Ringba runs the auction. Matthias Musters splits the setup into two layers. The ring tree target is a normal destination with hours, caps, and concurrency, plus a request URL your buyer supplies for pinging. The ring tree houses one or more of those targets. In the demo, the ring tree carries a minimum revenue of $29.01. If no dynamic bid clears that floor, the call drops to a static buyer paying $29, then to one paying $25 [WOqDYpchQOo @ 00:03:31]. Selling into RingbaX follows an exact recipe. Create RingbaX as a buyer, and name the ring tree target by category and traffic source. Toggle dynamic number so the destination arrives during the ping and post, and set Pacific time. Paste the seller offer's bid URL with appended parameters and use GET. Parse the bid with JPath on bidAmount and parse call acceptance with JavaScript. Then build a ring tree named with an RT suffix, with zero minimum revenue and zero failure revenue. It converts on dynamic call length from connect, with a 10-second dial timeout and a 3,000 ms ping timeout [uDzWrD17wqg @ 00:02:04]. On the buy side, RingbaX offers static or Ringba RTB destinations. A higher minimum duration lowers your priority in the exchange, and hitting the daily budget stops all bidding [wyW52qkPCrc @ 00:02:26].

ceohec runs the same machine on Retreaver, which they describe as free to open and pay-as-you-go at roughly 4 cents a minute. They enable performance marketing and RTB from the shopping cart, then split campaigns by vertical and even by buffer time. They quote auto insurance buffers at anywhere from 90 to 180 seconds. The route needs only dummy data, and the IVR prompts should be left alone [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:01:09]. The default conversion criteria act as a fallback, such as a $40 lowest bid on a 120-second auto buffer, while the real bids live as tags at the publisher level. For a publisher such as Market Call, ceohec creates an RTB postback key with a TTL of 45 seconds and a 5-second timeout. They send the link over Telegram and tag the conversion criteria to the exact campaign ID, because without a response to the publisher's ping you get no calls [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:08:03]. Buyers on RTB still need a dummy number plus a pass-through ping webhook. More negative priority wins first, and a buyer left out of the campaign receives nothing. TrackDrive's launch discipline closes the loop. Place real test calls end to end, then start with conservative caps and widen them as quality proves out [ncatAE6wf_0 @ 00:02:38].

3. Wire the ping
   - Generate the RTB postback key or ring tree and send or configure the bid endpoint.

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Static versus ping-post is the first fork, and the camps split hard. ceohec tells newcomers that most of the time you don't run static unless you have one buyer that can take the volume; static just means no ping post, while RTB pings the system to see if an available buyer exists and then posts the call through, so no DID is needed [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:04:42]. ceohec reserves static for offers that are fine with calls hitting a cell phone the buyer toggles with pause and play. Ringba's buyer-side RingbaX walkthrough offers both static and Ringba RTB destinations but highly recommends RTB for the most control over how calls get bought [wyW52qkPCrc @ 00:00:38]. Ringba's masterclass refuses the either-or: a call center paying a flat $10 on a 1:30 duration and a pay-per-call network paying $11 after two minutes sit on the same routing plan as programmatic bidders, and every buyer gets pinged before the routing decision [yat7hRHHsKY @ 00:05:10]. Matthias Musters builds the hybrid concretely with a ring tree of dynamic targets above static targets and a $29.01 minimum revenue floor; if no bid clears it, the call falls to a static buyer paying $29, then one paying $25 [WOqDYpchQOo @ 00:03:50].

How the winner gets picked is the second split. Ringba's masterclass argues the raw top bid is not automatically the winner: ping buyer three bids $12 on a 1:30 duration, yet ping buyer one at $8 on a one-minute duration carries a higher probability of conversion, so Ringba scores every buyer on estimated earnings per call from previous behavior before deciding where the call goes [yat7hRHHsKY @ 00:08:04]. TrackDrive lays the options out as a menu rather than a doctrine: priority, weight, highest bid, historical earnings per call, or ping post, where buyers answer in real time whether they want the call and what they will pay [9uZila67hss @ 00:02:34]. ExclusiveLiveCall pushes back on the reflex to dump everything on the $200 buyer over the $120 and $150 buyers, because that buyer may have limited capacity, you may hold contractual volume commitments, or certain publishers may carry different buyer relationships [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:04:56]. ceohec runs the blunt version inside Retreaver: priority is set so whoever is bidding more gets the calls before anybody else, with the more negative number earning the higher priority [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:11:41]. Four operators, four answers: modeled EPC, a selectable menu, contract-aware rules, and plain bid-ranked priority.

The fiercest contrast is what the system does with the caller before and after the bid. ceohec tells beginners to ignore the IVR prompts completely and leave them exactly as they are, using a 'this is Farmers Insurance, please state your name' greeting as the example of what not to build [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:03:25]. ExclusiveLiveCall treats caller data as the backbone of matching instead: the system asks for a five-digit zip code through an IVR, or a conversational AI receptionist asks what service the caller needs, hears 'my air conditioner stopped working,' tags it HVAC, and then collects the zip conversationally [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:03:10]. On dead calls, Ringba's answer is mechanical overflow: if ping buyer one doesn't answer, the call drops, or there's a busy signal, the platform reruns the process and routes to another ping buyer or a normal routing-plan buyer because it will not let that phone call die [yat7hRHHsKY @ 00:04:41]. ExclusiveLiveCall pushes past overflow into recovery: when a contractor is not answering at 8:00 at night, an AI receptionist schedules a slot on the buyer's calendar, turning a call worth $0 into a monetizable appointment, or banks the stated need and zip code as a lead record for a different monetization path [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:12:04].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive firsthand trap is billing duration drift. ceohec explains that connecting a call takes 3, 4, 5 seconds, so on a 90-second buffer you lose time to latency, and for Retreaver-to-Retreaver routing conversion propagation has to be switched on so the conversion on the buyer's end fires on your end too [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:06:05]. The bigger fix is negotiated, not technical: when buying from a publisher like Market Call on a 120-second buffer at $30, ceohec asks for 8 extra seconds so the call converts at 128 seconds, because ringing time otherwise leaves you paying the publisher for a call your buyer never paid you for [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:10:15]. RingbaX adds a buyer-side twist: minimum duration is only available on static routing, and the higher you set it, the less priority the campaign gets in the exchange [wyW52qkPCrc @ 00:02:26]. Ringba's masterclass shows why duration matters inside the auction itself, treating an $8 bid on a one-minute duration as a higher conversion probability than a $12 bid on 1:30 [yat7hRHHsKY @ 00:07:47]. ceohec's rule is flat: always add on that latency before the publisher deal is signed, or eat the gap between your buy-side and sell-side timers on every call.

Silent configuration errors kill volume before a single caller hears a ring. ceohec warns that when a publisher pings your system you need a response, and with no conversion criteria set up you won't get calls; the walkthrough then hits the trap live, forgetting that the conversion tag's campaign ID must match the auto insurance campaign ID 6DB and having to go back into the Market Call publisher to retag it [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:09:15]. The same build flags that a call buyer added to a campaign is live right away, so the safe move is to click pause buyer until that buyer is ready and let them press play themselves through collaborator access [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:15:37]. Ringba's account setup warns that skipping billing verification and the required document upload can disrupt service when the card cannot be billed [8ojiGzzsrV0 @ 00:00:19]. TrackDrive names test calls as the step most often skipped and the cheapest to do: place real calls start to finish, then check the call is tracked, routed to the right buyer, answered, timed correctly, and recorded as expected, so a mistake costs a few phone calls instead of a whole day of wasted traffic [ncatAE6wf_0 @ 00:02:38].

Unit economics break hardest on calls that look good and still go wrong. ceohec describes the area-code routing trap: a Texas-licensed buyer gets a 214 Dallas caller who has moved to Florida, where the buyer is not licensed, and you will most likely refund the buyer and still eat the call, a rare one-off but a real loss [RWVdTqjVYE8 @ 00:12:49]. ExclusiveLiveCall frames the no-answer loss: the publisher may already have paid Google $50 or $100 for the click, the consumer and the intent were real, and because nobody picked up, the opportunity disappears unless overflow or recovery catches it [Hnh5huLjUoE @ 00:10:01]. Recordings cut the other way too. One Ringba-titled upload is a parody of the onboarding pitch that ends in an investigator's exposé: a supposed travel company running campaigns named 'USA Blue Screen of Death' and 'UK adult' recorded every call through Ringba as its VoIP provider, and those recordings let outsiders listen in on fake tech-support pitches [RVobRCTpSEg @ 00:04:08]. A companion parody mocks the RTB pitch itself as a way to 'drop bids when lawsuits rise' [eLfL-CGZaa4 @ 00:01:21]. Every recorded call becomes a permanent trail of what the operation actually sells.
