## 1. Price the loading dock, the defect and the deadline before the hammer  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is treating an auction photograph as an inspection report; the second is treating the hammer price as inventory cost. GSA Auctions, GovPlanet, GovDeals, and PublicSurplus connect buyers with different sellers and sale conditions, not one uniform warranty regime. Download the listing, incorporated terms, amendments, photographs, payment instructions, and pickup rules before bidding. Record who owns the asset, who invoices it, and who can authorize release. An engine described as starting does not establish transmission condition, emissions readiness, diagnostic-code history, or roadworthiness. A pallet count does not establish usable-unit count. Ask for serial plates, hour meters, battery condition, missing attachments, and access dimensions; price unanswered questions as exposure rather than assuming favorable facts. Twelve usable units out of twenty nominal units turn a $2,000 purchase from $100 into $166.67 per usable unit before transport. If the seller cannot support the quantity assumption, bid on verified recoverable units or skip the lot. Keep inspection notes separate from seller representations so a later dispute identifies an actual description mismatch instead of buyer disappointment. These records also prevent the remarketing team from repeating unsupported claims to its buyers.

The landed-cost bid ceilings play starts with a defensible exit price, not the apparent discount from an obsolete catalog. For an illustrative $10,000 resale, reserve $1,200 for transport, $800 for repairs, $300 for selling costs, $500 for uncertainty, and $2,000 for contribution before shared overhead. That leaves $5,200 for hammer plus premium. With an illustrative 10% buyer premium and no acquisition tax due under a valid resale treatment, the maximum hammer is $4,727.27. If an illustrative 8% tax applies to hammer plus premium, the ceiling becomes $4,377.10. Actual taxability, premium rates, and taxable bases come from the jurisdiction and sale terms. Never substitute these example rates for the invoice. The third trap is capitalizing every optimistic repair assumption; the fourth is omitting buyer premiums, card surcharges, loading charges, or tax from the worksheet. Use auction alerts to discover inventory, but require a locked bid-limit worksheet for execution. A substitute exit at $7,500 must trigger a new ceiling, not a persuasive story. The fallback is a lower bid or no bid, preserving cash for a lot with observable demand.

Removal-slot reservation is the second play because a winning bid can create an immediate logistics liability. A municipality may require pickup during narrow staffed hours, prohibit seller loading assistance, and impose default consequences after a fixed deadline. Obtain the actual terms; there is no universal grace period. Suppose a machine can sell for $8,000 against $5,000 of acquisition and preparation costs. The expected $3,000 contribution falls to $1,000 after an $800 failed truck visit and $1,200 of additional rigging. That one dispatch error consumes one-sixth of Conservative annual owner profit. Reserve the carrier, identify forklift or crane responsibility, confirm dock geometry, and request a written release contact before bidding. Require a carrier certificate of insurance and confirm relevant cargo coverage rather than equating a certificate with coverage for every loss. Traps five through seven are assuming the seller supplies loading, assuming a dead vehicle can roll, and assuming the driver can resolve missing paperwork. The fallback is a prequoted alternate hauler within the removal window. If neither carrier can execute, do not bid; after purchase, seek a written extension immediately without treating silence as permission.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy the release path before buying the apparent bargain**
> 
> Experienced buyers make the seller's physical release conditions and the downstream buyer's acceptance conditions meet before bidding.
> - Ask the property custodian for a current serial-plate photograph and a photograph of the actual loading approach; listing images may omit both.
> - Have the carrier quote the exact lot, dimensions, loading responsibility, and appointment window rather than a generic origin-to-destination route.
> - Record municipal custodian hours separately from the platform payment deadline; payment completion does not create an available pickup appointment.
> - Obtain a written parts-buyer floor price for uncertain equipment, including collection costs and quote expiry, before using salvage value in the bid ceiling.

## 2. Separate ownership, eligibility and resale rights before promising delivery  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Title-first remarketing is the third play: ownership paperwork must support the use promised to the next buyer. An SF-97 is a federal certificate used to obtain a vehicle title, not itself a state-issued title or assurance that every state will register the vehicle for highway use. Compare the VIN on the chassis, listing, invoice, release, and certificate; identify any off-road, salvage, export-only, or destruction limitation. Check the receiving state's actual documentary and inspection requirements before advertising road-ready inventory. An illustrative $15,000 vehicle with $11,500 landed cost and $500 selling expense yields $3,000 before shared overhead. A 60-day title delay at $20 daily storage removes $1,200, even before additional capital costs. The fallback is a lawful, fully disclosed alternative buyer or parts disposition where permitted, not relabeling the vehicle or promising paperwork later. Reject unexplained serial alterations and inconsistent seller identities. UCC section 2-312 addresses title warranties in applicable commercial transactions, but the auction contract and governing law determine the actual allocation of risk. Have the documents reviewed when a defect threatens ownership, rather than assuming an as-is clause answers every title question.

Restricted-property segregation is the fourth play. GovPlanet branding, a government seller, or public visibility does not establish unrestricted civilian resale rights. Examine each lot's DEMIL designation, end-use conditions, buyer qualifications, and required certifications. A DEMIL code is not an export classification; neither automatically answers whether the Export Administration Regulations or International Traffic in Arms Regulations apply. Classify the actual article and proposed transaction, including destination, end user, and end use, before arranging export or release to a foreign buyer. An illustrative $4,000 acquisition plus $800 transport, $600 required processing, and $200 selling expense against $6,000 resale leaves only $400 before overhead. An additional $700 specialist requirement makes that route uneconomic. The fallback is an eligible domestic buyer, an authorized processing route, or declining purchase; never assume a fallback is permitted without reading the restrictions. Medical devices, communications equipment, batteries, and data-bearing computers create different controls. Keep quarantined inventory out of ordinary listings until the relevant restriction is resolved. A sales employee must not convert an unresolved classification into a shipping promise simply because a buyer offers immediate payment.

The sixth mapped play, authorized_distributor_grey_market_diversion_arbitrage, requires a tighter boundary than ordinary surplus resale. Its proposed 35–60% regional manufacturer price spread is a sourcing hypothesis, not verified margin or permission to import. Foreign purchasing entities and over-quota distributors must disclose accurate identities and transact within applicable restrictions; corporate layering does not cure missing rights. Kirtsaeng v. John Wiley & Sons concerns copyright first sale for lawfully made copies, not blanket clearance of trademarks, medical products, customs restrictions, or distributor contracts. For 100 authentic units purchased at $40 each, add $6 freight and duty, $4 inspection and labeling, and $3 selling expense per unit. Resale at $65 produces $6,500 gross proceeds and $1,200 contribution before shared overhead. A $15-per-unit return loss on twenty units reduces contribution by $300. Validate the complete return exposure separately. The preferred fallback is globally identical universal-packaging SKUs sold through documented direct B2B purchase orders, not dependence on one Amazon account. Universal packaging alone does not resolve warranty or material differences. TJX, Costco, and Burlington are potential counterparties, not assumed buyers. Exclude test strips or other regulated consumables when expiry, storage history, importer obligations, or labeling cannot be established.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before capital becomes trapped inventory**
> 
> A failed check stops commitment to the affected lot. It does not authorize changing documents, concealing the buyer, or substituting assumptions.
> - Verify buyer eligibility, accurate account ownership, and every lot-specific certification before entering a binding bid.
> - Match physical identifiers to the ownership documents and confirm the intended downstream title or transfer route.
> - Confirm the all-in bid ceiling, taxable case, removal funding, and downside resale route fit available unrestricted cash.
> - Document classification, end-use restrictions, and any required processing; obtain an executable carrier reservation within the actual removal window.

## 3. Reconcile gross proceeds to owner profit and watch the cash tail  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical scorecard recognizes gross resale proceeds: $150,000 Conservative, $575,000 Base, and $1,000,000 Scaled. Applying 8%, 14.0%, and 20% net margins produces exactly $12,000, $80,500, and $200,000 annual owner net profit. Corresponding total annual cost envelopes are $138,000, $494,500, and $800,000. Purchases, premiums, transport, refurbishment, selling expense, and operating costs must fit inside those envelopes; do not subtract acquisition cost twice or describe auction purchases as revenue. Staffing remains 2 FT + 3 C in Conservative and Base, and 3 FT + 4 C in Scaled. These staffing counts do not establish wage rates or hours. Build an actual compensation schedule and reject any local operating plan whose costs exceed the applicable envelope. At $575,000 gross proceeds, one percentage point of margin equals $5,750 of annual profit. A $10,000 unrecovered loss consumes 12.42% of Base owner profit and 83.33% of Conservative owner profit. The stated $3,000–$15,000 monthly operating range annualizes to $36,000–$180,000, so its upper end cannot coexist with the Conservative envelope. Startup capital of $25,000–$200,000 is funding capacity, not recognized sales or an additional profit line.

Aging-led liquidation is the fifth play and the antidote to confusing listed inventory with working capital. For an illustrative $10,000 resale target against $7,500 landed inventory cost and $500 selling expense, the expected contribution is $2,000. Waiting three months at $250 monthly holding cost leaves $1,250 before shared overhead. A $9,000 immediate sale with the same selling expense leaves $1,000 and releases cash earlier; whether waiting is better depends on credible buyer demand and the next use of cash. The fallback is a prearranged regional wholesaler, parts buyer, or documented auction liquidation route with a calculated net recovery. Set internal review points at 30, 60, and 90 days from receipt, not from the date somebody finally publishes the listing. At 30 days verify inquiry quality and comparable transactions; at 60 days reprice against an executable wholesale bid; at 90 days require a written retain-or-liquidate decision. These are operator policies, not platform deadlines. Inventory tracking should expose unlisted days, blocked-title days, repair-wait days, and buyer-payment days separately. Otherwise a respectable average inventory age can conceal one expensive lot that has never become legally or physically saleable.

Build forensic telemetry around lot identifiers rather than bank balance alone. Link each invoice, carrier bill, repair order, title document, listing, buyer acceptance, and refund to the same identifier in Airtable or a controlled spreadsheet. Separate estimate revisions from actual posted costs so the team cannot erase evidence of poor bidding by overwriting the original worksheet. Review landed-cost variance weekly: a 10% overrun on a $7,500 estimate adds $750 and removes 37.5% of the illustrative $2,000 contribution. Flag a projected contribution shortfall above 25%, any missed mandatory pickup milestone, and any unresolved ownership mismatch immediately. For a new operator, limit one lot's uncommitted cash exposure to an illustrative 15% of available purchasing cash; with $50,000 available, that means $7,500 before offsetting only reliable, collectible commitments. This is a proposed circuit-breaker, not a universal safe concentration. Review weekly batches of similar lots before interpreting defect percentages from tiny samples. Two damaged units among four inspections demand investigation, not a precise population forecast. Stop new bids in the affected asset class when repeated adverse results invalidate the pricing assumptions, while continuing lawful fulfillment of existing obligations.

## 4. Contain reversals, preserve evidence and keep platform access clean  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Clawback is an operating shorthand, not a single government remedy. Separate an auction cancellation or rescission under governing terms from buyer default damages, downstream refunds, payment disputes, tax assessments, and recovery of improperly transferred property. Each has a different trigger, claimant, evidence trail, and recovery path. Read seller withdrawal rights and description-dispute procedures before funding repairs or promising delivery. Do not assume labor and freight become reimbursable merely because purchase money is returned. If a $6,000 acquisition is refunded after $900 freight and $600 repairs, unrecovered cash expense remains $1,500 unless those costs are separately recovered. For downstream card sales, retain the accepted description, serial numbers, delivery evidence, and correspondence, while recognizing that a signature alone does not defeat every dispute. B2B wire payment reduces some card dispute exposure but does not eliminate fraud, restitution claims, or contractual liability. Verify changes to payment instructions through a previously established contact method. When a reversal arrives, reconcile cash recovered, goods recovered, and costs still owed separately. Calling the transaction canceled must not erase an outstanding carrier bill or a buyer's property interest.

The shortcuts most likely to destroy platform access are procedural dishonesty rather than clever bidding. Shill bidding, coordinated bid suppression, account sharing contrary to platform rules, restriction evasion through replacement identities, fabricated resale certificates, and false end-use statements can produce suspension, permanent exclusion, contractual claims, or legal exposure. Do not teach a purchasing assistant to solve a suspension with another entity's account. Use named users where supported, protect credentials, and keep accurate business ownership and payment records. Bid coordination with competitors can implicate Sherman Act section 1; arranging legitimate transport capacity does not authorize discussing who will win or suppress bids. Automation should remain within permitted alerts, inventory updates, and human-reviewed bid ceilings. Unauthorized scraping or automated bidding can also breach platform terms, which must be checked directly. A municipal employee's informal suggestion does not override published eligibility restrictions or public-sale procedures. If the platform questions activity, preserve logs, pause the disputed behavior, and respond through its documented review channel. The fallback is operating only through accounts and channels where participation remains authorized, not moving the same deception somewhere harder to see.

Run incident containment as a four-step operating routine: freeze new exposure, preserve evidence, calculate recovery paths, and authorize a documented resolution. Start with the affected lot, not an indiscriminate shutdown that creates fresh pickup defaults elsewhere. Within the same business day, assign an owner, capture the exact contractual deadline, isolate questionable goods, and notify any buyer whose promised delivery or description has materially changed. Compare at least two lawful recovery routes using incremental cash, elapsed time, and enforceable conditions. Spending another $800 to obtain $500 additional recovery is a $300 deterioration, regardless of the original hammer price. Escalate suspected theft, restricted transfers, or document fabrication to qualified counsel and the appropriate counterparty without altering records. After resolution, distinguish bad luck from a repeatable process failure: missing inspection, incorrect premium formula, unreserved loading equipment, or unsupported title assumption. Update the relevant worksheet and supplier notes, then require evidence that the failed control now works before resuming comparable bids. Maintain a weekly owner review of exception exposure in dollars. A count of three incidents is less informative than knowing that one unresolved vehicle threatens half the year's expected owner profit.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze only the affected exposure
   - Pause new bids, discretionary repairs, and unsupported resale promises for the affected lot or asset class.
   - Continue existing lawful payment and removal duties unless a documented instruction changes them; record the incident owner and next contractual deadline.
2. Preserve the original transaction record
   - Save listing versions, incorporated terms, invoices, identifier photographs, inspection notes, and correspondence without overwriting originals.
   - Build a dated event timeline distinguishing seller statements, buyer assumptions, completed payments, and obligations still outstanding.
3. Price two executable recovery routes
   - Compare correction, return, permitted wholesale resale, or authorized processing using incremental cash and timing.
   - Obtain written quotes and verify eligibility for each route; exclude speculative refunds and prohibited transfers from recoverable value.
4. Resolve and repair the failed control
   - Document the chosen route, obtain required counterparty confirmation, and reconcile actual recovery against every remaining bill.
   - Update the bid worksheet or operating procedure and verify the revised control before resuming comparable acquisitions.
