The street mechanics are hard numbers: priority bids climb in $100 steps, five-minute closes double or triple prices, and 30-second overtimes pushed one buyer from $9,500 to $12,500. A $10,200 win invoiced at $12,290.40, and broker shipping ran $850 from Georgia to Missouri. Screening keys on 6.5 overdrive trucks, zero blow-by, and wear on pedals and switches. The split: Kevin24Seven now leans toward Facebook Marketplace after watching GovPlanet trucks clear $35,000 to $50,000, while Rasmussen Equipment pulled a near-new non-runner that only needed batteries.

## 1. The Practitioner Consensus

Every buyer who has actually closed a GovPlanet Humvee describes the same front gate: build an account, request buying privileges, and get cleared before a single bid counts. Split Builds walks through it as entering basic info and a credit card that sits uncharged unless you win and fail to pay, with privileges granted inside roughly 24 hours [CkfJvTDwa2w @ 00:00:20]. Kevin24Seven had a rougher ride: the approval took a week or two, and GovPlanet's system flagged them as living in Canada until a phone call fixed it [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:02:07]. Their standing advice is to call the contact number, expect long hold times, and lean on staff who are understaffed but genuinely helpful [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:01:09]. MotorTrend's Dirt Every Day host frames the same step as signing up, getting a bidder number, and watching the rolling auction calendar [Wg0ziRtQ9JM @ 00:00:53]. Once inside, the shared reality is that you buy semi sight unseen. You cannot touch, start, or drive the truck; you get a written condition report plus dozens of high-detail photos covering interior, exterior, under the hood, and undercarriage. Kevin24Seven counted 82 photos on a single listing and warns to assume more is wrong than the limited function check admits [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:03:13].

The second point of total agreement is the paperwork tail. Winning is not owning: the buyer submits an end-use certificate, GovPlanet reviews it and forwards it to the branch that ran the truck, and that branch runs what Split Builds calls a mini background check before release [CkfJvTDwa2w @ 00:01:49]. Split Builds says it can take up to 12 weeks and theirs burned all 12, buying in October and not getting clearance until January. Kevin24Seven tells first-timers to expect a good two to three months, maybe more, with the truck sitting untouchable in the lot the entire time [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:10:40]. By 2024 the same operator was warning buyers to brace for five to nine months, possibly longer [TLQhxsY7JoE @ 00:07:05]. Behind the EUC sits the SF-97. Kevin24Seven calls it really important for getting the truck street legal and insists you confirm the listing sells with one, noting that in Missouri it functions sort of like a title; it landed in the mailbox roughly four to five months after purchase [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:12:42]. Midwestern Garage paid a little extra to have GovPlanet issue an actual title rather than the bare SF-97, reasoning that letting them handle it saves time [3QWRjujnz2E @ 00:08:45].

The third consensus point: whatever you win shows up stripped, dead, and partially robbed. HHWheels, hauling a friend's truck out of Albany, Georgia, found it extremely bare bones, with no batteries, no armor, and every military-related item pulled [09UUwyLab_0 @ 00:03:17]. Midwestern Garage opened their M1097R1 to find the fuel pump gone, the steering box removed, the power steering reservoir missing, and three steering box bolts loose on the frame after the ride from Fort Riley to Omaha [3QWRjujnz2E @ 00:05:01]. Rasmussen Equipment says every Humvee they have picked up had the engine breather cut with a saw or grinder or smashed as part of demil, plus missing start, headlight, and blinker switches [B8X-aaJ2DpY @ 00:02:19]. The electrical system is a known quantity: 24 volts running off two 12-volt batteries. The Midnight Mechanic charged each battery separately on an old 12-volt charger, then wired positive to negative between them with the remaining posts to the truck, and warns to replace both as a pair [_tJQ6S_OaBc @ 00:05:02]. Midwestern Garage also found the gauge cluster pulled with nothing unhooked, and the batteries flat in their gigantic military-style 24-volt setup, yet the motor cranked over once a loose battery cable was tightened [3QWRjujnz2E @ 00:09:29].

## 2. Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Spec selection comes first. Kevin24Seven's rule is to hunt for the 6.5-liter diesel with an automatic that shows park and overdrive; an M998 means the old 6.2 with three speeds, screaming at about 55 mph, while newer trucks like their own M1123 cruise at 60 to 65 [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:03:53]. In a later crash course they put it bluntly: get an A2 truck, not an A1, because overdrive lets you hit at least 80 and cruise at 65 [gtS8l93r5cw @ 00:01:09]. Midwestern Garage learned that lesson on an M1097R1 rebuilt in 2010 that still carries only the three-speed [3QWRjujnz2E @ 00:00:44]. Topless trucks are the money play. A no-door, no-roof M1123 closed at about $11,600 while authentic hard-top, hard-door units run around $40,000; a brand-new surplus soft top kit was about $5,000 and an aftermarket hardtop roughly $7,000 to $8,000 [gtS8l93r5cw @ 00:02:34]. Ignore the odometer, because failed clusters get swapped and reset to zero. Read wear instead: steering wheel, turn signal stalk, start-stop switch paint, the shift label ink, and pedals polished by boots [gtS8l93r5cw @ 00:07:37]. Rock-chipped radiators and coolers signal high miles. The hard kill is blow-by at idle; Kevin24Seven flagged visible mist at the radiator cap as a probable junk motor some sucker would pay ten grand for [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:08:01].

Bidding runs on priority bids. You set a ceiling in advance and the system climbs in $100 increments until you lead or your max is beaten; it tells you when you are outbid but never by how much [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:04:37]. Kevin24Seven describes a mass wave of 50 to 100 Humvees closing on the same day, typically Wednesday, each lot open roughly five minutes, with priority bids exposing instantly so a truck can jump to about $12,000 at the bell [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:06:13]. Then come the 30-second overtimes, with no limit that they know of. In one account their first truck was 'won' around $8,000 and finished at $10,200; in another they thought they had it at $9,500 and only closed at $12,500 after repeated extensions [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:09:46]. Kevin24Seven expects the price to double or triple inside the window and would not go over $15,000 on any of them. Opening numbers look cheap: TK's Garage scrolled Marine Corps lots starting at $4,800, with open and hard-top trucks showing $7,000 and $6,000 [6iKYdGlqqP4 @ 00:07:29]. Split Builds adds that the start price is effectively the reserve, so you only bid against other people, and payment is due within three business days of winning [CkfJvTDwa2w @ 00:01:30].

The invoice is where the bid becomes real. Kevin24Seven's $10,200 win printed at $12,290.40, built from a $1,030 transaction fee and a $50 SF-97 fee [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:09:22]. Rattlesnake Defense bid $4,400 on a 2008 M1097 R1 troop carrier and landed at about $5,500 in, plus another $600 to ship it from the Pennsylvania base [WzbjxUJRWKI @ 00:05:28]. For transport, Kevin24Seven was routed to the broker LTI Delivers, whose driver hauled the truck from Albany, Georgia to St. Louis for about $850, and they put the general range at $500 to $1,200 by distance [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:12:23]. Pickup itself is its own grind. Rasmussen Equipment, collecting at Yermo, California, reports the yard only loads until 2 p.m., only forklifts non-runners onto your trailer, and once left them waiting nearly two hours while a hand claimed no lunch break and then left for lunch [zqnmA4kt0RU @ 00:00:49]. They also watched a forklift driver smash a Humvee's side and mirror while loading, so they now tell yard crews not to use a forklift unless they have to [zqnmA4kt0RU @ 00:05:14]. They add there are no restrooms on site, and that a Facebook complaint page exists for loading damage, misrepresentations, and parts stolen off trucks [zqnmA4kt0RU @ 00:05:27].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Get cleared to bid
   - Create the GovPlanet account, request buying privileges, and put a credit card on file well before the target auction.
   - Privileges can come within about 24 hours [Split Builds — CkfJvTDwa2w @ 00:00:37], but account errors happen, so call the contact number and push through the hold queue [Kevin24Seven — F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:01:09].
2. Screen the listing like an inspector
   - Filter for 6.5 overdrive A2 trucks, then kill any listing with blow-by, hard starts, chipped coolers, or worn switches and pedals.
   - Prioritize the 6.5 with park and overdrive and skip three-speed trucks [Kevin24Seven — gtS8l93r5cw @ 00:01:09]; treat visible blow-by at idle as a deal killer [Kevin24Seven — F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:07:18].
3. Priority-bid and ride the overtime
   - Set a priority bid at your true ceiling, be online at the close, and stay live through every 30-second extension.
   - Overtimes keep reopening the lot and prices jump fast [Kevin24Seven — gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:06:28]; Kevin24Seven placed about $8,000 and would not go over $15,000 on any of these [Kevin24Seven — F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:08:50].
4. Pay, clear the EUC, and move the truck
   - Pay the invoice, submit the end-use certificate, then book pickup or a transport broker the moment it clears.
   - Once the EUC clears you get seven days to collect before daily storage charges [Split Builds — CkfJvTDwa2w @ 00:02:08]; budget $500 to $1,200 for transport depending on distance [Kevin24Seven — F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:12:23].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split runs through a single operator's own catalog. Kevin24Seven once steered every buyer toward GovPlanet, arguing private sellers price nine times out of ten higher and that mainstream resellers charge roughly double: a truck a buyer gets running and street legal for $20,000 themselves lists at $35,000 to $40,000 from a mainstream seller [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:14:50] [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:16:19]. By 2024 that same operator reported the Midwest Marketplace saturated with Humvees from flippers and burned-out owners whose wives want the oil-leaking truck out of the driveway, with Marketplace trucks clearing below current GovPlanet auctions by $2,000 to $3,000 or more, many already carrying street titles [TLQhxsY7JoE @ 00:04:26]. The private route buys a test drive, a gear-by-gear shift check, brake-pedal feel and an instant drive-home; the auction route buys end-use waiting of five to nine months, SF-97 chasing, $1,000 to $2,000 in fees and easily $1,000 in shipping. Kevin24Seven still leans toward doing it all themselves, admitting that instinct is probably to their own detriment. The updated verdict: steer the average buyer to Marketplace, keep GovPlanet for operators who want to do the wrenching and titling themselves, and accept that the old less-than-half-price advantage is gone in most states [TLQhxsY7JoE @ 00:12:49].

Operators read the same listings very differently. MotorTrend's Dirt Every Day host frames GovPlanet as semi sight unseen rather than blind: somebody has inspected every unit, non-runners are labeled as such, and runs-and-drives is stated in the description, with interior, exterior and under-hood photos instead of one blurry shot [Wg0ziRtQ9JM @ 00:01:41]. TK's Garage, normally a see-it-in-person buyer, said the drivetrain, suspension and joint photography was so detailed they could not see how a bidder would miss anything [6iKYdGlqqP4 @ 00:05:02]. Kevin24Seven counters that the yard only runs a limited function check, presumably starting the truck, cycling drive and reverse and confirming brakes, with no around-the-block test, because the contract is quantity over quality across thousands of units; a buyer who uncovers an undisclosed fault after signing is probably stuck with it [TLQhxsY7JoE @ 00:05:49]. Rasmussen Equipment, who hauls trucks out of the Yermo yard for buyers, goes further, pointing to a Facebook GovPlanet complaint page where buyers air misrepresentations and loading damage, and arguing Ritchie Bros has the money to deliver what buyers pay for without parts walking off or trucks getting busted up [zqnmA4kt0RU @ 00:05:44]. Treat the photo set as evidence, the condition notes as a floor, and assume more is wrong than listed.

Spec doctrine also splits by mission. Kevin24Seven calls the 6.5-liter with park and overdrive the most important things on the listing, because a 6.2 three-speed tops out around 55 mph with the motor screaming, while an A2 overdrive truck hits 80 and cruises 65 [gtS8l93r5cw @ 00:00:49]. Rattlesnake Defense won a three-speed 2008 M1097R1 troop carrier at $4,400 with weak oil pressure and some blow-by and is happy with it, because it is a property truck that will be lucky to see 35 mph on dirt roads and does not care about briers, brush or scratches [WzbjxUJRWKI @ 00:07:15]. The playbook follows the use case: a highway daily driver, a dirt-road workhorse, or a swap platform each justify a different bid sheet.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The first trap is believing the clock. Kevin24Seven thought they had won at about $9,500, then watched repeated 30-second extensions push the hammer to $12,500, and on another truck a quote-unquote win at $8,000 ran to $10,200 through overtime after overtime, with no known cap on how many extensions fire [F0AkAibFp2g @ 00:09:46] [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:07:32]. Priority bids are exposed the moment the roughly five-minute window opens, so a listing can jump to about $12,000 almost instantly and then climb again. The second trap is heart-bidding. Reviewing a January 12 round, Kevin24Seven found a 2008 M1123 sold at $50,000, then a $45,000 hard-top turbo truck whose own listing showed lights not all working and visible blow-by, plus a string of soft-top trucks at $33,000 to $39,000 [5QTEGYGbQsw @ 00:01:37] [5QTEGYGbQsw @ 00:05:49]. Their read: these trucks are worth about $15,000 because they will need work, $25,000 to $30,000 is the personal ceiling even with money to blow, and $50,000 buys a Bronco Badlands that is more capable off-road and reliable on road trips. Set the ceiling before the window opens; Kevin24Seven's own rule was never over $15,000, and they still had to fight to $12,500 for a near-new truck.

Delivered trucks routinely hide work the listing never mentioned. Midnight Machine Shop's 2003 truck arrived with radio cables hacked off and a bundle of cut wiring through the floor, coolant overflow lines cut when the radiator was pulled, the fan-clutch quick connect left open so turning the wheel pumped power-steering fluid straight out, and an injection pump that appeared stuck after years sitting in a field [oACqhA0f4EM @ 00:07:08]. Midwestern Garage's listing said the brakes were inoperable with the master cylinder removed; in reality the brakes held pressure, but there was no steering box, no power-steering reservoir and no fuel pump [3QWRjujnz2E @ 00:02:04]. The Midnight Mechanic's 2002 truck ran but would not steer; the hydro-boost brake test pointed to a power-steering leak, and after bleeding by turning lock to lock about 40 times it still stalled at stop signs unless the throttle was feathered [_tJQ6S_OaBc @ 00:07:02]. Rasmussen Equipment's accidental-buy M1114 showed up with a destroyed engine breather, missing start and headlight switches, saltwater rust through the slant back, and a fluid leak from the coolers that kept it stuck on the trailer [B8X-aaJ2DpY @ 00:10:04]. Budget for the missing parts, not the photographed ones.

The paperwork and the yard bleed time and money even on clean trucks. Split Builds paid within three business days, then waited the full 12 weeks for end-use clearance from October to January, with only seven days after clearance to collect before a daily storage rate kicks in [CkfJvTDwa2w @ 00:02:08]. Kevin24Seven tallied a roughly $10,200 win at $12,290.40 once a $1,030 transaction fee and a $50 SF-97 fee landed, then added about $1,000 to ship from Georgia to Missouri [gXjTPh7cGHU @ 00:09:22]. At pickup, Rasmussen Equipment waited nearly two hours in the Yermo desert after a yard hand claimed no lunch break and then left for lunch, and on an earlier visit watched a forklift operator smash the side and mirror of another buyer's Humvee and bounce it on the forks onto a transport trailer; their standing rule is to tell the yard not to use a forklift unless it has to [zqnmA4kt0RU @ 00:05:14]. Titling can stall after delivery: Rattlesnake Defense's West Virginia DMV rejected the notarized form as not a certificate, and nobody would accept the six-digit serial as a VIN for insurance [WzbjxUJRWKI @ 00:07:32]. Map the title route in your state before the hammer drops.
