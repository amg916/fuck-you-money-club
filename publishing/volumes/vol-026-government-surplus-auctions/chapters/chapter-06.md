## 1. Mine disposal triggers before auction alerts become crowded  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one asset family and a hauling radius, not a national feed of bargains. For the first month, assign an operator to municipal fleet replacement and another to shop equipment within 200 miles; use qualified contractors for inspection, transport and repair. Build saved searches on GSA Auctions, GovDeals, PublicSurplus and GovPlanet, but record the actual seller, contract terms and asset location separately. A marketplace name does not establish federal ownership, eligibility or identical purchase conditions. Capture auction identifier, serial number, closing time, buyer premium, payment deadline, removal deadline, loading assistance and inspection access in Airtable or a spreadsheet. Apply alerts to spelling variants, model families and broad categories; a poorly labeled listing can hide an asset, but poor disclosure also hides defects. Review the queue twice daily and assign every promising lot a next action within one business day. Under 41 CFR Part 102-38, federal personal-property sales operate within a defined disposal framework; municipal terms follow the selling jurisdiction and its contract. Preserve dated terms before bidding because the operational constraint is often a two-day removal window rather than the advertised asset price. Reject listings whose access rules make a competent inspection impossible.

Work backward from public operating changes. City council agendas, capital-improvement budgets, fleet replacement schedules, school board minutes, hospital district meetings and facility-closure notices reveal likely supply before a polished listing appears. A replacement appropriation is a research trigger, not evidence that an identified vehicle will be sold. Match the approved purchase with department inventory records, a disposal resolution and the auction calendar. Send narrow public-record requests for existing disposition schedules or equipment lists; specify department, date range and preferred electronic format. Federal requests fall under 5 U.S.C. 552, while local requests use the relevant state records statute. Neither route requires an agency to create your market analysis or disclose exempt material. Ask the published surplus contact how inspection and bidding work, never for preferential award treatment. In parallel, mine demand from municipal maintenance contractors, independent repair shops, regional equipment dealers and installation firms that publish completed projects. An awarded service contract can suggest equipment demand, but confirm specifications and timing directly. Score a signal higher only when supply timing, lawful access and a named buyer need converge. Archive the source URL and publication date so another operator can reproduce the reasoning without relying on a private conversation.

Use six chapter-level sourcing plays, with the medical-imaging route mapped explicitly to con_decommissioned_medical_imaging_arbitrage. The other five routes are fleet replacement resale, municipal shop-equipment resale, public-facility closure splits, inspected generator resale and restricted-screened federal equipment remarketing. Each starts with a different buyer objection: title, calibration, handling, load performance or transfer restrictions. The matrix gives illustrative dollars per completed resale, not additional scorecard scenarios. Calculate contribution as resale receipts less landed acquisition, direct selling costs and an explicit defect reserve; contribution still must cover allocated overhead. For example, $16,000 of fleet receipts less $12,000 of direct costs produces $4,000 contribution, not $4,000 owner profit. Require a written buyer specification and two independent demand checks before underwriting a new category. For imaging, those checks must involve technically qualified purchasers rather than general equipment brokers. Price recoverable equipment independently of any operating-site authority. The fallback must exist before bidding: a documented dealer offer, feasible parts recovery where permitted, or a decision not to acquire. A reserve-price reduction does not repair missing documents or create a buyer. Revisit these routes weekly and stop sourcing categories whose inspection burden consistently consumes their apparent spread.

## 2. Qualify demand and cap bids before capital becomes trapped  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the buyer specification before the bid worksheet. Interview prospects about required model, minimum capacity, acceptable condition, installation geography, purchasing authority and delivery deadline. Ask what would cause rejection after arrival and who can accept the asset. A buyer saying that used generators sell well is market commentary; a buyer requesting a tested 480-volt three-phase unit within thirty days is a qualified demand signal. Record the difference. Use a 100-point scorecard: twenty points each for specific buyer demand, inspected condition and verified economics; fifteen each for removal feasibility and documentation; ten for cash-cycle fit. Award full points only against evidence, half points for a documented but incomplete answer, and zero for an unanswered question. Advance at eighty points only if title, eligibility, restriction and removal checks also pass. A high score cannot offset a prohibited transfer or an impossible loading plan. Classify an unsigned price indication below an executed purchase commitment, and record any cancellation right. Require a second buyer for specialized inventory because the first buyer can change projects while the auction payment deadline remains fixed. Review every score with the person responsible for collecting payment rather than letting the sourcing operator grade their own enthusiasm.

Translate buyer evidence into a maximum hammer bid using actual auction charges. Assume a documented $20,000 resale, $1,500 transport, $1,000 refurbishment, $500 selling expense, $1,000 defect reserve, $1,500 allocated overhead and $3,000 target owner profit. These leave $11,500 for hammer price plus a ten-percent buyer premium; $11,500 divided by 1.10 yields $10,454.54 after rounding down to cents. Set the operational limit at $10,450 or lower, subject to permitted bid increments. This illustration assumes documented acquisition-tax treatment and no additional auction charge. If tax applies, calculate its actual taxable base and reduce the bid; never presume a resale certificate covers every fee or purchase. Include storage, loading, payment charges and title expenses when applicable. Treat sales tax collected for remittance separately from recognized resale revenue. Use a downside case with lower receipts and a longer holding period before approving the worksheet internally. Keep the live bid limit visible beside the lot number, and prohibit escalation because another bidder appears confident. If the next permissible bid exceeds the limit, lose the auction. The resulting purchase price determines whether good sourcing produces an actual margin.

Apply a separate diligence track to Certificate-of-Need (CON) & Decommissioned Hospital Imaging-Suite Arbitrage, the TRACK_1 play con_decommissioned_medical_imaging_arbitrage. An approved location may have strategic value to an eligible operator, but a scanner purchase does not carry the site's authority automatically. State programs differ by provider, service, project and transaction structure; New York Public Health Law section 2802 illustrates approval requirements for covered hospital projects. Obtain transaction-specific review before assigning value to continued operations or a change in control. Illustratively, $60,000 equipment receipts minus $25,000 acquisition, $8,000 qualified deinstallation, $4,000 transport, $5,000 technical testing and $3,000 reserve produce $15,000 contribution before overhead. Budget an MRI separately for magnet management, cryogens and specialized rigging; do not reuse a CT removal estimate. Confirm software rights, service access, data sanitization and required radiation-equipment obligations for the equipment involved. FDA rules in 21 CFR 1020.30 address diagnostic X-ray systems, not every imaging modality. A negotiated option may make specified approvals a closing condition; a binding auction purchase usually offers no invented exit right. If authority cannot transfer as contemplated, exercise an actual contractual contingency or sell inspected equipment separately without representing transferable operating authority.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before any binding bid**
> 
> Complete these checks against the specific lot. A worksheet score does not replace documentary evidence or the seller's binding terms.
> - Verify bidder eligibility, the seller's authority, applicable transfer restrictions and the exact documents supplied with the asset.
> - Confirm inspection scope, serial identity, repair exposure and written buyer requirements; exclude unsupported operating-capacity claims.
> - Obtain a timed removal plan with loading responsibilities, insurance requirements, transport pricing and a workable delay contingency.
> - Approve the all-in bid ceiling and cash forecast; include auction charges, applicable taxes, reserves and allocated overhead.

## 3. Turn buyer intent into replies, inspections and enforceable sales  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a buyer-first cadence against a small, researched account list. Select thirty regional businesses whose published work matches your chosen asset family, identify the purchasing role, and use publicly listed business contact channels. On day one, send a brief email naming the relevant specification and asking whether the business buys that equipment used. On day three, make one manual business call to confirm the appropriate contact. On day six, send a one-page specification sheet with inspection status, expected timing and a clearly labeled indicative price. On day ten, close the loop with a direct question about fit and stop if they decline. For an unowned auction asset, state that acquisition is pending; do not imply possession or guaranteed delivery. Commercial email must satisfy 15 U.S.C. 7704, including accurate headers, required identification, a valid postal address and a working opt-out process. Honor opt-outs within the statutory period and suppress them across your list. Avoid automated texts and prerecorded outreach; 47 U.S.C. 227 and implementing rules create additional requirements. Track the last touch, reply substance and next step, not merely email opens. Platform listings and trade-directory inquiries should feed the same evidence trail.

Use inbound content to answer purchase objections rather than collect anonymous traffic. Publish one category page containing supported makes, normal inspection scope, service radius and a form requesting model, budget, deadline and installation location. For owned inventory, publish original photographs, serial details appropriate for public disclosure, measured dimensions and known defects. Offer the complete inspection packet through a controlled link; remove personal records and seller information that you lack permission to redistribute. A regional equipment dealer, rigging company or independent service technician can introduce qualified buyers, but document any referral fee and avoid arrangements with public employees that could distort disposal decisions. Price freight as a distinct quoted component so a buyer can compare delivered and collected terms. When a prospect says the asset is too expensive, ask whether the comparison includes testing, loading and delivery; then show the specific difference without inventing competitor defects. When they require a warranty you cannot support, refer them to a service provider or decline the promise. When they need equipment immediately, offer only inventory whose release and transport timing are confirmed. Good objection handling narrows the transaction to facts both parties can accept instead of widening your liability.

Measure conversion by cohort and keep demand activity separate from recognized sales. An illustrative launch target is 120 researched accounts producing 60 verified contacts, 24 substantive replies, 12 qualified requirements, six written conditional purchase commitments and three completed, paid resales. The successive conversion targets are 50%, 40%, 50%, 50% and 50%; overall paid conversion is 2.5%. These are planning hypotheses, not observed industry rates. A conditional commitment is useful evidence but does not make an auction acquisition riskless. Define price, serial identity where available, condition, inspection rights, delivery responsibility and the consequence of failing to acquire the asset. Record deposits as liabilities until the applicable revenue-recognition conditions are met; never count a signed mandate or refundable deposit as gross resale proceeds. Track days from first contact to specification, specification to commitment and acquisition to collected resale. If replies are weak, revise targeting and relevance before buying more leads. If qualified requirements fail to convert, inspect pricing, condition evidence and delivery risk. If commitments fail after acquisition, investigate cancellation terms and acceptance disputes. Diagnose each bottleneck separately so outreach volume cannot conceal an inventory problem or justify speculative purchasing.

## 4. Execute the first three-to-ten wins and reconcile the actual margin  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Stage the first one-to-six-month campaign around evidence milestones. During weeks one and two, select the category, contact thirty buyers, identify ten recurring sellers and obtain inspection and hauling quotes. During weeks three through six, complete the qualification worksheets, inspect suitable lots and pursue one manageable acquisition whose payment and removal obligations fit available cash. During weeks seven through twelve, deliver the first paid resales and use acceptance results to improve the specification sheet. During months four through six, repeat only the combinations of seller, asset and buyer that produced positive realized contribution. Count a win as a completed sale with collected payment and documented delivery acceptance, not an auction victory. Three wins might establish a workable route; ten should demonstrate repeatability across more than one purchaser. Do not multiply the funnel mechanically to force ten transactions when inventory turns slowly. With startup capital of $25,000–$200,000 and monthly operating expense of $3,000–$15,000, the low end cannot support several specialized lots plus months of storage. Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast and reserve funds for removal, defects and operating bills before allocating another bid budget. Separate owner distributions from available buying cash.

Treat transfer and release documents as inventory attributes. For applicable federal vehicle sales, Standard Form 97 supports an application for state title; it is not itself a state-issued title or proof that every registration requirement has been satisfied. Confirm the destination state's process before promising a road-ready vehicle. For defense-origin equipment, review the listing's demilitarization designation, end-use documentation and release requirements with the seller. A DEMIL code is not a universal resale license. The U.S. Munitions List in 22 CFR 121.1 and controls under the Export Administration Regulations require transaction-specific classification and screening when applicable; domestic acquisition does not establish unrestricted export rights. At pickup, match serials, count accessories, photograph condition and obtain a signed release before loading. Follow sale terms for discrepancies rather than assuming returns are available. On resale, use a written agreement addressing condition, inspection, payment, risk transfer and delivery acceptance under the applicable state's enacted UCC rules. Keep inspection claims consistent with what was actually tested. Route imaging data sanitization through authorized personnel and retain a certificate identifying the handled equipment, without copying patient information into your deal file. Close the record only after funds settle and obligations are documented.

Reconcile completed transactions to the canonical annual scorecard without converting contribution into owner profit. Conservative revenue is $150,000 at 8%, yielding $12,000 owner net profit and $138,000 total annual costs with 2 FT + 3 C. Base revenue is $575,000 at 14.0%, yielding $80,500 owner net profit and $494,500 costs with 2 FT + 3 C. Scaled revenue is $1,000,000 at 20%, yielding $200,000 owner net profit and $800,000 costs with 3 FT + 4 C. These totals include acquisition, transport, refurbishment and operating expenses; do not deduct them twice. Team labels do not establish affordable compensation, so test actual hours and local rates within each cost envelope. Ten illustrative $16,000 fleet resales produce $160,000 receipts and, at $12,000 direct cost each, $40,000 contribution before overhead; they do not equal the Conservative case. Never include a customer's hauling revenue or hospital service receipts as operator resale revenue. Review contribution by lot, holding days, forecast error and buyer concentration each month. Use a prewritten aging policy to seek fresh dealer bids and reduce price when carrying costs exceed a defensible recovery upside. Reorder evidence-backed inventory, not merely categories that generated entertaining auctions.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create a matched supply-and-demand file
   - Pair one disposal trigger with two named prospective buyers.
   - Save the public record, auction terms, asset identifiers and written buyer specifications in one dated folder.
2. Inspect, score and price the entire transaction
   - Complete the 100-point worksheet and approve a fixed hammer limit.
   - Attach inspection results, transfer-document requirements, fee calculations, hauling quotes, cash timing and the downside exit.
3. Acquire and remove without changing the underwriting
   - Bid within the limit and execute the documented pickup plan.
   - Match serials, record discrepancies under the sale terms, collect release documents and preserve loading photographs.
4. Collect, reconcile and decide whether to repeat
   - Document buyer acceptance and reconcile the completed lot.
   - Separate gross receipts, direct contribution, allocated overhead and owner profit; update the scorecard with realized defects and holding days before the next bid.
