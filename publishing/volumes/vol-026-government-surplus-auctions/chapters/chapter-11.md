## 1. Assign six operator plays to five accountable people  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Design the pod around six mapped operator plays, not six departments: auction intelligence, bid underwriting, removal logistics, refurbishment control, B2B remarketing, and inventory-to-cash governance. These labels provide the chapter's execution map for Candidate #17. In the five-person configuration, FT1 is the acquisition and commercial lead, usually the owner; FT2 is the operations and inventory lead. C1 supplies transport services, C2 supplies mechanical or equipment refurbishment, and C3 supplies photography, specifications, and listing production. FT1 owns auction intelligence, bid underwriting, and remarketing pricing; FT2 owns removal, refurbishment acceptance, and inventory-to-cash records. Contractors execute defined deliverables without inheriting discretionary bidding authority or unrestricted banking access. Every lot receives one identifier before bidding, one accountable employee, and one evidence folder linking the auction listing to the resale invoice. Use GSA Auctions for federal opportunities and GovDeals or Public Surplus for municipal opportunities; evaluate GovPlanet listings according to their actual seller and contractual conditions. A marketplace name never establishes the property's legal status, inspection rights, or removal obligations. This allocation preserves five actual people while separating decisions from specialist execution and keeping the owner from becoming the undocumented dispatcher for every purchase.

At the seven-person configuration, retain those five seats and add FT3 as inventory controller and sales coordinator, plus C4 as an independent inspection specialist. FT3 takes ledger preparation, buyer follow-up, receivables tracking, and document completeness from FT2; FT2 retains physical custody and release authority. C4 performs risk-selected prebid inspections and independent acceptance checks on expensive repairs. The same person should not diagnose a defect, authorize their own expanded repair scope, and certify satisfactory completion. FT1 approves bid ceilings; FT2 verifies transport and removal feasibility; FT3, when present, verifies available cash against outstanding commitments. Where the smaller pod combines recordkeeping and custody, FT1 performs a documented weekly sample count and bank reconciliation review as a compensating control. Use individual Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace accounts and role-based QuickBooks Online or Xero access; preserve exports outside the auction platform. Do not create shared marketplace credentials merely to simplify staffing. Confirm delegated account rights with each platform and agency. Add the two seats only when documented workload and realized contribution support their cost, not because a revenue target has been crossed. Scheduled capacity reviews must distinguish hours spent moving assets from hours spent correcting incomplete purchase files.

The canonical staffing counts describe operating seats; they do not establish payroll rates, contractor hours, or owner compensation. Conservative revenue of $150,000 at 8% produces $12,000 annual owner net profit; Base revenue of $575,000 at 14.0% produces $80,500; Scaled revenue of $1,000,000 at 20% produces $200,000. Corresponding total expense envelopes are $138,000, $494,500, and $800,000, including purchases, transport, refurbishment, and other costs. Never add contractor charges outside those envelopes while repeating the same profit figures. Two market-paid employees plus specialist vendors may overwhelm the Conservative envelope, particularly where acquisition costs consume most proceeds. Document whether owners occupy FT seats, what compensation is already expensed, and how much work each contractor actually performs. Build a monthly labor-capacity schedule and a separate replacement-cost schedule rather than quietly treating unpaid owner effort as transferable earnings. The stated $3,000-$15,000 monthly operating expense range represents alternative operating conditions, not an amount automatically added to every scenario. Likewise, $25,000-$200,000 of startup capital must cover inventory funding and timing gaps without being mistaken for revenue. If actual costs exceed a canonical envelope, report the variance and redesign workload, purchasing, or pricing before claiming that scenario is executable.

## 2. Pay contractors for accepted outputs and enforce weekly evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Write a separate statement of work for each contractor lane, with deliverables, acceptance evidence, exclusions, response targets, and payment timing. For C1, an illustrative work order pays $650 for an agreed local collection and delivery, with preauthorized accessorial charges and signed custody receipts; actual quotes depend on weight, distance, permits, and equipment. For C2, use approved labor hours at a quoted rate plus documented parts, with a written stop-work threshold before teardown expands the job. For C3, an illustrative $125 accepted listing packet includes serial photographs, measured dimensions, tested functions, and disclosed defects; it does not purchase an unsupported claim that equipment is operational. For C4, quote inspection scope separately from repair work to preserve independence. Pay undisputed accepted work promptly under the agreement; define correction rights rather than inventing penalties after delivery. Do not pay a percentage of auction hammer value, which rewards larger purchases instead of profitable outcomes. Contractor status depends on the actual relationship, not the invoice label. Review federal and applicable state worker-classification requirements before imposing employee-like schedules, exclusivity, or continuous supervision on a purported independent business. Set service outcomes and safety requirements while allowing genuine vendors to control their lawful execution methods.

Run a forty-five-minute weekly scorecard meeting from a frozen Friday export, with FT1 accountable for decisions and FT2 accountable for evidence. Track bid discipline as purchases within authorized ceilings divided by all purchases; target 100%. Track timely removal as lots collected within contractual deadlines divided by lots due that week; target 100%, and escalate any threatened miss before the deadline. Track first-pass acceptance as deliverables accepted without rework divided by completed deliverables, with a starting management target of at least 95%. Track listing latency from physical receipt and completion of required tests, while separately exposing days blocked by missing documents. Report cash conversion through inventory days, receivable days, and payable days rather than celebrating booked sales while cash remains trapped. Present realized lot contribution after direct selling and handling costs alongside inventory age and warranty or return experience. Each red metric gets one owner, one dated corrective action, and one next-week verification artifact. Keep denominator counts visible: one missed removal out of two is operationally different from one out of fifty, even though both require action. Compare cohort medians and worst cases so a single successful fleet disposal cannot conceal repeated small-lot execution failures.

Enforce a maximum 25% concentration for each customer and material counterparty, using defined denominators rather than an ambiguous portfolio percentage. Aggregate related buyer entities and measure their trailing-twelve-month gross resale revenue, current receivables, and committed but uncollected orders separately. Measure each selling agency or related seller group against acquisition spend; measure transport and repair providers against their respective service-category spend and booked near-term capacity. An auction platform is a channel, not automatically the economic seller, so maintain a separate platform-dependence metric. At Base revenue, the customer revenue ceiling is $143,750; at Scaled revenue, it is $250,000. Use a 20% warning threshold to redirect prospecting, auction sourcing, and vendor qualification before the hard limit is reached. A new business with one completed sale cannot honestly claim diversified trailing revenue. Mark that startup concentration as an unresolved exception, avoid expanding the dominant exposure, and build independent counterparties before describing the portfolio as transferable. Document pre-transaction concentration projections and decline incremental exposure that would breach an established cap. Backup vendors need current quotes and demonstrated capability, not merely names in a spreadsheet. Correlated dependencies, such as two haulers sharing the same specialty trailer, count as one practical failure point during removal planning.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the operating evidence
   - Export the lot ledger, accounting reports, contractor work orders, and open removal commitments every Friday.
   - FT2 prepares the five-person pod packet; FT3 prepares it after expansion. Preserve the reporting timestamp and identify unposted invoices.
2. Reconcile metrics to source records
   - Trace every missed deadline, rejected deliverable, concentration warning, and aged lot to its underlying transaction.
   - Keep numerator and denominator counts, distinguish physical defects from document blockers, and assign correction responsibility without rewriting history.
3. Authorize bounded corrective work
   - Assign one owner, spending limit, due date, and acceptance artifact to each corrective action.
   - FT1 approves commercial exceptions; FT2 controls physical execution. Contractors receive written scope changes before incurring additional charges.
4. Close actions against observed outcomes
   - At the next meeting, verify delivery, accepted repairs, corrected records, or reduced exposure before closing an action.
   - Carry unresolved items forward with their original age and quantify any cash, margin, or removal consequence.

## 3. Build accrual lot books and a transferable evidence chain  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build accrual books around the asset, not around the bank feed. Capitalize acquisition price, buyer premium, inbound transport, and directly attributable refurbishment where appropriate under the adopted inventory policy; apply that policy consistently with the accountant's reporting framework. Expense selling activity and general overhead according to their nature rather than hiding them in inventory to protect current earnings. Recognize gross resale proceeds when the applicable revenue-recognition conditions are satisfied, with the related carrying cost released to cost of goods sold. A customer deposit before fulfillment ordinarily remains a liability, and sales tax collected for a taxing authority is not operator revenue. For a principal reseller, the canonical $575,000 Base figure is gross resale revenue, not merchant transaction volume or a commission basis. Illustratively, a lot purchased for $6,000 with a $600 premium, $900 inbound freight, and $500 qualifying repair cost carries $8,000 before sale. A $10,000 sale creates $2,000 gross profit; $400 of selling and outbound costs leaves $1,600 contribution before overhead. That lot example does not replace the canonical 14.0% Base net margin. Show both views so operational contribution cannot be mistaken for annual owner net profit, and reconcile cash receipts separately from recognized revenue.

Close monthly by acquisition cohort, asset class, seller, and disposal channel. Preserve original purchase dates when stock moves between yards or listings; relisting must never reset aging. Reconcile physical serial numbers to the inventory subledger, the subledger to the general ledger, and sale invoices to receivables and collections. Allocate mixed-lot costs using a documented, reasonable method applied consistently, such as supported relative resale values at acquisition. Do not assign every dollar to the first item sold and present the remainder as free stock. At thirty, sixty, and ninety days, review price, market demand, missing documentation, and probable recovery costs; these are management review intervals, not universal statutory deadlines. Record write-downs when required by the reporting framework and retain support for estimated realizable values. Run the saleability review before placing replacement orders, because a profitable income statement can coexist with deteriorating inventory quality. Separate restricted cash, unpaid auction purchases, removal commitments, customer deposits, and tax liabilities in the thirteen-week cash forecast. A buyer should be able to reproduce the monthly close without asking the founder which forklift was swapped, which invoice contains freight, or why an apparently sold asset remains in the yard.

Transferable books include rights and restrictions, not just ledger balances. Federal personal-property sales under 41 CFR Part 102-38 operate within applicable agency procedures and sale terms; municipal auctions require their own seller-specific review. An SF-97 supports the government vehicle ownership chain but does not itself establish state registration, roadworthiness, or completion of every state title requirement. For defense-origin property, preserve the stated demilitarization designation, end-use conditions, and required release evidence; resolve restrictions with the seller before bidding, modification, export, or resale. Do not infer unrestricted status from a commercial marketplace listing. Review UCC title and warranty provisions as adopted in the governing state, including California Commercial Code sections 2312 and 2316 where applicable; an as-is description does not automatically erase title obligations. Obtain signed copyright assignments for contractor-created photographs, listing copy, and custom operating materials under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), with preexisting material identified and appropriately licensed. Do not assume payment creates ownership or that every commissioned work qualifies as work made for hire. Finally, inventory platform account permissions, software licenses, customer contract assignment clauses, and lawful data-transfer conditions. A business sale cannot transfer rights that the operator never possessed.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight verifications before committing purchase cash**
> 
> FT1 records the decision; FT2 supplies execution evidence. An unresolved item stops bidding on that lot rather than becoming a post-purchase surprise.
> - Verify bidder eligibility, seller authority, complete sale amendments, and applicable restricted-item or DEMIL conditions using the actual listing and agency instructions.
> - Verify pickup appointment availability, loading responsibilities, transport quote validity, insurance, and a backup removal plan before the contractual deadline.
> - Verify the lot's bid ceiling, downside recovery estimate, available cash after existing commitments, and projected customer and counterparty concentration.
> - Verify the required title path, SF-97 availability where relevant, state resale requirements, inspection evidence, and the contractor rights needed for accurate remarketing.

## 4. Price replacement-adjusted earnings before choosing an exit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat valuation multiples as negotiated hypotheses, not automatic rewards for clean folders. For a small owner-operated surplus reseller, a preliminary screening range of 1.5-3.0 times normalized seller's discretionary earnings can be useful; this is an illustrative underwriting range, not a claim about verified transaction comparables. Seller's discretionary earnings typically restores one working owner's compensation and qualifying discretionary or nonrecurring expenses to the chosen earnings starting point. Define that bridge explicitly and avoid restoring costs that a buyer must continue paying. The canonical owner net profits of $12,000, $80,500, and $200,000 are neither automatically discretionary earnings nor automatically EBITDA. Obtain the underlying expense detail before selecting either measure. At the smallest case, required inventory and equipment may dominate recoverable value, and a goodwill multiple can be economically irrelevant. At Base, owner dependence, concentrated buyers, weak title files, and slow stock can push a deal toward asset recovery rather than an earnings valuation. At Scaled, seven people still do not create institutional management automatically. Buyers pay more credibly for repeatable earnings when the operations lead can run removal, repair, and collections without the founder's personal intervention, and when evidence survives independent sampling across multiple acquisition cohorts.

Build a replacement-adjusted bridge before discussing a premium. An illustrative Base bridge starts at $80,500 canonical owner net profit, restores $12,000 of documented genuinely nonrecurring cost, deducts $65,000 of incremental replacement labor only if that labor is absent from recorded expenses, and deducts an $8,000 normalized inventory-loss adjustment. The resulting $19,500 is replacement-adjusted operating earnings under those assumptions, not EBITDA unless the remaining interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization treatment also supports that label. It is not the original scorecard profit and must never silently replace it. If owner compensation is already expensed, calculate only the documented difference between existing and replacement cost; subtracting a full salary again would double count. An illustrative 2.0-3.0 multiple on this replacement-adjusted measure produces $39,000-$58,500 of earnings-based operating value, subject to the agreed asset perimeter and working-capital terms. This is a different buyer lens from owner-operator discretionary earnings and uses a separately negotiated multiple. It shows why attractive owner income can coexist with modest transferable value. Validate all assumptions against payroll, contractor invoices, inventory loss history, and the actual duties a buyer must replace.

Negotiate the transaction perimeter before arguing over the headline multiple. Specify whether ordinary saleable inventory is included through a working-capital target or purchased separately at an agreed value; never capitalize the same stock through both mechanisms. Exclude obsolete or restricted inventory unless its recovery and obligations are explicitly priced. Reconcile enterprise value to equity proceeds through the agreed treatment of cash, debt, debt-like liabilities, working-capital adjustments, transaction expenses, and any deferred consideration. A seller note or earnout is future exposure, not cash received at closing. Build a buyer room with monthly accrual statements, cohort margins, title exceptions, contractor agreements, signed IP assignments, concentration reports, and twelve months of scorecards where operating history permits. Test transferability through a documented two-week owner absence with bounded delegated authority, then measure exceptions and lost throughput. Compare a sale with continued ownership using replacement-adjusted cash generation after maintenance spending, working-capital requirements, and reserves. The durable cash-cow choice requires the same discipline as an exit: enforce purchase limits, liquidate stale stock, maintain independent counterparties, and pay for competent execution. Retaining ownership is rational only when the resulting return justifies the capital exposure and the owner's remaining workload.
