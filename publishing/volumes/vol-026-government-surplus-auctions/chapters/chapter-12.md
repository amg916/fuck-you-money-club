## 1. Days 1-7: Define Six Plays, Assign Five People, and Fence the Cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start Day 1 by separating an auction account from a repeatable buying business. This chapter uses six execution labels rather than asserting six independently scored candidates: P1 is GSA light fleet; P2 is GSA shop equipment; P3 is GovPlanet unrestricted rolling stock; P4 is GovDeals municipal equipment; P5 is PublicSurplus facilities assets; P6 is mixed-lot breakup for identified B2B buyers. Assign each opportunity one primary play; a mixed lot assigned P6 cannot also inflate another play's pipeline. These are sourcing and resale lanes, not representations that every listed asset belongs to a government seller. Verify the seller and sale authority on each listing. FT1 owns sourcing, buyer interviews, bidding, and collections. FT2 owns inspection coordination, inventory custody, removal, and disposition. C1 supplies mechanical or equipment inspection, C2 supplies insured transport and loading, and C3 handles title administration and bookkeeping. Contractors receive bounded work orders rather than permanent availability assumptions. On Day 2, select two active plays using local buyer density and transport radius; retain the other four as monitored lanes. Five people cannot simultaneously inspect six asset classes, repair purchases, and run outbound sales.

Use a $75,000 opening-cash illustration within the canonical $25,000-$200,000 startup range, not a new earnings forecast. Cap operating cash burn at $9,000 per month, inside the stated $3,000-$15,000 range, and cap total pilot inventory commitments at $15,000. No individual lot may consume more than $7,500 of fully loaded acquisition cash. Maintain $25,000 of unrestricted cash after unpaid commitments; tax collections and buyer deposits reserved for refunds are unavailable for bidding. The $9,000 ceiling must cover actual recurring payroll, employer charges, insurance, storage, software, and administration. Price inspection, transport, and repair work into lot costs where directly attributable; never count the same expenditure again in overhead. If lawful staffing and quoted services exceed the ceiling, revise funded capital and the operating plan before purchasing rather than hiding unpaid labor. With $15,000 deployed and no collections, cash falls to $51,000, $42,000, and $33,000 after months one, two, and three. Another $9,000 month would breach the floor. Day 3 therefore produces a thirteen-week cash schedule showing payment deadlines, removal charges, payroll dates, and a stop-buy trigger before new obligations create a predictable shortfall.

Days 4-7 establish the evidence system. Create seller-authorized alerts on GSA Auctions, GovPlanet, GovDeals, and PublicSurplus, using saved searches rather than unauthorized collection methods. Record twenty qualified listings across the two active plays; qualification requires seller identity, removal location, sale terms, asset identifiers, and a plausible buyer segment. FT1 conducts ten buyer conversations and obtains at least six written price indications across the shortlist. Ask about configuration, pickup location, payment timing, and rejection conditions, not merely whether someone likes cheap equipment. On Day 5, C3 checks auction eligibility, resale registration, and destination title requirements. Federal personal-property sales operate under 40 U.S.C. 545 and 41 CFR Part 102-38; municipal sales require their own seller-specific authority and terms. An SF-97 supports a vehicle title application but is not itself a state title or permission to drive. On Day 6, obtain loading and transport quotes; on Day 7, reconcile the cash ledger and rank the shortlist. Export restrictions and DEMIL conditions are lot-specific: a commercial auction interface does not establish unrestricted transferability. Exclude unresolved restricted-property questions from the first pilot.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Any Irrevocable Bid**
> 
> Treat a bid as a potential payment and removal obligation. Release bidding authority only when all four records exist.
> - Eligibility: retain seller terms, bidder registration, applicable resale documentation, and any required purchaser qualification before submitting a bid.
> - Transferability: match VIN or serial number to the listing; establish the SF-97 or title route and resolve every stated DEMIL or end-use restriction.
> - Removal: obtain a written carrier quote, loading responsibility, appointment confirmation process, and a backup capable of meeting the seller's deadline.
> - Cash: reserve the hammer amount, applicable premium, tax, transport, inspection, and repair contingency without breaching the $25,000 unrestricted-cash floor.

## 2. Days 8-14: Convert Buyer Evidence into Hard Bid Limits  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Day 8 converts the shortlist into comparable unit economics. For each lot, estimate collected resale proceeds excluding sales tax collected for remittance, then subtract every attributable cash cost. Contribution is resale proceeds less acquisition, premium, nonrecoverable purchase tax, inspection, loading, transport, repair, selling fees, and expected concessions; it is not owner profit. Overhead still comes out afterward. A worked example uses $12,000 resale proceeds, a 25% contribution target, a 10% buyer premium, and $2,000 of other direct costs including an inspection-based repair reserve. Assuming a documented purchase-tax exemption and no other hammer-linked charges, maximum hammer price is ($12,000 minus $3,000 minus $2,000) divided by 1.10, or $6,363.64. That produces $9,000 total direct cost, however, which exceeds this sprint's $7,500 single-lot cash cap. The cash cap therefore binds first: ($7,500 minus $2,000) divided by 1.10 permits only $5,000 hammer. At that price the example would contribute $4,500 before overhead. If taxes apply, replace the exemption assumption with the actual jurisdictional calculation. Store the formula, inputs, and timestamp so enthusiasm cannot silently replace arithmetic during bidding.

Days 9-10 replace listing adjectives with observable defects. C1 checks start condition, hour-meter plausibility, hydraulic leaks, missing accessories, and repair-part availability within the seller's inspection rules. FT2 photographs serial plates and loading access, then confirms whether site staff will lift the asset or merely unlock a fence. An untested forklift without a charger can erase the apparent discount through battery replacement, specialized freight, and buyer rejection. Vehicle records need matching VINs, documented ownership transfer, and applicable odometer disclosures under 49 U.S.C. 32705 and 49 CFR Part 580; exemptions require an actual basis rather than a blank field. For GovPlanet property, review the specific seller, end-user conditions, and any release documentation before assuming a lot is available for ordinary resale. DEMIL markings require interpretation against the governing sale documents and property category; an export classification is a separate question. Keep the pilot domestic and exclude controlled or unresolved items. On Day 11, FT1 sends the inspection findings to prospective buyers, requests revised written prices, and tests whether the market still exists after defects become visible. A verbal resale premium cannot finance an actual repair invoice.

Days 12-14 test execution without requiring a purchase. C2 confirms truck type, lift capacity, dimensions, loading labor, appointment lead time, and the contractual removal deadline. Record a backup transporter and a target pickup at least two business days before that deadline when the seller's window permits. If the window cannot support that buffer, require a confirmed feasible appointment and price the remaining risk explicitly; otherwise skip the lot. FT1 then runs a mock auction using the lower of the economic bid limit and cash-constrained limit. Separate proxy bidding from auction extensions so an automatic extension does not become permission to increase the cap. Day 13 authorizes at most two pilot lots, each within $7,500 fully loaded and together within $15,000. Day 14 records either winning obligations or disciplined losses. A lost auction is useful market evidence when clearing prices leave insufficient contribution. Do not buy a worse category merely to meet a calendar milestone. With a canonical one-to-six-month time to first deal, the sprint succeeds initially by producing executable bids and exposing bad economics, not by forcing inventory onto the balance sheet.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy the Removal Problem Only When You Can Solve It**
> 
> Veteran operators price the seller's physical constraints and the buyer's acceptance conditions before competing on hammer price.
> - Ask the property custodian for loading-access photographs and the narrowest doorway measurement; a cheap machine can require expensive rigging before transport even begins.
> - Request an SF-97 processing estimate and a copy of the sale's vehicle-document instructions before bidding; a document delay belongs in the holding-cost model.
> - Quote pickup and delivered resale prices separately with industrial buyers; otherwise a buyer's freight assumption can masquerade as your margin.
> - For mixed lots, identify the buyer for the slowest or costliest component first; subtract its storage and disposal burden before valuing the attractive pieces.

## 3. Days 15-21: Remove, Reconcile, and Sell Without Losing Asset Control  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Day 15, winning bidders reconcile the invoice against the recorded hammer price, premium schedule, tax treatment, and payment instructions. Independently verify changed payment details through an established seller contact; an emailed bank change is not sufficient evidence. FT1 pays only through the seller's permitted method and records when funds must clear for release. FT2 obtains the release document and schedules removal on Day 16. C2 receives the exact asset identifiers, pickup contact, loading scope, and photographs, while C1 remains available only for the previously contracted inspection or repair scope. Day 17 targets pickup where the sale window allows it; earlier deadlines override this illustrative calendar. At collection, photograph all sides, serial plates, included attachments, and any discrepancy before departure. Follow the seller's documented discrepancy process rather than assuming an as-is sale provides a refund. No-sale teams use these days for appointment rehearsals, additional inspections, and buyer qualification without inventing receipts. FT2 logs actual labor hours and carrier waiting time because missed loading assumptions often consume more contribution than the mechanical repair. An asset is not operationally controlled until custody, location, and release evidence agree.

Days 18-19 turn physical custody into a sellable, traceable inventory record. Use a simple Airtable register or a shared spreadsheet with restricted editing, supported by QuickBooks or an equivalent ledger. Keep one immutable lot identifier linking auction records, invoice, photographs, repair authorization, storage location, and buyer correspondence. Track vehicles individually by VIN and equipment by serial number; create child records for P6 breakup components while retaining the original acquisition total. Allocate mixed-lot cost using a documented relative resale-value method and reconcile all allocations to the actual purchase cost. Bookkeeping labels cannot create margin by assigning nearly every dollar to an unsold remnant. FT2 authorizes only repairs already supported by buyer evidence or a revised unit model. If a new defect pushes expected contribution below 25%, stop discretionary work, obtain an as-is exit quote, and compare incremental repair proceeds with incremental repair cash. Sunk purchase cost remains relevant to the realized result but does not justify further uneconomic spending. Keep equipment testing within competent personnel's capabilities, and document safe isolation or handling requirements before contractors touch unfamiliar systems.

Days 20-21 launch B2B remarketing with a complete condition packet rather than optimistic marketplace adjectives. FT1 contacts the previously qualified buyers first, then uses appropriate dealer networks and industrial listings. State serial identity, known defects, included accessories, pickup conditions, documentation status, and whether the price includes delivery. Do not describe a vehicle as road-ready merely because it starts or has an SF-97. Confirm applicable state dealer, title, and resale requirements before choosing a recurring vehicle-sales model. Require cleared funds before release unless a separately authorized credit arrangement exists; an apparent payment screenshot does not settle an invoice. C3 separates invoiced sales, recognized revenue, deposits, and collected cash. For owned assets, recognized gross resale proceeds are operator revenue when the applicable recognition conditions are met, not the auction marketplace's transaction volume or the operator's deployed principal. FT2 obtains a signed delivery or pickup record and closes custody. Unsold lots receive a next-contact date, market-supported markdown, and dated exit quote. Every active lot must have either a documented buyer conversation within seven days or a written disposition action scheduled by FT1.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the acquisition record
   - Attach the winning listing, invoice, bid worksheet, and release conditions to one lot identifier.
   - FT1 records the obligation immediately; C3 reconciles premium, tax, and due dates before payment.
2. Transfer physical custody deliberately
   - Confirm release, loading capability, appointment, carrier identity, and the backup removal path.
   - FT2 captures identifiers and condition at pickup; C2 records waiting time and transport exceptions.
3. Reprice from observed condition
   - Update direct costs, buyer indications, repair scope, and expected contribution before additional spending.
   - Below the 25% contribution target, compare repair economics with a written as-is exit quote.
4. Close cash and inventory together
   - Confirm cleared payment, document delivery, remove the asset from inventory, and reconcile realized costs.
   - C3 preserves the distinction between lot contribution, monthly overhead, and actual owner net profit.

## 4. Days 22-30 and Reviews at Days 60/90: Kill the Failure, Fix the Process, Scale the Evidence  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Day 22 reconciles every receipt and payable; Day 23 compares actual loading, freight, and repair costs with the bid worksheet. On Day 24, refresh buyer indications for unsold stock and obtain an exit quote. Day 25 audits title and restriction records, and Day 26 updates the thirteen-week cash schedule. Day 27 tests pod capacity against recorded hours and overlapping removal appointments. Day 28 calculates the review metrics; Day 29 conducts a counter-case review in which FT2 challenges FT1's resale assumptions. Day 30 records a written Kill, Fix, or Scale decision for each active play and the shared cash envelope. Kill means suspend new acquisitions in the affected lane, protect custody, and execute an orderly disposition plan; it does not mean abandon property or ignore payment obligations. A shared cash-floor breach stops purchasing across all plays. Apply Kill first, then Scale only when every listed condition is met; all remaining cases are Fix. First-month Scale authorizes more qualified sourcing within existing capital limits, not another employee, warehouse lease, or automatic doubling of inventory.

Define completed-sale contribution using realized proceeds and all attributable costs, weighted by resale dollars rather than averaging lot percentages. Measure cash cycle from cleared auction payment to cleared buyer payment. Inventory aging starts at acquisition, not when someone finally publishes a listing. On Day 30, limited sourcing expansion requires twenty qualified listings, six written buyer indications, at least one completed resale, contribution of at least 25%, and a cash cycle no longer than thirty days. Day 60 requires two completed resales, the same contribution floor, and no more than 20% of inventory cost aged beyond sixty days. If inventory is empty, aged exposure is zero rather than a fabricated ratio. Day 60 inventory authority may rise from $15,000 to $20,000 only when cash after commitments and the next thirty days' scheduled operating payments still exceeds $25,000. A lane with ten fully priced opportunities and zero positive-contribution candidates by Day 60 loses new-buy authority. Absent that negative evidence, a no-sale lane remains Fix because the canonical first-deal window extends to six months; prescribe dated buyer tests rather than declaring failure from the calendar alone.

Day 90 is the first review that can support broader inventory expansion: require five completed resales, at least $47,916.67 recognized revenue over the trailing thirty days, realized owner net margin of at least 14%, median completed-sale cash cycle of thirty days or less, and no more than 20% aged inventory exposure. Accrue unpaid expenses and include recurring overhead before computing margin. At $47,916.67 monthly revenue and $9,000 overhead, achieving 14% net margin requires approximately 32.78% direct contribution; passing the 25% pilot threshold alone is insufficient. Preserve canonical annual economics: $150,000 less $138,000 costs yields $12,000; $575,000 less $494,500 yields $80,500; $1,000,000 less $800,000 yields $200,000. These are Conservative, Base, and Scaled cases, not three sprint forecasts. The first two retain 2 FT + 3 C; the Scaled case uses 3 FT + 4 C. A single good month does not authorize that staffing jump. Increase inventory authority by at most 25% per subsequent review, subject to cash and removal capacity. Freeze expansion whenever the next funded operating period would cross the cash floor, even if accounting profit looks attractive.
