## 1. Protect the float before buying the first pallet  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with a six-envelope treasury rather than a shopping list. Put 55% of committed startup capital into protected working-capital float, 25% into initial auction purchases, 8% into transport and removal, 5% into inspection and repair, 4% into entity and insurance setup, and 3% into systems and equipment. At $25,000, that means $13,750, $6,250, $2,000, $1,250, $1,000, and $750 respectively. At $200,000, the same envelopes become $110,000, $50,000, $16,000, $10,000, $8,000, and $6,000. These are cash allocation ceilings, not instructions to spend every envelope or expense deposits immediately. Keep float outside the routine bidding account, transfer only against an approved lot worksheet, and reconcile cash weekly. A $13,750 reserve covers about 4.6 months at $3,000 monthly overhead before inventory losses or exceptional repairs; it does not fund a six-month wait comfortably. The stated one-to-six-month first-deal window therefore requires early buyer interviews and small acquisitions. Define a reserve breach before bidding: after all committed payments, unencumbered cash must still cover the agreed overhead runway and one failed removal. If it cannot, reduce the lot size rather than relabeling inventory as available cash.

Separate the startup envelopes from the recurring operating envelope. An illustrative $3,000 monthly floor assigns $800 to shared storage, $450 to insurance, $150 to communications and software, $250 to bookkeeping, $450 to recurring inspection and selling travel, $300 to utilities and security, and $600 to fixed-cost contingency. Auction acquisition, lot-specific freight, outsourced repairs, payroll, and payment fees still require separate entries; the floor is not a complete business cost statement. Avoid a warehouse lease merely because the capital account looks healthy. Use a written short-term yard agreement that allows the actual asset class, customer collections, loading equipment, and insured custody. Verify zoning, access hours, drainage, fire restrictions, and whether damaged vehicles or batteries are prohibited. A cheap space that cannot admit a flatbed during the seller's removal window is expensive storage. Request insurance quotes against specific operations: owned inventory, goods in transit, premises liability, commercial driving, and employees where applicable. A carrier's cargo certificate does not establish that every purchased machine is covered at replacement value. Record deductibles, exclusions, limits, and the claim evidence required before accepting the first load. Bind coverage before custody begins, not after the auction closes.

Preserve the scorecard without pretending that headcount is free. Conservative recognizes $150,000 of gross resale proceeds, allows $138,000 of total costs, and produces $12,000 owner net profit at 8%. Base recognizes $575,000, allows $494,500 of costs, and produces $80,500 at 14.0%. Scaled recognizes $1,000,000, allows $800,000 of costs, and produces $200,000 at 20%. Teams remain 2 FT + 3 C, 2 FT + 3 C, and 3 FT + 4 C respectively. These are canonical scenarios, not proof that local wage quotes fit. At $36,000 annual fixed overhead, Conservative has only $102,000 left for acquisitions, payroll, freight, repairs, and every other cost. Base with $60,000 overhead leaves $434,500; Scaled with $180,000 overhead leaves $620,000. Put actual employee compensation and contractor invoices into those residual budgets before hiring. If the staffing interpretation or market wages breaks the allowance, flag the case as unvalidated rather than hiding unpaid labor. Model owner compensation consistently and separately identify distributions. Inventory purchases consume cash immediately but generally become expense through cost of goods sold when sold; a cash-flow statement and a profit statement must therefore travel together. Never add customer-owned merchandise to operator resale revenue.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Spend on information and removal, not auction excitement**
> 
> The cheapest loss is the lot rejected before bidding; the next cheapest is the lot liquidated before storage and repairs compound.
> - Ask the municipal custodian whether a forklift and qualified operator will actually be available during your booked pickup window; platform photographs do not establish loading assistance.
> - Photograph serial plates, attachments, meter readings, and missing components during inspection, then price the asset using completed transactions for that exact configuration.
> - Negotiate shared-yard access by occupied bay or pallet position and require written permission for buyer collections before moving inventory there.
> - Book a backup carrier and a direct wholesale buyer for the first heavy lot; compare both quotes with the auction's actual removal deadline before bidding.

## 2. Install twelve lean layers, each with an exit path  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the stack around a single lot identifier that survives purchase, pickup, repair, listing, sale, and payment. A useful format combines auction source, sale date, and seller lot number; never reuse a number because a listing expired. Put the identifier in the bid worksheet, bank transfer memo, photographs, repair authorization, and customer invoice. Start with Airtable Free for structured inventory and Google Sheets for calculations, but keep the authoritative quantity and cost records in one designated system. Duplicate spreadsheets create phantom stock and stale price floors. Use platform-native auction alerts from GSA Auctions, GovDeals, Public Surplus, and GovPlanet where offered; filter on radius, category, removal terms, and inspection availability rather than broad bargain keywords. Federal ownership, municipal ownership, and private consignments require different seller-document checks even when the interface looks similar. Automate reminders, not binding purchases: a human must verify condition evidence and the final bid ceiling. Store dated sale terms alongside the lot record because fees and pickup rules can differ between sellers on the same platform. Archive cancellation emails and revised collection instructions rather than overwriting the original evidence.

Treat the matrix prices as explicit budgeting references, not verified live quotations. Listed paid subscription references reflect commonly published U.S. tiers and require a current checkout check for price, tax, billing term, and feature changes before purchase. Free account access never means free transactions: auction premiums, card processing, listing charges, and storage are separate costs. A two-user Google Workspace Business Starter plan at the $7 reference rate costs $14 monthly on annual commitment; one Backblaze Computer Backup seat at the $9 monthly reference adds $9. An eBay Starter Store at $4.95 monthly with annual subscription brings those three references to $27.95 monthly before taxes, devices, transaction fees, and any other services. That is enough software for an early operator, but not a complete overhead budget. Use individual accounts, multifactor authentication, and owner-controlled recovery methods. A personal free password vault is not a substitute for managed team credential sharing. Keep banking permissions distinct from inventory editing and document the second-person callback procedure for changed wire instructions. An attacker changing a freight invoice can erase months of margin without ever touching the auction account.

Physical infrastructure deserves the same cancellation discipline as software. Buy basic identification and testing equipment only after choosing a category: a multimeter, label printer, camera, and known-good power leads may serve office equipment, while vehicles demand different inspection competence and tools. Rent forklifts, trailers, and liftgate trucks against scheduled collections instead of carrying idle equipment. U-Haul's advertised cargo-van base-rate reference is not a freight quote; mileage, fuel, protection, taxes, availability, payload, and prohibited cargo can make it unsuitable. Put the actual transport quotation into the worksheet and use a qualified carrier when dimensions or weight exceed the rental's limits. Maintain a clean receiving area with separate untested, repair, sale-ready, and sold-awaiting-collection positions. Photograph goods before unloading and record damage immediately. Check that local fire and storage rules permit batteries, fuel residues, and stacked goods. Back up inventory exports and documents outside the working device, then restore a sample record monthly. A backup subscription alone does not protect cloud-only records that its product never captures. Every paid layer must have a named owner, cancellation date, and measurable reason to exist.

## 3. Map six plays to bid limits, not speculative revenue  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Six chapter operating plays organize the budget; the first five are descriptive surplus workflows, while the supplied cross-border play retains its exact identifier. For local equipment lot splitting, assume ten tested units can sell for $300 each: $3,000 gross resale proceeds. Deduct $300 selling costs, $400 removal, $300 repairs, and $600 required contribution toward overhead and profit. That leaves $1,400 for hammer price plus premium. With a hypothetical 10% premium and a valid purchase-tax exemption, the hammer ceiling is $1,272.72 after rounding down. Without exemption, include purchase tax on the applicable taxable base and lower the ceiling. Obtain actual seller fee terms rather than assuming 10% across platforms. For contractor-direct remarketing, an illustrative $8,000 sale less $5,000 acquisition, $600 freight, $400 testing, and $200 collection cost yields $1,800 contribution before shared overhead. A buyer conversation is not a purchase order; validate machine specifications, electrical supply, delivery access, and payment authority. If the intended contractor withdraws, use two alternative trade buyers' cash offers to set the fallback bid. Do not buy ten obscure units because one prospective customer liked a photograph.

For titled fleet resale, suppose a vehicle sells for $15,000 after $10,000 acquisition, $800 transport, $1,200 repairs, and $500 combined title, inspection, and selling costs. The remaining $2,500 is lot contribution, not owner net profit. A further $2,500 transmission failure consumes it entirely. Require diagnostic evidence, VIN matching, odometer documentation where applicable, and a destination-state registration pathway before bidding. SF-97 is a U.S. Government Certificate to Obtain Title to a Vehicle; it supports an application and is not itself a state title. Ask the receiving motor vehicle agency which supporting documents, inspections, taxes, and signatures it requires. For parts recovery, eight tested assemblies selling at $250 each produce $2,000 proceeds. Subtract a $700 donor purchase, $300 dismantling labor, $200 freight, $200 selling costs, and $150 residue disposal to leave $450 contribution. Failed testing or unpriced disposal can remove that margin quickly. Use a licensed dismantler where the activity requires one, check fluid and battery handling obligations, and prohibit harvesting restricted equipment without documented authority. The fallback is an inspected wholesale or recycling outlet with a written acceptance specification, not an assumed scrap price.

Deadline-driven clearance turns aging inventory into a predetermined decision rather than a hopeful listing. If a machine carrying $2,400 of invested cost receives a $2,500 net wholesale offer, accepting produces $100 contribution and releases cash. Waiting three months for a projected $3,000 net realization adds $360 storage at $120 monthly and an illustrative $200 deterioration allowance, leaving only $40 contribution before other overhead. A higher selling price can therefore deliver a worse result. Set a review at day 30, a price reset at day 45, and a wholesale decision at day 60, adjusted to the category's actual sales cycle. The sixth play, section_321_de_minimis_bonded_tariff_arbitrage, belongs in a separate research ledger because it is a service model, not ordinary surplus resale. Historical arithmetic of $100,000 customs value at an assumed 25% duty yields $25,000 nominal duty; charging 40% of genuinely avoided duty would yield $10,000 service revenue before costs. Neither $100,000 customer merchandise nor $25,000 customer savings is operator revenue. This calculation establishes a measurement convention only. Do not book the fee, reserve warehouse capacity, or promise a duty-free result before verifying the current entry regime.

## 4. Prove eligibility, custody, and cash before launch  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The cross-border premise needs a concrete correction before any capital allocation. Executive Order 14324 directed worldwide suspension of duty-free de minimis treatment effective August 29, 2025; do not treat the historical $800 threshold or Entry Type 86 as a present entitlement. Verify subsequent changes through current CBP instructions and a licensed customs broker. Routing through Tijuana under IMMEX or a Canadian bonded warehouse does not by itself change origin, erase U.S. duties, or authorize artificial splitting of shipments. IMMEX permissions and Canadian warehouse privileges impose their own conditions and are not interchangeable with U.S. entry eligibility. The suggested Vietnam, India, EU, Bangladesh, or Cambodia fallback cannot be described as universally exempt merely because goods avoid a particular Section 301 measure. Ordinary duties and other applicable measures still require classification and origin analysis. First-sale valuation, when supported by a bona fide earlier sale for export and complete valuation evidence, can affect the dutiable value under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a; it does not eliminate the duty rate. Keep this branch unfunded until a broker validates a lawful, costed service design. Prefer domestic surplus turnover when the documentation burden exceeds the prospective service contribution.

Set up the entity, tax records, and bank accounts before registration creates binding purchase obligations. Obtain the EIN directly through the IRS where applicable, align the legal buyer name across auction accounts and payment instructions, and maintain any required state seller permits or resale certificates. An EIN is not a resale authorization, and resale treatment depends on the jurisdiction and transaction. Confirm who may bid, who may release payments, and who may collect property. Auction eligibility can vary by program, asset, and purchaser location; a public auction login does not establish eligibility for every lot. For federal personal property, review the sale framework in 41 CFR Part 102-38 together with the actual contract. Save condition representations, inspection limits, payment deadlines, removal terms, and default remedies. For defense-related assets, distinguish auction eligibility, DEMIL requirements, end-use conditions, and export controls; a DEMIL code is not an export license. Identify the relevant disposition program and required destruction or mutilation evidence before buying. Decline restricted categories that exceed the team's competence. For ordinary municipal equipment, retain a bill of sale and serial-number trail even when no vehicle title exists.

Run a simulated first lot from discovery through cash settlement before spending real inventory money. Give the worksheet to someone other than its author and ask them to reproduce the maximum bid from archived fees, tax treatment, transport quotes, repair estimates, and buyer evidence. Then rehearse the actual removal: seller contact, release document, driver identity, loading arrangement, vehicle capacity, appointment time, and backup carrier. A paid invoice without collection authority can strand a truck at the yard. Verify that storage coverage begins when risk transfers under the sale contract, not merely when the truck reaches your premises. Test the inventory record, photographic evidence, listing template, invoice, and bank reconciliation using the same lot identifier. Have a qualified technician define the minimum sale-ready tests and disclose observed defects precisely. Before launch, require four signed checks: cash survives the downside case; buyer and asset eligibility are documented; removal and custody are executable; and resale economics survive a realistic liquidation price. A failed check stops that purchase, not the entire business. Switch to a smaller, local, inspectable lot while resolving the missing evidence, and preserve the reserve for opportunities the team can actually execute.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open the controlled buyer file
   - Align entity, auction account, bank account, and purchasing authority.
   - Store registration evidence, tax documents, insurance terms, and account recovery instructions with restricted access.
2. Build the first complete lot worksheet
   - Calculate the hammer ceiling from net realization and all direct costs.
   - Include actual premium, applicable purchase tax, loading, freight, testing, repair, selling costs, and required contribution.
3. Rehearse physical custody and recovery
   - Confirm removal, receiving, evidence capture, and inventory restoration.
   - Test release paperwork and backup transport, then restore the sample lot record from an independent backup.
4. Authorize one bounded acquisition
   - Complete all four checks and release only the approved purchase amount.
   - Assign the lot identifier, calendar payment and removal deadlines, and schedule the first aging review before bidding.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks that must pass before the first binding bid**
> 
> Record the reviewer, evidence location, and date for each check. An auction countdown does not replace missing evidence.
> - Cash check: preserve the 55% initial float allocation and document the post-payment runway after every outstanding purchase and transport commitment.
> - Eligibility check: verify bidder qualification, resale documentation, SF-97 or title pathway where relevant, and any DEMIL or export restrictions.
> - Removal check: confirm seller release instructions, loading responsibility, insured transport, storage acceptance, and a backup plan inside the contractual deadline.
> - Resale check: reproduce the maximum bid from current fees, realistic defects, verified buyer demand, and a written liquidation fallback.
