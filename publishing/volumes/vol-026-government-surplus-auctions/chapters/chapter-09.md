## The Practitioner Consensus

Freight is the first fault line every operator draws. Levi Salas opens flatly with 'the difference between pallet buying and truckload buying is freight' [bGxomNwtOhQ @ 00:00:00], then spells out what that means on the ground: Delaware buyers waited about eight days on freight and a truckload took two weeks, so anyone with limited capital should put it into local pallets, accept a smaller margin, and cycle inventory in '2, three days' [bGxomNwtOhQ @ 00:01:15]. T.J. Lovelady runs the same play out of Bargain Spot in Lenoir City, Tennessee: Google 'liquidation pallet places close to me,' find a yard, and walk in to talk to the owner first [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:01:12]. Their own yard sits 15 minutes away, and they have driven up to two hours for the right stock. Shannon Jean, buying through B-Stock from California, reaches the same answer from the online side: 'I always recommend you want to try to buy near a distribution center near you, so you can save on freight' [_JpPAyxnPLc @ 00:04:19]. They buy West Coast loads instead of pulling from Florida. The shared logic is blunt: distance turns into either freight dollars or dead days, and a beginner can afford neither one.

The second point of agreement is that the buy price has to sit at a small fraction of retail, and every operator states their own ratio. Operation Liquidation pays 15% of retail on jumbo Amazon returns and spells out the hope in plain dollars: 'So a $100 item I would have paid $15 for and my hope would be to get like $50, $60 back out of it' [f12reX7DS-U @ 00:01:01]. T.J. Lovelady's rule on Bulk.com is to pay about 10% of MSRP, using a Bed Bath & Beyond case with $559 MSRP across 16 items at $4.94 per item as the worked example [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:12:51]. On local pallets they convert cost into a per-item target instead: a $200 pallet holding 100 items carries a $2 target for every piece [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:05:33]. Jona Shone applies the same discipline on the sell side, pricing a no-manifest Gem Wholesale tool pallet by checking retail on the Carrera, wall chaser, Karcher and Kexus pieces, then halving roughly £1,000 of RRP to estimate about £600 [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:25:28]. Different ratios, same habit: nobody here treats a pallet as a win without first anchoring cost against retail value and an expected recovery rate.

Every speaker who has physically processed returns agrees the unboxing is grinding labor and the stock is never clean. Niche Pursuits ran three Amazon return pallets bought for $1,500 and closed with a flat warning: 'It's a ton of work' [orDRxYfVypw @ 00:17:52]. ConnorTV, flipping a giant $150 pallet from W&W Tri City Liquidation, still logged a crack, dents and a shattered plate in the haul [0RUIANcUFdg @ 00:20:51], and Operation Liquidation notes their supplier screens out salvage but not with 100% accuracy. Jona Shone names Gem Wholesale and Stockport Pallet Warehouse as trusted suppliers and warns that Facebook 'Amazon pallet return' sellers are a minefield: 'There are a lot of scams' [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:43:10]. Matt Goldberg pushes this point furthest from the brokering side. Direct Amazon, Wayfair, Temu and TikTok returns are 'tied up,' middlemen charge premium prices, and buyers of wholesale mixed returns pallets end up as 'bottom feeders' paying overpriced rates, which Goldberg calls being a 'busy fool' [Ey5jcenBhe8 @ 00:06:31]. The consensus underneath both camps: the supplier relationship and the condition of the goods decide the outcome long before a single item gets listed for sale on any platform.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The freight math is where landed cost gets made or broken, and T.J. Lovelady walks through it line by line. On Liquidation.com, a $1,999 MSRP lot showed a $280 current bid against a $209 minimum, shipped from Las Vegas, weighing 446 lb [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:18:11]. The listing advertised $100 minimum shipping, but the actual quote came to $543.96, and with a $40 residential fee and a $40 no-dock fee freight climbs to about $650, pushing the all-in total near $1,000 [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:19:39]. On Bulk.com, the old flat $200 shipping has become weight-based; a Mount Juliet, Tennessee buy came to $437 plus $123 shipping, about $500 total, versus over $600 under the old model. Shannon Jean's B-Stock checklist attacks the same surprises: shipping cost is driven by zip code, weight and truck space, buyer-arranged freight should be lined up before buying and quoted on freightquote.com [_JpPAyxnPLc @ 00:01:05], and liftgate and residential accessorials are 'pretty nominal' and 'You can see those fees before you buy' [_JpPAyxnPLc @ 00:02:35]. The listing's edit link shows real-time rates for a warehouse, residence or storage unit address, so the landed number is knowable before the bid goes in.

Once the pallet lands, the processing workflow is remarkably consistent across operators. Niche Pursuits splits everything into two piles, quick-to-price versus hard-to-price [orDRxYfVypw @ 00:03:57]. Their first pallet held about 500 items, about 300 of which went to bins, with value pegged near $1,800 and realistic profit of '$500… $600.' T.J. Lovelady scans barcodes with the Amazon seller app, noting some yards forbid digging through pallets [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:03:38], then logs every item into an Excel sheet; disorganization, they warn, means losing money on items that never get listed. They work out of a 10x20 storage unit rented at about $97 a month. They cross-list on Amazon, eBay, Poshmark and Facebook Marketplace and must delist everywhere the moment something sells [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:08:15]. Pricing runs aggressive until the pallet breaks even, after which a $100 item can go for $20. ConnorTV prices the simplest way possible: scan the item on Amazon, then discount for Facebook Marketplace, which is how a sink showing $320 on Amazon was slotted to sell for $200-plus and 'doubled the price of the pallet' [0RUIANcUFdg @ 00:09:31]. Operation Liquidation routes large items to Facebook Marketplace and dumps cheap leftovers into a garage-sale box.

The exit channel sets the final math, and the speakers who closed out a pallet report their numbers in full. Jona Shone sold an entire Gem Wholesale tool pallet on a Whatnot live auction with every item starting at £1 [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:09:34]. Individual results swung hard: a Stanley organizer worth £82 went for £27, a DeWalt grinder fetched £125 and a Yale lock that retails around £250 with a broken seal went for £75. After 4 hours 26 minutes live, gross hit £1,592.50, Whatnot took 13%, net landed at £1,385.47 and profit at £385.47 for two people across a full day [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:42:34]. T.J. Lovelady's haul shows the other end of the spread: about 77 pairs of shoes for $180, roughly $2.50 a pair, with Easy Spirit reselling at $25 to $35 for about $1,800 total [3LlA-ruuv7E @ 00:12:20], and a total spend near $650 against roughly $7,800 in value. Operation Liquidation works item by item off a 15% buy: running boards retailing at $149.95 cost $22.50 and target $100 plus shipping, and are no longer sold on Amazon [f12reX7DS-U @ 00:08:25]. Their clam ice tent at $489.99 retail cost about $75 and lists at $150 to $200.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Locate and Work a Local Yard
   - Google 'liquidation pallet places close to me,' visit the yard, talk to the owner, and learn the listing code words.
   - Yards post stock on their websites or Facebook groups and use code words, with 'HD' meaning Home Depot and 'high end' standing in for Amazon because yards can't name it [T.J. Lovelady — Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:03:24]
2. Quote Landed Freight Before Buying
   - Pull real-time freight for your exact address and add accessorials before placing any bid online.
   - Use the listing's edit link to see real-time rates for a warehouse, residence or storage unit, and line up any buyer-arranged carrier on freightquote.com before buying [B-Stock — _JpPAyxnPLc @ 00:04:49]
3. Set the Cost Basis and Sort
   - Divide pallet cost by item count, then split the haul into quick-to-price and hard-to-price piles.
   - Two piles separate fast listings from items needing research; the first pallet ran about 500 items with about 300 binned [Niche Pursuits — orDRxYfVypw @ 00:03:57]
4. Cross-List and Liquidate to Break-Even
   - Cross-list on Amazon, eBay, Poshmark and Facebook Marketplace, price aggressively until break-even, then clear remaining stock.
   - Pricing stays aggressive until the pallet breaks even, after which a $100 item may go for $20, with every sold item delisted across all platforms [T.J. Lovelady — Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:09:13]

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether you ever touch the goods. Matt Goldberg runs a pure brokering model, moving pallet and truckloads worldwide without ever seeing the stock, keeping no warehouse, and claiming never to use a penny of personal money [6Ln8SD4VKv4 @ 00:01:34]. Goldberg goes further, calling wholesale mixed returns pallets overpriced, a busy-fool trade, and warning that direct Amazon, Wayfair, Temu and TikTok returns are tied up, so middlemen charge premiums and buyers end up as bottom feeders [Ey5jcenBhe8 @ 00:06:31]. The unboxing camp buys anyway, but on strict ratios. T.J. Lovelady's rule is to pay about 10% of MSRP, citing a Bed Bath & Beyond case with $559 MSRP and 16 items landing at $4.94 per item [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:12:51]. Operation Liquidation pays 15% of retail and frames the target bluntly: a $100 item cost $15, and the hope is to pull $50 to $60 back out of it [f12reX7DS-U @ 00:01:01]. So the choice is a finder's-fee pipeline where you never hold stock versus a capital-in, sweat-out model where the margin lives in how cheaply you buy relative to retail and how fast you can sort, price and list what comes off the truck.

Scale is the second fault line. Levi Salas tells beginners with limited capital to buy local pallets: thinner margin, but cash cycles of two or three days, with turnover and sell-through ranked above margin [bGxomNwtOhQ @ 00:01:15]. The Salas case against truckloads is freight and floor space, with a truckload needing at least 2,000 square feet split between streaming, inventory and shipping [bGxomNwtOhQ @ 00:02:26]. Goldberg's own ladder shows a different trade-off, time versus share: members who only find the seller earn a 33% finder's fee, members who find both sides split 50/50, and the paid Surplus Trader Secrets tier, formerly $3,000 and now 199 a month, lets the member keep 100% of profit with Goldberg only guiding over WhatsApp [gRoLw2uovek @ 00:15:38]. Brandon Gamble at Pallet Pros Academy frames the same tension around coaching for the physical wooden pallet trade itself. Going solo, Gamble puts the first sale at two to six weeks and 10K a month in net profits at six to twelve months; with a mentor, first money lands in one to three weeks and 10K a month in four to eight months [tDyyYhvrSts @ 00:08:28]. Both sell a shortcut, and both openly price the slower, unassisted route against it.

Exit channels split the room too. Jona Shone pushes an entire UK tool-returns pallet through a Whatnot live auction with every item starting at £1, accepting that an £82 Stanley organizer can go for £27, and closes the whole pallet in a single 4-hour-26-minute show [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:40:32]. T.J. Lovelady goes the opposite way, cross-listing on Amazon, eBay, Poshmark and Facebook Marketplace, delisting everywhere the moment something sells, pricing aggressively only until break-even and then letting a $100 item go for $20 [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:09:13]. ConnorTV scans each item on Amazon and discounts for Facebook Marketplace, pegging a sink at $320 on Amazon to sell for $200-plus and claiming that one item doubled the $150 pallet price [0RUIANcUFdg @ 00:09:31]. Freight is the last disagreement. Shannon Jean at B-Stock buys shipped lots, lining up a carrier before buying, quoting on freightquote.com, checking liftgate and residential accessorial fees up front, and recommending buyers stay near a distribution center to save on freight, which is why a California operator buys West Coast rather than Florida [_JpPAyxnPLc @ 00:04:19]. Shipped lots buy reach; local hauling buys control and speed.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most brutal economics check comes from Niche Pursuits. After buying three Amazon return pallets for $1,500, a $400 high-piece-count pallet, a $700 Amazon monster and a $400 electronics pallet that might be broken, the quick math was $4,000 revenue minus $2,500 in teen wages minus $1,500 in pallets, which leaves exactly $0 in profit [orDRxYfVypw @ 00:12:16]. The same operator found free shipping chewing margin on the first sales, switched to charging shipping, and summed the exercise up as a ton of work. Jona Shone ran the numbers live: £1,592.50 gross after a 4-hour-26-minute Whatnot show, 13% to Whatnot, £1,385.47 net, and £385.47 profit on a £1,000-plus pallet, which in Shone's words is the take for two people over a full day [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:42:34]. T.J. Lovelady's first local pallet cost $200 and returned about $100, a crap pallet, following an earlier $1,300 liquidation.com buy [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:01:37]. Labor is the hidden line item: Niche Pursuits paid it in $2,500 of teen wages and erased the profit, while Shone paid it in a full day for two people and cleared under £400 [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:43:21].

Freight is where cheap lots turn expensive. On liquidation.com, T.J. Lovelady walked through a $1,999 MSRP lot from Las Vegas with a $280 bid: listed minimum shipping is $100, actual shipping on the 446 lb lot came to $543.96, and with $40 residential and $40 no-dock fees the all-in lands near $1,000 [Lvrvr_hNtcE @ 00:19:39]. Levi Salas saw Delaware buyers wait about eight days on freight and a truckload take two weeks, dead time when cash should be cycling [bGxomNwtOhQ @ 00:00:19]. Shannon Jean's counter is discipline before payment: confirm the address and whether it is commercial or residential, because liftgate and residential accessorials attach when there is no dock or forklift, and full truckloads need a dock or a rented forklift [_JpPAyxnPLc @ 00:03:41]. Condition is the second trap. Operation Liquidation's supplier screens out salvage, but not 100% accurately; auto parts have to be checked for completeness, air tanks came back because buyers mistook them for compressors, and some inventory sat around nine months [f12reX7DS-U @ 00:18:03]. ConnorTV logged a crack, dents and a shattered plate on a $150 pallet that was otherwise a winner.

The last traps sit at the source and at the platform. Jona Shone warns there are a lot of scams in Facebook 'Amazon pallet return' groups and sticks with trusted suppliers Gem Wholesale and Stockport Pallet Warehouse [WG6vp4_R0nc @ 00:43:10]. Shone also hit platform and authenticity walls on one pallet: knives cannot be sold on Whatnot, a NOCO booster needed a counterfeit check where the rubber molding should run into the screw hole, and a roughly £250 Yale Keyless lock arrived with its seal broken and sold for £75. Matt Goldberg warns that direct Amazon, Wayfair, Temu and TikTok returns are tied up, so middlemen charge premium prices and late buyers become bottom feeders [Ey5jcenBhe8 @ 00:00:00]. Levi Salas adds that contract holders favor consistent buyers over occasional ones [bGxomNwtOhQ @ 00:02:50]. In the wooden pallet trade, Brandon Gamble lists the solo pitfalls: wrong target companies, under- or overpricing, not knowing specs, yards that take only specific pallets, and supplier relationships that collapse into one-time deals, plus the warning that blue and red pallets belong to the 'Chef and Petco' resale programs and are not supposed to be touched [tDyyYhvrSts @ 00:02:05].
