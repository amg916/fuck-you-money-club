## 1. Map eighteen sources to six repeatable buying plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Define six operating plays before connecting a single source: P1 federal fleet resale, P2 municipal equipment resale, P3 commercial equipment bundling, P4 repair-led resale, P5 documented parts recovery, and P6 buyer-led B2B remarketing. These are this chapter's execution mapping for Candidate #17, not six additional revenue lines. A truck can move through P1, P4, and P6 without generating revenue three times. Give every acquired asset one inventory identity and recognize its resale proceeds once. Full-time operator A owns sourcing, inspections, and bid authorization; full-time operator B owns records, inventory, buyer development, and collections. The three contractors provide mechanical inspection, transport or rigging, and bookkeeping support through scheduled assignments rather than continuous staffing. Direct federal fleet opportunities toward VIN and title evidence; municipal machinery toward serial numbers, maintenance records, attachments, and removal conditions. Bundles need component counts, repair plays need fault isolation, parts recovery needs disposition restrictions, and buyer-led remarketing needs written specifications. This routing prevents a five-person pod from researching every listing equally. Restrict the initial territory to assets that existing carriers can remove within the posted window, then expand only after completed sales establish real handling costs and buyer demand.

Treat the directory below as an access map, not a claim that every vendor publishes an unrestricted API. GSA Auctions is a federal sales portal; GovPlanet, GovDeals, and Public Surplus are commercial marketplaces whose sellers, contract terms, and data permissions vary. A government seller does not make a platform's authenticated interface public infrastructure. Use account alerts, permitted exports, documented interfaces, and seller correspondence first. Do not reverse-engineer private endpoints or defeat access controls. The directory distinguishes free public lookup from paid reports, transaction charges, and credentialed developer access. Exact commercial prices and numeric quotas belong in the desk's dated provider register after checking current terms; invented universal figures would create bad purchasing rules. Refresh intervals below are desk schedules, not promises about source publication speed. Listings can change near closing, official records can lag, and provider reports can inherit that lag. Recheck auction amendments before bidding and preserve the seller's actual terms separately from aggregator summaries. When a source offers no supported bulk route, reduce the candidate list before manual lookup instead of building a fragile scraper. Record who retrieved each document, when it was retrieved, and which lot identifier it supports.

Apply an enrichment waterfall that spends effort only when the next answer can alter a decision. First capture lot identity, seller, location, closing time, payment terms, removal deadline, photographs, and restrictions. Second resolve manufacturer, model, serial number, VIN, quantities, and included attachments. Third obtain independent condition and ownership evidence. Fourth estimate resale demand and freight with sources appropriate to the asset. Fifth request missing records directly from the agency or seller before paying for deeper reports. Federal requests under 5 U.S.C. § 552 can target existing maintenance records, disposal descriptions, and releasable historical sale records; they do not compel an agency to create an appraisal or answer engineering questions. Municipal requests follow the relevant state records statute and custodian procedures. Specify equipment identifiers, date ranges, desired electronic format, and a fee ceiling. Request operational records rather than employee contact details. Records response time rarely fits an imminent auction close, so build recurring agency relationships and request prior-period records ahead of inventory cycles. Attach each result to its asset and retain a clear distinction between seller assertions, official records, commercial reports, and the pod's own inspection findings. Conflicting evidence should lower the bid ceiling or stop the purchase.

## 2. Build a permissioned evidence pipeline, not a scraping project  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a Python standard-library collector, SQLite, and a shared document store whose access is limited to the pod. Use urllib.request only for documented, permitted interfaces; use csv for authorized exports, json for responses, hashlib for document fingerprints, and zoneinfo for time handling. Manual entry is an acceptable adapter when a marketplace supplies no supported feed. Separate source records, lots, assets, inspections, quotes, bids, acquisitions, sales, and cash events. A lot can contain multiple assets, and the same asset can appear in a relisted lot. Build source keys from platform, seller, and lot identifier; build physical identities from verified VINs or manufacturer and serial combinations. Never merge two machines merely because their model names match. Store observed_at, source_updated_at when provided, retrieval method, document location, and content digest. Preserve closing and removal timestamps in UTC together with the original timezone and source wording. For date-only removal terms, retain date precision rather than fabricating a midnight deadline. Operator B owns import exceptions and identity merges; operator A resolves physical ambiguity with the seller. This division gives each discrepancy an accountable owner without requiring another full-time analyst or a custom software team before the first purchase.

Make retrieval behavior conservative and observable. Set connection timeouts, bound response sizes, validate content types, and reject unexpected redirects to credential collection pages. Use conditional requests with ETag or Last-Modified only where supported. Honor Retry-After, apply exponential backoff with jitter, and stop a failing adapter after a bounded retry budget. Keep credentials outside saved responses and versioned files; redact tokens from logs. A successful HTTP response is not proof of a successful import: a login page can arrive with status 200. Check required fields, pagination completion, duplicate identifiers, currency, timezone, and plausible item counts. Quarantine changed schemas instead of silently dropping inconvenient fields. Use sqlite3 integrity checks and foreign keys, transactionally commit complete imports, and test restoration from a dated backup. Build a small fixture collection from permitted, sanitized examples and run unittest against parsers before changing adapters. The first dashboard should show stale shortlisted lots, missing removal deadlines, unresolved identity conflicts, overdue invoices, and inventory without a buyer channel. It should not reward the team for collecting thousands of listings that nobody can inspect or remove. An outage should trigger a manual shortlist review, not unauthorized collection through a different endpoint or account.

Use the waterfall to protect the scarce resource: inspection and decision time. P1 requires VIN resolution before a paid history report. P2 requires attachment verification before freight pricing. P3 requires a recoverable component count before catalog enrichment. P4 requires a mechanic's bounded repair estimate before optimistic resale comparisons. P5 requires disposition permission before assigning parts value. P6 requires buyer specifications and credible demand before travel. Route only shortlisted assets into paid services, and record which answer justified the expense. Build a weekly exception meeting around purchases delayed by data gaps, not a tour of every dashboard chart. The mechanic receives photographs and fault questions in one packet; the carrier receives dimensions, weights, loading conditions, and the removal window; the bookkeeping contractor receives reconciled invoices, fees, and asset allocations. Contractors should receive only the records needed for their assignments. Operator B measures research minutes per purchased asset, duplicate-alert rate, stale-record rate, and the share of inspections that change a bid. Cancel a data subscription when its evidence duplicates an existing source without improving decisions. Keep a manual export and source-contact route for every critical adapter so a vendor outage cannot erase the pod's ability to operate during an auction week.

## 3. Turn title, DEMIL, recall, and transport evidence into purchase controls  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat SF-97 as evidence used in a state titling process, not as a universal substitute for a state title. Confirm what the selling agency will issue, whether the purchaser name must match the account, which original documents accompany delivery, and what the destination motor vehicle agency requires. A release document proves permission to collect property; it does not necessarily prove a complete ownership chain. Under 41 CFR Part 102-38, federal personal-property sales operate within a defined sales framework, but the actual solicitation and award terms still determine inspection, payment, and removal obligations. State registration, resale permitting, tax, and dealer requirements depend on location and transaction pattern. The Driver's Privacy Protection Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2721, limits disclosure and use of personal information from motor vehicle records; use authorized record-access procedures rather than searching for prior owners through unrelated personal-data services. Where security interests matter, investigate the relevant debtor, collateral, jurisdiction, and release documents. UCC search results require interpretation, especially for certificate-of-title property. Do not infer clean ownership from a government-looking seller name or a single database result. Operator A must connect the physical asset to the exact documents that support the planned resale channel.

Keep military disposition, export classification, and product safety as separate questions. A DEMIL code addresses a particular disposition framework; it does not automatically answer whether a component may be exported, sold to a particular buyer, or marketed for civilian use. Review DLA instructions, the selling activity's conditions, technical identifiers, and any mutilation or destruction requirements before assigning parts value. The Export Administration Regulations in 15 CFR Parts 730–774 can apply to equipment and components, while defense articles may fall under a different export regime. Route ambiguous military-origin material to qualified classification review rather than inventing a favorable classification. For consumer products within CPSC jurisdiction, 15 U.S.C. § 2068 prohibits specified sales involving recalled and otherwise noncompliant products. Match the actual model and affected serial range; a similar photograph is insufficient. Vehicle recalls require their own NHTSA and manufacturer workflow, and a public search cannot prove every repair was performed. P5 should never become a mechanism for stripping identifiers or separating parts to avoid a restriction. Retain disposition evidence for every restricted lot, and keep potentially affected goods physically separated from ordinary inventory until the desk establishes a permissible handling and sale path.

Removal feasibility deserves the same evidence standard as title. An attractive hammer price can become a loss when a machine cannot be started, tires cannot hold pressure, a forklift lacks capacity, or an agency allows collection only during a narrow appointment. Translate seller terms into a carrier packet with dimensions, weight source, lifting points, loading assistance, site restrictions, appointment contacts, and the final removal date. Verify carrier identity with FMCSA records where applicable, then obtain cargo coverage and equipment suitability evidence separately. A USDOT number alone does not establish adequate coverage or available capacity. Build a pre-bid exception checklist covering as-is defects, document delivery, restricted-item conditions, buyer fit, and deadline risk. Assign each unresolved item an owner and a decision date earlier than auction close. Operator A can reject a purchase; operator B can withhold a ready-for-sale status when documentation or inventory reconciliation is incomplete. Neither should silently convert a missing fact into a favorable assumption. Preserve the rejected packet as well as the purchased packet: repeated rejection reasons reveal which agencies, asset classes, and removal sites consume effort without producing usable inventory. That feedback improves sourcing more reliably than simply increasing alert volume.

## 4. Make every information expense reconcile to owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep the canonical scorecard visible beside the operating dashboard. Conservative revenue is $150,000 of gross resale proceeds at an 8% net margin, producing $12,000 annual owner net profit with two full-time staff and three contractors. Base revenue is $575,000 at 14.0%, producing $80,500 with the same staffing configuration. Scaled revenue is $1,000,000 at 20%, producing $200,000 with three full-time staff and four contractors. These figures describe the resale business, not the total auction value observed, seller transaction volume, or value of unsold stock. The corresponding total cost envelopes are $138,000, $494,500, and $800,000. Acquisition, buyer charges, transport, refurbishment, paid data, software, storage, selling expenses, and other operating costs must fit inside those envelopes under the scorecard's accounting convention. Do not add subscription spending beneath owner profit as though it were free. The stated $25,000–$200,000 startup capital range and $3,000–$15,000 monthly operating-expense range describe planning boundaries, not permission to combine every high-cost assumption with the Conservative case. Reconcile a specific budget before purchasing inventory. Track inventory acquisition as a cash requirement and recognize the relevant cost against sales consistently; cash deployment and period expense are related but different measures.

Use a transparent bid worksheet instead of a model that turns weak comparisons into false precision. For an illustrative asset expected to resell for $10,000, reserve $1,400 for owner profit and $2,200 for all non-acquisition costs, including allocated overhead. Assume a 10% buyer premium on hammer price and no additional acquisition tax in this particular example because the transaction's documented tax treatment supports that assumption. The hammer ceiling is ($10,000 minus $1,400 minus $2,200) divided by 1.10, or $5,818.18. Hammer plus premium therefore consumes approximately $6,400, total costs equal $8,600, and the resulting $1,400 profit matches a 14% margin. This is a teaching example, not evidence that every asset achieves Base economics. Replace the tax assumption and premium formula with the actual sale terms. Round down to an allowed bidding increment, and treat the ceiling as a stop price rather than a target. If inspection raises repair costs by $660, the hammer ceiling falls by $600 under the same premium structure. If buyer demand weakens, change expected proceeds first; do not preserve the bid by quietly deleting the profit reserve or ignoring storage.

Evaluate tooling through attributable changes in decisions and cash conversion. A proposed $200 monthly data service costs $2,400 annually, equal to 20% of Conservative owner profit and approximately 3% of Base owner profit before any benefit. Its justification must come from measured avoided losses, lower research labor, better realized proceeds, or faster turnover that fits the pod's actual capacity. Record the baseline, the decision changed, the evidence used, and the realized outcome; do not count a hypothetical avoided purchase as a booked gain. Review purchased assets by play, seller, age, repair variance, transport variance, and realized margin. Separate asking-price comparisons from completed transactions and net offers. Unsold inventory needs a dated repricing or liquidation decision, not a dashboard valuation that grows with optimism. Operator B closes the weekly exception report; operator A resolves bid and removal issues; contractors supply bounded technical, transport, and bookkeeping inputs. Add the third full-time role and fourth contractor only within the Scaled plan's seven-person structure and cost envelope. Automation should increase the number of fully evidenced decisions the existing pod can execute, not multiply weak leads, overdue collections, or assets stranded at a government yard.
