## 1. Assign eleven plays to four accountable seats before adding headcount  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the initial pod around one full-time principal and three contractors, with one named owner for every accepted artifact. For operating purposes, map the volume's eleven plays to P01 parcel sourcing, P02 substation screening, P03 utility deliverability validation, P04 fiber and water verification, P05 zoning diligence, P06 environmental screening, P07 title and mineral diligence, P08 option structuring, P09 developer matching, P10 milestone collection, and P11 assignment and development-carry administration. These are execution labels, not eleven independent departments. The principal owns capital allocation, landowner negotiations, utility communications, buyer relationships, and collection. A GIS contractor owns reproducible spatial screening; an electrical engineer owns technical interpretation and study coordination; transaction counsel owns title exceptions, option language, assignment mechanics, and closing records. Counsel must have the relevant state authority and transaction experience. The principal remains responsible for commercial judgments that cannot be outsourced to a map or document template. Use Airtable for site records, QGIS with GeoPackage exports for spatial evidence, and a permissioned document repository for executed instruments. Every parcel receives a permanent site identifier connecting its option, utility correspondence, invoices, and buyer activity. This keeps eleven plays inside four seats without hiding work in the founder's inbox.

Buy contractor capacity as bounded deliverables rather than unlimited availability. A Base planning envelope might allocate $1,200 monthly to GIS production, $1,800 to electrical review, and $1,200 to transaction counsel, leaving $1,800 of a $6,000 core monthly budget for systems, insurance, administration, and other recurring needs. These are illustrative purchasing budgets, not quoted market rates or entitlement to unlimited professional hours. Set included hours and parcel counts in each statement of work; authorize additional studies against an identified site budget. Require GIS delivery within two business days after complete inputs, engineering triage within three, and contract redlines within five. Utility response time remains outside those contractor clocks. Accept work only when another person can reproduce the finding from linked source material. Require correction of contractor-caused defects within two business days, with exclusions for changed utility facts and new title exceptions. Pay retainers for accepted capacity, fixed fees for accepted packages, and any lawful success compensation only against collected receipts. Do not pay engineers per favorable MW conclusion. Apply actual worker classification rules, including the common-law employee test under 26 U.S.C. section 3121(d)(2), rather than assuming an invoice makes a worker independent.

Add the second employee only when a trailing twelve-week record shows recurring coordination work exceeding twenty principal hours weekly and accepted projects fund the fully loaded seat under a downside collection schedule. That employee owns records, deadline control, billing support, and independent acceptance checks, not unlicensed engineering or brokerage. Add the fourth contractor as a land-use and environmental specialist when those investigations repeatedly delay viable projects; the Scaled pod then contains exactly two employees and four contractors. Keep specialist fieldwork inside separately approved project purchases rather than pretending the standing pod can perform every survey or wetland delineation. Before work begins, obtain confidentiality terms, permitted-use rules for utility materials, and a signed assignment of transferable contractor copyright under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a). Identify pre-existing templates and third-party GIS layers separately, with licenses that support the intended business transfer. Store source files, not merely flattened reports. Separate principal option trading from compensated services for others; Texas Occupations Code sections 1101.002 and 1101.351 illustrate why activity and state law determine broker licensing exposure. Escalate the actual structure to qualified local counsel, and use licensed brokerage where required. A principal label alone does not settle that analysis or make every assignment freely transferable.

>

## 2. Enforce weekly evidence scorecards and reject speculative megawatts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a forty-five-minute weekly operating meeting from a frozen Monday export, not a refreshed dashboard whose history disappears. Measure each play through accepted outputs, median elapsed days, first-pass acceptance, open exceptions, and cash at risk. For P01, count parcels meeting the written buyer mandate rather than every parcel scraped. For P02, record voltage class, substation identifier, source date, and whether published hosting information actually covers the requested service. Screening 69kV–345kV infrastructure creates a research lead, not a capacity asset. For P03, require utility-originated documentation identifying the project, requested service, study stage, assumptions, and unresolved facilities before describing MW as utility-supported. Distinguish preliminary indications from executed service arrangements and record confidence separately from quantity. A queue position does not establish deliverability, and a nearby transmission line does not prove an available connection. Review changes in upgrade exposure and energization dates weekly, even when the headline MW number remains unchanged. Score contractors on accurate negative findings as well as viable sites. Otherwise, a production bonus encourages optimistic classifications, and the principal pays option fees to preserve parcels that should already have been rejected.

Maintain separate underwriting lanes for standalone BESS parcels of 5–25 acres and AI data-center campuses. BESS screening must distinguish charging demand, export capability, equipment layout, fire access, setbacks, and the applicable interconnection process. AI campuses require utility load-service evidence plus carrier-confirmed routes, credible route diversity, water and wastewater arrangements where needed, and an executable zoning path. A carrier map is not a construction commitment; two fiber products can share one physical conduit. Apply 18 CFR section 35.28 where the relevant transmission-provider tariff and interconnection framework govern, while separately checking state utility service rules and the actual utility tariff for large load. Screen flood and wetland exposure early, then commission appropriate field investigation before relying on developable acreage. Clean Water Act section 404, 33 U.S.C. section 1344, addresses discharges of dredged or fill material into jurisdictional waters; a desktop polygon cannot decide every jurisdictional question. Price each option against utility-documented deliverable MW, upgrade exposure, and realistic energization timing. Stress delayed service beyond the 12–24 month option term, including extension cost, study security, and cash consumed before assignment. Close the file when downside contribution turns negative without a funded, documented cure.

Use concentration limits as acquisition constraints rather than decorative dashboard targets. Cap each buyer group at 25% of rolling twelve-month recognized operator revenue, 25% of receivables, and 25% of probability-weighted expected fee receipts, tested separately. Aggregate affiliates and common control so five purchasing entities do not masquerade as five independent buyers. Apply the same 25% ceiling to option capital exposed to one landowner group. During startup, one closing can mechanically exceed the revenue limit; report that breach explicitly and freeze additional discretionary exposure to that group until diversification restores the limit. Do not invent alternate denominators to make the dashboard green. Also report utility-territory and municipality exposure because a restudy or zoning moratorium can impair otherwise unrelated sites. Assign each exception a responsible person, cash consequence, remedy, and date. Track first-pass package acceptance with a target of at least 95%, but audit a sample against original documents so superficial completeness cannot inflate the score. Keep observed results separate from planning targets. The meeting ends with funded next actions, rejected sites, collection owners, and an updated thirteen-week cash forecast reflecting every committed extension and study payment.

## 3. Reconcile accrual cohorts to the canonical owner-profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep three linked records: an accrual general ledger, a site-level cohort ledger, and a thirteen-week cash forecast. The general ledger controls recognized revenue and expenses; the cohort ledger explains which parcels consumed capital and produced contribution; the cash forecast controls survival between closings. Tag every entry by permanent site identifier, origination quarter, BESS or campus lane, buyer group, and cost category. Recognize assignment fees and origination milestones when the applicable contractual performance obligations are satisfied under the selected accounting policy, not merely when a promising buyer appears. Evaluate variable consideration and development carry separately; projected developer profits are not current operator revenue. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis where relevant, recording only the retained consideration when the operator acts as agent. The canonical scorecard here already measures operator fee revenue, so do not multiply it by an invented retained-share factor. Land purchase prices, battery capex, utility construction budgets, and campus enterprise values remain outside that revenue basis. Maintain a written policy for option cost capitalization, impairment, abandonment, and release on assignment, reviewed by the reporting accountant. An expired option cannot stay booked as a healthy asset simply because management still likes the neighboring substation.

Reconcile all three cases before discussing hiring or valuation. Conservative revenue of $110,000 less $77,000 of total modeled costs leaves $33,000, a 30% margin. Base revenue of $505,000 less $292,900 leaves $212,100, a 42.0% margin. Scaled revenue of $900,000 less $414,000 leaves $486,000, a 54% margin. The first two cases use one employee and three contractors; Scaled uses two employees and four contractors. One illustrative cost allocation assigns Conservative spending to $21,600 core operations, $9,000 expired options, $18,000 successful option basis, $18,400 project studies and title work, and $10,000 variable closing compensation. Base assigns $72,000, $24,000, $80,000, $64,900, and $52,000 respectively. Scaled assigns $132,000, $36,000, $100,000, $86,000, and $60,000 respectively. Each allocation exactly matches its total cost envelope; these categories illustrate deployment rather than replace the canonical scorecard. Core monthly operations therefore equal $1,800, $6,000, and $11,000, while project expenditures remain separately visible. Do not interpret monthly overhead as total business spending. Conservative contractor scopes must be substantially narrower than the Base purchasing envelope, and project cash timing must fit available capital before any commitment becomes binding.

Use cohort reporting to prevent attractive closed deals from concealing the unsuccessful parcels that made them possible. The Base expired-option provision of $24,000 could represent six failed sites costing $4,000 each; that is an illustrative reserve construction, not a prediction of six failures. Allocate each loss to its actual vintage and failure cause, distinguishing utility rejection, upgrade escalation, zoning restriction, title defect, and commercial abandonment. Report gross fees, successful option basis, failed option expense, external diligence, variable compensation, and allocated core operations for every closed cohort. Preserve unallocated overhead transparently instead of shifting it into future projects. A hypothetical $100,000 earned assignment fee with $40,000 collected creates $60,000 of receivables; it does not create $100,000 of available cash. Conversely, an advance subject to unsatisfied obligations generally requires liability analysis rather than immediate revenue recognition. Reconcile bank balances, escrow statements, contract assets, receivables, and option schedules monthly. The principal signs the close in the four-person pod; the operations employee prepares it after scaling, with independent accounting review purchased as needed. Keep owner distributions separate from operating expenses and identify actual owner labor when presenting normalized earnings to a buyer.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly site and commitment register
   - Export site status, option deadlines, utility evidence dates, and authorized spending.
   - Retain a dated immutable copy and reconcile every new commitment to its signed contract and site identifier.
2. Validate earned revenue and surviving obligations
   - Match each proposed invoice or accrual to satisfied contractual conditions.
   - Separate assignment fees, milestone consideration, advances, and carry; document remaining performance and refund exposure.
3. Post failures and reconcile cohort contribution
   - Record abandonment, impairment, successful option basis, and external diligence in the correct cohort.
   - Compare cumulative losses with the scenario-specific expiry budget and revise the cash forecast without rewriting the original budget.
4. Approve funding and publish the operator scorecard
   - Reconcile cash, receivables, concentration, and deadlines before releasing additional project funds.
   - The principal records decisions; after scaling, the operations lead prepares the package while the principal retains capital authority.

## 4. Sell transferable earnings, or retain a documented cash-producing portfolio  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start exit preparation by deciding what a buyer would actually acquire: assignable options, enforceable fee contracts, transferable intellectual property, documented relationships, operating records, and a team capable of continuing production. None automatically transfers with a shared folder. Review assignment, change-of-control, confidentiality, and utility-data restrictions contract by contract. Record required consents, permitted disclosures, and retained obligations before granting diligence access. Assemble a buyer room containing monthly accrual statements, bank reconciliations, tax filings, cohort performance, option schedules, study status, concentration reports, contractor agreements, and signed IP transfers. Redact information that cannot yet be disclosed and use staged access consistent with contractual restrictions. Include failed sites and adverse utility updates; a sanitized portfolio collapses under quality-of-earnings review. Demonstrate founder independence with documented handoffs and a trial operating period during which the operations lead runs deadlines and reporting without undocumented founder intervention. Separate continuing origination earnings from one-time portfolio liquidation proceeds. A buyer may value an existing option inventory using project-specific downside analysis while valuing the repeatable sourcing operation on normalized earnings. Do not count the same expected assignment receipts once in operating earnings and again as an added portfolio asset without an explicit valuation bridge.

Use multiples as negotiation scenarios rather than asserted market comparables. For a small, founder-led, transaction-dependent operation, an illustrative 1.5–3.0 times normalized seller discretionary earnings range is a reasonable sensitivity to test, not evidence of an available offer. If diligence establishes that Base owner profit of $212,100 equals normalized discretionary earnings, that range implies $318,150–$636,300 before deal-specific cash, debt, working-capital, and portfolio adjustments. A managed business with repeatable collections, transferable relationships, and low concentration might instead be tested at 3.0–5.0 times normalized EBITDA, but the earnings base must include replacement management. Illustratively subtracting $100,000 of fully loaded replacement cost from Base owner profit leaves $112,100 only if all other EBITDA bridge adjustments net to zero. The resulting sensitivity is $336,300–$560,500. These alternative methods are not additive. Verify owner compensation, payroll burden, interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, and genuinely nonrecurring items before choosing either measure. Recurring failed-option costs and ordinary contractor fees are not discretionary add-backs. A higher multiple applied to a properly reduced earnings base can produce less value than the founder expects, especially when collections depend on personal introductions or one unusually profitable closing.

Negotiate proceeds through a transparent bridge from operating value to seller cash, including debt-like liabilities, excess cash, normalized working capital, escrow, transaction expenses, and contingent consideration. Keep an earnout tied to clearly defined collected operator fees rather than developer capex or claimed MW. Specify treatment of restudies, buyer cancellations, delayed energization, affiliate transactions, and post-close spending decisions that could change earnout results. A premium case requires evidence of repeatable cohorts and transferable rights, not simply a large unstudied pipeline. If sale terms discount the business more heavily than its risk-adjusted cash generation warrants, retain it as a managed distribution business. Maintain option-expiry reserves, study commitments, tax reserves, and working capital before distributing cash; canonical owner profit is not automatically the amount safely withdrawable each month. Keep the same 25% concentration limits and quarterly transferability audit even without a planned sale. Test whether the four-person pod can continue Conservative or Base production during a principal absence, and whether the six-person pod genuinely reduces founder dependence at Scaled volume. The durable asset is a documented process that rejects weak sites early, prices surviving options honestly, and converts earned fees into collectible cash without concealed obligations.
