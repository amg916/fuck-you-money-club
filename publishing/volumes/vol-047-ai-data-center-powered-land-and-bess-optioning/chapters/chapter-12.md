## 1. Days 1–7: Translate Eleven Plays into a Funded, Four-Person Search  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Launch a narrow search rather than a speculative land portfolio. Candidate #47 has eleven mapped operator plays; use the following execution labels to connect them to observable work products. P01 captures developer requirements; P02 screens 69kV–345kV substations and corridors; P03 reconciles queue records; P04 identifies parcel owners; P05 checks floodplain, wetlands, mineral interests, and access; P06 verifies zoning pathways; P07 obtains utility service evidence; P08 verifies fiber and water; P09 structures assignable contingent options; P10 packages developer diligence; P11 negotiates assignment, milestone, and carry economics. These labels organize this chapter, rather than purporting to reproduce an external play taxonomy. Start with two adjacent jurisdictions and one utility territory. Select three credible developers whose written acquisition criteria cover either standalone storage or AI campuses. Record requested MW, acceptable service dates, acreage, security requirements, and upgrade budgets separately for each buyer. The principal owns outreach and investment decisions; a GIS contractor owns screening; an electrical contractor owns utility questions; a property-rights contractor owns title and option coordination. Retain appropriately licensed counsel or brokerage expertise within that third workstream. No contractor may promise capacity, sign an option, or authorize additional spending.

Fund the illustrative sprint with $25,000, inside the canonical $10,000–$50,000 startup range, and cap first-month cash deployment at $7,500. Allocate $4,500 to operating expense: $1,200 GIS support, $1,500 electrical support, $1,200 property-rights support, and $600 for software, records, and communications. Allocate another $1,000 to targeted external diligence and $2,000 to option consideration, leaving $17,500 untouched. The principal takes no cash draw during this sprint; personal living costs require separate funding. These are procurement ceilings, not assertions that specialist work fits every local market. Obtain fixed-scope quotes before ordering, and reduce parcel volume if quotes exceed the envelope. Reserve the entire $2,000 option allocation as potentially unrecoverable because utility or zoning diligence can eliminate otherwise attractive sites. This loss allowance describes the same cash, not another expense added twice. No study deposit, extension payment, or nonrefundable reservation may sit outside the ledger. Maintain weekly committed, paid, refundable, and remaining balances. A $10,000-funded launch must use a smaller screening-only scope until additional funding or developer-paid diligence becomes available; it cannot responsibly copy this $25,000 cash plan.

Build one evidence register in SQLite or a controlled spreadsheet, with QGIS handling spatial overlays. Every parcel record needs its assessor identifier, owner source, acreage, utility territory, nearest relevant facilities, source date, and separate confidence fields for physical observations and utility conclusions. Import public PJM queue material where applicable, FEMA flood layers, USFWS wetland screening layers, county zoning maps, and recorded property instruments. Public layers identify questions; they do not establish service availability, jurisdictional wetland boundaries, or marketable title. Set a first-week funnel of forty raw parcels, twelve desk-screen survivors, and six owner contacts with verified contact provenance. Split the twelve survivors between the two asset types rather than pooling acreage or power assumptions. Storage candidates must fit a 5–25-acre usable footprint with access, setbacks, and a plausible interconnection pathway. Campus candidates require buyer-specific expandable area, cooling strategy, fiber access, and a credible load-service pathway. Put proximity measurements in a geography field, never a deliverable-MW field. At the end of Day 7, the principal signs the source register and rejects records that cannot be reproduced from dated evidence.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy Evidence Before Buying More Option Time**
> 
> Experienced originators protect bargaining leverage by resolving the most expensive uncertainties before increasing nonrefundable consideration.
> - Ask the utility which specific service study, tariff process, and customer authorization apply before purchasing an elaborate electrical concept package.
> - Request separate BESS charging and export assumptions; export feasibility does not establish import service or acceptable operating restrictions.
> - Ask fiber providers for route-level feasibility, construction responsibility, and diversity evidence; two carriers can share the same vulnerable conduit.
> - Negotiate diligence extensions against identifiable utility or planning events, with capped payments and written notice mechanics instead of automatic monthly fee escalation.

## 2. Days 8–14: Replace Apparent Headroom with Utility and Land-Use Evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The second week converts attractive map positions into explicit utility questions. P07 should produce a utility-authored response or a documented route to the required study, not an originator's estimate dressed as capacity. Ask for the proposed service point, voltage, import and export requirements, study prerequisites, upgrade responsibility, indicative timing, and treatment of competing requests. A queue entry establishes an application record, not available headroom. A hosting-capacity map may describe a different voltage class or operating condition from the proposed project. For storage, document charging load and discharging output separately, including restrictions that could undermine dispatch economics. For an AI campus, obtain the buyer's ramp schedule and critical service requirements before asking about firm supply. Use 18 CFR 35.28 to understand applicable transmission-provider interconnection obligations, while checking the actual tariff and jurisdiction; generator procedures do not automatically supply a campus load-service commitment. Maintain three evidence levels: inquiry submitted, preliminary utility response, and study-supported service parameters. Only the final category supports modeled deliverable MW, and even then quote the utility's conditions and remaining contractual dependencies alongside the number.

P05 and P06 run in parallel because electrical progress cannot repair unusable property rights or a prohibited land use. For a Virginia search, check local authority under Va. Code § 15.2-2280 and obtain the current ordinance, parcel district, use classification, application calendar, and any adopted moratorium. A staff conversation is a lead, not an entitlement. Record whether storage is treated as a utility facility, industrial use, or another local category, and identify setbacks, fire access, landscaping, noise, and equipment-height requirements. Examine the usable pad after constraints, not the assessor's gross acreage. Check mineral reservations, surface-use rights, easements, road access, and restrictions that could block foundations or utility corridors. Wetland screening under Clean Water Act Section 404, 33 U.S.C. § 1344, identifies potential permitting work; database absence does not resolve jurisdiction or authorize fill. Campus water diligence must specify cooling architecture, source, required volume, wastewater disposition, and provider response. Do not charge a standalone BESS parcel for a campus-scale water assumption, but do capture its actual fire-protection and emergency-response requirements. Each unresolved item gets a named resolver, price ceiling, and decision date.

By Day 14, classify six priority parcels against the actual developer briefs and reduce them to no more than four negotiation candidates. Use a downside residual rather than a headline price per nearby megawatt. Start with the developer's supported project value at the documented service date; subtract land consideration, remaining development spending, supported upgrade exposure, financing and delay costs, and the developer's required return. The resulting headroom limits an assignment fee; it is not itself booked revenue. Where utility-supported MW or upgrade responsibility is absent, leave capacity-derived valuation at zero and negotiate only inexpensive contingent control. Stress the buyer model using a twelve-month service delay and a fifty-percent increase in the currently supported upgrade estimate. These are internal stress assumptions, not predictions of utility outcomes. Obtain buyer confirmation that the stressed residual still supports the proposed fee before increasing consideration. If no usable upgrade estimate exists, the stress test remains incomplete and the site cannot pass the capital-expansion test. Send developers an evidence summary that distinguishes observed facts, utility conditions, operator assumptions, and questions still awaiting third-party resolution.

## 3. Days 15–21: Purchase Conditional Control and Build an Assignable Evidence Pack  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

P09 aims for two executed, diligence-contingent options, not maximum acreage under expensive paper. Seek twelve to twenty-four months of contractual control, with negotiated termination rights tied to utility feasibility, zoning, title, access, and buyer-defined physical requirements. That duration is a target, not an assumption that owners will accept cheap long-term exclusivity. If an owner requires consideration beyond the sprint allocation, negotiate staged extensions, obtain developer funding, or stop. Specify permitted assignees, assignment notice, any owner consent, diligence access, cooperation with utility requests, and treatment of studies after termination. Have counsel determine whether recording a memorandum is appropriate and authorized; avoid publishing commercial terms unnecessarily. Validate the principal-trading structure against applicable state licensing rules. Va. Code § 54.1-2100 defines broker activity, while § 54.1-2103 addresses specified exemptions; neither should be reduced to a universal claim that calling compensation an assignment fee eliminates licensing requirements. Where the activity requires brokerage licensure, use a properly licensed participant under a documented arrangement. Never market another owner's parcel as though the pod already controls it. Log execution, effective dates, payment receipts, and every termination notice deadline.

Keep the workload inside the one-FT-plus-three-contractor pod by enforcing work-in-process limits. The principal handles at most four active owner negotiations and three substantive developer conversations per week. The electrical contractor carries no more than six live utility inquiries; the GIS contractor updates only the twelve survivors; the property-rights contractor prepares no more than two executable option packages during this sprint. Batch factual questions daily and hold one thirty-minute exception meeting twice weekly. Automated reminders can track utility response dates, owner commitments, and option notices, but do not automate interpretations of capacity or legal rights. P10 should produce a versioned evidence pack containing the legal description, control instrument, rights exceptions, constraint map, zoning pathway, utility correspondence, study status, buyer infrastructure requirements, and financial assumptions. Redact personal contact details and share proprietary studies only with appropriate rights and recipient restrictions. Maintain a change log identifying what changed, its source, and which prior claims became stale. If a restudy or municipal action changes feasibility, withdraw the affected pricing sheet immediately and notify every developer who received it before negotiating further.

P11 separates executable economics from attractive but unearned deal value. An option assignment earns operator revenue only under the applicable contract and accounting treatment; a per-MW milestone must identify the verified capacity basis, triggering event, payer, and payment date. Development carry remains outside realized revenue until the relevant entitlement and recognition conditions are satisfied. Do not count land purchase price, battery procurement, campus construction spending, customer electricity purchases, or underlying project value as origination revenue. Review principal-versus-agent presentation under ASC 606 if the operator collects money on another party's behalf; pass-through collections do not become operator revenue simply by crossing its bank account. For each proposed transaction, maintain separate columns for contract face amount, recognition status, cash received, third-party obligations, and remaining collection risk. Give buyers an itemized transfer schedule rather than a single opaque premium. Where future upgrade costs remain unresolved, use conditional pricing or buyer-funded study milestones instead of manufacturing certainty. The first thirty days should create defensible control and a collection pathway; the canonical two-to-six-month first-deal window makes a Day-30 cash receipt possible but unnecessary.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the current evidence version
   - Export the parcel register and preserve the underlying utility, title, and zoning documents.
   - Assign a version date and identify every capacity statement that depends on a pending study or contract.
2. Reconcile rights and cash before signing
   - Confirm grantor authority, assignability, termination notices, and total committed consideration.
   - Include unpaid contractual commitments in the $7,500 sprint ceiling and preserve the $17,500 launch reserve.
3. Transfer evidence without overstating readiness
   - Send an authorized buyer pack separating storage import/export questions from campus load-service dependencies.
   - Document recipients, sharing rights, utility conditions, upgrade exposure, and the exact milestones proposed for payment.
4. Close the loop on each buyer objection
   - Assign one resolver, one cost ceiling, and one decision date to every material objection.
   - Withdraw stale pricing immediately after adverse utility, title, or municipal evidence and preserve the correction record.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Beyond: Reconcile Cash, Then Apply Kill / Fix / Scale Thresholds  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The final nine days test whether the pod has created evidence that a developer will actually underwrite. Days 22–24 close buyer questions and repeat the upgrade-and-delay stress test wherever the utility evidence supports it. Days 25–27 reconcile consideration, contractor commitments, and termination dates, then obtain written developer feedback on next actions. Days 28–30 produce a signed decision memo for each controlled parcel and the pod as a whole. Kill means stop new spending and exercise available termination rights when justified; it does not mean abandon contractual duties. Fix means one bounded remediation cycle with a named resolver, a deadline within fourteen days, and no more than $1,000 of previously uncommitted diligence spending. Scale at Day 30 means authorize another controlled diligence cycle, not hire a second employee or accumulate paid options. The evidence thresholds below are internal management rules, not universal conversion benchmarks. An unanswered utility inquiry can justify a bounded pause within the two-to-six-month sales cycle, but cannot justify a rising capacity valuation. A confirmed fatal flaw overrides every positive funnel count and every optimistic buyer conversation.

Reconcile the annual scorecard before interpreting a promising pipeline. Conservative revenue is $110,000, with $77,000 total costs and $33,000 owner net profit at 30%. Base revenue is $505,000, with $292,900 costs and $212,100 profit at 42.0%. Scaled revenue is $900,000, with $414,000 costs and $486,000 profit at 54%. Conservative and Base use one FT plus three contractors; Scaled uses two FT plus four contractors, six people total. Illustrative revenue bridges are two $40,000 assignments plus $30,000 milestones for Conservative; five $75,000 assignments, $100,000 milestones, and $30,000 realized carry for Base; and eight $85,000 assignments, $160,000 milestones, and $60,000 realized carry for Scaled. These bridges reconcile the supplied totals without claiming those fee sizes are market quotations. Annual costs include failed options, specialist work, transaction expense, and other operating costs; the $1,800–$11,000 monthly operating range is not the entire annual cost base. Treat the sprint's possible $2,000 expired-option loss as part of those costs when incurred, not an extra deduction from the canonical profit. Maintain separate realized results and scenario forecasts so pipeline enthusiasm never changes recognized operator revenue.

Apply the decision hierarchy consistently: a hard failure triggers Kill first; Scale requires every listed condition; all other viable cases enter Fix only if the remediation budget and deadline are credible. Day 60 requires documented utility study progress, two controlled viable sites, and a developer term sheet before expanding option exposure. Day 90 requires one executed developer agreement, a documented payment trigger, and study-supported parameters for any capacity-based pricing. Continuing utility delay can remain Fix when buyers support the revised timetable and fund the incremental work; otherwise freeze expansion. Preserve a cash floor equal to three months of committed operating expense plus unavoidable site obligations. At the illustrative $4,500 monthly operating level, that floor starts at $13,500 before site obligations. Without new receipts, another $4,500 month reduces the initial $17,500 reserve to $13,000, so the pod must cut commitments or secure funding before authorizing that month. Developer deposits count as available cash only to the extent contracts permit their use after associated obligations. Move toward the six-person Scaled configuration only when collected fees and contracted work support the added fixed cost without consuming this reserve.
