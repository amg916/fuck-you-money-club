## Executive Summary

The practitioners include turbine OEMs, recip packagers, gas marketers and builders. They agree that the grid clock now sets data-center timelines, not compute. Buildings rise in 12 to 24 months while wires take 3 to 7 years or more, so operators build their own gas plant. The street mechanics are consistent. Never size to the load. Overbuild for reliability with many smaller units plus redundancy, and put batteries between the generators to absorb AI load swings. Collicutt's math shows 115 units of 2.5 megawatts delivering three nines on a 250 megawatt islanded campus, with first power at 13 months. VoltaGrid permitted 200 megawatts in 21 days in a severe nonattainment zone. ## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every practitioner names the same forcing function: the timing gap between a data-center build and a grid connection, not land or chips. Behind the Ordinary contrasts Memphis, which energized a machine in 299 days, with an Ohio utility that said none of 13,000 megawatts of new data-center load could be reliably served until a transmission project expected in late 2031 [s-MjCamBeVI @ 00:00:00]. America2050 puts the mismatch in operator terms. A data center can be built in 12 to 24 months, while getting the grid ready to feed it can take 3 to 7 years [n3d_tHGh1Qk @ 00:01:31]. INNIO's Thomas Seeba says that a year and a half ago they would have called on-site generation a temporary workaround. Grid access is now quoted at 5 to 10 or more years, which is longer than one data-center life cycle, so it has become a strategic choice if you want to stay in the game [XjWqZlxWJuw @ 00:11:46]. Collicutt's Matthew Swinmer gives the Alberta version. Roughly 12 gigawatts of interconnected load took 80 years to build, and 20 gigawatts of data-center applications now sit in the queue [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:05:58]. The shared conclusion is blunt. If the wire is years out, operators can wait, move, or build their own plant, and the revenue goes to whoever energizes first.

On fuel, the practitioners converge on natural gas as the only option that is dispatchable, available and deployable on a data-center clock. Swinmer says that after an Alberta panel, the question came up six or seven times: why not solar, wind or nuclear? The answer given was that natural gas ticks all of the boxes in a timely manner, while the others are non-dispatchable, unreliable, slow or unattractive on cost [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:15:54]. The second point of agreement is that you never size the plant to the load. Behind the Ordinary notes that turbines are available 85 to 95 percent of the time, while a data center expects upwards of 99.9 percent. The IEA therefore estimates 1.3 to 1.7 gigawatts of on-site capacity per gigawatt of demand, a 30 to 70 percent overbuild [s-MjCamBeVI @ 00:02:09]. Summit Energy's Reed Paige turns that into procurement. Nobody building a 200 megawatt campus with on-site power puts up two giant turbines, because if one goes down you lose half of everything. The play is many smaller units at N plus two, with one always in the shop for an oil change [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:13:50]. Dominic AI puts it simply: installed capacity and dependable supply are different things [rt0yZyeY-ms @ 00:02:59].

Every operator who has modeled real AI load agrees that the generators cannot ride the swings alone. Behind the Ordinary cites one facility jumping from 6 to 30 megawatts in a quarter second. An aeroderivative turbine needs 5 minutes to reach full output, which is why batteries absorb the fast swings and the generator handles the slow ones [s-MjCamBeVI @ 00:01:28]. Swinmer's directly coupled model tripped the plant about 100 times in a single AI-training day. That flips the protection logic. Instead of protecting the data center from unconditioned grid power, you now use a battery to protect the power plant from the volatile load [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:36:22]. Paige sees the same thing in the field, with loads sitting at 150 megawatts and dropping 20 to 30 megawatts in seconds. Paige expects BESS to sit between the recips or smaller combustion turbines and take the brunt of the transients [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:17:40]. GE Vernova's Mandar Pandit adds the warning that ties the consensus together. Behind the meter is not a magic bullet: you are operating in island mode, and the owner takes on more responsibility for the power plant than the utility would [rED467a2820 @ 00:15:57].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Swinmer's sizing workflow starts with single-unit reliability: hours in a year minus service downtime, divided by hours in a year. With 8,760 hours and roughly 500 hours of downtime, that comes to about 94 percent per unit [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:22:22]. System reliability is then a binomial calculation across N installed units with K required to run. For a 250 megawatt islanded campus at 99.9 percent, 100 units of 2.5 megawatts at base load have statistically zero reliability. One extra unit lifts that only to about 1.4 percent, and 115 units reach three nines [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:26:50]. The comparison is the money shot. Turbines needed two extra 50 megawatt units and medium-speed engines needed four or five extras. The recip fleet added only about 40 megawatts of overbuild versus nearly 100, an overbuild reduction of up to 18 percent and as much as 100 million dollars of capex [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:29:18]. The model also caps load steps at 10 percent for continuous operation and 15 to 20 percent otherwise. For storage, Paige passes along a starting-point heuristic: battery capacity at about 30 percent of the designed natural-gas capacity, with two-hour duration [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:18:34]. Swinmer's own runs used a 50 megawatt BESS for training loads and 100 megawatts for the more violent inference profile.

Speed is the product, and the practitioners publish their clocks. Collicutt's modular 2.5 megawatt blocks reach first power 13 months from purchase order and a full 50 megawatt plant in 16 months. Equipment is ordered while the building goes up, and each unit's install and commissioning takes about 2 months, run in parallel [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:53:19]. Scaled to 250 megawatts, that means 290 megawatts installed over 26 months, with capital staged so revenue starts before the full spend. Barton Malow's central Ohio energy center runs 12 Wärtsilä 18 megawatt units, about 216 megawatts, paired with roughly 130 megawatts in the BESS yard. The engines are marketed as a 12-month install, and the team's goal is five. They plan to get there by self-performing civil, concrete, boilermaker, steel, millwright and rigging work [WNd-Q3GWyEI @ 00:00:55]. Paige reports recip packages delivering in under 15 months. Wärtsilä can roll out 100 to 160 megawatts in under 24 months and then keep delivering every other month [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:10:42]. On the turbine side, America2050 describes GE Vernova's LM2500XPRESS: about 35 megawatts per unit, about 95 percent factory assembled on arrival, reaching full output in around 5 minutes. The developer behind the Abilene campus ordered 29 of them, roughly a gigawatt combined [n3d_tHGh1Qk @ 00:03:52].

Permitting and fuel logistics are where the street operators separate from the announcement crowd. VoltaGrid's Dave says that no matter the design, if a plant cannot get permitted it cannot be built. Dave reports approval for 200 megawatts in 21 days at a severe nonattainment site in Texas. Selective catalytic reduction there cuts NOx by 98 percent, SO2 by up to 95 percent and CO by over 95 percent [rQu_8zcOaZU @ 00:02:16]. The first thing VoltaGrid checks on any proposed site is location, because a minor new source review caps emissions at 250 tons per year. Working with its generator and controls partners, the team pushed what fits in an attainment zone from about 300 megawatts to 700 megawatts [rQu_8zcOaZU @ 00:03:12]. VoltaGrid also bridges the gas itself. It compresses pipeline supply into CNG and trucks it as a virtual pipeline before the line gets built, feeding 3.3 megawatt trailerized units sound-attenuated to 65 dBA at 7 meters. America2050 shows the other edge. xAI ran portable turbines under a rule allowing up to 364 days without a permit, then agreed to remove 69 of them after legal action [n3d_tHGh1Qk @ 00:02:55]. Paige qualifies inbound developers by asking how they will tackle air quality. If they just stare back, they have not done the homework [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:22:14].

   - Confirm attainment status and the megawatts that fit under a minor new source review before committing to equipment.

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether self-generation is a bridge or the permanent design. Behind the Ordinary treats the plant as scaffolding. In Tennessee, the largest campus built around on-site generation ran over 400 megawatts of gas turbines. Then a 150 megawatt grid connection arrived with 150 megawatts of batteries, the turbines were scaled down, and one bridge was dismantled, because the grid is still the destination [s-MjCamBeVI @ 00:07:07]. Thomas Seeba of the Innio Group, speaking on Datacenter Dynamics, says he has changed his own view. A year and a half earlier he would have called on-site generation a temporary workaround. With grid access now 5 to 10 or more years out, longer than one data center life cycle, he now calls it a strategic choice if you want to be part of the game [XjWqZlxWJuw @ 00:11:46]. The Dirt To Data Podcast guest pushes the other way. Large loads should stay grid connected as good grid citizens, and a completely islanded campus is neutral, neither helping nor hindering the grid, except that it could be quite noisy [quCPqX-ExfQ @ 00:00:34]. America2050 shows the same ambiguity at Abilene. Oracle describes the on-site gas plant at the 1.2 GW campus as backup, while other reports call the turbines a bridge until the grid catches up [n3d_tHGh1Qk @ 00:06:34].

Machine selection is the second fault line. Matthew Swinmer of Collicutt Energy Services modeled a 250 megawatt islanded campus that needs 99.9% reliability within two years. He compared three fleets: seven 50 MW simple cycle turbines, about 18 medium speed 20 MW engines, or 115 Rolls-Royce MTU 20V4000 2.5 MW units. The high-speed fleet overbuilt only about 40 megawatts versus almost 100, an overbuild reduction of as much as 18% worth up to 100 million dollars in capex [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:29:18]. Over 25 years on a hosting load, his model put the high-speed recips at a 17% IRR, versus 14% for medium speed and 9% for simple cycle turbines. He credits most of the gap to faster first power [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:40:46]. Reed Paige of Summit Energy reaches the same many-small-units logic from the gas desk. Nobody building a 200 megawatt campus puts up two giant turbines, because if one goes down you lose half of everything. So the field runs N plus two, with one unit always in the shop [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:14:03]. America2050 documents the turbine-first camp. Crusoe ordered 29 GE LM2500 Express aero units, roughly a gigawatt, accepting that the approach gives up efficiency because speed to power is what developers care about [n3d_tHGh1Qk @ 00:04:52].

The fuel debate splits three ways. Tim Beggins of ELK Trading argues natural gas will be the primary behind-the-meter fuel for the next several decades. His reasoning is that gas plants can be permitted and built much faster than small modular reactors, and nuclear will likely take over only in the 2040s [mRxFZBnZSiM @ 00:01:05]. The Clean Energy Edge Podcast counters that large-scale solar plus battery storage is a tremendous opportunity, because solar projects are not waiting years for gas turbines. The host concedes that solar and batteries alone will not necessarily power a massive campus around the clock [CjD4mAL9NCA @ 00:03:16]. On hydrogen, Mandar Pandit of GE Vernova says the company has proven it can fire gas turbines on 100% hydrogen. It is also bringing the 600-plus MW Teesside project in the UK into operation with carbon capture attached [rED467a2820 @ 00:13:05]. Seeba reports that the hydrogen push that dominated discussion two years ago has slowed, because delivery infrastructure is not being built at the expected scale. His answer is hydrogen-ready engines that run natural gas today and can be upgraded later, so the plant never becomes a stranded or dead asset [XjWqZlxWJuw @ 00:07:21]. The open question is what the same equipment burns ten to fifteen years out.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive surprise is the load itself. Behind the Ordinary cites a documented case where a facility's load jumped from 6 megawatts to 30 in a quarter second. A typical aeroderivative turbine takes 5 minutes to reach full output, so batteries absorb the fast swings and generators handle the slow ones [s-MjCamBeVI @ 00:01:28]. Swinmer's model shows what happens without that buffer. Coupling generation directly to an AI training profile produced about 100 load exceedances in a single day, each one a trip to protect the plant, and even the recips and medium speed engines struggled on AI inference [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:34:16]. His fix inverts the old architecture. Instead of shielding the data center from unconditioned grid power, you protect the power plant from the volatile load with a battery. A 50 MW BESS sharply cut exceedances across all three technologies, and the inference profile needed 100 MW [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:36:22]. Reed Paige passes along a design heuristic he files under for-what-it's-worth: start with battery capacity of roughly 30% of designed gas capacity at two-hour duration. Some campuses swing from 150 megawatts down 20 or 30 megawatts in seconds, and a ton of spinning mass cannot simply slam the brakes. BESS units sit between the recips or smaller turbines to take the brunt of those transients [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:18:34].

The second trap is acting like a utility without a utility's engineering discipline. Pandit of GE Vernova says behind the meter is not a magic bullet. Developers secure their site and generation equipment, then realize they never planned for the compensation, protection and control that a utility connection normally provides. Island mode brings a whole grid management job on top of generation [rED467a2820 @ 00:16:43]. Permitting is the hard wall. America2050 recounts how xAI ran Colossus off an 8 MW grid connection by parking portable gas turbines outside. They operated under a rule allowing up to 364 days without a permit, environmental groups said dozens ran beyond what the permits allowed, and xAI later agreed to remove 69 of them after legal action [n3d_tHGh1Qk @ 00:02:55]. Dave of VoltaGrid puts it bluntly: no matter the design or reliability, if it cannot get permitted, it cannot be built. His team won approval for 200 MW in 21 days in a severe nonattainment zone, using selective catalytic reduction that destroys NOx by 98% [rQu_8zcOaZU @ 00:02:16]. Paige uses air quality to screen developers. Ask an inbound developer how they will handle air quality, and if they just stare at you, they have not done their homework [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:22:14].

The economics break on utilization and supply. Swinmer warns that AI inference campuses suffer from stranded power. Fuel burn looks low only because the plant is not generating much, while the full capital cost is still deployed. The result is much lower returns, negative NPV at a 5% discount rate, and a very long simple payback, and his figures exclude battery and power conditioning costs [CVfxSDNW3G4 @ 00:42:20]. Redundancy has to be paid for before the plant generates its first megawatt hour. Dominic AI relays International Energy Agency modeling that reliable service could take thirty to seventy percent more on-site gas capacity than demand. When a generator stops for maintenance, the remaining machines still have to carry the load [rt0yZyeY-ms @ 00:02:59]. Supplier risk is the next exposure. America2050 reports that Crusoe dropped its Boom Supersonic Superpower order with no unit delivered and switched to proven turbines. The question had become whether a new machine can be trusted when a billion-dollar campus depends on it [n3d_tHGh1Qk @ 00:08:14]. Paige adds that many large-load requests are duplicates, sometimes the same developer asking for power in 17 different places. His team now qualifies every inbound developer before spending time on it [169FuRa1T-0 @ 00:21:57].
