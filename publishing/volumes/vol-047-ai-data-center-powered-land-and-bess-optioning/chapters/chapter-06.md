## Executive Summary

Practitioners agree that hosting capacity is the load or DER a node, feeder or substation can absorb without reinforcement, and that published values are screening ceilings rather than connection rights. ESB Networks ties the hunt to lead time, since substations and transformers are the slowest connection component to build. The mechanics are concrete. LUMA flags feeders above 15% DG penetration for supplementary studies. National Grid's Foxboro 2 demo showed 6.65 MW available against an 8 MVA rating at 75% loading. ESB requires an LV substation within 300 meters. CAISO's POI heat map refreshes 30 days after each cluster study. PowerFactory runs expose the binding constraint, either line loading or connection-point voltage. The disagreements center on granularity and freshness. IREC says feeder-level results paint whole feeders red, ComEd updates quarterly and LUMA updates monthly. KorrAI prices a one-month delay on a 60 MW facility at up to $14 million.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every utility and software vendor on this beat defines headroom the same way. It is the amount of new load or generation a node, feeder or substation can absorb before a limit is hit or the utility has to build. DIgSILENT frames hosting capacity as the maximum additional load or DER that could be connected at a node without violating system constraints or requiring network development [7iequWeuZTQ @ 00:00:06]. ESB Networks pitches its heat map in near-identical terms. It shows how much demand or generation could be added into a substation without reinforcement, at all voltage levels, so customers are steered toward stations where they can connect without significant reinforcement [fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:00:22]. IREC's regulatory team pushes the same concept forward in time. They describe proactive hosting capacity analysis as an estimate of how much load or generation fits under forecast conditions without upgrades [AKo4FQtyC68 @ 00:08:08]. For a powered-land operator, the reason this matters is lead time. ESB Networks states flatly that substation and transformer installations are the longest lead time component of connections if they must be built or reinforced [fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:01:24]. That is why every operator below builds a map to surface transformer and feeder headroom before a request is filed.

The second point of agreement is that a published number is a ceiling for screening, not a connection right. Alfonso Areti, LUMA's renewable energy integration manager, tells users that supplementary studies are necessary for any feeder with DG penetration greater than 15%. LUMA's incremental capacity layer also covers only feeders already verified in the field [UnmedCjwEb0 @ 00:04:49]. Dan Rogers, a Massachusetts distribution planning engineer at National Grid, calls a displayed 5.2 MW value only an upper limit. A recloser without voltage supervisory control will lower the downstream values [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:14:37]. ESB Networks sends users to its details page, and specifically to the FAQ asking whether the capacity available value means a customer can connect the amount shown [fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:04:12]. California ISO requires an 'I agree' click before entry and labels its POI heat map results as for information only and subject to change [YAtPW03BCrY @ 00:01:08]. LUMA and ESB Networks also gate entry behind a disclaimer [UnmedCjwEb0 @ 00:00:52]. The read is consistent across four separate utilities and grid operators: the map tells you where to look, and the study tells you what you actually get.

The third shared conviction concerns granularity and freshness. Midhat of IREC, speaking on Atlas Public Policy's EV Hub, warns that feeder-level results often reflect the lowest-capacity node. One bottleneck can make a whole feeder look red even if there is usable capacity nearby, so IREC advocates nodal or section-level results [eIAGiv0LD8Y @ 00:08:25]. National Grid moved in the same direction: its November 2021 update added nodal results, bank ratings and DG by generation type [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:07:30]. IREC's proactive HCA team lists subcircuit granularity as a core input [AKo4FQtyC68 @ 00:25:24] and states that stale maps lose users' trust [AKo4FQtyC68 @ 01:00:31]. On refresh cadence, Midhat calls monthly updates the ideal. Midhat also wants maps to disclose the constraint type (voltage, thermal or protection), pointing to New York, which publishes both constraint types and magnitudes [eIAGiv0LD8Y @ 00:16:17]. LUMA says its penetration data is usually updated monthly [UnmedCjwEb0 @ 00:05:38]. ComEd's Shikar runs quarterly updates across 11,500 square miles and 5,500 feeders, a scale Shikar says requires automation [eIAGiv0LD8Y @ 00:13:02]. Shikar adds that it takes a separate publishing team, skill retention and change management [eIAGiv0LD8Y @ 00:14:07].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The portal workflows follow the same click path. On LUMA's map, after the disclaimer, the magnifying glass in the upper right searches by address. Users then toggle three layers: voltage level; DG penetration, banded green 0-10%, orange 10-15% and red above 15%; and incremental hosting capacity per segment [UnmedCjwEb0 @ 00:04:25]. ESB Networks has users pick a demand or generation view and filter pins by capacity range and voltage. Clicking a pin shows voltage, installed capacity, available capacity, parent feeder and parent station, and users then check the parent HV substation too [fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:03:08]. For low-voltage connections, ESB's rule is that a substation typically must be within 300 meters or less; otherwise a new substation must be provided nearer the site [fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:02:47]. Dan Rogers ran the live version on National Grid's Massachusetts portal. Foxboro 2 carries an 8 MVA rating, is 75% loaded and has a steady forecast. The feeder showed 6.65 MW available and 350 kW connected, with no ASO and with high side protection already in place, so 3V0 was not needed [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:10:39]. Rogers then measured 0.64 miles to the main line and about 3 miles to the substation. Rogers also flagged pink one- or two-phase lines as not ideal for large sites [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:11:42].

Behind the maps sit the study engines. In DIgSILENT's PowerFactory demo, the analyst activates the hosting capacity study case from the distribution network analysis toolbox. Hosting sites can be terminals, substations, feeders or grids. The analyst sets thermal and terminal voltage limits, and the chosen constraints decide which calculations run, here load flow only [7iequWeuZTQ @ 00:01:57]. The tabular reports give per-node capacity, the limiting factor and the limiting component. The highest-capacity node was capped by loading on a line at the start of the feeder. The lowest was capped by the maximum voltage limit at the connection point itself [7iequWeuZTQ @ 00:04:28]. The Lecture 18 tutorial on the Electrical Engineering - Power System channel runs the same command on a 2.5 MW wind farm test case. It sets 100% maximum thermal loading and plus-or-minus 5% voltage limits, caps THD at 5% based on grid codes, and returns 0.9 MW maximum active power at 99% loading and 1% maximum THD [9N3qVzFvgwg @ 00:04:21]. Josimar Tello Maita goes stochastic instead. The model pairs Gaussian mixture models with Monte Carlo simulation through OpenDSS and Matlab, running probabilistic power flow every 5 minutes on a 207-node Bogota network fed by one 40 MVA substation transformer [xyUNxe8t4I0 @ 00:01:58]. IREC's engineers name CYME, Synergi and Aspen as the utility-side tools [AKo4FQtyC68 @ 00:57:45].

At transmission scale, California ISO's Order 2023 POI heat map follows the same logic. The ISO refreshes it every 30 calendar days after a cluster study, about three times a year [YAtPW03BCrY @ 00:00:19]. The user selects a scenario, meaning an available cluster study, and enters an MW injection amount. Hovering gives an available-capacity popup, and a substation table lists substation, kV, available capacity, PTO and study area. In the live demo, a teal location showed 31.86 MW. When the presenter entered 115, Meadow Lane at 31.86 MW turned orange, which means less than zero, and red font in the results table flagged a poor POI [YAtPW03BCrY @ 00:04:27]. For corridor work, Shawn Yarter's ArcGIS Online Web AppBuilder project turns a drawn route into a landowner list. Draw a polyline, apply a 50-foot buffer to model a typical 100-foot transmission easement, extract the intersecting Waller County parcels and export a shapefile [RoQA5d_fDFw @ 00:03:33]. KorrAI supplies the data center stakes: one month of delay on a 60 MW facility can cost up to $14 million [MvUw50RZNRQ @ 00:00:36]. KorrAI also warns that Phase I and II work leans on 20 to 80 boreholes across roughly 1.5 million square feet [MvUw50RZNRQ @ 00:00:19].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Screen substation headroom first
   - Open the utility's substation-level heat map in demand view and filter by capacity range and voltage.
   - ESB Networks' map points customers to substations where they can connect without significant reinforcement. Clicking a pin returns voltage, installed capacity, available capacity, parent feeder and parent station [ESB Networks — fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:03:08].
2. Drill into the feeder with pending queue shown
   - Click the candidate feeder with the connected plus pending layer active and measure the distance to the main line and the substation.
   - Dan Rogers pulled 6.65 MW available and 350 kW connected on the Foxboro 2 feeder. Rogers measured 0.64 miles to the main line and about 3 miles to the substation [National Grid Distributed Generation — KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:11:42].
3. Run a node-level hosting capacity study
   - Set thermal and voltage constraints in PowerFactory and read the limiting factor and limiting component per node.
   - The highest-capacity node was limited by line loading at the feeder start, and the lowest by the maximum voltage at the connection point. Whole networks can be run with parallel computation [DIgSILENT GmbH – Power System Solutions — 7iequWeuZTQ @ 00:04:28].
4. Test the injection at transmission POIs
   - Select the latest cluster study scenario on CAISO's POI heat map, enter the planned MW, and check which substations stay blue.
   - At a 115 entry, Meadow Lane at 31.86 MW turned orange, while a darker blue location might fit the request [California ISO — YAtPW03BCrY @ 00:05:25].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is over how fine-grained a headroom number must be before an operator trusts it. Midhat of IREC warned on Atlas Public Policy's EV Hub that feeder-level maps often report the lowest-capacity node, so 'one bottleneck from a feeder can make a whole feeder look red even if there is usable capacity nearby' [eIAGiv0LD8Y @ 00:08:25], and pushed for nodal or section-level results. DIgSILENT's PowerFactory workflow already delivers that. Its tabular reports give maximum hosting capacity per node plus the limiting factor and limiting component. On the demo feeder, the high node was capped by line loading at the feeder start, while the weak node was capped by maximum voltage at its own connection point [7iequWeuZTQ @ 00:03:16]. Utilities publishing public maps land in between. LUMA's Alfonso Areti showed incremental capacity per feeder segment, but only for feeders already verified in the field, with coverage expanding periodically [UnmedCjwEb0 @ 00:06:02]. National Grid's Dan Rogers promised nodal results, bank ratings and DG by generation type in the November 2021 update [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:07:30]. Midhat's point for site hunters is that a red feeder on a coarse map can still hide usable capacity at nearby nodes, so section-level data decides the parcel.

Practitioners also split on how fresh and how forward-looking a map has to be. LUMA says its DG penetration layer is 'usually updated monthly' [UnmedCjwEb0 @ 00:05:38]. Midhat called monthly refresh the ideal and urged maps to disclose whether voltage, thermal or protection is the binding constraint, as New York does. ComEd's Shikar countered with the operating reality: 11,500 square miles and 5,500 feeders refreshed quarterly. That cadence only works with automation, a separate publishing team and skill retention [eIAGiv0LD8Y @ 00:13:02]. On the transmission side, California ISO refreshes its Order 2023 POI heat map every 30 calendar days after a cluster study, which works out to roughly three times a year [YAtPW03BCrY @ 00:00:19]. IREC's team argued that static snapshots are the wrong frame entirely. Proactive HCA should fold in forecast load including electrification, forecast and dependable DER, and planned upgrades on 2-, 5- and 10-year horizons. NV Energy's time slider is their working example [AKo4FQtyC68 @ 00:21:21]. Midhat's own target is 576 time points a year, because that resolution shows when capacity actually exists, not just whether it exists.

The deepest disagreement is whether flexible storage and aggregations can be counted as headroom at all. Stem's Ted Co argued that projects over 1 MW can already bid alone under Order 841, and that CAISO's 24x7 participation rule 'kills multi-use' [Mk3XaANjMd4 @ 00:23:09]. National Grid's Tim Rowan answered from the wires side. When a storage developer supplies no operating profile, the utility studies the conservative frequency-regulation case, which risks flicker and can saddle the developer with upgrades it may not need [Mk3XaANjMd4 @ 00:47:25]. NYISO's Mary McColgan reported the market model working anyway: over 350 MW enrolled, a 100 kW aggregation floor, a 20 MW cap on injection-capable facilities and no upper limit on aggregations [6tz6xMnl1D8 @ 00:07:58]. Energy economist Margarita Patray drew the opposite lesson for data centers. PJM expects about 30 GW by 2030, and unless flexibility is defined, measured and enforceable, planners assume near full load [6tz6xMnl1D8 @ 01:00:20]. Patray's prescription is grid-draw limits, firm curtailment, telemetry and cost rules. Anya at CAISO sat between the camps, pushing a 2026 initiative that allows end-user exports inside DR as long as the whole aggregation does not export.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most repeated trap is treating a published number as a connection right. National Grid's Dan Rogers stressed that the 5.2 MW shown on one feeder is only an upper limit. A recloser without voltage supervisory control will pull downstream values lower, while earlier DG-funded upgrades such as a bidirectional recloser push them up [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:14:37]. Rogers also flagged why map totals and field reality diverge. The map excludes 1.5 MW of batteries and natural gas backup generators for hospitals, and the utility will not publish backup versus peaking units because that is too customer specific [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:13:53]. ESB Networks points every user to its details page, including the FAQ 'does the capacity available value mean I can connect the amount shown' [fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:04:12]. California ISO gates its POI heat map behind an 'I agree' disclaimer and labels results for information only and subject to change. In the live demo, entering 115 MW flipped Meadow Lane, which showed 31.86 MW, to orange, and red font in the results table marks a poor POI [YAtPW03BCrY @ 00:04:27]. Rogers added that a 13.8 kV to 4 kV step-down lowers hosting capacity, and that flicker and 3V0 screens against minimum load were still coming in post-processing.

Headroom on a map also dies on distance, phase count and dirt. ESB Networks says a substation typically must be within 300 meters to suit a low-voltage connection; otherwise a new substation must be provided nearer the site. ESB also notes that substations and transformers are the longest lead-time component when they must be built or reinforced [fqIsW9Rhp48 @ 00:02:47]. Rogers' Foxboro demo showed 6.65 MW available with high-side protection already in place, but it exposed three problems. Pink one- or two-phase lines are not ideal for large sites, the measured run was 0.64 miles to the main line and about 3 miles to the substation, and the topography basemap exposed swamps [KxAPuQUu_II @ 00:11:21]. KorrAI warns that Phase I and II work leans on outdated weather models and 20 to 80 boreholes across roughly 1.5 million square feet. As a result, 'most of the ground still goes unmeasured' until heavy earthworks load it, and one month of delay on a 60 MW facility can cost up to $14 million [MvUw50RZNRQ @ 00:00:36]. Shawn Yarter's routing workflow shows the easement exposure: a 50-foot buffer around a drawn polyline yields the typical 100-foot easement, and every intersecting parcel becomes a landowner who must be contacted [RoQA5d_fDFw @ 00:03:33].

The economics checks are blunt. CAISO's Anya said 'wholesale market revenue is not enough of a business driver to make this market model viable' for developers or retail customers. That came after a decade-old CAISO aggregation model drew under 1 MW of participation [6tz6xMnl1D8 @ 00:18:04]. Mike Berlinsky of CES noted that Order 2222 does not guarantee revenue, so projects need value stacking. The ESA panel also heard that New England capacity prices had fallen to $1 to $2 per kW-month, which is cyclical merchant revenue [Mk3XaANjMd4 @ 00:56:56]. Patray put participation at less than one tenth of one percent: ISO-NE has 20 MW of aggregated DR against 25 GW, and PJM cleared 290 MW against 160-plus GW. Patray also noted that Order 2222 never defined who pays for metering, telemetry and upgrades [6tz6xMnl1D8 @ 00:57:29]. The IIT Roorkee BESS lecture runs the math at the feeder. Load creeping from X to 1.1X would otherwise force a 2X replacement transformer. Because demand charges bill peak kW, trimming 400 kW to 300 kW at Rs 3 per kW-month saves Rs 300 a month [Q5WOz2acUrM @ 00:10:15]. Its 33-bus case sized about 8 MWh and 2 MW across four batteries while forcing state of charge back to 50% at the end of every 24 hours.
