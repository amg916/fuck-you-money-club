## 1. Define the saleable unit before assigning a dollar to megawatts  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start the ledger with the operator's contractual consideration, not the developer's land purchase price, battery procurement budget, or campus construction commitment. The canonical Formula v2.0 outcomes fix annual operator revenue at $110,000, $505,000, and $900,000; the cohort composition here is an illustrative reconstruction rather than a claim about executed transactions. Conservative comprises two BESS assignments at $40,000 and three separately earned origination milestones at $10,000. Base comprises five BESS assignments at $65,000, six campus milestones at $25,000, and $30,000 of realized development carry. Scaled comprises six BESS assignments at $90,000, eight campus milestones at $35,000, and $80,000 of realized carry. A milestone can belong to a continuing project, so these counts describe revenue events rather than necessarily distinct parcels. Give each event a contract identifier, performance obligation, acceptance document, invoice date, and collection restriction. Exclude unvested carry and disputed contingent consideration from earned revenue. Apply ASC 606 where applicable to the actual arrangement; evaluate development interests under their governing accounting treatment. Client-funded deposits and escrow balances remain separate from operating sales, even when they temporarily pass through an account controlled by the operator.

Separate a standalone BESS parcel of 5–25 acres from an AI data-center campus before comparing acquisition costs. A battery site needs a credible charging and export pathway, equipment layout, fire-access geometry, and an applicable interconnection process; a campus adds sustained load service, fiber diversity, water or alternative cooling design, and jurisdiction-specific land-use constraints. Screening 69kV–345kV substations in QGIS identifies places to investigate, not electricity available for sale. Attach every valuation to utility-documented deliverable MW, stated service conditions, network-upgrade exposure, and a realistic energization schedule. Until deliverability is documented, give the parcel no powered-land premium in the underwriting model. An illustrative $25,000 campus milestone could be $1,000 per MW on 25 documented MW, earned only when the contract's utility-evidence and buyer-acceptance requirements are satisfied. A later restudy can still change economics; disclose that exposure in the evidence package rather than describing a study as an unconditional service commitment. Distinguish import capacity, export capacity, firm service, and interruptible service. None is interchangeable with nameplate battery output, transmission-line voltage, or a queue application's requested capacity. A mismatch requires repricing or withdrawal before another option extension is purchased.

For gis_landlocked_parcel_easement_assemblage, the unit is a voluntarily assembled, assignable access solution supported by title evidence, not a threatening holdout position. Within one illustrative $65,000 Base assignment, attribute $20,000 of value to resolving recorded access rights and $45,000 to the remaining site package; those components sum to the assignment fee and create no additional revenue event. A $3,000 strip option, $4,000 boundary survey, $2,000 title examination, and $3,000 easement drafting budget total $12,000 of attributable cost, leaving $8,000 access-component contribution before shared acquisition expense and overhead. GIS parcel geometry alone does not establish legal access, easement scope, maintenance obligations, or rights adequate for transformers and heavy construction vehicles. Require recorded instruments, survey reconciliation, and a title professional's review of assignability and beneficiary parcels. If the access theory fails, exercise available diligence contingencies and offer survey-backed voluntary access services instead. Price that fallback under a separate written scope and evaluate any broker licensing requirements before solicitation. An illustrative $5,000 service fee against $3,500 delivery cost yields $1,500 contribution, but it is alternative work, not an automatic recovery added to the canonical cohort. Never count the same survey twice.

## 3. Fund the cash gap instead of spending the paper margin  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Measure cash availability on a different clock from revenue recognition. For this model, DSO runs from an accepted, billable milestone to receipt into the designated collection account; payout lock is the additional contractual period before those funds become unrestricted. Conservative assumes 60 days DSO plus 15 locked days, Base assumes 75 plus 15, and Scaled assumes 90 plus 30. If your actual DSO metric already ends at unrestricted receipt, do not add the lock again. Use a 30-day month consistently. Recurring funded spend comprises delivery labor, CAC, technical diligence, overhead, and funding expense: $61,000 annually in Conservative, $232,000 in Base, and $333,000 in Scaled. Monthly averages are $5,083.33, $19,333.33, and $27,750. Multiplying by 2.5, three, and four months produces float requirements of $12,708.33, $58,000, and $111,000. These figures finance modeled expenditures, not the full face value of receivables. They also assume evenly spread spending and eventual collections; a concentrated survey campaign can create a larger trough. The stated $1,800–$11,000 monthly opex range describes startup operating context, not a ceiling on all-in cohort delivery and diligence spending. Build a weekly forecast before treating an annual average as spendable liquidity.

Add ring-fenced cash for the full annual failed-option budget and the modeled reversal exposure: $16,000 in Conservative, $60,900 in Base, and $81,000 in Scaled. Combining those buffers with recurring-spend float gives illustrative liquidity targets of $28,708.33, $118,900, and $192,000. This is deliberately conservative treasury planning, not another expense deduction from owner profit. The option budget already appears in the waterfall, while the reversal provision may precede an actual cash claim. Track funded reserve cash, booked provisions, and amounts already spent in separate columns so the same exposure is not replenished automatically after it has expired. A $10,000–$50,000 startup capitalization can cover the modeled Conservative target at the upper end but cannot independently finance the Base or Scaled targets. Bridge the difference with retained earnings, committed capital, staged contractor commitments, or negotiated buyer deposits whose use is contractually permitted. Borrowing proceeds are funding, not revenue. Refundable buyer money remains restricted to its allowed purpose and must not support owner distributions. The two-to-six-month first-deal window is a separate startup runway problem: six months of Conservative recurring spend alone is $30,500, before $16,000 of option and reversal protection.

Operate a thirteen-week cash forecast keyed to option expiry dates, utility invoice dates, buyer acceptance dates, and unrestricted collection dates. A Base invoice accepted on day zero might reach the collection account on day 75 and become available on day 90; paying a surveyor on day ten leaves an 80-day funding interval for that expenditure. Obtain invoice-level lock terms instead of assuming every title-company disbursement has the same schedule. Request partial milestone payments for discrete accepted work, subject to the actual contract and accounting treatment, rather than billing for speculative deliverable power. Distinguish landlord option deposits, earnest money, utility study deposits, and the operator's fee receivable; each has different refund rights and permitted uses. Tie the bank balance to a daily restricted-cash register and reconcile settlement statements after every assignment. A thirty-day collection slip adds one month of recurring funding need: $5,083.33, $19,333.33, or $27,750. It does not immediately reduce earned revenue, although interest and credit deterioration can subsequently reduce profit. Delay owner distributions whenever forecast unrestricted cash falls below committed expenditures plus surviving reserves. An available credit line helps only if its draw conditions remain satisfied during the same project delay.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Construct the contractual cash calendar
   - Record every option payment, study commitment, acceptance condition, and release date.
   - Separate refundable deposits from earned fees and identify the document controlling each restriction.
2. Reconcile earned fees to unrestricted receipts
   - Match contracts, acceptance evidence, invoices, settlement statements, and bank entries.
   - Measure DSO to account receipt, then add only genuinely separate payout-lock days.
3. Fund the next thirteen weeks
   - Forecast weekly committed expenditures and protect remaining option and reversal exposure.
   - Deduct spending already incurred from the relevant budget instead of counting the same reserve twice.
4. Release owner cash only after coverage
   - Compare unrestricted balances and drawable committed funding against the stressed cash trough.
   - Hold distributions when a thirty-day collection slip would interrupt a surviving contractual commitment.

## 4. Stress fee realization, utility exposure, and the legal right to get paid  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Stress the cohort before increasing option commitments. Holding costs fixed, a 10% decline in realized fee revenue reduces annual profit to $22,000, $161,600, and $396,000. Alternatively, a 20% increase in delivery labor plus CAC adds $6,400, $25,200, and $39,600 of costs, leaving $26,600, $186,900, and $446,400. Losing one modeled BESS assignment without recovering committed costs removes $40,000, $65,000, or $90,000 and leaves negative $7,000, positive $147,100, or positive $396,000. Run each shock independently first, then combine correlated failures rather than pretending failed utility diligence, delayed collection, and option expiry are unrelated. An illustrative additional $10,000 expired option payment reduces profit dollar for dollar unless already included in the annual failure budget. Do not subtract an exposure already captured in the reserve and then also report its settlement as a fresh loss. Track failure frequency by utility territory, county, buyer, and asset type. A low headline CAC can conceal a costly concentration of parcels whose zoning process takes longer than their options remain assignable. Stop buying similar exposure when the failure pattern survives independent technical and title review.

Network-upgrade exposure belongs in site valuation even when the operator is not the party funding construction. Suppose utility documentation supports a conditional 25 MW campus pathway with $5 million of identified upgrades: the developer faces $200,000 per MW before other project costs. A restudy increasing that estimate to $10 million doubles exposure to $400,000 per MW. Those amounts are developer capex, not operator revenue or automatically operator expense, but they can erase the buyer's willingness to pay a $25,000 milestone. Establish written limits on operator-funded study commitments, such as a separately approved $5,000 scope, and never sign a broad reimbursement undertaking without modeling its full exposure. Under 18 CFR 35.28, applicable transmission and generator interconnection requirements inform relevant tariff processes; they do not establish retail load service merely because a BESS queue record exists. Read the utility's actual service and interconnection documents. For a campus, verify fiber route diversity rather than two carriers sharing one conduit, and test cooling assumptions against available water service. For BESS, evaluate access, fire-response layout, and local siting requirements separately. Reprice, reduce the option payment, or exit when a revised energization date exceeds the remaining contractual control period.

The right to collect a fee must survive title, licensing, and land-use scrutiny. Trade as a principal holding an actual assignable option interest, and determine applicable state licensing requirements before marketing interests or negotiating for another party. Texas Occupations Code sections 1101.002 and 1101.351 define brokerage activity and require licensure for covered conduct; merely labeling compensation an assignment fee does not resolve the issue. Retain licensed brokerage support where the transaction requires it and budget its compensation without duplicating a netted revenue share. Under 33 U.S.C. 1344, discharges of dredged or fill material into covered waters can require authorization, making wetland impacts a concrete cost and schedule input rather than a cosmetic GIS layer. Check current municipal ordinances and effective dates for data-center or BESS restrictions before extending an option. Keep seller representations, recorded access evidence, utility study status, and buyer disclosures aligned with the same dated package. Close only when assignment mechanics, surviving refund rights, and collection instructions are understood. If an option fails diligence, record the actual burn, preserve any refund claim, and redirect effort toward documented voluntary access services rather than presenting an impaired site as powered inventory.
