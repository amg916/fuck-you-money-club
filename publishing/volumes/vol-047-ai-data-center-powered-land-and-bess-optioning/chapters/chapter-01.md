# Chapter 01: Powered Land: Site Qualification and Data Center Land Deals

*Buildable acreage minimums, powered vs. unpowered land, CBRE and Thor site-selection criteria, land option contracts and site control, Stargate's $1 land option, powered shells, and West Texas powered-land plays*

## Executive Summary

Practitioners from Thor Equities, datacenterHawk, CBRE, LandBridge, Foley & Lardner and Zego converge on one rule: power is the site, and acreage follows. Site hunts now start from available megawatts and work backward to land, fiber, water and gas. The street numbers are blunt: 1MW per acre with a 100-acre floor, power run-outs at $20M per mile and fiber at $2M, $30-100k non-refundable utility studies, about $500k to rezone, and $22M posted upfront to lock power on one deal. Powered land trades at 4-5x unpowered, and NoVA ground has hit $3-4M per acre. Developers control dirt through option contracts, from 2-3 year solar options to Stargate's $1 option. The split: LandBridge says go behind-the-meter on cheap Permian gas, while Zego treats grid interconnection privilege itself as the asset.

## 1. The Practitioner Consensus: Power Is the Site

Every serious operator in this group now starts with electrons, not dirt. Foley & Lardner's Faris and Conrad report that about two-thirds of respondents name power as the top obstacle, and that the old sequence is reversed: start from available megawatts and work backward to the parcel, because those attributes matter more than raw acreage or even land price [wOKA9jWA8fw @ 00:02:26]. Thor Equities' panel ranks the criteria the same way: power first, then proximity to lines and substations, then contiguous acreage for a secure perimeter, water and sewer, zoning buffers and tax exemptions [yg9V2befNR0 @ 00:18:34]. datacenterHawk calls powered land 'the new supply' and puts the price gap between unpowered and powered land at four to five times [dTHn2ejpXvM @ 00:18:51]. Kenny Schoolcraft at Zego defines the asset as land bundled with 'grid privilege': a parcel near an existing substation, an advanced queue position, or transmission with headroom, and frames the payoff as one to two years to connect versus three to five [2ORDbZvOirU @ 00:08:13]. The blunt summary from Foley: securing land without power is increasingly a speculative exercise, and being further along in the queue can decide whether a project builds or gets shelved for years.

The baseline physical checklist repeats almost word for word across channels. Franklin Andrew Cruz, marketing an owned 130-acre site that could be assembled to 288, works from a hard rule: an AI data center takes 1MW per acre, and without a 100-acre minimum the buyers will not look [YrpzmGX0-YM @ 00:00:47]. LandGate's team lists buildable acreage after topography, elevation and risk indices, then off-take capacity, which they define as how much power you can actually pull from a substation, then regional versus long-haul fiber, water proximity and a redundancy source such as a gas or coal plant, because downtime is so expensive [0_owMAznas0 @ 00:01:15]. Very large parcels can get 'wiggle room' on a missing criterion, LandGate adds. Mike Embry, the horizontal entitlement developer on Advanta IRA, boils it to power, fiber, water and sewer, and prices the penalty for being far from them: $20M per mile to run power off a line and $2M per mile for fiber [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:08:27]. Brandt Burnett at CBRE notes the scale jump behind all of this: hyperscalers now take 200MW on 100 to 300 acres, versus 20-acre, 20MW sites a decade ago, and crypto buyers want a path to 500MW at roughly 3-cent power [9IIIRj5ao20 @ 00:18:36].

Control comes before ownership in every deal here. US Energy Weekly's site-control explainer states that developers almost never buy outright: they sign two-to-three-year options, run environmental, permitting, interconnection and title work, then convert to a lease, and one missing parcel breaks the whole block [JwCWz91cMQc @ 00:00:40]. Farming continues on optioned ground meanwhile. Maclarens Lawyers frames the option the same way from the legal side: a right, not an obligation, with a stipulated exercise price and period, that lets a developer secure land through research and approvals without locking in the purchase, and that simply lapses if not exercised in time [q09nIjF9s5c @ 00:01:49]. The extreme version sits in the Stargate Abilene filings, where Systems Leverage found a $1 land option exercisable from May 31, 2039, sitting behind a ground lease of at least $200,000 a month and a $35M amendment tied to keeping power available [nx4eX4emlwE @ 00:00:21]. The other half of the consensus is that utilities now demand commitment back: Thor's panel reports utilities mandating long-term contracts and minimum usage, deposits used to filter out speculators, and Texas load requests tripling in 12 months, against a backdrop where the city of Calgary's entire load is about 1.5GW [yg9V2befNR0 @ 00:35:37].

## 2. Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The utility conversation is step one, and it costs money. Cruz's script is simple: call the utility, ask whether it can support 100MW or more, and expect to be routed to the team that handles 50MW-plus loads; then confirm a permanent natural gas supply for the 80 to 100 generators an AI site runs. Cruz also notes Nvidia systems carry their own batteries [YrpzmGX0-YM @ 00:02:30]. Embry's sequence on Advanta IRA is more expensive: find a line such as a 230, submit a concept plan, then pay a non-refundable preliminary study of $30k to $100k that exists 'to cover the overhead for their engineering department,' with total spend of $60k to $140k just to learn whether the deal works, and the answer usually comes back with required grid upgrades [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:15:17]. datacenterHawk confirms utilities now charge for studies, that CyrusOne's roughly 30MW will-serve letter with no expiry would 'no way' be granted today, and that one utility saw a single 400MW requirement shopped by four or five developers land as 2GW of requests [dTHn2ejpXvM @ 00:15:10]. That phantom demand is why Thor's panel reports deposits being used to filter out speculators, with Texas load requests tripling in 12 months [yg9V2befNR0 @ 00:35:37].

Entitlement is where the land developer earns the spread. Embry runs geotech, Phase I, environmental and sound studies, rezones at roughly $500k out of pocket, and sells near permitting; on one deal $22M had to be posted upfront to secure power after zoning cleared [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:47:42]. Power arrives in phases, for example 2027 then 2032, and the exit pays about three times a residential exit while closing faster, yet Embry always keeps a Plan B such as multifamily or a Kroger commercial deal in the drawer. Embry uses two standard footprints. Foley & Lardner lists the deal structures now in play: power-conditioned options, phased closings and joint development [wOKA9jWA8fw @ 00:04:24]. Up the value chain, datacenterHawk prices turnkey at roughly $12M to $13M per megawatt against $4M to $8M for powered shell, with turnkey leased per kW per month whether used or not and powered shell per square foot per year, and on a 500MW deal might split 200 turnkey and 300 powered shell [pt4hMec2LbM @ 00:13:43]. CBRE's Burnett stacks the per-square-foot cost at $250 for core and shell, about $1,000 more for fit-out, $2,000 for IT and $5,000 for software, roughly ten times other asset classes [9IIIRj5ao20 @ 00:10:16].

On the landowner side, the numbers are just as specific. LandBridge's Capobianco signed the first data center lease at about $4,000 per acre for 30 to 40 years and layers a 5% cut of net power-generation margin on top; with $3 gas producing power at about 2 cents per kWh against a 7 to 9 cent sale, a gigawatt could throw off $20M to $30M a year to the landlord [rab11GilV3g @ 00:08:20]. LandBridge bought 46,000 acres at about $5,000 per acre that now trade above $20,000 in the public market. US Energy Weekly's benchmarks for optioned solar land: a signing bonus, an annual option fee that often escalates, at least $1,000 to $2,000 a year, then rent of $250 to $2,000 per acre, higher in Texas, with escalators of 1.5% to 3%, and the standing rule is never to let a developer tie up land for years without paying [JwCWz91cMQc @ 00:02:41]. For screening, LandGate precomputes energy cost, source and interconnection queue for every single US parcel [0_owMAznas0 @ 00:04:06], while CBRE replaced the old orange-flag, spray-paint and manhole-stamp fiber tracing with Fiber Locator, which it acquired. Substations, Burnett adds, now take two years instead of one [9IIIRj5ao20 @ 00:14:21].

1. Screen parcels for megawatts, not acres

2. Fund the utility answer

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is over where the electrons come from. Kenny Schoolcraft of Zego defines powered land as land bundled with grid privilege: a parcel near an existing substation, holding an advanced interconnection queue position, or tied to transmission with headroom. He argues that privilege is a fungible asset worth shorter timelines, lower execution risk and sometimes higher exit valuations [2ORDbZvOirU @ 00:02:25]. David Capobianco of LandBridge plays the opposite hand in the Permian. He warns that very few grids or municipal water systems can supply a data center, so the load needs its own behind-the-meter power, fed by gas that has averaged about minus 60 cents per mcf over nine months [rab11GilV3g @ 00:03:17]. Mike Embry lands in the middle on his entitlement deals, running gas turbines as primary power and keeping the grid as backup [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:09:36]. The Foley & Lardner team flags the cost of leaving the grid: behind-the-meter gas, storage-paired renewables and hybrids each raise permitting and fuel-contract issues [wOKA9jWA8fw @ 00:04:59]. The operator read is that both camps sell speed and firm 24/7 capacity. One monetizes a queue position a buyer can plug straight into. The other monetizes cheap gas and brackish water sitting next to the site, and sidesteps the utility almost entirely.

Practitioners also disagree on how much dirt even qualifies. Franklin Andrew Cruz, shopping his own 130-acre tract that can be assembled to 288 acres, works off a rule of 1 megawatt per acre. He says buyers will not look at anything under a 100-acre minimum [YrpzmGX0-YM @ 00:00:47]. LandGate's team sets a much lower bar. It puts buildable acreage at an average of around 40 acres, scored alongside off-take capacity near a substation, fiber optic lines and environmental impact on the land [2mYrH35TasY @ 00:00:00]. The same LandGate crew concedes that very large parcels may carry wiggle room on criteria they are missing, so sheer size can offset a gap elsewhere in the score [0_owMAznas0 @ 00:07:57]. Brandt Burnett of CBRE describes the target moving up fast. Hyperscalers now take 200MW on 100 to 300 acres, against the 20-acre, 20MW sites that passed a decade ago [9IIIRj5ao20 @ 00:18:36]. For an operator, the gap bites at the pitch stage. A 40-acre parcel can score well inside a parcel index and still never get a call from a hyperscale buyer running Cruz's megawatt-per-acre math with a 100-acre floor. The parcel index tells you proximity; the buyer's acreage floor tells you whether anyone shows up to the table.

Location and fuel mix split the room as well. Thor Equities' team treats clustering as the safe play, building availability zones in threes and steering away from remote training sites, which it labels high-risk [yg9V2befNR0 @ 00:27:30]. Capobianco runs the opposite thesis from the Delaware sub-basin, a region with no large population centers. He argues that it is a lot easier to move bytes than it is to move energy, so builders should go to the place with the cheapest gas, with fiber already running along I-10 and I-20 [rab11GilV3g @ 00:23:59]. On fuel, Mike Embry says flatly that solar will not work as data center power and builds his sites around gas turbines with closed-loop cooling on treated gray water [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:10:26]. Kenny Schoolcraft goes further, saying powered land may have nothing to do with renewables at all, because some owners simply do not care about the renewable piece and the value sits in the interconnection [2ORDbZvOirU @ 00:16:12]. Operators pitching a remote gas site and a clustered metro grid site are therefore selling two different risk profiles. One promises the cheapest molecules and behind-the-meter control; the other promises latency, spillover between sister facilities and a labor market already in place.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most consistent firsthand pain is money spent before a single shovel moves. Mike Embry pays a non-refundable preliminary study fee of $30,000 to $100,000 after submitting a concept plan on a line, explaining that it exists to cover the overhead of the power company's engineering department. He warns you could be spending 60 to 140 thousand dollars just to find out whether the deal works [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:15:17]. After clearing zoning, which he puts at roughly $500,000 out of pocket, one deal required $22 million up front to secure the power [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:47:42]. The datacenterHawk hosts confirm utilities now charge for studies, and say CyrusOne's roughly 30MW will-serve letter with no expiry is the kind of commitment there is no way a utility grants today [dTHn2ejpXvM @ 00:13:48]. Thor Equities' panel adds the back-end squeeze: utilities mandate long-term contracts and minimum usage to recover costs, and water upgrade costs are fully borne by the developer [yg9V2befNR0 @ 00:53:09]. The economics check is simple. Every utility gate converts optionality into cash at risk, and a horizontal developer carries that cash long before any end user signs. Embry's answer is to always hold a fallback use for the dirt if the power answer comes back wrong.

The second trap is believing the demand and grid numbers on the surface. The datacenterHawk hosts describe one utility that saw a single 400MW requirement shopped by four to five developers, which landed on its desk as 2GW of requests. They also estimate that of roughly 6GW of planned power in Dallas, 2 to 3GW will likely not be built by whoever announced it [dTHn2ejpXvM @ 00:15:10]. Embry lost a site outright on redundancy. TVA areas lack it, so his Decatur, Alabama site failed, and his verdict on another location was short: can't put a data center there [3rM3iGzRRUA @ 00:30:40]. Brandt Burnett reports that a substation that used to take a year now takes two and costs about 150% of what it did, while land prices have literally quadrupled since the pandemic [9IIIRj5ao20 @ 00:23:54]. He also shows how textbook screens collapse in practice. Apply a five-mile airport buffer and there are no Dallas sites left, so TIA-942 checklists hold only until another factor trumps them, and his defense is building an auditable roadmap [9IIIRj5ao20 @ 00:33:38]. The pattern across all three is that the queue, the substation schedule and the redundancy picture each look healthier on announcement than they do at diligence.

Paper and people produce the slowest-burning losses. Maclarens Lawyers call option legislation rigid and unforgiving. A residential option cannot be exercised in its first 42 days, and in 2021 a 66ZF certificate attached to the call did not waive cooling-off on the put, so a buyer walked from 13 contracts because no 66W was in place. They say such fights can take up many years of everyone's lives [q09nIjF9s5c @ 00:04:31]. On assembly, US Energy Weekly warns that big projects sign options with dozens of landowners and that one missing parcel breaks the whole block, while telling owners never to let a developer tie up land for years without paying [JwCWz91cMQc @ 00:01:22]. Sellers have caught on. The datacenterHawk hosts saw a 200-acre offer come in six times below market, a sign that landowners are smarter than they were, and part of why buying unpowered land, powering it and flipping it is getting harder [dTHn2ejpXvM @ 00:08:17]. Systems Leverage's dig into Stargate's Abilene site shows the community bill: residents report destroyed roads, and university recruitment fell from 40 to 12 with apartments at $2,600, while the announced financing leaves $5.6 billion unexplained [nx4eX4emlwE @ 00:07:02].
