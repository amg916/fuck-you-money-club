# Chapter 05: Grid Operators, Wholesale Markets and the Cost of Waiting

*The three US grids and nine operators, PJM market basics, ERCOT nodal pricing, LMP, SCED and system lambda, ERCOT's energy-only market, MW vs. MWh, BESS revenue streams in markets, 5-year queue waits, and the $598M upgrade bill*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Grid-side operators converge on one brutal fact: the plug, not the panel or the chip, sets the clock. Berkeley Lab figures cited by THE PHYSICAL LAYER, The Pharos Project and Wattlore put the median 2025 build at 61 months from request to commercial operation, with only 13% of 2000-2020 requested capacity ever switching on. US Energy Weekly walks ERCOT's GIS Report, GINR tracking and a roughly 19-month on-paper study path, while Keentel, Modo Energy and US Energy Weekly break down nodal LMP, System Lambda, ORDC adders and the $5,000/MWh cap that set revenue in an energy-only market. PJM and CME Group frame the capacity-market alternative. Speakers split on queue counts, node counts, cost allocation and whether Order 2023 has sped anything up, and Wattlore's $598 million upgrade bill shows what waiting can cost.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every speaker starts from the same map: there is no single American grid. Infrastructure explained counts three interconnections and nine operators that do not coordinate in real time [Cym8njcfsfQ @ 00:00:00]. Avangrid Renewables calls that balkanization an obstacle for less experienced developers, because each project from California to New England needs its own rules assessment, and it splits the FERC-regulated world into organized ISO/RTO markets, which it rates as more attractive to renewable developers, and regulated bilateral markets where vertically integrated utilities control transmission and pick dispatch [T_Y5rMNtBVs @ 00:00:43]. Modo Energy pins down why Texas sits outside that split: ERCOT has no synchronous AC ties to the Eastern and Western interconnections, so FERC has no jurisdiction and the Public Utility Commission of Texas holds final say on ERCOT rules and protocols [ijPF1Cwm0wA @ 00:01:50]. Infrastructure explained fills in the last zone, the non-RTO Southeast, where Southern Company and Duke Energy own generation, transmission and distribution, skip open-market competition and get rates approved by state regulators, trading price competition and faster integration of new sources for stability and long-term planning [Cym8njcfsfQ @ 00:07:29]. For anyone optioning powered land, the operator footprint is the rulebook: it decides who studies a connection, who pays for upgrades, and whether capacity payments exist at all.

The queue verdict is unanimous and ugly. THE PHYSICAL LAYER, quoting Berkeley Lab's Queued Up 2026 edition, counts about 8,200 projects and roughly 2,061 GW of requested generation and storage at the end of 2025, and puts the median project built in 2025 at 61 months from request to commercial operation, up from 36 months in 2015 and 22 months in 2008 [v5uOMJOzPbk @ 00:04:02]. The Pharos Project adds the earlier milestone: a 45-month median just to reach a signed interconnection agreement, and withdrawal rates above 40% even after signing [Br7K7fGD4uk @ 00:01:07]. Wattlore frames the scale against what already runs, roughly 2,060 GW waiting versus about 1,370 GW of operating plants in the United States, so the line is one and a half times bigger than everything built [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:01:28]. On survival odds the three agree: of capacity requested from 2000 to 2020, only 13% had switched on by end-2025 and 75% had walked. US Energy Weekly lands the same point for Texas, warning that nearly 2,000 ERCOT projects totaling 432 GW is a headline, not supply, and that fewer than one in five queued projects nationally reaches commercial operation [DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:03:34]. THE PHYSICAL LAYER's rule is blunt: never add up queue capacity and call it future supply.

On market plumbing, the speakers agree on the menu even where they disagree on the recipe. CME Group says each ISO/RTO typically runs four markets, energy, capacity, financial transmission rights and ancillary services, with the energy market split into a day-ahead auction and a real-time market cleared in five-minute to half-hour increments and priced by LMP at each node [dYvEG3uQzsQ @ 00:01:31]. PJM's own channel describes its version: a daily least-cost pick of resources using weather forecasts, generator availability and transmission outages, a capacity market that secures power three years forward, and regulation plus synchronized and non-synchronized reserves for short-notice shocks [ulpPgF6UT1E @ 00:00:23]. Keentel Engineering confirms ERCOT runs the same day-ahead and real-time pair, with four ancillary services holding frequency at 60 hertz, but no capacity market, so generators are paid only for power produced [AgfRaZtdbh8 @ 00:00:31]. The demand pressure behind all of it is not disputed. THE PHYSICAL LAYER cites a Department of Energy report by Berkeley Lab showing US data centers used 176 TWh in 2023, about 4.4% of national electricity and up from 58 TWh in 2014, with 325 to 580 TWh modeled for 2028, and warns that when demand outruns new supply, existing capacity becomes more valuable [v5uOMJOzPbk @ 00:06:17].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Start the queue work in the files. US Energy Weekly's workflow for Texas: every application to ERCOT gets a GINR, the Generation Interconnection Request number, which tracks capacity, fuel and technology, point of interconnection and stage for the life of the project [DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:00:39]. ERCOT publishes the GIS Report, Generator Interconnection Status, monthly as a spreadsheet; each row is one project, and status reads Active, Inactive (usually missed information deadlines) or Withdrawn. The same files sit in ERCOT's Market Information System report library, and the System Planning Update in board materials carries total queue size and fuel mix; always quote the date with any number [DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:01:03]. The stage clock on paper: ERCOT's own Screening Study targets about three months, an SSR stability review follows if needed, the transmission service provider's Full Interconnection Study targets about ten months, the Interconnection Agreement about six, and ERCOT compliance review about three, roughly 19 months of studies and agreements before construction, and usually more in practice [DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:02:31]. THE PHYSICAL LAYER gives the three questions to ask of any queue figure: how much has a signed agreement, how much has actually been built, and how long the survivors took [v5uOMJOzPbk @ 00:07:44].

The revenue math in ERCOT runs on nodal LMP. Modo Energy counts around 17,000 nodes, of which just under 900 carry a unique LMP as Settlement Points, mostly single resource nodes such as a battery or wind farm, plus aggregated load zones and trading hubs, all repriced every five minutes [xZ1P-4F8Jf8 @ 00:00:23]. The first price component is System Lambda, the cost of the next cheapest unit of energy across the whole system; when solar drops off at sunset and gas gets turned up, Lambda climbs. Keentel Engineering lays out the full stack: System Lambda plus a congestion component plus a reserve price adder, with SCED dispatching every five minutes and settlements every 15 minutes on the average of three five-minute prices [AgfRaZtdbh8 @ 00:01:54]. US Energy Weekly gives the ORDC numbers that fire the adder: a $10 minimum adder below 7,000 MW of reserves, $20 below 6,500 MW, and a climb to the Value of Lost Load, capped at $5,000 per MWh, when reserves hit the roughly 3,000 MW minimum contingency level [sxulqIJRZrM @ 00:02:58]. Meanwhile, a 2019 study found simple-cycle gas peakers earned about 90% of real-time market value in just 1% of hours, and every generator and retailer must trade through a collateral-posting QSE [sxulqIJRZrM @ 00:01:01].

The cost-of-waiting mechanics are now written into federal rules. Under the old first-come, first-served line, Wattlore's example project posted a $10,000 deposit it got back on exit, and FERC found no meaningful money at stake, so developers filed several requests at several grid spots to shop for the lowest upgrade bill and went ahead with that one [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:05:28]. Order 2023, issued July 28, 2023, swaps that for first-ready, first-served cluster studies: The Pharos Project lists 90% site control at application, deposits rising toward 20% of estimated network upgrade costs, and a withdrawal penalty reaching 20% of upgrade cost once the agreement is signed [Br7K7fGD4uk @ 00:05:48]. Wattlore adds the operator side: a 150-day cluster study deadline, $1,000 per working day late, and up to $2,500 per day on the last study, the one that works out what gets built [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:07:17]. Pharos maps who eats upgrades: MISO, PJM, ISO-NE, NYISO and SPP make the generator pay, CAISO credits, and Texas connect-and-manage charges generators nothing for network upgrades. For storage sizing, Modo Energy's unit rule holds: a 10 MW, 20 MWh battery imports or exports at up to 10 MW and fills from empty in two hours, a two-hour duration asset [OIAK1RATT9M @ 00:02:06].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Pull the queue file
   - Download ERCOT's monthly GIS Report and log each project's GINR, point of interconnection, capacity, in-service date and status.
   - Search GIS Report on ercot.com or the Market Information System report library, then cross-check totals and fuel mix against the System Planning Update in ERCOT board materials [US Energy Weekly — DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:03:53].
2. Discount the headline
   - Price attrition into the projects ahead of you at your point of interconnection.
   - ERCOT studies projects one at a time, a withdrawal ahead of you can trigger restudies, and the security deposit is set only after the study lands; post it or you are out of line [US Energy Weekly — DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:03:12].
3. Split the upgrade exposure
   - Separate your own interconnection facilities from network upgrade costs in every study result.
   - Recent projects that cleared every study averaged about $84/kW in connection costs versus about $600/kW for those that withdrew, with wider-grid upgrades at 94% of the withdrawn projects' cost [Wattlore — CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:04:31].
4. Map the nodal revenue
   - Price the target node as System Lambda plus congestion plus reserve adder, and budget for curtailment under connect-and-manage.
   - LMPs recalculate every 5 minutes at roughly 900 points and can hit the $5,000/MWh cap when reserves drop below 3 GW [Keentel Engineering — AgfRaZtdbh8 @ 00:01:22]; connection may be fast, but output gets curtailed when the wires are full [US Energy Weekly — DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:04:55].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is over how a megawatt gets paid while it waits for scarcity. pjminterconnection pitches its capacity market as the long-term backstop, securing enough power to meet consumer needs three years in the future and paying incentives that stimulate investment in existing generation and new capacity [ulpPgF6UT1E @ 00:00:23]. CME Group states the mechanism bluntly: capacity revenues are paid to providers regardless of whether energy is produced or not [dYvEG3uQzsQ @ 00:02:55]. Texas rejects the whole model. US Energy Weekly says PJM, MISO, New York, and New England all pay generators twice, once for energy and once for standing by, while ERCOT pays only for production, so plants sell cheap most of the year and must earn the bulk of annual revenue in a handful of high-demand hours; simple-cycle gas peakers in 2019 earned about 90 percent of their real-time market value in just 1 percent of hours [sxulqIJRZrM @ 00:01:01]. Keentel Engineering calls the energy-only nodal design elegant in theory, with prices reflecting real-time scarcity and investment following value, but flags built-in volatility, since tight supply can send prices to $5,000 per megawatt-hour almost instantly [AgfRaZtdbh8 @ 00:03:15]. The operator choice is between availability money contracted years ahead and a revenue stack that pays mainly when reserves run short.

The second fault line is who eats the upgrade bill, and the regions answer differently. The Pharos Project lays out the map: in MISO, PJM, ISO New England, the New York ISO, and the Southwest Power Pool the generator pays for network upgrades, CAISO uses a crediting approach, and Texas, under connect and manage, does not charge generators for network upgrades at all [Br7K7fGD4uk @ 00:03:25]. US Energy Weekly flags the catch in the Texas playbook: ERCOT leans toward letting projects connect and managing congestion operationally, so connection may be fast, but output can be curtailed when the wires are full, and that hits revenue directly [DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:04:55]. ERCOT also studies projects one at a time in application order rather than in clusters, which US Energy Weekly calls relatively fast, while warning that a withdrawal ahead of you can shift results and trigger restudies for everyone behind [DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:02:52]. Wattlore lands on the same scoreboard from the other side, noting that Texas and the regions without a big grid operator tend to connect plants sooner, while PJM handed one New Jersey solar farm a bill dominated by network upgrades [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:08:48]. The operator trade is plain: pay network upgrades up front in generator-pays regions, or connect faster in ERCOT and carry curtailment and congestion exposure at the node.

Practitioners also split on whether queue reform is working. Wattlore describes FERC Order 2023 as replacing first come, first served with first ready, first served, studying projects in clusters where nobody is ahead of anybody and splitting each upgrade by how much each plant adds to the need, then layering on land-control proof, deposits that grow as you advance, and a 20 percent penalty for walking after signing [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:06:43]. THE PHYSICAL LAYER says the method should help over time but cannot instantly clear a decade-old backlog, manufacture transformers, build lines, or decide who pays for upgrades, and that Berkeley Lab calls it too early to measure full impact [v5uOMJOzPbk @ 00:05:43]. The alternative playbook is the fast lane. The Pharos Project reports PJM picked 51 of 94 applications for a one-time track for shovel-ready resources, about 11.8 gigawatts of selected capacity, roughly 69 percent natural gas, with 90 percent expected running by 2030, and asks the obvious follow-up: who gets in [Br7K7fGD4uk @ 00:07:20]. On grid-enhancing shortcuts, The Pharos Project quotes Commissioner Christie warning that some technologies work somewhere but not everywhere, and if one fails to fix a reliability problem, a later network upgrade, potentially more costly, will be required [Br7K7fGD4uk @ 00:10:31].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The headline trap is the upgrade bill that dwarfs the project. Wattlore walks through a 51-megawatt Cumberland County, New Jersey solar farm that a PJM study in August 2021 said would owe $598 million to plug in, of which only $6.4 million was its own connection and about $592 million was network upgrades [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:00:00]. Split over the farm's size, that is more than $11,000 per kilowatt, against about $84 per kilowatt for recent projects that cleared every study and about $600 for those that quit, where wider-grid upgrades made up 94 percent of the cost and were likely the key reason they left [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:04:31]. The Pharos Project confirms the pattern nationally: Berkeley Lab ties limited available transmission capacity to high interconnection upgrade costs, which contribute to high withdrawals and may deter developers from submitting new requests [Br7K7fGD4uk @ 00:04:03]. The economics check is to divide any study estimate by your kilowatts before posting security, because US Energy Weekly notes ERCOT studies first, then sets the deposit, and if you cannot post it you are out of the line [DzkVggOvJGs @ 00:03:12]. Wattlore adds that the New Jersey study warned in plain words that its cost could still change.

The second failure mode is the domino exit. Wattlore explains that under the old order-based rules the cost of an upgrade starts with the first project in line that makes it needed, so if a project ahead of you leaves, its share of the bill can land on you, a restudy can push costs way up and dates back, and the bigger bill can make the next project quit [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:05:47]. Signing does not end the exposure: The Pharos Project cites Berkeley Lab that withdrawal rates exceed 40 percent even after a developer signs an interconnection agreement, and that only about 13 percent of capacity requested from 2000 to 2020 had come online by the end of 2025 [Br7K7fGD4uk @ 00:01:51]. THE PHYSICAL LAYER counts about 549 gigawatts holding draft or signed agreements without reaching commercial operation, including 256 gigawatts of solar and 161 of storage [v5uOMJOzPbk @ 00:02:52]. Wattlore reports that PJM itself has 51 gigawatts of plants with signed deals, many not being built at all or slowed by state permits and supply chain backlogs [CDd-hsI3tKQ @ 00:08:31]. The underwriting rule that follows: treat both the projects ahead of you and your own signed deal as live withdrawal risk, not a settled position.

Price and hardware traps finish the list. US Energy Weekly reports that during Winter Storm Uri regulators directed ERCOT to hold prices at $9,000, so retail providers bought power at $9,000 per megawatt-hour and sold it at around $90, and several went bankrupt; the system-wide offer cap was then cut to $5,000 effective January 2022 [sxulqIJRZrM @ 00:01:51]. Infrastructure explained warns that high-voltage transformers, the equipment that steps power up for long-distance transmission, take 12 to 18 months to manufacture and cannot be quickly replaced, a lead time that sits underneath every substation upgrade a study can demand [Cym8njcfsfQ @ 00:08:46]. For storage sites, learnBESS says a single revenue stream rarely justifies the investment, so arbitrage, balancing, and capacity have to be stacked and executed by a route-to-market provider, and identical hardware earns different revenue in different countries because pre-qualification, imbalance pricing, and network tariffs are set nationally [m0yVO4ZOmdc @ 00:02:01]. Modo Energy supplies the unit check every bid depends on: a 10 megawatt, 20 megawatt-hour battery has 10 megawatts of rated power, the highest rate it can import or export, and 20 megawatt-hours of storable energy, which makes it a 2-hour asset [OIAK1RATT9M @ 00:02:06].
