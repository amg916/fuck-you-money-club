## 1. Kill Imaginary Megawatts Before They Consume Option Capital  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is buying a power narrative instead of a documented service pathway. A parcel beside a 230 kV line has geographic proximity, not reserved capacity, a permissible tap, transformer availability, or an energization commitment. Screen 69kV–345kV corridors in QGIS, then attach the utility's dated response, applicable study scope, requested load shape, service voltage, and outstanding conditions to each parcel record. Label public hosting maps and ISO queue entries as screening evidence; neither establishes deliverable load. Generation interconnection procedures under 18 CFR 35.28 do not automatically establish a data center's retail service rights. Separate the utility's documented quantity from the buyer's requested quantity and from any quantity merely assumed in marketing. If a buyer discusses $4,000 per MW for a requested 100 MW campus, the tempting fee is $400,000. If utility documentation supports only a conditional 20 MW first phase, the same illustrative rate supports at most $80,000 of conditional milestone consideration, subject to the actual agreement. Unresolved upgrades or dates can reduce present assignable value further. Never sell the unsupported 80 MW as inventory; obtain additional evidence or reprice the opportunity around the smaller phase before spending another extension dollar.

The second and third traps are treating storage as ordinary load and interpreting a queue position as protection from restudies. A standalone BESS option on 5–25 acres needs separate import and export assumptions, charging restrictions, discharge duration, and interconnection cost allocation. A 50 MW, 200 MWh proposal describes four hours of nominal discharge, not 50 MW of continuously available campus supply. Ask whether charging requires a separate service arrangement and whether export limitations alter dispatch economics. Obtain the current study agreement and distinguish deposits, withdrawal exposure, security postings, and potentially refundable balances. Suppose a buyer's underwriting initially assigns $3 million of upgrades to a project, then a restudy identifies $11 million. The $8 million increase can erase the buyer's development spread without creating any additional fee for the originator. Maintain a change register keyed to study revisions, affected facilities, and cost responsibility. As an illustrative house threshold, pause discretionary parcel spending when estimated upgrades rise more than 20% or the utility withdraws the prior service schedule. Resume only after the buyer updates its economics and accepts the revised exposure in writing. If that acceptance fails, use the option's negotiated exit rather than funding a queue obligation outside the origination mandate.

The fourth trap is underwriting a campus as if it were a larger battery parcel. AI facilities can require diverse fiber entrances, carrier route verification, cooling-water capacity or a viable alternative cooling design, acoustic treatment, backup generation permissions, and staged load acceptance. Two carrier brands sharing one conduit are not physical diversity. A nearby water main is not an allocation letter. Use carrier route evidence and utility correspondence rather than broker maps to establish each critical dependency. Align a 12–24 month diligence-contingent option with realistic service milestones; a nominal 18-month option does not solve a four-year energization schedule unless extension rights and the eventual buyer's holding economics support that mismatch. Track option days remaining, termination notice deadlines, next study delivery, and estimated service date as separate fields. Flag fewer than 120 days to expiry when material utility or zoning questions remain; this is a house intervention threshold, not a statutory period. A $3,000 monthly extension for six months costs $18,000 before surveys, consultants, or legal work. Require a named buyer sponsor and a documented next evidence event before authorizing that spend. Otherwise terminate on time, preserve reusable diligence, and stop describing the parcel as powered land in outbound materials.

## 2. Expose Title Defects, Licensing Risk, and Municipal Stop Orders  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The fifth trap is assuming every signed option creates transferable inventory. Read assignment, change-of-control, access, exclusivity, extension, and termination provisions together. A seller's discretionary consent can make a nominally assignable interest commercially unusable; a nonrefundable extension payable before the next study arrives can create forced speculation. Obtain the owner's authority documents, title commitment, legal description, and negotiated memorandum recording rights where appropriate. Recording is not a cure for defective ownership or an unenforceable agreement. Have local counsel and the title company reconcile lien releases, existing leases, access easements, restrictive covenants, and mineral interests. A mineral waiver must cover the relevant parties and surface impacts; a seller cannot waive rights they do not own. Model a simple loss ledger: three rejected options at $5,000 each plus three $2,000 diligence packages consume $21,000 before a successful assignment. That leakage belongs in the origination portfolio, not in an abandoned spreadsheet. If assignability depends on consent, obtain the consent before promising an unconditional transfer. If the seller refuses workable language, stop paying for portable studies whose commercial benefit may accrue entirely to the seller or a competing buyer after your option expires.

The sixth trap is confusing principal dealing with brokerage immunity. Operate through a genuine owned option interest or use an appropriately licensed state broker when the activity requires one. The legal analysis follows the actual conduct, compensation, disclosures, and jurisdiction, not a spreadsheet label saying assignment fee. Texas Occupations Code Sections 1101.002 and 1101.004 illustrate why equitable-interest transactions require fact-specific review and appropriate written disclosure rather than an assumed nationwide exception. Marketing another owner's parcel for a contingent fee without the necessary authority can create licensing exposure and jeopardize collection. The seventh trap is accepting a zoning map as an operational permit. Obtain the ordinance text, current amendments, hearing calendar, use classification, and written planning response addressing the actual battery or campus design. Track moratorium proposals alongside enacted restrictions, and distinguish existing entitlements from hoped-for approvals. A parcel zoned industrial may still require special treatment for battery storage, diesel generation, height, setbacks, or noise. Stop new nonrecoverable spending when a proposed local restriction would defeat the specific use unless counsel identifies a credible path and the buyer funds that risk. Do not quietly substitute a different use in the same marketing package.

The eighth and ninth traps are trusting desktop environmental screens and dismissing emergency-access geometry. FEMA flood maps, USFWS wetland mapping, and aerial imagery identify questions; they do not establish buildable acreage or permit eligibility. Under Clean Water Act Section 404, 33 U.S.C. 1344, discharges of dredged or fill material into jurisdictional waters can require authorization. Have qualified specialists evaluate jurisdiction and project impacts rather than treating every mapped wet area as either automatically prohibited or automatically harmless. Historic industrial use also warrants environmental assessment: CERCLA liability under 42 U.S.C. 9607 and the all appropriate inquiries framework in 40 CFR Part 312 matter when structuring acquisition and evaluating available defenses. An option does not eliminate every liability created by site activities or later ownership. For BESS, verify locally adopted fire requirements, apparatus access, separation distances, drainage, and emergency response expectations with the authority having jurisdiction. NFPA 855 and UL 9540A can inform that discussion but are not universal parcel approvals. If a ten-acre site loses four acres to constraints, recalculate the equipment layout and economic capacity on six usable acres. Terminate when the revised design cannot meet the buyer's minimum capacity within its accepted cost envelope.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Pre-Flight Checks Before a Nonrecoverable Payment**
> 
> These checks are internal spending controls. Each requires a dated artifact, an accountable reviewer, and a recorded decision.
> - Verify that the option binds the necessary owners and that assignment, access, extension, notice, and refund provisions match the planned transaction.
> - Verify that utility evidence supports the marketed quantity and conditions, with import and export treatment separated for storage projects.
> - Verify that current zoning, proposed restrictions, environmental constraints, and fire-access geometry leave a commercially usable project footprint.
> - Verify that licensing analysis, required interest disclosures, buyer funding evidence, and the maximum authorized loss are documented before payment.

## 3. Reconcile Clawbacks, Burn, and Alternative-Use Economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The tenth trap is counting an assignment headline as collected, unrestricted profit. Build a waterfall separating signed consideration, contingent milestones, cash received, escrow holdbacks, recognized revenue, direct costs, and remaining recourse. A $150,000 agreement with $90,000 payable at transfer and $60,000 payable after a service milestone is not $150,000 of immediately spendable cash. If $20,000 of the first payment remains in escrow, available cash is $70,000 before costs. Determine revenue recognition under the applicable contract and ASC 606 performance obligations and variable consideration constraints; cash timing alone does not settle recognition. A buyer may demand repayment for title defects, inaccurate study representations, missed transfer conditions, or expressly negotiated post-closing failures. Distinguish those clauses from a buyer's ordinary development risk. Negotiate clear triggers, claim periods, liability allocation, and limits appropriate to the transaction. Never describe a broad refund covenant as harmless boilerplate. As an illustrative circuit breaker, reserve the full reasonably exposed amount of a disputed payment and suspend owner distributions while the claim remains unresolved. A large bank balance can coexist with negative free cash when refund obligations, unpaid consultants, and near-term extension commitments sit outside the headline deal tracker.

Keep the canonical scenarios unchanged while testing failure sensitivity outside them. Conservative revenue of $110,000 less $77,000 of aggregate costs leaves $33,000, a 30% margin with 1 FT + 3 C. Base revenue of $505,000 less $292,900 leaves $212,100, a 42.0% margin with 1 FT + 3 C. Scaled revenue of $900,000 less $414,000 leaves $486,000, a 54% margin with 2 FT + 4 C. These revenues represent realized option assignment fees, origination milestones, and development carry, excluding underlying land purchase price, construction capex, and buyer project value. Where the operator acts as agent, assess principal-versus-agent presentation and recognize the retained operator consideration rather than routing third-party receipts into revenue. No separate retained-share factor is supplied here, so do not invent one. Explicitly allocate expired option fees within the aggregate cost budget: the illustrative $21,000 failed-site cohort must fit inside those costs unless identified as incremental stress. If incremental to Base, unchanged revenue and $313,900 costs produce $191,100 profit, approximately 37.84% margin; that is a stress test, not a replacement scorecard. Maintain a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast because the $10,000–$50,000 startup range and $1,800–$11,000 monthly opex range leave little room for uncontrolled extension chains.

The eleventh trap is treating alternative uses as free downside insurance. Apply dot_digital_billboard_and_ios_master_lease_flip only through separate, independently viable underwriting. Digital billboard options require Highway Beautification Act review under 23 U.S.C. 131, applicable state DOT spacing and digital operation rules, and local zoning permission. A battery setback does not establish a billboard entitlement. For a separate three- to ten-acre truck or equipment drop-yard IOS site, obtain explicit permitted use, assignment, and subletting rights. Illustratively, $8,000 monthly ground rent and $16,000–$22,400 monthly NNN sublease receipts produce the targeted 2.0x–2.8x ratio only if local demand supports occupancy and pricing. At the low end, annual receipts of $192,000 less $96,000 ground rent and $24,000 retained costs leave $72,000 before transaction-specific capital requirements. One vacant quarter removes $48,000 of receipts if no replacement tenant pays. A 16x–22x headline applied to $192,000 suggests $3.072–$4.224 million, but that is a gross-rent arithmetic illustration, not a supported sale price. An infrastructure REIT or other qualified buyer must evaluate actual net cash flow, transferable rights, remaining lease term, and renewal exposure. Exercise negotiated termination rights before conditions expire; avoid unfunded construction commitments and pursue only consented alternatives.

## 4. Instrument the Pipeline and Stop Trust-Destroying Shortcuts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The twelfth trap is manufacturing confidence through unauthorized data access or altered evidence. Shared utility portal credentials, scraped private developer rooms, fabricated study letters, misleading ownership claims, and paid permit promises can cause account termination, contract rescission, civil claims, or criminal exposure depending on the conduct. Never bypass access restrictions or present another applicant's study as transferable capacity. Public information can still carry reuse limits, and a buyer's confidentiality agreement may restrict redistribution of route maps or commercial terms. Use authorized utility channels, permitted bulk downloads, and a controlled data room with named recipients. Record source, acquisition date, usage restrictions, document version, and the person who validated each material assertion. If a portal denies access, stop automated requests and resolve access through its published support process rather than rotating accounts. If evidence is challenged, freeze the affected claim, preserve the original artifact, and notify recipients of the correction. Maintain separate internal and buyer-facing fields so a tentative analyst estimate cannot silently become a marketing fact. The recovery path is an accurate corrected package with permission to distribute it, not a replacement identity or a more aggressive outreach sequence designed to outrun the original defect.

Build telemetry around loss mechanisms rather than dashboard decoration. Define utility-validation rate as sites receiving a documented viable next service step divided by sites completing utility screening; do not include untouched leads in the denominator. Define expired-option burn as forfeited option and extension payments divided by collected operator fees over the same trailing period, while retaining cohort views for long-cycle deals. If collected fees are zero, display the dollar burn and liquidity runway instead of manufacturing a percentage. Illustrative house thresholds include expired-option burn above 15%, a service-date slip exceeding six months, upgrade exposure beyond the buyer's written ceiling, or any missed contractual notice deadline. Each threshold must name an action: freeze new options, reprice milestones, obtain funded extensions, or terminate. Track assignment-consent completion, refund exposure by buyer, and concentration in one utility territory. A cohort of ten screened sites with two documented viable paths has a 20% validation rate; that informs sourcing quality but does not establish a universal benchmark. Review adverse changes weekly and source-system changes daily where authorized feeds support that cadence. Count stale documents as exceptions requiring review, not automatically as proof that capacity has disappeared.

Run a weekly exception meeting with the owner, transaction counsel, utility specialist, and relevant contractor; the small team does not justify blurred decision rights. Every active option needs a maximum additional loss, a next evidence event, an accountable person, and a termination date that includes delivery requirements. Reconcile option notices against executed documents rather than relying solely on a calendar import. Consider a twelve-month option with $6,000 already spent, $4,000 of pending studies, and a $3,000 extension due next week. The immediate decision concerns $7,000 of incremental exposure, not defending the sunk $6,000. If the next study cannot answer the buyer's decisive service question, declining that spend may preserve more value than another month of nominal control. For disputed assignments, reconcile the factual record with counsel before accepting or rejecting a refund demand, and preserve all relevant communications. After termination, confirm notice receipt, cancel revocable vendor orders, remove stale marketing, and document continuing confidentiality or indemnity obligations. Retain only independently viable permitted operations and buyer-consented alternative uses. The operating objective is not maximum acreage controlled; it is collectible, defensible operator consideration per dollar and hour exposed across the entire origination portfolio.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Reconcile the Evidence Ledger
   - Compare each marketed assertion with its current source artifact.
   - Record utility quantities, conditions, study revisions, permit status, interest ownership, and authorized distribution rights; flag every unsupported assertion for immediate correction.
2. Calculate the Next-Dollar Exposure
   - Separate sunk costs from avoidable payments and binding obligations.
   - Update the thirteen-week cash forecast with extensions, study invoices, potential refunds, escrow restrictions, and expired-option reserves before considering another site.
3. Assign the Circuit-Breaker Decision
   - Choose a documented continuation, repricing, funded extension, or exit.
   - Name the responsible decision maker and the evidence required to reopen spending; record buyer acceptance of any revised quantity, cost, schedule, or transfer condition.
4. Close the Operational Loop
   - Deliver notices, update recipients, and verify the resulting obligations.
   - Retain delivery receipts, cancel revocable orders, remove obsolete marketing, reconcile remaining liabilities, and feed the loss reason back into parcel screening.
