## 1. Define the business by inventory rights, collection timing, and retained economics  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Buying-group, card-float, and ticket-allocation arbitrage combines three distinct economic engines: a contracted merchandise spread, an eligible payment reward, and a transferable inventory or brokerage spread. The operator first obtains a genuine buying-group commitment identifying product, quantity, reimbursement, warehouse, inspection rules, and settlement trigger. They purchase through an authorized retailer, preserve serial-number evidence, deliver against that commitment, and reconcile the resulting receivable. Separately, a ticket operator acquires or represents valid inventory and sells it through permitted exchange or corporate channels. Float is the interval between cash outflow becoming payable and settlement arriving; it is working-capital timing, not revenue. Candidate #41 earns its 93/100 score from revenue potential of 23, margin potential of 24, team efficiency of 20, startup accessibility of 13, and automation potential of 13. Those components describe a small-team operating hypothesis, not proof that a particular counterparty pays reliably. The proposed startup capital is $10,000–$55,000, with first transactions expected within one to three months. Capital must cover outstanding purchases, shipping, refunds, and a failed settlement cycle without assuming another card remains available. A warehouse address in Delaware, New Hampshire, or Oregon establishes a delivery location; it does not independently establish tax treatment, legal title, acceptance, or credit quality. Underwrite those facts separately before treating a purchase order as collectible.

The revenue boundary matters more than the headline turnover. The canonical gross activity basis combines wholesale buying-group reimbursements, card-rebate yields, and secondary ticket proceeds; Formula v2.0 retains 35% after landed merchandise and face-value ticket basis. That retained share is a planning convention for comparing operator economics, not a universal ASC 606 election. Under ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis, an operator controlling the specified goods or ticket rights before transfer may recognize gross customer consideration and record inventory cost separately. An agent arranging another party's transfer generally recognizes its fee or commission, not the client's entire transaction value. Assess control using the actual contract, fulfillment responsibility, inventory exposure, and pricing discretion; collecting the customer's money alone proves little. Eligible card rebates generally require analysis as purchase-related cost reductions rather than an additional customer sale, with classification following their substance and the accounting policy applied. Keep gross cash receipts, accounting revenue, retained contribution, and owner profit in separate ledger fields. A $100,000 corporate ticket order carrying a $7,000 agency commission contributes $7,000 of agency revenue, not $100,000 of revenue plus that commission. Likewise, do not add a rebate twice by reducing inventory cost and also crediting a reward-income line in the same contribution calculation. The scenario table therefore labels its gross basis explicitly.

Five operator archetypes organize the shelf, while eleven mapped plays specify executable offers. The plays are pre-committed buying-group fulfillment, contracted wholesale sourcing, eligible reward administration, authorized working-capital scheduling, owned-ticket resale, consigned-ticket brokerage, corporate-suite brokerage, season-seat-license transfer brokerage, corporate ticket procurement, event-inventory recovery, and conditional cross-border customs coordination. The last play is an adjacent service, not permission to assume historical tariff savings remain available. An SSL means a season-seat license in this chapter; its transferability depends on the venue contract and league or team procedures. Neither ownership of that license nor a suite booking automatically permits unrestricted resale of every associated admission. Durable moats are boring and measurable: written allocations, traceable inventory, dependable settlement, accurate listings, repeat corporate buyers, and a useful record of rejected deals. Fragile versions substitute projected rewards for margin, confuse available credit with liquidity, or buy tickets before checking transfer restrictions. The operator must be able to explain the spread without invoking a future price increase. Use the five-archetype matrix to select one initial execution lane, then add adjacent plays only when their contracts and ledgers fit the existing controls. Named buying groups and software vendors below are diligence targets and workflow examples, not endorsements, verified current offers, or evidence that any particular account is eligible.

## 2. Compare eleven mapped plays across fifteen executable sub-verticals  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The anchor play, wholesale_buying_group_card_float_cycling, is pre-committed fulfillment financed through authorized commercial-card purchases. For an illustrative order, spend $10,000 on merchandise, collect a contracted $10,150 reimbursement, earn $200 of eligible cashback, and incur $60 of insured shipping. Retained contribution is $290 before labor, returns, financing, and other overhead. At an actually eligible 4% reward rate, the same order contributes $490; never substitute a promotional headline for the issuer's exclusions, caps, merchant coding, or redemption terms. BuyForMeRetail and Pointsmaker are named counterparty research leads, with each current offer independently checked. A Net-3 commitment must specify whether the clock starts at delivery, inspection, or acceptance, and whether days are business days. Match that verified schedule to the actual statement closing date and payment due date. Approximately sixty days of float can occur only with suitable products and purchase dates; some commercial products require much faster repayment or charge interest differently. Maintain reserved cash sufficient for the outstanding obligation even if the group delays payment and the issuer freezes the account simultaneously. The fallback is to stop purchases, pay the card from reserves, enforce contractual recovery rights, and resell legitimately owned goods through permitted channels when appropriate. Circular purchases, fictitious sales, and issuer-limit evasion are outside this operating model.

The other ticket and service plays monetize permission, information quality, or execution rather than defeated purchase limits. SkyBox and Automatiq can support inventory synchronization and exchange distribution, but neither converts restricted inventory into transferable property. Obtain current subscription, integration, and per-transaction quotes; avoid booking assumed software savings before confirming included exchanges and settlement behavior. StubHub, SeatGeek, Ticketmaster resale, and other marketplaces can impose account-specific selling economics, delivery obligations, cancellations, and payout holds. Calculate an executable net price from the actual seller agreement instead of assuming a universal commission schedule. For illustration, a $200 sale with a modeled 10% seller charge and $150 acquisition basis leaves $30 before additional operating costs. A separate consignment agency earning 12% on $2,000 of completed sales recognizes a $240 commission before its own expenses. Neither percentage here represents a platform's published universal rate. Corporate buyers can justify a different service fee when the operator coordinates contiguous seating, invoices, and reliable transfer, but those services must actually be delivered. Apply 15 U.S.C. §45c to ticket acquisition and resale exposure: never bypass queues, CAPTCHA, access controls, or posted purchasing restrictions. Use authorized interfaces, enforce quantity limits, and retain delivery acknowledgments so automation improves accuracy rather than amplifying an invalid allocation.

The adjacent play section_321_de_minimis_bonded_tariff_arbitrage requires a corrected premise. Historically, qualifying low-value imports could receive treatment under 19 U.S.C. §1321, and operators considered Canadian bonded warehouses or Tijuana IMMEX facilities for consumer fulfillment. Executive Order 14324 suspended duty-free de minimis treatment globally effective August 29, 2025; do not build a current offer on blanket Section 321 or Entry Type 86 availability. Verify subsequent orders, legislation, CBP implementation, and any applicable exceptions with the filing broker before quoting. Non-301 origin goods from Vietnam, India, the European Union, Bangladesh, or Cambodia are not automatically exempt merely because their origin differs from China. A border warehouse does not change origin, erase ordinary duties, or authorize artificial splitting to evade shipment limits. Historically modeled savings of 15%–42.5%, or apparel rates of 16%–32%, require product-specific classification and cannot be treated as a current universal schedule. For arithmetic only, a verified $10,000 duty reduction multiplied by a 40% service share yields $4,000 before fulfillment expenses; if lawful savings are zero, that contingent fee is zero. The fallback is ordinary dutiable entry, separately evaluated bonded procedures, or documented first-sale valuation under 19 U.S.C. §1401a where its conditions are genuinely met. IMMEX is a Mexican customs program, not a United States duty exemption.

## 4. Engineer settlement, ticket delivery, and statutory requirements into the workflow  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Implement a transaction ledger before connecting purchasing or ticket-distribution automation. Assign one immutable deal identifier to the commitment, purchase invoice, warehouse shipment, receivable, card obligation, reward posting, and exception record. Track commitment expiry, accepted quantity, rejected units, contractual settlement trigger, expected cash availability, and actual payment separately. A buying-group deal-commit and tracking reconciler should flag any order exceeding its approved quantity or missing acceptance evidence. Use PostgreSQL or SQLite for structured records and QuickBooks Online for the accounting workflow when those choices fit the operator's existing stack; preserve raw exports so accounting does not depend on one dashboard. Restrict integrations to documented interfaces and retain the authority supporting each access credential. For high-value shipments, verify that the policy covers the actual commodity, destination, declared value, packing method, and claims deadline. Carrier tracking marked delivered is evidence of arrival, not necessarily contractual acceptance or proof that every serial-numbered unit was received. Diversify among independently vetted Net-3 and Net-5 counterparties, but measure correlated warehouse, ownership, and banking exposures rather than merely counting brand names. When an ACH payment slips, disable new commitments for that exposure, preserve recoverable inventory rights, and fund the card obligation from reserves. Restart only after the cause and collection reliability have been established.

For tickets, design the system around a single authoritative inventory record and explicit delivery states. SkyBox or Automatiq can distribute listings, but the operator still needs tested behavior for exchange outages, delayed sales notices, duplicate purchases, and failed transfers. Record each seat's event, section, row, acquisition basis, permitted listing channels, transfer status, and latest safe delivery time. A pre-event markdown curve should define time-based review points and executable net-price thresholds, not merely an asking-price reduction. For example, reassess a $150-basis seat fourteen days before the event, cut exposure at seven days when net bids fall below $140, and pursue a documented recovery decision before the transfer deadline. Those illustrative thresholds are inventory policy, not a promise of available buyers. Apply 16 CFR Part 464 when presenting live-event ticket prices: include mandatory fees in the total price as required and handle permitted exclusions accurately. California Civil Code §1770(a)(29) adds relevant advertised-price requirements for covered transactions. Check other applicable state resale licensing, refund, and price-disclosure rules before entering their markets. The federal BOTS Act also reaches certain ticket sales connected to prohibited circumvention, so provenance matters when buying from intermediaries. Reject suspicious allocations rather than treating a reseller invoice as proof that original purchasing restrictions were respected.

Merchandise and customs controls belong in the same evidence discipline, with different substantive tests. A resale certificate supports only the treatment permitted by the applicable jurisdiction and transaction facts; obtain a valid certificate, verify its scope, and retain the records required for the relevant sale. California Revenue and Taxation Code §6091 places the burden of proving a sale is not at retail on the seller unless the statutory conditions are met. A delivery warehouse outside a sales-tax state does not extinguish sourcing, downstream collection, registration, or use-tax questions elsewhere. For goods contracts, specify title, inspection, acceptance, rejection, and risk of loss using the governing state's enacted commercial code rather than a generic warehouse promise. For imported goods, require classification, origin, customs value, importer responsibility, and current entry treatment before any savings-based quotation. First-sale valuation requires evidence supporting the relevant sale for export and applicable valuation conditions; it is not a percentage discount selected by the operator. Close each cycle by reconciling bank cash, platform settlements, customer liabilities, inventory basis, rewards, and recognized fees. Keep gross throughput visible for operational capacity planning while judging expansion by collected contribution and stress-tested reserves. The resulting moat is a reliable chain of permissions and evidence that survives a disputed shipment, a canceled event, or a changed import rule.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Document authority and establish the economic baseline
   - Create the deal record before any merchandise purchase or ticket commitment.
   - Attach counterparty identity, inventory rights, contractual fees, actual issuer terms, expected settlement dates, and a contribution calculation excluding unsupported rewards or customs relief.
2. Reserve cash and execute within verified permissions
   - Approve only the quantity supported by documented rights and independent repayment reserves.
   - Record card due dates, shipment insurance, warehouse acceptance criteria, ticket transfer restrictions, and stop conditions; use authorized purchasing and distribution interfaces without circumventing access controls.
3. Reconcile delivery, acceptance, and available settlement
   - Match each external confirmation to the original deal identifier.
   - Separate carrier arrival from warehouse acceptance, ACH initiation from available bank cash, and ticket-transfer initiation from confirmed delivery; investigate differences before releasing further capital.
4. Close the accounting bridge and reassess exposure
   - Reconcile recognized revenue, retained contribution, remaining costs, and owner profit.
   - Apply the documented principal-or-agent policy, record rebate reversals consistently, compare realized mix with the 35% planning assumption, and update counterparty limits and event markdown decisions.
