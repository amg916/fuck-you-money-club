## 1. Days 1–7 — Establish the Pod, Play Register, and Cash Boundary  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start Day 1 with one full-time owner responsible for credit exposure, counterparty acceptance, and the daily cash forecast. Assign contractor A to purchase-order verification, receiving evidence, shipment tracking, and buying-group reconciliation; assign contractor B to ticket rights, exchange listings, transfer evidence, and price-decay monitoring. Budget eight hours weekly per contractor during discovery, with written escalation rules instead of continuous availability. Use this chapter's working register to operationalize the required eleven-play count without claiming additional canonical play names: P1 committed wholesale sourcing; P2 Net-3/Net-5 counterparty routing; P3 insured merchandise fulfillment; P4 deal-commit and reimbursement reconciliation; P5 eligible commercial-card rebates; P6 statement-cycle float; P7 authorized secondary-ticket sourcing; P8 multi-exchange inventory synchronization; P9 pre-event markdown execution; P10 transferable stadium seat-license allocation brokerage; and P11 authorized corporate-suite allocation brokerage. Confirm the instrument and transfer rights before treating any seat license as inventory. All eleven receive a checklist and an accountable person, but only P1–P6 qualify for initial funded testing. P7–P11 begin as documented sourcing and operating rehearsals; an unverified allocation never becomes a purchase merely because a seller advertises scarcity.

Set an illustrative $25,000 starting-cash envelope within the volume's $10,000–$55,000 range. Earmark $12,000 for procurement and matching card payoff obligations, $6,000 for settlement and refund reserves, $4,000 for untouched contingency liquidity, and $3,000 for thirty-day operating burn. The procurement envelope is cash backing for purchases, not an additional spending allowance layered onto card capacity. Limit the first merchandise order to $1,500 and all concurrent acquisition basis to $6,000 until two reimbursements clear. No single buying group may represent more than $3,000 of exposure or 50% of deployed basis, whichever is lower. Hold event-ticket basis below $1,000 per event and $2,000 collectively; these amounts remain inside the same procurement envelope. The $3,000 burn ceiling covers $1,200 of contractor work, $350 of software, $450 of setup and accounting, $500 of incremental operating charges, and $500 of exception capacity. Acquisition basis and transaction costs belong in deal economics, even when a cash forecast displays them beside operating expenditures. Commit no owner draw during this sprint. A business starting with $10,000 must shrink inventory limits rather than replicate the $25,000 deployment plan.

On Days 2–4, assemble a counterparty pack containing the contracting entity, independently verified payment instructions, written acceptance criteria, Net-3 or Net-5 definitions, and the person authorized to resolve discrepancies. Check issuer terms for eligible purchases, reward caps, exclusions, statement dates, grace periods, and review triggers. The advertised 2%–4% rebate band is a sourcing criterion, not an assumed yield; record only the rate that the actual agreement supports. A nominal sixty-day float window is a maximum planning label, not a universal entitlement. Record the transaction date and actual due date for every charge. On Days 5–7, build a shared spreadsheet or SQLite register with immutable deal IDs linking purchase orders, invoices, carrier scans, card charges, exchange orders, and bank receipts. Retain resale-certificate evidence applicable to each merchandise route and ticket-tax treatment applicable to each selling jurisdiction. Federal 16 CFR Part 464 requires covered live-event ticket offers to display total prices including mandatory fees, subject to the rule's permitted exclusions. Assign a jurisdiction-specific review before publishing. Separately enforce 15 U.S.C. § 45c: no queue circumvention, CAPTCHA bypass, or acquisition of tickets with knowledge of prohibited acquisition.

>

## 2. Days 8–14 — Run Small Closed-Loop Tests Without Manufacturing Volume  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Day 8 releases one merchandise order against an accepted purchase order; contractor A captures the SKU, serial number when relevant, quantity, landed cost, reimbursement amount, and acceptance deadline. P1 requires a real commitment, not a group-chat indication of demand. P2 routes the next eligible order to the second vetted group only when minimum shipment economics justify diversification. P3 records insurance exclusions, signature requirements, packaging evidence, and the exact point at which the counterparty accepts delivery risk. Apply the written agreement rather than assuming a carrier scan equals payable acceptance. On Days 9–10, match retailer confirmation to the card charge and carrier acceptance, then schedule follow-up against the contract's business-day clock. For a $1,000 eligible purchase reimbursed at $1,045, a posted $20 rebate and $18 of shipping and insurance produce $47 before remaining operating costs. Until the rebate posts, realized deal contribution is $27, provided the reimbursement has cleared. Neither number supports assuming the portfolio's canonical 35% retained share on every wholesale order. Record realized mixes honestly; a thin buying-group-only portfolio can validate settlement operations while failing to support the volume's aggregate earnings model.

Days 11–12 develop ticket evidence without racing to deploy capital. For P7, obtain a legitimate acquisition trail and confirm that the intended exchange accepts the inventory and seller account. For P8, select SkyBox or Automatiq only after checking current access requirements, supported exchanges, subscription charges, and inventory controls. Do not buy both systems merely to resemble a mature desk. Begin with one exchange and a manual inventory lock if automated synchronization cannot be tested economically. Measure time between a sale notification and removal from every other active listing; a successful sandbox rehearsal does not establish live reliability. P9 assigns reviews at fourteen, seven, three, and one days before an event, with more frequent checks inside seventy-two hours. P10 and P11 remain brokerage-first: obtain seller authority, venue permission where required, recipient eligibility, and written fee terms before making offers. Stadium seat licenses can carry renewal obligations and transfer fees separate from ticket rights. Corporate suites can prohibit splitting or onward distribution. Neither product should enter the card-float ledger until its actual obligations and permitted settlement path are understood and documented.

Days 13–14 close the first operating loop rather than opening ten new positions. Contractor A produces an exception list grouped by missing acceptance, short payment, disputed quantity, and overdue ACH. Contractor B produces a transfer-readiness list grouped by delivery delay, listing conflict, restricted rights, and event-date exposure. The owner reconciles bank receipts to receivables and posted rewards to the card statement, then checks reserves without counting inventory at asking prices. Require cleared reimbursement for the first order before repeating with that counterparty; require two cleared reimbursements before lifting the initial aggregate basis ceiling. Contractual Net-3 and Net-5 clocks start where the agreement says they start, often acceptance rather than dispatch. A payment exceeding the written due date by three business days freezes new orders to that group and creates a documented collection task. One group paying slowly must not be subsidized by fresh exposure to another. Keep weekly contractor utilization below 80% of assigned capacity so exceptions have working room. If evidence collection consumes the whole pod, reduce transaction count before adding tools or accepting a larger allocation.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Tactics That Preserve Spread Under Real Settlement Friction**
> 
> The durable edge is disciplined execution of ordinary transactions, not larger credit lines or optimistic inventory marks.
> - Ask each buying group which receiving exceptions suspend its payment clock, then photograph the exact SKU and packaging details needed to defeat those exceptions.
> - Place the first order just after a statement closes only when the issuer confirms the cycle mechanics; keep payoff cash reserved regardless of the apparent float window.
> - Reconcile rewards by transaction identifier and retain a clawback field; a reward estimate must never finance the next order or conceal a negative merchandise spread.
> - Before cross-listing tickets, test removal behavior during a delayed exchange acknowledgment; retain a single-market fallback and the ability to lock the inventory immediately.

## 3. Days 15–21 — Reconcile Realized Economics and Rehearse Failure  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Day 15 produces two reports: a cash bridge and an economic bridge. The cash bridge explains bank movement, card liabilities, refunds, and restricted reserves. The economic bridge starts with gross buying-group reimbursements, resale proceeds, and eligible rebate yields, then separates merchandise and face-value ticket basis from retained spread. Formula v2.0 uses a 35% retained share: Conservative gross transaction basis of $250,000 becomes $87,500 retained revenue; Base $1,025,000 becomes $358,750; Scaled $1,800,000 becomes $630,000. Corresponding owner profits are $20,000, $143,500, and $360,000. Therefore retained-margin percentages are 22.86%, 40.0%, and 57.14%, while gross-basis margins remain 8%, 14.0%, and 20%. Residual annual costs after acquisition basis are $67,500, $215,250, and $270,000 respectively. These residuals include applicable operating and transaction costs; they are not interchangeable with the stated $1,500–$9,000 monthly operating-overhead range. Preserve these canonical scenarios without forcing actual trades to resemble them. The supplied ASC 606 net-contribution convention is a planning presentation; contract-specific principal-versus-agent analysis determines actual accounting presentation. Underlying merchant sales, venue sales, card limits, and borrowed principal are not additional operator revenue. Keep the gross-volume dashboard distinct from the recognized-revenue ledger and owner-profit report.

Days 16–18 calculate a settled-cohort dashboard using original acquisition IDs, not exchange payout batches. One buying-group purchase order is one deal; one ticket order is one deal even when several seats transfer together. A closed deal has cleared proceeds, recorded direct costs, completed delivery, and an explicit allowance for any remaining refund or dispute exposure. Track cleared-on-time percentage using only deals whose contractual payment dates have arrived. Track oldest overdue receivable in business days and maximum unresolved cash mismatch in dollars. Define liquid coverage as unrestricted bank cash divided by card payments, refunds, taxes, and other unavoidable cash obligations falling due within fourteen days. Exclude undrawn credit, expected ACH, unposted rebates, and ticket asking prices from the numerator. Require at least 1.25 times coverage before any exposure increase. Define cohort contribution after acquisition basis, exchange charges, shipping, insurance, processing, interest, and explicit loss allowances, but before fixed operating overhead. Require positive contribution before counting a cohort as commercially validated. Log both dollars and percentages; a small positive percentage cannot pay a large fixed workload. Owner profit remains a separate measure after the full operating-cost allocation.

Days 19–21 rehearse three failures while positions remain small. First, delay every open buying-group reimbursement by ten business days in the forecast; card balances must still be payable on time from cash already available. Second, assume an issuer suspends new purchases and removes all unposted rewards; stop acquisitions without losing the ability to deliver existing commitments. Third, reduce executable ticket proceeds by 25%, deduct selling fees, and compare the result with recorded basis and the cash reserve. Use executable bids or conservative comparable transactions rather than unsold listings. For the pilot, a 10% projected loss on an event's basis triggers immediate owner review and a documented liquidation decision; 15% is the maximum authorized planned loss, not a promise that market gaps cannot exceed it. At seven days, remove unsupported premium pricing; at three days, prioritize executable exits; inside twenty-four hours, prohibit averaging down. Disable P7 expansion after a failed delivery or unresolved duplicate sale. P10 and P11 stay unfunded unless the rights pack and settlement rehearsal both pass. The objective is a survivable settlement chain, not a visually impressive inventory dashboard.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the source transaction population
   - Export card, bank, buying-group, and exchange records into one dated reconciliation batch.
   - Assign each record to an existing acquisition ID; preserve original source files and isolate duplicate or unmatched entries.
2. Match delivery and cleared settlement
   - Connect accepted merchandise or completed ticket transfers to cleared proceeds and direct costs.
   - Do not close deals using remittance notices alone; record short payments and remaining refund exposure explicitly.
3. Calculate contribution and available liquidity
   - Separate cohort contribution, operating overhead, reserved obligations, and unrestricted bank cash.
   - Exclude expected rewards and resale asking prices from cash coverage; identify every obligation due within fourteen days.
4. Authorize the next bounded operating cycle
   - Apply Kill, Fix, or Scale thresholds and publish the owner-approved exposure limit.
   - Retain the evidence pack, decision date, responsible person, and corrective deadline; never silently override a failed threshold.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Reviews at Days 60/90 — Kill Loss Engines, Fix Friction, Scale Evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 22–24 convert exceptions into dated corrections: obtain missing acceptance evidence, resolve short payments, repair inventory locks, and replace unsupported reward assumptions. Days 25–27 repeat only the smallest transaction needed to test each correction. Do not accelerate turnover merely to reach a sample threshold. Days 28–29 freeze the decision dataset and verify bank cash, open liabilities, contractor hours, and event exposures. Day 30 classifies each play and the overall pod separately. Kill means stop new commitments and execute a controlled wind-down; existing deliveries, refunds, and collections still require completion. Fix means hold or reduce limits while a named owner resolves a measurable defect. Scale means increase the validated lane's concurrent acquisition-basis limit by at most 25%, subject to available cash and all existing reserves. Brokerage fees without inventory exposure receive a workload limit rather than a fabricated inventory allocation. Because first-deal timing is normally one to three months, no settled trades at Day 30 is insufficient evidence, not proof of failure. Continue discovery only with bounded spend and a dated path to a real contract; do not call readiness revenue.

Apply universal Kill conditions before considering any Scale row: prohibited ticket-access methods, fabricated inventory, an inability to meet a committed payment, unapproved burn above $3,000 during the first thirty days, or realized trading losses above $1,250 during that period. Trading losses exclude the separately budgeted operating burn. A suspected rights violation immediately suspends the affected lane pending verification; confirmed prohibited conduct ends that lane. Coverage below 1.00 times is Kill for new commitments even if expected collections appear sufficient. Coverage from 1.00 to below 1.25, any unexplained cash difference, or insufficient sample size is Fix. Define a cycle as acquisition through cleared settlement, and define a matured deal as one whose contractual proceeds are due by the review date. For Scale, require zero overdue amounts beyond three business days, zero unresolved delivery failures, and no counterparty above 35% of open acquisition exposure after the initial two-reimbursement probation. The decision matrix adds progressively stronger samples, punctuality, and owner-profit tests. These are proposed pilot controls, not historical performance claims or substitutes for the scorecard's canonical annual scenarios.

At Day 60, review cumulative settled evidence and the trailing thirty-day cost base; at Day 90, require two consecutive thirty-day periods with positive owner profit after all booked operating costs. The Base annual case implies approximately $85,416.67 gross transaction volume, $29,895.83 retained revenue, and $11,958.33 owner profit per average month. Those are scenario translations, not mandatory launch quotas. If observed retained share is 8% rather than 35%, rebuild the forward operating forecast from 8% while leaving the canonical comparison intact. Do not make the forecast work by counting client purchase value twice or ignoring contractor costs. Maintain one full-time owner plus two contractors throughout the pilot; expansion requires at least 20% spare assigned capacity and documented backup coverage for settlements and event-day failures. The Scaled scenario's $360,000 annual profit belongs to two full-time staff plus three contractors, not this three-person pod. After Day 30, approve a fresh operating budget before incurring another month's costs. Where a lane fails twice after documented repairs, close it and retain the profitable lanes rather than preserving eleven funded plays for appearance.
