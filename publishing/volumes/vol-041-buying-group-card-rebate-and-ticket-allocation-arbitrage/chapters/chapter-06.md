## 1. Mine executable buyer demand before collecting cheap inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a demand ledger, not a shopping feed. In Airtable or HubSpot, create one record per buyer requirement containing legal entity, named purchasing contact, SKU or event identifier, quantity, delivered ceiling, acceptance conditions, settlement clock and evidence date. Mine buying-group member portals for currently open commitments, distributor clearance lists for dated surplus, and official venue calendars for announced corporate hospitality demand. Use public procurement awards, company expansion announcements and convention calendars to identify organizations likely to need client entertainment; these are outreach triggers, not proof of ticket budgets. Secretary of State records establish entity identity, while UCC searches can reveal financing relationships requiring further inquiry; neither proves solvency. Ask wholesalers which exact UPCs they would reorder this month and ask executive assistants which dates, capacities and delivery deadlines remain unresolved. Log the source URL and retrieval date so a contractor can reproduce each signal. A lead becomes actionable only when a reachable counterparty supplies measurable buying terms. An attractive retail discount without confirmed acceptance criteria stays outside the purchasing queue. Review stale requirements weekly and close records whose price, event date or buyer authority has changed.

Organize the 11 mapped plays into distinct prospecting lanes rather than one undifferentiated arbitrage list. Use domestic buying-group commitments, distributor closeouts, authorized cross-border surplus, repeat wholesale replenishment, commercial-card rebate stacking, statement-cycle float, transferable primary ticket allocations, secondary ticket resale, season-seat license sourcing, corporate-suite brokerage and corporate hospitality mandates. These are chapter operating labels, not additional canonical identifiers. The specified canonical play, authorized_distributor_grey_market_diversion_arbitrage, belongs in the cross-border surplus lane. Keep card rewards and float attached to an underlying purchase record; they are yield mechanisms, not separate customer sales. For example, a $5,000 eligible purchase earning 2% produces $100 of rewards, while delaying settlement creates no additional revenue by itself. A season-seat license purchased for $8,000 and transferred for $9,000 with a $500 transfer charge leaves $500 before other costs, but only if the venue permits that transfer. Require a written buyer mandate before acquiring such rights; otherwise broker the introduction for a disclosed fee. This lane separation exposes shared capital demands and prevents a seemingly diversified pipeline from depending entirely on one issuer, one wholesaler or one event promoter.

Run signal mining as a weekly production routine. On Monday, refresh open buying-group quantities and distributor offers through permitted exports or manual review. On Tuesday, compare those offers against written buyer ceilings, adding freight, duty, payment fees and expected return exposure before ranking spreads. On Wednesday, review official on-sale announcements, venue transfer terms and local convention schedules; do not automate access around waiting rooms, ticket limits or CAPTCHA. Use SkyBox or Automatiq only through supported workflows and authorized connections, with one inventory record governing every marketplace listing. On Thursday, contact buyers with matched opportunities rather than generic inventory blasts. On Friday, retire stale quotes and reconcile demand against available cash. A useful test is twenty units costing $180 each, a confirmed $205 delivered bid, and $12 per unit of freight and handling: the merchandise spread is $260 before rewards and overhead. If eligible 2% rewards add $72, record them separately until earned. If freight rises to $25 per unit, merchandise spread disappears; reject the purchase rather than presenting card rewards as proof that an otherwise weak deal is sound.

## 2. Qualify counterparties, imports and allocations with a cash-first scorecard  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Score each prospect out of 100 using buyer commitment worth 25 points, counterparty payment evidence worth 20, independently verified unit economics worth 20, product or transfer eligibility worth 20, and cash-cycle fit worth 15. Full commitment points require an executable purchase order or signed mandate, not a chat-room promise. Award payment points for recent trade references and a successful small settlement, not screenshots of account balances. Set 80 as the initial qualification threshold, but treat failed identity, admissibility or transfer checks as disqualifying regardless of total score. For buying groups, distinguish Net-3 after receipt from Net-3 after inspection and acceptance; a five-day inspection period changes the financing requirement. A $10,000 order held seven additional days consumes $10,000 of liquidity even when accounting profit is unchanged. Start with exposure that available cash can repay without the expected ACH arrival. Independently confirm changed bank instructions through a previously verified telephone number. Diversify among vetted Net-3/Net-5 counterparties only after each completes its own pilot. Multiple storefront names sharing one paying entity are a single credit exposure, and a pending ACH entry is not the same as completed settlement.

For authorized_distributor_grey_market_diversion_arbitrage, investigate reported 35–60% regional price gaps as hypotheses about identical goods, not automatic import margins. Foreign purchasing entities do not remove trademark, customs, product-labeling, manufacturer-contract or distribution restrictions. Kirtsaeng v. John Wiley & Sons addresses copyright first sale for lawfully made copies; it does not grant blanket permission to import every authentic branded product. Under 19 CFR 133.23, trademark restrictions can still affect gray-market goods, including materially different products. Medical consumables and OTC diabetic test strips require separate FDA admissibility and labeling analysis under 21 U.S.C. §381; authenticity alone does not establish eligibility. Start with globally identical universal-packaging fragrance or OEM consumable SKUs only after verifying the actual product requirements. Illustratively, 100 units at $50 cost $5,000; $900 of documented freight, duty and inspection yields $5,900 landed cost. A signed $7,200 wholesale order leaves $1,300 before returns, rewards and overhead. If eligibility or downstream acceptance remains unresolved, cancel sourcing or switch to documented domestic inventory. Container quantities come after sample approval and repeated matched orders, never before them.

Qualify ticket and suite rights with the same rigor as physical inventory. Obtain section, row, seat count, restricted-view status, delivery method, transfer opening date, cancellation treatment and every venue charge. A corporate-suite mandate must specify whether catering, parking and service staffing are included; a suite license does not automatically permit sublicensing. For eight tickets costing $120 each, a $155 resale price creates $1,240 of gross proceeds. With a 12% seller fee on those proceeds and $20 of other costs, contribution is $111.20 before overhead. At $130 each, contribution becomes negative $64.80 using the same assumptions. Choose an event-specific markdown schedule before purchase, such as reviewing executable bids fourteen, seven and three days before the event. A price floor is a decision trigger, not assurance that buyers exist. Prefer signed corporate demand or small transferable allocations for the first wins; reduce exposure when delivery remains locked until shortly before attendance. Follow 15 U.S.C. §45c without queue or CAPTCHA bypass software, and verify state broker licensing, disclosure and transfer requirements before accepting the relevant order.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before the first funded commitment**
> 
> A high prospect score never substitutes for transaction-specific evidence.
> - Verify the paying legal entity, bank instructions and settlement trigger through independently confirmed contact details.
> - Match the exact SKU or ticket rights against buyer acceptance terms, transfer rules and documented import eligibility.
> - Recalculate contribution after freight, duty, marketplace charges, returns and a delayed settlement; count rewards only when eligible.
> - Demonstrate cash coverage for the card payment and identify an executable fallback buyer or a pre-funded loss limit.

## 3. Convert matched signals into three-to-ten settled first wins  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the first prospect cohort around one merchandise lane and one corporate allocation lane so research remains comparable. An illustrative eight-week funnel starts with 120 raw signals, of which 80 have verified contacts and current intent, 24 produce substantive replies, 12 pass qualification, eight receive executable quotes and five become signed orders or mandates. Those transitions are 66.7%, 30%, 50%, 66.7% and 62.5%, respectively; they are planning assumptions to test, not established market conversion rates. Track settlement separately: five signatures might yield only three completed wins within the period if transfer or inspection delays intervene. Define a win as delivered merchandise accepted and paid, or a completed brokerage service with its fee collected. Exclude refundable deposits, tentative reservations and self-dealing transactions. Review every lost quote by cause: price, timing, missing documentation, unsuitable rights or insufficient buyer authority. If replies are scarce, improve targeting before increasing volume. If replies are strong but orders are scarce, examine delivered economics and credibility. A low settlement rate requires operational repair, not more prospecting that compounds unresolved exposure across the same constrained cash pool.

Use a restrained multi-channel cadence tied to a specific requirement. On day one, send a short commercial email identifying the buyer signal, exact opportunity and requested next step: 'Your team is hosting clients during the October convention. I can source a permitted twelve-person suite allocation with itemized inclusions. Who owns the event budget and required date?' On day three, call the publicly listed business number and confirm the correct purchasing contact. On day six, send one matched example with a real quote expiration and supporting terms. On day ten, ask whether to close the requirement or revisit a stated future date. Honor opt-outs immediately and maintain suppression records. Commercial email must satisfy 15 U.S.C. §7704, including accurate routing information, truthful subjects, required identification and address information, and a working opt-out process. Avoid automated texts and prerecorded calls without reviewing the applicable permission requirements. Inbound assets should be equally concrete: publish a buyer requirement form, a delivered-cost worksheet and a sample allocation brief. Request commercial specifications, not card credentials or unnecessary personal records. A useful form captures budget, timing and authority before offering a sales meeting.

Handle objections by changing evidence or transaction structure, not by promising away risk. When a wholesaler says the goods may differ from its domestic assortment, provide exact UPCs, packaging photographs, invoice provenance and a paid sample inspection; do not equate authenticity with interchangeability. When a buying group refuses a deposit, offer a smaller pilot that your cash reserve can carry rather than extending unpriced credit. When a corporate buyer questions an allocation premium, separate ticket cost, disclosed service fee and included delivery work in a written mandate. When they demand guaranteed resale value, offer sourcing against their budget instead of an investment proposition. A $3,000 suite sourced under a $3,450 agreed total leaves $450 before payment costs when you are the principal; an agency arrangement may instead recognize only the $450 service fee. Document the actual role rather than selecting whichever revenue presentation looks larger. For the first order, specify acceptance windows, permitted substitutions, cancellation treatment, remittance instructions and dispute evidence. Send a post-settlement summary showing promised versus actual timing. That short operating record is the strongest opening for the next, slightly larger order.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build a dated demand cohort
   - Collect 120 raw signals across one merchandise lane and one corporate allocation lane.
   - Record source, buyer identity, requested specification, budget evidence and expiration; target 80 verified records.
2. Qualify before obtaining inventory
   - Apply the 100-point scorecard and complete the four transaction checks.
   - Obtain exact buyer requirements and vendor terms; reject failed rights or eligibility checks regardless of score.
3. Run matched outreach and a small pilot
   - Use the day-one, day-three, day-six and day-ten cadence, then issue an executable quote.
   - Size the first order so cash can repay the card balance despite delayed reimbursement; secure written acceptance terms.
4. Settle, reconcile and request the repeat
   - Verify acceptance, collect payment and reconcile all transaction-level costs.
   - Count the completed win, compare realized contribution with the quote, and expand only after the pilot evidence supports it.

## 4. Reconcile sourcing economics and expand only proven cash cycles  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep the Formula v2.0 scorecard separate from the transaction ledger while reconciling both explicitly. Conservative gross scorecard receipts of $250,000 multiplied by the 35% retained share produce $87,500 of retained revenue; subtracting $67,500 of remaining modeled costs leaves $20,000 owner profit. Base receipts of $1,025,000 yield $358,750 retained revenue and $143,500 profit after $215,250 of remaining costs. Scaled receipts of $1,800,000 yield $630,000 retained revenue and $360,000 profit after $270,000 of remaining costs. Gross-basis net margins remain exactly 8%, 14.0% and 20%; retained margins are 22.86%, 40.0% and 57.14% after rounding. The 35% retained share is the supplied planning normalization, not a promise that each buying-group order earns that spread. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent presentation depends on the actual control and contractual facts; the retained measure does not itself determine statutory reporting. Keep underlying merchandise value, customer ticket spend, seller reimbursements and broker fees in separate fields. Treat eligible purchase rebates consistently with the adopted accounting policy and never record the same reward as both reduced inventory cost and additional revenue.

Underwrite card float as a timing constraint rather than a substitute for startup capital. The indicated startup range is $10,000–$55,000, with monthly operating expense of $1,500–$9,000; actual commitments must fit available cash and contractual payment dates. A ledger may track a sixty-day horizon, but a specific purchase receives only the interval allowed by its statement cycle, grace-period eligibility and issuer terms. For $10,000 of eligible spending, 2% rewards equal $200 and 4% equal $400 before fees; verify caps, exclusions, reversals and earning conditions before assuming an uncapped program. At an illustrative 24% annual borrowing rate, carrying $10,000 for thirty days costs about $197.26 using actual days over 365, nearly consuming the 2% reward. Model issuer financial review or shutdown by assuming no further purchasing capacity while existing balances remain payable. Maintain shipment insurance with coverage appropriate to the merchandise, value and carrier terms, and preserve serial numbers where applicable. If a buying group misses its promised ACH date, freeze incremental exposure immediately. More available credit does not cure a deteriorating counterparty or convert an overdue receivable into usable cash.

Use days one through thirty for verified contacts, sample checks and small signed pilots; days thirty-one through sixty for delivery, settlement and repeat orders; and days sixty-one through ninety for cautiously increasing the lanes that actually reconcile. Target three settled wins before expanding into additional product families, with ten as an upper first-cycle objective rather than a purchase quota. Assign the founder to underwriting and buyer relationships, one contractor to evidence collection and another to reconciliation in the Conservative and Base team design of 1 FT + 2 C. The Scaled design is 2 FT + 3 C, five people total. Automation should compare commitments, invoices, delivery scans, acceptance records and settlement references; exceptions return to a named operator. For tickets, synchronize inventory through supported SkyBox or Automatiq functions and test duplicate-sale prevention with a controlled listing before broad distribution. Review profit per funded dollar-day, settlement punctuality, loss severity and repeat purchase rate every week. Retire channels whose apparent spreads vanish after returns or markdowns. The next dollar belongs in a documented repeatable cash cycle, not whichever new offer advertises the largest percentage discount.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the spread between commitment and cash**
> 
> Experienced operators preserve evidence and liquidity before increasing purchasing volume.
> - Save a dated commitment snapshot showing the buying group's inspection clock; Net-3 language alone does not establish the cash date.
> - Buy a sample against the downstream buyer's exact identifier before quoting an imported assortment, then retain the acceptance record.
> - Allocate rewards to the originating order only once; separately flag reward reversals after returns and issuer eligibility changes.
> - Set ticket markdown reviews before purchase and compare executable net bids, not optimistic listing prices from unsold inventory.
