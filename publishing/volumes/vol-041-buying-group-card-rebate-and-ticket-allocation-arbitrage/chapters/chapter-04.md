## 1. Ring-fence settlement cash before purchasing inventory  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Allocate startup cash by settlement obligation, not by the size of the available card limit. The three illustrative launch budgets below use $10,000, $30,000, and $55,000; they are capital plans, not additional earnings scenarios. Each places 60% in working capital and float protection. Split that bucket internally between deployable purchase cash and an untouched issuer-payment reserve: $3,000 plus $3,000, $9,000 plus $9,000, and $16,500 plus $16,500, respectively. Neither the ticket pilot nor the operating cushion counts toward the 60% test. A credit limit is contingent purchasing capacity, not cash, and an anticipated rebate cannot pay a statement until it actually posts. With $3,000 deployable, a $2,000 pre-committed buying-group order leaves only $1,000 for another order, regardless of a larger unused card line. Require the purchase order to identify exact SKUs, accepted condition, quantity, reimbursement amount, delivery destination, and the event starting the Net-3 or Net-5 clock. Reserve funds remain available for the issuer even when receiving inspection delays the buying group's payment. Never count the same dollar as inventory capital, emergency liquidity, and the next month's contractor payment. That triple allocation is how profitable spreads become missed payments.

Size each buying-group commitment against the cash conversion cycle observed in completed transactions. Suppose a group accepts ten devices costing $400 each and commits to reimburse $410 per device. The merchandise spread is $100. A genuinely eligible 2% card rebate adds $80; $35 insured carriage and $15 packing leave $130 before overhead and loss allowances. A 4% reward would add another $80 only where written issuer terms support that rate without an exhausted category cap or excluded purchase. Do not buy merely because an advertised spread looks attractive. Confirm acceptance first, photograph serial numbers, retain the carrier's acceptance scan, and reconcile delivered units against the group's receiving report. Net-3 describes contractual timing, not guaranteed bank availability. A five-day inspection delay plus a weekend can materially extend the cash cycle. Set an initial exposure ceiling of the smaller of $2,000 or 20% of startup cash per unproven group, then increase only after three clean settlements. At the $10,000 launch size, the $3,000 deployable bucket remains the tighter aggregate constraint. If payment slips, stop new commitments rather than using another issuer to hide the aging receivable.

Keep the scorecard bridge beside the capital plan so gross throughput never masquerades as spendable earnings. Formula v2.0 uses a 35% retained share: $250,000 becomes $87,500, $1,025,000 becomes $358,750, and $1,800,000 becomes $630,000. Subtract total operating costs of $67,500, $215,250, and $270,000 to reach the exact owner profits of $20,000, $143,500, and $360,000. For a planning bridge, allocate recurring overhead of $18,000, $54,000, and $108,000 annually, corresponding to $1,500, $4,500, and $9,000 monthly. The remaining annual operating-cost allowances are $49,500, $161,250, and $162,000 for transaction charges, variable labor, claims, and other costs outside landed inventory basis. Assign every cost once; do not subtract face-value tickets or merchandise again after the 65% basis deduction. These allocations reconcile the model, rather than proving those expense ratios will occur. The resulting retained margins are 22.86%, 40.0%, and 57.14%. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis remains transaction-specific; the model's retained share is not a universal accounting election. Have the bookkeeper map gross activity, basis, recognized revenue, and owner compensation explicitly before publishing management statements or treating the modeled residual as a distributable cash balance.

## 2. Assemble twelve infrastructure layers without twelve subscriptions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the stack around one transaction identifier that survives purchase, shipment, reimbursement, listing, sale, and statement payment. Airtable can hold the commitment register, while Google Sheets calculates cash exposure and Wave holds bookkeeping entries. Those tools have different jobs; none should silently overwrite another system's settled amounts. A buying-group record needs group identity, purchase-order reference, SKU, quantity, card account suffix, purchase date, acceptance date, promised settlement date, reimbursement received, and evidence links. A ticket record needs event, venue, section, row, seats, transfer restrictions, acquisition basis, listing identifiers, exchange charges, and payout status. Track stadium seat licenses and corporate-suite allocations separately because transfer conditions and delivery obligations differ from ordinary event tickets. Store payment credentials in the issuer or approved payment platform, not in a spreadsheet. Give contractors task-specific access through named accounts. The matrix uses exact planning prices for selected tiers, not a claim that current checkout terms have been verified. Confirm region, taxes, annual commitments, included features, and business eligibility before purchase. A quoted or usage-priced service does not become free merely because the initial software budget assigns it no spending authority.

Start with a $100 monthly software and communications allowance inside the $1,500 lean overhead plan. One Google Workspace Business Starter seat at a $7 monthly equivalent on annual commitment and one Bitwarden Teams seat at $4 consume $11 before taxes, domain registration, and any additional seats. Treat these as procurement reference prices and replace them with accepted vendor quotations before signing. The remaining allowance covers small usage charges and avoids committing the full budget to subscriptions. An illustrative $1,500 monthly envelope also assigns $750 to limited contractor support, $250 to bookkeeping and tax administration, $200 to insurance, $100 to storage and supplies, and $100 to connectivity and bank-service contingencies. These are budget allowances, not vendor offers. They exclude owner living costs and do not imply that $750 buys a full-time employee. The Conservative and Base teams each contain one full-time operator and two contractors; contractor headcount does not mean full-time hours. At Scaled, two full-time people and three contractors must fit the full $270,000 cost bridge. If paid staffing exceeds that envelope, revise the operating plan rather than quietly retaining the canonical profit claim.

Automate reconciliation before automating purchases. Use Make only for supported integrations that copy authorized records, flag overdue settlements, and prepare exceptions for review. Do not build queue bypasses, CAPTCHA solvers, purchasing-limit circumvention, or credential-sharing workarounds. The federal BOTS Act, 15 U.S.C. §45c, reaches prohibited circumvention and specified resale of tickets obtained through it; a ticket POS is an inventory control tool, not purchase authorization. SkyBox can centralize ticket inventory and supported exchange distribution, but verify access terms, selling charges, payout holds, and synchronization behavior with the actual account. Automatiq is an optional repricing candidate only after a written quote and a controlled trial; its custom cost is deliberately excluded from the zero-license-cost launch design. Never invent an exact subscription price for a quoted service. First demonstrate that one authorized sale withdraws every duplicate listing before another exchange can sell the same seats. Schedule daily exports to an encrypted local backup, and test restoration using a separate working copy. If an automation breaks, freeze affected listings and reconcile manually. A small, recoverable ledger is cheaper than a sophisticated integration that creates duplicate obligations during an event's final selling window.

## 3. Separate entity cash, client spend, and insured custody  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Create the operating entity, obtain its EIN directly from the IRS, open business accounts in the matching name, and document authorized signers before accepting customer money. State formation charges vary; use the capital allowance rather than assuming a national filing price. Keep entity records, tax registrations, contracts, and issuer agreements in a restricted folder with renewal reminders. A commercial card may still require a personal guarantee, and business account protections differ from consumer-account protections. Read the actual agreement for payment obligations, reward exclusions, account review rights, and prohibited transaction patterns. Do not describe manufactured purchases, circular payments, or cash-equivalent transactions as ordinary client spend. For California merchandise resale, examine Revenue and Taxation Code §6091 and 18 CCR §1668: a resale certificate must support a genuine resale transaction, not personal consumption. Record the buyer's certificate, seller's permit details where required, goods description, and acceptance date. Separate merchandise tax handling from ticket tax and marketplace collection. A marketplace's collection service does not automatically resolve every registration, filing, or direct-sale obligation. Assign each jurisdictional question to a named accountant and retain the answer beside the affected transaction type.

The mapped play agency_ad_spend_card_multiplier_rebate_cycling belongs in this stack only when the agency has genuine client-authorized advertising activity and an eligible payment arrangement. Consider $50,000 of monthly media spend that the client has prefunded and the platform permits the agency to pay. A contract-permitted 2% reward produces $1,000. Subtract $300 of processing and funding costs, $400 owed to the client under the reward-sharing clause, and a $100 internal reversal reserve; $200 remains available before general overhead. The reserve is a cash-control deduction in this example, not automatically a booked expense. The $50,000 is underlying client media spend, not $50,000 of operator revenue; accounting follows the agency's actual performance obligations and principal or agent position. Document authorization, spending limits, platform eligibility, refund ownership, and reward allocation before attaching the card. Reconcile campaign invoices to client authorization and issuer charges weekly. If rewards drop to zero, this example loses its positive contribution; stop rewards-dependent spending immediately. Move to client-direct billing or prefunded approved agency terms instead of advancing personal cash or cycling payments to force a higher card limit. This play cannot borrow the buying-group reserve without an explicit capital reallocation.

Match insurance to custody rather than buying a generic policy and assuming every shipment is covered. Ask the broker about high-value electronics, unattended delivery, employee dishonesty, cyber incidents, transit limits, and contractual liability. A declared carrier value is not automatically equivalent to all-risk cargo insurance. Save the applicable wording, deductible, per-package limit, proof-of-value requirements, and claim deadline. For a $4,000 device shipment, a $1,000 commodity sublimit leaves a $3,000 exposure even if the label shows a larger declared value. Reject the shipment or obtain appropriate coverage before carrier acceptance. Ticket exposure needs different controls: event cancellation, postponement, restricted transfer, counterfeit claims, and marketplace payout holds are not ordinary cargo losses. For stadium seat licenses and suites, obtain written transfer rights, assignment charges, included benefits, and the issuer's required transfer process. Do not treat a suite deposit as liquid inventory merely because a broker expects demand. Maintain separate loss reserves and prohibit using refundable customer deposits as owner draws. A second bank relationship can reduce operational concentration, but it must not conceal activity from an issuer or replace accurate transaction descriptions during an account review.

## 4. Prove the settlement loop before increasing commitments  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use the expected one-to-three-month first-deal window to complete a controlled operating rehearsal, not to accumulate subscriptions. Start with one approved buying group, one commercial card, one business account, and one low-value order whose loss the deployable budget can absorb. Confirm the group independently through its business identity, contract, references, and a verified payment destination. Do not accept last-minute bank-detail changes solely through email; call a previously established contact using a previously verified number. Rehearse a missing package, partial acceptance, delayed ACH, duplicate invoice, reward reversal, and issuer account suspension. Each exception needs a responsible person, evidence location, deadline, and permitted next action. The decisive readiness test is whether protected cash pays the next statement without the delayed reimbursement. If the answer is no, reduce commitments before the purchase, not after delivery. Keep a rolling thirteen-week cash schedule showing opening cash, verified inflows, issuer payments, tax obligations, payroll, and minimum reserves. Count unsettled ticket payouts as receivables with conservative dates, not available bank funds. Release additional capital only when the pilot produces matched purchase, delivery, acceptance, cleared reimbursement, and issuer-payment records without an unresolved material difference.

Ticket launch testing must include price decay and a failed-sale exit. Suppose a transferable ticket costs $100 and the exchange deducts 15% of the seller's displayed sale amount. Ignoring separately identified taxes and other charges, break-even requires $100 divided by 0.85, or $117.65. An illustrative markdown curve might list at $140 twenty-one days before the event, $125 fourteen days before, and $110 seven days before. Net proceeds at those prices are $119, $106.25, and $93.50; the last stage accepts a $6.50 loss rather than pretending every position remains profitable. At seventy-two hours, a documented $95 liquidation limit produces $80.75 and a $19.25 loss if sold. An unsold ticket can still lose its entire $100 basis, so a price rule cannot ensure an exit. Configure the actual exchange's fee formula rather than copying this example blindly. The FTC's 16 CFR Part 464 requires covered live-event offers to present total prices with required charges and appropriate prominence, subject to the rule's specific permitted exclusions. Verify state resale licensing, disclosure, and pricing requirements before listing, including New York Arts and Cultural Affairs Law §25.13 where applicable.

Run a weekly close that distinguishes profit, liquidity, and permission to expand. Profit starts with the transaction ledger and includes actual basis, fees, posted rewards, losses, and allocated labor. Liquidity starts with cleared bank balances and subtracts issuer payments, protected customer amounts, taxes, and committed operating costs. Expansion depends on both measures plus reliable counterparties and valid transfer rights. A profitable week does not justify a larger purchase if settlement cash is locked inside an event payout hold. Assign the owner to issuer and counterparty decisions, one contractor to evidence matching, and another to listings or shipping. Use separate permissions so a contractor cannot both change bank instructions and release payment. At the five-person Scaled configuration, retain the same separation rather than adding management layers. Trigger a purchasing pause after any missed contractual reimbursement, reserve breach, unexplained ledger difference, or duplicate ticket sale. Reopen only after the specific defect is resolved and recorded. Track settlement days, overdue cash, reward realization, unsold ticket basis, contractor minutes per transaction, and cash coverage. These measurements tell the operator whether to increase volume, replace a counterparty, or return to the simpler manual stack before overhead consumes the retained spread.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Fund distinct cash buckets
   - Allocate startup funds and document protected issuer-payment cash.
   - Use the selected capital budget; label deployable working capital, reserve, ticket pilot, setup spending, and operating cushion without overlapping dollars.
2. Configure the minimum transaction spine
   - Create named accounts, a shared transaction identifier, and controlled evidence storage.
   - Connect the commitment register, bookkeeping records, shipping evidence, ticket inventory, and statement ledger through references rather than uncontrolled duplicate balances.
3. Complete one funded operating rehearsal
   - Process a low-value authorized order and simulate settlement, inventory, and recovery failures.
   - Document acceptance, cleared reimbursement, statement payment, duplicate-listing behavior, cash coverage, and successful restoration before treating the stack as ready.
4. Expand only from reconciled evidence
   - Review weekly metrics and increase commitments within remaining cash limits.
   - Require three clean buying-group settlements before increasing that counterparty's ceiling; pause immediately for overdue payments, reserve breaches, or unresolved ledger differences.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before the first meaningful commitment**
> 
> Record a dated pass result and supporting evidence for each check before increasing deployed capital.
> - Cash coverage: verify that at least 50% of startup funding remains allocated to working capital and float reserves, with protected settlement cash separately identified.
> - Counterparty and custody: verify the purchase order, receiving trigger, payment destination, commodity insurance limits, and authorized transfer rights.
> - Ledger and inventory: reconcile a pilot transaction end to end, restore its backup, and prove that duplicate ticket listings cannot remain active after sale.
> - Terms and pricing: verify issuer reward eligibility, client authorization, BOTS Act-compatible acquisition, applicable resale licensing, and required total-price presentation.
