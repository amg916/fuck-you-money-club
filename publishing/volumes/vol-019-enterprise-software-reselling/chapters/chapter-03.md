## Executive Summary

Distribution executives at TD SYNNEX, Pax8 and Ingram Micro Cloud, plus Canalys analyst Jay McBain, agree the money has moved from boxes to services and marketplaces: hardware is down to 16 percent of partner revenue, 80 to 90 percent of 1,400 surveyed partners have or plan services investment, and marketplace growth runs at 86 percent compounded. The street mechanics are billing and quoting automation: ID Sync pushes marketplace mailbox counts into ConnectWise agreements, Digital Bridge connects Autotask, ConnectWise, HubSpot, ServiceNow and QuickBooks Online, and deal registrations become quotes before the partner asks. Expansion pays: a five-day migration grew into full managed services, and a small support deal led to a $5 million-plus, 1,350-user contract. The split: distributors sell white-label delivery, while McBain says buyers now want seven named partners and no single throat to choke.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The distributors and the analyst covering them agree on one hard fact: the box alone no longer carries the business, and the money now sits in the services wrapped around it. Steven Andis, who runs technology acceleration for TD SYNNEX across Europe, frames the formula bluntly as product plus service equals solution, and says customers no longer ask for a firewall; they ask to enhance their security posture, which turns into a firewall plus assessment, implementation and rollout [ULy4t5kvUt0 @ 00:03:36]. The company's fourth Direction of Technology survey, covering 1,400 partners across 40 countries, found 80 to 90 percent of partners already have or plan to invest in services capability, with roughly 80 percent investing in managed services [ULy4t5kvUt0 @ 00:06:03]. Jay McBain of Canalys puts the same shift in revenue terms: hardware has fallen to 16 percent of the revenue mix and 13 percent of the profit mix for more than a thousand surveyed MSPs and VARs, so 84 percent of the business is now software and services [SwebwmaZR2I @ 00:02:55]. Graham Kelly of TD SYNNEX, working the InfoComm floor, says the old view of distribution as pick, pack and ship undersells what the house now does, from field services to stripping cost out of the supply chain [4HmZZT3HBnA @ 00:01:02].

The second point of agreement is that the distributor stays behind the partner, never in front of the customer. Andis calls TD SYNNEX an absolute two-tier distributor that sells services only to partners, filling gaps in competence, capability, skills or geography whether the partner covers 90 percent of the job or 10 percent, and its engineers can arrive on site wearing the partner's t-shirt [ULy4t5kvUt0 @ 00:14:41]. Mark Best, who runs European managed and support services, lists zero competition as the core pitch: the company does not sell directly and exists only to service partners [K3d3674aDZ8 @ 00:05:17]. Sophia from the same services organization backs the outsourcing math with a field example: a very large UK partner running roughly 85 percent services-led business delivers only 40 percent of those services itself and farms the other 60 percent out to over 300 providers in the UK alone, which is the management load the distributor pitches itself as the single point for [2qmTwyQCa_o @ 00:02:45]. Ben Elder at Ingram Micro Cloud UK sells the same white-label idea at level 1, with a 24/7 service desk acting as the single point of contact for end-user incidents in local languages, during whatever service hours the partner subscribes to [M5I7gKJnNwU @ 00:02:57].

Everyone on this beat also agrees that the buying surface is collapsing into marketplaces and single-pane platforms. Jay McBain tracks marketplace growth at 86 percent compounded and says Canalys predicted AWS would become a top-10 distributor by 2025, only to watch it get there by the end of 2023, with companies now announcing billion-dollar businesses run purely through AWS [3uRf7nlQ_JY @ 00:13:16]. Nikki Meyer of Pax8 describes the cloud-distributor version: the Pax8 Marketplace handles billing, quoting and provisioning of cloud products so an MSP sends the customer a single monthly bill [Ccgwwzx0ld4 @ 00:01:12]. Ingram Micro Cloud pitches the same single pane of glass, a marketplace built on the Odin service automation platform that automates billing across Microsoft CSP, McAfee SaaS, RingCentral phone systems and cloud backup, because the subscription model has replaced the old way software was sold [M5I7gKJnNwU @ 00:02:25]. TD SYNNEX's answer is StreamOne Ion, which Russell Rebane says pulls Microsoft, AWS and Google into one platform with automated billing, customer management, real-time usage insights and custom-branded storefronts [1Rg0jWCs65Y @ 00:08:57]. McBain's read on why this matters: 73 percent of the market flows indirect, so distributors are backing their reseller relationships with marketplace technology to keep the transaction there.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The plumbing behind marketplace resale is billing sync, and the vendors walk it click by click. Ingram Micro Cloud's ID Sync Billing Connector is bought inside the marketplace by logging in with IAM Online credentials, selecting the package and paying; the Billing Studio is then downloaded from the reseller control panel's ID Sync tab with a license key [ynfBBQclUKU @ 00:00:28]. After a one-time linking of marketplace accounts to ConnectWise agreements, the studio shows every customer's subscriptions and resource counts, such as two of ten 1 GB basic mailboxes in use; provision a third mailbox, hit force update, and ConnectWise logs a one-count adjustment on the agreement, spreadsheet free [ynfBBQclUKU @ 00:03:22]. Russell Rebane says TD SYNNEX built Digital Bridge because partners kept asking to integrate but lacked the time and resources, so it pre-built connectors into Autotask, ConnectWise PSA, HubSpot, ServiceNow and QuickBooks Online that a partner switches on by logging in with that system's credentials and picking sync functions such as SKU catalog, pricing and availability, order status, invoice details, PO submission and quote sync [1Rg0jWCs65Y @ 00:05:08]. Sophia describes the deep end of the same idea: TD SYNNEX is integrating its own service management system with a long-time reseller's, becoming part of that partner's invoicing chain [2qmTwyQCa_o @ 00:06:27].

Speed to quote is the second lever. Scott Frell, director of vendor management at TD SYNNEX, says every Scale Computing SKU is preloaded into the system, so a reseller who reaches out can have a full quote on the solution within hours, backed by a triangular effort between the distributor, the vendor's sales team and the partner [WF4x0_79W_w @ 00:01:55]. Rebane goes further: when a deal registration is created on the vendor side for Extreme, Cisco or Fortinet, the vendor sends it over and TD SYNNEX proactively builds the quote, either parking it in Partner First Commerce or emailing it, so the partner never waits on an SLA [1Rg0jWCs65Y @ 00:03:10]. The AI layer is an MCP server partners can wire into Copilot, Claude or whatever LLM they run; Rebane's example is a task firing at 8:00 a.m. that pulls status on a list of 50 priority orders before the workday starts, with agent-to-agent options for partners who cannot build the full integration [1Rg0jWCs65Y @ 00:06:40]. The payoff Rebane quotes from TD SYNNEX data: partners using the digital tools grow 28 percent year over year on average, versus less than 10 percent in some cases for those who do not [1Rg0jWCs65Y @ 00:09:51].

Expansion economics come next: land small, then expand. Sophia's case is a five-person SMB reseller that started ten years ago with a five-day Microsoft migration and now runs TD SYNNEX support, managed services and security managed services, working with the same contact the whole way [2qmTwyQCa_o @ 00:06:02]. Mark Best tells a bigger version: a relatively small cloud support deployment earned enough trust that TD SYNNEX helped that customer secure a 1,350-user managed service covering cybersecurity, infrastructure, compute, storage and networking, a three-year contract worth well in excess of $5 million to the partner [K3d3674aDZ8 @ 00:07:21]. Jessica Ash and Shyla Roquette of the TD SYNNEX high-growth team turn the expansion into a call script: a customer who calls to buy just Copilot should trigger five or six follow-up questions on data governance, Azure or AWS consumption readiness, adoption services and whether devices can run it, which is four or five revenue motions a partner misses by simply taking the order [4NI20Et258M @ 00:02:16]. Jeremiah Barnes and Bo Garrett of TD SYNNEX cite a reseller that learned at Inspire 2025 how to supercharge its Copilot business and grew from 10 percent to 35 percent and then 200 percent year over year, while similar peer resellers grew 22 percent in the same space [fllmKjJXmBk @ 00:05:10].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split in the channel is over whose logo the end customer actually sees. Steven Andis of TD SYNNEX pitches support and managed services delivered in the partner's name, with engineers arriving on site in either a TD SYNNEX shirt or the partner's shirt, so the reseller keeps owning the relationship [ULy4t5kvUt0 @ 00:14:41]. Ben Elder at Ingram Micro Cloud sells the same move as white-label level 1 support through a 24/7 cloud management service desk working in local languages [M5I7gKJnNwU @ 00:02:57]. Jay McBain of Canalys says the buyer has moved the other way: seven-layer deals now carry seven partners, there is no single throat to choke, and the buyer outsourcing security MDR says plainly, I don't want white labeled, I want to see that company [3uRf7nlQ_JY @ 00:08:45]. Sophia of TD SYNNEX sits between the camps: after finding a large UK partner outsourcing services to over 300 providers, the pitch is to make the distributor the central point, though Sophia hedges that it is not the one throat to choke kind of methodology [2qmTwyQCa_o @ 00:03:26]. McBain's counterweight is that partnering is a team sport where only one of the seven members may ever collect the money if they do it all.

Route to market splits the same way. McBain treats the cloud marketplace as the new distributor: Canalys predicted AWS would crack the top 10 distributors by 2025, and it got there by the end of 2023, with companies now announcing billion-dollar businesses run through AWS alone [3uRf7nlQ_JY @ 00:13:34]. Russell Rebane of TD SYNNEX answers by digitizing the distributor instead of ceding the storefront, citing 83% of B2B buyers preferring digital channels and building PartnerFirst Commerce, prebuilt ERP connectors, MCP servers and StreamOne Ion, while promising people stay behind the scenes for partners who want them [1Rg0jWCs65Y @ 00:01:16]. The InaCOMP chairman runs the opposite slogan, people first and personalizing IT second, crediting TD SYNNEX's Partner Link community and practice builder tools as the differentiator against other distributors [5GVULJyWQFc @ 00:01:10]. Sophia's case backs the human camp: a five-person SMB reseller started with a five-day Microsoft migration and, ten years on, buys support, managed services and security managed services, and is integrating its services management system into TD SYNNEX's, all worked by the same person throughout [2qmTwyQCa_o @ 00:06:47]. Two live playbooks emerge: go marketplace-native for the digital-first buyer, or win on a named human whose small first job compounds into trust and bigger scope.

Whether to build services in-house is the third fault line. Mark Best of TD SYNNEX refuses to call outsourcing stronger, only more viable, particularly in the early years, arguing that a partner's best experts belong in front of customers on sales, pre-sales and marketing rather than in a 24/7 back office [K3d3674aDZ8 @ 00:03:37]. Best adds the distributor will carry services for a period of time, however long that may be. Nikki Meyer of Pax8 runs a dual track: services delivered on the MSP's behalf, or skilling that teaches the MSP to deliver and monetize those services itself, so headcount never caps what the MSP can sell [Ccgwwzx0ld4 @ 00:06:17]. On breadth, Jessica Ash and Shyla Roquette of TD SYNNEX's high-growth team say the fastest-growing partners are not specialists in one layer but the ones converging infrastructure, cloud, security, AI and services around a single customer outcome [4NI20Et258M @ 00:04:01]. Jeremiah Barnes and Bo Garrett, also at TD SYNNEX, soften that line: a security shop concentrated in one vendor may find the market moving past it, yet they say that is okay, it is about strategy, and not every partner needs the same journey, which is why they run 152 model configurations instead of one ranking [fllmKjJXmBk @ 00:06:38].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The biggest firsthand trap in consumption reselling is fraud liability. Drew Simones, TD SYNNEX's CISO, lays it out: under the reseller agreement, consumption billed to the customer is the reseller's responsibility, and if the customer cannot pay or defaults, the reseller owns the bill [8xAn9TMt7Ew @ 00:03:23]. The hyperscaler is neither inclined nor required to waive costs from fraud or misuse of a customer's account or credentials, and Simones warns that many resellers lack the balance sheet to absorb three, four or five million dollars in fraudulent charges, losses Simones calls material and even existential [8xAn9TMt7Ew @ 00:04:17]. Speed is the killer: attackers can run up millions in days or sometimes hours, well before most organizations notice, and every hyperscaler is exposed to this kind of fraud, which makes it lucrative and attractive to criminals [8xAn9TMt7Ew @ 00:02:02]. The exposure scales with the market shift Jay McBain maps: 96% of the industry is partner-assisted whether sold direct, through a marketplace or indirect, because that is how money flows in a subscription, consumption and now micro-consumption model [SwebwmaZR2I @ 00:02:24]. Simones' bottom line is blunt: you cannot count on the hyperscaler absorbing those losses on your behalf, so awareness, early detection and prevention are the partner's job.

Simones breaks the attack down by vector and warns that criminals are financially motivated to keep inventing new ones. Most common is a compromised credential, almost always on an account with no MFA enabled and often lost to phishing, which the attacker then uses to spin up compute or extend the subscription, frequently for crypto mining [8xAn9TMt7Ew @ 00:05:54]. The vector most likely to land on the reseller's doorstep is impersonation: AI makes it easy to forge synthetic or stolen IDs and research a company convincingly enough to pass onboarding, and when the company does not exist there is nobody to go after except the reseller [8xAn9TMt7Ew @ 00:08:28]. The controls Simones teaches are concrete: MFA on every cloud, administrator and console account, no just-in-case access, budgets with alerts at 50, 90 and 100%, anomaly detection, daily consumption review, an AI agent doing that review if needed, and an immediate call to the customer on any spike, because that uncomfortable conversation beats millions in fraudulent consumption [8xAn9TMt7Ew @ 00:11:49]. Russell Rebane describes the same agent pattern on the order side, a task that runs every day at 8:00 a.m. against a list of 50 orders so the partner has current status before the day starts [1Rg0jWCs65Y @ 00:06:27].

The non-fraud failures are operational. Steven Andis says the constraint is not finding projects but finding people, with security, AI and cloud skills in high demand and short supply, and the TD SYNNEX partner survey flags an AI skills deficit as the core challenge behind the AI opportunity [ULy4t5kvUt0 @ 00:07:43]. Mark Best describes 24/7 managed services as everything underneath the iceberg, the systems, processes and maturity that are just too challenging for partners today, which is why more partners outsource parts of their own service delivery [K3d3674aDZ8 @ 00:03:03]. Best adds that vendors measure TD SYNNEX quarterly against stringent governance and certifications, a big overhead partners would otherwise carry alone. Sophia's large UK partner shows the sprawl cost of the alternative: buying product from one distributor while outsourcing services across more than 300 providers in the UK alone, a management load Sophia calls quite intense [2qmTwyQCa_o @ 00:03:05]. On the sales side, Shyla Roquette flags the single-sale trap: taking a lone Copilot order without asking about data governance, cloud consumption readiness, adoption services and device capability leaves four, maybe five revenue motions on the table, and Jessica Ash adds that the customer is buying all of that technology somewhere, just not from you [4NI20Et258M @ 00:02:37].
