## 1. Classify the revenue rail before pricing the nine mapped plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a signed transaction map, not the marketplace order total. For each enterprise VAR sale, AWS Marketplace channel partner private offer, or Microsoft CSP subscription, record the customer contracting entity, license supplier, party controlling the promised product or service, invoicing entity, and party bearing collection exposure. The canonical scorecard uses gross license resale and service billings with vendor costs included in margin. Its $1,400,000 Base revenue therefore cannot be replaced with $1,400,000 of customer cloud commitments or multiplied by a commission rate without rebuilding the scenario. A genuinely agent-only arrangement requires a separate net-revenue ledger under the applicable principal-versus-agent accounting analysis. On a $100,000 customer order with a $10,000 earned commission, that ledger starts at $10,000, not $100,000. Apply the ASC 606 control analysis to contractual facts rather than assuming every CPPO or CSP transaction has identical presentation. Exclude collected sales taxes from these operating billings and track remittances separately. Preserve the original offer, authorization, purchase order, acceptance evidence, and settlement statement so accounting can reconstruct both revenue and cash without treating a marketplace dashboard as the general ledger. Also separate implementation obligations when their delivery timing differs from the license entitlement.

The nine mapped plays below are chapter-level operating mappings, not a claim that eight additional canonical identifiers were supplied. The supplied identifier, multi_inbox_free_workspace_outbound_clusters, deserves a cost audit rather than an inbox-delivery promise. Its proposed 300-mailbox geometry produces 90,000 monthly messages at fifteen messages per mailbox across twenty sending days, or 150,000 at twenty-five. Those are attempted sends, not accepted deliveries, qualified opportunities, or guaranteed primary-inbox placement. Three hundred retail seats at $6 to $12 cost $1,800 to $3,600 monthly before verification, domains, relay charges, and labor. That $21,600 to $43,200 annual seat expense alone exceeds the Conservative acquisition budget. Microsoft developer benefits must expressly permit the intended commercial activity; isolation across tenants does not create that permission. Cloudflare Email Routing handles inbound forwarding and does not itself supply an outbound sending service. SMTP catch-all behavior cannot verify every individual recipient. Use authorized commercial accounts, documented suppression, and truthful sender identity under 15 U.S.C. 7704. Removing tracking can reduce data collection, but does not establish deliverability or permission. If provider restrictions or complaints emerge, pause and investigate rather than rotating domains to bypass enforcement.

Translate every acquisition and delivery play into an attributable cohort before increasing volume. The illustrative Conservative model contains ten $30,000 annual customer cohorts, Base contains twenty $70,000 cohorts, and Scaled contains twenty-five $100,000 cohorts. These are modeling units that may bundle licenses and services; they are neither vendor price quotations nor promises about actual deal size. Annual acquisition budgets of $12,000, $42,000, and $75,000 imply blended allocations of $1,200, $2,100, and $3,000 per cohort. A diagnostic $3,500 monthly Base acquisition budget buying two wins produces $1,750 acquisition cost per win, before renewal allocations; fewer than two wins changes the conclusion immediately. Keep that diagnostic separate from the annual scenario rather than silently assuming twenty-four wins inside a twenty-cohort model. Record salesperson time, list licenses, paid seats, referral fees, and proposal work even when a particular campaign reports only media expense. Track renewals as retention cohorts, not free new customers. Use HubSpot or Salesforce opportunity identifiers across quote, order, implementation ticket, and cash receipt. Stop a play when realized contribution after its allocated acquisition expense fails the target, even when its response rate looks attractive in the outreach dashboard.

## 2. Reconcile every cohort from gross billings to owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the waterfall as an explicit allocation model anchored to the supplied scorecard, not as evidence that vendors actually offer these discounts. Conservative starts with $300,000, deducts $195,000 of vendor purchases, $6,000 of transaction costs, $6,000 of reversal provisions, and $24,000 of contractor delivery labor, leaving $69,000 contribution. Acquisition consumes $12,000, fixed people and administration consume $27,000, and financing consumes $6,000, leaving exactly $24,000. Base starts with $1,400,000 and subtracts $868,000, $28,000, $14,000, and $98,000 respectively to reach $392,000 contribution; subsequent deductions of $42,000, $126,000, and $28,000 leave $196,000. Scaled starts with $2,500,000, subtracts $1,450,000, $50,000, $25,000, and $175,000 to reach $800,000; $75,000, $190,000, and $35,000 then leave $500,000. The implied vendor-cost ratios improve from 65% to 62% to 58%. That improvement requires demonstrable purchasing economics or a richer service mix; volume alone does not establish it. Keep unearned rebates outside the waterfall until contractual earning conditions are supported. Separate supplier credits from customer credits, since one does not automatically create the other. Every reduction must retain an invoice, contract clause, or documented estimation method.

Read the cohort results before accepting a discounted order. Conservative produces $6,900 contribution and $2,400 owner profit per $30,000 cohort. Base produces $19,600 and $9,800 per $70,000 cohort. Scaled produces $32,000 and $20,000 per $100,000 cohort. Delivery allocations are $2,400, $4,900, and $7,000 respectively. Illustrative workloads of forty hours at $60, seventy hours at $70, and one hundred hours at $70 reproduce those amounts exactly. Contractor counts describe roster size, not full-time equivalents: three contractors may share intermittent implementation tasks, while four support the Scaled book. The canonical two full-time roles in Conservative cannot both receive ordinary market salaries from a $27,000 fixed-cost envelope. This reconstruction therefore assumes owner-operated full-time roles compensated through the residual, with actual paid support and administration inside the stated expense lines. Scaled can include a paid third full-time role within its larger fixed envelope. Owner draws are distributions of the residual, not an additional hidden expense. Before adopting the plan, compare the residual with each owner's required income and add replacement salaries in a separate economic-return view. Do not present the Conservative case as a comfortably staffed salaried operation.

Build the scrub and reversal provision from separate failure buckets rather than one unexplained haircut. Track offer cancellations, duplicate seat credits, disputed implementation acceptance, uncollectible receivables, supplier clawbacks, and marketplace settlement corrections independently. For Base, a 1% net expected-loss allowance on $1,400,000 creates $14,000 of modeled expense. A diagnostic decomposition of $8,400 customer credit exposure, $3,500 credit losses, and $2,100 unrecovered supplier adjustments reconciles that allowance without implying these probabilities were observed. Estimate each bucket from exposure, event likelihood, recoverability, and claim timing, then replace assumptions with cohort evidence. Classify actual credits, bad debts, and supplier costs in their appropriate accounts; the waterfall groups their economic effect for comparability. When a reserved credit becomes real, consume the existing allowance rather than charging the same loss again. An accounting provision is also not a segregated bank balance. A marketplace withholding cash creates a liquidity asset or receivable question unless the amount is actually lost. Reconcile beginning exposure, new provisions, utilized losses, recoveries, and closing exposure monthly. For planning, read owner profit as the modeled operating residual before personal taxes, distributions, debt principal repayment, and reinvestment; confirm the final entity-level treatment with the actual books.

## 3. Fund the supplier clock and the payout clock separately  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Measure float from actual cash events. A customer invoice dated day zero, supplier payment due on day fifteen, and usable customer cash on day sixty create forty-five days of supplier-funded exposure. Under a deliberately smooth annual purchasing assumption, vendor purchases divided by 365 and multiplied by forty-five require approximately $24,041 Conservative, $107,014 Base, and $178,767 Scaled. Those amounts fund vendor principal only; they exclude payroll, contractor deposits, acquisition spend, taxes awaiting remittance, and liquidity buffers. They also understate peak exposure when annual licenses close together. A single Base cohort requires $43,400 for its supplier invoice, so three simultaneous supplier payments demand $130,200 before any other cash outflow. Customer DSO and marketplace payout delay are different clocks. If the customer pays on day sixty but the marketplace releases usable funds on day seventy-five, the supplier gap is sixty days. Do not add a payout delay again when the reported collection date already measures bank availability. Maintain invoice date, contractual due date, customer payment date, settlement eligibility date, and bank receipt date as separate fields. Build a weekly thirteen-week cash schedule from those fields and order-level supplier obligations rather than applying one blended DSO statistic to every transaction.

A fifteen-day extension increases steady-state vendor funding by approximately $8,014, $35,671, and $59,589 across the three cases. The corresponding sixty-day vendor bridges are $32,055, $142,685, and $238,356. Separately, a prudent illustrative month of nonvendor cash overhead includes delivery, acquisition, fixed expense, and financing: $5,750 Conservative, $24,500 Base, and $39,583 Scaled. Adding that cushion to the forty-five-day vendor bridge yields roughly $29,791, $131,514, and $218,350 before restricted cash and taxes. These are planning combinations, not substitutes for the weekly peak model, and rounded component totals can differ by a dollar. The supplied $25,000 to $150,000 startup capital range can support some early structures but does not automatically finance the Scaled book. Its $5,000 to $25,000 monthly operating range likewise does not cap expanded delivery and growth spending. If a platform hypothetically withholds 10% of a $70,000 eligible payout for ninety days, another $7,000 is temporarily unavailable; use the actual agreement rather than assuming that withholding exists. Never label the holdback a second revenue deduction unless its recovery deteriorates. Model committed facility availability after covenants, borrowing-base exclusions, and existing draws, not the headline credit limit printed in a proposal.

Improve float through contract design before borrowing against avoidable mismatches. A customer deposit of $43,400 could fund the illustrative Base supplier invoice, but receiving it does not by itself establish earned revenue. Alternatively, align supplier installments with customer billing or negotiate later supplier payment dates before executing the order. Avoid offering monthly customer payments against an irrevocable annual supplier obligation unless the funded exposure fits the cash plan. For federal transactions, 31 U.S.C. 3902 and FAR 32.904 address payment timing, but a proper invoice, acceptance, and the applicable contract clauses still matter; a government customer name does not convert every invoice into cash on a fixed date. Keep procurement order numbers and acceptance artifacts with the receivable. If using receivables finance, confirm assignment restrictions, permitted notice procedures, eligible account definitions, and recourse terms. Federal claims require particular attention to 31 U.S.C. 3727 and any applicable statutory exception. Track financing cost against average actual draws and unused-line fees, not simply gross billings. The waterfall financing budgets are allocations that require this subsequent validation. A profitable order should be deferred when its supplier due date would breach minimum cash, rather than funded through overdue payroll or withheld customer tax collections.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the order-level cash record
   - Attach the accepted quote, supplier commitment, customer payment terms, and settlement rules to one cohort identifier.
   - Record exact dates and distinguish customer payment from usable marketplace cash; retain the agreement version supporting each assumption.
2. Schedule every committed outflow
   - Place supplier invoices, contractor milestones, acquisition commitments, tax remittances, and financing charges into the thirteen-week forecast.
   - Keep noncash loss provisions outside cash outflows until settlement while separately tracking restricted balances and expected recoveries.
3. Stress collection and settlement timing
   - Move usable receipts fifteen and thirty days later and cluster the largest supplier invoices into their actual due weeks.
   - Compare the worst weekly balance with committed drawable credit and the minimum operating cash floor before accepting another exposed order.
4. Close the receipt-to-ledger reconciliation
   - Match bank receipts to customer invoices, marketplace deductions, supplier credits, and reserve utilization every week.
   - Escalate unexplained differences with transaction identifiers and revise forecast timing immediately rather than carrying stale expected receipts.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four cash conditions before accepting the order**
> 
> Release the commercial commitment only when the evidence supports the order-level cash path and contractual authority.
> - Confirm current reseller authorization, registered customer scope, permitted license use, and the exact supplier cancellation exposure.
> - Match customer billing milestones, supplier due dates, marketplace payout terms, and any withholding against available committed cash.
> - Confirm delivery capacity, acceptance criteria, contractor payment timing, and the minimum contribution after fully allocated acquisition expense.
> - Verify the purchasing entity, procurement authority, tax treatment, and necessary data-processing terms without assuming the marketplace supplies missing permissions.

## 4. Stress the margin thesis and define the stop-loss response  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run sensitivities against individual economic drivers, not only the final margin percentage. Holding other modeled dollars fixed, a one-percentage-point customer price concession reduces annual profit by $3,000, $14,000, and $25,000. A 3% increase in supplier purchase costs instead reduces profit by $5,850, $26,040, and $43,500 because its denominator is vendor purchases rather than revenue. A two-percentage-point increase in net reversal losses costs $6,000, $28,000, and $50,000. A 20% contractor delivery overrun costs $4,800, $19,600, and $35,000. These first-order shocks deliberately hold transaction fees and other allocations constant to expose the direct impact; a production forecast should recalculate contractually variable deductions after repricing. Combining the supplier increase, reversal deterioration, and delivery overrun leaves only $7,350 Conservative profit, $122,360 Base profit, and $371,500 Scaled profit. Those outcomes are stresses, not revised canonical scorecards. Respond by reducing unpriced service scope, recovering supported supplier credits, or renegotiating customer terms before relying on additional volume. More sales can amplify losses when the incremental license spread is inadequate. Rebuild the service mix and labor assumptions whenever vendor policy changes remove the pricing advantage that originally supported the order.

Separate liquidity shocks from loss shocks in the management dashboard. Fifteen extra collection days do not immediately reduce earned revenue, but financing the added vendor principal at an illustrative 12% annual rate costs approximately $962, $4,281, and $7,151 per year if the higher funding balance persists. The resulting profits become approximately $23,038, $191,719, and $492,849 before any associated collection losses. This is incremental interest above the waterfall budget, not a replacement estimate for the entire financing line. If the delay lasts only one quarter, apply the rate to the actual additional balance and days outstanding rather than charging a full year. A simultaneous customer default is a separate loss event and may consume both the reserve and additional equity. Acquisition deserves similar discipline: doubling the annual CAC allocation while holding cohort count fixed leaves $12,000, $154,000, and $425,000. Do not attribute all pipeline creation to the final email or referral touch. Compare campaign spend with collected cohort contribution, and measure sales-cycle length against the supplied three-to-twelve-month first-deal range. An attractive eventual payback can still exhaust a thinly capitalized reseller before the first usable settlement arrives.

Make the monthly review a decision meeting with traceable exceptions. Produce one report showing quoted contribution, booked contribution, recognized contribution, collected cash, supplier exposure, reserve utilization, and actual contractor hours by cohort. Renewal alerts should surface pricing changes before commitments become irreversible; quote automation should reject stale vendor price books; license reconciliation should expose disputed seats before the customer invoice ages. These automations improve control only when exception ownership is explicit. For covered financial-institution customers, 16 CFR 314.4(f) can drive service-provider oversight and contractual safeguards; it does not automatically make every software reseller a covered financial institution. Scope implementation access to necessary systems, price required security work, and identify the party responsible for customer data before promising a fixed implementation fee. Finally, distinguish contribution available for growth from cash available for owner distribution. Maintain the minimum cash floor, fund known supplier commitments, and settle accrued operating obligations before drawing the residual. If actual salary requirements or procurement costs exceed these allocations, publish the lower result rather than preserving a target margin through omitted expenses. The scorecard remains the comparison anchor; the ledger determines whether the business has actually earned it.
