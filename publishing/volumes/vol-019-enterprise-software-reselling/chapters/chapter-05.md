## Executive Summary

AWS, Microsoft and channel operators teach one resale model: the ISV authorizes a discounted wholesale price, the reseller marks it up, wraps in services and custom terms, and the marketplace collects and disburses, with AWS eliminating purchase orders and partner invoicing. AWS's worked CPPO example turns a $100 list into a $70 wholesale and a $91 customer price, leaving the partner $21; Microsoft's CSP example has a reseller keeping $70 on a $120 sale while Microsoft takes a 3% agency fee. AWS cash lands between the 7th and 10th of the month after invoicing, with buyers averaging 25 days to pay. WeTransact pays resellers 20% or 30% recurring margin. The live divergence is packaging: AWS multi-product offer sets versus Microsoft multi-party offers versus CSP distributor networks like Pax8.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every practitioner channel lands on the same skeleton: the software vendor hands a reseller a discounted wholesale price, and the reseller wraps margin, services and custom terms around it before a single customer sees a number. AWS compresses the whole loop into four verbs: authorize, customize, transact and report. On AWS, the ISV authorizes a consulting partner to resell its listing at a discount off public list price, which becomes the partner's wholesale price, and multiple discount types can run for different time windows to cover repeat deals [OhzDVC956Os @ 00:02:15]. AWS frames the channel partner as seller of record, owning the contractual and financial relationship from start to finish, with custom recurring and negotiated pricing [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:01:05]. Alison Johnson of the AWS Marketplace channel team adds that becoming seller of record means signing the same Marketplace terms vendors sign when they list, after which a vendor can authorize you on a one-time basis for a high-value negotiated opportunity or on a recurring discount across many customers [WP9Y1cRxhAI @ 00:03:24]. Microsoft runs the identical play through its cloud solution provider program: the ISV creates a private offer carrying margin to the reseller, and the reseller keeps the entire customer relationship, including pricing [yC9d52PsuOg @ 00:00:34].

The entry gates are hard and every platform states them up front. On AWS, the ISV must already run a paid listing; private offers built on bring-your-own-license or free products are not supported, the reseller must be registered through the Marketplace Management Portal as a seller, and the end customer must be an AWS customer to accept [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:01:43]. Microsoft's private offer demo lists its own checklist before you touch Partner Center: an existing public offer, the customer's billing account ID and email contacts, and the name of the customer administrator who can complete the purchase [REkZmdc2hYE @ 00:00:19]. On the multi-party side, Darren Sharp of Microsoft's UK ISV team is blunt that the ISV has to have a transactable solution before a services partner can be electronically authorized to sell it, and Sharp positions the services partner as the agent in the private offer flow [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:10:25]. Professional services entered the resale lane later: Archna of AWS explains that channel partner private offers originally supported software resale only, which forced buyers to purchase services directly from providers and negotiate with several of them, a gap the services resale launch closed [dkBRJN0WvX8 @ 00:03:09].

Practitioners agree the deal velocity comes from killing procurement friction. AWS's channel pitch is that no purchase order is required, the customer clicks a link to its offer, and Marketplace purchases help retire AWS spend commitments, with dedicated AWS resources for go-to-market and demand generation [OhzDVC956Os @ 00:01:38]. Sharp says buying through Azure Marketplace decrements the Microsoft consumption commitment by 100% of the software spend, which Sharp calls market leading among hyperscaler marketplaces, and the multi-party flow keeps that 100% decrement [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:07:15]. GP, CEO and founder of stackArmor, reports that finding and provisioning Marketplace software that once took two weeks now takes two minutes, and that customers now expect the firm to play the role traditionally held by a value-added reseller, turning stackArmor into a one-stop trusted source of cloud services [GMejbloTwGE @ 00:03:02]. Jared Smith of Pax8 pitches the channel to ISVs as a way to cut customer acquisition cost and lift lifetime value, because the partner network takes on tier one support, reducing the vendor's support costs too [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:16:39]. Across all four voices the reseller's real product is trust plus a frictionless checkout, with the software riding along inside it.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The unit economics are taught with exact worked examples. AWS's CPPO walkthrough runs a $100 list price, a 30 percent ISV discount that sets wholesale at $70, and a 30 percent reseller markup that lands the customer price at $91; once the customer pays, AWS pulls the $70 wholesale and disburses the remaining $21 to the channel partner, with VAT or other taxes stacked on top of the customer price at invoicing [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:10:08]. The same session confirms the partner earns the identical margin percentage on overage invoices as on upfront or scheduled charges, and that the AWS processing fee on a CPPO deal is quoted by your channel account manager, who responds within a business day [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:13:55]. Microsoft's CSP margin-sharing example runs on different numbers: on a $100 offer with a 50% ISV margin resold at $120, the reseller keeps $70, $50 of margin share plus $20 of markup, while the remaining $50 goes to Microsoft, which takes a 3% agency fee of $1.50 and passes $40.50 to the ISV [yC9d52PsuOg @ 00:02:38]. For resold professional services, Archna demonstrates a 10% markup layered on the seller's price inside the Management Portal [dkBRJN0WvX8 @ 00:12:07].

Cash timing is where operators win or lose the forecast. AWS invoices customers on the third of the month, Marketplace charges ride on the monthly AWS bill under AWS net terms, and payments are disbursed to ISVs between the 7th and 10th of the following month [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:12:39]. The same team warns that buyers typically pay within an average of 25 days because of their intake processes, so build that buffer into revenue forecasts [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:12:57]. Leanna Mijarez of AWS breaks invoicing down further: invoicing can trigger immediately on subscription, only private offers can carry a flexible payment schedule split monthly, quarterly or annually, usage products roll into the bill issued on the second or third, and invoiced accounts carry standard net 30 terms with some customers getting net 45 or 60 [vKzo7FINzss @ 00:02:42]. The worked example in that session is a $120,000 contract billed at $10,000 a month. On the services resale side, Archna notes sellers can choose a daily or monthly disbursement cadence, with the customer paying AWS on the agreed schedule and AWS paying out wholesale cost and margin [dkBRJN0WvX8 @ 00:06:13]. The takeaway: price the deal and the installment schedule together, because the schedule decides when your margin actually lands.

The tooling layer is what lets a small deal desk push volume. The AWS partner CRM connector puts resale authorizations inside Salesforce: the demo shows an authorization valid until October 31st, 2025 that supports multiple customized offers, a reseller contract the partner must accept before creating an offer, a markup percentage field that auto-calculates the customer's final price, optional custom terms, and an expiration date before the offer flips to succeeded [pOUxsKDRzvM @ 00:03:06]. The ISV-side connector demo adds comma-separated AWS account lists for multi-account offers and a quarterly installment builder that splits a $4 annual contract into four $1 payments [O_1B3vFfVJ4 @ 00:04:06]. Archna's services resale flow lets the reseller target up to 24 AWS account IDs per offer and attach duration, payment schedule, statement of work and expiration [dkBRJN0WvX8 @ 00:15:00]. Sid of the AWS Marketplace product team shows the new multi-product offer set, which wraps one private offer per component, each carrying its own pricing model, whether pay-as-you-go, fixed upfront or the new time-and-materials option for services [cOyZpsRxxE4 @ 00:31:32]. The buyer still accepts each component individually but approves one total contract value and can cut a single purchase order for the whole amount.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the resale authorization
   - Register as a Marketplace seller and get the ISV to issue a recurring or one-time authorization opportunity at an agreed wholesale discount.
   - Once the ISV creates the authorization opportunity in the Marketplace Management Portal it is immediately available to the channel partner, so chase the ISV, not AWS, for speed [Amazon Web Services — PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:13:38].
2. Price the offer on top of wholesale
   - Enter the customer's AWS account number, accept the reseller contract, set your markup percentage and attach any custom terms.
   - The partner CRM connector calculates the customer's final price from your markup and the seller's wholesale price, and lets you add your own terms beside the seller's EULA [Amazon Web Services — pOUxsKDRzvM @ 00:03:06].
3. Build the payment schedule and expiry
   - Configure a flexible payment schedule, set the offer expiration date, and save as draft if details are still open.
   - An offer that is not accepted by its validity date expires, so set the acceptance deadline deliberately and preview the generated installment schedule before creating the offer [Amazon Web Services — O_1B3vFfVJ4 @ 00:04:41].
4. Deliver the link and forecast the cash
   - Email the offer link with the targeted account ID, then track acceptance and reports in the portal.
   - Forecast collections with a buffer because buyers pay within an average of 25 days, ahead of the 7th-to-10th disbursement window [Amazon Web Services — PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:12:57].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first fork is margin architecture. On AWS, the ISV sets the reseller's floor by discounting off list, and the channel partner stacks markup on top. Amazon Web Services walks a $100 list price through a 30 percent ISV discount to a $70 wholesale price, then through a 30 percent partner markup to a $91 customer price. Once AWS pulls the wholesale cost, $21 is left for the partner [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:10:08]. Microsoft's CSP margin-sharing flow runs the other way. The ISV grants a percentage off its own price through a private offer, and the reseller sets pricing outside the marketplace. In Microsoft Cloud's worked example, a $100 offer carries a 50 percent margin and is resold at $120. The reseller keeps $70, Microsoft takes a 3 percent agency fee of $1.50 on the remaining $50, and the ISV is quoted $40.50 [yC9d52PsuOg @ 00:02:38]. WeTransact's chief revenue officer Juan argues that raw margin is only one of three levers. The other two are back margin, paid retrospectively when a quota is reached, and a services allocation for work such as onboarding or frontline support [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:31:46]. WeTransact's own tiering pays 20 percent recurring when a reseller brings a lead that still needs WeTransact's sales participation. It pays 30 percent when the reseller brings a qualified deal that closes without that help [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:32:05].

The second split is over who actually builds the offer. Alison Johnson of the AWS Marketplace channel team describes a concierge model. Once authorized, the partner fills out a transaction request form that captures final customer price, start and end dates, and billing details, and the AWS customer desk builds the custom offer in 24 hours or less [WP9Y1cRxhAI @ 00:04:23]. The operational walkthrough from Amazon Web Services shows partners creating offers themselves from the offers tab in the AWS Marketplace Management Portal. It also warns that the automated path does not cover every CPPO scenario, and partners stuck on creation are told to reach out [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:04:55]. Sales-driven teams push the work into Salesforce instead. The partner CRM connector surfaces shared resale authorizations, applies a markup percentage against the seller's wholesale price, and calculates the final customer price inside the CRM before the offer status changes to succeeded [pOUxsKDRzvM @ 00:03:06]. On Microsoft, everything runs through Partner Center, where One-Step Purchasing is enabled by default. The demo explains that disabling it forces customers to accept the private offer before purchasing, as an extra layer of governance [REkZmdc2hYE @ 00:01:17]. Each route trades hands-on control against offloaded effort, and operators pick based on deal volume and on where their sellers already work.

Third, practitioners split on packaging. The classic CPPO motion is one product per private offer. It runs under either a recurring authorization, which lets the partner keep issuing offers to multiple customers without renegotiating, or a one-time discount scoped to a specific buyer [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:03:41]. At re:Invent 2025, AWS Events presenters Sid and Vincent pitched multi-product solutions as the end of stitching deals together by hand. SCC's zero-trust endpoint bundle combines three CrowdStrike products, two Zscaler products, and SCC's own services. Before multi-product solutions, it would have required six separate private offers transacted one by one [cOyZpsRxxE4 @ 00:38:10]. Vincent also cites data that partners with diversified offerings and comprehensive professional services command a 6.4x revenue multiplier [cOyZpsRxxE4 @ 00:15:39]. Under the hood, the bundle is not a single contract. Asha explains that each component keeps its own private offer, wrapped inside an offer set, so pricing, EULA, and duration can differ per product [42TotVxOC44 @ 00:12:06]. Juan at WeTransact argues that the recruiting pitch to resellers should lead with business rather than product. On WeTransact's call to TB Cloud, the offer was 20 percent or 30 percent of the deal before any product training happened, because product learning follows once the revenue is visible [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:35:03].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

Forecasts break on the cash cycle. Amazon Web Services lays out the calendar. Customers are invoiced on the third of the month, marketplace charges follow the customer's standard AWS net payment terms, and payments are disbursed to ISVs between the 7th and 10th of the following month [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:12:39]. The same walkthrough warns that, because of unique intake processes, buyers typically pay within an average of 25 days, so that buffer belongs in every revenue forecast [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:12:57]. Leanna Mijarez's invoicing breakdown adds that only credit card purchases settle immediately. Invoiced buyers sit on standard net 30 terms, and some negotiate 45 or 60 days [vKzo7FINzss @ 00:02:42]. Flexible payment schedules stretch the timeline further. The example given is a $120,000 contract invoiced at $10,000 a month [vKzo7FINzss @ 00:01:44]. Fees are also hard to see in advance. When a partner asks how to find out the AWS processing fee on a CPPO transaction, the answer is to contact the AWS channel account manager and wait up to a business day for a reply [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:14:32]. On Microsoft, rerun the margin-sharing math yourself. The narrated example takes $1.50 off the remaining $50 and says the ISV receives $40.50, but $50 minus $1.50 is $48.50 [yC9d52PsuOg @ 00:02:38].

Most transaction-level traps are mundane, but they still kill deals. The AWS operational walkthrough flags that BYOL and free products cannot run through CPPO at all, because the ISV must have a paid listing [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:01:43]. Buyers must be logged into the exact AWS account the offer targets before the link works. That is why the partner's email should carry both the offer link and the account ID [PkznZxwv3eg @ 00:08:13]. Offers can also die quietly. The private offer demo states that failing to accept by the validity date leads to offer expiration, and that incomplete offers can be saved as drafts [O_1B3vFfVJ4 @ 00:04:41]. Microsoft Cloud's Partner Center demo adds that the purchase must be completed by someone with the appropriate permissions on the customer's Azure account [REkZmdc2hYE @ 00:02:42]. Before starting, the seller needs an existing public offer, the customer's billing account ID, and the name of the customer administrator [REkZmdc2hYE @ 00:00:19]. Professional services were a long-standing gap. Archna from Amazon Web Services says CPPO originally supported only software resale. Buyers had to purchase services directly, negotiate with several providers, and buy part of the solution inside the marketplace and the rest outside it [dkBRJN0WvX8 @ 00:03:27].

Reseller programs fail for predictable reasons, and WeTransact's Juan lists them. Some vendors pick a market that isn't ready or never check which customers the reseller actually sells to. Others make an offer too thin to bring any value to the reseller's sales force. Often nobody follows up on either side, and the lack of tools to work efficiently makes all of this worse [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:30:15]. On timing, the answer given is that tactical work with an MSP in an already developed market can show sales within one to two months, while global partnerships take longer [jJAskh3xNOM @ 00:38:04]. Back-office leakage is the next hole. Xtributor checks every invoice three ways: what you bill, what your distributors bill you, and what your books say. Wrong prices, quantity gaps, and missing invoices surface as exceptions, and fixing them pushes billing accuracy past ninety-nine percent [XAc9mTh05so @ 00:01:27]. Partner program cost is the final economics check. On the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program channel, one partner puts the annual benefits package investment at around $345. Another describes roughly $30,000 in value from a $4,000 package and about $150,000 a year in internal licensing avoided, while a third claims 15 to 25x ROI [RQe8RvOAGRw @ 00:02:54].
