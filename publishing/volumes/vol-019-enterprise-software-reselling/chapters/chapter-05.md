## 1. Break the Nine Plays into Verifiable Rights and Reversible Economics  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with the permission chain, not the discount sheet. Authorized VAR resale fails when an enthusiastic salesperson mistakes distributor access for permission to sell every vendor, geography, customer class, and subscription edition. AWS Channel Partner Private Offers, or CPPO, require the relevant selling authorization and offer configuration; an attractive wholesale price does not establish customer eligibility or control settlement timing. Microsoft Cloud Solution Provider, or CSP, requires the correct partner relationship, customer agreement, and subscription commitments. For each quote, archive the applicable program terms, authorization identifier, customer legal entity, tenant or account identifier, currency, term, cancellation rights, and distributor purchase conditions. A $100,000 sale against an $88,000 supplier commitment appears to create $12,000 before other costs, but a rejected customer purchase can leave the entire $88,000 payable. These are illustrative transaction figures, not alternative scorecard cases. The fallback is a nonbinding estimate until eligibility and acceptance evidence exist, followed by a customer commitment aligned with the supplier obligation. If the vendor will not permit the intended route, sell separately scoped implementation work rather than routing the license through another customer's account. Keep the evidence attached to the transaction, not buried in a salesperson's inbox.

The next traps are expired deal registration, duplicate opportunities, conditional rebates, and private offers priced against the wrong settlement basis. Deal-registration protection is a contractual benefit with conditions; it is not ownership of the customer. Record registration expiry and whether renewals, affiliates, expansion seats, or competing partners receive different treatment. Suppose a $120,000 order costs $108,000 before an expected $6,000 incentive. The transaction has $12,000 of initial spread, not $18,000 of secure contribution. Missing proof of customer acceptance can erase the incentive after commissions have already been paid. Treat incentive eligibility, submission, acceptance, settlement, and possible recovery as separate ledger states. AWS and distributor fees vary by route and agreement; never copy a percentage from a different seller's screenshot. For Microsoft subscriptions, verify the current cancellation and seat reduction windows for the actual product and commitment rather than promising monthly exit because billing occurs monthly. Freeze the specific SKU quote when the rights evidence expires. A valid fallback is a revised price without the incentive, a shorter commitment where available, or customer-funded prepaid exposure. A competitor's willingness to promise unsupported terms is evidence of risk, not a pricing benchmark.

Implementation spreads, managed renewals, license reconciliation, and procurement enablement complete the ordinary commercial workstreams; the ninth play, the supplied 340B workflow, receives separate treatment below. Each needs independent acceptance criteria. A $30,000 implementation budgeted at 120 contractor hours costing $100 each leaves $18,000 before internal labor and overhead. An additional 100 unpaid hours remove $10,000 immediately. Require a statement of work that defines migration volume, integrations, identity configuration, test cases, and the customer dependencies that pause the schedule. Managed renewals need written renewal authority, not an assumption that last year's purchase order repeats. License reconciliation needs permitted administrative access and exportable entitlement evidence, not shared global credentials. Procurement enablement must distinguish purchasing assistance from representing that the reseller holds a government schedule or manufacturer certification. Selling evaluation keys, misstating end customers, duplicating rebate claims, inventing certifications, or creating replacement accounts after suspension can produce termination, recovery demands, intellectual-property claims, and permanent exclusion under applicable program terms. Preserve logs and use the documented appeal process instead. The circuit-breaker is simple: when a promised right cannot be demonstrated, stop that promise while continuing separately authorized support and data-return obligations.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the Spread Before It Enters the Sales Dashboard**
> 
> Experienced channel operators separate commercial enthusiasm from evidence that survives settlement and audit.
> - Save a dated copy of the applicable authorization and offer economics with every CPPO or CSP transaction; retain the original file when terms later change.

> - Quote migration exceptions as unit-priced additions, including each extra source tenant, integration, data batch, and customer-caused remobilization.

## 2. Reconcile Gross Billings to Owner Profit and Cash at Risk  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the canonical scorecard without silently switching revenue definitions. Conservative is $300,000 of gross license resale and service billings at 8% net margin, yielding $24,000 annual owner net profit with 2 FT + 3 C. Base is $1,400,000 at 14.0%, yielding $196,000 with the same staffing count. Scaled is $2,500,000 at 20%, yielding $500,000 with 3 FT + 4 C. Total implied annual costs are therefore $276,000, $1,204,000, and $2,000,000 respectively. Those totals include vendor costs; subtracting license purchases again from the stated net profit would double count them. Conversely, license spread is not owner profit because payroll, contractors, service delivery, insurance, collections, and overhead still consume it. The staffing labels describe headcount, not five or seven fully utilized premium salaries. Validate actual hours, compensation, and owner-labor treatment before adopting the model. Gross billings are the supplied planning basis; statutory reporting may require a principal-versus-agent assessment by transaction. A commission-only arrangement does not become gross resale revenue because the customer's software spend is large. Maintain a bridge between the scorecard basis, booked revenue, supplier pass-throughs, and cash receipts so apparent growth cannot hide a deteriorating spread.

Model a marketplace transaction as a waterfall with explicitly assigned fees. On an illustrative $100,000 customer charge, assume $88,000 supplier cost, $2,000 contractually assigned marketplace and distributor charges, $3,000 delivery labor, and $1,000 sales compensation. Contribution is $6,000 before shared overhead, tax treatment, and any owner-profit adjustments. These assumed charges are not published AWS or Microsoft tariffs. If the customer receives a $5,000 credit that the supplier does not match, contribution falls to $1,000. If a separately expected $4,000 rebate is rejected, the transaction never earned that incremental amount; do not describe it as cash already secured. Store each fee against its payer, calculation base, settlement event, refund treatment, and source agreement. Currency movements, withholding, indirect taxes, and minimum distributor charges require separate fields rather than a miscellaneous percentage. Where a marketplace deducts a charge before remittance, reconcile gross customer consideration to the settlement statement and bank deposit. Where the customer purchases directly and pays the reseller only for services, exclude the vendor's license sale from reseller billings. The fallback for an unpriced fee is a corrected quote, not an assumption that implementation margin will absorb the shortfall.

Slow collections can kill a profitable model before the first renewal. At Base annual billings, average monthly sales are about $116,667. Under a simplified steady-state model in which all sales are credit sales, sixty days of receivables using annual revenue divided by 365 implies roughly $230,137 outstanding. That exceeds the upper $150,000 startup-capital reference before considering inventory-like supplier commitments or service payroll. It is exposure, not a precise funding requirement: payables, deposits, marketplace settlement mechanics, and actual sales seasonality change the cash gap. Build a thirteen-week cash forecast from dated obligations instead. A $50,000 unrecovered customer balance equals about 25.5% of Base annual owner profit if the associated costs remain payable and no recovery offsets the loss. Define credit limits using committed supplier cost plus unpaid services, less cleared deposits and contractually usable credits. Do not count a disputed rebate as available liquidity. Require intervention when projected unrestricted cash falls below the next thirty days of unavoidable obligations. Fallbacks include deposits, milestone billing, reduced incremental exposure, and vendor-approved customer-direct licensing with a separate service contract; existing service duties remain governed by their actual terms.

## 3. Fence the 340B Workflow and Public-Sector Representations  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The supplied play section_340b_contract_pharmacy_spread_arbitrage is a specialized customer workflow, not permission for a software reseller to acquire drugs or collect pharmacy reimbursement. Under 42 U.S.C. § 256b, eligible covered entities receive ceiling-price protections subject to statutory conditions, including diversion and duplicate-discount prohibitions. Eligibility must be established for the particular entity and relevant sites; being a rural hospital or treating an eligible population alone does not establish participation. Federally Qualified Health Centers, qualifying Ryan White entities, and hospitals must be evaluated under their applicable eligibility categories. The statutory ceiling-price formula is not a universal 25–50% discount promise, and commercial reimbursement is neither uniform nor necessarily retained in full. Manufacturer restrictions, payer adjustments, acquisition cost, dispensing costs, claim reversals, and contract terms determine actual economics. The reseller's authorized opportunity is implementing split-billing software, interfaces, reconciliation, and controlled administrative workflows. Do not book drug acquisition value, insurer reimbursement, or the covered entity's retained spread as software revenue. Require the covered entity to own patient-eligibility determinations, inventory policies, and exception resolution. Tele-referral capture cannot substitute for a qualifying care relationship or manufacture eligibility through a software flag. Test exception paths against the current program requirements and the customer's documented clinical and pharmacy operating model.

Use transparent unit economics to expose the boundary. Assume, solely for illustration, a pharmacy transaction receives $8,000 in payer and patient collections against $4,000 drug acquisition, $300 dispensing and fulfillment, and $200 other attributable expense. The remaining $3,500 belongs in the pharmacy and covered-entity contract reconciliation before further allocations; it is not reseller income. A software operator charging $30 per processed eligible claim across 200 monthly claims bills $6,000 monthly, or $72,000 annually. At $4,000 monthly directly attributable delivery cost, that example leaves $24,000 annual contribution before shared overhead, not the Conservative scenario's owner profit merely because the dollar amounts coincide. Flat fees do not automatically satisfy 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b); referral remuneration, federal-program business, and any applicable safe harbor require separate assessment. Do not price tele-referrals as purchased patient volume. The proposed fallback of an entity-owned specialty pharmacy requires its own licenses, payer arrangements, operational capacity, and entity-specific eligibility analysis. Automated 340B ESP submissions may support manufacturer-specific processes where required and permitted, but uploading claims does not automatically unlock restricted NDCs. If the route remains unavailable, suspend the affected workflow instead of substituting another entity's identifiers.

Data-processing and procurement failures can create losses far larger than software spread. When the reseller acts as a HIPAA business associate, execute the required agreement, bind relevant subcontractors, and implement access, audit, incident, and termination procedures appropriate to its actual role. A business-associate agreement does not itself authorize every analytics or manufacturer-data submission. Keep patient records out of ordinary partner-ticket attachments; use approved secure channels and the minimum data necessary for the task. For public procurement, verify solicitation-specific requirements, applicable contract clauses, reseller representations, and acceptance evidence. Knowingly false material claims or statements connected to government payment can implicate 31 U.S.C. § 3729; a vendor badge cannot cure a fabricated certification. FAR 52.212-4 and 52.232-25 matter only where applicable to the actual contract, and subcontract payment rights do not automatically mirror the government's obligations. A $75,000 accepted-looking invoice still creates a cash hole if the authorized buyer rejects the delivery record and the supplier has already billed $66,000. Preserve the order, authorized acceptance, license assignment, and submission receipt as distinct artifacts. If evidence is defective, correct the record and payment request rather than backdating acceptance or borrowing another partner's authorization.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Pre-Flight Checks Before Irreversible Commitments**
>

> - Verify current reseller authority, exact end-customer identity, offer scope, commitment term, and cancellation rights before issuing a binding supplier order.

> - For 340B implementations, obtain covered-entity ownership of eligibility rules, pharmacy operating responsibilities, authorized data uses, and exception handling.
> - Confirm truthful procurement representations, applicable customer acceptance requirements, secure administrative access, and any required business-associate or subcontractor agreements.

## 4. Instrument Early Warnings and Contain Failure Without Creating Another Breach  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build a daily exception ledger joining CRM opportunity, registration, quote revision, customer order, supplier order, subscription identifier, service ticket, invoice, settlement, and bank receipt. Salesforce or HubSpot can hold commercial records; a controlled SQL database or spreadsheet can perform the initial reconciliation without an expensive integration project. AWS Marketplace reports, Microsoft Partner Center exports, and distributor statements provide different evidence and must not be treated as interchangeable. Store original currency, transaction date, fee category, and the source document behind every adjustment. Start with operator-selected thresholds, then recalibrate using actual contract economics: review contribution erosion greater than two percentage points, receivables more than fifteen days past their contractual due date, and any registration expiring within thirty days. These are internal intervention settings, not vendor enforcement standards. A customer may legitimately have longer payment terms; measure lateness against the agreed date rather than calling every sixty-day balance delinquent. Separate leading indicators from realized losses. An unresolved license-assignment mismatch is a delivery risk; an accepted supplier credit is a potential offset; a cleared refund changes cash. Assign an owner, next action, and deadline to each exception so the dashboard produces decisions rather than a colorful backlog.

Use circuit-breakers that match the defect and avoid cascading damage. Any unverified administrative identity, exposed privileged credential, unsupported end-customer substitution, or unexplained duplicate entitlement warrants immediate security review and a hold on related new provisioning. That does not authorize disabling a customer's existing production service contrary to contract. Contain access through supported procedures, preserve evidence, and coordinate continuity with the customer and vendor. Alert when service hours exceed 110% of budget before acceptance, forecast contribution falls below the written deal floor, or conditional rebates represent more than 25% of expected transaction contribution. The rebate threshold flags dependence; it does not establish an accounting recognition rule. Where a $20,000 expected contribution includes $8,000 conditional incentives, the dependency is 40%, and the transaction must also survive the $12,000 no-incentive case. Put a separate watch on customer concentration and supplier concentration because one account's payment dispute and one vendor's policy change can hit simultaneously. When a channel account is suspended, stop offers requiring that account, secure customer records, and pursue the documented review process. Do not create nominee accounts, misrepresent ownership, or transfer transactions to hide the suspension; those shortcuts enlarge the original contractual and operational exposure.

Run a weekly loss review and a monthly reversal simulation. Take the ten largest open exposures, remove all conditional rebates, delay the largest collection by thirty days, and insert one customer refund that the supplier refuses to match. Recalculate cash, contribution, and staffing capacity without changing the published scorecard cases. These sensitivities describe resilience around the Conservative, Base, and Scaled plans; they are not replacement forecasts. Track both dollars and aging for unmatched credits, rejected incentive claims, unaccepted implementations, and vendor-policy exceptions. A falling exception count can conceal rising exposure when the remaining cases are larger. Require documentary closure: a bank receipt closes collection risk, an accepted credit closes the relevant payable adjustment, and verified customer acceptance closes that delivery milestone. A salesperson's assurance closes none of them. Retain alternate authorized distributors where commercially sensible, exportable entitlement records, and customer-specific transition instructions so vendor disruption does not become hostage-taking. Finally, compare actual profit with the $24,000, $196,000, and $500,000 annual targets using consistent cost classification. If growth requires repeated exceptions to credit, authorization, or delivery controls, reduce new commitments and repair the unit economics. More bookings cannot repair a transaction design that loses money whenever its promises are tested.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture the transaction evidence bundle
   - Join customer acceptance, vendor authorization, supplier obligation, and settlement terms before commitment.
   - Assign one transaction identifier across CRM, quote revisions, marketplace offers, distributor records, and the accounting ledger; preserve originals with controlled access.
2. Calculate exposure without contingent benefits
   - Recompute contribution and dated cash requirements with conditional incentives removed.
   - Include delivery effort, assigned channel charges, refund mismatches, currency exposure, and supplier commitments; compare the result with the written deal floor.
3. Contain the defective activity precisely
   - Suspend the affected new commitment and assign a responsible commercial, delivery, or security owner.
   - Keep existing contractual obligations visible; preserve records, contact the authorized counterpart, and avoid unsupported account substitutions or unilateral service interruption.
4. Close against external evidence
   - Resume affected activity only after the specific defect has a documented resolution.
   - Use cleared funds, accepted supplier credits, verified authorization, corrected access, or valid customer acceptance; record residual exposure in the weekly loss review.
