## 1. Assign Nine Plays to Five Seats Before Expanding to Seven  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the starting pod around two accountable employees and three bounded contractor engagements, not five interchangeable generalists. For this chapter, the nine-play execution map is P1 reseller authorization, P2 deal registration, P3 AWS Marketplace channel partner private offers, P4 Microsoft CSP subscription operations, P5 quote generation, P6 implementation delivery, P7 license reconciliation, P8 renewal management, and P9 collections. FT1 is the owner-led commercial principal: they own P1, P2, pricing exceptions, vendor relationships, and material customer commitments. FT2 is the revenue-operations and customer success lead: they own P4, P5, P7, P8, and the collections queue under P9. Contractor C1 supplies certified solution architecture for P3 and technical scoping; C2 delivers P6 through accepted milestones; C3 maintains the accrual ledger and collection evidence supporting P9. No contractor independently changes payment instructions, reseller eligibility, or contract commitments. Use a HubSpot company record as the commercial index, Jira Service Management for delivery evidence, and QuickBooks Online or an equivalent accrual ledger for accounting. Store the vendor account identifier, customer tenant identifier, contract identifier, and renewal anniversary on every opportunity. Those identifiers connect all nine plays without making the founder the only person who can reconstruct a transaction.

Separate ownership from execution so that absence does not become a service failure. FT1 approves a quote's commercial spread, while FT2 verifies the customer entity, quantity, term, billing frequency, and underlying authorization. C1 documents architecture assumptions before C2 commits implementation hours; C2 returns test evidence before FT2 marks delivery accepted. C3 reconciles the customer receivable, distributor payable, marketplace settlement, and bank receipt without possessing unilateral bank-release authority. Keep approval evidence in the deal record rather than an employee's mailbox. For AWS CPPO, record the selling authorization, permitted offer economics, expiration, and applicable seller-of-record arrangement; do not assume Microsoft CSP has the same money flow or transfer rules. In CSP, identify whether the business is an indirect reseller or direct-bill partner and which party owns provisioning, support, billing, and cancellation obligations. Escalate conflicting portal data before sending the customer an executable quote. Maintain two individually assigned administrators with least-privilege access and tested recovery procedures; never solve continuity by sharing credentials. A weekly absence drill should let the backup operator locate the signed order, accepted scope, next renewal action, and outstanding receivable within ten minutes. Record failures as specific missing artifacts and close them before another customer enters the same workflow.

Expand to three employees and four contractors only when workload, service quality, and cash support the added capacity. The third employee becomes the dedicated renewals and customer-success manager, taking P8 and routine P7 follow-up from FT2. The fourth contractor supplies independently scoped integration or testing capacity for P6, rather than becoming an uncounted permanent support desk. Use a four-week trigger: more than thirty accepted renewal actions waiting beyond two business days, or implementation commitments exceeding eighty percent of contracted delivery capacity, combined with adequate cash for the signed vendor obligations and the incremental staffing commitment. These are operating triggers, not additions to the canonical profit forecast. The Conservative case still has two full-time seats and three contractors; its $24,000 annual owner profit leaves little room for rework, recruiting errors, or richer compensation. Model actual wages, payroll burden, contractor hours, and owner duties before treating that case as a viable launch budget. Full-time seats are real work commitments, not permission to omit labor costs. Contractor engagements need defined outputs and genuine independence; labels alone do not determine worker status. Review federal tax classification under 26 U.S.C. section 3121(d), applicable wage rules, and relevant state tests before establishing recurring arrangements.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make Vendor Knowledge Portable Before the Founder Takes Leave**
> 
> The valuable asset is the documented operating relationship, not a founder's memory of which channel manager answers quickly.
> - Attach the actual CPPO selling authorization and offer expiration to the deal record; a saved price screenshot does not establish continuing resale rights.
> - Export CSP subscription identifiers and scheduled renewal changes into the operating register before staff changes; portal access alone does not explain customer intent.
> - Require C1 to document the rejected architecture alternative and its cost consequence, making replacement scoping faster and preventing repeated presales labor.
> - Run one founder-absent renewal every month using named backup operators, then repair the missing permissions, contract evidence, or customer introductions it exposes.

## 2. Buy Accepted Contractor Outputs and Run a Weekly Exception Scorecard  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Compensate contractors for defined deliverables while retaining a transparent view of effective hourly cost. An illustrative architecture statement of work pays $1,200 for discovery, a written design, a license bill of materials, and one revision within an agreed scope. An illustrative implementation engagement pays $4,800 against three accepted milestones: twenty percent for the tested plan, fifty percent for configured staging and test evidence, and thirty percent for production acceptance and handover. Those amounts illustrate procurement mechanics; they do not replace the scorecard's expense envelope. The agreement must define the customer inputs, review window, objective acceptance tests, change pricing, and payment deadline. Pay undisputed accepted work on time, and separate a specific defect dispute from unrelated invoices. Use a five-business-day review window and document whether acceptance was granted or which contracted criterion failed. For architect scoping errors or implementation defects within the original scope, specify a correction obligation with an agreed response target. Do not make the contractor insure vendor outages, customer scope changes, or disputed sales promises. Track rework hours against the responsible cause so a seemingly cheap contractor cannot hide costs inside FT2's workload or the owner's unrecorded nights and weekends.

Set service levels around business consequences rather than demanding constant availability from every contractor. A critical production defect requires acknowledgment within four contracted coverage hours, an incident owner, and a restoration plan; restoration time remains dependent on the failure and the agreed scope. A standard quote should reach internal review within two business days after complete inputs arrive. License reconciliation exceptions should have an owner within one business day and a documented resolution or vendor escalation within five. Define coverage calendars, time zones, escalation contacts, and customer dependencies explicitly. Measure first-pass acceptance as deliverables accepted without contractor-caused correction divided by deliverables reviewed; target at least ninety-five percent over a rolling quarter, displaying the denominator beside the percentage. Require every contractor creating code, scripts, templates, or documentation to sign an assignment covering the commissioned deliverables. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a copyright ownership transfer generally requires a writing signed by the owner or their authorized agent. Do not assume paying an invoice transfers copyright. Identify background tools, third-party components, and customer-owned materials separately, with sufficient licenses for continued operation and a permitted business transfer. Collect assignments before delivery begins and reconcile them to the repository and acceptance record at closeout.

Run a forty-five-minute Monday scorecard using a locked Friday snapshot. FT2 publishes the dataset; FT1 chairs exception decisions; contractors attend only the portions covered by their engagements. Start with cash: overdue receivables, vendor payments due within thirty days, disputed settlements, and contribution remaining after direct vendor and contractor costs. Then inspect renewal coverage at 120, 90, 60, and 30 days, quote cycle time, first-pass implementation acceptance, and unmatched licenses. Finish with concentration and missing transfer evidence. Never reward total license billings alone: a large discounted renewal can increase revenue while reducing cash and owner profit. Define gross-profit retention on a fixed opening renewal cohort, using retained gross profit before expansion; report expansion separately so a large new tenant cannot disguise customer losses. Measure days sales outstanding on the same gross or net accounting basis as the underlying receivable and credit sales. Assign each exception one owner, one next action, one deadline, and one economic exposure. Carry unresolved items forward visibly rather than rebuilding a cleaner-looking list. A red metric triggers an intervention, such as shortening credit terms or revising a scope template, not an automatic contractor penalty unsupported by the signed agreement.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the Friday operating snapshot
   - Export opportunity, subscription, delivery, receivable, and vendor-payable records.
   - FT2 records the extraction time, reporting period, source systems, and stable identifiers; C3 reconciles cash-related totals before publication.
2. Validate definitions and identify material exceptions
   - Recalculate denominators, inspect missing identifiers, and price each unresolved exception.
   - Separate scope changes from contractor defects, customer collections from marketplace settlements, and renewal losses from expansion gains.
3. Assign corrective work in the Monday review
   - Give every exception one accountable owner, one deadline, and one next action.
   - FT1 approves commercial decisions; FT2 records the decision and routes only contracted deliverables to external specialists.
4. Close with source evidence and preserve history
   - Attach acceptance, settlement, correction, or escalation evidence before changing an item to closed.
   - Retain the original snapshot and dated resolution so a buyer can reproduce the operating history without interviewing the founder.

## 3. Reconcile Accrual Cohorts and Cap Every Material Dependency  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Anchor the ledger to the canonical scenarios before adding operational detail. Conservative gross revenue is $300,000, with an eight percent net margin and $24,000 annual owner net profit; the implied total cost envelope is $276,000. Base gross revenue is $1,400,000, with a fourteen percent net margin and $196,000 owner net profit; the implied costs are $1,204,000. Scaled gross revenue is $2,500,000, with a twenty percent net margin and $500,000 owner net profit; the implied costs are $2,000,000. Vendor license costs sit inside those envelopes, together with delivery, labor, overhead, and other modeled expenses. Do not treat these gross billings as a commission-only revenue stream, and do not add a customer's wider cloud consumption or software estate to operator revenue. The scorecard uses gross license resale and service billings; actual reporting requires a contract-specific principal-versus-agent assessment under the applicable accounting framework. If a particular offer belongs on a net presentation, show a separate reconciliation rather than silently changing the canonical case. Keep customer cash advances, earned service revenue, vendor obligations, and marketplace receivables distinct. A bank balance boosted by prepaid subscriptions is not automatically distributable owner cash, especially when the related supplier payment or delivery obligation remains outstanding.

Close each month by contract, customer, vendor, and renewal cohort. Preserve original booking date, service period, invoice date, cash date, vendor cost, marketplace deductions, implementation cost, and the evidence supporting recognition. Recognize implementation work according to the applicable performance obligations, not merely because C2 submitted an invoice. Record customer prepayments as liabilities until the relevant recognition conditions are met; record unpaid earned amounts and supplier accruals consistently. Match credits, cancellations, incentives, and disputed rebates to their originating contracts so a cleanup month does not appear to be a sudden margin collapse. Treat contingent vendor incentives separately until their contractual and accounting conditions are satisfied. For renewal analysis, freeze the opening customer cohort and calculate retained license gross profit, lost gross profit, pricing changes, and incremental implementation contribution as separate bridges. A $100,000 license invoice with $88,000 vendor cost creates $12,000 before delivery and overhead, not $100,000 of recurring contribution. If a related implementation sells for $15,000 and consumes $9,000 of direct delivery cost, its $6,000 contribution is a distinct service spread. These illustrative units explain the ledger; they are not additional revenue assumed in any canonical scenario. Reconcile all cohort totals back to the closed general ledger before circulating them to lenders, prospective buyers, or the operating team.

Set a twenty-five percent maximum for any customer or common-control customer group on both trailing-twelve-month revenue and gross profit. Apply the same maximum to material independent counterparty dependencies: vendor-attributable gross profit, distributor purchase obligations, and marketplace settlement receivables. Calculate each category separately; averaging them conceals a single point of failure. Vendor groups under common control belong together, and two distributors selling the same indispensable publisher do not eliminate publisher risk. A young specialist may initially exceed a cap because the first few customers or one platform dominate. Report the actual breach, freeze discretionary increases in that dependency where commercially feasible, and assign a dated diversification plan rather than declaring the business diversified. Maintain existing contractual service commitments while redirecting new acquisition. Distinguish concentration from absolute cash exposure: a small overdue marketplace balance can threaten payroll even when its percentage looks acceptable. Stress-test a sixty-day collection delay, loss of the largest renewable gross-profit account, and a vendor discount reduction against signed payables and staffing commitments. Use those tests to size cash reserves and credit terms; do not quietly reduce the stated profit cases. A buyer should see historical breaches, corrective actions, and current exposure, not a chart that removes inconvenient accounts from the denominator.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify Four Preconditions Before Hiring, Distribution, or Buyer Outreach**
> 
> Proceed only with documented evidence. A missing record is an assigned remediation task, not a favorable assumption.
> - Confirm active reseller eligibility, applicable CPPO selling authorization or CSP role, customer license terms, and the actual change-of-control or assignment provisions.
> - Reconcile the closed ledger to contract cohorts, unpaid vendor obligations, marketplace settlements, and the canonical gross-billings presentation without adding customer consumption.
> - Verify signed contractor IP assignments, background-component licenses, customer acceptance evidence, and appropriate access rights for every material deliverable.
> - Calculate customer and counterparty concentration against the 25% caps, document any breach, and confirm cash coverage under a sixty-day collection-delay stress test.

## 4. Price Transferable Earnings, Then Choose an Exit or Durable Distributions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Value the business on transferable earnings rather than the face value of licenses passing through it. For initial negotiation planning, an owner-operated small reseller might be tested at two to three times verified seller's discretionary earnings; a more transferable operation with defensible recurring gross profit might justify testing three to four times. Treat these as illustrative screening bands, not observed transaction comparables or an entitlement. A strategic buyer may instead price adjusted EBITDA, customer contribution, or a portfolio of transferable renewals. Do not multiply the $196,000 Base owner net profit by an EBITDA multiple without reconciling the earnings definitions. Build the bridge from the closed ledger: identify owner compensation already expensed, genuine nonrecurring items, interest, depreciation, taxes under the chosen metric, and the recurring cost of replacing necessary owner work. Include contractor normalization if historical delivery depended on discounted introductory rates. For an owner-operator buyer, SDE typically adds back one working owner's compensation; for a manager-run valuation, replacement leadership cost remains a real expense. Explain which buyer and operating model each bridge serves. A documented renewal engine can support stronger pricing, but it cannot convert cancellable vendor permissions into perpetual assets or erase a concentration breach that threatens future contribution.

Use explicit assumptions when translating a screening multiple into dollars. If diligence establishes that normalized SDE equals the Base case's $196,000 owner profit, a two-to-three-times range produces $392,000 to $588,000 of illustrative enterprise value. That equality is an assumption to verify, not an accounting identity. If the Scaled operation's $500,000 owner profit likewise equals verified SDE and its transfer evidence supports the stronger three-to-four-times screening band, the illustrative enterprise value is $1,500,000 to $2,000,000. Do not present either range as proceeds available at closing. Bridge enterprise value to equity proceeds using the agreed treatment of cash, debt, debt-like obligations, transaction expenses, escrow, and normalized working capital. Define whether distributor payables and customer prepayments belong in working capital or another purchase-price category, and prevent double counting. A renewal earnout should specify the opening cohort, measurement period, gross-profit definition, collection requirements, permitted pricing changes, and buyer obligations that affect retention. Prefer measures the seller can inspect over an unrestricted buyer-wide profit metric. Test how vendor repricing, customer cancellation, and delayed marketplace settlements change the payout before agreeing to a headline price that depends heavily on future contingent consideration.

Build the transfer room while operating, not after signing an exclusive sale process. Index customer contracts, reseller and distributor agreements, authorization histories, renewal cohorts, signed assignments, delivery acceptance, incident history, employee responsibilities, and contractor replacement plans. Keep commercial diligence separate from production access: share redacted evidence under an executed confidentiality agreement and restrict customer data to the diligence purpose. Identify which contracts require assignment consent or notice and which vendor programs require fresh partner qualification. Selling shares does not automatically bypass change-of-control provisions. For United States federal contracts, examine 41 U.S.C. section 6305 and FAR 42.1204 when evaluating whether recognition of a successor requires novation; do not assume commercial customer assignment rules apply. If retaining the company, use the same evidence to run a durable distribution policy: close the ledger, fund taxes and signed obligations, reserve for collection delays and delivery commitments, then distribute genuinely available cash. Review owner hours quarterly and charge replacement labor in the internal operating model even when the owner performs the work. The strongest cash cow and the strongest sale candidate share one characteristic: customers renew because the pod reliably delivers and reconciles value, not because the founder personally rescues every quote, invoice, implementation, and renewal.
