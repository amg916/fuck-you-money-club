## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every reseller in this chapter agrees that the public sector buys through vehicles, not handshakes. Clovity's leadership, working alongside Carahsoft's Atlassian team, puts it bluntly: 'Commercial is all about relationship and public sector is all about efficiency.' Leveraging an existing contract vehicle can 'quadruple' that efficiency [fwAcWt5Q8oA @ 00:06:37]. Mid-size firms struggle to hold vehicles themselves. That is why Carahsoft fields a dedicated contracts and redline team and absorbs co-terming, multi-year deals, and renewals so the solution partner never has to [fwAcWt5Q8oA @ 00:05:30]. AWS Public Sector shows the same logic inside Marketplace. A reseller's private partner offer runs through the same flow as buying direct from the vendor. The step the demo calls 'most important' is replacing commercial terms with the 'naso' contract, a university systemwide contract, or one-off bid terms [F4gkR0s1Blw @ 00:03:26]. FEDSched adds the baseline vocabulary that operators keep fumbling. 'GSA schedule,' 'GSA number,' and 'GSA list' all mean the same Multiple Award Schedule contract. It is organized into categories and SINs, and vendors can propose at any time [FnZjjws-i_s @ 00:00:01]. The baseline prerequisite is simple: the contract paper carries the public sector deal, not the sales rep. The Atlassian 'triangle' of vendor AEs, distributor, and solution partner exists to keep that paper unified.

Two independent GSA trainers land on the same money mechanics. Robin, a 14-year GSA veteran in the Acquisition Workforce Training Branch, explains that GSA receives no congressional appropriation and runs on the Industrial Funding Fee. The fee is 0.75% of each transaction. The customer pays it, it is embedded in contract pricing rather than shown as a line item, and the vendor collects and remits it [NVfgGOTkm4Q @ 00:02:34]. Jennifer Shouse, teaching with the Virginia PTAP, repeats the 0.75% figure and tells vendors to treat GSA as a 'for-profit entity.' Then comes the hard truth. The schedule 'is absolutely absolutely not required to do business with the government' and is a 'marketing tool only.' It touches only about 10-15% of federal purchases, and more than 60% of the roughly 18-20k holders report zero schedule sales [p3DsAuDE32c @ 00:10:31]. GovClose's former Air Force procurement officer confirms the optional status from the buyer's chair: GSA is currently optional, and that officer never used it while in uniform [WZkYFEEekIk @ 00:03:34]. FEDSched reports that over $30B a year flows through MAS but warns that holding the contract does not hand anyone sales [FnZjjws-i_s @ 00:01:37]. The shared conclusion is that the schedule is a door, not a pipeline. Operators who win treat the award as permission to sell and then do the selling themselves.

On the risk side, the practitioners converge on one rule: paper and records are the only controls that survive an exam. De Leon, after 32 years at the OCC, says regulators would tell institutions 'you can outsource the function, but you can't outsource the risk.' The Ncontracts panel adds that policies that are not enforced get cited [GsLnrTOe4bw @ 00:17:08]. Virtualization Options compresses the same idea into contract language: 'If it is not written in the contract, it simply does not exist.' Its fix list therefore includes SLA penalties as a percentage of monthly value and data export within 30 days of termination [jVnAwe2gfHg @ 00:06:31]. Rahul Tyagi extends the logic to the exit. Organizations obsess over onboarding questionnaires while rushing offboarding, yet the relationship only ends 'when all access is removed and the risk is fully closed,' not when the contract expires [TjkI3hqYZJY @ 00:01:34]. devguard is candid about what tooling does and does not do. Its register 'is a record, not a gate,' it does not stop anyone from using a blocked supplier, and risk comes only from your own ratings [3j2StNSWzVs @ 00:08:01]. Every speaker places the judgement on the operator. Neither the software, the vendor's self-attestation, nor a bare certification carries the decision.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The AWS Public Sector demo lays out the exact build for a private partner offer. The reseller creates an offer and selects the vendor and product; nationwide resellers are often authorized for 'thousands or tens of thousands' of items. The reseller then enters the customer's AWS account ID, sets a term from 1 to 60 months to match the outside agreement, and loads license counts and the negotiated discounted price, for example after winning a bid [F4gkR0s1Blw @ 00:01:34]. Payment scheduling is where the deal gets shaped to government budgets. The buyer can pay everything upfront on any date or spread up to 60 payments over five years. Amounts can vary, two payments can land in one month, and a buyer can pay a small amount now and push the remainder past the fiscal-year change [F4gkR0s1Blw @ 00:02:10]. On the buyer side, the agency verifies the term, dates, licenses, discount, and payment schedule, adds a PO, and confirms the T&Cs. It can attach the 'naso' agreement, award letter, winning bid from a three-bid competition, and PO. License keys then download immediately instead of arriving by email days or weeks later [F4gkR0s1Blw @ 00:06:51]. Clovity layers co-terming on top so that migrations and cutovers never drop service [fwAcWt5Q8oA @ 00:02:06].

Jennifer Shouse's virtual class gives the most granular schedule build in the set. FEDSched frames the proposal as administrative, technical, and pricing sections [FnZjjws-i_s @ 00:00:38], and Shouse walks through each one. Pricing starts with research on CALC for services and GSA Advantage for products. A vendor billing a project manager at $200 against a CALC average of $100 may not fit the schedule, and one CALC screenshot showed a PM average of $140/hr [p3DsAuDE32c @ 00:24:08]. The workaround is tiered labor categories: PM1 at $75, PM2 at $100, and PM3 at $150. These are backed by a justification hierarchy of invoices first, contracts second, and proposals last [p3DsAuDE32c @ 00:41:39]. The administrative section needs 24 months of balance sheet and income statement, with no audit required, plus an SF 527 bank reference. It also needs the one-hour Readiness Assessment and the 30-minute Pathway to Success, each valid for 12 months, and an IdenTrust certificate at $100-300; Shouse says to buy the three-year option. The technical section wants 3-5 references (aim for 5), and product resellers need a Letter of Supply. Shouse calls the Commercial Sales Practices disclosure the most important document [p3DsAuDE32c @ 01:17:17].

Post-award, the mechanics shift to tracking and controls. Robin's reportable-sale test has three conditions: contract items, an eligible user, and no evidence the customer intended to use another contract. On a mixed $1,000 order, the vendor reports the $500 table and never the $500 fish tank, because reporting open-market items means paying IFF out of the vendor's own pocket [NVfgGOTkm4Q @ 00:22:53]. Reporting runs through 72A for quarterly summary data or through the piloted FAS Sales Reporting/TDR for monthly line items. Assessors pull transactions two to three years old, so sales and admin staff both need training [NVfgGOTkm4Q @ 00:31:04]. Ayden Connor's payment-fraud routine is equally mechanical. Never use contact details from the change request itself, and call the vendor on a known number instead. Enforce dual approval and separation of duties. Refuse payment if the vendor denies the request, and delay if the vendor is unreachable [fOUGaSjlTBY @ 00:00:17]. devguard handles the evidence layer. Upload the ISO 27001 certificate and the DPA, and remember that 'Valid' only means the date has not passed. Documents surface under Deadlines 90 days before expiry. Check that the certificate's scope actually covers the service you buy [3j2StNSWzVs @ 00:05:11].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Benchmark and Tier Your Pricing
   - Pull CALC comparables for services and GSA Advantage comparables for products. Then build tiered labor categories that sit inside market averages.
   - Research rates on CALC and GSA Advantage, and flag any rate far above the CALC average, such as $200 against $100. Use tiers like PM1 $75 / PM2 $100 / PM3 $150, backed by invoices first and contracts second [How To Sell To & Win Federal Government Contracts — p3DsAuDE32c @ 00:19:16].
2. Assemble the MAS Proposal Package
   - Confirm eligibility, then compile the administrative, technical, and pricing sections.
   - Confirm healthy financials, at least two years in business, past performance, and TAA compliance. Then gather 24 months of financials, an SF 527 bank reference, 3-5 references, and a Letter of Supply for product resellers [Federal Schedules, Inc. | FEDSched — FnZjjws-i_s @ 00:00:55].
3. Build the Private Partner Offer on Public Terms
   - Create the offer in AWS Marketplace and enter the customer account ID, term, licenses, discount, and payment schedule. Then replace the commercial terms.
   - Swap the commercial terms for the 'naso' contract, a university systemwide contract, or one-off bid terms. Reissue any offer that expires, and let the buyer attach the award letter, winning bid, and PO [AWS Public Sector — F4gkR0s1Blw @ 00:03:26].
4. Track Reportable Sales and Lock Payment Controls
   - Apply the three-part reportable-sale test to every order and harden vendor payment changes.

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether a reseller needs a GSA Multiple Award Schedule at all, and the answer depends on which practitioner is counting the money. Federal Schedules, Inc. sells the market size, with over $30 billion per year flowing through MAS, while admitting that sales are not guaranteed and that approval runs from months to over a year [FnZjjws-i_s @ 00:01:37]. Jennifer Shouse of How To Sell To & Win Federal Government Contracts sizes the dead weight instead: roughly 18,000 to 20,000 schedule holders, with 60 percent or more logging zero sales through the schedule. That is why the class frames MAS as a marketing tool rather than a requirement [p3DsAuDE32c @ 00:10:31]. GovClose speaks as a former Air Force procurement officer who never used GSA. That speaker doubts the March 2025 executive order centralizing common procurement will land well given the layoffs. Their playbook is to get a schedule for simple services, IT, and office supplies, keep a backup plan, and use OTAs as the faster lane [WZkYFEEekIk @ 00:07:43]. GSA's own trainer Robin counters on speed, pegging procurement cycle time at about 10 days through GSA versus about 270 days on the open market [NVfgGOTkm4Q @ 00:08:51].

On routing public sector deals through a master distributor, the Atlassian sessions and the exposé channel describe the same machine in sharply opposite terms. Kevin Howard of Carahsoft and the Clovity team pitch the aggregator as the efficiency layer. In their account, Carahsoft absorbs co-terming, multi-year deals, and renewals, sometimes turns paperwork faster than going to Atlassian directly, and passes discounts down to the client [fwAcWt5Q8oA @ 00:03:36]. Boring, But Billions frames that same chokepoint as the problem. It describes the alleged scheme behind the September 2024 raid this way: Carahsoft bought software licenses at 40 percent wholesale discounts, then sold them to federal agencies at full catalog prices [ZFZMcTSC-0M @ 00:04:47]. The rule-of-three quoting setup it describes allegedly let favored sub-resellers win against inflated rival quotes. The AWS Public Sector demo offers a third lane that cuts the paper chase. A reseller builds a private partner offer, picks the vendor and product, and keys in the customer's AWS account ID and the negotiated price. Then comes the step the presenter calls most important: replacing commercial terms with the naso contract, a university systemwide contract, or one-off bid terms [F4gkR0s1Blw @ 00:03:26]. On the buyer side, license keys download immediately instead of arriving by email days or weeks later [F4gkR0s1Blw @ 00:06:51].

Vendor oversight splits between trusting paper and verifying behavior. De Leon and Zurlin on the Ncontracts panel concede that big cloud, CRM, and 365 providers will not renegotiate their contracts. A regulated firm therefore falls back on diligence and documentation instead of leverage it does not have [GsLnrTOe4bw @ 00:15:30]. DevOps Simplified runs a harder clock on that documentation: any report or assessment older than 365 days gets flagged. The fictional vendor Brightwage shows up with a report 380 days old and an assessment at 423 days, and Cloudnest has no right-to-audit clause at all [YF1T0fd9wPE @ 00:01:14]. devguard pushes the judgement back onto the buyer even harder. It warns that a certificate marked valid only means its date has not passed, and that the document surfaces under Deadlines 90 days before expiry. The operator still has to check that the certificate's scope covers the service actually being bought [3j2StNSWzVs @ 00:05:11]. Virtualization Options takes a contract-first line over the documentation-first line, arguing that if it is not written in the contract, it simply does not exist. The channel builds the negotiating team around a TEAM framework in which nobody disagrees in front of the supplier [jVnAwe2gfHg @ 00:06:31].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The schedule's money traps are mechanical, and they hit after award. Jennifer Shouse warns about the price reduction rule: any non-schedule rate below the GSA rate forces the GSA rate down. In practice, you are not going to be allowed to offer anybody outside of GSA a rate lower than your GSA rate [p3DsAuDE32c @ 00:12:11]. Robin at GSA flags a self-inflicted loss on the reporting side. The 0.75 percent Industrial Funding Fee is embedded in contract pricing, so reporting open-market items or travel as schedule sales means paying that fee out of the vendor's own pocket [NVfgGOTkm4Q @ 00:11:31]. Shouse also calls the Commercial Sales Practices disclosure the most important document in the pricing section. The warning is to separate standard from non-standard discounts. A one-time 15 to 20 percent Boeing discount is the kind of deal that must be broken out, and an 80 percent revenue concentration gets flagged as a problem [p3DsAuDE32c @ 01:22:00]. The bottleneck that catches small shops is time lag. Assessors review transactions two to three years old, which is why Robin says both sales and admin staff need training on what counts as a reportable sale [NVfgGOTkm4Q @ 00:29:52].

Contract lifecycle failures carry hard dollar costs, and three practitioners put numbers on them. Leegality's cleanest economics check: one company missed a cancellation window, the agreement auto-renewed, and the company was held liable for 75 percent of the cost of the remaining contract. That loss is the predictable result of tracking renewals in an untracked Excel sheet with no assigned owner, which is the gap the obligation module's owner and reminders are meant to close [DeATzRK3azc @ 00:03:22]. Virtualization Options shows how vendors claw back headline concessions. A 15 percent discount gets recovered through escalators, 90-day auto-renewal windows, and volume commitments, so all 12 levers have to be treated as tradeable rather than celebrating the discount line [jVnAwe2gfHg @ 00:03:46]. The same channel's procurement-bypass session spells out the personal stakes once a vendor routes around procurement with executive briefings, pilots, internal champions, and false urgency. The exposure includes fraud claims and civil penalties, criminal exposure when kickbacks are involved, and personal liability for the unauthorized buyer who signed [rJtYtwh2Ees @ 00:02:32]. The threshold trap sits right behind it. Anything over $100,000 per year requires competitive bidding, and the speaker tells buyers to avoid single-source awards made to dodge that threshold [rJtYtwh2Ees @ 00:04:10].

The back-office failure modes are where money and data walk out the door. SecureOps with Ayden Connor sets three rules for bank-detail change requests. Never use contact details from the change request itself, pause and call the vendor on a known number, and run dual approval with separation of duties [fOUGaSjlTBY @ 00:00:17]. If the vendor denies making the request, don't pay; if the vendor is unreachable, delay. Once the money has already gone, notify the bank, knowing that recovery is not guaranteed [fOUGaSjlTBY @ 00:01:10]. Rahul Tyagi flags the offboarding gap that most teams leave open after rushing through onboarding questionnaires and approvals. APIs and automated workflows can stay live even after user accounts are removed, so a terminated vendor can still be wired into production. Integrations therefore have to be killed explicitly alongside the VPN, SSO, and privileged-account revocations [TjkI3hqYZJY @ 00:01:01]. The Ncontracts panel closes the loop on the exam side. Examiners request the incident response program, MSP contracts, and risk assessments, and policies that exist on paper but are not enforced are what actually get cited [GsLnrTOe4bw @ 00:28:51].
