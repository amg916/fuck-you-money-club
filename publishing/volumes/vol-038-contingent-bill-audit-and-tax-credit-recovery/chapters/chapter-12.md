## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus: The Other Side Only Audits in Its Own Favor

The baseline every recovery operator starts from is blunt: when the other side audits you, it is hunting underpayments, not handing back overpayments. PM Business Advisors says state auditors typically focus only on underpayments, so someone inside the company or a third-party consultant has to identify the overpayments and recover them from the state or the vendors [ePf76grjr8U @ 00:00:16]. Carr, Riggs & Ingram calls state audits pro-state by design, with agents who are not going to consider the pro-taxpayer provisions of the law. That is exactly why a reverse audit run during a recent, pending or live state audit puts someone in the room on the company's side [HpOuVeLrWBs @ 00:01:03]. Cherry Bekaert sales tax partner Lauren Stinson describes the work as a sweep of every purchase against the exemptions states already grant manufacturers on machinery, repair parts, production supplies and utilities, asking whether the company is actually taking them [S7Qlojol4pE @ 00:03:17]. Her client Mike O'Donnell, a financial analyst at Carlisle Interconnect Technologies, is running cost-saving initiatives after his plant cut about 40 percent of its workforce. The reverse audits had already delivered a substantial refund from the state of Florida, cash in the door when cash was king [S7Qlojol4pE @ 00:05:12]. Nobody on the other side volunteers that money.

The second point of agreement is the time window and the fee model. PM Business Advisors anchors on the statute of limitations, typically three years and four in some states. It says an ongoing audit that reaches past the statute opens refunds as far back as it goes, which is how the firm has worked old audits seven to ten years deep [ePf76grjr8U @ 00:00:30]. Workers compensation recovery runs on the same clock. SMP Consulting Group, pitched by Greg Walker, hunts incorrect governing codes, misclassified employees, overlooked credits and misreported losses that inflate the experience modification rate. It then recovers the overpayments made over the last three years while lowering the rate going forward [sM254iPKhSo @ 00:00:52]. Both shops sell on pure contingency. PMBA says its projects run 100 percent contingent with no risk to the client and that it does all the work until the check is in hand. SMP says it only gets paid after the refund is in the client's pocket [sM254iPKhSo @ 00:02:20]. Carr, Riggs & Ingram adds that the odds grow with size: the larger the company and the more purchases and transactions it runs, the higher the probability that it has overpaid state sales and use tax [HpOuVeLrWBs @ 00:01:55].

The same logic runs through lease true-ups and carrier contracts: the bill or the offer is the other side's estimate, and you only win by checking it against hard records. Commercial Real Estate Escalations Made Simple defines CAM reconciliation as comparing monthly estimated charges with the landlord's actual spend, so a tenant billed $12,000 against $13,500 of real cost owes $1,500. It tells tenants that a good lease gives them the right to audit, including the landlord's general ledger, invoices for big-ticket items and definitions of controllable expenses [CWHu33HRBVY @ 00:01:42]. Coastline Equity warns that the landlord or management company must keep unit-specific renovation costs out of the common-area pool [u1Y-WxOKXpc @ 00:04:57]. On the FedEx side, Route Consultant says the most important thing in a mid-contract renegotiation is showing FedEx why its previous rates were wrong or need updating, backed by unique characteristics, receipts and a written case [-AnzYDP3LQw @ 00:06:25]. Amy Byers, who runs five P&D contracts, adds that good standing is what earns a contractor the right to a seat at the table at all. Contractors who are not in good standing just get told what FedEx will pay them to keep working [39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:02:39].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

PM Business Advisors lays out its sales tax recovery run in order. It pulls all of the client's data for the last three years, pushes it through its proprietary tool BART to flag refund opportunities, collects the supporting documentation, which is typically the invoice, and assembles the refund claim [ePf76grjr8U @ 00:00:59]. The claim gets reviewed with the client. Only once both sides agree the items are true overpayments does PMBA go to the company's vendors directly or to the state, doing 100 percent of the work until the check is in hand [ePf76grjr8U @ 00:01:15]. Cherry Bekaert's client confirms how light the lift is on the company side. The firm's team did the heavy lifting while the client only handed over schedules, even though much of the invoice paper lived in boxes [S7Qlojol4pE @ 00:04:31]. Atlantax Systems, which has run outsourced state and local compliance since 1991, shows how fast it onboards the related monthly filing work. Within 30 days its team validated the billing output data format, opened a client-specific bank account, mapped the returns in its proprietary Tax Pack compliance software and started processing jurisdictional returns [v9ZkvElAVE4 @ 00:01:28]. The same desk also sells transactional tax rating, exemption certificate management and reverse audits.

Route Consultant's FedEx P&D math is built on one number. Amy Byers calls stops per resource FedEx's love language. If you run 10 resources six days a week, you divide the week's total stops by 60, and a result of 120 is your stops per resource [39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:38:17]. Instead of asking for a bulk truck or more money, she makes the case in that language. If Network 2.0 pushes a route from 100 stops per resource down to 80, that means two or three more drivers. FedEx then agrees the contract needs a couple hundred thousand dollars to hire people and put trucks on [39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:20:15]. The annual cycle runs on fixed clocks. Contractors get three MISO offers and seven days to pick one for a one-time bonus of 1 percent of annual revenue, or $10,000 on a $1,000,000 contract. If they pass, they get seven more days of traditional negotiation, and the bonus gets rolled into their rates instead of paid up front [-AnzYDP3LQw @ 00:04:13]. eTruckBiz supplies the density benchmark behind all of this: contractors can make money in the 115 to 120 stops-per-dispatch range, and down at 96 it just gets a lot harder [wO3DQr14wiU @ 00:02:47].

On CAM, the Commercial Real Estate Escalations Made Simple database case runs a 50,000-square-foot office building split 40, 20 and 40 percent across three tenants, with a $480,000 budget and $500,000 in actual operating expenses [mgDY9HBbVA8 @ 00:01:47]. The move is to annualize January-through-July actuals at the end of July and reset each tenant's July-through-December charges. The database deliberately leans slightly over so the year ends in small credits. In the example, the tenant letter shows $8,000 due with a breakdown of taxes, insurance and utilities [mgDY9HBbVA8 @ 00:02:51]. Adrian Schultz of I Love Winnipeg Real Estate takes the same approach from the landlord side. He projects the year off the highest CAM total of previous years because crediting a tenant is easier than collecting extra [hvDoePHFWi4 @ 00:04:00]. On customs refunds, Flexport's presenter, a former nine-year CBP auditor, lays out how CAPE phase one works. Importers upload a CSV of up to 9,999 entry numbers through the ACE portal. Only unliquidated entries or those no more than 80 days past liquidation qualify, and refunds pay only to a US bank account linked by ACH. Anyone else collecting needs a Form 4811 plus the notified party listed on every entry summary [xtorVFI9g4c @ 00:03:53].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Pick the trigger and the window
   - Time the look-back to a trigger event and size it to the statute window.
   - Run it when a state audit is recent, pending or live, after legislation changes, after major capital purchases, or simply because transaction volume is high [Carr, Riggs & Ingram — HpOuVeLrWBs @ 00:00:42]. Scope three years, or four in some states, and go deeper when an open audit already extends past the statute [PM Business Advisors — ePf76grjr8U @ 00:00:30].
2. Pull the raw data and receipts
   - Gather the records the counterparty will have to answer to.
   - For sales tax, run three years of data through the refund tool and pull invoices [PM Business Advisors — ePf76grjr8U @ 00:00:59]. For CAM, request the general ledger, big-ticket invoices and controllable expense definitions [Commercial Real Estate Escalations Made Simple — CWHu33HRBVY @ 00:02:02]. For FedEx, pull your current and prior PCAT, DSW or WSW volume, and your settlement statements [Route Consultant — 39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:17:32].
3. Build the case with an exact number and buy-in
   - Write the business concern, the fix and an exact dollar ask, then get the terminal to agree before you file.
   - Lay out the business concern, the recommendation and the spreadsheet costs, then ask for an exact figure like $121,417.12 instead of just asking for more [Route Consultant — 39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:23:22]. 4. Submit, track and collect
   - File the claim or offer response and follow it until the money lands.
   - Review the claim with the client, then work with the vendors or the state until the check is in hand [PM Business Advisors — ePf76grjr8U @ 00:01:15]. For CAPE, file the entries and set up ACH, or refunds are held until there is an account [Flexport — xtorVFI9g4c @ 00:46:48]. ## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split in rate renegotiation is whether to grab a MISO or fight for a custom structure. Route Consultant lays out the base case: FedEx tends to be conservative on volume projections, which also exclude peak season, so the firm has traditionally advised MISO 3, where more compensation rides on per-stop and per-package variable charges [-AnzYDP3LQw @ 00:03:11]. Amy Byers runs the same play from the contractor seat, saying the Byers operation has always rolled the dice on MISO 3 because its Medina area grew from one truck to at least eleven or twelve drivers, while warning that a rural operator may want the fixed-heavy guarantee instead [39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:31:53]. eTruckBiz goes further and tells operators never to run their own negotiation unless they are simply picking a MISO, which it says is really not the thing to do either, because reconfigured Network 2.0 territories make it darn near impossible to tell a good offer from a bad one [wO3DQr14wiU @ 00:14:41]. Route Consultant pushes back for first-time buyers: without knowing a territory's unique characteristics, traditional negotiation is hard, so selecting one of the three MISOs is often easier [-AnzYDP3LQw @ 00:11:47]. The real variable is how well the operator knows the volume curve.

Practitioners also split on what happens to the early-acceptance money. Route Consultant explains that taking a MISO inside the first seven days pays a one percent upfront bonus, ten thousand dollars on a million-dollar contract, and that walking into traditional negotiation does not forfeit it: the money is rolled into the rates, which in a lot of scenarios alone can make a big difference in the year-end bottom line when FedEx undershoots volume [-AnzYDP3LQw @ 00:05:38]. Amy Byers reads the same mechanic as a wash, saying that if you negotiate, the chunk goes away into the contract and you actually don't make more money; under deadline pressure the play is to take the upfront cash, sign, and then hit the renegotiation button, knowing the bonus will not come twice [39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:34:08]. eTruckBiz adds a Network 2.0 wrinkle: the transition money is not tied to the contract the way the normal one percent is, which the hosts call all the more reason to negotiate [wO3DQr14wiU @ 00:15:39]. Everyone agrees the FedEx negotiator exists to keep bottom-line projected revenue from moving at all, so the real fight is over rate structure, which Route Consultant says can still have a dramatic effect on what the contractor actually earns [fd7XjYGTb8c @ 00:02:47].

On the tax side, practitioners disagree about timing and exposure rather than mechanics. Carr, Riggs & Ingram name a recent, pending, or ongoing state audit as the first trigger for a reverse audit, arguing that state audits are pro-state and agents will not weigh pro-taxpayer provisions, so the company needs someone working its side of the table during that window [HpOuVeLrWBs @ 00:00:42]. Cherry Bekaert's client Mike O'Donnell describes the opposite worry, the misconception that a big refund claim sheds light on the company; the plant was already on Florida's audit schedule, got audited right after the refund, and called it as smooth as it could have gone with no large assessment, crediting the firm for acting as middleman [S7Qlojol4pE @ 00:05:34]. CAM operators split on how to keep tenants from year-end shock. I Love Winnipeg Real Estate projects this year's charges off the highest of prior years' CAM totals and refunds any overage, because crediting a tenant beats collecting extra [hvDoePHFWi4 @ 00:04:00]. Commercial Real Estate Escalations Made Simple instead annualizes January-through-July actuals and resets July-through-December billing so it leans slightly over, turning reconciliation into a small credit rather than a surprise bill [mgDY9HBbVA8 @ 00:02:51].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most repeated firsthand bottleneck is paper. Cherry Bekaert's client Mike O'Donnell admits being nervous going in about how much internal work the project would take, and the three-year lookback meant the firm's team digging through invoices sitting in boxes, which was not fun, to isolate vendors the plant had consistently paid sales tax to when it most likely should not have [S7Qlojol4pE @ 00:04:52]. Flexport's Ton Win, who spent nine years as a customs auditor, describes the same failure from the government side: one request covering only ten entries came back at the last moment as more than a thousand pages, because the supplier kept manufacturing records in cardboard boxes locked in a warehouse room [xtorVFI9g4c @ 00:28:38]. Win also warns that any inconsistency between the 7501, the commercial invoice, payments, weights, or dates fails the smell test and triggers the assumption that the records are inaccurate or not the real documents [xtorVFI9g4c @ 00:19:31]. In CAM work, RE BackOffice puts numbers on the grind: each location can take four to over twenty hours to reconcile and involve sixty to five hundred documents [5wJlP07wwrM @ 00:08:45]. Whoever runs the recovery has to price that document labor into the engagement before promising anyone a fast check.

FedEx contractors bleed money by being late or vague. Amy Byers says unique characteristics must land four to six months before negotiation; if the invitation email arrived four months ago and nothing went in, it is in fact potentially too late, leaving only escalation or signing and renegotiating immediately [39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:10:06]. Route Consultant warns not to assume last year's submission is still saved when FedEx reviews the next contract, and to resubmit every single year with anything new highlighted [-AnzYDP3LQw @ 00:08:04]. The MISO bottom-line revenue figure can mislead in both directions. Route Consultant has recently seen plenty of contracts that looked like a revenue decline but were actually better than the year before [-AnzYDP3LQw @ 00:12:33]. Byers caught the reverse: a standup extension that would have paid $1,000 less per week even though the operation had picked up an extra zip code. Instead of screaming or threatening to walk, Byers sent the activity-week calculator, built in FedEx's own stops-per-resource terms, to the negotiator, who agreed, went back to engineering, and fixed it [39UxCZgtvHU @ 00:32:47]. The lesson repeated by both Route Consultant voices is that a raw delta, positive or negative, means nothing until current settlement, PCAT, and all three MISOs are compared side by side.

Economics checks cut against easy recovery stories. Cherry Bekaert's client asked the partner point-blank what is in it for the firm on another three-year lookback, since the fixed purchasing process means the next refund most likely will not be huge, a built-in ceiling on repeat contingency engagements [S7Qlojol4pE @ 00:10:13]. Greg Walker's SMP Consulting pitch shows why that work pays on contingency: incorrect governing codes, employees misclassed among more than 700 possible classifications, overlooked credits, and misreported losses inflate the experience modification rate and compound over time, and SMP recovers overpayments from the last three years [sM254iPKhSo @ 00:00:52]. eTruckBiz reports service-provider churn running about three times normal around Network 2.0, roughly half voluntary and half operators helped toward the exit door, and separately warns that buying too many trucks before engineering a reconfigured CSA has already burned contractors [wO3DQr14wiU @ 00:10:29]. WKRN News 2 frames the macro squeeze on manufacturing clients: tariffs on parts, steel, and machinery coincided with plant closures and the sector losing 11,000 jobs in July and 15,000 in June, with employers freezing hiring and builders backing off projects out of uncertainty [FI_QPEhBhEE @ 00:01:57]. Shrinking headcount and frozen projects are precisely the cost-cutting climate in which recovered cash gets a client's attention.
