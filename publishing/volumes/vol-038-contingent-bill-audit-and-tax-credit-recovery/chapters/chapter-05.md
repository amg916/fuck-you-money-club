## 1. Kill False Entitlement Before Automating Twelve Recovery Plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The question's 80% figure is a framing device, not an observed failure rate; no cohort evidence supports that percentage here. The operational problem is concrete: newcomers automate candidate discovery before proving entitlement. Organize the twelve mapped plays as parcel service refunds, parcel duplicate charges, parcel accessorial corrections, utility tariff corrections, utility meter corrections, cooling-tower sewer exclusions, waste invoice corrections, telecom inventory cleanup, telecom contract-rate corrections, property-tax appeals, WOTC processing, and specialty tax-credit recovery. These are operational mappings, not twelve independently scored businesses. For each, preserve the signed Letter of Authorization, governing contract version, original invoice, calculation, filing deadline, and payment evidence. A FedEx or UPS service delay can produce zero collectible recovery when the applicable service guarantee is suspended, contractually waived, or subject to an exclusion. Suppose software flags 1,000 shipments with $20 potential refunds: $20,000 is candidate exposure, not operator revenue. If only 400 qualify and settle, a 35% earned share produces $2,800 before delivery costs. Submitting all 1,000 does not improve that arithmetic; it adds rejection handling and account risk. Freeze the affected service lane when waiver coverage cannot be established, then inspect the actual contract and current carrier terms before resuming.

Duplicate billing and accessorial correction are different defects from late delivery, so route them through their applicable dispute processes rather than assuming every claim shares a guarantee deadline. Reconcile tracking identifier, invoice number, charge code, account, and adjustment identifier before submission. A $15 credit already issued cannot support another $5.25 fee at a 35% split. Set the duplicate-submission target to zero, and quarantine a batch when its internal duplicate rate exceeds 0.5%; this is an illustrative operating threshold, not a carrier rule. For utility tariff and meter work, compare rate class, demand interval, meter multiplier, and occupancy against the governing tariff. Cooling-tower sewer exclusions require the local utility's accepted measurement and application process; an equipment photograph alone does not establish deductible discharge volume. A verified $12,000 utility refund at 30% supports $3,600 of operator fees, whereas an engineering estimate supports only a prospect record. Waste audits must reconcile container count, pickup frequency, contamination charges, and contract escalators. Stop a projected $500 monthly saving when a replacement contract adds $600 in minimum charges. The fallback is a narrower invoice correction, not a fabricated service dispute or pressure campaign against a vendor.

Telecom inventory cleanup fails when an apparently idle circuit carries alarm traffic, emergency routing, or backup connectivity. Require a customer-owned dependency map and written disconnect authority before issuing a termination request. Telecom contract-rate corrections need service-order dates, promotional expirations, minimum commitments, and early termination charges in the same calculation. Ten unused lines costing $80 monthly suggest $800 of gross savings; $300 of unavoidable replacement service reduces the defensible baseline to $500. A 40% share then produces $200 monthly, not $320, and a 36-month arrangement yields $7,200 only if all required months actually qualify. Separate historical refund shares from prospective savings splits so the same correction does not generate two fees. Establish a baseline hierarchy using signed contracts, invoice snapshots, measured usage, and documented adjustments for seasonality or site closures. Keep client-requested scope changes outside the original savings denominator. Pause billing whenever an unresolved baseline variance exceeds 5% of the proposed fee; release the invoice only after an identified reviewer resolves the difference. The reliable fallback is verified realized savings billed monthly, with a clearly defined termination reconciliation, rather than accelerating three years of hypothetical benefit into today's earnings.

## 2. Separate Lawful Filing Authority From Attractive Recovery Math  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The play property_tax_assessment_appeal_contingency begins when the county releases annual valuation notices, not when a generic national deadline appears on a calendar. Ingest the assessor roll, retain its publication date and parcel identifiers, and run an automated valuation model that flags assessments above defensible comparable-neighbor equity ratios. Screen sales comparables, property condition, use, exemptions, and local assessment rules before treating an AVM signal as an appeal. A $1,000,000 assessment reduced to $900,000 at an illustrative 2% effective tax rate produces $2,000 of first-year tax reduction; a 40% contingency produces $800, not $40,000. The stated 30–50% range applies to verified tax reduction, never the assessed-value reduction. Local rules may use different taxable-value mechanics, so reconcile the actual corrected bill. Texas Tax Code §41.44 generally ties protest timing to May 15 or thirty days after delivery of the notice, whichever is later, with additional statutory exceptions. Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1152 regulates property-tax consultants subject to its scope and exemptions. Partner with qualified local consultants or attorneys of record where required, and automate pre-deadline batch electronic filing only after representation authority and portal acceptance are documented.

WOTC processing fails asymmetrically because an inexpensive missing document can destroy the entire expected credit. Under 26 U.S.C. §51, the employer must satisfy the applicable targeted-group, certification, wage, and service requirements. Form 8850 prescreening generally must be completed on or before the employment offer, and the certification request generally must reach the designated local agency within twenty-eight days after work begins. Preserve the offer date, signature events, start date, transmission receipt, agency determination, qualifying hours, and wages. Do not backdate forms or infer an employee's eligibility from neighborhood demographics. For many eligible groups, $6,000 of qualified first-year wages at 40% yields a maximum $2,400 credit after the relevant 400-hour threshold; other groups and circumstances differ. A $150 processing fee remains $150 of operator revenue, not $2,400. Section 51(c)(4) contains a December 31, 2025 commencement cutoff; verify any enacted extension before representing later starts as eligible. Agency acceptance of paperwork alone does not establish a statutory extension. Escalate cases at day twenty-one, and stop representing a missed deadline as recoverable unless a specific applicable relief provision or agency procedure actually supports that treatment.

Specialty tax-credit recovery adds a different trap: copying the expense audit contingency agreement into federal return preparation. Treasury Circular 230 §10.27 restricts contingent fees in covered federal tax matters and specifies exceptions; judicial decisions also limit aspects of its reach. Have the responsible tax professional determine the permitted engagement structure for the actual service, rather than assuming every amended return qualifies for a refund exception. Separate fixed processing charges, permitted recovery compensation, return preparation, and representation in the scope and ledger. A proposed $50,000 credit with a 20% recovery share suggests $10,000 only if the fee arrangement is permissible and the compensation is earned. It does not justify filing an unsupported position. Preserve wage allocations, project records, eligibility analyses, and reconciliation against other incentives where double use is restricted. Circular 230 §10.22 requires diligence within its scope; 26 U.S.C. §6694 addresses specified preparer understatement conduct. Route uncertain positions to the responsible practitioner and suspend unsupported submissions. The fallback is a documented eligibility study or properly priced processing engagement, not rebranding return preparation as consulting while leaving the same prohibited fee economics and unsupported claims intact.

## 3. Detect Clawbacks, Baseline Drift, and Cash Conversion Failure  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Formula v2.0 counts earned contingency refund shares, earned shared-savings splits, and WOTC processing fees as operator revenue. It excludes the client's underlying freight, utility, property-tax, and wage base, and it excludes client recoveries belonging to the client. The Conservative case is $110,000 revenue less $77,000 costs, leaving $33,000 at 30%. Base is $505,000 less $287,850, leaving $217,150 at 43.0%. Scaled is $900,000 less $396,000, leaving $504,000 at 56%. Teams remain 1 FT plus 3 C, 1 FT plus 3 C, and 2 FT plus 4 C, respectively. These are the canonical annual cases, not forecasts rebuilt from example claims. Where the operator acts as agent, apply the ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis to the promised service and present the retained fee appropriately; routing a client's refund through an operator bank account does not create gross operator revenue. Recognition also depends on performance obligations and constrained variable consideration, not merely an internal discovery status. Keep candidate recovery, submitted recovery, approved adjustment, client receipt, earned fee, invoice, and operator collection as separate fields. Their differences explain why a persuasive pipeline can coexist with an empty payroll account.

Clawbacks are usually contractual and evidentiary before they become accounting problems. A carrier can reverse an erroneous credit; a utility can correct a provisional adjustment; a tax authority can disallow a credit; and a client can dispute whether an expense reduction belongs to the agreed baseline. Define whether each event reduces an unpaid invoice, requires repayment, offsets future fees, or triggers an indemnity review. For an illustrative $100,000 collected recovery at a 35% split, the operator initially earns $35,000 only under the applicable recognition and contract conditions. If $20,000 of the underlying recovery is later reversed and the agreement passes that reversal through proportionally, operator exposure is $7,000. Do not assume exposure stops there if the contract shifts penalties, interest, or defense costs. Model those obligations separately and negotiate clear allocation before signing. A provisional reserve of 10% of exposed fees would hold $3,500 and would not cover that $7,000 event. Calibrate reserves from actual claim cohorts, reversal severity, and open disputes rather than a comforting round percentage. Segregate client funds from operating cash and prefer direct client receipt where the engagement permits it, reducing both custody complexity and false liquidity.

Monitor cash conversion by recovery lane because municipal refunds and parcel credits rarely share the same issuance cycle. Define recovery aging from accepted submission to client receipt, invoice aging from fee invoice to payment, and reversal rate as reversed earned fees divided by the corresponding cohort's earned fees. Illustrative intervention thresholds are invoice aging above sixty days, utility issuance beyond the documented expected date by thirty days, fee reversals above 3% over a rolling ninety-day cohort, and more than 20% of receivables concentrated in one client. These are management choices, not statutory limits. Stop expanding the affected lane when two thresholds breach together; continue valid deadline-sensitive work while fixing collection terms and evidence defects. Track handling cost per accepted claim: a $12 fee requiring twenty minutes at $45 hourly costs $15 in direct labor before software or overhead, creating a negative $3 contribution. Batch only cases sharing the same evidence standard and use minimum viable recovery thresholds defined in the engagement. The stated $1,500–$9,500 monthly operating range is not the complete annual cost stack; contractor, staffing, and delivery costs must still reconcile to each canonical scenario's total costs.

## 4. Install Circuit-Breakers Before Credentials, Filings, or Clients Fail  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The fastest route to a platform ban is treating access as permission to ignore the platform's operating rules. Use authorized FedEx and UPS integrations only for the endpoints, accounts, and purposes actually enabled; do not assume every refund action has a public API. Where a permitted electronic claim interface is unavailable, use the approved portal or documented service channel. Shared client passwords, fabricated tracking records, repeated duplicate claims, account rotation after suspension, and bypassing rate limits convert a recoverable workflow defect into a trust failure. Store delegated credentials in a managed secret store, separate clients by access scope, and record which identity submitted each transaction. Treat any confirmed cross-client access as an immediate stop condition. Treat repeated authorization errors as a credential incident, not an invitation to retry with broader access. For rate-limit responses, honor the published retry policy and reduce concurrency; do not distribute the same prohibited workload across replacement identities. Keep an emergency disable switch per carrier, client, and action type so one broken submission path does not halt safe invoice ingestion. Preserve the platform notice and request reinstatement through its stated process rather than creating an undisclosed replacement account.

Tax and employee records require tighter separation than ordinary invoice totals. WOTC packets can contain sensitive identifiers and eligibility information that should never appear in shared claim queues or general sales notes. Restrict access by assigned task, encrypt stored documents, and log exports. When acting as a tax return preparer within the relevant rules, assess 26 U.S.C. §7216 and 26 CFR §301.7216-3 before using or disclosing return information outside permitted purposes. An expense-audit Letter of Authorization is not automatically a valid tax-information disclosure consent. Minimize information copied into OCR prompts, support tickets, and contractor worksheets; approved service providers still require a purpose and access boundary. For utility meter OCR, compare extracted multipliers and billing periods with source images, and route material mismatches to human review. A single misplaced decimal can inflate a claimed recovery tenfold. Use amount and date reconciliation rather than trusting the OCR engine's confidence score alone. Quarantine documents with missing account identifiers or inconsistent totals before any external filing. Preserve records under the engagement's applicable retention schedule and suspend routine deletion when an audit, dispute, or preservation obligation requires the evidence to remain available.

Run a weekly exception meeting around recoverability, not the number of claims generated. Review the ten largest proposed fees, every missed deadline, every reversal, unresolved authorization failures, and any client whose direct handling costs exceed earned fees. Assign an owner and next action to each exception; otherwise the dashboard merely decorates the failure. Before scaling a lane, replay a sample from original invoice through authorized submission, client receipt, earned fee, and collection. An illustrative sampling rule is twenty cases per active lane each month, expanded to the entire affected batch when a material eligibility defect appears. A sample is process surveillance, not proof that every unsampled claim is valid. For property-tax batches, preserve portal acknowledgments per parcel; a successful upload does not establish acceptance of every protest. For thirty-six-month savings agreements, recheck usage and contract changes at each billing cycle. For WOTC, reconcile certifications with payroll hours and qualified wages before passing amounts to the responsible preparer. The circuit-breaker succeeds when it contains the defective lane without abandoning valid client obligations. Resume only after the root cause is corrected, affected cases are reconciled, and a named reviewer documents the successful replay.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Quarantine the smallest affected operating lane
   - Disable the implicated client, service, credential, or submission action.
   - Preserve safe ingestion and valid deadline-sensitive work; record the incident time, responsible operator, and affected claim identifiers.
2. Reconstruct authority and entitlement
   - Replay the disputed cases from original evidence and governing rules.
   - Check signed authorization, contract versions, statutory timing, duplicate adjustments, professional scope, and parcel-level or claim-level receipts.
3. Reconcile fee and client exposure
   - Separate client recovery reversals from operator fees and additional obligations.
   - Calculate credits, repayments, reserve changes, handling costs, and any contractually allocated penalties or defense costs without treating client funds as operating cash.
4. Verify the fix before restoring submissions
   - Require a named reviewer to approve an evidence-complete replay.
   - Restore only the corrected scope, monitor the next batch, and document remaining disputes, approaching deadlines, and client communications.
