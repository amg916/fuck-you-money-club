## 1. Identify the economic buyer, benefit owner and actual signer  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The client writes the operator's check; the carrier, utility, landlord or taxing authority usually delivers the benefit that funds it. Sell those as separate events. A controller buys defensible cash recovery and a clean close, while a CFO buys working-capital improvement without adding permanent analysts. A facilities director supplies meter history but may lack contracting authority. A logistics manager can validate late shipments but cannot necessarily amend a carrier agreement. A property manager may coordinate an appeal while the owner, lender or lease determines who receives the reduction. For WOTC, human resources controls onboarding evidence, payroll controls wages and hours, and the employer's tax team controls credit utilization. Build a stakeholder record naming the contracting entity, benefit owner, authorized signer, budget owner, accounts-payable contact and evidence custodian. Ask who can approve a $20,000 fee after a $50,000 refund, whether a purchase order is required, and whether supplier enrollment must precede work. Zero upfront pricing removes one objection, not procurement. The acute pain is a shrinking claim window, unexplained expense leakage, missed onboarding documentation or trapped cash; the signature follows when the buyer sees an auditable route from that pain to a measurable benefit and a bounded fee.

For utility_waste_cam_tariff_overcharge_audit, request up to 36 months of electric, water/sewer, waste and lease CAM records, then shorten each review to the applicable tariff, contract and jurisdictional recovery limit. A $100,000 supported refund at a negotiated 40% share produces $40,000 of operator fees and leaves $60,000 with the client. The permitted commercial range is 35%–50% for this play, within the broader shelf's 25%–50% expense contingency positioning. Demand ratchets require interval and billing-demand evidence; cooling-tower sewer credits require an accepted evaporation or deduct-meter method; manufacturing exemptions require jurisdiction-specific eligibility. Waste escalation disputes turn on signed amendments, while CAM recoveries turn on the lease, allocation schedules and audit deadlines. Do not promise a universal three-year refund right. If the historical claim fails, withdraw unsupported items, preserve surviving disputes, and price documented prospective metering or classification changes separately. For example, $2,000 of verified monthly savings at 35% produces $700 monthly fees, or $25,200 across a fully realized 36-month term. Historical and prospective periods must not overlap. The buyer receives a schedule showing which invoices support each dollar, which items remain disputed, and who must approve the correction before savings begin.

Use high_velocity_dm_outbound_systems to reach buyers through an expressed interest, not scraped inbox saturation. A LinkedIn comment inviting an invoice-check worksheet, an Instagram reaction to a metering case study, or an inbound message can create a permissioned routing event where the platform supports that workflow. An illustrative funnel of 200 trigger actions, 80 replies, 20 qualified calls and four signed audits yields 40% reply, 25% qualification and 20% closing conversion at successive stages. At $4,000 expected earned fees per audit, that cohort represents $16,000 of potential fees, not booked revenue on signature. Route warm signals by company domain and account owner so several inboxes do not contact the same controller. Require user-initiated comment, reaction or inbound activity before automated dispatch, and send external links only after a reply. PeerFloodError, delivery restrictions or a platform warning means stop and use permitted manual follow-up or an inbound form, never identity rotation. ## 2. Choose a payment rail that matches authority and realization  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with client-direct settlement. The utility posts a credit or sends money to the account holder; the carrier credits the shipper; the tax authority applies or refunds an amount to the taxpayer. The operator then invoices the contracted fee against evidence of realization. A Letter of Authorization permits specified information access or claim communications; it does not automatically authorize fund custody, ACH debits, tax representation or a lien. Secure separate documents for those distinct powers where appropriate. A posted utility credit deserves special treatment when the account closes or has insufficient future charges: define whether the fee arises at posting, application against an enforceable bill, or cash refund. Prefer actual application or cash when collectability is uncertain. For a $50,000 refund and 40% fee, the client receives $50,000 and pays $20,000; the operator does not report $50,000 as revenue. If a permitted settlement agent splits the distribution, its statement should show $30,000 to the client and $20,000 to the operator with the same underlying claim identifier. A split instruction requires acceptance by the actual disbursing party, not merely language in the engagement.

Match rail friction to counterparty risk and ticket size. ACH credit is efficient for recurring shared-savings invoices because the client pushes payment through established treasury controls. Authorized ACH debit can reduce missed installments, but mandate business-account verification, retained authorization, an agreed notification process and processor-specific return handling. Do not assume consumer and business account protections or return periods are identical. Wires fit larger recoveries, provided bank changes are confirmed using a previously verified telephone number. Card or platform checkout helps small processing invoices but adds dispute exposure and processing costs. At an illustrative 3% checkout cost, collecting a $10,000 fee costs $300 before any other expenses; price that cost explicitly rather than pretending settlement is free. Escrow can solve a genuine trust problem when an independent provider can hold funds under clear release instructions. It is excessive for a routine $700 savings installment. Retainers and milestone holds are commercial funding structures rather than bank networks, but both belong in the comparison because they determine when the operator has usable cash and who bears the remaining performance risk.

Budget authority is not escrow authority. A procurement manager may issue a purchase order yet lack permission to place company funds with a third party. Ask treasury to confirm acceptable institutions, signers, release conditions, fees and dispute procedures before offering escrow. Likewise, a statutory refund process can require payment to the taxpayer of record despite a private fee arrangement. Federal claim assignments implicate 31 U.S.C. §3727, and tax refund check negotiation by return preparers implicates 26 U.S.C. §6695(f); do not build collection around diverting government payments. Factoring belongs only after an enforceable, earned invoice exists and the factor accepts the debtor, documentation and recourse structure. Selling a $20,000 receivable at a 3% discount produces $19,400 before additional charges, not a new $20,000 revenue event. A customer can still dispute the original work, and recourse may return the loss to the operator. For each rail, record the settlement reference, gross fee, transaction cost, withheld amount and bank receipt. Reconcile those fields to the client ledger weekly. The chosen mechanism should make collection observable without turning the audit business into an unplanned custodian of customer or government funds.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Verify every relevant entity and signer
   - Match the engagement, supplier record, account holder and bank beneficiary.
   - Record separate claim authority, contracting authority and payment authority; resolve any owner, tenant or employer mismatch before submission.
2. Lock the realization definition and payment route
   - Select the rail and define which evidence creates an invoiceable fee.
   - Specify cash receipt, applied credit or verified savings, plus the fee percentage, due date, transaction costs and any bounded holdback.
3. Match settlement evidence before invoicing
   - Attach the relevant account statement, claim identifier and fee calculation.
   - Separate principal benefits, interest, historical refunds and prospective savings according to the signed fee schedule.
4. Reconcile receipts and resolve exceptions
   - Match bank receipts to earned invoices and escalate differences weekly.
   - Track processing charges, returns, reversals, withholding and aged balances without recording customer benefits as operator sales.

## 3. Protect the fee with evidence, bounded terms and credit screening  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Secure the engagement and signed Letter of Authorization before disclosing actionable tariff findings. Describe covered legal entities, account numbers, service periods, claim categories and authorized communications. Preserve baseline invoices with receipt timestamps and file hashes so a later rate correction cannot rewrite the starting point. The fee schedule should identify the percentage, eligible benefit, realization event, due date, reversals and expense allocation. Define a narrow protection period for findings delivered during the engagement: if the client submits the same documented claim directly, a contractually earned fee remains payable under the agreed terms. Exclude preexisting claims listed at onboarding and unrelated improvements developed independently. This protects work without asserting ownership over every future cost reduction. For prospective savings, state the 36-month maximum term, rate and consumption normalization, seasonality method, business changes and monthly reconciliation process. A closed facility cannot keep producing modeled savings merely because last year's invoice was large. Give the client an objection window and a workable dispute process, while requiring payment of undisputed amounts. Preserve an evidence delivery log and signed scope amendments. A clear causal chain from finding to realized benefit is stronger collection protection than an expansive penalty clause.

Screen the entity that owes the fee, not simply the recognizable brand on the building. Confirm legal existence through the relevant secretary of state, inspect available business credit reports from Dun & Bradstreet or Experian Business with appropriate authority, request trade references, and review public insolvency indicators through PACER where warranted. Ask about disputed supplier balances, payment cycles, lender restrictions and treasury centralization. A profitable tenant can still be a poor debtor if the engagement is signed by a thinly funded property affiliate. Set exposure limits using the work already invested, earned unpaid fees and credible settlement timing. For example, a $12,000 fee payable 60 days after a utility refund expected in 120 days creates roughly 180 days between submission and cash; the refund itself is not interim working capital. Where credit is weak, shorten invoice periods, require an accepted split-disbursement mechanism when available, reduce scope or use a separately negotiated paid diagnostic. A parent guarantee requires the parent's actual signature and review of enforceability. Do not infer security from a purchase order, an authorization letter or a promise that the municipality usually pays quickly.

Tax work needs its own fee architecture. Treasury Circular 230, 31 CFR §10.27, restricts contingent fees for covered services connected with matters before the IRS, subject to specified exceptions and applicable judicial limits. Do not transplant a utility's 40% refund share onto original return preparation or credit claims without a qualified review of the precise service, practitioner status and governing law. Use separately priced evidence assembly or processing where appropriate, and keep representation with properly authorized practitioners. For WOTC, verify that the employee's start date falls within the credit's enacted authorization period before forecasting value. Section 51(d)(13) generally requires prescreening on or before the offer date and submission to the designated local agency within 28 days after work starts, subject to applicable relief. Certification is not cash and does not establish usable tax capacity. An illustrative eligible worker with $6,000 qualified wages and the applicable 40% rate produces $2,400 potential credit, subject to group rules, hours, limitations and substantiation. A $150 processing fee can be earned at a defined delivered milestone without claiming the employer realized $2,400. If authorization, certification or evidence fails, stop the affected filing and correct the forecast.

> 
> Proceed only when the responsible person can produce the evidence behind each check.
> - Verify the contracting debtor, benefit owner, signer, purchase-order requirement and payment contact against actual entity and account records.

## 4. Reconcile benefit realization, earned fees and the three profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Operate three linked ledgers: client benefits, operator receivables and operator cash. The first records submitted, disputed, allowed, applied and refunded amounts by claim. The second records earned fees, invoices, credits and aging under the engagement. The third records actual bank movement and processing costs. A $100,000 refund at 40% creates $40,000 fee revenue when the applicable recognition requirements are satisfied; the remaining $60,000 belongs to the client and is never an operator expense used to manufacture a larger revenue figure. Under ASC 606, evaluate the service promise, performance obligations, variable consideration and principal-versus-agent presentation for the actual arrangement. No separate retained_share input is supplied for this scorecard; do not invent one or apply a second haircut to its earned-fee totals. Contractor costs belong in the appropriate operating expense treatment unless the actual arrangement requires net presentation. Neither a claim submission nor a projected 36-month savings stream is automatically current revenue. Recognize shared-savings fees as contract performance and verified benefits support recognition, and treat advance payments according to remaining obligations. Track reversals through credit notes tied to the original claim rather than silently reducing a later client's invoice.

Conservative combines $70,000 historical recovery fees, $30,000 earned shared-savings fees and $10,000 processing fees for $110,000 revenue. Base combines $315,000, $150,000 and $40,000 for $505,000. Scaled combines $540,000, $270,000 and $90,000 for $900,000. At a blended illustrative 40% expense recovery fee, those historical fee components require $175,000, $787,500 and $1,350,000 of realized client recoveries respectively; those benefit figures are not additional sales. At a 35% savings split, the three annual savings-fee components require approximately $85,714.29, $428,571.43 and $771,428.57 of verified client savings during the recognized periods. Processing is illustrated with fixed deliverable units, not a presumed lawful tax contingency. Conservative expenses of $77,000 leave $33,000 owner net profit at 30%; Base expenses of $287,850 leave $217,150 at 43.0%; Scaled expenses of $396,000 leave $504,000 at 56%. Staffing remains 1 FT + 3 C for Conservative and Base, then 2 FT + 4 C for Scaled. The higher margin requires demonstrated repeatability and disciplined collections, not merely a larger pipeline or more claim submissions.

Use a weekly exceptions meeting to defend those economics. Review carrier claims nearing deadlines, utility refunds awaiting issuance, missing WOTC certifications, client disputes and invoices that have exceeded terms. FedEx and UPS EDI feeds or available authorized claim interfaces can accelerate matching, but availability, refund rights and contractual permission must be verified for each account. Utility tariff and cooling-tower meter OCR should retain source images and flag uncertain readings for human review. ATS integration can timestamp Form 8850 workflows without replacing statutory eligibility checks or state agency submission evidence. Use role-based access and encrypted storage for employee tax records, and provide contractors only the accounts they need. The $6,000–$35,000 startup capital range and $1,500–$9,500 monthly operating-cost range are planning envelopes, not substitutes for the full scenario expense totals, which include broader delivery costs. First-deal timing of one to four months does not promise first cash in that window. If a customer pays slowly, freeze additional discretionary work within contractual rights, preserve claim deadlines and agree a documented payment plan. Measure earned fees collected, reversal rates and days to cash alongside claim value; otherwise a profitable spreadsheet can hide a practice that cannot fund its next filing cycle.
