## 1. Define the earned fee before pricing twelve recovery plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start the ledger with the operator's contractual entitlement, not the customer's utility spend, parcel charges, tax liability, or refund principal. A $100,000 utility correction at a 30% contingent share creates $30,000 of potential operator consideration, not $100,000 of revenue. Recognition still depends on performance, enforceability, and the applicable variable-consideration constraint. Under ASC 606, assess principal versus agent for each promised service; a third-party commission received as agent is not permission to gross up the underlying transaction. The scorecard's gross revenue label therefore means earned operator fees before operating expenses. This model uses retained_share equal to 1.00 only after excluding customer proceeds and other parties' entitlements from the revenue perimeter. It does not imply the operator retains every refund dollar. Record collected customer money separately when an authorized arrangement requires handling it, with a matching payable rather than sales. Before revealing findings, obtain a signed Letter of Authorization, the executed fee schedule, baseline invoice snapshots, and the customer's permitted disclosure contacts. Put carrier waivers, purchasing eligibility, and refund limitations into the intake record before estimating recoverable dollars. A theoretical overcharge without an enforceable recovery route has zero collectible fee value.

The purchasing lane is private_b2b_gpo_umbrella_rebate_arbitrage, a TRACK_1 play built around authorized umbrella agreements rather than invented buying power. Pool 10,000 to 75,000 or more seats across hundreds of SMB members only where Una, OMNIA, or a private vertical SaaS, parcel, or telecom GPO accepts the specific buyers and categories. Membership count alone establishes neither rebate eligibility nor benchmark access. In an illustrative annual cohort, $2,000,000 of eligible paid purchases at a verified 3% vendor administrative rebate produces $60,000 of potential operator compensation. Separately, $100,000 of documented Year-1 customer savings at 25% produces $25,000. Combined consideration is $85,000 only if both contracts permit the payments and customers receive clear dual-compensation disclosure. The rebate is not another $60,000 of customer savings. Test negotiated 3% to 12% rebate brackets and 25% to 35% savings splits against exclusions, returns, payment dates, and quarter-end true-ups. Use authorized Vendr or Tropic benchmarking access to challenge price floors without assuming their information is freely redistributable. Suspend disputed fees, reconcile member and supplier baselines, refund unearned amounts, and transfer affected members to eligible agreements.

Map the other eleven lanes separately because their failure clocks differ. FedEx and UPS service refunds depend on current guarantee coverage and contract waivers; a successful API submission is not an accepted claim. Parcel billing corrections require invoice-level evidence distinct from late-delivery evidence. Utility tariff, cooling-tower sewer, waste, and telecom recoveries require account-specific service classifications and defensible before-and-after baselines. Assessment appeals require jurisdiction-specific authority and any applicable consultant license. WOTC processing requires Form 8850 timing, certification evidence, eligible wages, and verification that the worker's start date falls within the credit's legislatively authorized period. Specialty federal tax work requires a separate engagement and a current analysis of Circular 230 section 10.27 before any contingent fee is offered. Use a fixed processing fee when permitted and operationally appropriate rather than automatically taking a credit percentage. Finally, a 36-month savings contract earns fees against measured periods, not three years of forecast savings on signature. The matrix supplies underwriting examples, not additive revenue forecasts: customer recoveries and purchasing rebates can overlap, and each opportunity needs one unique economic identifier before it enters the scenario ledger.

They are removed before the canonical revenue line, never deducted again as delivery expense. Recognized consideration remains subject to the applicable accounting policy, including constraints on variable amounts. A claim rejected before earning is different from an earned invoice that becomes uncollectible. Preserve that distinction in the chart of accounts and in the pipeline dashboard. Otherwise a sales team can inflate conversion by booking estimates, then conceal weak eligibility inside an undifferentiated reserve account. Holding cash aside does not itself create an accounting expense. ## 3. Fund the complete payout gap rather than the invoice term  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Measure cash latency from the first committed cohort expenditure to unrestricted operator cash. Conventional DSO measures an invoiced receivable interval; it does not capture months of claim preparation, municipal processing, or an unreleased supplier rebate. For this planning model, use a combined pre-invoice processing and collection interval of 120 days Conservative, 90 days Base, and 60 days Scaled, followed by incremental payout locks of 30, 30, and 15 days. The total funded gaps are therefore 150, 120, and 75 days. These assumptions are not carrier or utility service commitments. Maintain separate timestamps for work authorization, completed submission, approved refund, customer receipt, operator invoice, due date, and bank settlement. Calculate actual invoice DSO only between invoice and collection, and report the earlier interval separately. If a rebate administrator's lock already sits inside recorded collection days, do not add it again. Similarly, a customer who pays only after using a utility credit creates a different cash profile from one receiving a check. A cohort's blended timing should be weighted by collectible operator fees rather than underlying customer spend, which otherwise lets a large low-fee account distort the forecast.

Funded operating cash burn excludes the separately funded risk allowance and equals delivery, acquisition, systems, and overhead: $68,000 Conservative, $242,000 Base, and $338,000 Scaled annually. Under a uniform-spending approximation, working float equals annual cash burn multiplied by funded gap days and divided by 365. That produces approximately $27,945, $79,562, and $69,452. Add dedicated risk liquidity of $9,000, $45,850, and $58,000 to obtain planning liquidity of $36,945, $125,412, and $127,452. The second layer is cash availability for modeled adverse events, not a second expense deduction from owner profit. This conservative funding choice assumes the entire annual risk-cost allowance could need liquidity before replacement collections arrive. Scaled has lower operating float than Base because its assumed cash cycle is shorter, not because size automatically improves collections. The startup capital range of $6,000 to $35,000 funds initial launch activity, not every steady-state cohort at once. Even the Conservative liquidity target slightly exceeds that range's upper endpoint. Ramp signed engagements in batches, retain early collections, negotiate lawful contractor milestones, or obtain committed funding before accelerating acquisition. Never bridge operating costs with money owed to customers.

Replace the uniform-spending approximation with a thirteen-week cash calendar before committing contractor capacity. Acquisition may be paid in week one, document extraction in week two, specialist review in week four, and the first customer fee only after a utility adjustment in month five. That front-loaded sequence can exceed average-burn float even when the annual margin is unchanged. Group invoices by customer, recovery rail, approval status, and expected unrestricted settlement week; retain a separate column for contractual holdbacks. In QuickBooks or Xero, reconcile the operator invoice to a customer statement and bank receipt, while the claim register preserves carrier or municipal evidence. Do not silently move aged items forward each week. Require a new dated event to change the forecast and mark unsupported collections as delayed. Customer concentration matters because a single treasury department can hold multiple otherwise valid fees. Stress the largest payer independently of average DSO. Where assignment or receivables funding is contemplated, review contractual restrictions and the applicable state's UCC Article 9 requirements with the funding provider. Funding proceeds are debt or asset-sale proceeds according to their substance, never additional recovery revenue or an improvement in operating margin.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the evidence-backed earning population
   - Export authorized accounts, unique claims, baseline versions, and enforceable fee schedules.
   - Remove duplicate, waived, expired, and otherwise ineligible opportunities before estimating earned operator fees.
2. Separate every cash-clock component
   - Record submission processing, invoice DSO, customer refund dependency, and incremental payout restrictions separately.
   - Use dated supporting events and avoid adding a lock already included in the observed collection interval.
3. Schedule committed costs and restricted balances
   - Place acquisition invoices, contractor milestones, overhead, risk liquidity, and customer payables into a thirteen-week forecast.
   - Exclude client-owned funds from available operating cash and test the earliest negative unrestricted balance.
4. Reconcile forecast changes to actual settlements
   - Match each operator receipt to its fee invoice and claim evidence every week.
   - Keep original expected dates visible, investigate aging, and release excess risk liquidity only after exposure expires.

## 4. Stress collectible yield, delivery overruns, and legal earning limits  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run sensitivities against the reconciled cases instead of replacing their canonical outputs. With all modeled costs held constant, a further 10% reduction in earned revenue lowers owner profit to $22,000 Conservative, $166,650 Base, and $414,000 Scaled. This is an incremental downside beyond the original eligibility scrub, not a restatement of that scrub. A separate 20% delivery labor overrun reduces profit by $6,400, $22,400, and $31,200, leaving $26,600, $194,750, and $472,800. Combining both shocks leaves $15,600, $144,250, and $382,800. These comparisons deliberately assume no immediate cost relief, even though a longer-term operator could stop acquisition or renegotiate specialist capacity. At unchanged modeled costs, revenue break-even is $77,000, $287,850, and $396,000, respectively; it is not a universal shutdown threshold because some expenses vary with volume. Examine which contracts can actually be reduced within thirty days before promising a margin recovery. For GPO cohorts, specifically stress a lower eligible purchase base and a delayed quarterly true-up. For parcel cohorts, stress expanded waiver exclusions rather than merely lower claim submission volume. Those mechanisms destroy collectible consideration even when the extraction software continues producing apparently complete work queues.

An extra thirty funded days consumes another $5,589 Conservative, $19,890 Base, and $27,781 Scaled under the same cash-burn assumptions. Delay alone does not reduce accrual profit when the earned fee remains collectible, but financing charges, extra collection labor, and ultimate credit losses do. If the incremental draw remains outstanding for an entire year at 12%, its approximate interest costs are $671, $2,387, and $3,334. Actual interest must use the draw balance and days outstanding; those annualized illustrations are not automatic deductions from the canonical cases. Maintain distinct eligibility, credit, and service-cost registers rather than assigning one unexplained haircut to every account. Eligibility records identify whether consideration was earned. Credit records identify an enforceable receivable whose customer may not pay. Service-cost records identify specialist rework or substantiation defense. A carrier reversal can affect revenue or a liability depending on the contract and prior recognition; obtain the appropriate accounting treatment before posting it. Review each reserve by recovery rail and aging bucket monthly. Release balances only against evidence that the exposure ended, never simply because the sales forecast requires a higher margin or the owner wants a distribution.

Protect revenue validity with precise operating requirements. Treasury Circular 230 section 10.27 governs contingent-fee arrangements within its applicable scope and contains specified exceptions; the restriction's application to particular services also requires attention to controlling judicial decisions. Do not treat a corporate expense-audit contract as blanket permission to charge a percentage on federal tax-return work. Separate engagements, service descriptions, qualified practitioners, and fee analysis before quoting specialty credits. Under 26 U.S.C. section 51(d)(13), the WOTC certification process generally requires the prescribed pre-screening notice and submission to the designated local agency within twenty-eight days after work begins; confirm any applicable relief and current program authorization. Section 6001 supports maintaining records sufficient to substantiate tax positions. When tax-return information is involved, section 7216 and its implementing regulations require evaluating permitted uses and disclosures by preparers. For Texas property-tax consulting, check Occupations Code Chapter 1152 registration requirements and exemptions before assigning representation. Keep carrier credentials scoped to the authorized account and comply with current API terms. The operating decision is simple: stop the affected earning stream when authority or substantiation fails, preserve unrelated valid work, and fund only the collectible remainder.
