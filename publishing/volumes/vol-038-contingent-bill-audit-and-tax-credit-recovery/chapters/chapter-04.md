## 1. Protect the collection reserve before buying production capacity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with the collection clock, not the software catalog. The startup range of $6,000–$35,000 buys a narrow production capability and a protected reserve, not twelve fully staffed service lines. Ring-fence $3,300 at the lean endpoint and $19,000 at the expanded endpoint, representing 55.0% and approximately 54.3% of starting cash. Keep that money in a separate business savings account with a weekly transfer rule tied to actual operating needs. The remaining $2,700 or $16,000 funds formation, insurance, specialist review, implementation, and measured acquisition. None of those allocations authorizes client refund advances. At $1,500 monthly fixed overhead, the lean reserve covers only 2.2 months before variable delivery costs; at $9,500, the expanded reserve covers only two months. Because first deals commonly take one to four months and refund issuance can take longer, neither endpoint automatically funds a worst-case launch. Maintain outside owner living reserves, reduce committed overhead, or secure additional unrestricted working capital before increasing delivery volume. A signed engagement is evidence of demand, not a substitute for collected cash. Put this distinction on the first page of the operating budget so a promising pipeline cannot silently consume the survival reserve.

Use the allocation matrix as spending ceilings rather than purchase targets. Formation spending covers the actual state filing, registered-agent requirement where applicable, and operating agreement; obtain an EIN directly from the IRS without paying an intermediary. Open operating and reserve accounts in the entity's name, document authorized signers, and keep client money outside both accounts. Prefer refunds paid directly to clients, followed by a separate fee invoice, rather than routing recovered funds through the audit business. Obtain written quotes for professional liability and cyber coverage describing expense auditing, tax-credit administration, subcontractors, and access to payroll records. A cheap policy excluding the actual work provides no useful operating protection. The specialist allocation purchases engagement-language review and case-specific technical decisions, not unlimited representation. Require contractors to sign confidentiality, access, work-product ownership, and deletion terms before receiving records. Purchase equipment only after checking whether existing encrypted devices meet the access policy. For a two-person expansion, price replacement hardware inside implementation rather than pretending it is free. Record every commitment with its cancellation date, recurring consequence, and account owner. This produces a cash map that survives an overdue utility refund without forcing rushed borrowing or premature sale of a weak claim.

Construct a thirteen-week cash forecast with separate rows for contracted work, accepted claims, client receipts, earned fees, and actual fee collections. Suppose a utility audit supports a $30,000 refund at a 35% share: the eventual operator fee is $10,500, but the treasury forecast remains zero until the expected payment date, and the revenue ledger follows the contract's earning conditions. If delivery requires $1,200 of specialist work immediately and the municipality takes sixteen weeks, the business must carry that $1,200 plus intervening overhead. A reserve test should therefore use fixed cash burn multiplied by delay months, add committed case costs, and subtract only reliable collected cash. At $1,500 overhead for four months plus $1,200 case costs, the requirement is $7,200, already above the lean launch's entire capital. The fallback is to defer the case, negotiate milestone payments compatible with the engagement, or begin with shorter-cycle parcel recoveries. Do not bridge the gap using another client's refund. Stop discretionary spending when projected unrestricted cash falls below eight weeks of fixed costs plus already committed delivery bills. Recalculate after every material rejection, refund delay, or contract amendment rather than waiting for month-end bookkeeping.

## 2. Assemble twelve software layers without buying twelve enterprise contracts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The twelve-layer matrix is a procurement baseline, not a claim that vendor checkout prices were verified today. Its exact tier amounts are explicit planning inputs, excluding tax, negotiated discounts, and implementation; confirm regional pricing and contractual features before purchase. Start with two identities, one production environment, and one recovery lane. Google Workspace supplies business mail and document collaboration, Bitwarden holds secrets, and Cloudflare manages DNS. Tailscale supplies restricted administrative connectivity rather than exposing database ports. Amazon S3 stores original evidence; Amazon Textract converts selected documents; GitHub stores code and versioned audit rules. Self-hosted n8n schedules approved jobs, while Supabase provides a structured case database if its selected plan meets the actual access requirements. Zoho CRM tracks prospects without tax identifiers, Wave records the operator's own invoices, and DocuSeal handles signed authority documents. Zero subscription price does not mean zero operating cost: self-hosting still consumes patching, backup, monitoring, and incident-response time. Assign a named owner and an exit export to every layer. Avoid paid connectors until a manual export proves the source contains useful claim evidence. A polished dashboard connected to the wrong carrier account merely accelerates incorrect work and makes its mistakes look more credible.

Provision one client at a time with separate storage prefixes, database access policies, encryption, and an explicit evidence-retention schedule. Preserve original invoices unchanged, calculate SHA-256 hashes, and store normalized tables separately so corrections do not overwrite the source. Use S3 versioning and a tested backup policy; reserve retention locks for records whose retention period has been deliberately approved. Extract account numbers, service dates, billed dimensions, tariff references, and charge amounts into a documented schema. Route low-confidence OCR fields to review, especially decimal placement, meter multipliers, and negative credits. A one-character extraction error can turn a valid utility credit into a duplicate demand. Put retry limits, backoff, idempotency keys, and exception queues around each integration. Read-only ingestion credentials must remain separate from any authorized claim-submission credential. Where carrier permissions are coarse, use approved exports or a proxy enforcing allowed operations rather than describing a broad credential as read-only. GitHub tests should cover duplicate invoices, credit reversals, missing service commitments, and account reassignment. Never place Form 8850 records, Social Security numbers, customer contracts, or production secrets in repository fixtures. Synthetic examples exercise parsing logic without spreading sensitive evidence across developer machines and hosted test logs.

The sample monthly subscription calculation is deliberately small: two Workspace seats cost $14, two Bitwarden seats $8, two active Tailscale users $12, two GitHub Team seats $8, and Supabase Pro contributes a $25 project baseline. At the matrix's storage and extraction assumptions, 100 GB of S3 Standard costs $2.30 before requests and transfer, while 20,000 Textract text-detection pages cost $30. The resulting $99.30 subtotal excludes hosting, backups, signatures requiring paid services, OCR features beyond text detection, taxes, and overages. Allocate another explicit allowance for those items rather than presenting $99.30 as total monthly overhead. The operating envelope also includes insurance, bookkeeping, specialist retainers, acquisition, and administrative support. Keep employee eligibility records out of general CRM and invoicing tools; grant payroll specialists access only to cases they service. Before production, export a case, restore its database record, retrieve its original invoice, and reproduce the calculation on a clean device. If the cheapest tier cannot enforce required access or export controls, upgrade that layer or use an appropriately secured alternative. Do not pay for enterprise branding while leaving recovery evidence without a tested restoration path or a responsible owner.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy evidence quality before buying more automation**
> 
> The strongest early savings come from reducing invalid claims and repeat handling, not from minimizing every software invoice.
> - Retain the carrier agreement version covering each shipment date; a current contract cannot establish an earlier service guarantee.

## 3. Price actual recovery units and reconcile every operating-cost dollar  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The required freight_detention_audit_contingency play starts with authorized UPS, FedEx, and LTL billing feeds, not speculative access to shipper accounts. Obtain signed Letters of Authorization and baseline invoice snapshots before disclosing tariff or refund findings. For an illustrative parcel cohort, 100,000 annual shipments with 1,200 eligible, timely claims averaging $18 produce $21,600 of client recovery. A 40% contingency yields $8,640 of operator revenue, within the play's 35%–50% split range. Neither total shipping spend nor the client's $21,600 refund belongs in operator revenue. If the carrier contract waives service guarantees or suspends a relevant commitment, remove late-delivery claims from the forecast immediately. Pivot toward dimension errors, duplicate charges, LTL classification evidence, and contract-rate benchmarking; these are not dependent on a service-guarantee payout, although their own terms and dispute deadlines still control. For example, 160 substantiated duplicate or rate errors averaging $55 recover $8,800 and earn $3,520 at 40%. Treat that as an alternative cohort, not an automatic addition to the first example. Freight class changes require shipment characteristics and applicable rating rules, not a model's guess. Preserve submission acknowledgments and client credits so the eventual fee invoice can be tied to realized recovery.

Customs work shares ingestion plumbing but requires a separate eligibility and filing workflow. Import CBP Form 7501 entry summaries through the client's authorized broker or ACE export route; do not assume a public read-only API exists for every entry. Match entry lines, duty payments, exported or destroyed merchandise, and claimant rights before describing an amount as eligible for drawback under 19 U.S.C. §1313 and 19 CFR part 190. For an illustrative provision allowing 99% recovery, $20,000 of fully qualifying duties supports $19,800 of potential recovery and a $7,920 fee at 40%, subject to the actual contract and permitted service arrangement. That arithmetic is not a universal eligibility rule. Route filing and customs-business questions to a licensed broker when required, and remove cases lacking export linkage or complete records. Across the volume's 12 mapped plays, reuse evidence storage and collections controls while maintaining distinct eligibility rules. A utility shared-savings example of $2,000 monthly verified savings at 35% earns $700 monthly and $25,200 across 36 months, but only $8,400 during twelve earned months. Never book the full contract duration as first-year operating revenue merely because the agreement was signed. Record baseline changes explicitly when rates, occupancy, or production volumes change.

Reconcile overhead to the canonical scorecard before adding another service. Conservative revenue of $110,000 less $77,000 total costs leaves $33,000 at 30%; Base revenue of $505,000 less $287,850 leaves $217,150 at 43.0%; Scaled revenue of $900,000 less $396,000 leaves $504,000 at 56%. The matrix offers one illustrative cost bridge: annual fixed overhead of $18,000, $60,000, and $114,000, with remaining delivery and other operating costs of $59,000, $227,850, and $282,000. These allocations explain the monthly overhead envelope; they do not replace the canonical totals or imply that all expenses fit inside $1,500–$9,500 monthly. Contractor counts represent available specialist roles, not three or four full-time salaries. Maintain the specified teams of 1 FT + 3 C, 1 FT + 3 C, and 2 FT + 4 C while testing actual capacity separately. Recognize only the operator's earned contractual consideration, assess variable consideration and principal-versus-agent presentation under ASC 606, and avoid applying the contingency percentage twice. WOTC processing fees should use a documented per-case or other permissible arrangement; employee wages and employer credits are not operator revenue. Count fee collections separately from recognized revenue when testing cash survival.

## 4. Launch only after authority, deadlines, restoration, and collections survive testing  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate commercial expense auditing from regulated representation before the first proposal leaves the business. Treasury Circular 230, 31 CFR §10.27, restricts contingent fees in covered practice and contains specific exceptions; its application must also account for relevant court decisions and the actual service performed. Do not copy a 40% freight recovery clause into federal return preparation or tax-credit representation. Obtain a qualified practitioner's written scope and permissible fee structure, and use Form 2848 or Form 8821 only when its distinct authority fits the task. Property-tax representation requires a state-specific review; Texas Occupations Code chapter 1152 supplies a concrete example of property-tax consultant registration requirements and exceptions. For WOTC, 26 U.S.C. §51(d)(13) generally requires prescreening on or before the offer date and submission to the designated state agency within 28 days after work begins. The statutory termination provision must also be checked for the worker's start date; do not assume a post-2025 extension. Configure ATS reminders around the applicable deadline, retain agency receipts, and exclude ineligible cohorts from sales forecasts. Circular 230 §10.22 reinforces diligence for covered practice, making substantiation a production requirement rather than an optional file-cleanup task.

Run a dry case through the entire production path before accepting a large invoice archive. The case file should contain signed authority, a baseline snapshot, source hashes, extraction results, applicable tariff or contract language, a reviewer decision, and a client-facing calculation. Submit nothing during the rehearsal unless the engagement expressly authorizes that action. Use carrier sandboxes where available, otherwise validate request construction offline and obtain the necessary production permissions. Read-only collection does not authorize filing a claim, changing an account, or communicating with a taxing authority. Test negative cases deliberately: an expired filing window, a waived service guarantee, a previous credit, an employee record missing timely prescreening, and a customs entry without export evidence. Each should produce a specific exception rather than a payable claim. Then disable a contractor account and verify that access disappears from storage, workflow credentials, and shared documents. Restore a deleted test record from backup and confirm its source hash. Record reviewer identity and timestamps without placing sensitive documents in application logs. Launch readiness is a demonstrated chain from lawful access to reproducible fee calculation, not a collection of subscription receipts or a successful login screen.

Finish with a collections rehearsal because technically correct claims can still produce an insolvent business. Agree who receives refunds, what evidence establishes receipt, when fees become due, and how reversals or partial credits are handled. For shared savings, define the baseline, exclusions, measurement period, and dispute process before revealing the opportunity. Simulate a $10,000 client credit with a 35% fee: issue a $3,500 operator invoice only when the contract's earning conditions are satisfied, then test a $2,000 credit reversal and calculate the corresponding $700 fee adjustment where the agreement requires it. Match collected fees to bank activity and keep unresolved differences in an exception queue. The owner reviews reserve coverage weekly; the technical reviewer signs calculation changes; the collections owner confirms receipts without altering claim evidence. These responsibilities can be held by a small team, but conflicting changes should receive independent review. Expand from one repeatable lane only after two closed cases demonstrate source completeness, defensible calculations, timely submission, and collected fees. If municipal issuance stalls, preserve the valid case and shift new production toward shorter-cycle eligible work. Scale the workflow that returns cash, not the dashboard that reports the largest theoretical opportunity.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the launch budget
   - Allocate startup cash and segregate at least half as working capital.
   - Use the endpoint matrix, replace allowances with quotes, and document recurring commitments before spending.
2. Provision the minimum authorized case
   - Configure the twelve layers around one signed engagement and one recovery lane.
   - Create separate evidence storage, least-privilege credentials, source hashes, and an exportable case record.
3. Rehearse valid and invalid claims
   - Run one supported calculation and the specified negative cases without unauthorized submission.
   - Retain reviewer decisions, deadline evidence, restoration results, and contractor-access revocation results.
4. Test the fee-to-cash close
   - Simulate client receipt, operator invoicing, fee adjustment, and bank reconciliation.
   - Update the thirteen-week forecast and release additional production only within demonstrated carrying capacity.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks that must produce evidence**
> 
> A reviewer records the result and supporting artifact for each check. A verbal assurance does not satisfy the production checklist.
> - Authority verified: signed client authorization names accounts, records, allowed communications, and separately permitted submissions.
> - Eligibility verified: contract terms, statutory dates, fee restrictions, and required practitioner credentials match the selected recovery lane.
> - System verified: least-privilege access, duplicate detection, source hashing, contractor removal, and full case restoration pass recorded tests.
> - Cash verified: the forecast includes refund delays, committed specialist costs, fee reversals, and at least eight weeks of fixed-cost coverage.
