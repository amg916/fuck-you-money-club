## 1. Sell a documented recovery, not a percentage of the client's spending  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Contingent corporate bill-audit and tax-credit recovery converts a documented billing error, tariff mismatch, assessment defect, or eligible credit workflow into an earned service fee. The operator does not buy the customer's receivable, own the utility account, or acquire the tax credit merely by discovering an opportunity. The engagement starts with signed Letters of Authorization, contractual access rights, baseline invoice snapshots, a defined lookback, and a precise payment trigger. For commercial expense recovery, a negotiated 25%–50% share can align payment with realized benefits; tax work requires a separate fee-eligibility analysis. A $40,000 utility refund at 35% creates $14,000 of operator fees, not $40,000 of revenue and certainly not the customer's $800,000 annual utility spend. The client ordinarily receives the refund directly and pays the earned invoice. This separation preserves a clean audit trail and avoids unnecessary custody of client funds. The service is therefore closer to specialized receivables investigation and contract administration than passive arbitrage. Finding an anomaly is only the first milestone; collecting a defensible fee requires proof, timely submission, acceptance, benefit verification, and a contract that survives reversals without an argument over who actually produced the recovery.

Five operator archetypes organize the work: parcel recovery specialists, utility and facilities auditors, telecom expense auditors, property-assessment specialists, and tax-credit processing teams. Each uses different evidence and different clocks. A parcel claim joins invoice lines to service commitments, shipment events, customer agreements, and any applicable guarantee exclusions. A sewer adjustment joins billed consumption to approved cooling-tower meter readings and the municipality's applicable rules. A property appeal needs assessment records, comparable evidence, a filing calendar, and appropriately authorized representation; it cannot be treated as another carrier refund ticket. WOTC processing connects timely prescreening, state workforce certification, eligible wages, and tax preparation, rather than promising cash merely because a new hire belongs to a potentially eligible group. The smallest viable team chooses one repeatable evidence pattern and contracts specialists for exceptions. A founder who markets every recovery category before securing that specialist bench creates an expensive coordination problem. Select clients with retrievable records, material recoverable errors, and a controller willing to confirm realized benefits. Reject accounts where another auditor already owns the same recovery rights or where contract language prevents the proposed claim. Exclusivity should identify specific accounts, categories, periods, and termination treatment rather than capture every future saving.

The commercial offer should distinguish historical refunds, prospective savings, and processing deliverables. Historical refunds depend on an accepted correction to a past charge. Prospective savings depend on a negotiated baseline and a measurable future reduction, commonly shared for 36 months. Processing fees compensate defined work such as screening, certification submission, and evidence assembly when that fee structure is permitted. These products should not share an ambiguous definition of 'recovered value.' For example, reducing a valid parcel minimum through negotiation creates prospective savings; it does not prove the old minimum was an overcharge. Similarly, a lower utility bill after a factory shutdown does not establish tariff audit performance. Write normalization rules for volume, service mix, rates, taxes, weather, occupancy, and customer-directed changes before disclosing findings. Give each finding a unique identifier tied to source records and a benefit category. Document prior disputes and concurrent vendor negotiations so attribution remains testable. The moat is accumulated evidence about what particular contracts, utilities, and agencies actually accept, together with a reliable handoff to specialists. A generic anomaly detector is useful triage, but it cannot substitute for enforceable rights, calibrated estimates, or records that survive the customer's next finance-team review.

>

> - For cooling-tower sewer adjustments, verify municipal meter approval and reading requirements before estimating an exclusion from billed discharge.
> - Create separate refund, prospective-savings, and tax-processing schedules so each fee has its own evidence requirement, payment trigger, and reversal treatment.

## 2. Reconcile earned revenue, owner profit, and the 36-month savings ledger  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical scorecard measures operator service revenue, not client economic throughput. Conservative annual revenue is $110,000 at a 30% net margin, producing $33,000 owner net profit and $77,000 of implied costs. Staffing remains 1 FT plus 3 contractors in both Conservative and Base, then 2 FT plus 4 contractors in Scaled. Contractor counts describe participating people, not four full-time salaries disguised as one employee. These figures are prescribed annual scenarios, not a claim that every recovery category supports identical margins. The stated $1,500–$9,500 monthly operating-expense range cannot be substituted for total scenario costs: delivery labor, specialist allocations, acquisition spending, and other modeled costs must still reconcile to the annual difference between revenue and profit. Startup capital of $6,000–$35,000 and a one-to-four-month first-deal window make collections timing important. A signed contingency engagement can consume analyst time for months before a municipality issues a credit. Track cash runway independently from booked revenue, and do not present the scenario's owner-profit measure as a personal after-tax cash calculation.

ASC 606 requires the operator to identify its customer, promised services, performance obligations, and transaction price, including the constraint on variable consideration. A speculative claim queue is not earned revenue. Neither is an entire 36-month savings estimate automatically recognizable at signature. Recognition depends on the specific performance obligation and when the associated consideration can be included without a probable significant reversal. Billing and cash collection can occur at different times from recognition. If a $100,000 refund generates a 40% earned fee, operator revenue is $40,000 when the applicable recognition conditions are satisfied; the remaining $60,000 belongs to the client. If the client pays a $50,000 service fee and another specialist receives $10,000, principal-versus-agent analysis determines whether the operator reports $50,000 with specialist expense or only a $40,000 retained share. Paying a subcontractor does not automatically require net presentation. Where a retained_share model applies, apply it once to the service-fee pool, never to underlying utility spend and never twice through both revenue and expense. No additional retained-share percentage is specified for these three canonical cases, so their supplied revenue and profit figures remain unchanged. Maintain a written accounting policy and reconcile claim status, invoices, recognition, and collections monthly.

An illustrative 36-month agreement shows why the savings ledger needs invoice-level discipline. Suppose an accepted tariff correction produces $2,000 monthly normalized savings and the operator earns 35% as benefits occur. The fee is $700 per verified month, $8,400 over twelve complete eligible months, and $25,200 over thirty-six complete eligible months. Those are different periods, not interchangeable revenue labels. Twenty such agreements operating for a complete year produce $168,000 of annual fees before reversals, not $504,000 booked immediately from their full terms. To illustrate the Base total without changing it, combine that $168,000 with $280,000 of earned historical-refund shares and $57,000 of earned processing fees: the result is exactly $505,000. At a 40% refund share, the $280,000 component requires $700,000 of accepted client refunds; that refund pool remains outside operator revenue. The processing component could represent 570 completed billable units at $100 each under lawful, clearly defined terms, rather than 570 promised tax credits. This mix is an arithmetic bridge, not supplied production history. Monthly controls should reconcile baseline changes, client benefit confirmations, disputed invoices, refunds reversed after review, and outstanding receivables. Track concentration by carrier, municipality, client, and specialist because one delayed payer can impair cash even when annual margins appear attractive.

Only the two supplied mappings carry canonical play identifiers; the remaining ten are descriptive operating lanes. The parcel mapping, parcel_carrier_refund_tariff_audit_contingency, joins authorized FedEx Billing Online, UPS Billing Center, and DHL invoice exports with shipment events, measured dimensions, and effective service agreements. At a negotiated 40% share, sixty accepted $25 refunds generate $1,500 for clients and $600 of operator fees. An additional $4,000 of accepted dimensional-weight or duplicate-billing corrections adds $1,600 in fees at the same rate. Neither calculation includes rejected, expired, suspended, or contractually waived claims. Negotiating a $0.20 reduction on 10,000 eligible monthly shipments creates $2,000 prospective monthly savings; a 35% share yields $700, subject to the agreed baseline. A valid minimum charge is not an error merely because another customer negotiated better terms. The acquisition mapping, multi_inbox_free_workspace_outbound_clusters, should be evaluated as a cost-and-capacity hypothesis, not a delivery entitlement. Microsoft 365 developer or partner benefits must expressly permit the proposed production use; Cloudflare Email Routing is not itself an outbound SMTP sending service. Do not build the economics around tenant fragmentation, domain swapping after restrictions, or unsupported claims that catch-all testing validates every recipient. The durable fallback is a smaller, relevant audience, verified acquisition rights, accurate sender identity, a permitted sending service, centralized suppression, and prompt handling of opt-outs under 15 U.S.C. §7704. Removing tracking pixels may reduce unnecessary data collection, but it does not create permission to send. Stop campaigns when account restrictions or complaint patterns require investigation. Measure booked qualified audits and recovered-fee contribution per acquisition dollar, rather than advertising mailbox count as the business moat.

The comparison matrix separates sixteen execution situations across those twelve plays because superficially similar claims often need different evidence. Utility tariffs are jurisdiction-specific public rate instruments, while waste and telecom recoveries frequently turn on negotiated contract terms. Property work adds state licensing and representation rules; Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1152 is one concrete example, not a nationwide license. Federal tax lanes demand particular care. Existing eligible cohorts can still require processing and substantiation. Section 51(d)(13) generally requires prescreening by the offer date and certification-request submission within twenty-eight days after work begins. A common qualified first-year-wage example is $6,000 multiplied by 40%, or $2,400, after satisfying the applicable group and hours requirements; this is a client credit, not operator revenue. A $100 processing charge is the operator's separate service fee. Research-credit support under Section 41 and state tax refunds require their own factual tests and fee review. Where evidence, authorization, or fee permissibility fails, use an eligible fixed-fee scope or decline the assignment rather than relabeling an unsupported recovery.

## 4. Build the evidence pipeline and enforce the conditions for repeatable profit  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A lean delivery system needs a claim ledger, not a dashboard that counts every detected anomaly as money. Store original invoices, effective agreements, shipment events, tariff versions, authorization documents, and submission receipts under client-specific access controls. Amazon S3 with versioning and appropriate retention settings can preserve source packages; PostgreSQL can hold normalized invoice lines and claim states; an approved OCR service can extract scanned tariff or meter records. OCR output remains a hypothesis until arithmetic, dates, units, account identity, and tariff applicability reconcile. Define states such as detected, qualified, submitted, accepted, realized, invoiced, collected, and reversed. A rejected claim must retain its reason so the next automation run does not resubmit it blindly. Use idempotency controls to prevent duplicate submissions and account-level reconciliation to detect missing invoice periods. Authorized carrier EDI feeds and available APIs can reduce handling time, but availability and permitted operations vary by account and carrier. Never assume a public tracking API also permits refund submission. When access changes, preserve the evidence package and move eligible work to a supported channel rather than bypassing restrictions. Publish weekly exception reports that identify blocked amounts, approaching deadlines, responsible specialists, and the specific evidence needed to advance each claim.

Fee design and specialist authority belong in intake, not in a footnote added after the client signs. Treasury Circular 230, 31 CFR §10.27, restricts contingent fees for covered matters before the IRS, subject to specified exceptions and the applicable judicial treatment of its scope. Do not generalize those exceptions into permission for every original-return credit engagement. Have the responsible qualified practitioner document whether the proposed work, representation, and compensation structure are permitted. Sections 10.22 and 10.34 also matter for diligence and return-position standards where applicable. A fixed processing fee does not cure unsupported eligibility or authorize an unqualified person to represent a taxpayer. Property-assessment assignments require an equivalent state-by-state review of licensing, authorized representation, and fee restrictions. Sensitive hiring and tax records need role-limited access, encryption, retention schedules, and vendor review. Where the operator is acting as a tax return preparer, 26 U.S.C. §7216 and its implementing rules govern certain uses and disclosures of return information; the FTC Safeguards Rule also applies to covered financial institutions, including tax preparation firms. Avoid collecting complete identity records when a documented status or specialist-held record suffices. These requirements shape architecture, staffing, and unit costs rather than merely adding paperwork.

Durable small-team economics emerge when the founder standardizes qualification and delegates bounded exceptions, not when they chase every apparent refund. The canonical 94/100 score combines revenue potential of 24, margin potential of 24, team efficiency of 19, startup accessibility of 14, and automation potential of 13. Treat that score as a prioritization framework, then test the actual operation with accepted-claim rates, analyst minutes per accepted dollar, recovery reversals, collection days, and contribution after specialist payments. A workflow producing $600 of fees but consuming eight hours at $75 per hour leaves zero before acquisition and overhead; automation cannot rescue a systematically invalid claim population. Conversely, a reusable tariff evidence package may lower handling effort across similar accounts without weakening review. Budget rework and delayed refunds explicitly. Specify who funds a fee reversal when a carrier rescinds a credit or a client benefit changes after appeal. Run a monthly cohort review by acquisition source and sub-vertical, and stop selling categories with weak substantiation or negative contribution. Scale from one proven evidence pattern to adjacent patterns only after the first produces collected fees and reproducible outcomes. The resulting asset is a trusted operating system for recovering documented value, not a pile of scraped invoices or a large inventory of mailboxes.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Scope and preserve
   - Execute category-specific engagement schedules and archive baseline records.
   - Record account identifiers, covered periods, existing auditor rights, permitted access methods, and the specialist responsible for regulated work.
2. Qualify and calculate
   - Join each proposed recovery to an effective rule and reproducible calculation.
   - Separate historical errors from negotiated future savings; apply exclusions, deadline checks, and normalization before assigning an expected fee.
3. Submit and track
   - Use supported channels and retain acknowledgments for every eligible submission.
   - Assign a claim identifier, reviewer, next follow-up date, and explicit reason whenever a carrier, utility, assessor, or agency rejects the request.
4. Verify and reconcile
   - Confirm realized benefits and reconcile recognized fees with invoices and cash.
   - Apply the accounting policy, maintain the 36-month savings ledger where relevant, resolve reversals, and update contribution metrics by client cohort.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before a finding becomes a submission**
> 
> A claim advances only when access, entitlement, evidence, and economics are documented. A missing condition creates an exception record, not a larger sales forecast.
> - Confirm signed Letters of Authorization, permitted platform access, category ownership, and any required professional license or representation authority.
> - Verify the effective contract or statute, available filing window, applicable exclusions, and an objectively supported eligibility calculation.
> - Preserve baseline invoices, source measurements, submission evidence, and a second-person review of material tax or contract interpretations.
> - Confirm the fee structure is permitted, the recognition policy fits the service, and reversal obligations still leave acceptable expected contribution.
