# Chapter 10: Claim Mechanics: Forms, Deadlines, and Documentation

*WOTC prescreening, IRS Form 8850 and DOL Form 9061, state workforce agency processing and IRS relief, R&D credit and Section 174A updates, 179D and 45L claims, sales and use tax refund expectations, and documenting contingencies*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Claim mechanics across WOTC, R&D, sales-tax refunds and 179D come down to one rule the practitioners keep repeating: the paper must exist before the benefit, and it must survive verification afterward. WOTC operators agree on a hard 28-day window for filing the paired Form 8850 and ETA 9061 with the state workforce agency. They also agree on a 120-hour floor and on credits of $1,200 to $9,600 per hire, with no cap on the number of hires. Cherry Bekaert's R&D team works three levers: the 280C election, which keeps 79% of the credit, the $500,000 payroll offset, and the new Form 6765 Section G. 179D engineers require a certified energy model before installation, prevailing wage and apprenticeship compliance, and a signed letter filed on Form 7205. The disagreements center on 8850 timing, SNAP lookback definitions, whether to amend prior R&D returns, and the minimum viable building size.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every WOTC operator hammers the same clock: the packet lives or dies inside a 28-day window. Workforce3One tells employers to submit the forms within 28 days after the new employee starts working. It also warns against sending them to the Internal Revenue Service or the U.S. Department of Labor in Washington, DC [w5ydUbSH5TI @ 00:03:41]. SC DEW repeats the 28-calendar-day rule, measured from the start date. It lists late filings alongside rehires, relatives and 1099 workers as ineligible [ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:05:22]. Nicole Larrison, the Louisiana Workforce Commission WOTC State Program Manager, says the application must be postmarked or uploaded within 28 days of hire. She calls it 'an irs mandated deadline so there's no way around it' [xtpFtLZJyrM @ 00:20:41]. Jason D. Knott adds the second non-negotiable: the employer files a completed Form 8850 and the ETA 9061 with the state workforce agency, and 'you can't just send one' [RmnPUe0eO7s @ 00:08:06]. The working consensus has three parts. The two forms travel as one unit. The state agency is the only inbox. A packet that lands on day 29 is dead paper. SC DEW goes a step further and tells employers not to hold the request while waiting on supporting documentation. The same session confirms there is no cap on the number of qualified hires an employer can push through [ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:05:42].

Outside WOTC, the agreement is just as blunt: a claim is only as strong as its paper trail. Stephanie Thomas and Eric Thomas of Sales Tax Sisters say the required documentation depends on the basis of the refund. The basis might be a client who was overcharged and reimbursed, tax overpaid on a purchase, or an error on a return. They state flatly that 'if you're going to go for a refund you have to be able to substantiate that the refund is due' [LibBgjGxja4 @ 00:02:50]. Their closing takeaway is documentation, documentation, documentation, backed by an audit trail for the entire audit period [LibBgjGxja4 @ 00:05:34]. Rich Smith applies the same logic to energy deductions: 'The documentation is just as important as the upgrade because without that 179D falls apart' [jW2x9diynM0 @ 00:05:14]. On R&D, Cherry Bekaert points to the new Form 6765 Section G, which requires business-component detail covering 80% of QREs or the first 50 business components [IYUnmXCy8I0 @ 00:39:33]. One presenter argues the heavier disclosure is 'going to reduce the incidence of of audit' rather than invite more scrutiny [IYUnmXCy8I0 @ 00:32:09]. The Section 174A session adds two points. Qualifying activities are measured by business component, and the component counts reported on the form should stay consistent from year to year [nR3lgfqRNqg @ 00:28:09].

The third point of agreement is sequence. The paperwork that unlocks a benefit has to exist before the triggering event, and the benefit is claimed only after an outside party signs off. IRS stakeholder liaisons Anika Pampei and Cheryl Jackson make three points about Form 8850. Page 2 carries four employer dates. The 'gave information' date must fall on or before the 'offered' date. The whole form is signed under penalty of perjury [onLA9L1sYIc @ 00:18:03]. SC DEW lays out the claim order step by step: obtain certification first, verify the retention minimum, apply the wage cap, and only then pass the result to the CPA [ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:07:29]. Rich Smith runs the same playbook on 179D. The energy model must be certified before installation, and the December phone calls arrive too late to rescue anything [jW2x9diynM0 @ 00:04:24]. In Smith's words, the best savings are 'earned in Q2 when you still got time to plan' [jW2x9diynM0 @ 00:09:16]. Regenerative Shift's panel of Caleb Quaid, CPA Randy Lucas and engineer Billy calls the signed certification letter 'The Magic Ticket.' The deduction is then claimed in the placed-in-service year on Form 7205, separate from depreciation [qYAmJlpkzAw @ 00:39:41]. Across all four programs the order is identical: qualify, document, certify, then claim.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The WOTC math is mechanical once the packet is certified. Jason D. Knott pegs the credit at 40% of wages for hires who work 400 or more hours and 25% for hires who work 120 to 400 hours. There is no cap except for veterans and long-term family assistance recipients, whose caps sit in the Form 5884 instructions [RmnPUe0eO7s @ 00:01:32]. SC DEW publishes the per-group ceilings: $2,400 for ex-felons hired within a year of conviction or release, $4,000 in year one and $5,000 in year two for long-term TANF, $1,200 for summer youth hired May 1 to September 15, $5,600 for veterans unemployed six months or more, and $9,600 for disabled veterans unemployed six months or more [ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:04:04]. Nicole Larrison frames the typical maximum as about $2,400, based on first-year qualified wages capped around $6,000. She stresses there is no limit to the number of new hires who qualify [xtpFtLZJyrM @ 00:08:58]. On the claim side, the IRS liaisons route taxable employers through Form 5884 into Form 3800, with a one-year carryback and a twenty-year carryforward. Tax-exempt employers use Form 5884-C against Social Security tax, for veteran hires only. The liaisons also warn against reducing payroll deposits in anticipation of the credit [onLA9L1sYIc @ 00:19:46].

Cherry Bekaert's R&D practice works the Section 174 and 280C levers with hard numbers. Domestic R&D is deductible again in 2025, and the method change is a one-page statement with no Form 3115. Unamortized 2022 to 2024 costs can be deducted in 2025 or spread over two years, and spreading can help with the QBI wage limit [IYUnmXCy8I0 @ 00:10:42]. The 280C election keeps 79% of the credit. The Section 174A session puts it plainly: 'if you're entitled to $100,000 of a credit, you get $79,000' [nR3lgfqRNqg @ 00:13:08]. Carolyn Smith Driscoll tells clients to run 280C both ways [IYUnmXCy8I0 @ 00:33:07]. Startups can apply up to $500,000 of credit against payroll, split 250 OASDI plus 250 Medicare. Startups also get 'five bites at the Apple' on the payroll offset [IYUnmXCy8I0 @ 00:34:55]. Form 6765 becomes mandatory for 2026. Filers with QREs under $1.5 million and gross receipts under $50 million are exempt from Section G. Officers go on line 38, and the IRS looks for 'whales' at the $10M level [nR3lgfqRNqg @ 00:30:16]. For refund claims, the CCM perfection window is extended to January 10, 2027. Disclosures now need three items, and job titles are enough. Cherry Bekaert adds that its prepared claims have drawn no 45-day letters [IYUnmXCy8I0 @ 00:43:20].

179D runs on engineering arithmetic. The Regenerative Shift panel sets the floor at 25% better than ASHRAE 90.1 and the maximum at 50%. The IRA lifted the rate from $1.80 to $5 per square foot and indexed it, to $5.36 in 2023 and $5.65 in 2024 [qYAmJlpkzAw @ 00:09:42]. Missing the prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements triggers an 80% cut. The apprenticeship ratio is 4:1, and a good-faith effort counts as an exemption [qYAmJlpkzAw @ 00:23:38]. Models run in Trace, HAP, EnergyPlus or DOE-2, with the proposed and baseline models run side by side and a non-design engineer doing the site visit. One lighting-only 115,000 square foot plant hit 35% and $3.75 per square foot, for $431,438 [qYAmJlpkzAw @ 00:44:14]. Rich Smith's client Greg owned a 100,000 square foot mixed-use building that already had cost segregation and 45L. A $600K upgrade, modeled by architect John, produced a $500K deduction. That delivered about $185K of same-year savings instead of decades of depreciation [jW2x9diynM0 @ 00:03:02]. Smith also stacks 45L at up to $2,500 per energy-efficient unit, with a five-unit minimum. In one modeled $800K purchase with $200K down, 179D adds about $25K and 45L adds up to $15K on top of cost segregation [6593ENbIrIY @ 00:09:37].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Screen Every New Hire at Offer
   - Run the Form 8850 pre-screen on all new hires on or before the day the job offer is made.
   - SC DEW tells employers to screen all new hires [SC DEW — ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:00:24]. Jason D. Knott recommends that employer and employee sit down and complete the forms together, because it makes the process easier [Jason D. Knott — RmnPUe0eO7s @ 00:10:59].
2. Assemble the Two-Form Packet
   - Pair the signed 8850 with the ETA 9061, attach eligibility documentation, and clear any consultant authority first.
   - Attach eligibility documentation to the 9061. With a conditional certification Form 9062, complete only the employer part [Workforce3One — w5ydUbSH5TI @ 00:02:38]. Consultants need a notarized, approved IRS 2848 power of attorney before they submit anything [Louisiana Dept of Public Safety and Corrections — xtpFtLZJyrM @ 00:23:27].
3. Submit Inside 28 Calendar Days
   - File through the state e-file portal or by mail with a true postmark before day 28.
   - SC DEW accepts e-file or mail only, with no email or fax, and consultants must request an account [SC DEW — ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:06:02]. Do not hold the request while waiting on documentation [SC DEW — ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:05:42].
4. Claim After Certification and 120 Hours
   - Confirm the certification letter and the 120-hour minimum, then file Form 5884 and retain records.
   - The hire must have worked at least 120 hours before the employer files. File Form 5884, or Form 5884-C for tax-exempt employers hiring veterans, and keep records for at least three years [Workforce3One — w5ydUbSH5TI @ 00:04:42]. ## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split on WOTC paperwork is when the Form 8850 lands in front of the applicant. Workforce3One draws a hard line: the 8850 needs to be completed on or before the day the job offer is made [w5ydUbSH5TI @ 00:02:11]. Workforce3One also wants eligibility documentation attached to the 9061. Jason D. Knott runs a looser pre-hire play where the employer may ask before hiring and the worker is free to refuse. His worked example walks an April 22 information date, an April 29 offer, and a May 2 hire before pulling line 24 documents such as police reports and criminal records for an ex-felon driver [RmnPUe0eO7s @ 00:07:14]. Knott also has the 9061 completed jointly. The IRS stakeholder liaisons on the VA OSDBU webinar take a third position on proof: certification rides on the employee's self-check, and if the applicant checks the box saying yes to receiving payments, that is all you need [onLA9L1sYIc @ 00:37:25]. Those same liaisons say the gave-information date must sit on or before the offered date and call handing the form over at the job offer best practice. SC DEW resolves the documentation standoff operationally, telling employers never to hold a request while waiting for documentation [ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:05:42].

Target-group definitions do not line up across the agencies running the program. SC DEW pays the $2,400 SNAP credit on six consecutive months of benefits for ages 18 to 39 [ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:01:38], while the IRS liaisons read the same group as the previous six months or at least three of the previous five months [onLA9L1sYIc @ 00:13:02]. Veteran SNAP splits too: SC DEW measures three of the 15 months prior to hire, and the IRS speaker said first 15 months of employment. Filing location is another fault line. The VA OSDBU slides said file in each employee's state, the form instructions say the business's state, and the speakers told employers to follow the form; meanwhile Louisiana's Nicole Larrison insists the program is the same in every state. Sizing the prize diverges as well. WOTC Planet pitches anywhere from $1,200 to $9,600 per qualifying hire across 14 detailed situations [OtVpur6j8k8 @ 00:00:39], and Workforce3One quotes the same $1,200 to $9,600 band for each WOTC-eligible hire. Larrison instead frames the typical maximum near $2,400 on first-year wages capped around $6,000, with veterans reaching $9,600, and walks nine target groups [xtpFtLZJyrM @ 00:08:58]. Both camps agree there is no cap on the number of qualifying hires.

On R&D catch-up, Cherry Bekaert's two panels converge on a playbook but leave operators real choices. One team says the most advantageous move in most cases is taking the deduction in the current year [IYUnmXCy8I0 @ 00:10:25], and recommends 2025 or 2026 to dodge the refund wait and keep gross credits; spreading over two years can help with the QBI wage limit. The other team lays out four options, running out amortization, deducting in 2025, splitting 50/50 across 2025 and 2026, or amending, then concludes it does not make sense to go back and amend for the myriad of reasons listed [nR3lgfqRNqg @ 00:20:26]. Carolyn Smith Driscoll adds that 280C should be run both ways. On 179D, the Regenerative Shift panel openly disagrees on minimum building size: Caleb Quaid calls 12,500 square feet on the edge and says 15,000 to 20,000 works, while CPA Randy Lucas puts the cutoff around 20,000 to 25,000, down from 50,000, warning against spending $12 to get a $10 tax deduction [qYAmJlpkzAw @ 00:50:47]. Rich Smith pushes the other direction, pitching roughly $330,000 in claimed savings on a two-story building just over 5,000 square feet by stacking cost segregation, 179D, and 45L [6593ENbIrIY @ 00:00:17].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The WOTC kill switch is the 28-day clock. Workforce3One says forms must be submitted within 28 days after the new employee starts [w5ydUbSH5TI @ 00:03:41], and Louisiana's Nicole Larrison calls it an IRS mandated deadline with no way around it, postmarked or uploaded inside the window [xtpFtLZJyrM @ 00:20:41]. SC DEW adds a mailroom trap: postage meter stamps do not satisfy the postmark rule, and email or fax submissions are refused outright, leaving only e-file or mail [ujWX1IZPyFc @ 00:07:11]. The IRS liaisons expect the state agency to decline anything past 28 days. The only reported escape hatch was narrow: Notice 2021-43 extended the deadline to November 8, 2021 solely for designated community resident and summer youth hires made during the empowerment zone lapse. The second trap is hours. Jason D. Knott warns that a hire working only 20 hours in the year produces no credit, since nothing federal is available below 120 hours [RmnPUe0eO7s @ 00:11:55], and Workforce3One repeats the 120-hour floor before filing. Knott's percentages bite at the margin too: 25 percent of wages between 120 and 400 hours, 40 percent at 400 or more. Rehires, relatives, and 1099 workers are dead on arrival per SC DEW.

Sales Tax Sisters Stephanie and Eric Thomas report the refund side bites back. A material refund can trigger an audit of the whole company, because the state is not going to hand over $200,000 if you owe it a penny [LibBgjGxja4 @ 00:01:30]. The old shortcut of self-crediting on a return or filing an amended return is closing: one client tried it recently and was refused, with the state's position that it will not give you the use of a refund it has not verified [LibBgjGxja4 @ 00:02:28]. Expect desk audits, and expect clients who fat-finger an extra zero to grind through the full refund process anyway. Their remedy: documentation, documentation, documentation; do not throw money at this; keep records from the old system when switching platforms; and remember that automation is garbage in, garbage out, so the audit trail must cover the entire audit period [LibBgjGxja4 @ 00:05:34]. Auditor John Kelly of Professional Workshops Inc. confesses a parallel blind spot on the review side: he always forgets to read the minutes, and missing items buried there makes the auditor look really silly, even though reading minutes is required in five or six or 10 sections [loBLG0SW_90 @ 00:01:22].

On R&D, Cherry Bekaert's Vivian Kors and Brian Moors list the amending traps firsthand: a reduced credit under 280C, refund claims taking up to a year, IRS data scans that may wrongly trigger CCM, and deferred-tax-asset pricing exposure from ownership and rate changes [nR3lgfqRNqg @ 00:14:03]. They report court results running 90 to 95 percent unfavorable to taxpayers, citing Meyer Borgman, where payment was not contingent on success, and Moore, where the COO's time was undocumented; examiners attack funded research more than whether you performed qualified research [nR3lgfqRNqg @ 00:52:48]. Have contracts reviewed before execution, they advise. State windows slam shut fast: once a 30-day window closes you are out of luck, and one $5 million pool was exhausted by March. On 179D, Rich Smith warns the energy model must be certified before installation, so December calls are too late, and that documentation is just as important as the upgrade because without it 179D falls apart [jW2x9diynM0 @ 00:05:14]. The Regenerative Shift panel adds that missing prevailing wage and apprenticeship means an 80 percent cut, and the 90.1-2007 baseline gives way after 2027 to a lighting baseline 56 percent stricter and an envelope baseline 25 percent stricter [qYAmJlpkzAw @ 00:23:38].

> **OPERATOR GATE — Kill-Switch Checks Before Any Claim Goes Out**
