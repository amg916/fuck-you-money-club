# Chapter 07: Protecting Commissions and Collecting at Closing

*Aircraft and yacht fees, jet-broker commission agreements and markups, circumvention risks, buyer-broker agreements, seller-paid compensation disputes, escrow instructions, and wire-fraud prevention*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Jet, yacht, commodity and real estate closers agree on one rule: lock the fee in writing before the deal gets exposed. That means a listing, an off-market exclusive, a one-page commission agreement naming the buyer, or a non-circumvention clause. Then strip tail and serial numbers so nobody can go around you, and treat closing-day wires as the main point of loss. The numbers are blunt. Aircraft fees run 1% to 10%. A 10% yacht commission nets the working broker about 2.5% after splits. Charter margins swing from 5% to 30% per trip, and a clean disbursement form pays in 24 hours. The disagreements center on the spread. Jet brokers teach net-price and intermediary resale structures, while MarginCallMedia calls the undisclosed markup a conflict that drains clients and squeezes operators. Residential agents instead fund buyer-paid fees through rate credits and seller concessions.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every closer in this group starts from the same rule: no signature, no specs. The Ultimate Jet Guide host says the single most important thing is having something in writing that spells out how you get paid before you start sending specs. When two end users deal through you, you need a signed piece of paper from at least one of them committing to your fee [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:05:44]. Sam Cutting runs the commodity version of the same play. Source the seller and get a commission or partnership agreement giving you permission to sell and promote. If you hold the buyer, get the buyer to sign a commission agreement instead. The broker who actually connects buyer and seller collects far more often than the broker who introduced a broker to a broker [QguZjBADFoA @ 00:01:04]. The Pryderi Podcast treats the paper as protection for a real asset. Introductions to family offices, billionaires and ready buyers take months or years to build, and a non-circumvention clause makes going around the middleman a contractual violation exposed to damages [rnUvOWiFpDQ @ 00:01:35]. Cutting adds a hard filter on top of the paperwork. When an NCNDA lands with 14 intermediaries attached, that deal is never going to close, so get out of any chain with more than two entities [QguZjBADFoA @ 00:00:22].

The second point of agreement is that commission is a percentage of price, so asset size drives the check more than the rate does. Alex Bengoechea quotes aircraft brokers at 1% to 10% and walks the math. A 2006 Hawker 850 XB at $5 million and 1.5% pays $75,000. A 1978 Cessna 414 at $500,000 and 5% pays $25,000. A 1946 Aranca Champ at $50,000 and 10% pays only $5,000 [Uhqo4raDQ0k @ 00:00:38]. The headline rate also never lands whole. Christopher of Yacht Experts says yacht deals typically carry 10%, split 5% to the buyer's broker and 5% to the seller's broker. The employing licensed broker then takes 50% of each side, so the working broker nets about 2.5% of the sale price [NUVwWnSq3A0 @ 00:01:38]. Yacht Zero describes the same 10% being carved between the listing broker, the person bringing the buyer, and the brokerage houses they are employed by [oEXYo1_yeOU @ 00:00:28]. MarginCallMedia separates the two aviation money streams. Aircraft sales brokers earn a predictable commission paid at closing on low-frequency, high-value deals. Charter brokers take anywhere from 5% to 30% per trip on smaller, frequent bookings [PWGk1rSbFhA @ 00:01:09].

The last consensus point is that the commission is only real once the wire clears, and closing day is where money gets lost. Hauseit calls double- and triple-confirming wire details the single most important takeaway, because an email account at the attorney's firm can be compromised. Call the office, ideally reaching someone you met, read the account and routing numbers aloud, and match them before sending, since a wire fraud victim has little remedy [KrsoinTC_10 @ 00:00:00]. Family Split Strategy explains how the attack works. Hackers get into the weakest inbox on the CC chain, such as a small realtor, inspector or insurer working off home Wi-Fi. They register a look-alike title domain, build a matching website in an hour or two, and email fake wiring instructions [DZe5D7_TCDA @ 00:06:22]. The same speaker applies the defense to brokers directly: a title company should send a test transaction before sending off a commission to a realtor [DZe5D7_TCDA @ 00:12:44]. Lauren of Title's Not Borin' adds the structural constraint. Escrow is a neutral third party that can only act on mutually agreed instruction, and any contract amendment has to reach escrow in writing within three days of acceptance [IK3LgAFr-QQ @ 00:02:05].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The Ultimate Jet Guide host lays out the off-market capture sequence step by step. If the owner will not sign a normal listing, ask for an exclusive limited to off-market activity for 30, 60 or 90 days, or even a week. In exchange, promise not to publish on Controller, or agree to remove the tail number from everything [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:00:58]. If the owner only says bring me a buyer, scrub the tail and serial numbers from every photo, including cockpit shots. Avoid sending maintenance reports unless you trust the broker, and send only a spec sheet with times and upgrades. Anyone who can identify the plane can call the seller and say let's not pay the broker [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:01:38]. Get the seller to agree verbally to a number such as 6% or 4% early. When a buyer bites, send a one-page commission agreement naming that buyer and the amount, payable only if that specific deal goes through. Once it is signed, the floodgates open and the buyer can get anything [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:03:13]. Alex Bengoechea notes why starting on smaller aircraft is easier. Jets and turboprops demand JetNet or Amstat, professional pictures and Controller ads, none of them cheap, plus a solid track record [Uhqo4raDQ0k @ 00:01:20].

When neither side will pay, the jet playbook shifts to the spread. The Ultimate Jet Guide host first asks the buyer to cover the fee. If the buyer refuses, he asks the seller for a net number, say $2 million, and keeps anything above it [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:04:13]. The transparent route writes that split into the agreement, but if pre-buy squawks force a $100,000 price cut, the broker earns nothing. The alternative uses an intermediary company that contracts the aircraft at $2 million and offers it to the buyer at $2.1 million with the commission included. The broker pockets the $100,000 difference, and each side chooses whether to proceed [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:05:04]. MarginCallMedia maps three charter pay models. The operator cut is kicked back out of wholesale profit. The transparent fee appears as separate line items. The undisclosed markup, which it calls the most prevalent method, buys wholesale and resells at whatever the market tolerates, and sub-brokering stacks margin at every hop [PWGk1rSbFhA @ 00:01:42]. Sam Cutting's control play is to take payment yourself: collect from the buyer, pay the seller and keep the exact commission you set. Do this only after full due diligence on the seller, ideally including a face-to-face factory visit [QguZjBADFoA @ 00:01:45].

Splits with outside parties get negotiated down to an exact figure. The Ultimate Jet Guide host asks every broker, pilot or management company early how they are getting paid. When one says whatever you think, he forces a number. If they demand $50,000 out of a $50,000 commission, he counters at $10,000 for just bringing the guy, or $25,000 for getting the deal across the line [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:07:46]. In residential, Brandon Mulrenin and his lender guest show how to fund a buyer-paid fee on a $460,000 purchase. Raising the rate from 6.125% to 6.625% yields a 1% credit, about $4,300, and lifts the payment from $2,725 to $2,867 [5YNeLidiqqU @ 00:05:44]. The other route writes the price at $473,800 with a $13,800 seller concession. The payment rises only $81, a 170-month or 14-year break-even. The buyer agent fee counts as a customary fee outside the 3% concession limit, but it has to be in the offer before the appraisal is ordered [5YNeLidiqqU @ 00:11:38]. Think Real Estate reports that well over 90% of sellers still agree to pay a buyer broker when asked in the offer. The average buyer's agent commission sits at 2.37%, down from 2.45% [wBbi84Xi1Bc @ 00:01:32].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock The Fee Before Exposure
   - Secure a listing, off-market exclusive, or verbal fee agreement before any identifying details leave your hands.
   - Ask the owner for a short exclusive limited to off-market activity, then confirm a commission number such as 6% or 4% verbally before pitching any buyer [The Ultimate Jet Guide — 1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:02:49]
2. Anonymize And Paper The Specific Buyer
   - Send only a scrubbed spec sheet, then convert interest into a signed one-page commission agreement naming the buyer.
   - Strip tail and serial numbers from photos and reports, and if the buyer's side is the one you control, get the buyer to sign a commission agreement instead [Sam Cutting - Red Flags Verification — QguZjBADFoA @ 00:01:25]
3. Settle Every Split In Writing
   - Ask each outside broker, pilot or management company how they are getting paid, and agree on an exact dollar figure.
   - Have the conversation early and do not let them control it; split 50/50 only when they are actually doing the work to get the deal across the line [The Ultimate Jet Guide — 1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:06:29]
4. Run The Disbursement And Wire Check
   - Complete every field on the commission disbursement form and confirm the funding entity's side a day or two before closing.
   - Verify wire details by phone using a number you found yourself, and require a test transaction before the commission wire is released [Family Split Strategy — DZe5D7_TCDA @ 00:12:44]

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether a broker should get paid from a signed commission line or from a spread. The Ultimate Jet Guide treats the spread as a fallback. If the seller refuses to pay and the buyer refuses too, you ask the seller for their number and take anything above $2 million. You then either write that into the agreement transparently, or run it through an intermediary company that puts the aircraft under contract at $2 million and presents it to the buyer at $2.1 million including commission [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:04:13] [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:05:04]. Sam Cutting goes further for freelance brokers: add the fee on top of the seller's price, or take payment yourself, pay the seller, and control the deal after face-to-face due diligence at the factory [QguZjBADFoA @ 00:01:45]. MarginCallMedia attacks exactly that model from the charter side. It calls the undisclosed markup the dominant compensation method and a blind premium that clients can neither verify nor negotiate, and says fee-only consultancies passing the operator's wholesale price through with zero markup are fracturing the industry [PWGk1rSbFhA @ 00:02:17]. The practical decision is whether your buyer will ask for two distinct numbers, because a broker who deflects that question gets read as hiding a spread.

Practitioners also split on how much weight a non-circumvention agreement can carry. Pryderi Podcast frames it as extremely valuable. Leads and relationships built up over months or years are worth real money, and an NCNDA makes going around the introducer a contractual violation that can expose the circumventing party to damages [rnUvOWiFpDQ @ 00:01:35]. Sam Cutting is blunter about enforcement. If you lack the money to take someone to court, and the other side is likely in another country, it is going to be difficult. Some attorneys may take a case on a no-win, no-fee basis, but only if you hold irrefutable evidence that you were cut out and the NCNDA was breached [QguZjBADFoA @ 00:02:07]. Cutting's real defense is structural rather than legal: get out of any situation with more than two entities. The Ultimate Jet Guide runs a third playbook built on information control instead of paper. When the seller won't sign a listing, strip tail and serial numbers from photos, cockpit shots, and spec sheets, and hold back maintenance reports. Then convert the deal to a one-page commission agreement naming the specific buyer before the floodgates open [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:01:24] [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:03:13]. Even Pryderi concedes the agreement is not always necessary.

On the size of the commission itself, the yacht and real estate camps pull in opposite directions. Christopher of Yacht Experts calls the typical 10% yacht fee part of an entrenched and inefficient model. The fee splits 5% per side, then employing licensed brokers take 50% of each side, so the working broker nets 2.5% while the employing broker adds very little if any value. Christopher's response was to form a licensed employing brokerage in Florida to run a more reasonable commission structure [NUVwWnSq3A0 @ 00:01:59]. Yacht Zero accepts the 10% and argues that the only thing more expensive than agreeing to it is not paying it, because unsold yachts lose value as every similar sale drops the price. Yacht Zero's leverage play is to make the listing broker carry professional photos, dockage, and boat show costs [oEXYo1_yeOU @ 00:00:10]. In residential, Think Real Estate reports that well over 90% of sellers still pay a buyer broker commission when the offer asks for it. The average buyer's agent fee slid only from 2.45% to 2.37%, about an $800 discount on a $1 million sale [wBbi84Xi1Bc @ 00:01:32]. Brandon Mulrenin agrees that sellers will still pay the vast majority of buyer agent commissions, and builds loan-structure workarounds only for the rare deal where the seller pays nothing [5YNeLidiqqU @ 00:15:26].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure The Ultimate Jet Guide describes is the transparent spread that evaporates in pre-buy. If you stand to make a hundred grand above the seller's $2 million and squawks force a hundred-grand price reduction, you get paid nothing [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:04:39]. The same speaker warns against letting a referring broker or pilot take control of the conversation. When someone asks for 50 grand and there is only 50 grand of commission in the deal, you pin them to an exact number. You then negotiate toward 10 grand if they only brought the buyer, or 25 if they are helping get the deal across the line [1ILeO7AapJM @ 00:07:46]. Sam Cutting describes the chain-length trap in terms of payout odds. The broker who introduced a broker to the broker who is direct to the buyer or seller has slim chances of getting paid, while the one who connects buyer and seller directly fares far better [QguZjBADFoA @ 00:02:30]. MarginCallMedia shows the downstream cost of sub-brokering. Every layer quietly takes its own margin, and squeezed operators respond by trimming crew rest, hiring less experienced pilots, or delaying crucial maintenance [PWGk1rSbFhA @ 00:02:50]. Every extra hand in the chain shrinks the check and raises the odds that it never clears.

At closing, the commission and the down payment both ride on wires, and Family Split Strategy describes how they get stolen. Hackers break into the weakest inbox in the email chain, often a small realtor, inspector, or insurance office working on home Wi-Fi or in a coffee shop. They read who the title company and buyer are, register a lookalike domain, and build a matching website in an hour or two. Then they email fake wiring instructions complete with logos and a phone number that rings to the scammer [DZe5D7_TCDA @ 00:04:08] [DZe5D7_TCDA @ 00:06:22]. The fix they teach costs close to zero. Send a test wire of $1 to $1,000 and confirm receipt by walking into the real office or calling a number you looked up yourself. Then send the full balance to the same unchanged account. Title companies should run the same test before they release a commission to a realtor [DZe5D7_TCDA @ 00:12:30]. Hauseit adds a verbal check: call the attorney you have actually met and read the account and routing numbers aloud. Once a fraudulent wire lands, there is not much you can do to remedy it [KrsoinTC_10 @ 00:01:37]. The recovery window Family Split describes is narrow: file with IC3 and push the financial fraud kill chain, usually within 72 hours.

Slower failures come from paperwork and logistics rather than theft. Equity Real Estate warns that any missing or wrong field on the commission disbursement and wire instructions form can hold up the check. The agent fills in every field, especially the email addresses. The agent then coaches the title or escrow side to list the exact wire amount, their bank name, and contact information, and touches base a day or two before closing so the commission lands by direct deposit within 24 hours [mVFdzEqW2M4 @ 00:01:06]. Giuseppe Battaglioli has seen buyers hit daily wire limits at banks with no local branch. One client had to drive to Kansas to get a cashier's check before closing [YtS2MXC9Bps @ 00:01:07]. Lauren's escrow rule is just as rigid. As the neutral third party, escrow acts only on mutually agreed instructions, so contract changes must reach escrow as written amendments within three days of acceptance [IK3LgAFr-QQ @ 00:02:05]. Steve, the lender on Brandon Mulrenin's episode, hit the timing trap on a live loan. The appraisal came back exactly at contract price, which killed a late request to raise the price and add concessions [5YNeLidiqqU @ 00:15:11]. That is why any commission-funding concession has to be written into the original offer.
