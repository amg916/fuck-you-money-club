# Chapter 05: NDAs and Non-Circumvention Agreements

*NDA and NCND distinctions, commodity-trading commission protection, disputed ICC templates, confidentiality walkthroughs, notarization questions, non-solicitation, non-compete provisions, and signing risks*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Brokers, commodity intermediaries, and CRE listing agents converge on one point: an NDA only stops talking, while a non-circumvention clause stops the deal from closing without you. Practitioners demand identified and authorized signatories, a specific defined opportunity, substantive non-public information, execution before disclosure, and a reasonable term, commonly two to five years with two treated as the safe default. The ICC model occasional intermediary contract, publication 769, is the only genuine ICC NCNDA; any paper quoting ICC 400, 500, or 600 regulations is flagged as fake. Street mechanics include auto-populated DocuSign NDAs that unlock data rooms, liquidated damages clauses, and arbitration chosen deliberately by seat. Disagreements center on arbitration (plaintiff-friendly versus defendant-risky), term length, whether to leave governing law blank, and whether buyers should accept commission-liability language in broker NDAs.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The first thing every operator in this space hammers is that an NDA and a non-circumvention promise are two different weapons. Attorney Nancy Greene frames the NDA as a 'don't tell anyone about my stuff' clause and names the loophole bluntly: if the other side tells no one and simply uses your opportunity without you, the NDA probably has not been violated, because they just cut you out of the deal. Her fix is a non-circumvention provision, a 'don't do my stuff without me' clause, which she calls possibly the most powerful contract term operators are not using [FOUV15TeRLY @ 00:01:39]. IXH Trading Academy opens with the exact nightmare: a broker introduces a supplier to a buyer, and three weeks later the two close directly with no call, no commission, no credit [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:00:01]. Their definition stacks two promises into one contract: nobody goes around the introducer without written permission and fee sharing, and prices, client names, shipping data, and terms stay confidential [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:00:42]. Dr. Chad Michael Altieri puts the asset plainly: in commission, commodity, financing, and cross-border deals, the real asset is not the paper but the access [sm21N5L0nl8 @ 00:00:43]. That gap is why the combined NCNDA exists.

Enforceability prerequisites line up across camps. Trade Base Hub says courts do not enforce agreements where parties cannot be clearly identified, so each party should complete a client information sheet or KYC process and provide valid government-issued photo ID [yrJW_zsMUXM @ 00:00:33]. The same desk names vague scope as one of the most common reasons NCNDAs fail, and insists on a defined opportunity or a formal annexure of scopes updated and executed by all parties. IXH backs the identity layer: list full legal names, representatives, and official addresses, then confirm the signatory holds a power of attorney or right of signature for the company [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:02:03]. iTopixs, walking a three-party Russian export blend crude NCND, points to the authority warranty in which each party represents it has actual authority and has taken every step required, including a board resolution if needed, and notes the companies sign, not the individuals [l9rVUJ2duT8 @ 00:18:43]. On substance, Trade Base Hub says only substantive and unique information qualifies, while publicly available material is not protected. Timing is non-negotiable too: the NCNDA must be executed before any protected information is shared, signed by authorized parties, and stored with proper records [yrJW_zsMUXM @ 00:01:53].

Two more consensus points separate pros from victims. First, an NCNDA is a shield, not a paycheck: Trade Base Hub states flatly that NCNDAs do not define payment, commissions, or services, and using one as a substitute for a compensation agreement is a common and costly mistake [yrJW_zsMUXM @ 00:01:36]. IXH agrees, saying the intermediary's commission is often secured by a separate fee protection agreement and must be put in writing with amount, currency, timing, and payment route [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:08:10]. Second, the fake ICC paperwork circulating online is a scam signal. Cangem Global says there is no such thing as an ICC irrevocable master fee protection agreement, that 100% of IMFPAs and 99% of NCNDAs you will encounter are fake, and that any document citing ICC 400, 500, or 600 belongs in the garbage bin [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:00:24]. Brufokes African Foods Export recorded their own screen while buying the authentic copy, confirmed the fake version carries phony references to ICC regulations 400, 500, and 600, and said such a document will not be enforced by the ICC [suL1t0XynNw @ 00:00:42]. The genuine document is ICC publication 769, the model occasional intermediary contract, sold through the ICC business bookstore and national committees.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The ICC model is the commodity desk's gold standard, and Cangem Global walks its mechanics line by line. It is ICC publication 769E, a 27-page e-copy bought directly from the ICC website; purchasers can reuse it for any number of deals but cannot redistribute or resell it [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:04:58]. The fillable special conditions start around page 15 or 16, and the structure is rigid: one intermediary, who receives commission, and one counterpart, who pays it. You cannot stack sellers and buyers in one column, and Cangem warns that taking commission from both sides gets complicated and can fall into conflict of interest, so pick one payer [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:06:53]. UpCounsel describes the same split as Part A special conditions completed by each party and Part B standard ICC clauses, with intermediary roles running from passive introductions to active negotiation support and performance monitoring [B7w7HuoPnsA @ 00:00:38]. IXH notes an updated edition of the ICC model was published in 2024 and that it covers intermediary services, remuneration, confidentiality, the undertaking not to compete, term, and applicable law and dispute resolution [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:03:19]. Remuneration options inside the form include a lump sum, a percentage, or case-by-case pricing, since one document can cover many deals rather than a single transaction.

On the real-estate and business-brokerage side, the mechanics are about speed and leverage. Ron Rohde, on Adventures in CRE, says roughly 99% of listing-broker confidentiality agreements are now electronic signature documents that automatically grant data-room access, and recommends auto-populating the prospect's name straight from CRM entries [EL2eIKZ_OV8 @ 00:01:01]. He wants the property identified precisely even across multiple parcels and outparcels, a carve-out for public information, use limited solely to the purchase, and separate forms for direct principals versus brokers receiving information for a client. His arbitration logic is tactical: as the plaintiff holding the data you want arbitration because it is cheaper and faster; on the defense side you avoid it, since a single arbitrator makes it very high risk [EL2eIKZ_OV8 @ 00:03:33]. David C Barnett flips the script for buyers facing broker NDAs that demand financials. If a deal needs $100,000 down and you hold $500,000, move $100,000 into a separate account, write '$100,000 plus' on the net worth statement, and show a redacted statement over Zoom [wHxYmcrcq4M @ 00:04:06]. He also tells buyers to hand the broker their own NDA protecting buyer information; brokers can refuse, but it signals sophistication [wHxYmcrcq4M @ 00:05:43].

The clause stack that holds up is consistent and numeric. Santiago Cueto lists four essentials: an iron-clad non-circumvention clause covering existing and potential contacts, a confidentiality clause, a liquidated damages clause that pre-sets the financial penalty so enforcement is predictable and costly litigation is minimized, and a duration-and-remedies clause naming injunctive relief or actual damages [6G7yTaLd04k @ 00:01:11]. On numbers, iTopixs treats two years from disclosure as reasonable, has seen one, two, and five, and says courts may strike or revise anything drifting toward five years or more as an unreasonable restraint; the same template adds a two-year non-solicitation of staff, a reasonable-care floor, need-to-know access, and return-or-destroy backed by a sworn statement covering electronic copies [l9rVUJ2duT8 @ 00:16:17]. Cangem puts the street ceiling lower: one to three years, with three already 'a hard bet' and anything beyond it 'daydreaming' [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:09:23]. Clausehound adds that non-compete and non-solicit terms are not core to every NDA and are often too preliminary for early talks, but where client lists or sales leads change hands, a non-circumvent stops the recipient exploiting those leads, and these covenants usually survive termination [HN8Y7XiYRGE @ 00:01:56].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether only one paper counts. Cangem Global Corporation takes the hard line: 100% of documents calling themselves an IMFPA are fake, 99% of documents circulating as NCNDAs are fake, and the only genuine ICC version is ICC publication 769E, the occasional intermediary non-circumvention non-disclosure model sold on the ICC website [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:00:24]. Cangem still grades a non-ICC NCNDA as an okay document you can work with, but warns there will be no ICC or third-party arbitration behind it and a lot of limitations [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:03:38]. Trade Base Hub runs the opposite playbook: properly structured NCNDAs are enforceable both domestically and internationally because courts focus on clarity, identification, scope, and substance, not geography, and poor drafting fails everywhere [yrJW_zsMUXM @ 00:02:09]. iTopixs Videos sits in the working middle, walking through a three-page, three-party NCND on Russian export blend crude that sends disputes to arbitration in the District of Columbia, lets a court of competent jurisdiction enforce the award, and picks a body of international contract principles instead of any national law [l9rVUJ2duT8 @ 00:19:05]. IXH TRADING Academy bridges both camps: many parties start from the ICC model, but whether the agreement holds up depends on the drafting and on the governing law, not on the logo at the top of the page [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:05:03].

Term length is where the numbers diverge most. Cangem Global Corporation tells intermediaries to fill the ICC term field at one, two, or three years, says even three years is a hard bet with a counterpart, and calls anything beyond that daydreaming [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:09:43]. IXH TRADING Academy publishes a wider band, commonly two to five years and longer in some sectors, as long as the duration stays reasonable and clearly stated [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:02:58]. iTopixs Videos anchors on two years for both the confidentiality tail and the non-circumvention window, notes that some parties push for ten, and warns that once a restriction reaches the five-year range courts may call it an unreasonable business restraint and strike the clause or revise it to a shorter period [l9rVUJ2duT8 @ 00:15:59]. John Ferrell, Esq. comes at it from the signer's side: months, a year, or perhaps three years is reasonable, but long contracts get lost, people change positions, and contractual integrity is hard to maintain, so anything spanning multiple years should be questioned on how it will actually be managed [4L_UjIQJoNA @ 00:08:02]. The working read for an introducer: two years is the only number that sits inside every one of these ranges, while anything past three draws resistance from Cangem and Ferrell and a judicial haircut risk from iTopixs.

Whether money belongs inside the NCNDA splits the room. IXH TRADING Academy builds fees into the core: the non-circumvention clause bars closing with introduced parties without written consent and fee sharing, and commission is secured as its own building block, often backed by a separate fee protection agreement [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:02:39]. Trade Base Hub draws a hard line the other way: NCNDAs are protective instruments, not commercial agreements, they do not define payment, commissions, or services, and using one as a substitute is a common and costly mistake [yrJW_zsMUXM @ 00:01:36]. Cangem Global Corporation adds a structural rule from the ICC 769E special conditions: one intermediary, one counterpart, and commission from either the seller or the buyer, never both, because taking it from both sides gets complicated and can fall into conflict of interest [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:07:09]. On the buy side, David C Barnett Small Business and Deal Making SMB flips the question entirely, recounting a California Association of Business Brokers standard NDA that made a breaching buyer liable for the broker's commission and telling buyers to cross that language out, because the commission sits strictly between seller and broker [wHxYmcrcq4M @ 00:07:16]. Barnett's posture rests on leverage: a buyer's only leverage is willingness to walk, and a broker who refuses to strike the clause is likely sitting on a listing too crowded to produce a good deal anyway [wHxYmcrcq4M @ 00:08:27].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure the speakers describe is an introduction with nothing on paper. IXH TRADING Academy walks through a broker in Alexandria who introduces a grain supplier to a West African importer with nothing signed; the two trade directly and the broker hears about it later with no proof of the role, the fee, or the confidentiality of the contacts [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:07:09]. Attorney Nancy Greene exposes the second trap: a plain NDA has a huge loophole, because a counterpart who tells no one and simply uses your opportunity has probably not breached it; in Greene's example a mover takes a relocation package to market without the originator, which only a good non-circumvention provision would catch [FOUV15TeRLY @ 00:00:42]. The Altierior Motive with Dr. Chad Michael Altieri warns that even a signed clause fails when it matters most if it is vague, too narrow, too broad, or missing key parties, then lists the audit questions, including whether it covers affiliates and related parties, how long protection survives, and what remedy applies [sm21N5L0nl8 @ 00:01:00]. Trade Base Hub closes the timing gap: the NCNDA must be executed by authorized parties before any protected information is shared, stored with proper records, and poor execution undermines enforceability [yrJW_zsMUXM @ 00:01:53].

The second trap is counterfeit paper. Cangem Global Corporation shows an elaborate NCNDA and IMFPA carrying an ICC logo and references to ICC 400, 500, and 600, says no such regulations exist, and tells operators that if this document ever lands in front of them they should not sign it and should throw it in the garbage bin [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:01:10]. Brufokes African Foods Export Ltd traced the same problem through an article linking to the ICC's own warning, states that any NCNDA quoting regulations 400, 500, 600 is fake and will not be enforced by the ICC, then recorded the purchase of the genuine publication 769 from the ICC business bookstore [suL1t0XynNw @ 00:00:42]. Going genuine carries its own constraint: Brufokes cannot share the purchased document except with trade partners for the intention of trade [suL1t0XynNw @ 00:01:23], and Cangem reads the ICC copyright as unlimited use by the purchaser across any deals but no redistribution or resale [HldBP9R47Bg @ 00:04:58]. IXH TRADING Academy ties fake paper to fraud, flagging requests for advance fees to issue guarantees or documents as a pattern the ICC's commercial crime services have warned about for years, and telling operators to verify company registration, bank references, and compliance checks before trusting anyone [5VMFuBur4kI @ 00:08:10].

Signers get burned too. Rafael Ludwig received NDAs with non-compete language buried inside, sent at least a couple back because one barred them from working for two years on anything similar, as generic as video production, and signed others that warned of social-media monitoring and heavy, heavy penalties [WjchmVIKv5M @ 00:01:54]. The deals Ludwig did sign left a three-and-a-half-year hole in the reel, forcing a rebuild of client relationships and personal projects just to prove capability [WjchmVIKv5M @ 00:03:30]. David C Barnett Small Business and Deal Making SMB flags the reverse leak: broker NDAs protect the seller's information, not the buyer's, so a buyer holding $500,000 who needs a $100,000 down payment should move $100,000 into a separate account, show only that statement, write $100,000 plus on the net worth form [wHxYmcrcq4M @ 00:04:06], and ask the broker to sign the buyer's own NDA [wHxYmcrcq4M @ 00:05:43]. John Ferrell, Esq. adds that overly broad NDAs discourage collaboration, create confusion, and breed disputes over what was agreed [4L_UjIQJoNA @ 00:06:31], and that insisting on NDAs for readily available public information reduces the credibility of the insisting party [4L_UjIQJoNA @ 00:07:08]. Adventures in CRE confirms that the public-information exclusion is the most common defense a recipient raises [EL2eIKZ_OV8 @ 00:02:16].
