## 1. Assign the 21-play workload to four accountable seats  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with responsibility boundaries, not a collection of interchangeable dealmakers. Candidate #26 supports one full-time principal and three contractor seats in both Conservative and Base cases. The principal owns mandate acceptance, licensed negotiations, representations, pricing, and closing authorization. Contractor one handles origination research and permissioned buyer matching; contractor two coordinates diligence, document checklists, and milestone evidence; contractor three maintains commission records, vendor administration, and monthly reconciliation. Contractor titles do not authorize regulated activity. Before assigning outreach or negotiations, determine the asset, jurisdiction, activity, and applicable license or exemption. For securities transactions, evaluate Exchange Act Section 15(a) and the conditional M&A broker exemption in Section 15(b)(13); an exemption analysis must address the actual transaction, not merely its M&A label. Use the seven three-play bundles below as this chapter's implementation register for the 21 mapped plays, rather than claiming these local labels are an official taxonomy. Every bundle receives one accountable owner, one evidence location, and one escalation path. Outside counsel and escrow providers remain transaction vendors, not invisible recurring operating seats; their charges still belong in the cost ledger. Keep their engagement boundaries explicit when reporting team efficiency.

Buy contractor outputs through scoped statements of work with acceptance criteria, revision rules, confidentiality duties, and signed rights transfers. A research package might require verified ownership, asset category, contact permission provenance, and a documented investment thesis; a diligence package requires an indexed room, unresolved exceptions, and dated source references. For planning, an accepted research packet could carry a $150 fixed fee, an accepted diligence refresh $300, and a controller service a $750 monthly retainer. These are illustrative purchasing units, not additions to canonical scenario costs. Ten packets and four refreshes plus that retainer equal $3,450 monthly, or $41,400 annually, before other expenses. Purchase only the volume the case can fund. Avoid automatic success-fee splits for unlicensed assistants; fixed fees also do not cure prohibited activity. Specify one-business-day exception acknowledgment and two-business-day correction of rejected deliverables, with acceptance contingent on evidence rather than hours online. Apply contractor classification tests to actual working conditions. IRS common-law factors and California Labor Code Section 2775 can produce different analyses; a contractor agreement alone does not resolve status. Where the operating design requires employee-like control, revise the engagement model and budget before representing the four-person configuration as sustainable.

Expand to two full-time staff and four contractors only after proving that execution congestion, rather than weak origination, limits retained commissions. Promote execution ownership into the second full-time seat and retain the diligence contractor for document production and exception tracking. Add a fourth contractor for sector-specific valuation research, separating evidence assembly from the principal's advice and representations. The scaled pod therefore contains six people, not six people plus an undisclosed operating bench. Require eight consecutive weekly reviews showing stable accepted deliverables, bounded rework, and enough available cash to fund the proposed commitments under a delayed-closing stress case. Use signed mandates and verified milestones to explain demand, but do not spend probability-weighted pipeline as though it were cash. Cross-train the execution lead on every live mandate through introductions, meeting notes, and delegated authority that counterparties actually recognize. Preserve the principal's restricted decisions in a written authority matrix. A buyer needs proof that execution continues during the founder's absence, not proof that contractors can forward messages. Run a five-business-day absence drill each quarter and record stalled decisions, unauthorized commitments, missing access, and client complaints as defects requiring closure before further expansion.

## 2. Enforce weekly evidence scorecards without rewarding fragile volume  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a forty-five-minute weekly operating review from a frozen CRM snapshot and a linked exception register. HubSpot or Pipedrive can hold mandates and buyer records; a permissioned Google Workspace or SharePoint repository can hold signed documents and source evidence. Separate stages for accepted mandate, qualified buyer introduction, signed indication, diligence, definitive agreement, and settled commission. Advance a record only when its defined evidence exists. Report opening inventory, additions, reversals, completions, and ending inventory so a contractor cannot manufacture progress by repeatedly moving the same opportunity. The principal reviews exceptions and capital allocation; contractors explain rejected outputs and overdue dependencies. Track acceptance rate as accepted deliverables divided by submitted deliverables, including resubmissions in a separate rework measure. Track milestone timeliness against the latest jointly documented due date while retaining the original date to expose chronic rescheduling. Sample all high-risk assertions and at least five routine deliverables weekly, or every deliverable when volume is lower. Reward verified accuracy and actionable handoffs, not emails sent, room uploads, or optimistic probability changes. Finish with a named owner, next evidence requirement, and dated resolution for each red item. Preserve each weekly snapshot for later buyer diligence.

Apply a maximum 25% concentration limit separately to retained commission revenue by client group and by ultimate counterparty group, using trailing twenty-four-month results and a trailing-twelve-month warning view. Aggregate affiliates under common control; four acquisition vehicles backed by one sponsor are not four independent buyers. Set a 20% internal target to leave room for normal commission variability. Measure signed-mandate fee exposure separately on the same group basis, distinguishing committed contract economics from probability-weighted forecasts. For cash protection, also monitor receivables and unrecovered deal spend by group. A young brokerage with one closing will exceed the revenue cap; show the breach plainly rather than changing the denominator or excluding the largest deal. Pause incremental discretionary spend on that group, document the diversification plan, and avoid claiming a diversified book in a sale process until the evidence supports it. Four equal independent commission sources merely meet 25%; five equal sources reach the 20% target. Analyze shared sectors and capital providers as correlated exposures even when the legal entities differ. Exclusivity and NCNDA provisions protect defined relationships and introductions, but neither creates demand nor eliminates counterparty dependence. Review contractual scope and enforceability before treating either as durable revenue protection.

Use compensation quality controls that distinguish errors from legitimate deal failure. A buyer withdrawing after accurate diligence is not equivalent to a contractor misrepresenting verified funds or hiding an adverse report. Pay accepted fixed-scope outputs on the contracted schedule; do not make ordinary payment depend on a closing unless the arrangement is permitted and expressly structured that way. Require correction of inaccurate work under the agreed revision terms, and document fee disputes against specific acceptance criteria. Any transaction-linked compensation needs activity-specific licensing analysis, contractual allocation, and correct accounting treatment. Set weekly operating targets such as at least 95% accepted-first-pass deliverables, no unexplained milestone more than two business days overdue, and zero unsupported claims transmitted externally. These are managerial targets, not observed performance claims. Evaluate close rate by mandate cohort and maturity, not by dividing this month's closings by this month's new leads. Attribute losses to price mismatch, unavailable capital, seller withdrawal, diligence defects, or execution failure using documented reasons. Review attribution jointly to prevent blame shifting. The principal should see which of the 21 play workloads creates rework and which creates accepted progression, then rebalance purchases inside the same four-person pod before increasing headcount or promising faster closings.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make the evidence survive a disputed commission and a buyer review**
> 
> Experienced brokerage operators build records for the moment incentives diverge: a seller disputes an introduction, a buyer delays, or an acquirer challenges earnings.
> - Preserve the introduced-party schedule, dated acknowledgment, and exact NCNDA scope together; an unsigned contact export is weak introduction evidence.
> - Record shared sponsors behind acquisition vehicles before reporting counterparty diversity; entity counts can conceal a single capital source.
> - Keep original milestone dates beside revised dates so contractors cannot improve timeliness by quietly moving deadlines.
> - Reconcile escrow closing statements to the contractual commission waterfall before celebrating a headline fee or paying permitted splits.

## 3. Reconcile accrual cohorts to cash and the canonical cost envelopes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the ledger around retained transaction commissions, never underlying asset prices, enterprise values, or escrow principal. A $10 million asset sale with a $200,000 contractual fee and a $50,000 outside brokerage allocation produces a $150,000 retained-fee management measure when that allocation belongs to the other broker. Determine separately whether formal reporting requires gross presentation with an expense or net presentation under the applicable principal-agent analysis; reconcile either presentation to the scorecard's retained basis. Do not deduct the same allocation again when calculating owner profit. Use QuickBooks Online or Xero with a deal identifier on every receivable, expense, and receipt, supported by an independent retained-fee bridge. Under an accrual policy aligned with applicable reporting standards, recognize commissions when the performance obligation is satisfied and entitlement is established, considering contingent fees and reversal risk. A signed mandate, attractive pipeline probability, or unsigned closing statement does not independently establish earned revenue. Record advance amounts according to their actual terms and performance obligations. Maintain a separate cash forecast because earned receivables can remain uncollected. Client funds held by an escrow provider remain outside operating cash and cannot fund payroll, contractor retainers, or distributions to the owner.

Reconcile every planning model to the three canonical cases before adding seat-level detail. Conservative retained commissions of $100,000 less $75,000 total costs produce $25,000 owner net profit and a 25% margin. Base retained commissions of $550,000 less $343,750 costs produce $206,250 and 37.5%. Scaled retained commissions of $1,000,000 less $500,000 costs produce $500,000 and 50%. Conservative and Base each use one full-time principal plus three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time staff plus four contractors. These totals constrain allocation, but they do not reveal the original treatment of owner wages, taxes, or every expense category. Preserve those definitions rather than silently relabeling profit as EBITDA. The stated $3,000–$18,000 monthly operating range does not by itself reconcile to every annual all-in cost envelope: distinguish recurring overhead from variable delivery expense and other included costs, then investigate the remaining bridge. Do not invent a historical allocation. With illustrative retained commissions averaging $50,000 per closing, the cases require two, eleven, and twenty closings respectively. That arithmetic explains throughput, not diversified client coverage; the two-closing Conservative example necessarily breaches a 25% client cap if each closing comes from a different client and no other commissions exist.

Close each month with four linked cohorts: mandate acceptance month, closing month, revenue recognition month, and cash receipt month. Preserve all four dates because a March mandate can close in September and pay in October. Track direct contractor purchases, external specialist charges, permitted broker allocations, write-offs, refunds, and unresolved disputes by deal. Allocate shared overhead consistently for cohort contribution analysis while retaining an unallocated company view for reconciliation. A failed mandate keeps its costs; moving them into a successful mandate disguises acquisition efficiency. Run receivable aging from contractual due dates and retain evidence supporting collection assumptions. Review subsequent receipts and post-close claims before finalizing buyer-facing earnings schedules. Stress the next thirteen weeks by delaying the largest expected commission ninety days and removing the second-largest unsigned opportunity. Calculate cash needs from dated obligations, not from the annual margin percentage. The $15,000–$100,000 startup range and three-to-twelve-month first-deal window make commitment timing material, especially when contractors require retainers. Release owner distributions only after preserving the chosen reserve against that stress case and segregating obligations already due. A durable cash cow is a reconciled collection system with controlled commitments, not a profitable annual spreadsheet hiding a midyear cash shortfall or borrowing from client escrow.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze deal evidence
   - Export the month-end CRM snapshot and lock the commission support package.
   - Capture contract terms, milestone evidence, settlement status, fee allocations, disputed amounts, and all four cohort dates.
2. Reconcile entitlement and cash
   - Match earned commissions to invoices, settlement statements, and subsequent bank receipts.
   - Document recognition judgments and gross-to-retained presentation differences; keep escrow principal outside operating balances.
3. Close costs and concentration
   - Post accepted contractor obligations and recompute client-group and counterparty-group exposure.
   - Preserve failed-deal costs, investigate unreconciled items, and show every concentration breach without altering denominators.
4. Release the operating packet
   - Publish reconciled cohort results, cash stress tests, and signed corrective actions.
   - The principal approves distributions and new commitments only after reviewing dated obligations and the reserve calculation.

## 4. Build transferable earnings before negotiating an exit multiple  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Transferability starts with ownership and permission, not a broker's promise that the buyer list is valuable. Obtain signed written assignments for contractor-created playbooks, original research, automation code, matching logic documentation, and other copyrightable deliverables under 17 U.S.C. Section 204(a). Do not assume the work-made-for-hire label automatically covers independent contractor output; statutory conditions are narrower. List preexisting contractor materials separately and secure the required license rather than purporting to own everything. Copyright assignment does not transfer client confidential information, third-party databases, or rights that the contractor never held. Maintain a rights register linking each asset to its creator, agreement, permitted uses, and transfer restrictions. Review exclusivity terms, tail periods, introduced-party schedules, NCNDA beneficiaries, assignment clauses, change-of-control terms, and client termination rights before describing mandates as transferable. Identify which licenses and exemptions depend on specific people or entities; a business sale does not automatically transfer regulatory authority. Store company systems under company-controlled accounts with role-based access, recovery procedures, and dated offboarding records. Demonstrate a lawful, permissioned path for buyer diligence using redacted records and controlled access. The saleable asset is an authorized operating system with retained relationships, not an unrestricted export of sensitive counterparties and deal documents.

Treat multiples as negotiating sensitivities, not observed market quotations. For a small owner-operated brokerage, test a two-to-three-times seller's discretionary earnings range only after defining SDE and validating add-backs. For a genuinely manager-run operation, test a three-to-five-times normalized EBITDA range, recognizing that concentrated, episodic commissions may attract lower bids or no acceptable bid. Do not apply either range directly to canonical owner net profit without a bridge. As an explicit scaled illustration, assume the $500,000 owner profit is before tax and debt costs, excludes owner compensation, contains no depreciation or amortization differences, and requires $150,000 annual market replacement labor inclusive of employment costs. Also assume no other normalization adjustments. Resulting normalized EBITDA is $350,000; three-to-five times produces $1,050,000–$1,750,000 indicative enterprise value. Each assumption must be tested against the ledger and job scope. If replacement labor is already expensed, subtracting it again understates earnings. Under the same incremental replacement-cost assumption, Base leaves only $56,250 and Conservative becomes negative $125,000 before other adjustments. Those sensitivities explain why a profitable owner job can lack a manager-run valuation. Separate enterprise value from equity proceeds by reconciling debt, agreed cash treatment, working-capital adjustments, transaction costs, and contingent consideration.

Earn a stronger bid through evidence that survives transition. Present twenty-four months of cohort results, bank-supported collections, concentration schedules, contractor acceptance records, rights assignments, and founder-absence results. Distinguish repeat mandates from contractually recurring revenue: a client who returned twice has demonstrated behavior, not a binding future purchase. Give buyers a clean bridge from the 21-play implementation register to accountable seats and measurable outputs, including which restricted activities require replacement credentials. A premium should follow lower dependence on the founder, cleaner earnings, and defensible relationship continuity, rather than the mere presence of automation. Model offer structure as carefully as the headline multiple. For an earnout tied to collected retained fees, define eligible legacy mandates, permitted allocations, refund treatment, collection efforts, reporting access, dispute procedures, and treatment of diverted opportunities. Discount deferred payments for timing and collection risk when comparing offers. If offers underpay the verified cash stream, maintain the business as a controlled cash cow with documented succession rather than chasing a forced sale. Continue quarterly absence drills, annual rights reviews, and concentration repair. Whether selling or holding, the same operating objective applies: commissions must arise from a repeatable, authorized process that another qualified operator can run without reconstructing the founder's memory, personal inbox, or undocumented promises.
