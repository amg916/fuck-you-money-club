# Chapter 01: High-Ticket Sales: From First Offer to Repeatable Closings

*Remote closing roles, coaching-sales frameworks, offer outreach, low-ticket buyer follow-up, business-broker success steps, and real estate sales-production case studies*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

High-ticket sales means closing $3K-$25K coaching, course, and service offers over Zoom for commission, with no base pay. Greyson's Daily, Asher Shaw, and Hudson Cosper converge on the split: setters at roughly 5-7% of cash collected, closers at 10-15%, self-setters at 15%, and affiliates at 30-45%. The street math is consistent: 5-10 calls a day, 30-45 minute diagnostic calls, and a 30-40% close rate as the bar. Daniel Botero and Daniel Coutts teach pain-first discovery before any pitch. Landing a seat is itself a sales process, run on 100 DMs a day and 60-second Looms. The fault lines: Paul Vu says skill beats the offer, while Frankie Isaac and Colby Lyman say to vet the offer hard. Lyman caps realistic income near $30-40K a month and calls closing a glorified job, which Cosper dismisses as cope.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every practitioner describes the same base model. A founder sells a $3K-$25K transformation, and because buyers, as Greyson's Daily puts it, 'usually need to talk to someone first,' the company contracts out setters and closers instead of selling through a checkout page. Greyson's Daily traces the setter/closer split to Aaron Ross at Salesforce and Daniel Locke's 2016 'high ticket closer' rebrand. They then lay out the pay: setters run a 5-10 minute call qualifying fit and finances for about 5% of cash collected, and closers run a roughly hour-long call on pains and goals for about 10% [t49Ab42PNxE @ 00:01:44]. Asher Shaw widens the ranges: setters get 5-7% plus bonuses for booked calls and revenue, and closers get 10-15%. At 15%, a $20K real-estate coaching close pays $3,000, and Shaw's closers take 4-6 calls a day [aFs-x6HJabU @ 00:04:00]. Hudson Cosper standardizes the split at 10% for closers, 5% for setters, and 15% for self-setters, with refunds triggering clawbacks. Cosper adds that neither seat needs a license or an LLC [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:07:25]. Andy Elliott pushes the top end to 10%-30% commissions, which turns a $20K deal into $6K. At the low end, 10% of a $25K ticket is $2,500 per call [AYjsFZOlEK8 @ 00:08:00]. The definition holds across all four: a human conversation is the product, and the commission is the paycheck.

Practitioners agree that the seat matters before the script. Hudson Cosper's minimum is a $3K ticket, ideally $5-10K, sold only to warm inbound leads and never outbound. Cosper also wants testimonials and a manager who actually reviews calls. The red flags are pay-to-apply, murky commission, tickets under $2K, and '10K guaranteed' promises [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:08:04]. Frankie Isaac runs the same filter from the hiring side. Tickets of $5-20K at 10% pay $500-$2.5K per close, and reps should demand proof of the numbers. Fewer than 20 calls a week is a flag. Financing through Affirm, Klarna, or Splitit is 'essential.' Evasive answers, unrealistic ramp-ups, and high closer turnover are disqualifiers [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:08:14]. Isaac's warning case is the '10K a month OTE' seat that closed a single $3.5K deal in three months [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:06:28]. Colby Lyman tells reps to interview the owner back: ask how many reps they run, what those reps make, and how long the program has existed, and ask for case studies and testimonials. Lyman's reasoning is that when ads or YouTube posting stall, the leads stop and the rep's income goes with them. Influencers who skip fulfillment also make the rep look like the scammer [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:03:13]. All three end at the same rule: research the offer before taking a single call.

Nobody credible sells this as passive. Greyson's Daily calls it 'the most active income ever': no base pay, no time freedom, and you are not a business owner. A $10K first month happens, but most people make $3-5K, and the average is AUD $2-3K [t49Ab42PNxE @ 00:05:13]. Paul Vu started at 12 calls a day and ran 2-3x the dial volume of peers. Vu argues that 'anything that you're missing in terms of skill set needs to be made up for with work ethic and volume' [LeKXSFb84sA @ 00:01:22]. Hudson Cosper's guest Alazar shows the upside of the same math: 10-12 calls booked and 6-8 taken in about four hours. On a $5K program at 10%, three closes is $1,500 a day. Even so, Cosper prescribes 8-10 hour days for beginners and advises working a job and saving money first [Wb6IlK5noKw @ 00:05:40]. Colby Lyman confirms the grind at the top: 8-10 hours a day while making $20-30K a month, with no PTO and no guarantees. In Lyman's words, 'You eat what you kill' [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:04:23]. Cosper's own ramp timeline is month one for learning, months two and three for first money, and months four through six at $10K. Cosper also reports that 60-80% of new reps quit somewhere in weeks four through eight.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

On the call itself, the shared rule is to diagnose before you pitch. Hudson Cosper's 'doctor method' spends 25-30 of 45 minutes on diagnosis, and Cosper bans fake deadlines, robotic reading, and over-explaining [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:21:53]. Daniel Botero uses six stages: rapport, agenda, discovery, recap, pitch, and objections. Botero does 10-15 minutes of LinkedIn research before dialing and spends 20-30 minutes of a one-hour call on discovery, with the prospect talking about 75% of the time. Botero's warning is never to skip the pain: 'stab the knife and twist it' [r2vbi9gCm-o @ 00:08:04]. Daniel Coutts runs a longer chain. Coutts frames the call, extracts a 'tangible outcome' goal, and establishes the time gap. Then Coutts prehandles limiting beliefs (time, money, having been scammed, the wife, the parent), treats every objection as a smokescreen, and probes with what, why, and how while acting 'really dumb' [RHobLxb_gLQ @ 00:15:24]. Both state the price flat. Coutts says 'investment is 6k' and closes for paid in full, weighing it against one to three years of staying the same. Botero says 'It's $5,000' confidently, then backs it with opportunity cost: a $100K salary is $8,300 a month, so landing a job two months faster is worth $16,000 against the $5,000 fee [r2vbi9gCm-o @ 00:12:32].

Landing the seat runs on the same mechanics. Frankie Isaac's reframe is that you are the product and the offer owner is the prospect. That means skipping free Facebook groups and job boards, finding owners on YouTube and Instagram by spotting CTAs, VSLs, and testimonials, and never DMing 'are you hiring?' [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:04:06]. Isaac's Loom runs 60 seconds and covers hook, track record, character, and CTA. The interview gets treated as a sales call and closes on 'anything...make you hesitant?' Hudson Cosper's version is a three-line DM plus a 60-second Loom, followed by follow-up. Cosper's student Zach sent about 100 messages, got 4 offers, and made $8K in 30 days [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:08:57]. The Closer ID pushes raw volume. They sent 480 messages in their first week and say 20 messages take about 10 minutes, so 100 a day is achievable for anyone. They block social apps with Opal and generate 100-1,000 Instagram keywords once, using ChatGPT with Whisper Flow dictation [sh92NTG_jgs @ 00:03:10]. The opener is deliberately unpersonalized: 'Yo, boss. I think it's so sick what you're doing. Do you have your own coaching or course?' Only after the coach replies does the closer reference one of their reels and ask whether they need a high-ticket closer [sh92NTG_jgs @ 00:07:34].

Upstream, Scale Like ZK works the weekly math backwards. One buyer a week at about a one-in-three close rate needs 3-4 calls. That takes 5 bookings and 8-9 applications, which comes to roughly 170 page visitors at their 20-25% page-to-application rate, against 5% generally [uBQvqV_-nz0 @ 00:01:09]. ZK's application states a 'multi four-figure' investment level and asks about cash on hand. A $500 answer means cancelling the call or offering a low-ticket downsell. Speed to lead is measured in minutes. KPIs get checked weekly, because a bad show rate alone can turn $50K into $5K [uBQvqV_-nz0 @ 00:13:44]. Hernan Vazquez benchmarks low-ticket ascension at about 10% of buyers reaching a call, or 7-10 calls per 100 buyers. From there Vazquez runs a 2-3 email consumption sequence, then one to two weeks of testimonial and case-study emails. Daisy-chained webinar and VSL campaigns follow, and finally a daily broadcast with a book-a-call link. Vazquez reports leads converting after 2-3 years on the list [PeOr6rOEN2Q @ 00:05:35]. Brandon Mulrenin, working real-estate listings, reports that only 10-15% of first calls set an appointment, while 85-90% of deals come from follow-up. That is why Mulrenin has the prospect author both the next time to talk and the reason for it [phMoT_2rYN4 @ 00:13:51].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Vet the seat
   - Screen each offer on ticket size, call volume, financing, and owner track record before accepting it.
   - Target $5-20K tickets at 10%, flag any offer with under 20 calls a week, and treat Affirm, Klarna, or Splitit financing as essential [Frankie Isaac — _ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:08:14]. Walk from pay-to-apply, murky commission, or '10K guaranteed' seats [Hudson Cosper — 4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:08:04].
2. Run outreach at volume
   - Send 100 unpersonalized openers a day to coaches found by keyword, then follow up with a DM plus Loom.
   - Use 'Do you have your own coaching or course?' as the opener and mine the following lists of coaches you find [The Closer ID — sh92NTG_jgs @ 00:07:34]. Follow with a three-line DM and a 60-second Loom; Zach turned about 100 messages into 4 offers [Hudson Cosper — 4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:08:57].
3. Diagnose before pitching
   - Spend the first 25-30 minutes on pain, desired outcome, and the time gap before presenting anything.
   - Research the prospect for 10-15 minutes beforehand, let them talk 75% of the time, and never skip the pain [Daniel Botero — r2vbi9gCm-o @ 00:08:04]. Diagnosis should take 25-30 of the call's 45 minutes [Hudson Cosper — 4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:21:53].
4. State price, close, and lock follow-up
   - Say the price flat, close for paid in full, and have the prospect set the next contact if they stall.
   - Say 'investment is 6k,' close for PIF, and weigh the fee against 1-3 years of staying the same [Daniel Coutts — RHobLxb_gLQ @ 00:21:04]. When a prospect stalls, have them author the next time to talk and the reason, since 85-90% of deals come from follow-up [Brandon Mulrenin — phMoT_2rYN4 @ 00:22:09].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split is over how many hours this pays for. Hudson Cosper's guest Alazar describes waking at 11:20, closing on a 30 to 40 minute call, and moving the remaining three or four calls to times they prefer. The host claims a six-month stretch of three to four hours a day at roughly 150k a month [Wb6IlK5noKw @ 00:02:25] [Wb6IlK5noKw @ 00:06:20]. Paul Vu attacks exactly that pitch. They reject gurus who sell 'two hours a day' for '$20,000 a month' and report starting at 12 calls a day, running two to three times the dial volume of peers [LeKXSFb84sA @ 00:00:51]. Colby Lyman puts the real load at 8 to 10 hours a day while making 20 to 30k a month [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:08:02]. Greyson's Daily calls it 'the most active income ever,' with no base pay and no time freedom because the closer is not the business owner [t49Ab42PNxE @ 00:05:13]. Even inside the Cosper camp, beginners are told to put in 8 to 10 hours a day, so the short day comes late, not at entry. Vu's resolution is that the seat delivers financial and location freedom early, with closers in Asia or South America getting 5 to 10x buying power [LeKXSFb84sA @ 00:02:26].

The second fault line is whether the offer or the operator drives income. Paul Vu says gurus have the order backwards: skill comes first, then the offer. Vu reports turning down roles advertising 20 to 50k and even $100k-month OTE [LeKXSFb84sA @ 00:05:28] [LeKXSFb84sA @ 00:06:22]. Hudson Cosper's camp goes further and dismisses another creator's claim that high ticket sales is a 'glorified 9 to 5' dependent on the offer, calling it 'cope' [Wb6IlK5noKw @ 00:00:46]. Colby Lyman lands on the other side of both. To Lyman, being a closer is 'a glorified job,' and income 'all leads back to making sure you're doing research to get on the right offer' [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:10:07] [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:04:11]. Influencers who skip fulfillment, get canceled, or stop running ads take the rep's pipeline with them. Frankie Isaac splits the difference by turning vetting into a checklist. Tickets of 5 to 20k at 10% pay 500 to 2.5k per close, the numbers must be proven, and fewer than 20 calls a week is a flag [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:08:14]. Isaac also takes the sting out of a bad pick: one bad offer doesn't mean sales doesn't work, it just means you need a better seat [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:23:29].

Practitioners split just as sharply on how to land a seat and where leads come from. The Closer ID pushes raw volume: 100 messages a day is achievable for anyone, and 20 messages take about 10 minutes. When a participant defended 30 a day 'for quality,' the host answered 'You're overthinking it' and advised skipping personalization on the first message [sh92NTG_jgs @ 00:04:07] [sh92NTG_jgs @ 00:10:20]. Frankie Isaac argues for a different route. Isaac reports that every 20k-plus-a-month offer came from network because referrals look low risk, and warns that a Loom showing 30-plus views has gone unwatched [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:04:48] [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:13:20]. Hudson Cosper's middle path is a three-line DM, a 60-second Loom, and follow-up. A student named Zach sent about 100 messages, got 4 offers, and made 8K in 30 days [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:08:57]. On the client side, Cosper wants warm inbound leads only and never outbound [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:05:56]. Brandon Mulrenin calls real estate 'a direct outbound sales business.' Mulrenin's team runs a double dial, voicemail, video text, and social follow, all within five minutes [phMoT_2rYN4 @ 00:05:58] [phMoT_2rYN4 @ 00:12:38]. Mulrenin's volume math is equally blunt: if you want to sell 50 houses, you need 50 contacts per day [phMoT_2rYN4 @ 00:19:37].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failure on record is Cole Ferguson's broker launch. Ferguson lost about $80,000 in year one with zero deals before closing 14 in year two. In hindsight, they would have charged down-payment and valuation fees and raised a first-year minimum commission of $10,000 [FpH555COYJI @ 00:00:35] [FpH555COYJI @ 00:06:52]. Their fix is blunt: save about 50 grand and don't start part-time [FpH555COYJI @ 00:01:32]. On the closer side, the trap is the advertised OTE. Frankie Isaac describes '10K a month OTE' offers that closed one 3.5K deal in three months. Isaac lists evasive answers, unrealistic ramp-up, and high closer turnover as red flags [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:06:28] [_ZU2OAxnSMI @ 00:11:58]. Greyson's Daily warns that a 150k OTE listing on Seek depends entirely on the company's marketing. Most people make 3 to 5k in month one, the average sits around AUD 2 to 3k, and placement programs deserve suspicion [t49Ab42PNxE @ 00:04:04] [t49Ab42PNxE @ 00:05:35]. Hudson Cosper adds that refunds lead to clawbacks. Cosper's red flags are pay-to-apply roles, murky commission, tickets under 2K, and any '10K guaranteed' promise [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:07:50] [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:08:04]. Each of these traps shows up only after the operator has already committed weeks to the seat.

The second cluster of failures sits outside the closer's control, right up until it lands on their income. Colby Lyman reports influencers who skip fulfillment, which makes the rep look like the scammer. Lyman also describes owners whose ads or YouTube posting stall so the leads stop, and the plain risk that an influencer gets canceled and the job disappears [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:01:28] [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:03:48]. Scale Like ZK measures the damage in rates. A bad show rate alone can turn $50K into $5K, intent is perishable, and a slow invite becomes one of 40 to 60 notifications [uBQvqV_-nz0 @ 00:13:44] [uBQvqV_-nz0 @ 00:09:18]. ZK also warns against fully automating the process or handing leads to an AI chatbot, because real setters and SDRs are required [uBQvqV_-nz0 @ 00:14:39]. Nick Dadamio's failure mode is the buying committee. Without a champion, a deal decays into 45 days of 'maybe' follow-ups because the buyer cannot sell you internally [zmqAbj7T7F8 @ 00:01:19]. Brandon Mulrenin has coached 6,000-plus agents and names 'happy ears' and lead stuffing as the follow-up killers. Mulrenin insists the prospect author the next time to talk and the reason [phMoT_2rYN4 @ 00:22:09]. With 85 to 90% of deals coming from follow-up, dropping that thread forfeits most of the pipeline [phMoT_2rYN4 @ 00:13:51].

The last set of traps is behavioral. Hudson Cosper puts the dropout window at weeks 4 to 8, with about 60 to 80% quitting. Cosper's ramp is learning in month one, first money in months two and three, and 10K by months four to six [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:18:15] [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:13:57]. Cosper also bans fake deadlines, robotic reading, and over-explaining [4HgyBCSnkLE @ 00:10:54]. That echoes Daniel Coutts, who insists the 16-step call is a framework, not a script read word by word [RHobLxb_gLQ @ 00:00:52]. Daniel Botero's warning is about skipping pain in discovery. Botero cites a Google/Meta recruiter who struggled without rapport [r2vbi9gCm-o @ 00:02:28]. The prescription is 10 to 15 minutes of LinkedIn research before the call, then 20 to 30 minutes of discovery in which the prospect talks about 75% of the time [r2vbi9gCm-o @ 00:04:30]. Colby Lyman supplies the burnout check. At 8 to 10 hours a day, slipping on follow-ups and objection handling gets you fired, and settling at 8 to 10k a month is its own trap [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:09:19] [IlInXj9aPFg @ 00:10:45]. The common thread is that discipline on the call and in the follow-up decays first, long before the offer or the market gives out.
