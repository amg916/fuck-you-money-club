## 1. Assign Eight Plays to Two Seats Before Adding Headcount  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one full-time owner-operator and one bounded contractor, not eight miniature departments. The chapter's eight-play mapping is P1 brandable acquisition, P2 exact-match acquisition, P3 expired-authority acquisition, P4 drop-catch bidding, P5 trademark and dispute screening, P6 outbound upgrades, P7 inbound conversion, and P8 renewal optimization. These are operating lanes, not eight additional revenue streams. The owner controls capital allocation, final pricing, counterparty acceptance, registrar transfers, and escrow release. The contractor prepares comparable-sale packets, checks historical use, drafts prospect research, routes inquiries, and assembles renewal exceptions. Keep purchasing credentials and transfer authorization outside that contractor seat. Use NameBio for observed sale evidence, Internet Archive for historical use, ICANN Lookup for registration information, and the registrar's own ledger for ownership evidence. A comparable transaction is a pricing input, not proof that today's buyer exists. Every candidate record needs acquisition channel, all-in maximum bid, renewal date, intended buyer category, rights-screening evidence, and a named decision owner. This structure protects the Conservative and Base team's two-person limit while leaving specialist reviews as explicitly budgeted external services rather than invisible permanent seats. Keep the registry's premium renewal classification visible before any bid.

At Scaled, add a second full-time seat responsible for portfolio operations and transaction execution, then split contractor work between research and outreach administration. The resulting pod is exactly two full-time people plus two contractors. The founder retains investment policy and exceptional negotiations; the operations employee owns listing synchronization, renewal queues, escrow evidence, and bookkeeping handoffs. Research produces P1 through P5 packets. Outreach administration handles approved P6 messages and P7 routing, while supporting P8 exception research. Do not assume the $120,000 owner-profit case proves that local wages, benefits, and replacement management already fit. Build those actual costs into the staffing bridge before hiring. Use delegated registrar access where supported, hardware-backed multifactor authentication, a company password manager, and independent verification of changed payment instructions. Where delegation is unavailable, keep transfers with the authorized owner rather than sharing a master password. A backup operator must be able to locate transaction evidence without being able to empty the portfolio. Test coverage during a planned owner absence: respond to inquiries, stop an unauthorized price change, and complete a renewal from a documented instruction. Record missed handoffs as process defects, not personality judgments.

Pay contractors for accepted evidence, not names purchased or emails blasted. An illustrative research agreement pays $30 per accepted screening packet, capped at twenty packets monthly; that is $600 monthly and $7,200 annually before other work. Even this modest commitment consumes a material share of the Conservative case's $17,000 total annual cost envelope. Reduce volume when cash cannot support it. A separate hourly allowance can cover inquiry administration, with logged tasks and a preauthorized monthly ceiling. Define acceptance as complete source links, reproducible comparable filters, historical-use notes, and explicit adverse findings. Require delivery within two business days, correction of material omissions within one business day, and immediate escalation of disputed ownership or suspected impersonation. Review every packet initially, then sample only after sustained accuracy; rights and transfer exceptions always receive full review. Avoid acquisition commissions that reward buying weak inventory. Written agreements should address confidentiality, permitted systems, data return, and assignment of copyright in original research and copy under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a). Domain ownership itself requires separate contractual and registrar transfer mechanics. Worker status follows actual control and applicable law, not the contractor label or payment method.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy Evidence, Preserve Optionality, and Keep Credentials Separate**
> 
> Small domain pods outperform larger loose teams when every research dollar reduces a specific purchasing or transfer error.
> - Require NameBio comparisons to state extension, sale venue, date, and name length; a retail end-user sale should not silently anchor a wholesale auction bid.
> - Check registry-premium renewal pricing inside the actual registrar account; an attractive first-year registration price can conceal expensive annual carrying costs.
> - Remove or update fixed-price marketplace listings immediately after a direct sale, then retain confirmation to prevent a second buyer purchasing unavailable inventory.
> - Keep a funded near-term renewal queue separate from speculative bidding capital; do not depend on an unclosed buyer inquiry to preserve valuable inventory.

## 2. Run a Weekly Scorecard That Cannot Be Improved by Buying Junk  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Freeze the weekly operating snapshot at the same time every Friday. Count domains owned, domains actively marketed, accepted research packets, qualified inquiries, executed sales, completed transfers, and renewal dollars due within ninety days. Distinguish a signed agreement from a funded transaction and a funded transaction from recognized revenue. Define quarterly sell-through as completed domain sales divided by average daily saleable inventory during that quarter; disclose newly acquired inventory and do not annualize a short hot streak without qualification. Track median days from inquiry to first substantive response, median days from contract to transfer, and contribution per completed sale after allocated acquisition cost, marketplace charges, escrow fees, and carrying costs. Contribution is a management metric, not a replacement for the general ledger. For P1 through P4, compare approved bids with realized cohort outcomes. For P5, count unresolved rights exceptions. For P6 and P7, measure qualified conversations and realized sale contribution rather than message volume. For P8, measure renewal exposure and documented retain-or-drop decisions. A beautiful response-time dashboard cannot compensate for a portfolio whose renewal commitments exceed available cash. Show both operating activity and economic results on one page.

Set concentration policy at no more than 25% for any single buyer or commonly controlled buyer group, measured against trailing-twelve-month recognized revenue. Also cap any single broker or marketplace at 25% of that revenue as a channel dependency target. These limits describe distinct risks and must not be merged into one comfortable average. In a young portfolio, one sale can mechanically exceed the threshold; label the breach, calculate the required diversification, and avoid representing the business as already diversified. A $50,000 sale within $160,000 annual proceeds represents 31.25%, not an acceptable 25% exposure. Keeping that buyer at 25% requires at least $200,000 total revenue, assuming no additional sales to that buyer. Do not reject a profitable legitimate sale merely to beautify a ratio; instead stop adding relationship dependence and disclose the exception to potential acquirers. Track open receivables and unsettled escrow balances separately by counterparty because trailing revenue misses settlement risk. Route new listings and relationships toward alternative channels only after comparing actual fees, buyer reach, and transfer controls. Apparent diversification across multiple brand names is meaningless when one underlying buyer controls them all.

Make the weekly review a thirty-minute decision meeting with a fixed exception queue. The contractor submits source-backed counts before the meeting; the owner reconciles closed sales to escrow statements and chooses next week's bid ceiling. At two people, use one shared ledger and one task board rather than maintaining competing CRM reports. At four people, the operations employee signs the reconciliation while the founder samples evidence independently. Define research accuracy as accepted packets without material correction divided by reviewed packets, with sample size visible. A 98% target from two reviewed files proves little. Use a rolling sample of twenty when volume permits, while reviewing all ownership and trademark exceptions. Measure outreach quality through accurate recipient selection, suppression compliance, and substantiated claims. Commercial email requirements under 15 U.S.C. section 7704 include accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, required sender disclosures, and functioning opt-out handling. Stop a defective campaign before paying a performance bonus. Record each corrective action with one responsible seat, one due date, and evidence of closure. If a metric improves while cash conversion deteriorates, investigate definitions before celebrating productivity or increasing contractor hours. Weekly discipline should release capital, not merely produce cleaner charts.

## 3. Build Accrual Books That Survive a Domain-by-Domain Examination  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Create a permanent asset identifier for every domain and use it across the registrar export, purchase invoice, marketplace listing, escrow record, and accounting ledger. Group inventory by acquisition quarter, extension, acquisition channel, and play, keeping P3 expired-authority purchases distinguishable from P1 brandables. For domains held for resale, establish the inventory and revenue-recognition policy with the bookkeeper or accountant before posting transactions. Normally capitalize acquisition cost and directly attributable purchase charges to resale inventory, then release the assigned basis when the domain is sold. Document treatment of renewals, impairment, abandoned names, and multi-domain purchases consistently rather than changing policy to rescue a weak quarter. Allocate bundle cost using a defensible method at acquisition and preserve the original allocation. Record revenue when the applicable recognition requirements are met, supported by the contract and transfer evidence, not simply when escrow receives funds. Keep customer advances separate until earned. A domain registrar's ownership record proves an operational fact; it does not establish the entire accounting treatment. Reconcile cash receipts separately so an unpaid receivable cannot masquerade as spendable renewal capital. Retain invoice-level evidence for each material allocation and subsequent adjustment.

The canonical scorecard uses gross domain sale proceeds as revenue, with acquisition and renewal costs included in margin. Conservative therefore reconciles $20,000 revenue less $17,000 total costs to $3,000 owner net profit, a 15% margin. Base reconciles $160,000 less $116,000 to $44,000, a 27.5% margin. Scaled reconciles $300,000 less $180,000 to $120,000, a 40% margin. These are separate annual operating cases, not consecutive promises of growth. Marketplace remittances must be grossed up where appropriate to show the actual sale price and corresponding selling fee; recording only the payout understates both revenue and expense. Conversely, a brokered sale for someone else's domain cannot automatically become gross principal revenue: assess the actual principal-agent relationship and keep it outside these owned-inventory scenarios unless explicitly reconciled. Split the annual cost envelope into cost of sold inventory, renewals, selling charges, labor, systems, and other overhead without omitting any category. Explain cash absorbed by unsold acquisitions separately from recognized expenses. A profitable income statement can coexist with a cash shortage when auctions consume funds faster than completed sales replenish them. Neither buyer enterprise revenue nor website merchant volume belongs in this operator revenue calculation.

Close monthly with a three-way match among registrar control, domain subledger, and general ledger, then reconcile escrow receivables to subsequent collections. Flag sold names still listed, expired names still carried at cost, duplicate assets, and renewals charged after ownership changed. A cohort report should show original cost, additional carrying spend, realized proceeds, remaining cost basis, write-downs, and remaining inventory count. Do not value unsold names at asking price to create operating earnings. Maintain a separate evidence-based liquidation estimate for acquisition discussions, explicitly distinguishing wholesale bids from optimistic retail expectations. Preserve purchase contracts, dispute correspondence, historical-use captures, and trademark search records alongside transaction documents. ACPA exposure under 15 U.S.C. section 1125(d) concerns bad-faith intent to profit and specified marks; a registration's availability does not establish a right to exploit another party's brand. UDRP is a contractual dispute framework, not a substitute for the statute. For P3, historical links neither transfer copyright in old website content nor establish durable search rankings. Obtain written assignments for commissioned original copy and preserve the chain of title. These records support both dispute response and buyer diligence without pretending that screening eliminates every contested claim.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the monthly inventory snapshot
   - Export registrar holdings, marketplace listings, escrow transactions, and the domain subledger.
   - Timestamp each export and reconcile asset identifiers before adjusting accounting balances.
2. Match each completed transaction
   - Connect the sale agreement, transfer evidence, gross sale price, fees, and assigned acquisition basis.
   - Investigate unmatched records individually; do not force the reconciliation through a miscellaneous adjustment.
3. Close cohorts and obligations
   - Update renewal commitments, abandoned inventory, impairment decisions, receivables, and contractor accruals.
   - Preserve the documented accounting policy and show how unsold acquisitions affected cash rather than recognized profit.
4. Publish the evidence-backed management pack
   - Issue the profit bridge, cash forecast, concentration report, and unresolved exception register.
   - Have the owner review corrections and retain a dated close package that a successor can reproduce.

## 4. Price Transferable Earnings, Then Decide Whether to Sell  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Value the operating machine and the domain inventory explicitly rather than multiplying the largest sale by an attractive number. A small buyer-operated business may be discussed using seller's discretionary earnings, while an absentee buyer needs earnings after replacement labor. Neither measure automatically equals the scorecard's owner net profit. Reconcile reported profit to the selected measure, including documented owner compensation, genuine one-time items, recurring carrying costs, and the cost of replacing the seller's actual duties. Do not add back necessary renewals, routine research, or recurring trademark screening. As an illustrative negotiation sensitivity, test 1.5 to 3.0 times normalized annual owner-operated earnings; this is a modeling range, not a verified domain-business transaction benchmark. If normalized earnings happened to equal Base's $44,000, the resulting range would be $66,000 to $132,000. If they equaled Scaled's $120,000, it would be $180,000 to $360,000. The Conservative $3,000 case produces only $4,500 to $9,000 under that assumption, making a separately supported asset transaction potentially more relevant. Actual bids can fall outside these ranges, including a preference for selected domains rather than any going-concern premium. State the valuation perimeter before sharing a headline price.

Separate the price components in the letter of intent: operating goodwill, included saleable inventory, cash, debt, receivables, and any renewal funding adjustment. An earnings multiple that assumes delivery of a normal working portfolio cannot also justify adding the same portfolio at full retail asking value. Negotiate a specific inventory schedule and explain whether excess or nonoperating names receive separate consideration. Support any additional amount with executable wholesale interest, comparable evidence, and realistic selling costs. A seller-dependent Base operation illustrates the labor problem: if replacing the owner's work requires $50,000 annually and no corresponding expense is already included, $44,000 of owner profit becomes a $6,000 operating deficit for that buyer. This adjustment changes valuation, not the canonical Base scorecard. Buyers paying more need evidence of repeatable sourcing, diversified demand, controlled renewal exposure, and clean rights records. Maintain monthly results over multiple acquisition cohorts so a single exceptional sale cannot impersonate recurring earnings. Show concentration breaches plainly, including related buyers and marketplace dependence. An earnout based on cash actually collected from identified assets can bridge disagreement, but its calculation, reporting access, permitted expenses, and dispute process must be explicit.

Build the transfer pack while operating, not after a buyer arrives. Include the asset schedule, acquisition evidence, registrar transfer constraints, marketplace obligations, renewal calendar, contracts, copyright assignments, dispute register, monthly close packs, and written seat procedures. Identify which agreements require counterparty permission before assignment; do not promise that a marketplace account or its reputation moves with the domain portfolio. Confirm current registrar and registry rules for transfer timing, locks, authorization codes, and account changes before setting the closing timetable. Share sensitive records through access-controlled diligence rooms, with redaction and staged disclosure rather than mass exports of prospect data. Rehearse a ten-business-day owner absence using the same SOPs offered to the buyer. If the team cannot price routine inquiries or identify upcoming renewals, fix that dependency before negotiating a premium. The alternative is a durable cash cow: retain profitable cohorts, stop uneconomic bidding, distribute only cash remaining after obligations, and preserve the option to sell individual names. Compare after-cost sale proceeds with conservative retained cash generation and the owner's required labor. A clean, transferable operation improves either choice without requiring an exit on an arbitrary calendar.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify the Asset, the Earnings, the Rights, and the Handoff**
> 
> Complete these four evidence checks before circulating a sale memorandum or committing to a portfolio transfer timetable.
> - Match every offered domain to current registrar control, purchase evidence, renewal obligations, and a documented transfer path; resolve restrictions before quoting a closing date.
> - Reconcile the selected annual case to gross proceeds and total costs, then produce a separate normalization bridge for owner labor and documented transaction adjustments.
> - Resolve missing copyright assignment signatures, disclose pending domain disputes, and identify contractual permissions needed to transfer customer relationships or vendor agreements.
> - Calculate buyer-group and channel concentration against the 25% caps, disclose breaches, and demonstrate that the pod can operate for ten business days without the founder.
