## 1. Define the inventory business before buying the first registration  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Domain investing means purchasing or registering transferable domain registration rights and reselling those rights at a price that exceeds acquisition, carrying, selling, and operating costs. The operator controls a renewable contractual position through a registrar, not a perpetual deed to an address. The registry maintains the extension, the registrar manages the customer relationship, and marketplaces introduce buyers or facilitate distribution. A website, trademark, email account, customer database, and hosting contract are separate assets unless the sale agreement expressly includes them. That distinction matters when a buyer expects a functioning business but the seller owns only the name. In the core model, the operator is the principal: they choose inventory, bear expiration risk, set pricing, and deliver the registration rights. Recognized operator revenue is the gross sale consideration when the applicable recognition conditions are satisfied, not the marketplace valuation, asking price, or aggregate value of unsold holdings. A signed offer, funded escrow, completed transfer, and released cash are different events. Track them separately so pipeline enthusiasm cannot become reported revenue. Each inventory record should identify the registrar account, acquisition evidence, expiration date, renewal price, intended buyer class, rights screening, and the exact assets promised at closing.

Five operator archetypes organize the market more usefully than broad claims about digital real estate. Brandable specialists curate memorable names for emerging companies; exact-match specialists sell descriptive upgrades to established operators; expired-authority specialists reconstruct useful topical properties; auction traders exploit sourcing and pricing differences; and buyer representatives procure names for clients. The last archetype earns a service fee rather than claiming the client's purchase principal as its own turnover. Expired-authority work also creates a boundary: affiliate commissions and accepted-lead fees are website operating revenue, not domain resale proceeds. Maintain separate ledgers if both models coexist. The eight chapter-mapped plays sit within these five archetypes because one specialist can execute several closely related strategies without becoming a generalist. A geographic service name and an exact-match upgrade, for example, may share the same buyer research and outreach workflow. A short-name liquidity strategy instead requires wholesale pricing discipline and a different customer network. Choose the archetype whose buyer conversations you can actually conduct, then define exclusions. A portfolio assembled from unrelated auction bargains creates fragmented research, inconsistent valuation, and renewal obligations without a coherent distribution advantage. Specialization should reduce the number of unsupported judgments required for every purchase and eventual sale.

Durable advantage comes from information and execution that another bidder cannot reproduce with one automated appraisal. Useful moats include a documented buyer universe, verified comparable transactions, relationships with repeat acquirers, language-specific naming judgment, and a clean transfer history. NameBio can inform comparable-sale research, but reported transactions are a selected sample; they do not reveal every failed listing or the renewal burden behind the winners. Separate retail sales to operating businesses from wholesale trades between investors, and adjust for extension, length, meaning, timing, and venue. An eight-character descriptive name is not equivalent to a shorter established category term merely because both contain the same keyword. Underwrite the downside as well as the attractive sale: a $300 purchase with three $15 renewals and $75 of allocated research costs consumes $420 before any selling fee. If a $1,000 exit costs 15% to distribute, contribution is $430 before broader overhead. Those renewal and commission figures are underwriting assumptions, not universal vendor quotes. A bid ceiling must survive a lower selling price, a delayed sale, and a complete failure to sell. Otherwise the apparent moat is simply optimism financed by annual renewal invoices.

## 2. Map eight plays to buyer demand, acquisition math, and fallback exits  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first three plays start with buyer usefulness rather than speculative traffic. Brandable inventory monetizes naming fit: buying for $250, carrying two $15 renewals, and selling for $2,500 with a modeled 15% distribution charge leaves $1,845 before research and overhead. Its fallback is selective wholesale liquidation or nonrenewal, not repeated price increases without inquiries. Exact-match upgrades monetize a descriptive improvement for an existing business. A $600 purchase sold for $4,000 produces $2,800 before renewals and overhead under the same fee assumption; contact multiple legitimate category participants rather than targeting one rights holder. Geographic service names narrow that logic to a city and service combination. A $100 acquisition sold for $1,000 leaves $750 before carrying costs at the modeled fee, but weak local demand can erase the entire thesis. Use Google Maps and company websites to verify operating buyers, without treating search results as proof of available marketing budgets. These three plays share research infrastructure but require separate comparable sets. Their fallback is to reduce exposure to evidence-supported pricing, not to imply existing traffic, rankings, customer relationships, or exclusive rights that the domain itself does not convey. Quote only what the transfer actually delivers.

The fourth play, expired_domain_traffic_arbitrage, is the TRACK_1 expired-authority rebuild strategy. Acquire eligible expired names with clean historical backlinks from government, educational, media, or trade-publication pages; the targets are ordinarily unrestricted domains receiving those links, not restricted government or educational registrations. Review Internet Archive snapshots, live referring pages, historical anchors, and prior ownership transitions. A backlink count alone cannot establish surviving authority, human visits, or permission to reuse old content. Rebuild original material with strict topical continuity and monetize through relevant lead routing or affiliate offers. An illustrative month with 1,000 qualified visits, a 2% lead rate, 70% acceptance, and $40 per accepted lead produces $560 of lead revenue. Subtract $200 for content and $100 for hosting and processing to leave $260 before acquisition recovery, other labor, and overhead. At those unchanged results, a $2,600 acquisition would require ten months to recover from that contribution alone. These site receipts are outside the canonical domain-sale scenarios. If rankings fail, retain topical continuity, develop direct distribution, and capture first-party email opt-ins on entry with clear purpose and subscriber choice. Avoid redirects or unrelated offer stuffing designed only to exploit the previous publisher's reputation.

The remaining four plays require particularly clear exit and agency boundaries. Drop-catch resale acquires released registrations through services such as DropCatch or SnapNames; a $400 winning cost and $1,200 resale leave $620 before carrying costs under a modeled 15% selling charge. Stop bidding at the precomputed ceiling, and use an established wholesale buyer or deliberate nonrenewal if retail demand never appears. Liquid short-name trading prioritizes bid depth: a $3,000 purchase sold for $3,600 leaves only $240 after that same charge, before other expenses. Its fallback is a documented executable bid, not a comparable from a different character pattern. Portfolio liquidation aggregates inventory for a reseller; acquiring ten names for $2,000 and selling the bundle for $3,500 leaves $975 before renewal and processing costs at the modeled fee. Audit each name because one disputed asset can obstruct the package. Buyer-side brokerage instead earns a contracted fee: a 10% fee on a $20,000 client purchase is $2,000 of service revenue, not $22,000 of operator sales. Stop work or return unearned funds according to the mandate when ownership cannot be verified. All fee examples are scenario assumptions; actual route-specific quotes control purchase decisions and settlement instructions.

## 3. Reconcile gross sale proceeds to the three owner-profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical scorecard measures gross domain sale proceeds, with acquisition and renewal costs included in margin. Conservative revenue of $20,000 at 15% produces $3,000 annual owner net profit; Base revenue of $160,000 at 27.5% produces $44,000; Scaled revenue of $300,000 at 40% produces $120,000. The associated teams are 1 FT + 1 C, 1 FT + 1 C, and 2 FT + 2 C respectively. Those labels describe staffing capacity, not an assertion that the Conservative result funds a market-rate owner salary. Owner profit is the residual economic return in this model; do not subtract an invented owner salary and still claim the canonical result. Paid nonowner labor and contractor costs must fit within the operating budget. The chapter's illustrative sales bridge uses four $5,000 sales, twenty $8,000 sales, and thirty $10,000 sales. These combinations explain the arithmetic without predicting deal flow. The 15% selling-cost allowance in each case is a modeling choice rather than a quoted Afternic, Sedo, or Atom rate. If the chosen distribution route costs more, another expense must fall or achieved owner profit will be lower. Preserve the scorecard as the comparison baseline while recording actual deviations honestly.

The cost bridge below allocates exactly $17,000, $116,000, and $180,000 against the three revenue totals. Acquisition basis charged to sold inventory is distinct from cash spent buying additional unsold domains. Under an inventory accounting approach, an unsold purchase can consume cash without immediately becoming the cost of a completed sale; impairment, abandonment, and applicable tax treatment require separate assessment. Reconcile opening inventory, purchases, dispositions, write-downs, and closing inventory rather than mixing auction deposits with expense totals. Renewal treatment should follow a consistent accounting policy, while the management model still captures the full carrying burden. Seller commissions are expenses when the operator controls and sells the asset as principal; receiving a net marketplace payout does not by itself change the gross sale amount. Conversely, an agent who never controls a client's purchased domain should not record the client's settlement principal as operator revenue. Keep escrow balances and refundable deposits out of completed-sales reporting until their conditions are resolved. For installment arrangements, determine transfer of control, collectibility, continuing obligations, and financing components from the contract. A payment schedule alone does not establish the recognition schedule, and an unsigned inquiry establishes neither revenue nor a receivable.

Capital sufficiency depends on timing, not just annual margin. The stated startup range is $10,000–$150,000, monthly operating expense range is $500–$5,000, and time to first deal is 3–24 months. Treat those ranges as planning constraints rather than evidence that any particular portfolio will sell. At $500 monthly overhead, eighteen months without a sale consumes $9,000 before acquisition spending and renewals. A $10,000 starting balance therefore cannot support aggressive buying plus that waiting period without additional cash. Renewal arithmetic is equally unforgiving: five hundred names renewing at an assumed $15 consume $7,500 annually even if none sells. Twenty sales from one thousand opening names imply 2% simple annual sell-through; that is an illustrative bridge to the Base deal count, not an industry benchmark or a probability assigned to each name. Track cohort exposure and time held so newly added inventory does not distort the denominator. The cost bridge allocates $6,000, $32,000, and $60,000 to annual overhead and paid support, corresponding to $500, about $2,667, and $5,000 monthly. The Scaled team only fits if actual employment and contractor arrangements fit that ceiling; otherwise the canonical profit is not achieved. Renewal tracking protects runway but cannot manufacture buyer demand.

## 4. Build a repeatable rights, renewal, outreach, and transfer workflow  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Rights screening begins before bidding and continues when the intended use changes. Search USPTO Trademark Search, ordinary web results, company records, and the domain's historical content; a clean federal search does not eliminate unregistered rights. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1125(d), cybersquatting exposure turns on statutory conditions including bad-faith intent to profit and the relationship between the domain and a protected mark. The statutory factors make acquisition purpose, prior conduct, and proposed use operationally important. Section 1117(d) permits statutory damages of $1,000–$100,000 per domain name in qualifying actions. The UDRP is a separate contractual dispute mechanism, not a statute: complainants generally must establish confusing similarity, lack of rights or legitimate interests, and registration and use in bad faith. A generic dictionary word can still create exposure when acquired or used to target a specific mark. Do not assume an old creation date establishes your own acquisition chronology or defeats a complaint. Preserve purchase documents, historical use evidence, buyer research, and the commercial rationale at acquisition. Reject typo variants, impersonation opportunities, and outreach strategies whose only credible buyer is the targeted rights holder. Rebuilding a former publication also requires original or licensed content; registration transfer does not transfer copyright in archived pages.

Outbound upgrades work best as narrow, evidence-based sales conversations, not bulk claims about traffic or search placement. Identify an operating business, explain the descriptive upgrade, state the asking price or negotiation process, and accurately identify the seller. Commercial email requirements under 15 U.S.C. § 7704 include accurate routing information, nondeceptive subject lines, required sender disclosures, a valid physical postal address, and a functioning opt-out process. Honor opt-outs within the statutory period and maintain suppression records across campaign tools. Rebuilt authority sites should disclose material affiliate relationships clearly under the principles addressed in 16 CFR Part 255; do not present paid routing as an independent recommendation. Lead buyers may reject duplicates, unsupported geography, fabricated submissions, or traffic outside contract terms, so report submitted leads, accepted leads, reversals, and collected fees separately. Automation should support these distinctions. Use registrar exports or authorized APIs for renewal tracking, saved comparable searches for pricing research, and a CRM for inbound routing. Apply least-privilege access and avoid placing transfer authorization codes in ordinary CRM notes. A human should review rights conflicts, unusual payment instructions, and material pricing exceptions. Fast automation is valuable only when it preserves accurate asset identity, authorized access, and evidence of the buyer's actual request.

Closing discipline protects both the asset and the reported economics. Verify control through an agreed registrar or DNS-based check, recognizing that a screenshot alone is weak evidence. Confirm transfer eligibility, expiration timing, registrar locks, and any applicable transfer-policy restrictions before promising a delivery date. Certain recent registrations, transfers, or registrant changes can impose waiting periods; check the specific registrar and current policy instead of assuming every name moves immediately. Use a written asset schedule and a settlement provider whose domain-transfer workflow matches the transaction. Independently verify payment instructions through a known channel when details change. Do not release authorization codes merely because an email displays an escrow logo. Record buyer acceptance, registrar completion, settlement release, fees, and any surviving obligations. Remove sold inventory from Afternic, Sedo, and other listings promptly to prevent a second sale of an asset no longer controlled. Review the portfolio monthly for impending renewals, rights developments, inquiries, and stale price assumptions. Compare completed sales with the original underwriting rather than rewriting the thesis afterward. A specialist business becomes durable when it can explain why each asset was bought, who can use it, what it costs to carry, and exactly what evidence permits the next operating action.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the acquisition evidence packet
   - Record registrar data, seller authority, historical use, rights searches, and relevant comparable transactions.
   - Attach dated source evidence and identify the specific buyer class before entering an auction or making an offer.
2. Set the maximum bid and reserve
   - Calculate expected contribution after acquisition, renewal, distribution, and allocated operating costs.
   - Stress the model with a lower exit price and an eighteen-month delay; reserve renewal cash before increasing inventory.
3. Distribute with consistent asset and price records
   - Publish verified inventory to selected marketplaces and route inquiries into one controlled CRM record.
   - Keep asking prices, exclusivity obligations, registrar control, and opt-out records synchronized across every sales channel.
4. Close, reconcile, and remove sold inventory
   - Complete authenticated settlement, verify transfer, record gross proceeds and costs, and delist the transferred name.
   - Reconcile settlement statements with the inventory ledger and compare realized contribution against the original acquisition thesis.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before purchase or irreversible delivery**
> 
> A promising price does not substitute for evidence that the domain is transferable, commercially defensible, affordable to carry, and deliverable under the contract.
> - Verify the seller's registration control, the exact domain spelling, current expiration, renewal pricing, and all transfer restrictions.
> - Complete trademark, archive, backlink, and prior-use review; document why demand does not depend on impersonation or another party's rights.
> - Recalculate contribution using the actual acquisition price, quoted selling charges, a delayed-sale renewal reserve, and a realistic wholesale fallback.
> - Authenticate settlement instructions and written delivery conditions; distinguish gross owned-asset proceeds from brokerage fees and client purchase principal.
