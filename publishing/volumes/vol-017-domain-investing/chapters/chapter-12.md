## 1. Days 1–7: Define Eight Plays and Protect the Buying Budget  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Day 1 begins with a written investment mandate and a separate ledger, not an auction account deposit. Use eight explicit working play codes: P1 brandable acquisition, P2 exact-match acquisition, P3 expired-authority diligence, P4 drop-catching, P5 wholesale exits, P6 inbound retail, P7 outbound upgrades, and P8 renewal-led portfolio optimization. These are this sprint's operating mapping, not eight independent revenue forecasts. Every domain receives one acquisition code, while exit and maintenance codes can overlap without duplicating inventory or proceeds. Limit the pilot to English-language commercial names in one extension and two buyer categories the owner understands. The full-time owner controls underwriting, bids, negotiations, and transfers; the contractor collects evidence and checks records without payment or registrar administration access. Allocate thirty owner hours weekly across ten research hours, eight buyer-development hours, six operations hours, and six review hours. Contract for eight hours across the first month at an illustrative $25 hourly rate, with evidence collection prioritized over broad list building. The pod is 1 FT + 1 C, not two full-time researchers. Start with thirty screened candidates, advance no more than ten to complete underwriting, and buy no more than five names. Missing the purchase target is acceptable when available inventory fails the mandate.

For a $10,000 starting account, allocate $2,500 to acquisitions, $1,000 to a segregated renewal reserve, $1,500 to three months of operating cash, and $5,000 to untouched liquidity. The month-one operating ceiling is $500: $200 contractor time, $150 research and listing services, $50 communications, and $100 contingency. Acquisition spending includes auction charges and mandatory transfer or registration costs; operating spending excludes those purchases but includes actual tool charges. This produces maximum cumulative cash outflows of $3,000 at Day 30, $3,500 at Day 60, and $4,000 at Day 90 before any separately documented expansion. Transfers between bank subaccounts are allocations, not expenses. Do not count expected sales as available cash or spend the renewal reserve on an attractive auction. At five names and an illustrative verified $20 annual renewal each, expected annual renewals equal $100; the larger reserve accommodates price differences and holding-time uncertainty rather than becoming acquisition capacity. Record each registrar's actual renewal quote, including premium renewal terms. Owner living costs remain outside this pilot cash budget and need separate funding. This narrow experiment does not recreate the annual scorecard cost structure. If the owner needs immediate income, the stated 3–24-month first-deal horizon makes this launch unsuitable for that need.

Days 2–7 convert the mandate into evidence that survives a rejected bid. Build the ledger in Google Sheets or SQLite with acquisition channel, seller identity evidence, timestamps, renewal date, transfer restrictions, comparable sales, buyer categories, and rejection reason. Use NameBio for comparable-sale discovery and verify venue, date, extension, length, and commercial context before treating a result as relevant. Search USPTO Trademark Search, business websites, and ordinary web results for conflicts; federal registration searches alone miss common-law rights. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1125(d), confusing similarity and bad-faith intent to profit can create cybersquatting exposure. The UDRP separately addresses trademark similarity, rights or legitimate interests, and bad-faith registration and use. Neither a generic-looking string nor expiration removes those risks. For P3, inspect Internet Archive captures and referring-page samples rather than buying an authority score. For P4, inspect current DropCatch or SnapNames terms, duplicate commitments, and auction settlement obligations before placing a backorder. A $500 maximum all-in bid is a ceiling, not a valuation. Reduce it whenever documented wholesale evidence, holding costs, or transfer uncertainty demands a lower number. By Day 7, retain ten complete files, including rejected candidates, and a written explanation of why each approved name has identifiable commercial users.

> 
> The owner signs each condition in the domain ledger. A missing condition suspends that purchase rather than lowering the standard.
> - Confirm current seller control, domain spelling, extension, and the specific transfer procedure; registration data alone does not prove transferable title.
> - Document trademark and historical-use review, including a reason the commercial thesis does not depend on another business's distinctive identity.
> - Reconcile maximum simultaneous auction obligations against the $2,500 acquisition allocation, including platform charges and mandatory registration costs.
> - Verify actual renewal pricing, transfer restrictions, and protected reserves before signing an offer or placing a binding auction bid.

## 2. Days 8–14: Acquire Selectively and Make Every Listing Transfer-Ready  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 8 and 9 are controlled execution windows for P1 through P4, not a requirement to spend the allocation. Place only bids whose worst-case settlement obligations fit the remaining acquisition budget. Track open bids as commitments and completed purchases as deployed capital so five concurrent auctions cannot silently consume the same dollars. On Day 10, reconcile invoices, seller messages, registrar records, and domain spelling character by character. Confirm access using a fresh authenticated session, enable strong multifactor authentication, and store recovery material outside the shared research folder. Give the contractor read-only evidence access rather than credentials or authorization codes. A newly registered or recently transferred domain may face a 60-day transfer restriction; certain registrant changes can also trigger restrictions under applicable policy and registrar procedures. Check the actual domain state and permissible account push options rather than advertising immediate inter-registrar delivery. Day 11 assigns each purchased name an internal minimum acceptable price and a public asking price. The minimum includes acquisition cost, expected selling charges, relevant holding costs, and a positive contribution target. Keep the internal floor private. If market evidence cannot support that floor, record the underwriting error immediately rather than disguising it with an inflated listing.

Use transaction arithmetic before choosing Afternic, Sedo, direct escrow, or another venue. As an illustrative transaction worksheet, a $2,000 completed sale with a 20% selling charge, $500 acquisition cost, $20 renewal allocation, and $30 additional closing cost produces $1,050 contribution before shared operating costs. The operator records $2,000 gross domain sale proceeds, not the $1,600 marketplace remittance, when following this volume's revenue basis. The $400 charge remains a cost. Fee percentages are worksheet assumptions, not assertions about a venue's current schedule; obtain the actual quote and determine which party pays escrow charges. Offers, appraisal estimates, listing views, and domains moved between the owner's accounts create no sale proceeds. For installment transactions, maintain separate contracted price, collected cash, and recognized revenue fields under the selected accounting treatment rather than booking the entire headline figure automatically. Day 12 publishes consistent retail pricing and a functional landing page with tested inquiry routing. Day 13 checks venue-specific fast-transfer eligibility and cross-listed inventory synchronization. Remove incompatible automatic sale settings: two simultaneous purchase paths can promise the same domain to different buyers. Record timestamps and screenshots so a later dispute has an auditable sequence.

Day 14 runs a complete settlement rehearsal without inventing a buyer or sending funds. Prepare an Escrow.com transaction draft or the chosen marketplace's equivalent checklist, verify the actual service domain independently, and document identity checks, funded-status confirmation, delivery method, inspection terms, and payout timing. Never treat an emailed screenshot as confirmation that funds are secured. Confirm status inside the service directly, and release transfer credentials only at the documented stage. Put the domain string, price, payment responsibilities, delivery method, and transfer timing in the transaction record. For P3 inventory, disclose observed historical use accurately and avoid promising ranking improvements, inherited traffic, or search-engine treatment that the seller cannot control. A historical link profile is evidence to investigate, not recurring revenue. For P5, request two genuine wholesale opinions from independent counterparties while clearly identifying whether either is a firm offer. Those opinions inform liquidity and do not establish a realizable floor. Complete the week with every owned name listed or deliberately withheld for a documented reason, every renewal date monitored, and every transfer restriction visible to the owner. If a listing cannot be delivered as advertised, pause that listing before generating more buyer interest.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Habits That Keep a Small Domain Desk Solvent**
> 
> Experienced operators preserve optionality by separating auction excitement, pricing evidence, deliverability, and actual cash.
> - Screenshot premium renewal quotes before buying; an inexpensive opening registration can conceal a recurring charge that destroys the holding thesis.
> - Save comparable-sale dates and venue context; a decade-old end-user purchase should not anchor today's immediate wholesale exit estimate.
> - Maintain one master inventory status and reconcile every marketplace after a sale; stale automatic listings can create conflicting delivery obligations.
> - Ask wholesale counterparties whether a number is a funded offer, its expiry, and its payment method; informal enthusiasm is not liquidity.

## 3. Days 15–21: Test Buyer Demand Without Manufacturing Signals  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Week three tests P6 inbound demand and P7 outbound upgrades while P5 supplies a separate liquidity reference. Day 15 builds a manually reviewed list of twenty businesses with a plausible operating use for the owned names. A plausible buyer already sells the relevant product, uses a materially weaker generic domain, or is launching a documented adjacent offering. Do not target a trademark owner with a demand premised on their mark, and do not imply affiliation, existing traffic, or a competitor's offer without evidence. Day 16 reviews the message and contact sourcing. Commercial email requirements under 15 U.S.C. § 7704 include accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, applicable advertising identification, a valid physical postal address, and a functioning opt-out mechanism. Honor opt-outs within ten business days and suppress them immediately as an internal standard. Recipient location can introduce additional rules, so restrict the initial cohort to a researched jurisdiction rather than exporting a purchased global list. Days 17 and 18 send ten individualized first contacts each, with no deceptive urgency and no tracking-dependent claims. Keep a record of why each recipient fits the domain. A smaller lawful, relevant cohort is more useful than hundreds of messages that obscure actual buyer intent.

Define the funnel before reading replies. An eligible contact is a verified business recipient selected under the sourcing rules; a delivered first contact is one not returned as undeliverable after the review window. Exclude internal tests, follow-ups, and duplicate people from that denominator. A qualified reply must come from an identifiable prospective buyer and discuss price, intended use, procurement authority, or transfer terms. Courtesy acknowledgments, automated responses, spam proposals, and broker solicitations without an identified buyer do not qualify. A written offer must specify a price, domain, and counterparty; keep offers separate from funded transactions. Day 19 classifies every response and records objection themes. Day 20 runs one clearly documented pricing or positioning adjustment per domain rather than changing the price, audience, and copy simultaneously. Day 21 checks inbound response time using business-hour timestamps and rehearses an inquiry when no real inbound message exists. Target responses within one business day. With twenty delivered first contacts, two qualified replies equal 10%, but that tiny sample is directional evidence, not a stable conversion estimate. Report both counts and rates. A zero-sale month remains compatible with the stated holding horizon; it does not excuse missing records or broken routing.

Keep demand testing inside the pod's actual capacity. The owner writes and sends the twenty first contacts, handles negotiation, and evaluates whether a reply changes underwriting. The contractor uses their remaining scoped time to verify business identities, remove duplicates, and audit renewal dates. Do not quietly add unpaid full-time contractor work to make the sprint appear efficient. Automation should handle renewal reminders, comparable-sale monitoring, and inbound inquiry routing, matching Candidate #16's specified levers. A calendar plus a spreadsheet can serve the first five names; additional software must replace a measured task while staying inside the $500 monthly operating ceiling. Use alerts at ninety, sixty, and thirty days before expiry, with the owner responsible for confirming registrar status rather than trusting email alone. Register explicit outcome codes for price objection, weak fit, timing, delivery restriction, and no response. Preserve the original outreach and negotiation record so a change can be evaluated against the same cohort. Do not scrape private contacts, rotate identities to bypass blocks, or keep contacting a recipient who opted out. An objection can improve P8 portfolio decisions even when it produces no transaction. The useful output is attributable market evidence, not a large activity counter.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the eligible cohort
   - Assign one identifier to each domain and prospective buyer pair.
   - Record sourcing, jurisdiction, commercial fit, and suppression status before sending; exclude duplicate recipients from first-contact counts.
2. Capture delivery and buyer evidence
   - Reconcile returned messages and classify replies against written definitions.
   - Store delivered first contacts, qualified replies, written offers, funded transactions, and completed transfers as separate events.
3. Reprice one testable variable
   - Change either audience fit, message positioning, or asking price.
   - Document the hypothesis and preserve the earlier version; never attribute improvement to several simultaneous changes.
4. Reconcile costs and decide
   - Compare evidence with the applicable Day-30/60/90 decision row.
   - Apply safety and cash stops first, then Scale conditions; all remaining cases enter a time-boxed Fix queue.

## 4. Days 22–30: Close the Evidence Loop and Apply Day-30/60/90 Decisions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Day 22 reconciles domain ownership, cash, open auction obligations, and listing status. Day 23 completes missing buyer-fit notes and evidence files without buying replacement inventory. Day 24 reviews objections and performs one documented asking-price test on names with defensible comparables. Day 25 rehearses transfer instructions against current registrar restrictions and removes stale listings. Day 26 reviews every renewal quote and assigns a hold, reprice, wholesale, or expiry review status; do not cancel a registration merely to improve an activity metric. Day 27 updates wholesale evidence and distinguishes firm bids from opinions. Day 28 calculates cumulative cash outflows, realized transaction contribution, and the delivered-contact funnel. Day 29 drafts the Day-30 decision with supporting links and a specific next-review date. Day 30 applies the matrix below and signs a thirty-day work order. These dates produce a complete operating loop even when no domain sells. Carry unresolved questions forward with an owner and deadline rather than substituting optimism. For the next two months, maintain the $500 monthly operating ceiling and review spending weekly. Early Scale means more attention to a proven lane within the existing acquisition allocation, not permission to consume protected cash or hire the Scaled scenario's larger team.

Treat the decision thresholds as explicit pilot policies, not industry conversion benchmarks. Apply Kill conditions first and interpret Kill as stopping new acquisition in the affected lane; preserve existing assets, transaction records, and necessary renewals while selecting an orderly exit. Suspected title defects, unresolved material trademark concerns, and reserve shortfalls override attractive reply rates immediately. Then apply every Scale condition conjunctively; anything neither killed nor qualified for Scale enters Fix. Day 30 requires at least twenty delivered first contacts and two qualified replies for bounded process scaling. Day 60 requires forty delivered first contacts, four qualified replies, and one written offer above its calculated transaction floor. Day 90 requires sixty delivered first contacts, six qualified replies, and two completed sales to independent buyers with aggregate realized transaction contribution at least equal to cumulative operating cash spend. An inbound sale can count toward sales evidence, but its origin must remain separate from outbound conversion calculations. No sale by Day 90 therefore blocks capital scaling, not continued careful ownership. After Day-90 qualification, release at most $1,000 additional acquisition cash only if at least $5,000 remains liquid afterward, including the $1,000 renewal reserve. Otherwise keep the existing capital cap despite favorable demand.

Keep pilot evidence separate from the canonical annual scorecard. Conservative means $20,000 gross domain sale proceeds at 15% net margin, producing $3,000 owner net profit and $17,000 implied total costs with 1 FT + 1 C. Base means $160,000 gross proceeds at 27.5%, producing $44,000 profit and $116,000 implied costs with the same pod. Scaled means $300,000 gross proceeds at 40%, producing $120,000 profit and $180,000 implied costs with 2 FT + 2 C. Acquisition and renewal costs are already inside those margins; do not subtract them twice or portray the inventory's asking value as revenue. For arithmetic illustration only, a $4,000 average completed sale would require five, forty, and seventy-five annual sales respectively. That count does not establish portfolio size, sell-through, or time to settlement. Five pilot names cannot credibly support the Base case without demonstrated replenishment and sufficient capital. Likewise, a $1,050 contribution on one transaction is not $1,050 annual owner profit after all overhead. The chapter's budget tests learning efficiency during the first ninety days; the scorecard describes annual operating outcomes. Expand staffing only after sustained workload and funded economics justify it. If every promising deal depends on ignoring renewal costs, assuming instant resale, or contacting protected-name owners, kill the thesis rather than revise the denominator.
