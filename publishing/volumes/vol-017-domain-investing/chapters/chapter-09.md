## 1. Screen acquisition intent across all eight domain plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat every acquisition as an evidence problem before treating it as inventory. The eight-play map for Candidate #16 is P1 brandable resale, P2 exact-match resale, P3 expired-authority acquisition, P4 drop-catch auctions, P5 portfolio acquisition and resale, P6 inbound marketplace sales, P7 outbound upgrades, and P8 lease-to-own exits. These are operating routes, not eight additional revenue streams layered onto the scorecard. The full-time owner sets purchase limits, interprets adverse findings, and signs agreements; the contractor gathers search results, comparable sales, registrar facts, and prior-use evidence. For each proposed name, retain the acquisition date, seller identity, purchase rationale, relevant product classes, intended buyers, and dated search evidence. Use USPTO Trademark Search, ordinary search engines, WIPO decisions, and the Internet Archive together: a clean federal search does not eliminate common-law rights, foreign rights, or confusing similarity. Document why a descriptive phrase has independent commercial utility rather than assuming dictionary words are automatically safe. For expired inventory, inspect historical branding, redirects, backlinks, and sale listings. An abandoned website does not establish abandoned trademark rights. Record the facts supporting the purchase before bidding, because a later explanation written after a complaint is materially less persuasive than contemporaneous evidence.

The Anticybersquatting Consumer Protection Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1125(d), addresses bad-faith intent to profit from a protected mark through specified domain registration, trafficking, or use. Analyze its statutory factors rather than reducing the test to whether the spelling exactly matches a registration. Typos, confusing combinations, famous marks, misleading contact information, and a pattern of targeting rights holders can change the risk sharply. Section 1117(d) permits an election of statutory damages from $1,000 to $100,000 per domain name for an ACPA violation. By contrast, the UDRP is a contractual dispute policy, not a federal statute; its usual remedies are transfer or cancellation. A complainant must establish confusing similarity, absence of rights or legitimate interests, and registration and use in bad faith. An acquisition by a new holder may be treated as a new registration under UDRP analysis; ACPA treatment of renewals and transfers can depend on the facts and governing circuit. Do not promise immunity because the domain predates the buyer's company. Identify when relevant rights arose, when the current holder acquired control, and what the holder actually did with the name. Parking advertisements and outbound messages belong in that same evidentiary timeline.

Apply the screening intensity to the play's actual failure mode. P1 needs phonetic and commercial-impression checks, not merely exact-string searches. P2 needs evidence of ordinary descriptive demand outside one trademark owner's market. P3 requires a clear separation between the domain and any copyrighted articles, customer databases, analytics accounts, or historic endorsements formerly associated with it. P4 requires a bid ceiling and pre-auction review because winning first and investigating later reverses the useful sequence. P5 needs domain-by-domain exceptions rather than a single portfolio-wide cleanliness representation. P6 requires control of marketplace descriptions and parked advertising. P7 requires prospect selection based on legitimate category fit, not threats about traffic loss or competitors. P8 adds payment-default, DNS-control, renewal, and operating-use risks throughout the term. Use NameBio comparisons as pricing evidence, not proof of legal entitlement, and use Ahrefs or Majestic observations as historical measurements rather than promised search performance. Check registry eligibility rules for restricted namespaces separately from trademark analysis. A registration agreement may impose conditions even when no governmental license is required. The pod should reject a deal when preserving a clean ownership narrative requires inventing historical facts, concealing prior correspondence, or implying affiliation with a business that never authorized it.

## 2. Contract for the asset, custody, payment, and licensing boundary  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start the purchase agreement with an exact asset schedule: ASCII spelling, Unicode display where relevant, punycode, extension, current registrar, expiration date, and seller's legal identity. State whether the transaction conveys only contractual registration rights or also specified trademarks, content, software, social accounts, and records. Domain ownership does not automatically convey those neighboring assets. Require the seller to represent authority to transfer, identify liens and pending disputes, and disclose registrar locks or restrictions. Define acceptance through objective registrar evidence, not the buyer's subjective satisfaction with future traffic. Specify price, currency, escrow charges, taxes where applicable, renewals during closing, and responsibility for failed transfers. Escrow.com or another verified provider can support settlement, but the agreement must match the provider's actual custody and release workflow. Independently verify payment instructions through a previously established channel; an email announcing changed bank details is not sufficient. Name the receiving account and distinguish an internal registrar push from an inter-registrar transfer. Current ICANN and registrar policies determine applicable locks and exceptions, so check the live policy before promising a delivery date. The contractor can prepare the closing checklist; only the owner should authorize changed payment instructions, release conditions, or negotiated exceptions.

Define the fee trigger before anyone introduces a buyer. Selling owned inventory creates gross domain sale proceeds; arranging another owner's sale creates a different service relationship whose commission cannot be booked as the underlying domain price. An introduction agreement should identify protected prospects, the qualifying transaction, the commission base, exclusions, payment timing, and a finite tail. A narrowly drafted anti-circumvention clause can protect documented introductions without claiming every future transaction involving the buyer. Pair it with evidence rules and a dispute process rather than punitive amounts detached from actual exposure. For the sale agreement, allocate liability with a negotiated cap, explicit exceptions, and a defined survival period; enforceability and permissible exclusions vary by state. State governing law, venue or arbitration mechanics, notice addresses, and how urgent transfer-related relief will be handled. Specify which transaction records each party may retain and exclude unrelated personal data. For P8, add installment amounts, payment application, renewal funding, permitted DNS changes, cure periods, default consequences, and final transfer conditions. If the structure creates a security interest, analyze the applicable state's UCC Article 9 attachment and perfection rules rather than assuming retained registrar access supplies equivalent protection.

There is no single federal occupational license covering ordinary resale of one's own domains, but that observation does not resolve every business model. Register the business where required, check local operating rules, and review state treatment of the actual services offered. California Business and Professions Code § 10131 includes specified compensated activities involving business opportunities; a bundled operating-business sale can raise issues that a standalone domain transfer does not. Holding customer funds or operating an independent escrow service requires a separate analysis; California Financial Code § 17200 generally requires licensing to engage in escrow business unless an exemption applies. Use a provider whose authority covers the transaction instead of collecting buyer money in the operator's ordinary account. Similarly, consumer installment structures can introduce state credit rules, and subscription products can introduce automatic-renewal requirements beyond a simple asset sale. Sector branding also matters: a name suggesting insured banking, licensed medical care, or governmental authority needs review of the actual representations and intended use. Under 12 U.S.C. § 1828(a)(4), false representations of deposit-insurance status create a distinct problem. The two-person pod should keep its standard offer narrow and purchase specialist review when a proposed bundle, custody arrangement, or financing structure crosses these boundaries.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before signing or releasing the domain**
> 
> Pause the transaction when a check fails; resolve the specific defect instead of replacing evidence with verbal assurances.
> - Verify the seller's identity and authority using registrar control evidence plus independently corroborated contracting details.
> - Confirm the trademark review covers intended use, acquisition timing, historical branding, and any pending demand or dispute.
> - Match the signed asset schedule, receiving registrar account, escrow instructions, acceptance event, and applicable transfer restrictions.
> - Confirm the outreach suppression record, payment allocation, renewal responsibility, and installment default process are complete before execution.

## 3. Run truthful outreach with state-aware contact controls  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Outbound upgrades are commercial solicitation even when the message is individually written and sent to a business address. CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. § 7704, requires accurate routing information, nondeceptive subject lines, required advertising identification, a valid physical postal address, and a functioning opt-out mechanism. Federal law generally does not require advance consent for ordinary commercial email, but that does not override other jurisdictions, provider terms, or rules governing particular data sources. Honor opt-outs within ten business days, keep the mechanism operational for at least thirty days after sending, and do not charge recipients to unsubscribe. Make the pod's internal standard immediate suppression upon receipt. California Business and Professions Code § 17529.5 and Washington RCW 19.190.020 address specified deceptive commercial-email practices; analyze their application alongside federal preemption rather than assuming every state provision governs identically. Maintain a suppression table keyed to normalized addresses and relevant contact identifiers, with access restricted to those executing outreach. Store the minimum evidence needed to honor the request, not a growing dossier about the recipient. Do not move a recipient who opted out of email into automated texts or calls. Those channels require a separate TCPA and state-law review, including technology, purpose, consent, and do-not-call requirements.

Use this verbatim initial email only when its factual statements are true and the signature fields have been populated: 'Subject: Domain purchase proposal for your brand portfolio. Hello, I own the domain identified below and am offering it for sale. I found your business through its public website because the domain describes your service category. This is a commercial advertisement from the seller, not a notice from your registrar. The offer covers the domain registration rights only; it does not include traffic, rankings, trademarks, or customer records. Would you like the asking price and proposed escrow process? If this is irrelevant, reply stop and I will remove your address from future sales outreach. My legal business name, offered domain, contact email, and valid physical postal address appear below.' Do not send the script unless those details actually follow it. For an authorized follow-up, use: 'I am following up once on my domain sale proposal. If you want the price and transfer steps, reply here; otherwise I will close this conversation. Reply stop to end future sales outreach.' Never insert fabricated deadlines, fictitious competing offers, or claims that buying the domain is necessary to protect the recipient's existing trademark.

At discovery, read this script: 'Are you purchasing for your own business, and who can authorize the agreement? What use do you intend for the domain? Which registrar account will receive it? Do you need an outright purchase or an installment proposal? I can document current registration control and the transfer process; I cannot sell another party's trademark rights or promise search rankings. What evidence does your purchasing team need before signing?' If the buyer asks for inherited audiences, answer: 'This offer excludes prior customer records and mailing lists. Any additional asset would require separately verified ownership and an express schedule.' If a rights holder objects, answer: 'I have paused promotional outreach concerning this domain and preserved the transaction record. Please send the asserted mark, relevant jurisdiction, and basis of the claim to the contact address in this message.' Escalate rather than bargaining through threats. California's CCPA can reach qualifying businesses handling resident contact information, including business contacts; coverage depends on statutory thresholds and other conditions. Other state privacy laws differ in exemptions and scope. The contractor records source, purpose, and suppression status; the owner handles access or deletion requests and verifies which obligations actually apply before responding.

## 4. Preserve margin through controlled closing and exception handling  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Keep the economics anchored to gross domain sale proceeds, not a buyer's future merchant volume, website revenue, or business valuation. The Conservative case recognizes $20,000 of annual revenue at a 15% net margin, leaving $3,000 of owner net profit and $17,000 of implied total costs. The Base case recognizes $160,000 at 27.5%, leaving $44,000 and $116,000 of implied costs. The Scaled case recognizes $300,000 at 40%, leaving $120,000 and $180,000 of implied costs. Acquisitions and renewals are already included in those margins; do not subtract them again from the published profit cases. Build an internal ledger that reconciles inventory acquisitions, renewal costs, marketplace charges, escrow fees, contractor expense, professional review, and other operating costs to the applicable accounting treatment. Do not assume an installment contract's full face value becomes current cash or current recognized revenue on signing. Track cash collections, receivables, and recognized proceeds separately under the adopted policy. The Conservative and Base teams remain one full-time person plus one contractor; the Scaled team is two full-time people plus two contractors. Those staffing labels do not establish salaries, contractor hours, or a separate owner wage deduction absent corresponding model assumptions.

Run each closing through four controlled stages: qualify, contract, settle, and reconcile. Qualification confirms the domain, seller, buyer authority, screening evidence, and transfer restrictions. Contracting fixes scope, price, custody, acceptance, and exception handling. Settlement starts only after the verified escrow process confirms the required payment status; a screenshot supplied by the counterparty is not proof of funded escrow. Transfer through the agreed registrar process, document control change, and satisfy the agreed inspection or acceptance mechanism before release. Reconciliation compares the executed contract, registrar record, escrow statement, bank receipt, and ledger entry. For installment inventory, repeat renewal and permitted-use checks throughout the term. Use registrar alerts and a calendar for renewal tracking, NameBio watchlists for comparable-sale monitoring, and CRM rules for inbound inquiry routing. These are the three automation levers supporting the scorecard; none should automatically accept a risky bid, settle a rights complaint, or release a domain. Give the contractor a scoped account rather than the owner's shared credentials. Separate research access from transfer authority, require multifactor authentication, and verify unexpected account-change requests out of band. A technically successful transfer to the wrong account is still an operational failure.

Keep an exception register that translates legal defects into inventory and cash decisions. A credible trademark complaint freezes new promotion while the owner preserves records and evaluates the claim. A formal UDRP notice triggers deadline tracking under the provider's current rules; do not delete evidence, evade a registrar lock, or attempt a transfer around the proceeding. A stolen-domain allegation requires immediate coordination with the registrar and escrow provider before funds or control move further. A failed payment invokes the signed cure and default process, not an improvised website shutdown. For P3 and P5, newly discovered content or customer-data defects may require removing those assets from scope while preserving the domain-only transaction if the parties agree. Review unsold names before renewal using expected sale probability, acquisition thesis, legal exposure, and carrying cost. The stated $10,000–$150,000 startup capital range, $500–$5,000 monthly operating expense range, and three-to-twenty-four-month time to first deal describe liquidity pressure, not extra deductions from the canonical profits. Allocate the contractor to evidence gathering and repeatable checks so the owner can focus on pricing, negotiation, and exceptions. A cleanly declined acquisition protects both deployable capital and the pod's limited response capacity.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Qualify the asset and parties
   - Assemble the ownership, trademark, buyer-authority, and registrar-control packet.
   - The contractor gathers dated evidence; the owner resolves conflicts, confirms the mapped play, and sets the binding commercial limit.
2. Execute matching contract and custody instructions
   - Sign the exact asset schedule and verify escrow release conditions.
   - Align domain spelling, account destinations, price, renewal funding, acceptance evidence, and any installment provisions across every document.
3. Settle through independently verified channels
   - Confirm funded status and complete the agreed registrar transfer process.
   - Use the provider's verified interface, preserve control-change evidence, and obtain the contractual acceptance event before authorizing release.
4. Reconcile proceeds and maintain residual obligations
   - Match settlement statements, bank receipts, registrar records, and ledger entries.
   - Separate collections from recognized revenue, retain required evidence, update suppression records, and schedule any continuing renewal or installment checks.
