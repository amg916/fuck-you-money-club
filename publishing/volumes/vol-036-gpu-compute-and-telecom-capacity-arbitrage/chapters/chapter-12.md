## 1. Days 1–7: Price the Fifteen-Play Backlog Before Renting Capacity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Begin with a working execution map for Candidate #36's fifteen plays, rather than treating every available capacity spread as a launch product. P01 is reserved H100 resale; P02, H200 memory premium resale; P03, B200 performance premium resale; P04, supported NVIDIA MIG slice packaging; P05, scheduled batch GPU windows; and P06, interruptible GPU capacity. P07 is multi-provider inference routing; P08, asynchronous inference batches; P09, dedicated private inference endpoints; and P10, inference overflow and failover. P11 is wholesale outbound SIP routing; P12, inbound DID and SIP capacity packaging; P13, registered 10DLC messaging capacity; P14, authorized IPv4 capacity leasing; and P15, secondary-carrier telecom failover. These labels are this chapter's operational decomposition of the supplied edge areas, not fifteen independently scored investments. Open P01 and P07 as the initial commercial experiments; substitute P02 only when a buyer's measured memory requirement justifies it. Keep P11–P15 in supplier and customer discovery until their separate operating requirements are satisfied. Every play receives a buyer, upstream resource, billing unit, minimum commitment, cancellation rule, and evidence link. An attractive catalog price without these fields cannot become a purchase order. This prevents a three-person pod from confusing a broad market map with executable weekly capacity.

Assign the full-time operator forty scheduled hours weekly across customer discovery, pricing, collections, supplier negotiation, and incident ownership. Budget contractor A twelve hours for infrastructure, LiteLLM, vLLM, DCGM, and tenant isolation; budget contractor B eight hours for carrier onboarding, telecom classification, abuse review, and reconciliation. These are sprint allocations, not assumptions that contractors provide continuous coverage. Require a named upstream escalation contact and sell only a support window the pod can actually cover. Use a shared issue board with a maximum of three active implementation tasks. During the first week, complete ten buyer interviews and request three written upstream offers, including a cancellable alternative. Record hardware generation, region, interconnect, available MIG profiles, driver constraints, permitted resale, and termination exposure. For telecom, record destinations, billing increments, number rental, failed-call charges, campaign charges, fraud limits, and supplier suspension rights. The operator owns pricing decisions; contractor A owns reproducible benchmarks; contractor B owns documented carrier requirements and ledger exceptions. A contractor's completed ticket is evidence of work, not permission to activate an untested customer route. Finish Day 7 with one signed pilot scope or a dated buyer objection that changes the next week's experiment.

Make the scorecard bridge explicit before discussing margins. Conservative gross billings of $150,000 less $52,500 upstream cost leave $97,500 retained revenue; $70,500 remaining costs leave $27,000 owner profit. Base gross billings of $675,000 less $236,250 upstream cost leave $438,750 retained revenue; $249,750 remaining costs leave $189,000 profit. Scaled gross billings of $1,200,000 less $420,000 upstream cost leave $780,000 retained revenue; $324,000 remaining costs leave $456,000 profit. Those profits equal 18%, 28.0%, and 38% of gross billings, or 27.69%, 43.08%, and 58.46% of retained revenue. The supplied monthly opex range of $2,000–$14,000 cannot represent every residual cost in every case: Base remaining costs average $20,812.50 monthly. Maintain separate fixed, variable, labor, and other cost lines rather than silently forcing the totals together. Use the supplied 65% retained share for comparison, while documenting actual ASC 606 principal-versus-agent treatment by contract. Prepaid wallet balances are liabilities until service is earned, not immediate revenue. Set a $6,000 first-month launch exposure ceiling, including noncancelable obligations, excluding refundable customer funds. With only $10,000 available capital, reduce that ceiling further whenever the remaining cash fails the runway test.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy the Customer's Bottleneck, Not the Newest Accelerator**
> 
> Preserve the option to change suppliers until the customer's workload, payment behavior, and service requirements are measured.
> - Benchmark the buyer's actual context length and concurrency; a cheaper GPU-hour can produce a more expensive successful request.
> - Ask suppliers to align reservation start times with funded customer windows; unused setup days consume the same commitment as productive days.
> - Price telecom from completed destination-specific records, including rounding and surcharges; a headline domestic minute rate hides route-level losses.
> - Require written resale rights and supported isolation profiles before selling MIG capacity; hardware capability alone does not establish contractual permission.

## 2. Days 8–14: Build Prepaid Metering and Prove the Service Boundary  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the prepaid control loop before exposing an endpoint. Stripe collects payment, but an internal double-entry wallet ledger controls available service credit. Verify webhook signatures, deduplicate event identifiers, and reconcile payments, refunds, disputes, holds, and earned usage daily. A successful client redirect cannot fund a wallet; server confirmation must establish the payment state. Keep payment processing fees outside upstream capacity cost so the 65% retained-share bridge remains readable. Before each inference request, reserve credit against its bounded maximum output, then settle actual metered usage and release the difference. For GPU rentals, authorize a short rental window and stop renewal before credit exhaustion. For SIP, combine wallet checks with duration limits, destination restrictions, concurrency limits, and a supplier-side spend ceiling. Keep a separate allowance for delayed carrier records rather than pretending usage arrives instantly. Never finance launch payroll from refundable, unearned customer balances. By Day 10, replay duplicate webhooks, delayed records, payment reversals, and exhausted balances in a test tenant. The completion criterion is zero duplicate credit and zero unbounded exposure, not merely a successful payment screen. The operator signs the reconciliation while contractor A retains reproducible test evidence for every failure case.

Instrument each service with the correct denominator. DCGM reports GPU health and utilization; it does not independently prove customer billable time or model token counts. Join scheduler leases, tenant identifiers, wall-clock allocation, and provider invoices for GPU billing. For inference, record model version, input tokens, output tokens, retries, cache treatment, and upstream charges under a stable request identifier. Configure LiteLLM routes and vLLM limits so a fallback cannot silently change the promised region, model quality, or data handling. Restrict private-endpoint P09 and failover P10 until those constraints survive fault tests. Test tenant isolation with separate credentials, network policies, storage permissions, and supported GPU partitioning; MIG is not a replacement for the surrounding isolation design. Disable prompt and response logging wherever a zero-data-retention contract requires it, and verify the upstream provider's retention terms independently. Preserve content-free usage metadata only within the agreed scope. Benchmark P05 and P08 with restartable batch jobs before offering interruption-sensitive P06 capacity. A single cross-tenant read, exposed credential, or unauthorized content log stops the affected service immediately. Performance tuning resumes only after containment, credential rotation where needed, and a repeatable passing isolation test.

Treat telecom readiness as a separate workstream rather than copying GPU onboarding. Contractor B documents whether the operator is acting as a voice service provider, intermediate provider, reseller, messaging participant, or capacity lessor for each offer. Apply the relevant STIR/SHAKEN and Robocall Mitigation Database requirements under 47 CFR 64.6301 and 64.6305 to that role; buying authenticated wholesale termination does not erase the operator's own obligations. Determine FCC Form 499-A registration, reporting, contribution, and exemption treatment from the actual services and current official instructions. Do not equate an exemption from one contribution obligation with exemption from every filing. For P13, complete the applicable carrier and Campaign Registry brand and campaign process, then separately implement the calling and texting requirements relevant under 47 U.S.C. 227 and 47 CFR 64.1200. Registration does not authorize unwanted messages. Restrict trial traffic to documented customer-controlled destinations, with suppression handling and abuse escalation tested. For P14, verify address-holder authority, leasing rights, routing authorization, and reputation history before listing inventory. Day 14 may legitimately conclude with compute ready and telecom still in qualification. The pod should report that difference plainly instead of manufacturing a unified launch status.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Verifications Before Any Paid Production Traffic**
> 
> A failed check blocks the affected service, not unrelated discovery work. Retest the exact failure before restoring traffic.
> - Payment verification: duplicate and reordered payment events produce no duplicate credit; all usage paths stop within the documented prepaid exposure allowance.
> - Service verification: cross-tenant access tests pass, credentials are scoped, and promised retention and regional restrictions survive provider failover.
> - Economic verification: quoted upstream expense includes idle commitments and remains at or below 35% of eligible gross billings under the tested workload.
> - Telecom verification: applicable provider filings, campaign onboarding, lawful traffic requirements, destination limits, and supplier escalation responsibilities are documented before activation.

## 3. Days 15–21: Sell Bounded Pilots and Measure Earned Contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Sell a prepaid pilot with a measurable workload and an explicit end date, not an unlimited discounted account. For P01, specify GPU generation, allocated hours, storage, egress, interruption treatment, and benchmark acceptance. For P07, specify model set, context limits, output caps, latency measurement, eligible regions, and whether retries are billable. Let P02–P06 and P08–P10 enter the active backlog only when a pilot reveals a priced requirement. A request for larger memory can support P02; a vague interest in newer hardware cannot support P03. For telecom, activate P11 or P13 only after the preceding checks pass, and keep P12, P14, and P15 tied to specific buyer requirements. Require verifiable customer identity, intended use, and an enforceable acceptable-use schedule. Review suspicious destination patterns, abrupt GPU saturation changes, and resale behavior against that schedule; crypto-mining is a stop condition when the contract prohibits it, not merely because utilization is high. The operator owns two pilot relationships at most during this week. Contractor A handles one reproducible technical issue queue, while contractor B reviews identity, traffic purpose, and supplier exceptions. This keeps customer learning inside the pod's actual support capacity.

Translate the 65% retained-share target into a reservation test. Let C be the committed supplier cost for a period, H the available sellable hours, p the realized customer price per hour, and v the variable upstream cost per sold hour. At utilization u, upstream expense is C plus H times u times v, while gross billings are H times u times p. To keep upstream expense within 35% of billings, utilization must reach C divided by H times the quantity 0.35p minus v. Use parentheses in the spreadsheet so the entire product is the denominator. A nonpositive denominator or a required utilization above 100% rejects the offer at that price. Illustratively, a $700 weekly reservation, 168 sellable hours, $20 realized hourly price, and $0.50 variable upstream hourly expense require 64.1% utilization. These inputs are a worked pricing example, not a market quote or a replacement scorecard. At 70% utilization, billings are $2,352, upstream cost is $758.80, and retained contribution is $1,593.20, or 67.74%, before payment fees, labor, support, and other expenses. Recalculate whenever discounts, credits, hardware prices, or reservation lengths change.

Close the pilot ledger daily using earned service rather than wallet deposits. Record gross usage charges, service credits, upstream consumption, allocated idle commitments, processor fees, refunds, support time, and attributable contractor expense. Present owner-profit estimates only after remaining operating costs are included using a consistent scorecard policy. Track replacement labor separately as a sensitivity rather than silently changing the canonical owner-profit definition. For SIP, reconcile call detail records by destination and billing increment; for messaging, include carrier and campaign charges; for IPv4, include rights, provisioning, and reputation-related support costs. Calculate service success on eligible attempts, count retries explicitly, and distinguish an upstream failure from a client-invalid request. Adopt a 99.5% technical success objective for the pilot and test latency against the customer's written p95 budget. Neither metric should be marketed as a contractual service level without matching remedies and supplier support. By Day 21, complete at least two funded pilot evaluations, even if only one becomes recurring demand. If no buyer funds a pilot, document offer, price, decision-maker, objection, and next decision date. The expected one-to-four-month first-deal window makes weak early conversion a bounded sales repair problem, not evidence that idle reservations should be enlarged.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Reserve exposure before admitting work
   - Validate customer status, available credit, service eligibility, and spending limits.
   - Reserve bounded request or rental exposure; reject work whose worst permitted usage exceeds funded allowance.
2. Execute within the promised service envelope
   - Route only to qualified resources matching model, region, isolation, and retention requirements.
   - Attach stable request and tenant identifiers without storing customer content contrary to the service agreement.
3. Settle measured usage and reconcile upstream cost
   - Post earned charges, release unused holds, and compare supplier records with internal meters.
   - Allocate idle commitments and investigate differences above one percent after matching billing periods and units.
4. Review contribution and contain exceptions
   - Update per-play margins, customer balances, service failures, and remaining supplier exposure.
   - Suspend only affected services when possible, preserve evidence, notify responsible parties, and require a passing retest.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Reviews at 60/90: Kill Exposure, Fix Evidence, Scale Demand  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Close the month against a cash plan, not a dashboard of deposits. Allocate the illustrative $6,000 launch ceiling across $1,800 cancellable capacity and benchmarks, $2,000 contractor work, $600 telemetry and payment tooling, $600 service-specific onboarding, and $1,000 contingency. These are spending limits, not supplier quotations. Count a noncancelable future payment against exposure when signed; avoid counting it twice when paid. Calculate runway as unrestricted cash after refundable balances, taxes payable, and termination obligations, divided by the greater of current weekly cash burn or unavoidable weekly operating payments. Require eight weeks after any proposed commitment. A $10,000 capitalization does not automatically permit spending $6,000: if the remainder cannot cover that runway, reduce experiments and commitments first. Keep an additional refundable-customer-balance register rather than assuming all Stripe cash is deployable. Days 22–29 reconcile every supplier minimum, contact each pilot buyer, complete at least twenty cumulative qualified discovery conversations, and issue at least five priced proposals. Measure operator support load against a ten-hour weekly ceiling and contractor work against the twelve-hour and eight-hour allocations. Persistent overtime means the offer needs simplification before the next customer is accepted.

Apply Kill before Fix before Scale at every review. Kill means suspend the affected service or stop new commitments while completing orderly customer obligations; it does not mean abandon existing contracts. Immediately stop affected traffic for cross-tenant exposure, prohibited use, missing required service authority, or unbounded prepaid leakage. Stop new reservations when projected runway falls below eight weeks or when achievable pricing cannot produce positive contribution after upstream and direct variable delivery costs. Day-30 Scale requires two paying organizations, at least $3,000 collected and earned billings over the preceding fourteen days, 65% retained contribution, and a costed owner-profit model of at least 18% of gross billings. Require 99.5% technical success, meter variance no greater than 1%, and two successful wallet-exhaustion tests. Committed GPU utilization must exceed both 55% and the calculated 65%-retention utilization floor; other lanes use their own contribution and prepaid-exposure tests. Passing permits only one additional paid pilot and at most a 25% increase in cancellable capacity. Missing a commercial threshold without a hard failure triggers Fix: assign one owner, one hypothesis, a fourteen-day deadline, and at most $1,000 additional exposure within the continuing runway limit.

At Day 60, require four paying organizations, $22,500 collected and earned trailing-thirty-day gross billings, 65% retained contribution, and at least 18% actual owner-profit margin after the full period's operating costs. Require repeat purchase from at least half of customers eligible to renew and cap the largest customer at 50% of billings. GPU commitments must exceed both 65% utilization and their retention floor. Day-90 Scale requires six paying organizations, $56,250 trailing-thirty-day gross billings, 65% retained contribution, and 28% owner-profit margin: $36,562.50 retained revenue and $15,750 profit, matching Base monthly arithmetic without claiming a completed year. Require 70% eligible-customer repeat purchase, no customer above 35% of billings, and GPU utilization above both 70% and its retention floor. Carry forward the service, meter, support, wallet, and runway tests. A second consecutive missed repair deadline stops the affected offer's new spending. Zero funded pilots by Day 90 after fifty qualified conversations and ten priced proposals also stops that offer; a documented procurement milestone may justify a cash-capped wait without capacity purchases. Expand toward the Scaled case only with the separate 2 FT + 3 C staffing plan and contracted demand. The fifteen-play backlog remains sequenced by evidence, not by catalog completeness.
