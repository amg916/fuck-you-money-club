## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The money line is drawn in the same place by every operator who put a number on it. Daniel of Data Value People tells teams to stay on third-party APIs below £10k a month, treat anything above £100k a month as "a pretty strong push to start self-hosting," and call the band in between a judgment call [WiA5Hm6IpZs @ 00:13:30]. The trigger for even reopening the question is failed SLOs or roughly £30k a month, and Daniel is blunt that "that's the trigger to start thinking about it, not to start doing it" [WiA5Hm6IpZs @ 00:23:47]. Jono Finds lands inside that band: under 20k a month, run the managed gateway; regulated, air-gapped, or high-spend shops self-host [XZPNjgJ4E0c @ 00:05:13]. Mariselle Hartwell frames the same split as stage and risk, pointing weekend projects at the marketplace and pushing teams that need RBAC, team budgets, audit logs, PII redaction, or data residency toward heavier routing [pa2rbXDnUYM @ 00:02:48]. Compliance is the one axis where the scales tip hard: Daniel says it always favors self-hosting and that a criminal-defense firm had to self-host because of legal privilege, while CodeFlow Daily warns that "compliance is not a label on the model" and lives in org settings, contracts, and platform boundaries [WT00OA0ml2g @ 00:00:57].

Across the gateway camp, nobody argues about the shape of the stack: one OpenAI-compatible endpoint in front of many backends, with money and failure policy enforced in the middle. Professor Py calls the LiteLLM proxy "a router with a wallet and a clock," and names the price plainly as one extra network hop plus upfront configuration [2nHPifw-TkE @ 00:01:20]. Ready Tensor wires GPT-4o, 4o-mini, and a vLLM summarizer running on Modal behind arbitrary alias names through an OpenAI-compatible base URL, so the client never knows which box answered [9t-W51EHgzY @ 00:02:14]. Yeyu Lab shows the same compatibility on the marketplace side, dropping OpenRouter into AutoGen 0.2.2 and into CrewAI via LangChain through an OpenAI-compatible client, for under one cent of total spend [opR3LozV3NM @ 00:04:27]. The fallback is the part everyone leans on. Prompt Engineer 48 ran the same request with fallbacks on and got an answer, then with fallbacks off and got a 404, which makes resilience a configuration choice rather than a property of the platform [FpNPRj38T6s @ 00:07:28]. The shared prerequisite is discipline before rollout: Professor Py insists latency and policy get tested first, measured against a capped max-tokens baseline [2nHPifw-TkE @ 00:07:05].

On cheap hardware the agreement is harsher: fitting a model is not running it. Byte Forensics draws the line flatly, separating placement from performance and warning that mmap and the page cache are not VRAM, so a model that loads off NVMe can still crawl [ux5n4VAhUNU @ 00:02:52]. Remy Runs puts numbers on why: a 3090 has roughly 4.5 times the bandwidth of eight-channel DDR4, and Optane reads around 8 GB/s while running about three times slower on random reads [o6WzuMA5VL8 @ 00:06:21], which is why a RAM-disk tweak alone made the 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 rig 41 percent faster. Cian Mag hit the same wall horizontally: four ThinkPad L380 boards on 2.5GbE ran Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B over llama.cpp RPC at 4 to 5 tokens per second, and adding the fourth node made the cluster slower than three because every extra machine is one more box the rest have to wait for [kgSARM9iGF4 @ 00:05:47]. Bruno Vega's free Kaggle build only works because the Strata engine parks about 72 percent of experts on the two T4s, leaves the rest in RAM on three CPU threads, and keeps the 28.8GB N-gram table on disk [KVXaHQu32Rw @ 00:04:57]. Placement is the whole game.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The LiteLLM drill is concrete. Professor Py measures a latency baseline with a capped max-tokens call, then hits /key_generate to mint a virtual key with a $0.5 max budget, 1 request per minute, and 2,000 tokens per minute [2nHPifw-TkE @ 00:03:00]. A two-request burst proves the limiter by counting 429s, a call to bad.model proves the fallback by landing on GPT-4o-mini, and /key_info returns spend so remaining budget is simply max budget minus spend. Ready Tensor runs the proxy in Docker on port 4000 with an admin/admin login and a Postgres backend, sets budget blocking with a Slack alert at 80 percent, and hands a virtual key a $500 budget [9t-W51EHgzY @ 00:06:18]. Using deliberately absurd test pricing of $1 per input token and $2 per output token, a test of 41 prompt tokens and 39 completion tokens priced out at $119, and the request that crossed $500 still went through before the next one bounced with a 400 budget-exceeded error and a Slack ping [9t-W51EHgzY @ 00:12:43]. The LiteLLM team's Auto Router layers heuristics, keyword rules, and a classifier reading the last three turns to send simple prompts to GPT 5.6 Luna, medium to Terra, and reasoning to Claude [blSVb26UQYg @ 00:02:25].

On the marketplace side, the levers are routing suffixes and per-token price. After Hugging Face moved LLM routing onto OpenRouter with :fastest, :cheapest, and :provider suffixes and no markup, Prompt Engineer 48 hit a 402 on HF credits and ran the test straight through OpenRouter: 180 requests across three models, three modes, and 20 prompts, reading medians [FpNPRj38T6s @ 00:01:27]. On GPT-OSS 120B the default ran 1.87s, cheapest via Venice 3.89s, and fastest via Cerebras 1.34s at 5.6 times the cheapest cost; on Qwen 3.8 27B cheapest crawled to 8.99s with a 22-second tail while fastest hit 1.83s for about 20 percent more money [FpNPRj38T6s @ 00:04:45]. The whole run cost 2.3 cents, with the fastest-to-cheapest price ratio spanning 1.3 to 8 times, and Venice was serving Int4 while Cerebras served FP16. Yeyu Lab loaded $5 of credit and logged Llama 13B under 15 cents, Mistral 7B at 27 cents, Yi 34B at 72 cents, and CodeLlama 70B at 81 cents per million tokens at roughly 50 tokens per second, with free 7B models capped at 10 requests per minute [opR3LozV3NM @ 00:03:30]. Jono Finds prices the toll: a 5 percent commission, so 100,000 a month in usage means 5,000 a month for routing [XZPNjgJ4E0c @ 00:02:24].

Self-hosting mechanics come in three flavors. Daniel's production path is five steps: rent a GPU instance on AWS or GCP, install the NVIDIA Container Toolkit, run one docker run of the vLLM image that pulls the model from Hugging Face, configure security, and repoint the client's base URL [WiA5Hm6IpZs @ 00:27:57]. vLLM then exports time to first token, tokens per second, and KV-cache metrics to Prometheus and Grafana, and KV caching alone is pitched as a 10x to 1000x speedup depending on prompt length. Bruno Vega's free-tier path runs on Kaggle's two T4s, 16GB each, with 29GB of RAM, 12-hour sessions, and roughly 30 GPU-hours a week, pushing 26.6 to 30.1 tokens per second at IQ2XS, worth about $1.50 a week across 3.24M tokens [KVXaHQu32Rw @ 00:14:53]. Remy Runs shows flags are money: the -cpu-moe switch took GPT-OSS 120B from under 1 to about 10 tokens per second [o6WzuMA5VL8 @ 00:10:33]. Duality Technologies wraps the confidential version: encrypt the serving image into your own bucket, store key and digest in your secret manager as the trust anchor, boot a confidential H100 TEE, and release weights only after attestation [nXTWdWFiGvM @ 00:01:00]. Only CPU, GPU, and memory samples leave the enclave each minute.

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split is over what the gateways actually are and who should run one. TWiz frames OpenRouter as open-source and community-driven, and frames LiteLLM as a lightweight on-premise engine that runs models locally, best for strict compliance needs and restricted networks [NwDtEKBC_uo @ 00:00:16]. Jono Finds describes the opposite stack. In that version, OpenRouter is a managed, closed-source marketplace with 300+ models across 60+ providers that charges a 5% commission forever. LiteLLM is free software you host yourself, either as a library or as a proxy with a database, per-key budgets, per-team cost tracking, RBAC and guardrails [XZPNjgJ4E0c @ 00:03:14]. Only the Jono Finds version comes with hard numbers: at 100,000 a month in usage you pay 5,000 a month, or 60,000 a year, purely for routing [XZPNjgJ4E0c @ 00:02:24]. Mariselle Hartwell adds a third lane between the two. It is an enterprise router clocked at 8ms versus 55ms latency, with PII redaction and data residency, which matters for agents making 50 calls. Hartwell pitches the marketplace as a five-minute weekend-project setup with no RBAC, team budgets or audit logs [pa2rbXDnUYM @ 00:01:19]. The verdict is not a single winner: pick by stage and risk [pa2rbXDnUYM @ 00:02:48].

The break-even line for leaving third-party APIs moves depending on who draws it. Daniel of Data Value People, speaking at Data Science Festival, says to stay third-party under £10k a month, to treat anything over £100k as a strong push to self-host, and to call everything in between 'it depends' [WiA5Hm6IpZs @ 00:13:30]. Daniel stacks the costs plainly. GPUs run £1-10k a month, and an ML engineer at £800 a day is about £16k a month. Daniel also warns that third-party prices may be VC-subsidized. Jono Finds puts the managed-gateway ceiling at 20k a month, which lands inside that band, and sends regulated, air-gapped or high-spend shops to self-hosting [XZPNjgJ4E0c @ 00:05:13]. Remy Runs comes at the same question from the hardware side and lands on renting. Kimi K3 costs roughly $15 per million output tokens through an API, against 89 hours of grinding on the Optane box to produce the same output [o6WzuMA5VL8 @ 00:26:57]. Daniel's own trigger is deliberately cautious: reconsider only when SLOs fail or spend reaches about £30k a month. In Daniel's words, that is 'the trigger to start thinking about it, not to start doing it' [WiA5Hm6IpZs @ 00:23:47].

Inside the routers, 'cheapest' and 'fastest' are different bets. Prompt Engineer 48 ran 180 requests through OpenRouter, covering 3 models in 3 modes with 20 prompts each. Cheapest-mode Venice served GPT-OSS 120B at a 3.89s median, against 1.34s on fastest-mode Cerebras, which cost 5.6x more [FpNPRj38T6s @ 00:03:34]. On Qwen 3.8 27B, fastest came in at 1.83s for only about 20% more cost, while cheapest dragged to 8.99s with a 22s tail. Venice serves Int4 while Cerebras serves FP16, and answer quality was never graded. Prompt Engineer 48's closing advice is to check billing, check enabled providers, and pick a mode deliberately. LiteLLM AI Gateway skips the provider auction and routes by prompt difficulty instead. A heuristic runs first, then keyword rules such as 'security review' or 'incident' force the complex tier, then an LLM classifier reads the last 3 turns. Session renaming gets pushed to the cheapest tier [blSVb26UQYg @ 00:02:25]. On self-hosted hardware, the no-GPU answers diverge just as sharply. Cian Mag chains four broken ThinkPads over llama.cpp RPC for 4-5 tok/s on an 80B model [kgSARM9iGF4 @ 00:05:00]. Bruno Vega squeezes a ~180B model onto free Kaggle T4s at 26.6-33.2 tok/s [KVXaHQu32Rw @ 00:07:53].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The gateway layer fails in quiet, billable ways. Ready Tensor set a $500 budget on a LiteLLM virtual key and watched the request that crossed the line still succeed. Only the next call returned a 400 'budget exceeded' error plus a Slack alert, so the overshoot lands on you [9t-W51EHgzY @ 00:12:43]. Jono Finds lists the self-hosted proxy's operational debt. Import time runs 3-4 seconds, which hurts serverless. The database degrades after 1M entries, and at 100,000 requests a day that threshold arrives in 10 days. The dashboard dies when you disable logging. A September 2025 release caused memory failures, and a founder advised avoiding it [XZPNjgJ4E0c @ 00:04:07]. On the managed side, Jono Finds flags credits that expire after a year, failed free-tier requests that still count against the limit, and agentic workflows that produce surprise bills [XZPNjgJ4E0c @ 00:02:24]. Mariselle Hartwell's sharpest warning is about security. Hartwell cites a credential-harvesting compromise and says that self-hosting the open-source router means you own the attack surface, including the database, caching and patching [pa2rbXDnUYM @ 00:01:57].

Cheap hardware breaks at the slowest link. Cian Mag added a fourth laptop to the llama.cpp RPC cluster and got slower output than with three, because 2.5GbE moves only about 312 MB/s. In Cian Mag's words: 'Otherwise, you're adding another machine that the rest of the cluster has to wait for' [kgSARM9iGF4 @ 00:07:10]. Cian Mag also calls the RPC backend fragile and insecure, keeps it off open networks, and admits to having no verified power or cost data [kgSARM9iGF4 @ 00:08:36]. Byte Forensics attacks the NVMe-offload pitch directly. Fitting a model is not the same as running it efficiently, mmap and the page cache are not VRAM, and heavy swapping makes the workload unresponsive [ux5n4VAhUNU @ 00:02:52]. Remy Runs confirms the bandwidth math on a live rig. A 3090 has roughly 4.5x the bandwidth of 8-channel DDR4, and Optane reads around 8 GB/s and is about 3x slower on random reads. A 32K-token prefill took over a minute, stretching to about 2.5 minutes over Thunderbolt [o6WzuMA5VL8 @ 00:12:04]. Remy also found that a single flag, -cpu-moe, was the difference between under 1 and about 10 tok/s on GPT-OSS 120B. Supply is turning against the build too: Optane is end-of-life, and DRAM contract prices rose about 90% in a quarter.

Free and cheap endpoints collapse under agent traffic. Bruno Vega's Kaggle server faces GPU queues over 15 minutes and a 32K context cap. Agent harnesses send 8-30K tokens up front, about 85 seconds of prefill, while the Cloudflare tunnel cuts requests at 100 seconds [KVXaHQu32Rw @ 00:12:40]. Tooling mattered: the same edit task took 29.5s through the pi harness versus 92.7s through open code. Kaggle's AUP also lists server farming as abuse, so multiple accounts are out, and prompts pass through Google and Cloudflare [KVXaHQu32Rw @ 00:16:09]. Bruno's own value math is blunt: 3.24M tokens a week is worth about $1.50 at $0.15/$0.47 per million. Daniel at Data Science Festival warns that third-party providers change models silently. Daniel adds that 99% uptime means about 7 hours of down or degraded time a month [WiA5Hm6IpZs @ 00:15:26]. CodeFlow Daily maps the compliance traps that blindside teams who assume a vendor setting covers them. ZDR does not carry over to new orgs. The HIPAA setting is permanent and returns HTTP 400 on non-eligible features. Cached schemas lack PHI protection. Claude Code cloud sessions, mobile and gateways all sit outside HIPAA coverage [WT00OA0ml2g @ 00:15:31].
