## 1. Assign Fifteen Plays to Accountable Seats, Not Fifteen Specialists  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the 15 mapped plays as one controlled capacity book, not 15 businesses requiring separate teams. For seat allocation, use this chapter's operating decomposition: P01 H100 reservations, P02 H200 reservations, P03 B200 reservations, P04 supported GPU partition resale, and P05 GPU burst fulfillment form the compute lane. P06 multi-provider token routing, P07 batch inference, P08 dedicated endpoints, P09 inference failover, and P10 private zero-retention inference form the inference lane. P11 SIP origination, P12 SIP termination, P13 registered 10DLC messaging, P14 authorized IPv4 leasing, and P15 carrier overflow form the telecom lane. These identifiers organize the supplied opportunity scope; they are not additional scorecard revenue assumptions. Initially activate only plays whose support and accounting evidence the three-person pod can maintain. The founder, the single full-time seat, owns pricing, customer qualification, commitments, collections, and vendor negotiations. Contractor one owns compute reliability across P01 through P10; contractor two owns carrier operations across P11 through P15. The founder retains accountability for both lanes and the consolidated ledger. Give every play one accountable owner, a documented backup, an economic unit, and a suspension procedure before selling it. Capacity without a supportable operating owner stays outside the active catalog.

Buy contractor deliverables and bounded coverage rather than pretending two part-time specialists provide continuous human coverage. Illustrative procurement bands are $90-$140 per hour for GPU engineering and $70-$110 for carrier operations, with monthly hour ceilings and separately priced scheduled coverage. At the Conservative case, even two eight-hour monthly retainers at $100 and $85 consume $17,760 annually, leaving $52,740 of the $70,500 post-upstream expense envelope for every other modeled cost. That arithmetic rules out an unfunded round-the-clock promise. Use prepaid shutdown controls, upstream support, and explicit service windows instead. Contracts should identify deliverables, acceptance tests, response windows, invoicing evidence, security duties, and remediation of defective work. A priority-one incident receives acknowledgment within 15 minutes only during purchased coverage; outside coverage, automated containment runs and the published escalation schedule applies. Pay the fixed accepted-work fee first; any bonus should depend on collected contribution and measured quality, never merely GPU occupancy or terminated minutes. Require reproducible tests for routing changes, tenant isolation, wallet exhaustion, and carrier rollback. Worker status follows the actual relationship, including control and independence, rather than the contract heading. Review applicable classification requirements before imposing employee-like schedules on contractors.

Expand to two full-time seats and three contractors only when measured workload and contribution support the replacement capacity. The second full-time seat becomes service delivery and revenue operations lead, taking daily exceptions, customer onboarding, invoice reconciliation, and incident coordination away from the founder. The third contractor provides independent metering, security, and ledger QA; this person must not approve their own production changes. Existing compute and telecom contractors retain specialist execution. Track founder hours by sales, operations, and engineering for eight weeks before authorizing the hire, then verify those hours actually move. A useful internal hiring test is collected incremental contribution covering 1.5 times the added fully loaded monthly cost for three consecutive months, with six months of that cost reserved in unrestricted cash. These are management thresholds, not changes to the canonical Scaled case. That case allows $324,000 of annual post-upstream expenses before $456,000 owner profit. Fit the proposed staffing, software, insurance, support, and service-credit budget inside that envelope or publish a separate downside forecast. Maintain organization-owned repositories, vendor accounts, and recovery methods so the new lead can operate without borrowing the founder's personal identity or receiving shared administrator credentials.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make Specialist Knowledge Portable Before It Becomes Leverage**
> 
> The strongest small pod stores operational judgment in tested artifacts, not private contractor conversations.
> - Require the GPU contractor to attach DCGM samples, scheduler allocation IDs, and tenant-isolation results to each partitioning change; verify the exact GPU and software combination supports the promised configuration.
> - Keep LiteLLM routing policies and vLLM deployment manifests in organization-owned version control, with provider credentials injected from a secret store rather than copied into support tickets.
> - Pay telecom quality bonuses only after accepted SIP calls, customer disputes, and carrier invoices reconcile; raw minutes reward expensive low-quality routes and abuse.
> - Record a quarterly replacement drill in which the backup suspends a tenant, rotates scoped credentials, restores routing, and explains the resulting ledger entries without founder intervention.

## 2. Run a Weekly Scorecard That Rewards Collected Quality  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Publish one weekly scorecard by Monday noon using the preceding Sunday cutoff, with immutable source extracts and named exception owners. The founder reviews economic exposure; contractors explain controllable operating causes. For compute, separate reserved GPU-hours, allocatable hours, sold hours, delivered hours, and credited hours. Utilization means sold billable hours divided by committed allocatable hours, with maintenance treatment stated explicitly; it is not average GPU activity reported by DCGM. For inference, report contribution per million delivered tokens by model and provider, separately tracking input, output, cache, retry, and batch billing rules. Use LiteLLM request identifiers and provider usage records to reconcile customer charges, while vLLM throughput and latency metrics explain delivery quality. For telecom, measure contribution per accepted minute or message, answer-seizure ratio, average call duration, post-dial delay, and disputed usage by destination. Do not reward answer-seizure ratio improvements created by rejecting difficult legitimate destinations. Compare each metric with its contracted service target and a four-week baseline. A red metric requires an owner, quantified exposure, next action, and due date, rather than a narrative about traffic growth. Preserve definitions across weeks so a denominator change cannot masquerade as an operational improvement.

Set internal escalation thresholds before contractor bonuses are calculated. Investigate a meter-to-invoice difference above 0.5% of sampled billings, any confirmed cross-tenant exposure, or any spend exceeding an approved prepaid limit. Treat these as management controls; contractual remedies still follow the signed service terms. Wallet enforcement must reserve estimated cost before dispatch and settle actual usage afterward, because Stripe payment status alone cannot stop concurrent requests from overspending a balance. Bound in-flight GPU jobs, token requests, and SIP sessions; account for delayed carrier records, settlement reversals, and chargebacks. Suspend new dispatch when available credit cannot cover the maximum permitted exposure. For QA, sample at least five transactions per active play weekly and all manual adjustments, then increase sampling when errors cluster. Score contractor work using accepted changes, repeat incident frequency, evidence completeness, and response performance within purchased coverage. A proposed bonus pool of 5% of accepted monthly fees is an explicit expense, not an invisible deduction from customer credits. Do not withhold ordinary accepted fees because upstream pricing moved. The founder owns procurement risk; specialists own the measurable deliverables and operating controls they agreed to perform.

Enforce a 25% cap separately on customer concentration and upstream counterparty concentration, aggregating entities under common control. Measure customer concentration using both trailing-quarter gross billings and retained contribution; use the higher percentage for decisions. Measure supplier concentration using both trailing-quarter upstream spend and next-quarter noncancelable commitments. A routing dashboard showing four brands is insufficient when all four depend on one bare-metal owner or carrier group. Record underlying dependencies where contracts and supplier disclosures allow them to be established. A small launch book may breach the cap immediately because its first customer represents all sales. Label that condition a launch exception, limit irreversible commitments, diversify, and do not represent the book as meeting the mature operating standard. Once diversified, block incremental concentrated commitments until exposure falls within limits. Maintain at least four independent material counterparties where feasible, but do not force technically unsuitable traffic onto a provider just to improve a percentage. Test substitution with representative workloads and calls. A portable customer contract still has little value when its model behavior, number inventory, or latency requirement depends on one nontransferable upstream arrangement. Report the tested substitution cost beside each concentration measure.

**Weekly Scorecard Definitions, Evidence, and Escalation Actions**

| Metric and economic denominator | Primary evidence and accountable owner | Internal review threshold or target | Required response when performance deteriorates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sold GPU-hours divided by committed allocatable GPU-hours | Compute contractor reconciles scheduler allocations against reservation invoices | Investigate contribution deterioration against the four-week operating baseline | Reduce new reservations before discounting already profitable workloads |
| Collected contribution per million delivered inference tokens | Compute contractor joins LiteLLM identifiers with provider usage | Every active model route must show positive incremental contribution | Reprice or suspend routes with negative incremental economics |
| Telecom contribution per accepted minute or delivered message | Carrier contractor reconciles rated records with customer invoices | Investigate quality shifts against destination-specific contracted service targets | Rollback affected routes while preserving evidence and customer communications |
| Metered charges compared with sampled customer invoice amounts | Independent reviewer retains reproducible calculations and adjustment histories | Escalate sampled billing differences exceeding one-half of one percent | Correct affected cohorts and expand sampling before further settlement |
| Unreserved exposure across all concurrently dispatched customer work | Service owner reconciles wallet reservations and unsettled usage | Allow no dispatch exceeding approved available prepaid exposure | Stop new work and settle outstanding reservations deterministically |
| Largest customer share of billings and retained contribution | Founder aggregates related customers using trailing-quarter ledger records | Keep both concentration measures at or below twenty-five percent | Restrict incremental exposure and pursue independent replacement customer cohorts |
| Largest supplier share of spend and forward commitments | Founder aggregates related suppliers and documents infrastructure dependencies | Keep both concentration measures at or below twenty-five percent | Diversify commitments only after representative technical substitution testing |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly evidence
   - Export wallet events, scheduler allocations, provider usage, rated carrier records, and incident histories at the agreed cutoff.
   - Store access-controlled extracts with timestamps, source versions, and checksums; keep customer content outside the scorecard packet.
2. Reconcile economics and exposure
   - Join usage to invoices and commitments, then calculate contribution, prepaid exposure, concentration, and disputed adjustments.
   - Separate settled cash, earned amounts, deferred customer credits, and estimated upstream accruals instead of blending them into a cash total.
3. Assign corrective work
   - Hold a 30-minute review that assigns every exception an owner, exposure amount, due date, and acceptance test.
   - Escalate security and wallet failures immediately; the weekly meeting never delays containment of a live incident.
4. Accept work and archive decisions
   - Verify corrective evidence, approve accepted contractor invoices, and record changes to pricing or commitments.
   - Carry unresolved exceptions into the next scorecard and preserve the original metric definitions for buyer diligence.

## 3. Close Accrual Books That Survive a Change of Owner  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Reconcile the scorecard through three distinct layers: customer gross billings, retained contribution after upstream charges, and owner net profit after the remaining modeled expenses. Conservative produces $150,000 gross billings, $52,500 upstream cost, $97,500 retained contribution, $70,500 remaining expenses, and $27,000 owner profit. Base produces $675,000 gross billings, $236,250 upstream cost, $438,750 retained contribution, $249,750 remaining expenses, and $189,000 owner profit. Scaled produces $1,200,000 gross billings, $420,000 upstream cost, $780,000 retained contribution, $324,000 remaining expenses, and $456,000 owner profit. Owner profit divided by gross billings yields 18%, 28.0%, and 38%; divided by retained contribution it yields 27.69%, 43.08%, and 58.46%. The 65% retained-share convention is a scenario bridge, not proof that every contract receives identical ASC 606 presentation. Document principal-versus-agent conclusions by performance obligation, including control before transfer, fulfillment responsibility, inventory risk, and pricing discretion. An agent generally recognizes its fee rather than underlying pass-through billings; a principal may recognize gross revenue with corresponding cost of service. Never substitute customer model spending, carrier traffic value, or prepaid wallet deposits for earned operator revenue. Preserve both the management bridge and the reporting-policy reconciliation so buyers can compare contracts consistently.

Close by service month, not by the date Stripe transfers cash. Record unearned prepaid customer balances as contract liabilities until the corresponding service is delivered, subject to the applicable contract and accounting policy. Accrue upstream GPU reservations and carrier usage in the same period as delivery, including estimates for late carrier records with subsequent true-ups. Track committed but unused capacity separately so apparently healthy customer cohorts cannot conceal idle reservations. Cohorts should retain acquisition month, customer group, play identifier, provider, model or destination, contract term, and pricing version. Report gross billed usage, earned revenue under the documented presentation policy, retained contribution, service credits, refunds, chargebacks, and support burden without netting away operational failures. Reconcile opening wallet liability plus top-ups minus earned consumption, refunds, and other supported adjustments to the closing balance. Reconcile bank cash separately. A buyer inheriting prepaid balances inherits a delivery obligation, so sale proceeds cannot treat that cash as automatically surplus. Keep customer acquisition expense and recurring account service costs visible by cohort. The founder signs a monthly close checklist covering cutoff, accrual reversals, usage completeness, disputed invoices, deferred balances, and comparison with each canonical scenario's expense envelope.

Build transferability while contracts are still easy to negotiate. Obtain written copyright assignments signed by the rights owner for custom code and documentation under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a); do not assume contractor work automatically belongs to the company. Identify preexisting contractor tools, third-party licenses, model licenses, and open-source obligations separately because an assignment cannot transfer rights the contractor never owned. Customer and supplier agreements should specify assignment, change-of-control treatment, service credits, data handling, and responsibility for prepaid balances. Review actual transfer mechanics for numbers, messaging campaigns, address leases, and reserved hardware instead of assuming credentials are the asset. For voice operations, map the entity's actual role to 47 CFR sections 64.6301 and 64.6305, including applicable STIR/SHAKEN and Robocall Mitigation Database duties. Evaluate contribution and reporting obligations under 47 CFR sections 54.706 and 54.711 and current FCC Form 499 instructions. TCR registration is an industry messaging requirement, not a replacement for statutory calling or texting rules. Enforce authorization, AUP screening, tenant isolation, and promised zero retention. Provide buyers aggregated usage and redacted contracts first; move restricted customer records only through documented, authorized diligence procedures.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify Four Conditions Before Scaling or Marketing the Book**
> 
> Record a dated pass or an explicit blocking defect for each condition; evidence must be reproducible by someone outside the founder seat.
> - Reconcile the last three monthly closes from bank and wallet records through usage, upstream accruals, retained contribution, and owner profit, with every material adjustment explained.
> - Verify customer and upstream concentration are each at or below 25% under both defined measures; identify any launch exception rather than presenting it as diversified.
> - Demonstrate written IP assignments, organization-controlled accounts, tested recovery, and a contract-by-contract transfer map for GPU commitments, SIP numbers, messaging campaigns, and IPv4 leases.
> - Verify applicable voice-provider filings, Form 499 responsibilities, messaging registration, AUP screening, tenant isolation, and zero-retention behavior against actual entity roles and signed customer promises.

## 4. Value Transferable Earnings, Then Choose Exit or Distribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Price the business on an explicit earnings definition before discussing a multiple. Owner net profit in the scorecard is not automatically seller's discretionary earnings or adjusted EBITDA. Build a bridge from reported results to normalized earnings, identifying owner compensation already expensed, genuine nonrecurring costs, recurring contractor work, and the cost of replacing founder labor. Do not add back an essential engineer merely because they invoice as a contractor. For an illustrative Base acquisition, assume the $189,000 owner profit requires an additional $90,000 annual replacement manager cost that is not already included in modeled expenses. Transferable earnings then equal $99,000. Applying an underwriting range of 2.5 to 4.0 times those earnings implies $247,500 to $396,000 of enterprise value. This is a negotiating model, not an observed comparable-sales claim. If management cost already sits in the expense ledger, deduct only the incremental replacement requirement. A small owner-operated buyer might instead analyze SDE and personally perform the founder role, but their multiple and debt capacity must match that labor assumption. Present both labor bridges clearly rather than selecting whichever produces the largest headline price. Substantiate every add-back with invoices, payroll records, or contracts.

For the Scaled case, assume the $456,000 owner profit still requires $120,000 of incremental founder replacement cost beyond expenses already modeled. That leaves $336,000 of illustrative transferable earnings. A 3.0 to 4.5 times underwriting range produces $1,008,000 to $1,512,000 of enterprise value. The higher range requires evidence of renewal behavior, durable contribution after hardware repricing, low concentration, transferable commitments, clean ownership, and operation without founder escalation. It does not follow simply from using H200 or B200 hardware. Stress the book by reducing gross billings 10% while holding upstream commitments and other modeled expenses fixed: Base owner profit falls from $189,000 to $121,500, and Scaled falls from $456,000 to $336,000 before additional replacement costs. These are downside sensitivities, not revised canonical cases. At Conservative scale, $27,000 owner profit supports little independent management capacity; a buyer may prefer a customer-book or asset purchase. Translate enterprise value into expected proceeds by addressing debt, cash treatment, working capital, prepaid obligations, transaction costs, escrow, and contingent consideration. Avoid counting wallet obligations twice if they are already included in the agreed working-capital mechanism. Value an earnout by its measurable collection conditions, not its advertised maximum.

Run an exit rehearsal before hiring a broker. For 30 days, have the service lead or designated backup conduct daily operations while the founder performs only documented commercial exceptions. Log every intervention and repair the underlying dependency. Assemble a controlled diligence room containing monthly accrual statements, cohort contribution, concentration schedules, signed contracts, IP assignments, incident summaries, supplier commitments, regulatory responsibility records, and tested recovery procedures. Use redacted configuration inventories rather than live secrets, customer prompts, call content, or unrestricted traffic records. Ask prospective buyers to specify earnings treatment, replacement staffing, working capital, transfer conditions, and payment certainty in their proposals. Compare expected net proceeds with retaining the business, reserving capital for renewals and disputes, and distributing only genuinely surplus cash. A durable cash cow still needs refreshed GPU pricing, carrier quality reviews, customer diversification, and contractor backup; harvesting is not abandonment. Keep the same weekly scorecard whether selling or holding. When the pod can produce accurate books, enforce prepaid exposure, and recover service without the founder, the owner gains two credible options: accept a well-supported transfer price or retain a controlled stream of distributions. That operational choice is more valuable than a multiple attached to unverifiable gross traffic.
