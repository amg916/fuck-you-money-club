## 1. Classify the device and the work before acquiring inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the legal file around the actual device and transaction, not the phrase refurbished equipment. Candidate #14 has three mapped operator plays in this chapter: documented diagnostic resale, service-backed surgical resale, and specialty veterinary/aesthetic resale with intended-use separation. For each prospective purchase, record the manufacturer, model, serial number, available UDI, intended use, accessories, software revision, labeling, and ownership chain. Search FDA recall records and relevant clearance or approval records using the exact model family; a registration entry does not establish clearance, approval, or current condition. The commercial lead owns supplier representations, while the operations lead owns the evidence folder and exception log. A contracted biomedical technician verifies identity and condition against manufacturer documentation. Another contractor handles specialist work only within documented competence. For diagnostic systems, distinguish ordinary ultrasound equipment from radiation-producing systems with additional installation duties. For surgical equipment, identify reusable components and processing instructions before promising a complete usable package. For specialty equipment, separate veterinary labeling from human aesthetic claims. Changing the advertised patient population or treatment purpose can change the regulatory analysis even when nobody opens the enclosure. Stop purchase negotiations when identity cannot be reconciled across the chassis, service records, invoice, and installed software.

The central FDA boundary is between restoring a finished device to its established specifications and performing work that significantly changes performance, safety specifications, or intended use. FDA describes the latter as remanufacturing; calling the invoice servicing does not control the outcome. Use 21 CFR 820.3 and FDA's servicing and remanufacturing guidance to document the actual activities, including replacement components, software changes, energy output, alarm behavior, and treatment claims. The Quality Management System Regulation became effective February 2, 2026; determine the applicable manufacturer obligations under current Part 820 rather than recycling an obsolete quality-system checklist. Ordinary domestic distribution may qualify for the registration exemption in 21 CFR 807.65, but importing, relabeling, repackaging, or remanufacturing requires a separate role analysis. Registration, listing, premarket authorization, reporting, and quality-system duties are distinct questions. Require the biomed contractor to describe both the work performed and the specifications used for release. A successful power-on test does not prove alarm accuracy, electrical safety, image quality, or performance under load. When a replacement board or software image lacks a traceable specification, keep the unit outside saleable inventory until the responsible specialist resolves the change and records the resulting regulatory position.

Apply a second screen for condition, claims, and post-sale events. The Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act prohibits specified dealings in adulterated or misbranded devices under 21 U.S.C. 331; a used-device label does not erase those restrictions. Prescription-device labeling under 21 CFR 801.109 also makes purchaser qualification and intended use operational questions, not just marketing preferences. Keep manufacturer instructions and warnings with the delivered configuration, and identify missing manuals before quoting. Avoid promising FDA approval merely because a device has a 510(k) clearance or an establishment has registered. Veterinary-only devices generally do not follow the human-device premarket authorization pathway, yet adulteration and misbranding concerns remain; human aesthetic claims cannot borrow that distinction. Establish a complaint channel tied to serial numbers, with immediate escalation for injury allegations, unexpected treatment output, contamination, and failed safety functions. Determine whether Part 803 medical-device reporting or Part 806 correction and removal duties apply to the business's actual role. Do not assume every reseller has identical reporting obligations. Contractually require prompt cooperation, record preservation, and customer-location tracking. The operations lead should be able to identify every affected buyer without searching personal inboxes or relying on a departing contractor's memory.

## 2. Resolve destination licensing, patient data, and contamination  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Create a location-and-activity worksheet before soliciting binding purchase orders across state lines. List the seller's premises, storage site, service location, buyer destination, installer, device type, and intended clinical use. State requirements can attach to manufacturing, prescription-device distribution, home medical equipment services, radiation equipment, or professional operation; one business license does not answer all six questions. California Health and Safety Code section 111615 addresses device manufacturing licensing, while Florida Statutes section 499.01 establishes permits for covered activities, including prescription-device wholesale distribution. Check definitions, exemptions, and the responsible agency's current instructions against the actual transaction rather than assuming every used-equipment seller needs the same permit. Diagnostic X-ray transactions require additional attention to state radiation registration, qualified installation, shielding, and inspection requirements. Federal assembler reporting under 21 CFR 1020.30 may also apply to diagnostic X-ray assembly activities. Aesthetic laser ownership does not establish permission to practice medicine or operate the device in every state. Veterinary purchasers likewise need an intended-use and operator analysis appropriate to their practice. Assign counsel a bounded question with the worksheet attached, request a written answer, and record the renewal or recheck date alongside the inventory record.

Treat stored patient information as a purchase-condition problem before transport begins. Ultrasound consoles, imaging workstations, endoscopy processors, and connected service laptops may retain identifiers, images, worklists, credentials, or remote-access settings. HIPAA does not automatically cover every reseller; determine whether the seller is a covered entity and whether the reseller or sanitization contractor will act as a business associate. Where that relationship exists, use the required agreement under 45 CFR 164.504(e) before permitted access, and address subcontractors. The device and media controls in 45 CFR 164.310(d) distinguish disposal from media reuse. Prefer seller-side sanitization verified before pickup, with an exception process for equipment that cannot be cleared without specialist intervention. Specify authorized methods using current NIST SP 800-88 guidance and manufacturer constraints; factory reset alone is not evidence that all storage has been cleared. Record media identifiers, method, verification results, technician, date, and custody transfers without copying patient content into the certificate. Where storage removal disables licensed software or calibration, price authorized restoration before accepting the unit. Prohibit diagnostic uploads containing patient records to ordinary sales tools, and restrict quarantine access to the personnel needed to resolve the exception.

Decontamination evidence must travel separately from the electrical test report because the two answer different questions. Ask the seller which surfaces, lumens, probes, accessories, and packaging were processed, using which instructions and by whom. A wipe-down receipt cannot support a sterile claim, and a passing leakage-current test cannot establish infection-control readiness. For reusable surgical components, preserve manufacturer processing instructions and define whether final processing occurs before shipment or at the buyer's facility. Do not let ordinary resale expand silently into reprocessing single-use devices, which can introduce manufacturer obligations. Where employees face occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials, apply 29 CFR 1910.1030 through exposure controls, training, protective measures, and appropriate handling procedures. Establish physically separated received, quarantined, serviced, and released inventory areas. The two employees remain the commercial and operations leads; the four contractor slots cover biomed service, data sanitization, logistics, and specialist legal or regulatory support. These slots are planned capacity, not permission to use unqualified generalists for every procedure. Use external certificates as evidence while retaining an internal release decision. If either sanitization or contamination status remains unresolved, stop shipment and reserve the resulting storage and remediation cost against that unit's expected contribution.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Open the transaction classification record
   - Capture device identifiers, parties, locations, intended use, and proposed service activities.
   - The commercial lead supplies transaction facts; the operations lead assigns unresolved regulatory questions to the specialist contractor.
2. Secure custody and handling authority
   - Execute acquisition terms and any required data-handling agreement before pickup or access.
   - Specify title evidence, contamination status, permitted media work, quarantine routing, and responsibility for rejected equipment.
3. Complete separate technical and hygiene records
   - Collect biomed testing, media verification, processing evidence, and applicable installation documents.
   - Match every certificate to the inventory identifier and investigate discrepancies before changing the unit's release status.
4. Release against buyer-specific requirements
   - Confirm contractual acceptance tests, destination requirements, transport coverage, and handover documents.
   - The operations lead signs the release record; the commercial lead issues the final shipping and payment instructions.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four facts that must be proven before release**
> 
> The operations lead records a pass or a named corrective action for each item; commercial urgency cannot substitute for evidence.
> - Confirm device identity, intended use, recall disposition, and any transaction-specific authorization requirements.
> - Verify required licenses or documented exemptions for the actual locations, activities, and destination.
> - Attach verified patient-data sanitization and decontamination evidence to the correct serial-number record.
> - Confirm buyer acceptance criteria, installer responsibilities, transferable software rights, and shipment insurance.

## 3. Write contracts that preserve title, acceptance, and unit economics  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use paired acquisition and resale agreements with serial-specific schedules, not a generic invoice followed by contradictory email promises. The acquisition schedule should identify included probes, carts, cables, software rights, manuals, service records, and excluded consumables. Require the seller to represent ownership, authority to sell, and disclosed liens, leases, security interests, and recall history. UCC section 2-312 addresses title warranties; state enactments and negotiated language determine the actual transaction terms. Search relevant UCC records using the seller's correct legal identity and obtain specific lien releases where necessary. Under UCC section 9-315, a security interest can continue in collateral after disposition unless an authorization or other applicable rule changes that result. Do not assume possession or a paid invoice cleans title. Identify who can reject the unit, which evidence triggers rejection, and who pays return freight, storage, and hazardous-condition handling. For the diagnostic play, attach image-quality and accessory tests. For the surgical play, attach function, processing, and component criteria. For specialty resale, attach the exact intended-use statement and software configuration. The commercial lead owns negotiation; the operations lead confirms the promised deliverables exist before either agreement is signed.

Define the payment trigger and acceptance process independently. A deposit reserves identified inventory under stated refund conditions; it does not prove delivery or authorize immediate revenue recognition. The sale agreement should specify shipment terms, risk of loss, insurance, installation responsibilities, an objective inspection window, the test protocol, and remedies for documented nonconformity. If acceptance depends on installation, state what happens when the buyer delays site readiness and distinguish that delay from a failed device. For a separate sourcing engagement, identify the fee trigger precisely, such as a completed qualifying purchase, and avoid charging an unstated fee alongside the resale price. These canonical scenarios describe principal equipment resale, not agency commissions. Gross proceeds of $250,000 at 10% yield $25,000 annual owner profit; $1,025,000 at 16.0% yields $164,000; $1,800,000 at 22% yields $396,000. Acquisition and refurbishment are included in those margins. As an illustrative unit check, a $41,000 sale supporting the Base margin permits $34,440 of fully allocated costs and leaves $6,560 profit. Twenty-five such sales reproduce the Base totals exactly. Include freight, contractor work, warranty provision, storage, and allocated overhead in that cost envelope rather than subtracting only the purchase price and calling the balance owner profit.

Draft warranty and liability language around evidence the business can actually support. Under UCC sections 2-313 and 2-316, factual sales promises and warranty exclusions interact; an inspection report or listing can create obligations that a broad condition label does not erase. Define warranty duration, covered components, response process, exclusions, and responsibility for travel and freight. A negotiated liability cap might reference the affected unit's purchase price, but specify treatment of bodily injury, fraud, title defects, indemnities, and data incidents rather than assuming every claim is capped. UCC section 2-719 and state law can limit remedy restrictions. Use a narrow anti-circumvention clause only for identified introductions, with a defined duration, preexisting-relationship exception, and proportionate remedy reviewed for enforceability. Select governing law, venue or arbitration, notice methods, and an escalation timetable. For IP and data ownership, separate physical hardware title from OEM software licenses, service manuals, calibration tools, and patient records. Patient information is not saleable inventory merely because it remains on a drive. Require authorized license transfer where needed and preserve the buyer's access to agreed service records. Finally, make insurance requirements match actual device categories and territories; a certificate alone does not establish that product liability or installation work is covered.

## 4. Use precise outreach scripts and retain the evidence behind every claim  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build outreach around equipment identity and documented capability, not dramatic revenue claims for the buyer's practice. For commercial email, CAN-SPAM requirements under 15 U.S.C. 7704 include truthful routing information, nondeceptive subject lines, required advertising identification where applicable, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out mechanism; business-to-business messages are not categorically exempt. Honor opt-outs within the statutory period and retain suppression records when contractors manage campaigns. For telephone and text outreach, analyze 47 U.S.C. 227, current FCC rules, number type, dialing technology, prerecorded content, applicable permission, and state restrictions before activating automation. A published clinic number is not universal permission for every channel or technology. The default small-pod workflow is individually researched business email and manually placed calls through an approved contact list. Use this seller discovery script verbatim: 'We purchase documented used medical equipment for resale. Are you authorized to sell this unit, and can you provide the model, serial number, service history, lien or lease status, and processing record? Please do not send patient images or records. We can discuss value after reviewing identity, condition, software transferability, and pickup requirements.' Record the answers without converting unverified seller statements into advertising claims.

For diagnostic buyers, use this discovery script: 'Which procedures and installation environment must this system support? We will quote the exact model, software revision, probes, documented service work, and acceptance tests. Please identify your authorized purchasing contact and technical reviewer. We will confirm available evidence before making performance or delivery commitments.' For the surgical play, ask separately about reusable accessories, processing capability, site power, and the party responsible for final preparation before use. For specialty veterinary/aesthetic resale, use this boundary script: 'Is the intended use veterinary or human clinical treatment, and in which state will the equipment operate? We will evaluate the device's labeling and configuration for that use rather than assume the same unit can serve both markets.' If asked whether a unit is FDA approved, answer from the record: 'We distinguish clearance, approval, registration, and device condition. We will provide the applicable authorization reference and our inspection scope; we will not describe registration as approval.' These questions identify real purchase dependencies while keeping the seller outside clinical recommendations. Never promise reimbursement eligibility, treatment outcomes, or professional permission from the equipment sale alone. Route clinical selection questions to the buyer's qualified personnel and document the configuration they request.

Close with an evidence-first quotation rather than an unsupported urgency pitch. Use this script: 'Our quotation covers the identified unit and listed accessories, documented service scope, shipping terms, and agreed acceptance tests. Software transfer, installation, processing responsibilities, and any unresolved dependencies are stated separately. Please confirm your technical reviewer and destination requirements before we schedule shipment.' When a prospect declines, reply: 'Understood. We will stop promotional outreach through this channel and record your preference.' Do not require a purchase, login, or explanatory call to process an email opt-out. The commercial lead maintains approved scripts, quote versions, and suppression records; the operations lead approves serial-specific evidence and release status. Inventory tracking and service-history indexing are the two automation levers: use them to assemble quote attachments and flag missing documents, not to invent inspection results. Review exceptions weekly, including title gaps, stale licenses, unresolved recalls, storage exposure, and unpriced warranty work. The Conservative and Base cases retain two employees plus four contractors; the Scaled case requires three employees plus five contractors. Do not claim the higher case preserves the four-person pod. With $20,000–$95,000 startup capital, $4,000–$18,000 monthly operating expense, and three to nine months to first deal, disciplined rejection protects runway as much as disciplined selling generates receipts.
