## 1. Mine replacement events, not indiscriminate equipment lists  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with three mapped plays and reject opportunities that fit none of them. Play one captures hospital replacement cycles: diagnostic systems, surgical equipment, and compatible accessories displaced by a funded replacement rather than by technical failure. Play two recovers practice-closure assets: veterinary clinics, ambulatory practices, and aesthetic operators selling identifiable equipment under a documented ownership chain. Play three sources against qualified specialty demand: a buyer specifies an application, configuration, budget, and installation window before the pod locates supply. The first employee owns seller discovery, acquisition economics, and inventory records; the second owns buyer qualification, quotations, and acceptance scheduling. Four fractional contractors cover biomed inspection, decontamination and data handling, insured transport, and bookkeeping with transaction-document administration. Contract specialists remain subject to competency checks; a bookkeeper cannot substitute for device counsel when a classification question arises. Begin with two supported device families and one geographic service radius. Favor equipment whose accessories, service access, and test procedures the pod can actually obtain. A cheap laser without transferable activation or a diagnostic platform without supported probes is not undervalued inventory; it is an unresolved restoration project. Record the exclusion reason so the same attractive photograph does not consume another inspection slot.

Create a weekly signal queue from hospital board packets, capital-budget resolutions, public procurement awards, state facility-license changes, bankruptcy sale notices, and disclosed clinic relocations. A replacement award is a timing hypothesis, not proof that the outgoing device belongs to the hospital or will be sold. Capture the source URL, publication date, facility, likely equipment family, expected removal month, and named public contact. Use SAM.gov and public hospital purchasing portals for award research, then check whether an incumbent vendor already holds a trade-in right. Review GovDeals and Public Surplus descriptions for serial lists, inspection windows, removal obligations, buyer premiums, and seller authority. PACER can identify a proceeding, but the applicable sale order and authorized representative determine what transaction is actually available. Do not treat a corporate dissolution filing as permission to collect property. Where a record is ambiguous, ask one factual question: who controls disposition of these named assets, and when can their condition be verified? Route every answer into inventory tracking rather than an unstructured inbox. Schedule fresh research twice weekly, deduplicate by facility and event, and expire replacement signals after thirty days without confirmation so stale awards cannot inflate the sourcing pipeline.

Mine buyer intent in parallel because seller abundance does not establish resale demand. Watch state facility applications, veterinary practice acquisitions, new specialty locations, public equipment solicitations, and job postings that indicate a service expansion. These signals justify a conversation, not a claim about an organization's purchasing budget. For diagnostic buyers, ask about probe compatibility, DICOM requirements, room power, service coverage, and clinical workflow. For surgical buyers, establish the exact accessory chain, sterilization requirements, and biomedical acceptance process. Veterinary buyers may prioritize ruggedness and local support, while aesthetic buyers often depend on consumable availability, activation rights, and an appropriately licensed operating setting. Verify each device's intended use rather than assuming that a veterinary destination removes every relevant requirement. Build narrowly scoped inbound pages naming supported models, available configurations, inspection deliverables, and service geography. Use HubSpot or a structured spreadsheet for demand records and Google Search Console to identify search terms that produce qualified configuration requests. Do not advertise inventory you merely saw at auction as inventory you own. Publish procurement briefs for sought equipment, with condition requirements and response instructions. The useful inbound conversion is a documented buying requirement, not a download, an anonymous price inquiry, or an unverified expression of interest.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the spread inside configuration and timing**
> 
> Experienced resellers price the complete usable system and the removal calendar before negotiating the headline purchase price.
> - Ask the hospital biomed department for the outgoing asset configuration and service chronology; reconcile them to the disposition officer's serial schedule.
> - Photograph probe labels, footswitches, handpieces, carts, and installed options separately; one missing proprietary accessory can erase the apparent equipment discount.
> - Obtain a written OEM or authorized-provider answer on software transfer and reactivation costs before treating an unlocked demonstration as transferable functionality.
> - Offer a documented pickup window after seller authority is verified; reliable removal can win a lot without increasing the acquisition price.

## 2. Qualify demand and underwrite serial-specific inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Score each opportunity out of one hundred using five weighted fields: verified buyer demand earns thirty points, usable and supportable condition twenty-five, documented seller authority twenty, landed contribution fifteen, and executable pickup timing ten. Award demand points only against a named buyer's recorded requirements, purchasing authority, budget, and target date. Award condition points against inspection evidence rather than the phrase working when removed. A score of eighty merits a quotation and reserved inspection capacity; sixty through seventy-nine remains research only; anything lower leaves the active queue. These are internal operating thresholds, not measured industry predictors. Critical failures override the total: unresolved ownership, inaccessible stored patient data, missing essential accessories, or an unresolved recall affecting safe operation prevents purchase. Match seller identity, serials, bills of sale, lease releases, and relevant lien records. Under UCC section 9-315, a security interest can continue in disposed collateral unless an applicable exception or authorized disposition applies; verify the governing state's enactment and obtain releases where needed. A search result alone does not establish clean title. Assign an employee to resolve each failed field, give the task a dated next action, and prohibit a persuasive seller narrative from silently changing the score.

Underwrite the delivered configuration, not the auction hammer price. An illustrative unit sold for $25,000 might require $12,000 acquisition, $600 buyer premium, $2,400 refurbishment, $1,200 freight and rigging, $500 data handling and decontamination, $300 payment costs, and a $1,000 warranty reserve. Those direct charges total $18,000, leaving $7,000 contribution before allocated overhead, carrying costs, and other business expenses. Applicable unrecoverable taxes and additional installation costs must be added when present; the example assumes none beyond the stated charges. That twenty-eight-percent contribution is not the canonical net margin. Backsolve the purchase ceiling from the buyer's supported delivered price less every nonpurchase charge and the required contribution. Use written carrier quotes and a biomed scope rather than rounded guesses. Within the $20,000–$95,000 startup-capital range, keep a separate reserve for operating expenses, warranty work, and delayed collections. Initially cap each purchase at twenty-five percent of deployable inventory cash and hold no more than two unaccepted lots. Both are conservative internal controls, not scorecard assumptions. Request a time-limited seller inspection option where possible; do not describe a refundable buyer reservation as earned resale revenue or spend it as unrestricted margin.

Make the inspection packet answer what the next owner will need to operate the equipment. Index service history by serial, record firmware and licensed options, check FDA recall records, and have a competent biomed document model-specific functional and electrical-safety testing. Record instruments, calibration status, test conditions, deficiencies, corrective work, and final results. Cleaning evidence must identify the method and its compatibility with manufacturer instructions; a cosmetic wipe is not decontamination evidence. For ultrasound consoles, imaging workstations, and other storage-bearing devices, isolate storage before any demonstration or transfer. Determine whether the reseller or contractor is acting as a HIPAA business associate; when applicable, establish the required agreement and safeguards before handling protected information. Apply the seller's authorized sanitization method, retain verification evidence without patient content, and quarantine media that cannot be cleared. Use NIST SP 800-88 as technical guidance, not as a substitute for HIPAA obligations. Evaluate modifications against FDA's servicing-versus-remanufacturing distinction; changes that significantly alter performance, safety specifications, or intended use require a different regulatory analysis. Assess applicable registration, listing, quality-system, labeling, and state dealer or radiation-equipment requirements before expanding the offering. The pod should reject a category it cannot competently inspect rather than manufacture confidence through a longer sales description.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before the pod commits acquisition cash**
> 
> A high aggregate score never compensates for missing authority, unsafe condition, inaccessible data, or an unexecutable delivery.
> - Match seller authority, serial schedule, ownership evidence, and required releases; resolve leased equipment and security interests before executing the purchase.
> - Obtain configuration-specific biomed results, accessory reconciliation, recall disposition, and a service path that covers the buyer's location.
> - Verify authorized data sanitization and decontamination records; prohibit transfer of unresolved patient-bearing storage or inadequately processed equipment.
> - Confirm written delivered-price economics, available acquisition cash, contractor capacity, and buyer acceptance terms before releasing funds.

## 3. Run a permission-aware cadence that earns the first purchase orders  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run separate seller and buyer sequences so each message asks for one appropriate next action. On day one, send a short business email referencing the public replacement or expansion event and naming the equipment family. For sellers, request the disposition contact and serial schedule; for buyers, offer a configuration worksheet and ask which application must be supported. On day three, make a manual call to a published business number during appropriate local hours. On day six, send one useful artifact: a removal checklist to the seller or a redacted sample acceptance packet to the buyer. On day ten, follow up through a legitimate professional introduction or platform message without scraping restricted information. On day fifteen, close the sequence with a direct question about timing, then stop unless the contact requests continued engagement. Follow CAN-SPAM requirements for commercial email, including accurate headers, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out honored within the statutory period. Maintain suppression records across employees and contractors. Avoid automated calls, prerecorded messages, and unsolicited texts without a documented assessment of TCPA and applicable state requirements. A public telephone number is not blanket permission for every outreach technology. Measure replies that advance qualification, not merely opens.

Treat conversion benchmarks as a testable cohort plan, not a market fact. For an initial ninety-day buyer cohort, model 120 researched accounts producing forty-eight qualified requirements, twenty-four configuration calls, twelve accepted inspection packets or demonstrations, six signed purchase orders, and three delivered units with completed acceptance. The stage rates are forty percent followed by four successive fifty-percent transitions; the overall researched-account-to-accepted-sale rate is 2.5 percent. These are deliberately explicit planning assumptions that actual results must replace. Define a qualified requirement as a named buying organization with application, configuration, budget authority, and timing recorded. Count a purchase order only when its commercial conditions are understood; count a completed win only after contractual delivery and acceptance. Track seller sourcing separately, because several potential lots can support one buyer and one lot can contain several units. Use distinct account, lot, serial, and transaction identifiers to prevent double counting. At day thirty, inspect the largest stage loss and revise one variable: target segment, configuration fit, evidence packet, or commercial terms. Do not solve weak qualification by buying more equipment. Extend the same measured system toward three-to-ten wins over three-to-nine months while respecting the pod's actual inspection and delivery capacity.

Handle objections with evidence and scoped commercial choices rather than discount reflexes. When a buyer says a marketplace listing is cheaper, compare serial-specific accessories, tested functions, activation, freight, installation, and warranty coverage line by line. If they want OEM service, seek written eligibility and pricing from the relevant provider; an independent reseller's optimism cannot establish manufacturer support. When they fear downtime, specify response hours, available parts, escalation contacts, and the exact remedy the pod can fund. Do not promise a loaner unless compatible stock is reserved. For a seller demanding immediate payment, explain which ownership and inspection evidence allows a faster close and offer a dated inspection appointment. For a buyer seeking a trial, price freight, damage responsibility, acceptance tests, return timing, and restocking treatment explicitly before shipment. If the objection is cash timing, refer the buyer to an independent equipment lender and verify funding before releasing inventory; keep lender fees separate from equipment economics. End every substantive call with an agreed document, responsible person, and date. Send a concise recap distinguishing confirmed facts from open conditions. A quotation should expire when its freight, inventory availability, or service assumptions expire, not remain indefinitely available because the CRM never scheduled a follow-up.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build paired supply and demand records
   - Assign every lead to one of the three mapped plays.
   - Store the source, event date, equipment family, authorized contact, and next action; maintain separate buyer-account and seller-lot identifiers.
2. Qualify before reserving technical labor
   - Complete the weighted score and identify overriding blockers.
   - Reserve a biomed slot only after configuration, seller authority, plausible delivered economics, and a credible buyer requirement justify inspection.
3. Quote the documented usable configuration
   - Issue a serial-specific quotation and acceptance packet.
   - Include accessories, licensed options, test evidence, sanitization status, delivery scope, warranty terms, payment conditions, and a defined quotation expiry.
4. Close and reconcile the accepted transaction
   - Collect the required payment and document delivery acceptance.
   - Match the purchase order, invoice, serial, freight evidence, acceptance record, direct charges, and warranty reserve before updating realized contribution.

## 4. Pace the first ten wins against cash, labor, and canonical economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the first month to choose the supported equipment families, contract the four specialist functions, create document templates, and build the initial buyer cohort. Months two and three concentrate on inspection-ready seller relationships and configuration calls, not warehouse accumulation. The earliest completed transaction may fall in month three; complex replacement schedules or site preparation can push first-deal timing toward month nine. Treat three-to-ten completed wins during that operating window as a target range, not a substitute for the stated three-to-nine-month time to first deal. Assign weekly capacity explicitly: the sourcing employee spends two research blocks on signals and two on seller verification; the sales employee runs qualification calls, quotations, and acceptance coordination. Reserve two biomed inspection slots weekly only where contractor availability supports them, and batch nearby visits to reduce travel costs. Limit simultaneous active acquisitions to two until actual turnaround and warranty workload are measured. Four contractors means four specialist relationships, not four full-time people waiting for instructions. Maintain backup providers for critical functions without silently counting them as permanent staff. Move a delivery date when competence or safe handling cannot be secured; the calendar does not override technical readiness or the buyer's receiving requirements.

Keep the scorecard visible while reviewing early deals. Conservative annual gross equipment resale proceeds are $250,000 at a ten-percent net margin, producing $25,000 owner net profit with two full-time employees and four contractors. Base proceeds are $1,025,000 at 16.0 percent, producing $164,000 with the same staffing structure. Scaled proceeds are $1,800,000 at twenty-two percent, producing $396,000 with three employees and five contractors, eight people total. The corresponding total expense envelopes are $225,000, $861,000, and $1,404,000. Acquisition and refurbishment sit inside those expenses, not outside the margin calculation. The stated $4,000–$18,000 monthly operating-expense range does not represent the entire cost of purchased inventory. Gross resale proceeds also exclude the buyer's patient billings, procedure volume, and equipment-financing principal. These scenarios assume equipment resale as principal; a separate brokerage service must assess its own revenue presentation rather than booking the asset's entire selling price as a commission business's revenue. Do not annualize three successful early transactions into a claimed Base result. Instead, reconcile realized contribution, remaining overhead, inventory aging, warranty exposure, and available cash monthly. The Scaled margin requires an actual operating improvement; adding inventory and a third employee does not mechanically create a twenty-two-percent outcome.

Instrument the system so failures become specific operating decisions. Inventory tracking should retain acquisition date, serial, configuration, direct costs, storage location, reservation status, and days since the last qualified buyer interaction. Service-history indexing should connect each claim in the quotation to a dated report, photograph, or provider confirmation. Keep patient content out of the CRM and sales attachments. Use document permissions and a transfer log when sharing technical evidence with buyers. Review unsold equipment at thirty, sixty, and ninety days: obtain fresh buyer feedback first, revise configuration or price second, and execute a documented exit plan when carrying costs defeat the original thesis. For every lost deal, record one primary reason such as unsupported activation, weak title evidence, excessive landed cost, or installation delay. For every accepted deal, request a factual reference and permission to describe the equipment category and service experience without exposing patient or confidential operational details. Feed repeat demand into the next acquisition search. Expand from the initial four-person pod only after measured inspection backlog, repeatable demand, and cash conversion justify the extra capacity. The durable advantage is a reliable chain from signal to supported equipment, not a larger spreadsheet of sellers or a warehouse full of apparently inexpensive machines.
