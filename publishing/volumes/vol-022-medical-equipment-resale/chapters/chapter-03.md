## 1. Define the sale, the serial cohort, and the three operating plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the ledger around a serialized device, not a marketplace listing or a hospital purchase order. Assign acquisition cost, accessories, software-transfer permissions, test records, and freight to that serial before quoting a buyer. For this model, one standardized cohort unit produces $25,500 of invoiced equipment consideration and $500 of expected price credits, leaving $25,000 of recognized resale revenue. Ten, forty-one, and seventy-two equivalent units therefore reconcile to $250,000, $1,025,000, and $1,800,000. Equivalent units are a planning denominator, not a claim that every ultrasound console, surgical tower, veterinary monitor, or aesthetic platform sells at the same price. Exclude collected sales tax and refundable buyer deposits from revenue. Recognize equipment sales when the applicable control-transfer conditions are satisfied, including substantive acceptance provisions, rather than when inventory leaves the warehouse. Document whether quoted freight is part of the equipment transaction or a separate service. The invoice-to-revenue bridge prevents gross billings from silently replacing the canonical scorecard basis. In QuickBooks or an equivalent ledger, use inventory items and a serial register together; reconcile credits against their original invoices rather than burying them in miscellaneous expenses. Require the same reconciliation for returned devices subsequently resold under another invoice.

The diagnostic refurbishment resale play buys title, controls repair work, and earns the equipment spread. At Base economics, a $12,500 purchase plus $1,750 parts, $1,250 contractor delivery labor, $750 logistics, and $500 expected warranty expense leaves $8,250 contribution on $25,000 recognized revenue before selling and fixed costs. Test the actual configuration: a console without a transferable software license or a usable probe can erase that contribution. Fallback to a documented return right, a lower acquisition price, or a parts-only disposition that is technically and commercially appropriate; never relabel a failed device as patient-ready. Surgical consignment is the second play and a different revenue model. If the operator acts as agent on a $25,000 sale and earns a disclosed 15% commission, operator revenue is $3,750, not $25,000. Deducting $900 inspection and freight coordination, $350 selling expense, and $250 expected dispute expense leaves $2,250 before fixed overhead. Keep this illustrative commission ledger outside the canonical owned-inventory cases. When ownership, service history, or software rights cannot be verified, return the consignment rather than funding repairs against an unenforceable promise of reimbursement.

The supplied TRACK_2 mapping, mca_ucc_stacking_default_restructure_brokerage, is an adjacent cash-flow review activity, not a device acquisition shortcut. A changed UCC filing can prompt consensual outreach, but it does not prove default, establish a payoff amount, or authorize access to merchant records. With permission, verify contracts, bank debits, outstanding obligations, security interests, and the merchant's stated objective. An illustrative $100,000 obligation review might carry a disclosed $2,000 merchant fee and $1,000 counterparty fee only where permitted and accepted in writing. Subtract $1,000 specialist work, $400 acquisition CAC, and $600 expected refunds to leave $1,000 contribution before overhead. The $100,000 obligation is never operator revenue, and neither those fees nor that contribution enters the equipment scorecard. Dual-sided compensation requires clear conflict disclosure and a jurisdiction-specific review of licensing and fee rules. Do not propose undisclosed additional advances to disguise distress. If obligations, authority, or permissible compensation cannot be established, stop placement, refund improper fees, and refer the merchant to independent licensed restructuring counsel. Separately, require authorized lien releases before buying encumbered equipment; a restructuring conversation does not extinguish a secured party's rights.

## 2. Reconcile every gross-to-net deduction to the canonical profit cases  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the waterfall below as an explicitly illustrative cost allocation that reconciles exactly to the supplied scorecard, not as a claim that these expense accounts came from audited statements. Conservative recognized revenue of $250,000 supports $225,000 total deductions and $25,000 owner profit. Base revenue of $1,025,000 supports $861,000 deductions and $164,000 profit. Scaled revenue of $1,800,000 supports $1,404,000 deductions and $396,000 profit. Acquisition, refurbishment parts, contractor labor, logistics, and warranty expense are deducted before the contribution subtotal. Acquisition CAC, collection charges, and fixed operating expense follow it. Credits appear above recognized revenue and must not be deducted again below it. The implied purchase cost per equivalent unit falls from $13,000 to $12,500 to $12,000 as sourcing improves; the model does not assume that technicians magically become free. Parts, labor, and shipping also improve through repeat configurations and consolidated routes. Validate each reduction with vendor quotations and closed work orders. A low auction hammer price means little when buyer premiums, rigging, missing accessories, or a failed display are omitted from landed cost. Capitalize eligible acquisition costs consistently and reconcile inventory movements to the general ledger.

Separate three different loss mechanisms. Commercial credits reduce transaction consideration: the model allows $500 per equivalent unit for negotiated concessions and expected sales reversals. Warranty expense funds post-sale obligations: $750, $500, and $400 per unit across the three cases. Inventory scrub means a pre-sale technical, title, data, or obsolescence review that can force rework or a write-down. The acquisition and parts budgets assume usable inventory; material scrap losses require an additional adjustment rather than an optimistic reclassification. Build reserve estimates by device family, failure mode, sale age, and actual claim severity. For example, a 5% probability of a $10,000 covered repair supports $500 expected warranty expense per sale, but one early failure still needs $10,000 of immediate cash. Credit accruals and warranty accruals are accounting estimates, not separate cash accounts automatically funded by posting entries. Maintain a liquidity buffer and reconcile settlements against the original estimates. Handle a returned device through both the sales reversal and its recoverable inventory value; deducting the full invoice and the full original purchase again would distort the cohort's actual economic loss.

Contractor delivery labor means hands-on biomed inspection, repair, calibration, and commissioned installation included in the quoted sale. It is not a substitute label for customer acquisition or founder time. Base labor of $51,250 across forty-one units equals $1,250 per unit; a ten-hour allowance implies $125 per hour before any separately quoted travel. CAC is $750 per unit in Conservative and Base and $700 in Scaled, covering attributable selling work rather than equipment purchases. Payment and collection charges remain 1% of recognized revenue; actual card-heavy sales can exceed that assumption. The residual fixed expense is $32,500, $133,250, and $212,400, covering the modeled employee cash compensation and other fixed operating costs. Those amounts are reconciliation budgets, not demonstrated staffing quotes. In particular, $32,500 cannot fund two ordinary market-rate full-time employees plus premises and insurance. Treat the Conservative 1 FT + 3 C designation as capacity involving owner labor or otherwise supported resources until actual payroll proves feasibility. Owner profit here precedes personal taxes and distributions; unpaid owner replacement labor is not silently included. If replacing that labor adds $90,000 of annual expense, Conservative economic profit becomes negative $65,000 without changing the published canonical case.

## 3. Fund acceptance delays, DSO, and payout locks before buying inventory  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Cash conversion begins when the supplier receives money, not when accounting recognizes a sale. In the Base calendar, pay $12,500 acquisition cost on day zero, $3,000 combined parts and labor around day twenty, and $750 logistics around day thirty. Complete substantive buyer acceptance on day forty-five, collect under thirty-day terms on day seventy-five, and receive unrestricted processor funds after a fifteen-day payout lock on day ninety. This separates forty-five inventory and acceptance days, thirty DSO days, and fifteen additional restricted-cash days. If a platform reports DSO through final payout, do not add its hold period again. With no supplier credit, the planning cash conversion interval is ninety days. A conservative prefunding approximation places the full $16,250 direct cash cost at the start: forty-one annual units divided by 365, multiplied by ninety days and $16,250, requires approximately $164,281. Actual staged disbursements reduce average funding, while clustered auction purchases increase peak funding. Use the approximation for an initial capital screen and a weekly serial-level cash schedule for purchase authorization. Neither an issued invoice nor a processor balance under restriction can pay the next supplier.

Apply the same conservative prefunding method to all three cases. Conservative uses $17,500 direct cash cost per unit and 120 conversion days, creating approximately $57,534 of direct working capital. Base requires $164,281 at ninety days. Scaled uses $15,200 per unit and seventy-five days, requiring approximately $224,877. Add a fixed-expense runway covering each conversion interval: approximately $10,685, $32,856, and $43,644. Add separate illustrative liquidity cushions of $10,000, $25,000, and $40,000 for claim timing, selling costs, refunds, and uneven receipts. The resulting screening targets are approximately $78,219, $222,137, and $308,521 before extraordinary setup costs or debt service. These targets fit inside the supplied $20,000–$95,000 startup range but do not establish that its lower bound supports every operating case. The cushions are cash-planning allowances, not additional deductions from canonical profit. Replace them with observed weekly peaks as evidence accumulates. A $10,000 refundable customer deposit can reduce temporary external funding only if its contractual use permits that treatment and refund liquidity remains available. Maintain a deposit liability and never count the same deposit as both earned profit and unrestricted emergency reserves.

Attack float through contract mechanics before borrowing against it. Obtain equipment-specific acceptance tests, named receiving personnel, delivery appointments, and purchase-order matching instructions before dispatch. A hospital invoice rejected for an incorrect entity name can age for weeks despite a functioning device. Negotiate supplier terms only when the discount foregone is cheaper than the funding benefit; a 2% discount lost for thirty extra days represents an expensive source of short-term credit. Obtain actual marketplace and processor hold terms instead of assuming immediate ACH availability. Where deposits are practical, match them to evidenced procurement or refurbishment milestones and clearly state refund conditions. When a receivables facility is considered, model its interest, minimum charges, recourse, concentration limits, and disputed-invoice exclusions outside the unchanged canonical case. A lender advance against an invoice is debt proceeds, not a second equipment sale. Forecast unrestricted cash weekly over thirteen weeks, connecting each payment to a serial and each collection to an acceptance event. Pause purchases when the stressed forecast cannot cover existing service commitments and refunds. Liquidating a slow device at a measured loss can preserve more enterprise value than financing another quarter of warehouse optimism.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the serial-level cash commitment
   - Record supplier payments, quoted parts, technician milestones, and freight dates before bidding.
   - Include lien-release conditions and distinguish refundable deposits from earned customer receipts.
2. Attach acceptance and payout evidence
   - Link buyer acceptance terms, invoice requirements, due dates, and processor restrictions to each sale.
   - Use the unrestricted bank-availability date rather than a marketplace dashboard balance.
3. Run the thirteen-week downside forecast
   - Shift collections thirty days later and insert the largest plausible near-term warranty claim.
   - Compare weekly unrestricted cash with supplier obligations, payroll, refund exposure, and service commitments.
4. Release only funded purchases
   - Approve a purchase only when its stressed cash schedule remains supportable.
   - If funding fails, renegotiate milestones, choose consignment, reduce the bid, or decline the device.

## 4. Stress the spread and convert technical defects into explicit cash decisions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run sensitivities against unchanged throughput before assuming sales growth will repair weak economics. A 10% acquisition-cost increase reduces annual owner profit to $12,000 Conservative, $112,750 Base, and $309,600 Scaled. A five-percentage-point reduction in realized selling price is different: using a 5% reduction in recognized revenue and holding all modeled costs constant produces $12,500, $112,750, and $306,000 profit. An additional warranty expense equal to 2% of original recognized revenue leaves $20,000, $143,500, and $360,000. Combining the acquisition shock and that warranty shock leaves $7,000, $92,250, and $273,600. These are stress outcomes, not replacements for the canonical scorecard. Extending collections thirty days changes liquidity first: the direct prefunding requirement rises by approximately $14,384, $54,760, and $89,951. If that incremental requirement remains outstanding for a full year at 12%, annual interest is approximately $1,726, $6,571, and $10,794. A one-time thirty-day loan instead incurs interest only for its actual duration. Add any fees separately and avoid confusing a recurring working-capital balance with a single delayed invoice.

Technical and statutory checks determine which losses belong in those sensitivities. Under 21 CFR 807.20, registration and listing duties depend on the establishment's actual activities; a resale label does not settle whether work constitutes manufacturing or remanufacturing. Use FDA's servicing-versus-remanufacturing framework to assess changes to intended use, performance, or safety specifications, and document the device-specific conclusion before expanding work scope. Where the operator is a covered manufacturer, 21 CFR 820.10 establishes applicable quality management system requirements. Allocate the resulting inspection, documentation, and corrective-work costs rather than assuming all repairs are interchangeable bench labor. Separately, 45 CFR 164.310(d)(2)(i) and (ii) address disposal and media reuse for regulated entities handling electronic protected health information. Determine whether the reseller's services create business-associate duties; removing a patient name from the startup screen is not complete sanitization. Require validated media handling and device-appropriate decontamination evidence without destroying calibration or software functionality. Human-use, veterinary, and aesthetic configurations can have different intended-use and state licensing implications. Confirm the actual jurisdiction and device category instead of treating every used medical machine as subject to identical requirements.

Close each month with an exception review that connects reserves, physical inventory, and collectible receivables. For each aging serial, compare its current achievable selling price with remaining repair cost, freight, likely concessions, and warranty exposure. Obtain a real buyer indication or parts bid rather than rolling forward the original listing price. For every reversal, identify whether the root cause was a missing accessory, misdescribed software entitlement, failed acceptance test, incomplete data handling, or an unpaid purchase order. Feed those findings into acquisition limits and the next cohort's reserve assumptions. Measure service-history indexing by the reduction in technician search hours and duplicated tests, not by how many documents were uploaded. Measure inventory automation by fewer serial mismatches, faster aging reports, and recovered accessories. In the Base case, saving two contractor hours per unit at $125 across forty-one units creates $10,250 of potential annual benefit before software and implementation costs; book it only after invoices confirm the reduction. The practical decision rule is simple: buy only when documented resale contribution survives a credible defect scenario and the cash forecast survives delayed acceptance. A margin percentage cannot rescue inventory that cannot lawfully or practically ship.
