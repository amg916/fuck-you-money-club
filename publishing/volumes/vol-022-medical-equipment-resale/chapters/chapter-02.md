## 1. Sell the Budget Holder a Recoverable Operating Outcome  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The person who wants the machine rarely controls every condition needed to buy it. In a hospital, a department director establishes clinical need, biomedical engineering evaluates supportability, supply chain creates the purchase order, and treasury or accounts payable releases funds. An ambulatory surgery center may compress those roles into an administrator and physician owner, while a veterinary practice often relies on an owner and equipment lender. Build a buyer map before negotiating price: legal purchasing entity, authorized signer, budget source, purchase-order threshold, delivery site, acceptance signer, and payment-release owner. Ask what happens if the current device remains unavailable for thirty days, then separate lost appointments from genuinely recoverable contribution. A scheduled procedure is not automatically collectible revenue. Verify whether the purchase replaces an existing asset, adds a service line, or consumes an already approved capital allocation. Request the procurement checklist and vendor-onboarding timetable before reserving stock. A signed quotation from a clinician without purchasing authority creates inventory exposure, not an enforceable treasury commitment. Record each decision maker's specific objection and required evidence in HubSpot or Salesforce, attached to the serial-number opportunity rather than a generic account note.

The first chapter-level operating play, replacement-capacity resale, monetizes speed without pretending every outage creates unlimited pricing power. Suppose an ultrasound outage displaces twelve appointments weekly, each yielding $180 of contribution after incremental clinical costs. Four weeks represent $8,640 of potential contribution exposure, but only if staffing, referrals, collections, and patient demand support those appointments. A refurbished unit priced at $25,000 can therefore compete on delivery certainty and service continuity rather than a dramatic discount against an irrelevant new-system list price. Obtain the buyer's probe, software, network, and room requirements before promising a replacement. Confirm whether existing service coverage transfers and whether the manufacturer will support the configuration. If compatibility fails, the fallback is a separately contracted rental, another documented configuration, or a canceled reservation under agreed terms; it is never an improvised clinical substitution. Aesthetic buyers may instead value predictable treatment capacity, while veterinary buyers may prioritize parts availability and technician access. Do not present human-device regulatory requirements as identical for veterinary-only equipment. In every segment, sell a supported operating outcome with measurable delivery conditions, not a vague promise that the purchase pays for itself.

The second operating play, documented refurbishment with milestone settlement, turns uncertain condition into evidence a budget holder can defend. For an illustrative $40,000 surgical-equipment sale, use a $4,000 reservation deposit, $32,000 released after specified bench tests and shipment authorization, and $4,000 following installation acceptance. These amounts total the equipment price; they are not three separate revenue streams. Before accepting the deposit, disclose included accessories, excluded consumables, software-license transfer conditions, warranty scope, and the identity of the servicing organization. The buyer pays for fewer surprises, traceable testing, and a bounded remedy if performance misses the agreed specification. Establish an acceptance period long enough to perform the tests, but do not allow an unrelated construction delay to suspend all payment indefinitely. For a financed purchase, obtain the lender's actual funding conditions: some lenders require delivery confirmation and a signed acceptance certificate before paying the seller. If those conditions leave the operator financing the entire shipment, require funded escrow or revise the delivery sequence. The fallback for unresolved performance is repair, replacement, or return under a priced remedy schedule, with transport responsibility and refund timing written before the device leaves the warehouse.

## 2. Choose the Settlement Mechanism Before Committing Inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A payment method and a release condition solve different problems. Wire and ACH move money; escrow governs custody and release; milestone holds allocate performance risk; factoring monetizes a receivable. Compare all eight arrangements in the matrix rather than treating them as interchangeable bank products. For a $25,000 diagnostic sale, an illustrative $5,000 deposit plus $20,000 before dispatch minimizes seller credit exposure but requires sufficient buyer confidence. A buyer requesting payment thirty days after installation is asking for unsecured credit unless collateral or a separate structure changes that exposure. Verify receipts through the receiving bank's authenticated channel, not a screenshot, emailed remittance, or shipping agent's assurance. Check the purchasing entity against the originating account and document legitimate differences, including lender-funded purchases. A wire is generally difficult to reverse once completed, but fraud recovery processes and legal claims still exist. ACH availability does not remove return or unauthorized-payment risk. Set release rules with the bank for the actual transaction type and risk profile. Do not copy consumer return periods into commercial-credit procedures or assume one waiting period eliminates every payment dispute. Treasury evidence must match the sale, amount, currency, and authorized payer.

Escrow works only when the agreement specifies what evidence causes money to move. Name the custody provider, verify its identity and applicable authority, identify fees, and define dispute handling before funding. For the $40,000 milestone example, a buyer might fund the full price into escrow, with releases of $4,000, $32,000, and $4,000 under the written sequence. The buyer's funding confirmation is distinct from the operator receiving released cash. Attach the serial schedule and objective tests so the provider does not need to judge whether a surgeon merely likes the device. Specify what happens if the buyer refuses inspection, the carrier damages the shipment, or a required software entitlement cannot transfer. A marketplace checkout through a provider such as Stripe or PayPal may offer convenient collection, but payout timing, reserves, prohibited categories, and dispute rights must fit the equipment transaction. Never bypass a marketplace's contractual fee rules by moving its introduced buyer off-platform. A reservation retainer also needs explicit earned, refundable, and creditable portions. Calling a payment nonrefundable does not itself resolve defective performance or conflicting contract provisions. Reconcile escrow balances separately from cash available to purchase the next unit.

Factoring becomes relevant only after examining whether a collectible, assignable receivable actually exists. On an illustrative accepted $100,000 invoice, an 80% advance provides $80,000 initially; a hypothetical $3,000 total fee leaves $17,000 released from the reserve after full customer payment. Net proceeds are $97,000, before other transaction costs, rather than $180,000 of revenue. Confirm recourse, dilution adjustments, customer notification, anti-assignment terms, and existing lender liens. Factor quotes can become uneconomic when installation disputes delay payment. A statutory disbursement is fundamentally different: a bankruptcy distribution follows court and plan mechanics, not the seller's preferred settlement schedule. Keep that rail confined to the claim-investing play described later. Operationally, require two-person verification for bank-detail changes, with a callback to a previously authenticated number. A new email signature or urgent closing deadline is insufficient evidence. Maintain an invoice-to-bank reconciliation showing deposit, remaining balance, taxes collected, freight charges, credits, and retained amounts. When a buyer demands an unexplained third-party refund, stop the refund workflow and investigate through established contacts. Do not turn an equipment transaction into a pass-through transfer for unrelated parties.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Authenticate the payer and authority
   - Match the contract entity, signer, purchase order, and funding source.
   - Verify lender involvement directly and record who can authorize escrow release or invoice payment.
2. Lock the acceptance and settlement schedule
   - Attach the serial schedule, test criteria, payment amounts, and release evidence.
   - Specify tax treatment, freight responsibility, inspection deadlines, cure rights, and refund routing.
3. Verify money before relinquishing control
   - Confirm bank receipt or authenticated escrow funding through independent channels.
   - Apply the agreed rail-specific release rule and obtain two-person review of changed banking details.
4. Close the evidence and cash loop
   - Reconcile delivery, acceptance, funds, fees, taxes, credits, and remaining holds.
   - Keep the complete packet under the equipment serial and pursue exceptions before extending additional credit.

## 3. Protect the Spread with Credit, Title, and Device Evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Counterparty screening should answer whether this entity can pay this invoice on these terms. Verify formation and standing through the relevant secretary of state, corroborate the operating address, obtain trade references independently, and examine a commercial credit report where available. For meaningful unsecured exposure, request recent statements, accounts-payable aging, existing lender restrictions, and the source of repayment. PACER litigation and bankruptcy searches can reveal context, but a matching name alone proves nothing; resolve entity identifiers. Establish a credit limit against aggregate exposure, including devices already shipped and unresolved acceptance holds. A buyer offering a large order while refusing basic identity verification deserves smaller exposure, not a larger discount. On the supply side, check ownership, lease status, and security interests before acquiring equipment. UCC search results require interpretation by debtor name, jurisdiction, and collateral description; possession is not proof of clean title. UCC section 9-315 generally preserves a security interest after disposition unless an applicable rule or authorization changes that result. Obtain authenticated payoff and release instructions when needed. A low purchase price cannot compensate for inventory that belongs to a lessor or remains encumbered.

Device evidence protects payment because condition disputes often become collection disputes. Index service history, serial photographs, recalls, maintenance intervals, accessories, and measured acceptance results in the inventory record. Distinguish servicing that restores intended performance from changes that can constitute remanufacturing; significant changes to safety, performance specifications, or intended use require device-specific regulatory analysis before marketing. Applicable manufacturer obligations can include registration, listing, and the Quality Management System Regulation in 21 CFR Part 820. Do not assume every reseller is a manufacturer, or that describing work as refurbishment settles the classification. For patient data, identify whether HIPAA applies to the parties and services, establish any required business-associate arrangement, and use an approved sanitization process that preserves lawful device operation. HHS requires covered entities and relevant business associates to address device and media controls under 45 CFR 164.310(d). A factory reset is not automatically verified data removal. Record the method, affected media, verification result, and technician. Obtain decontamination evidence suitable for the device and handling task; dirty equipment should not travel through ordinary receiving workflows while staff debate who was supposed to clean it.

Fee protection starts by deciding whether the operator is principal, broker, or service provider. The canonical model assumes equipment resale as principal: the operator buys inventory and recognizes gross resale proceeds, with acquisition and refurbishment embedded in margin. A disclosed broker earning $2,000 on another party's $40,000 sale has a different revenue basis and must not report the entire sale as its fee. For brokerage work, define protected introductions, transaction scope, fee trigger, payment timing, exclusions for existing relationships, and a reasonable duration in the signed engagement. Use dated introduction records and a closing statement rather than hiding seller identity until the last minute. For principal sales, protect the spread through enforceable price, substitution, acceptance, warranty, and default terms. Do not confuse a security-interest clause with completed perfection or priority. UCC section 2-401 generally limits retained title in delivered goods to a security interest, so credit sales need an Article 9 strategy where appropriate. Keep equipment sourcing compensation distinct from patient referrals. The federal Anti-Kickback Statute can reach remuneration tied to federally reimbursable business; a sourcing-fee label does not determine the substance of the arrangement.

**Six Evidence Controls That Protect Collectibility and Equipment Ownership**

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sales operations verifies purchasing identity and signing authority | Entity record, verified address, purchase order, and authorized signatory | An enthusiastic employee commits funds the organization never authorized | Require authorized execution before reserving or purchasing dedicated inventory |
| Credit owner reviews aggregate open customer exposure | Credit report, references, financial statements, and existing unpaid invoices | Multiple individually small shipments create an unmanageable unsecured balance | Reduce credit limit or require funded settlement before shipment |

| Deal owner documents compensation and protected commercial introductions | Executed engagement, dated introduction record, fee trigger, and closing statement | Counterparties close directly while disputing the intermediary's earned compensation | Use contractual collection remedies without obstructing legitimate device servicing |

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Releasing a Serial Number**
> 
> Keep equipment under operational control until the evidence supports the specific release decision.
> - Verify ownership and resolve identified leases or liens with authenticated release documentation.
> - Complete configuration testing, serviceability review, decontamination evidence, and applicable patient-data sanitization.
> - Match the buyer's authorized commitment to funded escrow, verified receipt, or an explicitly approved credit limit.
> - Confirm signed acceptance criteria, freight responsibility, warranty remedies, and independently authenticated payment instructions.

## 4. Separate Chapter 11 Claim Economics from Equipment Revenue  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The required adjacent play is chapter11_trade_claim_convenience_class_rollup: Chapter 11 Bankruptcy Schedule E/F Trade-Claim & Convenience-Class Rollups, TRACK_1. Equipment suppliers may hold unpaid trade receivables against a restructuring hospital or practice group and prefer immediate cash to uncertain distributions. The operator purchases a documented claim at a discount; the seller receives the purchase price, while the plan administrator later pays the recognized claim holder under applicable plan terms. Start with Schedule E/F, invoices, delivery records, credits, contracts, proofs of claim, and the claims register. A scheduled amount is a diligence lead, not proof of an allowed collectible asset. Under 11 U.S.C. 1111(a), certain scheduled claims are deemed filed, but disputed, contingent, or unliquidated scheduled claims fall outside that treatment. Evaluate bar dates, objections, setoff, recoupment, asserted priority, and possible claim-disallowance issues. Separate equipment ownership disputes from unsecured invoice claims. A retained security interest, lease, or reclamation position can change both rights and valuation. Never buy solely because a schedule shows a familiar equipment vendor and an apparently attractive face amount. Confirm who actually owns the receivable and whether it has already been assigned or factored.

Convenience-class economics depend on the actual plan, not a generic small-claim threshold. Section 1122(b) permits a separate class of small unsecured claims when the court approves the amount as reasonable and necessary for administrative convenience. Read eligibility, election mechanics, aggregation provisions, anti-splitting terms, distribution caps, and amendment history before bidding. Suppose a supported $10,000 claim has an illustrative 70% probability of receiving a $6,000 distribution and a 30% probability of zero recovery. Expected cash is $4,200. Paying $2,800 plus $400 of transfer and administration costs produces $1,000 of undiscounted expected surplus before funding costs and taxes; success produces $2,800, while zero recovery loses $3,200. Apply a timing discount and sensitivity analysis rather than presenting expected value as a promised payout. If supported general-unsecured recovery falls to 20% of face, the $2,000 receipt would lose $1,200 against that same cost basis. Do not manufacture convenience eligibility by splitting claims or assume buying several claims preserves each seller's treatment. Stop affected purchases, enforce documented seller recourse where available, and price only supported general-unsecured recoveries. Seller recourse is worth less when the seller cannot perform.

Transfer procedure controls whether the expected payment reaches the intended account. For qualifying transfers after a proof of claim is filed, Bankruptcy Rule 3001(e) provides filing and notice mechanics, including a transferor objection period generally measured at twenty-one days after mailed notice. Different transfer situations have different procedures; follow the rule, local requirements, and case orders rather than one universal form. Confirm the claims agent's register, distribution instructions, tax documentation, and recognized holder before projecting settlement timing. Keep claim purchases in a separate ledger and capital allocation. The volume scorecard remains exclusively equipment resale: Conservative revenue of $250,000 less $225,000 total costs yields $25,000; Base revenue of $1,025,000 less $861,000 yields $164,000; Scaled revenue of $1,800,000 less $1,404,000 yields $396,000. Those costs collectively include acquisition, refurbishment, and other expenses needed to reach the specified net margins. Claim face value, lender principal, escrow deposits, and expected distributions cannot inflate those equipment figures. With startup capital of $20,000 to $95,000 and monthly operating expense of $4,000 to $18,000, cash trapped in speculative claims competes directly with saleable inventory. Preserve capacity for the stated three-to-nine-month first-deal window before funding this adjacent strategy.
