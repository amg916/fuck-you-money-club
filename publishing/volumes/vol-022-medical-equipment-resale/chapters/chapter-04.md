## 1. Ring-fence cash before buying equipment or signing a lease  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Allocate startup capital by function, not by the apparent discount on a hospital auction lot. At $75,000, reserve $41,250 for working capital, $11,250 for initial inventory, $6,000 for bench qualification, $4,500 for premises and handling, $4,500 for formation and insurance, and $7,500 for systems and launch execution. At $200,000 and $400,000, apply the same 55/15/8/6/6/10 percentage split shown below. These are allocation ceilings, not instructions to spend every dollar immediately. The 55% reserve begins as unrestricted cash; customer deposits, available credit, and estimated inventory resale values do not satisfy it. Maintain separate ledger buckets for operating runway, transaction funding, and warranty liquidity inside that reserve. A purchase must leave enough cash to complete existing repairs, pay committed freight, and meet the next thirteen weeks of unavoidable payments. At the lean $5,000 monthly overhead level, $41,250 covers only 8.25 months before payroll or additional inventory purchases. Because the first deal can take three to nine months, the lowest capital tier requires deferred purchases, owner-funded living expenses, and a narrow product scope. It does not support a broad speculative showroom.

Map the three chapter plays to different cash exposures. Diagnostic buy-refurbish-resell requires acquisition cash before bench findings are complete, so start with models whose manuals, probes, batteries, and service support remain obtainable. Matched-buyer surgical/veterinary resale begins with a written buyer specification and an identified source; verify that the equipment's intended use and configuration fit that buyer rather than assuming human and veterinary applications are interchangeable. Aesthetic buy-to-order resale demands serial-specific confirmation of handpiece life, software transfer, consumable availability, and manufacturer support before committing funds. In all three modeled plays, the operator purchases and controls the equipment for resale; recognized revenue is the gross resale price when the applicable recognition conditions are met. A separate agent transaction may recognize only its fee and must not be mixed into these scorecard totals. Write maximum landed cost backward from the achievable selling price, subtracting repair allowance, freight, installation, warranty exposure, and required contribution. Establish a buyer-backed exit route for slow inventory before purchase. A cheap unsupported platform can consume more reserve than a properly documented unit purchased at a smaller headline discount, especially when a proprietary accessory blocks acceptance.

Keep the scorecard fixed while testing a transparent expense bridge. Conservative revenue of $250,000 less $100,000 acquisition/refurbishment, $20,000 transaction-variable expense, $45,000 cash payroll, and $60,000 fixed overhead produces $25,000 owner profit. Base revenue of $1,025,000 less $620,000 acquisition/refurbishment, $45,000 variable expense, $100,000 payroll, and $96,000 overhead produces $164,000. Scaled revenue of $1,800,000 less $1,000,000 acquisition/refurbishment, $70,000 variable expense, $154,000 payroll, and $180,000 overhead produces $396,000. These expense allocations are illustrative, not additional canonical scorecard inputs. Include contractor work in the relevant repair, transaction, or overhead bucket exactly once. The Conservative bridge assumes one founder works full time without a separate salary and the other full-time position fits the $45,000 burdened payroll allowance; local wages may invalidate that assumption. Owner distributions come from profit, not a duplicate expense deduction. Monthly fixed overhead is $5,000, $8,000, and $15,000 respectively; the $22,000 range ceiling is not mandatory spending. Paying two market-rate salaries or carrying expensive debt requires a revised cost structure, not an unsupported claim that canonical profit survives unchanged.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy evidence and liquidity before buying breadth**
> 
> Experienced resellers price the missing accessory, the failed acceptance test, and the delayed buyer before celebrating an auction discount.
> - Request the exact probe, handpiece, pedal, cable, and software-option list before bidding; compare photographed serials with the seller's inventory schedule.
> - Rent a specialist analyzer or purchase qualified contractor bench time before buying an instrument that only one occasional device family requires.
> - Ask the manufacturer or authorized service channel about support and transfer restrictions using the actual serial number, not merely the product-family name.
> - Negotiate a documented inspection window and freight-damage evidence procedure; define remedies before the unit leaves the seller's custody.

## 2. Install twelve lean layers without building twelve competing databases  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Deploy the twelve layers below in dependency order: identities, credentials, accounting, serial inventory, evidence storage, and then sales automation. Prices are explicit U.S. planning references rather than live quotations; verify current regional terms, included limits, tax, and renewal pricing before purchase. Free software still needs an accountable maintainer, patched hardware, and tested recovery. Two Google Workspace Business Starter seats at $7 each per month under annual commitment, two Bitwarden Teams seats at $4 each per month billed annually, and two Backblaze Computer Backup subscriptions at $99 per computer annually create a $38.50 monthly equivalent before storage and processing. Allow another $11.50 as a provisional storage and request-cost allowance, making a $50 software baseline rather than a complete infrastructure budget. Endpoint hardware, domain registration, external backup media, professional support, and cloud transfer charges remain separate. The four contractors receive restricted task access only when needed; any paid seats they require increase the budget explicitly. Do not share credentials to preserve a cheap tier. Turn on multifactor authentication, assign a recovery owner, and remove access when a repair, transport, or bookkeeping engagement ends.

Make Snipe-IT the serial and custody authority, Paperless-ngx the document index, and Wave the accounting ledger. Trello holds temporary work queues; HubSpot holds commercial relationships; neither becomes a competing equipment register. An Airtable intake base can normalize seller submissions, but accepted records transfer into the serial register and the staging copy is closed. The common key is an internal asset identifier linked to manufacturer, model, serial, acquisition invoice, source organization, location, condition, service events, and disposition. Never use a mutable sales nickname as the primary key. Index a repair report by both asset identifier and service date; preserve the original signed report instead of replacing it with an optimistic summary. Inventory tracking and service-history indexing are the two priority automation levers. A weekly export and reconciliation is adequate initially; custom integrations wait until a measured error rate or labor burden justifies them. Restrict cloud uploads to sanitized commercial and technical records. Patient images, worklists, names, and embedded identifiers remain outside this baseline stack. Free tiers and ordinary business subscriptions do not establish permission to process protected health information or supply the required contractual arrangements.

Spend physical infrastructure money on segregation and measurement, not cosmetic warehouse space. A shared industrial unit or qualified service partner can provide locked inbound quarantine, clean work surfaces, released-stock storage, and a protected packing zone. Confirm permitted use, electrical capacity, loading access, environmental limits, and insurance compatibility before signing. Diagnostic units may need manufacturer-specified performance checks beyond a basic electrical safety test. Surgical equipment can require accessory-specific function testing and documented cleaning boundaries; aesthetic systems can require laser-specific competence, controlled test conditions, and calibrated output measurement. Purchase a Fluke Biomedical ESA-series analyzer only when its functions match the chosen devices and utilization supports ownership; otherwise obtain qualified service capacity by written scope. No universal analyzer certifies every device safe or effective. For the two full-time operators, split commercial purchasing from operations and release coordination. Use four contractor relationships for biomedical service, specialty device service, insured freight/rigging, and bookkeeping. These are contracted capacities rather than four additional full-time salaries. Record turnaround commitments and backup suppliers so a single unavailable technician does not strand the entire month's selling inventory. Keep technical acceptance with a suitably qualified person, not a sales deadline.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish controlled identities
   - Create two named operator accounts, enable multifactor authentication, and document recovery ownership.
   - Grant contractors only the access required for their engagement; price any additional paid seats before enabling them.
2. Create the serial evidence spine
   - Register a sample asset and link its purchase record, service report, storage location, and release status.
   - Use sanitized demonstration documents and confirm that the original report remains retrievable after indexing.
3. Test accounting and recovery
   - Post a sample purchase, buyer deposit, sale, and refund, then restore an exported evidence packet.
   - Reconcile the sample ledger without recognizing the deposit as earned resale revenue before the transaction qualifies.
4. Rehearse a restricted contractor handoff
   - Assign a bench task, receive its signed report, revoke access, and verify the retained audit evidence.
   - Record elapsed time and unresolved dependencies before permitting the first live equipment purchase.

## 3. Separate entity, banking, insurance, and device obligations before money moves  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Form the operating entity, obtain its EIN directly from the IRS, and open dedicated operating and reserve accounts before accepting a buyer deposit. The bank must understand that the business buys and resells used medical equipment, including the intended device families and expected ticket sizes. Keep beneficial ownership and authorized signer information accurate under the bank's onboarding requirements. Collect state resale documentation and determine where sales tax registration, used-device dealer licensing, radiation-equipment registration, or other device-specific permissions apply. These requirements vary with activity and jurisdiction; attach the actual authority and determination to the launch file rather than borrowing another reseller's conclusion. Supplier payment instructions require independent callback verification using a previously validated contact. The purchasing operator prepares the payment, while the operations operator verifies payee, serial schedule, and terms before release. Obtain evidence of ownership and investigate liens when the seller or circumstances warrant it; a signed invoice alone does not extinguish a secured party's interest. Under state enactments of UCC Article 9, evaluate the specific collateral and applicable exceptions rather than assuming every ordinary-course purchase resolves title risk. Keep borrowed funds, equity contributions, deposits, and sales proceeds distinct in the ledger.

Ask an insurance broker for written confirmation that the proposed activity includes used medical equipment resale and the actual refurbishment, installation, storage, and shipping work. Review commercial general liability, products-completed operations, property or inland marine, cargo, cyber exposure, and workers' compensation where required. Read exclusions for medical devices, lasers, professional services, used products, and property in the operator's care. A certificate summarizes coverage; the policy wording and endorsements determine it. Require service and freight contractors to carry coverage appropriate to their work, but do not treat their certificates as substitutes for the reseller's policy. Maintain purchase and sale terms identifying the exact device, included accessories, condition, acceptance procedure, warranty scope, installation responsibility, and freight claim process. Align those promises with what technicians can actually document. Avoid selling a performance warranty based on a seller's unverified screenshot. Set aside warranty liquidity using observed failure frequency and expected claim severity as data accumulates; initially use conservative device-family estimates and track deviations. Exclude warranty reserves from spendable acquisition cash in the weekly forecast. A single uncovered output-related injury allegation can overwhelm the software savings that looked important during setup.

Classify each planned activity before choosing the technical scope. FDA's servicing and remanufacturing distinction turns on the work performed and its effect on safety, performance specifications, or intended use, not the label printed on the invoice. Replacing components to restore established specifications can differ materially from changing output, controls, software behavior, or intended use. For activities that make the operator a regulated manufacturer, determine the applicable registration, listing, quality-system, reporting, and market-authorization duties before proceeding. The Quality Management System Regulation under 21 CFR Part 820 became effective February 2, 2026; do not copy an obsolete checklist without reviewing current applicability. Pure resale does not automatically create every manufacturer duty, and veterinary-only equipment does not inherit every human-device requirement. Separately, obtain source decontamination records and define who can safely inspect equipment that arrives dirty. Devices with storage require an authorized data-handling workflow before resale. HIPAA obligations depend on the parties' roles, but a reseller receiving patient information must not assume ownership of hardware authorizes access to its contents. Prefer documented sanitization by the source or a qualified provider, then verify residual data and functional integrity without browsing patient records.

## 4. Rehearse one complete transaction before expanding the four-person pod  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run a tabletop transaction and a physical handling rehearsal before launching paid acquisition campaigns. Use a sanitized demonstration record or inexpensive training unit to test seller qualification, serial intake, custody transfer, bench scheduling, acceptance evidence, quotation, collection, shipment, and reconciliation. The commercial operator owns seller and buyer commitments; the operations operator owns inventory status and cash forecast accuracy. Assign the biomedical contractor documented baseline checks, the specialty contractor device-specific work, the freight contractor packing and handling, and the bookkeeper reconciliation. Never let a contractor's informal message replace a signed result with test conditions and acceptance limits. Mark inventory states explicitly as quarantined, awaiting service, under test, commercially available, reserved, dispatched, and closed. Availability requires technical release, not merely arrival at the warehouse. Record every exception with an owner and next action. Measure the rehearsal's calendar delays, not just hands-on minutes: waiting for a replacement handpiece or an insured carrier is what traps cash. If a required provider cannot commit to the turnaround assumed in the acquisition model, change the purchase price, delivery promise, or product scope before the first real deal. Do not hide the delay inside a generic contingency percentage.

Build the weekly control sheet around cash conversion and evidence completion. Report unrestricted reserve, thirteen-week minimum cash, landed cost by serial, days since purchase, service spend against authorization, buyer deposit liabilities, receivable aging, and warranty exposure. Track gross resale proceeds separately from gross contribution and owner profit. A simple throughput illustration is ten $25,000 sales for Conservative revenue, twenty-five $41,000 sales for Base revenue, and forty $45,000 sales for Scaled revenue; these ticket counts are planning examples, not additional scorecard commitments. Rising average ticket size can concentrate risk even when headcount stays small. One delayed $45,000 unit matters more than several minor subscription overruns. Escalate stock aging against the expected sale window for that device family, not a universal arbitrary deadline. Obtain revised market comparables, check support changes, and document whether to discount, repair, return, or part out only where lawful and operationally suitable. Never increase inventory carrying value merely because repair costs exceeded expectations. Review inventory accounting with the bookkeeper and accountant. Stop new purchases when the forecast shows insufficient cash to meet existing commitments, even if the next supplier offers an attractive bulk discount.

Treat readiness as four independently evidenced conditions: funded runway, controlled technical scope, recoverable records, and executable delivery. The first condition requires the opening 55% reserve plus a forecast showing how it will be consumed; maintaining that initial percentage forever is not the objective. The second requires device-family acceptance procedures, qualified service capacity, and documented regulatory determinations. The third requires restricted access, a successful restore, and complete sanitized serial evidence. The fourth requires an available carrier, appropriate insurance, collection terms, and a buyer acceptance process that matches the sales promise. Launch with one narrow device family and one primary play rather than activating all three simultaneously. Add the next play after completed transactions demonstrate actual repair costs, demand, and warranty behavior. The Scaled case changes the pod to three full-time operators plus five contractors, eight people total; add the third employee to the measured bottleneck and the fifth contractor to a distinct technical or logistics constraint. Recalculate payroll, supervision time, and floor capacity before hiring. Growth is justified when completed, collected transactions support the expense bridge, not when advertised inventory value or an impressive pipeline makes the operation appear larger.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight conditions with evidence, ownership, and a stop response**
> 
> The two full-time operators record these checks before the first acquisition; unresolved findings restrict activity rather than becoming undocumented assumptions.
> - Cash: verify 55% opening reserve in bank records and a thirteen-week forecast covering existing commitments; defer purchases if projected liquidity fails.
> - Technical scope: retain device-family test procedures, qualified contractor capacity, coverage confirmation, and the servicing applicability assessment before accepting inventory.
> - Records: restore a sanitized serial packet from backup, verify access restrictions, and confirm decontamination and media-sanitization evidence before technical release.
> - Delivery: rehearse payment verification, packing, freight insurance, buyer acceptance, and refund handling; correct broken handoffs before promising a shipment date.
