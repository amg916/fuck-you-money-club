## 1. Days 1–7: Contract the Pod and Reject Bad Jurisdiction Before Spending  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Launch Candidate #45 as a recovery service for an identified provider, not as a speculative portfolio of arbitration awards. The starting pod has one full-time owner, one part-time claims contractor, and one part-time clinical or coding contractor. The owner handles provider sales, contracting, cash controls, and final filing accountability; the claims contractor assembles remittances and maintains clocks; the clinical contractor validates coding and evidence within their qualifications. Use an operational map of twelve plays: P1 provider targeting, P2 engagement and authority, P3 protected intake, P4 ERA normalization, P5 jurisdiction screening, P6 deadline control, P7 open negotiation, P8 eligibility and batching, P9 offer evidence, P10 entity coordination and submission, P11 award-to-cash recovery, and P12 fee reconciliation and cohort management. These are this chapter's execution labels, not additional revenue streams. Assign one accountable person and one backup to each play. Start with one specialty and two payer cohorts so the team can understand recurring defects before adding volume. Reserve qualified external counsel for jurisdiction disputes and representation questions; do not assume a billing engagement authorizes every form of advocacy in every state. The first week's deliverable is an executable service boundary, a signed authority chain, and a screened pilot queue.

P2 and P3 begin with a signed provider agreement, a Business Associate Agreement where required, and documented subcontractor obligations under 45 CFR §§ 164.502(e) and 164.504(e). Configure a contracted, appropriately secured document environment before importing protected records; a Microsoft 365 subscription alone does not establish compliant operation. Require multifactor authentication, named accounts, minimum-necessary access, retention settings, and an incident contact. Take a twenty-claim discovery sample containing the original claim, service date, location, payer remittance, adjustment codes, initial-payment date, plan identification, and provider authorization. A CARC or RARC suggests a workflow branch; it does not establish federal eligibility. Under 42 U.S.C. § 300gg-111, determine whether the service and setting fall within federal surprise-billing protections, whether a specified state law or applicable All-Payer Model Agreement determines payment, and whether excluded coverage is involved. A self-funded ERISA plan can point toward federal routing, but ERISA status alone cannot complete the analysis. Record the source supporting each decision, including plan documents or payer confirmation when available. Do not file unresolved classifications merely to make the dashboard appear productive. Ground ambulance claims require separate treatment rather than automatic inclusion in this federal workflow. Keep patient cost-sharing adjustments outside the operator's recovery incentive.

Set the sprint's cash envelope before buying software or paying filing charges. Within the supplied $10,000–$45,000 startup range, choose an illustrative $18,000 funded reserve and cap launch cash outflow at $7,500 through Day 30. Allocate $2,000 to contracting and specialist review, $1,000 to secured tooling and setup, $3,000 to contractors, $1,000 to provider acquisition, and $500 to incidentals. Keep a separate, provider-funded case-cost deposit for authorized administrative and certified-entity charges; neither that deposit nor its replenishment is operator revenue. If the operator advances those charges, count the advance inside the cash ceiling instead of disguising it as a recoverable asset. Target $3,500 monthly recurring cash overhead after setup, within the canonical $1,800–$10,500 range. The remaining $10,500 then supports three months at that run rate before case advances, taxes, or owner draws. Maintain a separate loaded-cost view that prices owner labor even when the owner postpones cash compensation. Require written authorization before any case expenditure and freeze new commitments when projected unrestricted cash falls below eight weeks of recurring obligations. A first deal can take one to four months; a thirty-day sprint must therefore prove operating readiness without depending on an early arbitration remittance to meet payroll.

## 2. Days 8–14: Build Business-Day Clocks and Price Only Collectible Incremental Recovery  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build P6 as a dated state machine, not a spreadsheet column labeled due soon. Under 45 CFR § 149.510, the open-negotiation process generally must be initiated within thirty business days after receipt of the initial payment or notice of denial. The negotiation period itself runs thirty business days, beginning when the initiating party first sends the required notice. Federal IDR initiation ordinarily follows within four business days after that period ends. Entity selection has its own requirements; do not rename the four-day initiation window a universal entity-selection or batching deadline. Store the original event, receipt evidence, calendar version, computed deadline, responsible person, and exception basis. Test weekends, federal holidays, missing receipt dates, corrected records, and overlapping disputes. Published extensions or special circumstances belong in an explicit override record supported by the applicable official instruction. A thirty-calendar-day launch usually cannot complete negotiation and arbitration for newly opened claims. Older claims may be actionable sooner only when their inherited notices and dates withstand verification. Run a daily morning deadline report and an afternoon exception review. Any filing clock without a validated trigger stays outside automated submission, even if an estimated deadline appears comfortably distant. Dual-check every near-term deadline before the responsible person ends the workday.

P7 sends complete open-negotiation notices through the required channels and preserves transmission evidence, attachments, and payer replies. Prepare a factual settlement range using coding, service complexity, contracting history where relevant, and evidence allowed by the governing rules. The qualifying payment amount is an input to the statutory process, not proof that the initial payer transfer equals that amount. Maintain separate fields for disclosed QPA, initial allowed amount, actual initial payment, patient cost-sharing, and later incremental payer cash. Do not manufacture uplift by comparing different claim units or treating an amended remittance as an additional payment. P8 groups only disputes satisfying the applicable batching conditions; verify payer or plan identity, provider identity, services, and service dates against current requirements before submission. Maintain a ninety-calendar-day cooling-off register for the same initiating party, opposing party, and same or similar services following a determination. Track affected claims and the applicable post-period initiation window rather than assuming negotiation stops or every claim is lost. Regulatory text, court orders, and agency implementation can affect operating details. Save the official guidance version used for each submission and resolve conflicts before committing nonrecoverable charges. Never batch unrelated claims merely to lower the apparent fee per line.

Price P12 on collected incremental cash after the defined case-cost waterfall. For each claim, let I equal additional payer cash actually received above the documented initial payment, excluding baseline provider receipts and patient collections. Let A represent non-refundable administrative charges, E unrecovered certified-entity charges, and W authorized direct casework costs. The illustrative contingency basis is max(0, I minus A minus E minus W); the operator earns the contracted share of that basis. With $12,000 incremental cash, $1,200 combined unrecovered charges, $800 direct casework, and a 25% share, recognized operator fees equal $2,500 on a $10,000 net recovery basis. The provider retains $7,500 after those costs and that fee. Those illustrative charges are arithmetic inputs, not a statement of current CMS pricing. Separately expense the operator's own unreimbursed acquisition, labor, and overhead costs; do not deduct the same reimbursed casework twice. Apply this waterfall consistently in the contract, ledger, and invoice. An award with no payment creates no earned cash-based contingency under this launch model. Record a prevailing party's entity-fee refund only when received; reserve losing or ineligible-dismissal exposure without assuming recovery. Administrative charges remain non-refundable, and any differing entity treatment requires documented resolution rather than optimistic budgeting.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Establish the payment baseline
   - Match the original 835 and bank settlement to the disputed service.
   - Preserve initial payer cash, disclosed QPA, allowed amount, and patient responsibility as separate values.
2. Validate jurisdiction and time
   - Complete the federal eligibility worksheet and independently calculate business-day deadlines.
   - Record the evidence source, reviewer, governing guidance version, and any supported exception before sending a notice.
3. Authorize the economic exposure
   - Approve the case-cost budget and document downside collection assumptions.
   - Include non-refundable administrative charges, unrecovered entity charges, direct casework, and available funding before initiating IDR.
4. Recognize only reconciled fees
   - Match incremental payer cash and received refunds before issuing the operator invoice.
   - Apply the contracted cost waterfall, reconcile provider proceeds, and expense operator costs without duplicating reimbursed case charges.

## 3. Days 15–21: Assemble Defensible Offers and Engineer Award-to-Cash Recovery  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

P9 converts a clean claim into a concise evidentiary packet rather than a stack of medical records. The clinical contractor identifies relevant acuity, complexity, training, experience, and service-specific facts; the owner checks whether each item is permissible and supported. Address the QPA and credible additional information without presenting billed charges, usual-and-customary charges, or public-payer reimbursement rates as prohibited substitute benchmarks. FAIR Health material is not automatically usable: inspect the underlying measure and exclude content that would introduce a barred factor. Distinguish a licensed analytical dataset from evidence the decision-maker may lawfully consider. Use a brief template with the claim identity, disputed amount, offer, relevant facts, supporting exhibits, and an exhibit index. The process selects between offers; an aggressive number is not valuable when its supporting theory is defective. Require the clinical contractor to sign factual assertions and the owner to sign the amount and filing checklist. Generative drafting may summarize authorized records only within an approved protected environment, and every quotation and citation must be checked against its source. Remove irrelevant patient details rather than expanding disclosure to make the packet look thorough. One reusable, verified specialty template is more useful than ten unsupported arguments copied across claims.

P10 treats certified-entity coordination as a separate production lane with its own selection, conflict, offer, payment, and submission events. Verify the entity's current certification, fee schedule, required forms, and procedural instructions through official sources and the entity's published terms. Record administrative charges and each party's entity charges separately; provider deposits are liabilities or pass-through funds according to the actual arrangement, not sales. Do not budget an entity refund merely because the brief appears strong. For operating reserves, treat losing and ineligible-dismissal charges as unrecoverable unless a documented actual refund changes the ledger. Have the claims contractor stage documents, the clinical contractor validate exhibits, and the owner execute the final submission. Capture the portal confirmation and exact uploaded versions immediately. A portal draft is not a completed filing, and a payment receipt alone does not prove the dispute was accepted. Stop duplicate submissions using a provider, payer, service-date, and claim-line key. If no pilot claim has lawfully reached initiation by Day 21, complete a dry run using de-identified training records without sending a premature dispute. The sprint measures readiness and sound judgment, not the number of portal transactions created. Preserve every correction and its reason in the case history.

P11 starts before an award exists. Collect the payer's remittance contact, provider payment destination, ERA delivery route, and escalation owner during onboarding. Under 42 U.S.C. § 300gg-111(c)(6), the determined payment is generally due within thirty calendar days after the determination. Calendar that obligation separately from business-day negotiation and initiation events. Match subsequent deposits to claim lines, subtract the original payment only once, and identify recoupments, reversals, duplicate deposits, and payments applied to other balances. Send a documented payment inquiry when an expected remittance is missing; use the applicable CMS complaint route or other appropriate escalation when warranted. A favorable determination is an award receivable, not collected operator revenue. Maintain separate totals for submitted offers, awarded incremental amounts, due incremental amounts, collected incremental cash, recovered entity charges, and earned contingencies. Calculate collection performance only on awards whose payment deadlines have passed, and retain the complete denominator, including unpaid outcomes. For settled claims, use the actual settlement payment terms instead of inventing an IDR determination date. Review aging every Friday and assign a named person to each overdue balance. Measure how much contractor time collection consumes; a strong award result can still produce weak economics when the payer repeatedly requires expensive follow-up before releasing cash.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Tactics That Protect Recovery Without Inflating Results**
> 
> Experienced operators optimize eligible cash recovery, not offer size or portal activity.
> - Keep disclosed QPA and actual initial payer payment in separate ledger fields; a copied QPA baseline can silently distort the contingency invoice.
> - Create the cooling-off record when the determination arrives, then attach affected later claims and their applicable post-period initiation deadlines.
> - Require the actual submitted packet and portal receipt in one versioned evidence folder; reconstructed packets cannot prove what the entity received.
> - Track entity-fee refunds as separate receivables and recognize received refunds in the cost waterfall without counting them as payer uplift.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Days 60/90: Kill Broken Cohorts, Fix Measured Defects, Scale Collected Contribution  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use Days 22–30 to turn the pilot into a measured operating cadence. The owner reviews acquisition conversion and cash; the claims contractor owns exceptions and aging; the clinical contractor audits evidence quality. Target fifty screened claims by Day 30, at least twenty fully documented eligible candidates, one executed provider engagement, and ten properly initiated negotiations when their clocks permit. These are illustrative launch thresholds, not assumptions embedded in the canonical annual revenue cases. An eligible candidate needs documented jurisdiction, a supportable payment baseline, an intact deadline, provider authority, and positive downside economics. Report missing source records separately from rejected jurisdiction so poor intake does not look like a bad market. Set pilot work-in-progress limits at forty open negotiations and ten active submissions for the three-person pod, then revise only after time records show sustainable capacity. Alert at 80% of either limit and stop additions at the limit until work clears. Sample ten files weekly for eligibility and clock integrity, or inspect all files when fewer exist. The target is zero missed statutory deadlines and zero ineligible filings. One material error freezes new submissions in the affected workflow while the owner contains exposure and verifies the correction across every similarly situated claim.

Apply Kill, Fix, and Scale to a defined cohort or process, not indiscriminately to the whole business. At Day 30, Kill a target cohort if fewer than ten of fifty screened claims are documented federal candidates or downside contribution is nonpositive; retain records and meet existing obligations. Fix if ten to nineteen qualify, if required-record completeness is below 95%, or if any deadline test fails. Scale only intake, by at most 25%, when at least twenty qualify, completeness reaches 95%, tests pass completely, and eight weeks of runway remain. At Day 60, require ten mature outcomes before drawing a collection conclusion. A mature outcome is a paid settlement or a settlement or award whose payment deadline has passed; unpaid items remain in the sample. Measure cash realization as incremental cash received divided by incremental amounts due for those outcomes. Below 50% realization or nonpositive cohort contribution means Kill new commitments; 50% to below 80% means Fix collections; 80% or better supports limited expansion if contribution is positive and controls remain intact. Insufficient maturity means Fix or hold, not failure inferred from zero early receipts. Never replace missing collection evidence with prevailing-offer statistics.

At Day 90, require twenty mature outcomes, at least 85% cash realization, no statutory deadline misses, at least eight weeks of runway, and positive contribution after loaded casework and acquisition costs before expanding active capacity. Also require a trailing thirty-day operator net margin of at least 30% on collected earned fees, with costs consistently assigned and zero revenue treated as insufficient evidence. Kill the affected acquisition channel when contribution remains nonpositive after one documented remediation cycle; Fix positive but subthreshold performance. Reconcile planning to Formula v2.0 without treating a short pilot as an annual forecast. Conservative operator revenue of $110,000 less $77,000 costs produces $33,000 profit at 30%. Base revenue of $505,000 less $287,850 costs produces $217,150 at 43.0%. Scaled revenue of $900,000 less $396,000 costs produces $504,000 at 56%. Both first cases use 1 FT + 2 C; Scaled requires 2 FT + 3 C, not the unchanged launch pod. At an illustrative 25% contingency, those revenues require $440,000, $2,020,000, and $3,600,000 of net collected recovery bases respectively. They are not provider gross billings, total awards, or baseline collections. Add the second full-time person and third contractor only against measured workload, funded payroll, and sustained cash contribution.
