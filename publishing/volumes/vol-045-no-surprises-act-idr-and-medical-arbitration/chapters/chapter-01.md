## 1. Define the recovery desk before acquiring a single claim  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

This business sells disciplined recovery work to physician groups and other eligible providers, not arbitration outcomes to patients. The provider already delivered care and received an initial commercial-plan payment or denial. An authorized recovery desk identifies disputes potentially covered by the No Surprises Act, establishes the correct payment-resolution route, conducts open negotiation, and supports federal independent dispute resolution when negotiation fails. Federal IDR generally uses final-offer selection: the certified entity chooses one party's offer rather than inventing a midpoint. The desk earns an agreed percentage of additional payer cash actually collected after contractual deductions. Baseline provider collections, patient cost sharing, submitted charges, and unpaid awards are outside operator revenue. The useful production unit is therefore an eligible, timely, evidence-supported dispute with positive expected net recovery, not an uploaded claim. Start with one provider group and one service family whose encounter records, remittance files, and payer correspondence can be reconciled. Require written representative authority and a HIPAA business associate agreement before receiving protected health information. Use counsel where representation, contract interpretation, or state practice rules require legal work. A nonlawyer workflow vendor should not silently expand its engagement into unrestricted legal representation.

Jurisdiction is the first economic filter. Emergency services, qualifying nonemergency services from nonparticipating providers at participating facilities, and eligible air-ambulance services have different coverage hooks and factual requirements. Ground ambulance is not simply another federal IDR specialty. Medicare and Medicaid payment disputes do not become federal commercial IDR inventory because an ERA shows an adjustment. Determine the service date, facility participation, provider participation, plan identity, funding arrangement, service category, and whether a specified state law or All-Payer Model Agreement determines the payment amount. ERISA status is relevant but does not independently settle that analysis; state opt-in arrangements and the particular parties also matter. CARC and RARC codes are routing clues, not jurisdiction decisions. An 835 parser can flag a possible underpayment, but the supporting 837, explanation of benefits, plan documents, and clinical record establish what happened. Preserve the original payer payment and separately record the qualifying payment amount, or QPA. The QPA is a statutory benchmark, not necessarily the initial payment and not an automatic entitlement to a particular award. Reject attractive-looking balances when eligibility cannot be established before the applicable deadline. That rejection saves filing expense and protects scarce reviewer capacity.

The chapter maps twelve operating plays: Federal Open-Negotiation and Certified-IDR Recovery; Comparison-Intent Acquisition; Emergency Physician Recovery; Anesthesia Event Reconciliation; Radiology Component Recovery; Neonatal Acuity Evidence; Hospitalist Encounter Recovery; Assistant-Surgeon Documentation; Air-Ambulance Eligibility; State-Route Triage; Cooling-Period Cohort Control; and Post-Award Cash Reconciliation. The first two correspond to the supplied TRACK_1 and TRACK_2 identifiers; the remaining ten are chapter-local execution plays, not additional scorecard candidates. They share a collection ledger but require different eligibility evidence and failure responses. Build five service archetypes rather than presenting every specialty as interchangeable: an integrated recovery desk, a clinical evidence boutique, a jurisdiction-and-deadline service, a post-award collection desk, and a comparison-led acquisition channel. Only the integrated desk necessarily owns the entire contingency engagement. Other archetypes may support it under written subcontracts without multiplying the provider's fee burden. A defensible moat is a repeatable match between a narrow claim inventory and reliable execution, not a claim that automation eliminates professional judgment. Store acceptance criteria, service limitations, and subcontractor responsibilities in the engagement record. Refuse assignments that require unauthorized access, unsupported factual assertions, or patient collection practices inconsistent with applicable protections.

## 2. Recognize earned fees, not provider balances or paper awards  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use a recovery waterfall that an accountant and a physician owner can independently reproduce. Let C equal cumulative payer cash received for the covered dispute and P0 equal the initial payer payment already received. Incremental cash starts at C minus P0, never at submitted charges minus the QPA. If the engagement expressly establishes a higher contractual QPA floor, use the greater of that floor and P0; otherwise do not manufacture an additional threshold. Deduct nonrefundable administrative fees, certified-entity fees that remain unrecovered, and agreed direct casework disbursements before applying the contingency percentage. For example, total payer cash of $5,000 less a $1,000 initial payment, $200 in unrecovered process fees, and $600 in direct external casework leaves $3,200. At 25%, operator fee revenue is $800 and the provider retains $2,400 of that distributable increment. A separately disclosed $1,200 QPA does not change this example unless the contract makes it the floor; that alternative produces a $750 fee. These process-cost amounts illustrate arithmetic, not a current federal fee schedule. Refunds and later payer recoupments require corresponding waterfall adjustments under the engagement's reconciliation terms.

The canonical revenue basis is the operator's earned contingency share, not provider gross collections. Under an ASC 606 analysis, identify the actual customer, promised recovery service, performance obligations, variable consideration, and principal-versus-agent treatment. Receipt of provider money in a clearing account does not make the whole receipt operator revenue. Design the engagement so the contingent service fee becomes earned on qualifying incremental collection, subject to contractual reconciliation; have the accountant document the resulting recognition policy rather than assuming all cash-basis tax rules determine book revenue. Do not record an unpaid determination as earned contingency revenue merely because the offer prevailed. In the canonical scenarios, retained_share is already embodied in earned contingency revenue; applying the contingency percentage again would understate the business. Keep provider-funded external disbursements separate from operator payroll, evidence supervision, software, insurance, and acquisition expense. Direct external costs deducted before the split must not also appear as unreimbursed operator expense. Internal casework costs that the operator actually bears remain expenses when calculating owner profit. This separation prevents both overstated revenue and double-counted costs. Maintain a claim-level audit trail from payer deposit through deductions, fee invoice, remittance to the provider, and any subsequent correction.

Reconcile every annual plan to Formula v2.0 rather than substituting a dramatic award multiple. At an illustrative 25% blended contingency, Conservative requires $440,000 of distributable incremental collections to produce $110,000 of earned revenue; $77,000 of operator expense leaves $33,000 profit. Base requires $2,020,000 to produce $505,000 revenue; $287,850 expense leaves $217,150 profit. Scaled requires $3,600,000 to produce $900,000 revenue; $396,000 expense leaves $504,000 profit. Those collection bases are after the waterfall deductions, and they are not medical practice revenue forecasts. A $2,000 average distributable recovery would require 220, 1,010, and 1,800 collected units respectively; a unit is a financial modeling convention, not permission to batch claims. Teams remain one full-time operator plus two contractors for Conservative and Base, then two full-time staff plus three contractors for Scaled. Startup capital is $10,000–$45,000, with a planning range of $1,800–$10,500 monthly operating overhead and one to four months to a first engagement. That overhead range does not replace the full annual expense budgets, which include variable delivery costs. Reserve working capital for process fees and collection delays before expanding intake.

## 4. Build the deadline, evidence, and collection machine before scaling  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Implement a state machine with separate dates for payment or denial receipt, open-negotiation initiation, negotiation completion, IDR initiation, entity selection, offer submission, determination, and payment follow-up. Generally, a party initiates open negotiation within thirty business days after receiving the initial payment or denial, the negotiation period lasts thirty business days, and federal IDR initiation follows within four business days after that period ends. The four-day window is not a generic deadline for entity selection or a blanket batching permission. Use the applicable business-day definition and preserve evidence of receipt and delivery; a practice-management posting date can differ from the operative receipt date. Version the calendar rules against current agency instructions, applicable court orders, and available extensions rather than hard-coding assumptions from an old presentation. Require a human reviewer to verify the destination, parties, jurisdiction, notice contents, and deadline before submission. Python's datetime and zoneinfo can support timestamp handling, while an explicit holiday calendar controls business-day calculations. A daily exception report should surface missing receipt evidence, unresolved payer identity, and approaching expirations. Automation that confidently counts from the wrong event destroys value faster than a slower but documented manual calendar.

Batching and the cooling period deserve independent controls. Apply the then-current federal criteria to the actual parties, services, codes, and service dates; shared specialty or similar reimbursement alone does not authorize a batch. Following a determination, the initiating party generally faces a ninety-calendar-day restriction on initiating another dispute involving the same other party and the same or similar items or services. The rules provide a subsequent submission mechanism for affected disputes, including a thirty-business-day period after the restriction ends; confirm applicability and prerequisites for each cohort instead of treating it as a universal deadline extension. Track each certified entity's published fee and the governing administrative fee schedule at commitment time. Administrative fees are generally nonrefundable. Entity-fee refunds and allocations depend on prevailing status and applicable settlement or dismissal rules; never budget an ineligible or losing dispute as though a refund were assured. After a determination, track the applicable payment deadline, generally thirty calendar days, and reconcile actual deposits. Escalate delayed payment through documented payer follow-up, appropriate agency complaint channels, and counsel where enforcement requires legal action. An aging award is a collection problem, not fresh earned fee revenue.

The small-team advantage depends on separating machine work from accountable judgment. Parse 835 segments, normalize CARC and RARC codes, identify candidate plan types, assemble timelines, and draft evidence indexes automatically. Treat the ERISA classifier as a triage aid whose outputs retain source evidence and confidence, not as a final jurisdiction engine. Have a trained reviewer decide legal routing and a qualified clinical reviewer validate medical assertions. Under 45 CFR § 164.504(e), the business associate agreement must address permitted uses, safeguards, subcontractors, and required reporting. Implement role-based access, minimum-necessary data handling, audit logs, secure transmission, retention controls, and incident procedures appropriate to the records and engagement. Evaluate every external evidence or language processing service before transmitting protected information; a convenient upload button does not establish permitted use. Report a weekly funnel of screened, eligible, negotiated, filed, decided, collected, reversed, and invoiced units, with dollars at each stage. Compare collected fee contribution against actual staff time and acquisition cost by provider cohort. Expand only when another trained reviewer can reproduce the decisions from the retained file. The moat is transferable operating discipline, not dependence on one person's memory.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create an authenticated intake packet
   - Receive authorized 835 and 837 records, payer notices, plan evidence, and clinical documentation.
   - Record source ownership, receipt timestamps, initial payment, separately disclosed QPA, service dates, and provider identifiers before making eligibility assertions.
2. Resolve jurisdiction and calendar the negotiation
   - Complete the routing analysis and initiate timely open negotiation when the federal pathway applies.
   - Retain delivery evidence, calculate the full negotiation period, and assign a named reviewer to the subsequent four-business-day initiation window.
3. Submit supported offers through the correct process
   - Confirm current batching rules, entity requirements, permitted evidence, and positive expected recovery.
   - Separate submissions only when still timely and permitted; decline negative-value filings and preserve available state remedies or permissible negotiation.
4. Close the collection and fee ledger
   - Match actual payer deposits, apply the recovery waterfall, and invoice only the earned contingency.
   - Track unpaid determinations separately, reconcile returned entity fees, and true up provider distributions and operator fees after any reversal or recoupment.
