## 1. Reject jurisdiction defects before they become paid filings  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is treating every out-of-network short payment as federal IDR inventory. Coverage, service category, date, location, and the applicable payment-setting law determine the route; a payer logo does not. The second is equating ERISA status with automatic federal eligibility. Distinguish self-funded plans, fully insured coverage, applicable specified state law, and relevant state opt-in arrangements before assigning a filing queue. Capture the plan sponsor, administrator, funding evidence, service location, emergency status, facility network status, and any notice-and-consent documentation. Ground-ambulance claims generally sit outside these federal surprise-billing protections; air-ambulance disputes require their own analysis. A denial involving coverage or medical necessity may require an appeal rather than a payment-amount arbitration. Chapter-local play P01, jurisdiction proof, buys a second check before money leaves the account. If screening costs $20 per claim, screening 100 claims costs $2,000; preventing four defective filings with $750 of otherwise stranded fees and work avoids $3,000, leaving $1,000 of illustrative protection. The fallback is a documented state-law, contractual, or benefit-appeal route, never relabeling the claim to fit federal eligibility. Retain the source document behind every routing decision, including the effective date of the rule used.

The third trap is confusing the initial filing clock with the negotiation period. Ordinarily, open negotiation must be initiated within 30 business days after receipt of the initial payment or denial; the negotiation period itself lasts 30 business days, followed by a four-business-day federal IDR initiation window. Extensions and special circumstances require evidence, not a salesperson's promise. Chapter-local play P02, deadline provenance, stores the actual receipt event, negotiation delivery evidence, applicable holiday calendar, and calculated last permissible action. Set an internal initiation target two business days before the legal deadline and require a second reviewer whenever receipt dates disagree. An 835 transaction date is not automatically proof of when the provider received the payment or denial. Chapter-local play P03, authority validation, links each submission to the correct provider entity, NPI, tax identifier, and written representative authority. A shared spreadsheet that silently changes time zones can destroy an otherwise strong recovery. For 50 cases carrying $400 of expected operator contribution each, one missed batch risks $20,000; a $300 monthly calendar and review budget is inexpensive by comparison. When authority or timing remains unresolved, stop new submissions for that cohort and preserve the available correction or extension evidence.

The fourth trap is batching for convenience instead of legal eligibility. Chapter-local play P04, batch integrity, groups only disputes satisfying the applicable version of the batching requirements and preserves a manifest of every included item. Do not assume that a shared payer brand, specialty, or spreadsheet tab establishes the required party and service relationships. Validate the service window, provider identity, plan or issuer identity, and permitted item or service grouping against current controlling rules and CMS instructions. Chapter-local play P05, cooling-period separation, tracks the 90-calendar-day restriction after a determination for the relevant parties and same or similar items or services. Preserve affected claims for the applicable post-period initiation pathway; the rule provides a subsequent 30-business-day window for qualifying suspended disputes, not permission to ignore their records. Litigation and implementation changes make a versioned rule register essential. At an illustrative $600 entity-fee exposure per defective filing, five bad submissions consume $3,000 before staff time. The fallback is a smaller eligible batch or individual filing supported by the effective rules, not a manipulated code. Keep the original coding intact and send genuine coding errors back through the provider's authorized correction process.

## 2. Reconcile earned fees to collected cash, not arbitration headlines  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The fifth trap is booking the selected offer as collected revenue. Chapter-local play P06, baseline reconciliation, distinguishes the payer's disclosed QPA from its actual initial allowed amount and payment. They are not interchangeable fields. Define the baseline using the signed engagement and claim-level remittance record, then identify only additional payer cash actually received and matched to that claim. Patient cost-sharing, the original provider collection, and outstanding awards do not become operator revenue. Chapter-local play P07, fee-adjusted settlement, applies the contracted contingency percentage only after unrecovered administrative fees, entity fees, and agreed casework costs. Consider an illustrative $12,000 initial payer payment followed by $8,000 of additional deposited recovery. Assume a $125 administrative charge, a $600 entity deposit subsequently refunded, and $600 of casework costs. These are modeling inputs, not representations of current published charges. After the refund clears, the distributable recovery base is $8,000 minus $125 minus $600, or $7,275. A 30% contingency earns $2,182.50. Before that refund clears, retain the $600 reserve and limit the provisional fee base to $6,675. The remaining $600 is neither earned fee revenue nor spendable working capital merely because the operator expects to prevail.

The sixth trap is assuming all unsuccessful or dismissed cases have identical refund treatment. Budget the CMS administrative fee as nonrefundable and reserve the certified entity fee through actual reconciliation. The prevailing party's entity-fee refund follows the applicable process; losing cases expose that fee to forfeiture. For ineligibility dismissals, conservatively reserve the full charge unless the effective instructions and entity policy establish a return, and recognize recovery only when received. Chapter-local play P08, reversal reserve, also covers payer recoupments, duplicate payments, corrected remittances, and provider refunds. Never distribute against an unmatched EFT. In the example, a failed claim with no incremental recovery can strand $125 plus $600 plus $600, or $1,325. The contract must identify who bears that loss; concealing it inside the next provider's settlement creates both trust and accounting problems. At cohort level, 100 initiated disputes with 75 favorable determinations but only 60 paid recoveries of $2,000 generate $120,000 of incremental cash, not $150,000. If unrecovered fees total $20,000 and casework costs total $10,000, the contingency base is $90,000 and a 30% operator share is $27,000. The other fifteen favorable outcomes remain collection work, not another $9,000 of earned fees.

The seventh trap is double counting revenue while hiding the expense burden. Formula v2.0 fixes Conservative operator revenue at $110,000, expenses at $77,000, and owner net profit at $33,000, a 30% margin with 1 FT + 2 C. Base uses $505,000, $287,850, and $217,150, respectively, a 43.0% margin with 1 FT + 2 C. Scaled uses $900,000, $396,000, and $504,000, a 56% margin with 2 FT + 3 C. These revenues are earned contingency shares, not underlying provider collections or award face values. The model treats the operator's retained earned share as revenue; document the ASC 606 principal-versus-agent assessment rather than automatically grossing up money handled for providers. Expenses already deducted in the provider settlement must not be deducted again as unreimbursed operator costs. Conversely, costs the operator actually absorbs remain expenses. At an illustrative uniform 30% contingency, the three revenue targets require approximately $366,667, $1,683,333, and $3,000,000 of fee-adjusted collected recovery bases. Those bases are operating requirements, not additional revenue scenarios. A monthly close should bridge bank deposits, provider liabilities, refunds, retained fees, overhead, and owner profit, with every unresolved difference assigned to a person and a dated resolution task.

## 3. Detect operational decay before access restrictions or recoupments spread  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The eighth trap is replacing evidence with a persuasive template. Chapter-local play P09, offer integrity, requires a sourced explanation of the proposed payment and a documented review of relevant permitted factors. QPA is an input, not a license to invent clinical acuity or a universal multiplier that wins every dispute. Licensed FAIR Health material can inform research where permitted, but identify the statistic, source rights, population, and relevance; an impermissible charge benchmark does not become usable because a vendor sold it. The federal framework excludes certain considerations, including usual and customary charges, billed charges, and public-payer reimbursement rates. Respect the effective rules and court decisions governing how permissible information is weighed. A clinician validates acuity statements against the actual encounter; the operator never changes medical documentation to improve the offer. For 40 briefs requiring 30 minutes of expert review at $120 per hour, quality assurance costs $2,400. Avoiding four $750 defective filings already offsets that amount. The fallback for weak evidence is a narrower supportable offer or negotiated settlement. Copying another provider's outcome, fabricating a comparator, or deleting unfavorable records can turn an ordinary collection dispute into contractual exposure, referral risk, and a loss of portal access.

The ninth trap is treating patient information as harmless sales material. Chapter-local play P10, access containment, begins with a HIPAA business associate agreement when the relationship requires one, approved subcontractor arrangements, role-based access, and audit retention. An 835 ERA parser should extract CARC and RARC signals into a controlled queue, not send raw patient records to a personal mailbox or an unapproved public AI service. Apply minimum-necessary practices where required and follow the applicable Security Rule safeguards for electronic protected health information. Portal accounts must identify authorized people; do not share credentials, rotate fictitious identities, or evade rate limits after access is restricted. Use supported interfaces and permitted automation, with retries bounded by documented service behavior. Suspend the affected integration on the first unexplained access denial or material authorization mismatch; preserve the event and contact the appropriate administrator. For a five-person operation, one shared login creates five indistinguishable sources of error, while named accounts make investigation and revocation practical. The fallback is a controlled manual workflow using authorized access, not an alternate account designed to bypass enforcement. Report suspected incidents through the established response process, assess notification duties, and prevent the same credentials from reaching unaffected provider workspaces.

The tenth trap is scaling intake while collection quality decays. Chapter-local play P11, cohort circuit breakers, measures eligibility dismissals, deadline misses, unpaid determinations, refund aging, and reversals by provider, payer, service category, and filing month. The thresholds here are internal triggers, not statutory tolerances. A single missed statutory deadline freezes new work in the affected queue until the clock defect is corrected. More than 2% eligibility dismissals across the latest 50 resolved filings triggers full re-screening; fewer than 50 observations require review of every dismissal rather than a reassuring percentage. Payment is generally due within 30 calendar days after determination under the federal framework; track the specific obligation and escalate overdue balances through documented payer contacts and appropriate complaint channels. If more than 20% of favorable determined dollars remain unpaid beyond 45 days, stop increasing that payer's exposure. If reversals exceed 3% of trailing 90-day incremental cash, recalculate the reserve before distributing more fees. These are conservative management choices, not evidence of industry averages. A $10,000 reserve funding $1,000 weekly net cash burn lasts ten weeks; favorable outcome statistics cannot extend that runway. The fallback is fewer paid filings, tighter evidence review, and active collections, not pressure on patients to fund the shortfall.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the affected cohort
   - Stop additional paid submissions and discretionary distributions for the identified defect.
   - Keep unrelated validated work moving. Name the incident owner, impacted provider and payer cohort, available cash, next deadlines, and the exact reason for intervention.
2. Preserve and reconstruct evidence
   - Rebuild the claim history from source records rather than dashboard status labels.
   - Collect receipt evidence, delivered notices, authority documents, fee receipts, determination records, remittances, bank matches, and access logs without altering original records.
3. Reprice exposure and select the remedy
   - Calculate stranded charges, reversible operator fees, outstanding provider liabilities, and runway.
   - Use the documented correction, collection, appeal, complaint, or incident-response route. Do not conceal the defect through duplicate filings or substituted identities.
4. Resume only the corrected workflow
   - Test the repaired rule against the affected cohort and independently review results.
   - Require supported dates, valid authority, balanced cash records, and resolved access issues. Keep enhanced monitoring until the next cohort confirms that the defect has stopped recurring.

## 4. Keep adjacent benefits revenue from contaminating the recovery engine  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The eleventh trap is selling a benefits restructuring project as a guaranteed cure for disputed provider payments. The supplied mapped play, erisa_level_funded_transparent_pbm_bor_arbitrage, belongs to TRACK_1 and is the twelfth play in this chapter's map, alongside local controls P01 through P11. Use public Form 5500 Schedule A and Schedule C information, where filed and informative, to identify renewal timing and compensation-review opportunities. These filings have reporting limits and lags; they do not establish current claim jurisdiction, hidden misconduct, or permission to contact individual patients. Obtain employer authorization for a CAA 2021 service-provider compensation-disclosure review under ERISA section 408(b)(2)(B). A properly licensed advisory team may seek a broker-of-record appointment and disclose direct and indirect compensation. At $40 PEPM for 250 covered employees, the advisory fee is $10,000 monthly and $120,000 annually; at $65 PEPM it is $16,250 monthly and $195,000 annually. Define the billing population explicitly so employee and member counts cannot be interchanged. These are separate illustrative advisory receipts, not additions to the canonical recovery scenarios. If licensing, scope, or compensation transparency is incomplete, narrow the assignment to authorized disclosed work with qualified partners rather than collecting brokerage compensation through an unrelated IDR invoice.

The twelfth trap is assuming unbundling necessarily lowers employer cost. Compare the existing bundled arrangement with separately contracted TPA administration, stop-loss coverage, suitable captive participation, and a transparent pass-through PBM only after defining equivalent benefits and risk assumptions. Specify attachment points, lasers, exclusions, terminal liability, runout funding, collateral, and claim-payment timing. NADAC is a published acquisition-cost reference, not a complete pharmacy contract. An explicitly defined NADAC-based methodology needs the applicable publication date, dispensing fees, specialty-drug treatment, rebate ownership, audit rights, and a negotiated fallback for drugs without a usable NADAC value. For 250 employees, a quoted $12 PEPM administrative reduction equals $36,000 annually. If incremental stop-loss and collateral carrying costs total $45,000, the proposal is already $9,000 worse before adding advisory fees or transition work. Do not bury those costs in a different exhibit. The fallback is to retain or competitively rebid the existing structure, obtain explicit pricing fallbacks, and postpone implementation until the employer accepts the risk-adjusted economics. Keep employer advisory records separate from provider recovery records; neither relationship creates automatic authority to reuse the other's protected information or negotiate on behalf of an adverse party without a documented conflict assessment.

The thirteenth trap is financing an evidence-heavy recovery business with fee commitments that exceed its cash buffer. The canonical startup range is $10,000 to $45,000, monthly operating expense range is $1,800 to $10,500, and first-deal timing is one to four months. Those ranges are planning inputs, not complete annual cost bridges: case charges, reimbursable pass-throughs, staffing, and the owner's compensation treatment must still reconcile to the three canonical scenarios. At $10,500 monthly overhead, four months consumes $42,000 before incremental filing exposure, leaving only $3,000 from a $45,000 opening balance. Do not commit that remainder to a large payer cohort whose determinations have not converted into cash. Maintain a thirteen-week cash forecast separating provider money, earned operator fees, refundable deposits, nonrecoverable charges, and reversal reserves. Require a positive stress-case balance after assuming no new collections for the next eight weeks and funding already committed case costs. If that test fails, reduce intake, renegotiate authorized expense funding, or return incomplete files before incurring charges. The durable advantage is selective underwriting of collectible claims with defensible evidence, not the maximum number of portal submissions a small team can generate in a day.
