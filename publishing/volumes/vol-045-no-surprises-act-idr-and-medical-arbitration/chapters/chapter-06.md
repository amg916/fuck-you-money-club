## 1. Mine account-level intent without confusing public signals with eligible claims  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with independent emergency medicine, anesthesiology, radiology, pathology, and neonatology groups serving facilities where federal surprise-billing protections may apply. Build an account universe from the CMS National Plan and Provider Enumeration System downloadable files and NPI Registry API, then resolve organization names against practice websites and state business registries. An NPI taxonomy identifies a prospect category; it does not establish network status, coverage, standing, or a recoverable dispute. Store organization NPI, specialty, operating states, facility relationships, public business contact, source URL, and retrieval date. Treat the billing manager, revenue-cycle director, and practice administrator as separate buying roles rather than buying three copies of the same lead. Use CMS federal IDR public-use reports to prioritize broad service and payer patterns, never to infer a specific practice's unpaid award. A public hiring notice for an arbitration analyst, a new facility staffing contract, or a billing-vendor transition is a stronger conversation trigger than generic specialty membership. Screen each trigger manually before outreach. A practice that recently moved entirely in-network can look attractive in stale directories while having almost no new federal IDR inventory. Refresh high-priority accounts monthly and preserve historical source snapshots.

Implement public_record_api_intelligence as a modest, auditable collection pipeline, not an indiscriminate scrape. A scheduled Python urllib job can fetch permitted official feeds, while sqlite3 retains source hashes, timestamps, and deduplication keys. Use county civil dockets for public practice ownership disputes or billing-vendor litigation that indicates operational change; use probate registries only when a publicly documented practice succession creates a legitimate business contact. Unclaimed property ledgers can reveal a provider entity's stale payment address, but those assets are a separate recovery category and must never enter the IDR revenue forecast. Do not extract patient details from court exhibits or assume that public accessibility authorizes unrestricted reuse. A practical research budget is 100 accounts at 12 minutes each, or 20 hours; at an illustrative $40 hourly research cost, account discovery costs $800 before outreach. If access restrictions, unstable interfaces, or missing coverage undermine an API, switch to scheduled bulk FOIA/public-record exports, official data feeds, and low-frequency cached queues. Respect access terms and request only business-level fields. The fallback preserves research continuity, not filing timeliness: statutory clocks must come from authorized claim records, never delayed public-record responses.

The twelve plays below are a chapter-local sourcing map; only public_record_api_intelligence is a supplied cross-series identifier. Combine them into three queues: immediately contactable practice triggers, partner-introduced accounts, and inbound education requests. Every queue feeds the same qualification process. An HFMA chapter presentation can generate permission-based introductions, while an RBMA educational session may surface radiology groups with fragmented remittance workflows. A billing company is valuable because it can introduce several authorized clients, not because it can disclose every client's records. Require each provider's authorization and the appropriate HIPAA arrangements before receiving protected information. Do not offer percentage referral payments to clinicians or billing vendors without evaluating the actual federal and state restrictions; unpaid introductions avoid making referral compensation the acquisition thesis. At an illustrative 20 accounts per weekly research batch, four batches produce 80 distinct accounts, not 80 qualified mandates. Deduplicate across partner lists and events using organization identity rather than contact email. Measure qualified meetings per source and subsequent cash contribution per cohort. Retire a channel after two completed test cohorts if it produces meetings but no timely, eligible, economically workable inventory.

## 2. Score eligibility, deadline runway and collectible contribution before accepting inventory  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Qualification begins with a protected evidence package, not a spreadsheet of billed charges. Execute the service agreement, HIPAA Business Associate Agreement, and access instructions before handling protected health information; extend appropriate obligations to subcontractors. Request a limited recent sample containing the 835 remittance, underlying claim, service date, initial payment or denial receipt evidence, provider and facility identifiers, plan information, and prior negotiation correspondence. An 835 CARC/RARC classifier can identify adjustment patterns, but cannot conclusively determine federal jurisdiction or plan funding. A commercial payer logo does not distinguish its insured products from self-funded administrative-services business. Confirm coverage, applicable state law, service setting, network relationships, and the payment-determination method before calculating an opportunity. Some self-funded plans can opt into specified state arrangements; some services follow an applicable All-Payer Model Agreement. Exclude Medicare and Medicaid inventory from this commercial federal IDR sourcing model and route ordinary contractual disputes elsewhere. The federal mechanism is not a universal underpayment remedy. Record the basis for each eligibility decision and its reviewer. If a missing document could change the jurisdictional result, put the claim in a document-request queue rather than purchasing an arbitration filing on an assumption.

Build separate states for initial-payment receipt, open-negotiation initiation, negotiation completion, IDR initiation, entity selection, submissions, determination, and cash receipt. Under the federal framework, the open-negotiation notice generally must be initiated within 30 business days after receipt of the initial payment or denial; the negotiation period itself then runs for 30 business days. IDR initiation generally occurs during the four-business-day period beginning after negotiation ends. The four days are an initiation window, not permission to batch unrelated claims. Verify current CMS instructions, applicable extensions, litigation effects, and the rule version governing the particular dispute before scheduling action. Keep service dates distinct from receipt dates and retain proof of notice transmission. The 90-calendar-day restriction following a determination requires party and same-or-similar-service tracking, with the applicable later initiation window handled explicitly. Do not reduce it to a single universal payer/CPT timer. Batching requires its own current-rule eligibility check; a shared payer brand alone is insufficient. Assign an accountable human to each imminent deadline and an escalation backup. A nightly exception report should surface missing receipt evidence, disputed plan classification, expired clocks, and cooling-off conflicts before staff prepare an offer.

Use a 100-point account score only after mandatory eligibility checks pass: verified federal inventory earns 25 points, deadline runway 20, evidence completeness 20, collectible contribution 20, and administrator access 15. Seventy-five points qualifies a practice for a bounded pilot; no score rescues an ineligible claim. Define the contractual fee base as incremental cash collected above the actual initial payer payment, less non-refundable administrative fees, unrecovered entity fees, and agreed casework costs, floored at zero. QPA and initial allowed payment are distinct fields; never replace the actual baseline with an assumed QPA payment. Illustratively, $20,000 initially collected plus $10,000 subsequently collected creates $10,000 of incremental cash. Deduct $1,500 of administrative and unrecovered entity fees and $1,000 of casework costs: the distributable recovery is $7,500 and a 30% operator share is $2,250. These are aggregate sample costs, not published fee quotes. A $12,000 paper uplift with only $10,000 collected still supports only that $2,250 fee. Track entity-fee deposits separately: recognize a refund only when supported by actual disposition and receipt, and conservatively reserve losing or ineligible cases for full forfeiture unless the governing refund process establishes otherwise.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four checks before any paid filing commitment**
> 
> A signed mandate authorizes scoped work; it does not establish that every submitted claim belongs in federal IDR.
> - Verify service eligibility, plan funding, specified state law, and any applicable alternative payment-determination method.
> - Reconstruct receipt and notice dates from retained evidence; calculate both negotiation periods and the separate IDR initiation window.
> - Validate current batching requirements and any same-party, same-or-similar-service cooling-off restriction before combining disputes.
> - Confirm authority, protected data handling, current fees, loss reserves, and positive cash-based economics after every agreed deduction.

## 3. Run a narrow outbound cadence that converts evidence into signed mandates  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run a five-touch, fifteen-business-day sequence around one documented business trigger. On day one, email the practice administrator a short observation and offer a twenty-line eligibility-and-deadline review, without requesting patient information by email. On day three, place a manual call to the published business number and ask who owns out-of-network recovery operations. On day six, send a permission-respecting professional-network message with a one-page explanation of the cash-based fee waterfall. On day ten, send a useful deadline-control worksheet; on day fifteen, close the loop and stop unless they invite further contact. Commercial email must use accurate routing and subject information, the required sender identification and postal address, a functioning opt-out, and timely suppression under 15 U.S.C. § 7704. Manual business outreach does not justify automated texts or prerecorded calls; evaluate the actual channel under 47 U.S.C. § 227 and applicable state rules before adding automation. Maintain one suppression list across email and partner campaigns. Never claim to represent CMS, a payer, or a certified IDR entity. The useful promise is a bounded assessment with documented exclusions, not an expected award or a guaranteed collection date.

Use explicit planning assumptions rather than fabricated industry conversion benchmarks. A starter cohort of 300 raw organization records might yield 180 verified eligible-profile accounts, a 60% research yield. If 30 engage in a relevant conversation, that is 16.7% of verified accounts; 15 discovery meetings represent 50% of conversations. Eight authorized sample reviews represent 53.3% of meetings, and three accepted mandates represent 37.5% of sample reviews, or 1% of raw records. These are test targets, not observed performance. Review source quality if the first 60 verified accounts produce fewer than six relevant conversations; review qualification or positioning if ten meetings produce fewer than two authorized samples. Do not compensate for poor conversion by filing marginal claims. If total acquisition spend is $4,500 and three mandates sign, acquisition cost is $1,500 per mandate. At an illustrative $2,250 earned fee per completed recovery cohort and $750 of additional operator delivery overhead, one collected cohort contributes $1,500 before acquisition expense, merely recovering that acquisition cost. Require evidence of repeat eligible inventory before paying for scale. Ten mandates at the same raw conversion would require 1,000 records and enough delivery capacity to service them without missing deadlines.

The discovery call should establish authority, inventory, process failure, and economics in that order. Ask who can authorize representation, how many recent potentially covered claims exist, which person owns receipt-date evidence, and whether another vendor already controls the same disputes. When the administrator says their billing company handles appeals, distinguish contractual appeals from timely federal open negotiation and IDR, then request a scoped coordination discussion rather than attacking the incumbent. When they cite a high published prevailing-offer percentage, explain that selection outcomes are not cash realization rates and request actual remittance history. When they demand a recovery guarantee, offer a fixed sample scope, documented eligibility findings, and cash-contingent compensation instead. When they resist data access, begin with aggregate counts and move to a minimum necessary protected sample only after contracting. The pilot mandate should identify the authorized provider entities, service-date range, payer scope, fee waterfall, expense funding, dispute authority, termination treatment, and reconciliation process. Require affirmative instructions about settlement authority; a contingency agreement alone should not imply unlimited discretion. Send the diagnostic findings within five business days of receiving complete records, while escalating any nearer statutory deadline immediately rather than waiting for the scheduled presentation.

## 4. Land three mandates, prove the cash waterfall and scale only repeatable cohorts  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Sequence the first four months around evidence delivery, not a premature filing target. During weeks one and two, configure the CRM, source register, suppression list, secure transfer environment, service agreement, and Business Associate Agreement. During weeks three and four, complete the first account cohort, run discovery calls, and accept only samples with adequate procedural runway. During weeks five through eight, deliver diagnostics, sign the first three bounded mandates, and initiate authorized negotiations on eligible inventory. During weeks nine through sixteen, expand toward ten mandates only if staffing and deadline coverage remain adequate. Count a sourcing win when the provider signs and the operating team accepts a viable scoped inventory; count a collected recovery separately. Fresh negotiations and subsequent arbitration can push cash beyond the acquisition window. Maintain distinct dashboard columns for signed mandates, accepted claims, negotiations initiated, disputes initiated, determinations, incremental collections, and earned fees. Existing disputes inherited from another vendor require explicit authority and procedural verification before any handoff. Never advertise a first-month collection timetable simply because one prospect has an old unpaid award. The acquisition plan must survive a slower cash cycle than the sales cycle.

Turn the diagnostic into a concise operating exhibit: eligible inventory, rejected inventory with reasons, next procedural dates, permitted evidence gaps, projected expenses, and a cash-waterfall example. Separate QPA-related information from prohibited pricing evidence. FAIR Health products may assist internal research where permitted, but a usual-and-customary charge benchmark must not be repackaged as permissible federal IDR valuation evidence. The federal framework excludes usual and customary charges, billed charges, and public payer reimbursement rates as determination factors. Use documented patient acuity, provider training and experience, facility characteristics, market share where relevant, and other permitted credible information without counting a factor twice through QPA and supplemental evidence. Assign submission drafting and eligibility review to different people when possible. After determination, track the statutory payment timetable, payment exceptions, and actual bank or remittance evidence; unpaid awards remain receivables rather than collected contingency revenue. Escalate delayed payment through the appropriate payer and CMS processes using retained records. Never transfer a protected patient balance to the patient to finance the dispute. Keep administrative fees, entity deposits, returned deposits, casework costs, baseline receipts, and incremental receipts in separate ledger fields so a provider can reproduce every invoice.

Reconcile scale to the canonical scorecard, not to physician collections. Conservative annual operator revenue is $110,000, with $77,000 of operator expenses and $33,000 owner profit at 30% margin using one full-time worker and two contractors. Base revenue is $505,000, expenses $287,850, and owner profit $217,150 at 43.0%, with the same team structure. Scaled revenue is $900,000, expenses $396,000, and owner profit $504,000 at 56%, using two full-time workers and three contractors. An illustrative $2,500 average earned fee requires 44, 202, and 360 collected recovery cohorts annually, respectively; cohort size is a modeling unit, not an authorized batching rule. Provider cash, paper awards, and refunded deposits are not substitutes for those fee totals. Costs already reimbursed or deducted in determining the contingency base must not be charged again as unreimbursed operator expenses. Use separate settlement and operator ledgers to prevent that duplication. Before hiring or buying another lead list, stress collection timing by an additional 60 days, reduce expected cash realization, and include losing or ineligible-case fee exposure. Pause expansion if the resulting working-capital requirement exceeds funded reserves, even when the mandate pipeline looks strong. High margin comes from disciplined selection and repeatable execution, not from celebrating uncollected awards.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build one evidence-backed account cohort
   - Resolve 100 specialty practice accounts from official sources and documented operating triggers.
   - Deduplicate provider organizations, retain source dates, identify business contacts, and exclude patient information from the prospecting database.
2. Qualify a protected twenty-line sample
   - Secure authority and HIPAA arrangements before reviewing claim-level records.
   - Verify jurisdiction, receipt dates, negotiation status, batching constraints, permitted evidence, and the incremental cash waterfall for each sampled line.
3. Convert diagnostics into three bounded mandates
   - Present exclusions, deadlines, expense funding, fee mechanics, and explicit settlement authority.
   - Count a mandate only after signature and operational acceptance; coordinate with existing vendors and avoid duplicate representation of the same disputes.
4. Reconcile collections before increasing acquisition spend
   - Match payer remittances and deposits to baseline receipts, incremental receipts, expenses, and earned fees.
   - Compare acquisition cost and delivery contribution by source cohort, stress a 60-day collection delay, and expand only within funded capacity.
