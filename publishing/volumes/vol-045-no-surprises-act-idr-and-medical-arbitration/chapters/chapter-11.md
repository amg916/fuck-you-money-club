## 1. Assign Twelve Recovery Plays to Named Seats Before Adding Headcount  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the initial pod around one full-time accountable operator and two specialist contractors, not three interchangeable claim processors. The operator owns provider relationships, jurisdiction exceptions, cash reconciliation, and the weekly queue. Contractor A owns intake and deadline operations; Contractor B owns evidence assembly, offer support, and submission quality. Use this chapter's twelve-play operating map: P01 provider qualification, P02 engagement and BAA execution, P03 835 ingestion, P04 jurisdiction classification, P05 incremental-recovery screening, P06 open negotiation, P07 IDR initiation and entity selection, P08 batching and cooling-off control, P09 evidence and offer assembly, P10 determination tracking, P11 remittance reconciliation, and P12 renewal and book transfer. These are assigned workstreams, not twelve additional jobs. The operator retains accountability for every play while delegating production through explicit acceptance criteria. Federal eligibility under 42 U.S.C. section 300gg-111 depends on the service, coverage, and applicable payment regime; an ERISA label alone does not establish the correct route. Capture plan funding, service location, provider specialty, service category, and applicable state payment rules before spending entity fees. Store the classification rationale alongside source documents so another reviewer can reproduce the decision without interviewing the founder. Escalate unresolved jurisdiction questions before filing, rather than rewarding a contractor for sending an unsupported case.

Run the deadline engine as a controlled state machine with an assigned owner, backup, timestamp, and delivery evidence for every transition. The ordinary federal sequence includes initiating open negotiation within the applicable thirty-business-day window after initial payment or denial, completing the thirty-business-day negotiation period, and initiating IDR within four business days after that period ends. The four-day window is not a blanket deadline for every entity-selection or batching task. Configure later milestones separately under the operative version of 45 CFR section 149.510 and applicable extensions. Use PostgreSQL for the event ledger and a private task interface for assignments; retain portal confirmations in an access-controlled document store. A calendar reminder without acknowledged service is not completion evidence. Contractor A prepares notices and verifies addresses; Contractor B checks disputed dates and submission attachments; the operator resolves exceptions. Record holidays, timezone conventions, outages, and corrected inputs in an append-only audit history. For P08, explicitly record the parties, item or service classification, determination date, and relevant ninety-calendar-day cooling-off restriction. Do not pool unrelated provider claims merely because a payer name matches. Require an independent batch-eligibility check before submission and preserve the rule version applied.

Move to two full-time seats and three contractors only when accepted workload, rather than gross award announcements, exceeds demonstrated capacity. The original operator becomes portfolio and commercial lead; the second employee becomes production and quality lead. Retain the intake contractor and evidence contractor, then add a remittance and exception-resolution contractor for P10 and P11. This is five people in total, not five employees plus a hidden service bureau. Budget specialist consultations separately and disclose their expense without representing occasional advice as a permanent production seat. Measure touch minutes by play for four weeks, then estimate committed capacity after leave, rework, and deadline reserves. An illustrative eighty-percent utilization ceiling leaves room for short filing windows and payer exceptions; it is an operating target, not a statutory requirement. Every critical play needs a trained backup, but a person's backup assignment does not create extra capacity. Execute HIPAA business associate agreements and applicable subcontractor agreements before protected information moves, with role-based access and auditable offboarding. Keep provider files inside contracted systems rather than personal inboxes. Document a two-week founder-absence exercise before expansion: another named person must run the queue, reconcile cash, and explain unresolved exceptions from the records alone.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the Thin Pod From Expensive Recovery Friction**
> 
> The highest-value efficiencies remove preventable rework without weakening jurisdiction, evidence, or cash verification.
> - Keep an immutable copy of each original 835 and corrected remittance; payer adjustments can otherwise erase the initial-payment baseline used for contingency calculations.
> - Prebuild specialty-specific evidence indexes, but make the reviewer confirm patient-specific acuity and permitted factors before reusing any argument.
> - Maintain a separate cooling-off register keyed to the relevant parties and item or service classification; a payer-wide calendar entry is too coarse.
> - Assign every determination a remittance owner immediately; a favorable offer selection does not deposit money or establish an earned contingency.

## 2. Pay Contractors for Accepted Work and Publish One Weekly Scorecard  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Contractor compensation should purchase defined outputs without encouraging ineligible filings or inflated offers. For planning, use illustrative hourly rates of $35 to $55 for intake work and $65 to $95 for specialized evidence work, then validate actual proposals against the expense envelope. A piece-rate alternative should specify exactly what an accepted package contains, how revisions are handled, and which disputed tasks remain billable. Pay for properly documented ineligibility findings as well as eligible packages; otherwise the contractor has an economic reason to suppress bad news. Avoid bonuses based solely on award size, portal submissions, or headline prevailing rates. A modest quality component can reward timely, accepted work and accurate reconciliations, with exclusions for payer delay outside the contractor's control. Define a one-business-day acknowledgment target, a two-business-day routine defect correction target, and same-day escalation for deadlines inside five business days. These are internal service targets, not substitutes for statutory clocks. Have the reviewer identify the defect category and affected claim instead of returning a vague rejection. Specify correction economics in the statement of work, and avoid unilateral payment deductions. Worker classification follows actual relationships and applicable law; a contractor label does not settle employee status.

Publish the scorecard at the same weekly cutoff using stable denominators and claim-level drilldowns. For P03 through P05, show complete-intake rate, independently confirmed eligibility rate, and screening turnaround. For P06 through P08, show notices served on time, initiation deadlines met, rejected submissions, and preventable ineligibility dismissals. For P09, show first-pass acceptance, hours per accepted brief, and defects by evidence category. For P10 and P11, separate favorable determinations, dollars awarded above baseline, incremental dollars collected, collection lag, and fees actually returned. A prevailing percentage must identify whether its denominator is determinations, cases, or dollars; none is a cash collection rate. Cohort cash realization divides incremental receipts by the corresponding incremental amounts due for the same matured cohort, with reversals handled consistently. Do not compare this week's deposits with this week's awards. Track the thirty-calendar-day payment requirement following the federal determination separately from observed payer behavior. Age unpaid balances from their actual due dates, assign a collection action, and preserve correspondence. A weekly meeting should finish with named corrective owners and dates, not a discussion about who was busiest. Repeated eligibility defects trigger expanded review before more fees are exposed.

Use concentration limits to protect both workload continuity and saleability. Set an internal ceiling of twenty-five percent for any single provider customer and separately for any payer parent group, measured on trailing-twelve-month earned operator revenue. Also inspect incremental receivables and forecast fee exposure because a payer can dominate unpaid awards before dominating collected revenue. Aggregate affiliated practices under their controlling relationship and payer brands under the economically relevant parent; multiple logos do not necessarily diversify risk. A new pod may initially exceed the ceiling. Report that breach explicitly, cap additional discretionary exposure, and pursue other accounts while preserving existing client duties and deadlines. Do not describe a concentrated launch book as diversified. Apply the same discipline to contractor dependency: each critical play must have an operational backup with tested access. Review capacity, concentration, and unresolved defects together each Friday. If new business would consume the deadline reserve, delay onboarding rather than accept volume the pod cannot service. Revenue growth does not justify weak evidence or unexplained fee losses. Retain weekly snapshots so a buyer can distinguish genuine improvement from a dashboard rewritten immediately before diligence. Compare rolling four-week results with the prior period to detect persistent drift rather than overreacting to individual case outcomes.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly measurement set
   - Export the claim ledger, cash matches, deadline queue, and accepted-work register at the agreed cutoff.
   - Retain the export version and calculation definitions so later corrections remain visible rather than silently rewriting history.
2. Resolve imminent deadlines before discussing growth
   - Inspect every required action due within five business days and confirm its owner, backup, and supporting evidence.
   - Separate genuine legal extensions from internal scheduling changes; retain the source authorizing each extension.
3. Trace exceptions to their economic consequences
   - Review defects, forfeited fees, overdue awards, and concentration breaches using claim-level records.
   - Assign each corrective action a responsible person, completion date, and measurable acceptance condition.
4. Release accepted contractor work and next-week capacity
   - Approve invoices against contracted acceptance terms and reserve hours for deadlines, rework, and collections.
   - Authorize additional volume only within measured capacity; archive decisions with the weekly scorecard snapshot.

## 3. Reconcile Cash-Based Fee Waterfalls to Clean Accrual and Cohort Books  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start every engagement with a written recovery waterfall that separates provider money from operator revenue. The initial payer allowed payment is the baseline; the QPA may inform that payment but is not automatically identical to it. Measure incremental cash against the actual documented baseline, including subsequent adjustments without double counting. For an illustrative claim, assume $2,000 initial payment and $8,000 total payer cash ultimately received. Incremental cash is $6,000. If actual non-refundable administrative fees are $150, an entity-fee deposit of $600 is returned following a prevailing determination, and attributable casework costs are $450, the distributable incremental pool is $5,400. At a contracted twenty-five-percent contingency, operator revenue is $1,350, not $8,000 and not the award amount. The example's fees are assumptions, not a current CMS fee schedule. Until the $600 refund actually arrives, an unrecovered-fee waterfall produces a $4,800 pool and $1,200 fee, followed by a $150 true-up after receipt. Losing or ineligible cases retain the applicable forfeited entity-fee exposure. Record actual allocations for settlements and other procedural outcomes separately. Never assume all deposits return or calculate a fee from an uncollected favorable determination.

The canonical Formula v2.0 scenarios remain fixed: Conservative operator revenue of $110,000 less $77,000 expenses yields $33,000 owner profit at thirty percent; Base revenue of $505,000 less $287,850 yields $217,150 at 43.0 percent; Scaled revenue of $900,000 less $396,000 yields $504,000 at fifty-six percent. Conservative and Base use one full-time person plus two contractors; Scaled uses two full-time people plus three contractors. These revenue figures are gross operator fee revenue, not gross medical collections. At the illustrative twenty-five-percent share, the necessary post-cost incremental pools are $440,000, $2,020,000, and $3,600,000 respectively. This sensitivity explains the cases without inventing a universal claim count or collection rate. Amounts deducted before calculating the share must carry explicit funding and reimbursement treatment. If a provider bears casework cost through the waterfall, record that reimbursement or settlement once; do not silently charge the identical cost again against operator profit. Operator-funded unreimbursed casework, contractor overhead, technology, and other operating expenses belong in the stated expense envelope. The $1,800 to $10,500 monthly opex range is not an all-in reconciliation for every scenario; annualized stated expense envelopes exceed that range in Base and Scaled because the full cost bridge must be shown.

Maintain separate dates for service, intake, negotiation, initiation, determination, payer receipt, fee earning, invoicing, and operator collection. The earned contingency condition is incremental payer cash actually collected under the engagement, not merely a favorable award. When that condition and the applicable revenue recognition requirements are satisfied, recognize operator fee revenue and a provider receivable even if the provider pays the invoice later. Keep potential awards and unearned contingent fees in operational schedules rather than booked revenue. Evaluate principal-versus-agent presentation under ASC 606 for reimbursed costs and pass-through funds; do not label provider collections as operator turnover. Separate cash custodial obligations from earned revenue and reconcile any handled client funds by provider. Create cohorts by intake month, provider, payer group, jurisdiction route, and service category. Each cohort should expose submitted volume, eligible volume, fee losses, collected uplift, contractor cost, and time to cash. Accrue incurred contractor work and other expenses in the proper period, estimate collectibility of earned receivables, and record reversals transparently. A buyer should reconcile ledger totals to invoices, payer remittances, and bank records without relying on a founder's spreadsheet explanation. Close monthly with a documented review of unmatched cash and all outstanding entity-fee deposits.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Exposing Fees or Booking a Contingency**
> 
> Use the first three checks before filing and repeat the cash check before recognizing or invoicing the earned recovery share.
> - Verify the applicable federal or state route using service facts, plan funding evidence, and documented exceptions; do not equate every ERISA plan with federal IDR eligibility.
> - Verify notice delivery, business-day calculations, batch eligibility, relevant cooling-off restrictions, and the operative rule version before committing submission fees.
> - Verify current administrative and entity charges, losing-case exposure, casework funding, and the signed post-cost contingency waterfall against claim-level expected economics.
> - Verify incremental payer cash, the original payment baseline, actual fee refunds, attributable costs, and the resulting operator fee before recording earned revenue.

## 4. Make the Recovery Book Transferable Before Discussing an Exit Multiple  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A transferable book consists of enforceable relationships, reproducible work, clean ownership, and evidence that collections continue without the founder. Provider agreements should address assignment, change of control, termination, access to records, recovery-tail economics, and responsibility for outstanding fees. Obtain required permissions before transferring contracts or sensitive records; a transaction term sheet does not itself authorize disclosure of protected health information. Begin buyer diligence with aggregated cohorts and properly de-identified information where appropriate, then structure necessary access under applicable HIPAA permissions and safeguards. Contractor deliverables need signed intellectual-property assignments under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a) where copyright ownership is transferred. Do not rely solely on a broad work-made-for-hire label for independent contractor software or templates. Identify preexisting contractor tools and secure sufficient transferable licenses for required components. Inventory source code, deadline rules, brief templates, evidence indexes, and operating manuals in a controlled repository. FAIR Health access and other licensed sources require their own usage and transfer review; purchased access does not establish ownership of the underlying material. Keep credentials outside the sale repository. Prove continuity through tested backup access, documented renewals, and a named successor for each of the twelve plays.

Value a normalized earnings stream, not the face amount of pending determinations. For an owner-operated practice, an illustrative two-to-three-times seller discretionary earnings range can serve as a sensitivity, provided the earnings definition, owner compensation, and required owner labor are reconciled. Using Base owner profit of $217,150 as a provisional SDE proxy produces $434,300 to $651,450; it becomes a usable valuation input only after that bridge is verified. These are planning assumptions, not observed transaction comparables. An independently managed operation can instead test a three-to-five-times normalized EBITDA range. If Scaled owner profit of $504,000 excludes a necessary $120,000 replacement manager cost, and no other adjustments apply, the normalized proxy becomes $384,000 and the enterprise-value sensitivity becomes $1,152,000 to $1,920,000. Do not deduct replacement cost twice if it is already inside the $396,000 expense envelope. Owner profit, SDE, and EBITDA are different measures; provide the bridge rather than relabeling them. Determine equity proceeds separately after debt, cash adjustments, working-capital terms, transaction costs, and holdbacks. A higher multiple requires defensible retention, limited concentration, stable cohort collections, and demonstrably transferable execution. A backlog of unpaid awards is not a substitute for those attributes.

Prepare a rolling diligence package even if the intended outcome is a durable cash cow. Include monthly income statements, expense bridges, provider and payer concentration, cohort collection curves, fee-loss history, contractor acceptance records, ownership documents, and the founder-absence test. Separate recurring revenue from one-time catch-up recoveries and expose provider churn rather than blending departed accounts into historical totals. For a sale, define which party owns preclosing awards, postclosing receipts, refunds, and collection costs. If an earnout is used, base it on an auditable measure such as collected operator fees from identified transferred cohorts, with agreed deductions, collection responsibilities, reporting rights, and dispute procedures. An award-based earnout can overpay for balances that never convert to cash. For continued ownership, reserve cash for payroll, contractor commitments, unrecovered fees, and delayed payer remittances before distributions. Stress-test the loss of the largest provider and a prolonged payment delay from the largest payer group. The twenty-five-percent caps limit exposure but do not eliminate it. Choose growth only when it improves normalized cash generation after replacement labor; otherwise preserve the smaller pod. The same disciplined records support either decision without manufacturing a premium narrative around uncertain collections.
