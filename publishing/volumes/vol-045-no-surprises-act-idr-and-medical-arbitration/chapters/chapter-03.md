## 1. Convert provider collections into a cash-earned contingency base  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start with the 835 electronic remittance advice, the payer's initial payment, and the matching bank deposit, not the provider's charge master. The qualifying payment amount, or QPA, is a statutory benchmark; it is not necessarily the initial allowed amount or actual remittance. Preserve those fields separately. For each claim, calculate incremental payer cash as subsequently collected reimbursement above the documented initial payer payment, excluding patient money and unrelated adjustments. Then subtract nonrefundable CMS administrative fees, unrecovered certified entity fees, and attributable casework before applying the contracted share. The illustrative share here is 25%, not a prescribed market rate. A negative case balance remains attached to its contractually defined cohort; do not erase losses by moving winning claims into a fresh invoice. Provider collections remain provider revenue, while the operator records only its earned service fee. Under an ASC 606 principal-versus-agent assessment, custody of provider funds alone does not make the operator principal for medical services. Treat the retained economic share as 25% of the eligible net recovery pool, never 25% of provider gross billings. This boundary makes the scorecard's revenue figures interpretable and prevents a second, fictitious medical-revenue business from appearing in the management accounts.

Consider 100 intake claims with $100,000 of initial provider payments already collected and excluded. Eligibility review identifies 60 potentially federal claims; negotiation and economic screening leave 40 filings. Assume 28 favorable determinations, eight losses, and four eligibility dismissals. Each favorable determination represents $2,500 of incremental reimbursement, producing $70,000 of awarded uplift, but only 24 have paid: incremental cash is $60,000. For arithmetic only, budget $100 administrative expense and $500 entity expense per filing; these are planning assumptions, not current fee quotations. Initial disbursements are $4,000 administrative, $20,000 entity, and $6,000 direct casework, or $30,000. Assume documented entity refunds of $14,000 for the 28 prevailing cases, leaving $6,000 unrecovered. Budget dismissal deposits as fully exposed until the applicable entity policy and actual refund establish otherwise; dismissal treatment must be checked rather than inferred from winning-party treatment. Net cash eligible for sharing is $60,000 minus $4,000 minus $6,000 minus $6,000, equaling $44,000. The operator earns $11,000; the provider retains $33,000 after those costs and that fee. The four unpaid favorable determinations contribute no contingency revenue yet. A later receipt or refund requires a cumulative cohort true-up, not a duplicate fee calculation.

The annual bridge below preserves the canonical scorecard without pretending that every portfolio repeats the sample cohort's payer mix. Conservative collects $600,000 of uplift and deducts $160,000 before sharing; Base collects $2,700,000 and deducts $680,000; Scaled collects $4,800,000 and deducts $1,200,000. At 25%, eligible pools of $440,000, $2,020,000, and $3,600,000 produce exactly $110,000, $505,000, and $900,000 of operator revenue. These upstream costs are provider-borne amounts under the illustrative agreement, funded from a restricted expense balance or deducted from recoveries. They are not operator revenue and are not deducted again in the operating waterfall. Direct casework here means separately reimbursed, claim-specific external work; operator delivery labor means unreimbursed internal management, collection follow-up, quality control, and contractor capacity. Give each invoice line exactly one classification. Require a contractual loss-carryforward rule, refund ownership rule, and settlement statement before launching a cohort. Otherwise a winning determination can trigger a fee despite an aggregate provider loss. Use claim identifiers, payer references, and bank transaction identifiers to reconcile cumulative earned fees against amounts already invoiced, with a documented exception queue for reversals and unmatched deposits.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Price the bank receipt, not the determination letter**
> 
> The highest-value reconciliation work happens between the 835 adjustment, the bank deposit, and the cumulative provider settlement statement.
> - Store QPA, initial allowed amount, initial payer cash, and subsequent payer cash separately; otherwise an allowed-amount adjustment can masquerade as recovered money.
> - Carry unrecovered expenses across the contractually defined cohort before issuing contingency invoices; prohibit winner-only fee extraction from a losing pool.
> - Track entity refunds as expense recoveries until settled; do not book the same refund as both fresh payer uplift and reduced filing expense.
> - Separate direct reimbursable casework from operator delivery labor using invoice-line tags; require the bookkeeper to resolve duplicate classifications before closing each month.

## 2. Reconcile earned fees to owner profit without hiding labor or reversals  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Once the cash-earned fee enters the operator ledger, the second waterfall measures the cost of running the recovery company. Conservative deducts $77,000 from $110,000, leaving $33,000 and a 30% margin. Base deducts $287,850 from $505,000, leaving $217,150 and a 43.0% margin. Scaled deducts $396,000 from $900,000, leaving $504,000 and a 56% margin. These are the canonical annual outcomes; the category allocations below are illustrative budgets that reconcile exactly, not claimed historical expense observations. Owner profit means operating earnings available to the working owner before personal income taxes, debt principal repayments, and distributions. It includes compensation for that owner's own full-time effort; subtract a replacement salary separately when comparing this business with passive investments. Conservative and Base each use one full-time owner plus two contractors. Scaled uses that owner, one additional employee, and three contractors. Its delivery budget includes the additional employee's loaded compensation and contractor payments. Contractor count does not imply full-time availability. The operating plan must translate the dollar budget into contracted hours, expected claim touches, and overflow arrangements before assuming the margin improvement is achievable.

Delivery spending of $32,000, $125,000, and $190,000 must buy specific capacity rather than an abstract automation percentage. An illustrative $50 hourly loaded rate purchases 640, 2,500, and 3,800 paid-equivalent hours annually; the Scaled conversion is a blended capacity measure because its payroll includes an employee. At 1.5 hours per completed workflow, those budgets support approximately 427, 1,667, and 2,533 workflow equivalents, excluding the separately reimbursed external casework already deducted upstream. Measure actual touches for eligibility classification, brief assembly, remittance matching, and unpaid-award escalation. An 835 CARC/RARC parser can triage underpayments, but ERISA status and federal eligibility require plan and service evidence; remittance codes alone cannot establish jurisdiction. Acquisition budgets of $15,000, $60,000, and $85,000 could represent 10, 30, and 50 activated provider accounts, implying acquisition costs of $1,500, $2,000, and $1,700. Those account counts are planning assumptions, not canonical scorecard inputs. Allocate outbound labor, conference spending, and referral-channel administration consistently. Calculate payback using collected contribution after delivery costs rather than the face value of a newly signed provider's backlog. If an account supplies ineligible claims, stop paid acquisition until its service mix is corrected.

The scrub and reversal provisions are $6,000, $25,250, and $18,000, equivalent to approximately 5.45%, 5%, and 2% of earned operator revenue. They are economic loss allowances within these planning margins, not a second cash fee deducted from the provider's sharing pool. Cover identifiable fee refunds, post-payment recoupments, duplicate recovery corrections, and unreimbursed remediation after preventable eligibility errors. Rejected intake that never generates a fee reduces conversion; it does not create revenue followed by an invented reversal. Likewise, an unpaid award has no earned fee to reserve against in this model. Maintain a separate exposure register for fee receivables earned on provider cash but awaiting operator remittance. The accountant should determine whether each realized event belongs in contra-revenue, credit loss, or expense; the management waterfall must count it once regardless of presentation. Ring-fencing equivalent cash is a liquidity policy, not another profit deduction. Scaled's 2% assumption requires evidence from mature cohorts, not confidence in a brief generator. If its observed loss rate remains 5%, the extra $27,000 reduces owner profit to $477,000. Record that deterioration immediately rather than preserving the headline margin by releasing reserves before payer recoupment exposure has matured.

## 3. Fund the collection clock before expanding the filing queue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run two clocks: the statutory dispute clock and the economic collection clock. Federal open negotiation generally lasts 30 business days, and initiation generally follows within four business days after that period ends, subject to applicable extensions and current process instructions. Neither interval guarantees cash. Model eligibility review, negotiation, entity selection, submissions, determination, payer payment, bank matching, and provider-to-operator remittance separately. The federal framework requires payment of the determined amount within 30 calendar days after determination, but actual delinquency still needs collection capacity and cash reserves. Use a 90-day Conservative, 120-day Base, and 90-day Scaled cash-conversion planning lag from funded work to usable operator receipts. These are stress-model assumptions, not reported industry averages or legal extensions. Because fees are earned only on recovered cash here, most pre-collection pipeline value is unearned contingent opportunity rather than accounts receivable. Report that aging separately from genuine invoice DSO after provider collection. A provider payment lock of 15 days would extend a 105-day operating cycle to the Base model's 120 days. Do not add that lock again when sizing liquidity. Weekly aging should identify who currently controls the next action and the exact evidence needed to release payment.

For a launch-period funding test, remove the noncash economic provision from operating deductions while retaining budgeted financing cash expense. Annual cash operating spend becomes $71,000 Conservative, $262,600 Base, and $378,000 Scaled. Dividing by twelve gives monthly burn of $5,916.67, $21,883.33, and $31,500. Using 30-day planning months, the assumed lags require $17,750, $87,533.33, and $94,500 of operating bridge cash. Add a separately ring-fenced reversal cushion of $6,000, $25,250, and $18,000 to obtain liquidity targets of $23,750, $112,783.33, and $112,500, excluding provider-funded claim expenses, startup purchases, taxes, and owner distributions. This conservative launch test assumes no usable receipts during the stated funding interval; a mature staggered portfolio should instead use a weekly receipts-and-payments forecast. An extra 30 days adds $5,916.67, $21,883.33, or $31,500 before additional interest. The stated $10,000–$45,000 startup capital range can support a staged Conservative launch, not automatic Base throughput. Likewise, the $1,800–$10,500 monthly overhead envelope is not the fully loaded scaled burn once acquisition and delivery capacity are included. Bridge the difference with retained earnings, smaller cohorts, or committed financing rather than supplier invoices left unpaid.

Keep filing float outside the operator's unrestricted payroll account. The worked 100-intake cohort needs $30,000 of initial provider-funded disbursements even though its eventual net upstream expense is $16,000 after entity refunds. Requiring only $16,000 at inception leaves a $14,000 timing hole. Document replenishment thresholds, refund ownership, and permission to debit specific expenses; reconcile provider balances individually rather than lending one practice's funds to another. If the operator advances expenses, record the resulting contractual reimbursement asset separately, test collectibility, and add the peak advance to the liquidity forecast. For illustration, financing a $30,000 advance for 120 days at 18% simple annual interest costs approximately $1,775 using a 365-day year, before facility charges. That increment must fit the financing budget or reduce profit. QuickBooks or Xero can hold provider control accounts, while a PostgreSQL ledger links claims, filings, refunds, and deposits; execute required business associate agreements before vendors handle protected health information. Maintain a 13-week cash forecast with actual receipt dates and a separate forward pipeline schedule. When cash headroom falls below the next payroll plus committed filing obligations, reduce new intake immediately while protecting existing statutory deadlines and collection follow-up.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the claim-level cash spine
   - Join 835 remittances, initial payer deposits, subsequent deposits, and filing identifiers.
   - Preserve QPA separately, deduplicate adjustments, and place unmatched cash in a suspense queue that cannot generate contingency fees.
2. Fund gross expense exposure
   - Collect the provider's approved expense advance before committing filing and external casework costs.
   - Size the advance before expected refunds, record each provider balance separately, and stop new commitments when replenishment is overdue.
3. Close cumulative cohort settlements
   - Subtract verified upstream expenses and prior operator invoices from the cumulative cash-earned calculation.
   - Carry contractual cohort losses forward, distinguish refunds from payer uplift, and classify each realized correction only once.
4. Refresh the weekly liquidity forecast
   - Update thirteen weeks of actual obligations, expected collections, locked payouts, and available financing.
   - Stress an additional thirty-day receipt delay and restrict distributions before payroll or existing filing obligations become exposed.

> 
> A filing proceeds only when jurisdiction, timing, funding, and fee-base integrity are supported by retrievable evidence.
> - Verify the service, facility context, plan funding, state-law interaction, and applicable exceptions using plan documents and claim records rather than an ERISA guess from remittance codes.
