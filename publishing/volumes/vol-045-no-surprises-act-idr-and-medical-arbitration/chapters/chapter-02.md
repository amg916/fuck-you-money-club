## 1. Identify the provider buyer, the funded obligor, and the person who can release cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The provider writes the operator's check, while the responsible health plan or issuer pays the underlying covered claim. A surgical center's CFO buys recoveries because aged underpayments consume working capital, internal collectors lack specialized dispute capacity, and missed filing windows destroy otherwise actionable inventory. The revenue-cycle director evaluates evidence quality and workload; treasury controls bank instructions; procurement signs vendor terms; privacy personnel validate access to protected health information. For a self-funded employer plan, a recognizable insurer logo may identify only the third-party administrator, not the entity bearing benefit expense. For insured coverage, the issuer generally bears that expense, subject to the actual policy and applicable jurisdiction. Obtain the plan identifier, funding classification, administrator delegation, claim number, and payment contact before estimating recovery. An arbitrator decides an eligible dispute but does not insure collection or supply settlement capital. A successful sales meeting therefore ends with two authority maps: who can bind the provider to a contingency agreement, and who can resolve and fund the payer obligation. Ask for an actual remittance example and a recent payment trace rather than accepting a payer directory entry as proof of operational authority.

The acute payer pain is an enforceable payment obligation combined with dispute expense, documented administrative burden, and potential regulatory scrutiny, not embarrassment from an aggressive demand letter. For eligible federal IDR disputes, the selected offer establishes the out-of-network rate; the payment calculation must still reconcile prior payments and lawful patient cost sharing. Under 45 CFR § 149.510, payment following the determination is generally due within 30 calendar days. Do not convert that deadline into a forecast that every award becomes cash on day thirty. Provider executives care about net dollars and forecast reliability, so show award date, payment deadline, trace status, disputes, and collected balance separately. Payer negotiators care about authority limits, repeatable evidence, and avoiding avoidable certified-entity expense; give them an offer supported by claim-specific facts and an executable settlement document. Escalation should identify the missed obligation and competent oversight channel, rather than threaten collection from the patient. Protected patients are not the reserve account for an unpaid payer award. Maintain existing patient protections and correct any patient ledger allocation affected by a settlement. The operator's commercial leverage comes from a complete, timely, collectible file, not from shifting prohibited balance bills onto households.

The first specified play, hospital_lien_medical_receivables_arbitrage, operates here as Out-of-Network Medical Claim & ERISA Appeal Contingency Recovery, with a 20–35% contractual share. Its identifier does not create a hospital lien or authorize attaching patient assets. Recruit surgical centers, specialty clinics, and ambulance businesses only after separating eligible service categories. Ground ambulance claims generally fall outside the federal No Surprises Act balance-billing and IDR framework; air ambulance disputes have distinct provisions, including 45 CFR § 149.520. Nonparticipating facility and clinician claims also require service-setting and coverage analysis. For an illustrative $10,000 initial payment followed by $8,000 additional collected cash, deduct $800 of unrecovered dispute charges and agreed external casework costs, leaving a $7,200 contingency base. At 25%, earned operator revenue is $1,800; the provider retains $5,400 of net incremental recovery, plus its original payment. The $18,000 total payer cash is neither operator revenue nor profit. If the IDR window has expired, evaluate available ERISA benefit appeals under the governing plan and 29 CFR § 2560.503-1. That fallback requires an actual appealable benefit issue, standing, and a live deadline; an administrative appeal cannot simply resurrect a forfeited federal rate-dispute window.

## 2. Build jurisdiction, deadline, and fee economics into the same claim ledger  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the intake record from the 837 claim, 835 electronic remittance, explanation of benefits, clinical documentation, service location, network status, and relevant plan information. CARC and RARC combinations identify investigative leads; they do not establish federal jurisdiction or prove that every denial is an arbitrable payment-rate dispute. Determine whether a specified state law or applicable All-Payer Model controls the payment amount before selecting federal IDR. ERISA status alone does not resolve every jurisdiction question, particularly where state opt-in arrangements or service-specific exceptions matter. Record the initial payment or denial receipt date, the open-negotiation notice delivery evidence, and the parties receiving it. Distinguish the period for initiating open negotiation from the negotiation period itself: federal procedures generally allow initiation within 30 business days after initial payment or denial, followed by a 30-business-day open-negotiation period. The subsequent IDR initiation window is generally four business days. That four-day window is not a universal deadline for selecting the certified entity or assembling every batch. Use a jurisdiction-specific calendar with federal holidays, documented triggering events, and current applicable extensions. A missing date must create a research task, not a fabricated deadline that silently consumes a provider's rights.

Batching economics must follow the applicable rule rather than a spreadsheet's desire to spread fees across many claims. Validate parties, service characteristics, service dates, and the currently operative batching requirements before combining disputes; requirements and implementation can change through rulemaking and litigation. Track the 90-calendar-day suspension affecting subsequent initiation by the same initiating party for the same or similar items or services involving the same opposing party. Evaluate the associated post-suspension initiation provision instead of assuming every held claim becomes permanently unavailable. A cooling-off register should retain the determination date, affected counterparties, service grouping rationale, and next permitted action. Do not treat a code match alone as a complete legal test. For each proposed batch, reserve the nonrefundable administrative charge and the certified-entity fee using the applicable published schedule. The prevailing party generally receives its certified-entity fee back, while the nonprevailing party bears that fee; settlement and administrative dispositions require separate reconciliation. Budget dismissed or ineligible matters with no assumed refund until the actual disposition and applicable refund rules establish one. This conservative cash treatment prevents a pending refund receivable from disguising a loss-making book or financing the next filing prematurely.

Maintain separate fields for initial allowed payment, disclosed QPA, patient responsibility, selected offer, additional payer obligation, cleared additional cash, returned funds, administrative charges, entity charges, refunds, and casework expense. The QPA is not automatically the initial payment, and neither number automatically equals the final out-of-network rate. Use the actual initial-payment baseline when calculating incremental recovery. Define eligible contingency base as cleared incremental cash minus unrecovered dispute fees and agreed external casework costs, floored at zero. Subtract a refund from unrecovered costs only when received or otherwise contractually settled with adequate evidence. If a $20,000 selected total payment amount implies $10,000 above the established baseline but only $6,000 arrives, a $1,200 unrecovered cost balance leaves $4,800 currently eligible for sharing. At 25%, invoice $1,200 rather than $2,500. Allocate lawful patient cost sharing and prior payments before calculating that additional obligation; the example assumes both already reconciled. Provider-funded expense reimbursements are tracked separately from the operator's earned share and are not quietly added to canonical revenue. Internal labor and operating overhead then reduce operator profit without deducting the same external case cost twice. Reversals require a contractual true-up, with credit notes tied to the original claim and invoice.

## 3. Choose a settlement rail that proves receipt without creating a second collection problem  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Default to payer payment into the provider's established account, followed by a separately authorized operator invoice. This preserves the ordinary claims-payment chain and avoids turning the recovery firm into an unnecessary custodian of medical receivables. For healthcare EFT and ERA, use the adopted transaction standards and reassociation data, including the ACH CCD+ payment information and matching 835 trace information where applicable. A bank credit without allocation evidence is unresolved cash, while an 835 without a corresponding settled deposit is not collection. Reconcile both before computing the contingency share. If the payer uses a virtual card, assess processing deductions and available payment alternatives rather than letting convenience shrink the provider's net recovery invisibly. For any banking change, use a callback to a previously validated contact, dual approval, and a retained change log. Do not place clinical narratives or patient names in ACH descriptions, wire references, card receipts, or payment-platform memos. Use a restricted internal case token and maintain the protected mapping inside the authorized claim system. The BAA and secure EDI pipeline cover permitted handling of protected information; they do not authorize publishing patient details to a payment vendor that lacks the required contractual and security arrangements.

Payment rails solve different problems, and eight alternatives deserve explicit comparison. A wire accelerates a known settlement but does not cure an insolvent counterparty. ACH supports routine payer and provider transfers, but authorization, returns, bank reconciliation, and remittance linkage still matter. Escrow can reduce performance risk when both parties actually agree to a funded arrangement administered by a suitable independent custodian; federal IDR does not automatically create escrow. A retainer or expense reserve finances case preparation, not a recovery already earned. Statutory disbursement describes the payment obligation following the determination, not a distinct government-operated bank rail or public guarantee. Platform checkout is usually better suited to the provider's service invoice than to moving underlying medical benefits. Factoring exchanges a receivable for discounted liquidity and may introduce recourse, assignment restrictions, servicing duties, and UCC priority questions. Milestone holds can allocate vendor-performance risk, but a private arrangement cannot unilaterally extend the payer's statutory payment deadline. Select the smallest workable arrangement, documenting payer acceptance and the provider's treasury instructions. If the operator cannot explain who owns the funds at every stage, abandon that routing design and restore direct provider collection with a transparent service-fee invoice.

Fee protection belongs in the engagement contract and evidence trail rather than in surprise liens or unrestricted account access. Define covered inventory, exclusions, the baseline methodology, expense allocation, collection verification, invoicing dates, payment authorization, termination, and dispute resolution. A narrowly drafted collection tail may preserve fees on specifically identified work completed before termination; it should not claim every future payer dollar. Require periodic remittance exports, access to relevant settlement correspondence, and an obligation to report direct payments and offsets. Prohibit duplicate contingency charges where another recovery vendor already owns the assignment. Review applicable state restrictions on collection activity, professional fee sharing, and receivable assignments before choosing the compensation and custody structure. If the provider refuses automatic debit, use ordinary accounts payable with a clear payment period and limited audit rights. A $1,800 earned invoice paid thirty days late creates a working-capital burden, not a reason to collect from the patient or divert payer money. Suspend new elective work for persistent vendor nonpayment while preserving agreed duties on active deadlines and arranging an orderly handoff. When escrow is justified, release conditions should name the exact evidence required and an independent dispute mechanism instead of letting either party freeze all funds indefinitely.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Validate beneficiary and payment authority
   - Confirm provider ownership of the receiving account and identify the payer's authorized payment contact.
   - Use a known-number callback for changes; retain verification separately from clinical evidence.
2. Reconcile obligation against baseline
   - Translate the settlement or determination into the additional payer amount actually owed.
   - Deduct prior payments and applicable patient responsibility without replacing the actual baseline with the QPA.
3. Match cleared cash and unrecovered costs
   - Pair the bank transaction with the remittance, claim allocation, fee receipts, and received refunds.
   - Hold unmatched deposits outside the contingency calculation and record offsets or reversals explicitly.
4. Invoice and monitor both counterparties
   - Invoice the provider for the contractual share and track payer and provider balances separately.
   - Escalate overdue obligations through the appropriate contractual or regulatory route without altering patient protections.
