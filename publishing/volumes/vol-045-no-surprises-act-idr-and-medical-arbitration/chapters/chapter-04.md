*Three-part eligibility test, specified-state vs. federal jurisdiction, 30-business-day open negotiation and 4-day initiation windows, CMS Notice of IDR Initiation and Resubmission web form demos, IDR entity selection, CMS disputing-party Q&A, dispute-management software, outsourced filing services, and catching underpaid OON claims before deadlines close*

## Executive Summary

Practitioners from CMS, K&L Gates, Lido, Staffingly, Vee Healthtek, CoGenetix and ANS Solutions, and Adam Cartabiano on the Ryan Downs show agree the money in federal IDR is won or lost at the screen. Claims qualify through three doors: ED-admitted out-of-network events, out-of-network clinicians on in-network events, and emergency air ambulance. Plan funding decides whether the claim goes federal or to a state process. Open negotiation must start within 30 business days of the initial payment or denial and is mandatory proof before arbitration. Initiation then opens a four-business-day window that Staffingly calls absolute. Cartabiano targets claims paid 15 to 17 percent of charges. Staffingly reports an 88 percent win rate at roughly 450 percent of QPA, but one in five disputes is ineligible. The speakers split on clocks, IDRE strike counts, and vendor economics.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every practitioner screens before filing, and the screen starts with three qualifying buckets. Adam Cartabiano lays them out on the Ryan Downs show: a fully out-of-network event where the admit source was the emergency room, an out-of-network charge on an in-network event, and emergency air ambulance [0BGnlP4Q71M @ 00:00:23]. Cartabiano flags a common misperception. The rule is not limited to ED patients; it turns on admit source. A patient who came through the ED and matriculated to observation or inpatient status still qualifies, and inpatient is where the big dollars come into play [0BGnlP4Q71M @ 00:00:40]. The second bucket drives most of the volume in the public CMS files: anesthesiologists, ED docs, hospitalists, pathologists, radiologists and surgical groups. Team Health and SCP are named as the biggest private submitters [0BGnlP4Q71M @ 00:01:32]. K&L Gates partner Steve Pine maps the same perimeter from the statute side. It covers emergency services at an out-of-network facility including facility fees, emergency and non-emergency services by out-of-network providers inside in-network facilities, and out-of-network air ambulance. Ground ambulance is excluded [VbqFYBvnpas @ 00:01:30]. CMS presenter Amanda Brander repeats the identical three triggers as step one of the pre-IDR sequence [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:03:30].

The second consensus point is that plan funding decides the route, and getting it wrong ends the claim. Cartabiano splits the map into two kinds of states. Federal states push everything through the CMS process. In bifurcated states, fully funded plans governed by the state Department of Insurance go to the state process, while ERISA self-insured populations still follow the federal track [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:00:20]. The instruction is blunt: read the insurance card or call the payer to confirm self-insured versus fully funded, because you'll get kicked out if you send it to the wrong place [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:01:25]. Andres, CBO at Lido, has codified this fork in Lido's eligibility audit engine. A fully insured Texas plan goes to SB 1264 arbitration, and self-funded ERISA plans go federal. The team calls the full rule set quite complicated, which is why they hard-coded it [NHWPEtUOyK0 @ 00:04:44]. Brander adds the detail that keeps volume federal. Some states let self-funded plans opt into the state process, but nothing forces them to. The federal process stays open to every self-insured plan that has not opted in, and to FEHB carriers whose Office of Personnel Management contract does not direct them to a state process [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:17:56]. Plan type is therefore the first field verified, not the last.

Third, nobody skips open negotiation and nobody files everything. Brander sets the clock: open negotiation must be initiated within 30 business days, starting the day the provider receives the initial payment or denial notice. Both sides must participate in good faith and hand over the information needed if talks fail [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:05:08]. Cartabiano explains why the step is non-optional in practice. You must show proof you went through it, and if you skip it the arbitration organization rejects the claim. A payer that simply ignores you does not owe the demand; you just advance to arbitration [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:02:51]. On selection, Lido's Andres opens the Lido workflow with a two-part filter. Is the claim NSA eligible? Even if it is, is the expected award value worth the IDR route [NHWPEtUOyK0 @ 00:00:51]? Vee Healthtek turns that filter into a triage grid. Every paid out-of-network account goes into one of four bins: payment complete, recovery justified, value undetermined, or likely recovery that does not justify the cost. Each bin gets its own action: close accurate accounts, prioritize supported recovery, correct missing data before estimating the gap, and monitor low-value patterns without chasing every balance [GpHExH5dquQ @ 00:00:32].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The execution clock runs in fixed blocks. The open negotiation notice must carry dates of service, service codes, the initial payment or denial notice, an out-of-network rate offer, and sender contact details. Brander relays a payer-side complaint that the form cannot identify the customer. Providers solve it by adding their own fields or attaching an EOB; otherwise the payer reaches back for information [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:09:52]. Cartabiano anchors that offer to the in-network commercial equivalent. Audits of client organizations find a 50/50 split. The half paid around 15 to 17 percent of billed charges, against a QPA typically couched at roughly 100 to 115 percent of Medicare, is the population targeted toward 45, 50, or 55 percent of charges [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:03:38]. When the 30 business days expire without a deal, the CMS web form demo allows four business days to initiate [cw4MI_h6Gns @ 00:00:05]. Staffingly calls that window absolute: a miss forfeits the claim entirely [ASHyX7v13Eo @ 00:02:49]. Cartabiano explains why four days hurts. The arbitration package is far heavier than the single negotiation letter, and fresh claims keep rolling off their own negotiation periods while the team builds it. Without structure, the workload compounds fast.

Inside the Notice of IDR Initiation web form, the traps are mechanical. The demo warns the form must be completed in one session and times out after 60 minutes of inactivity. An open negotiation start date more than 34 business days back triggers an extension-justification prompt, and at least one upload proving that start date is mandatory [cw4MI_h6Gns @ 00:04:22]. Each line item needs a claim number, date of service, QPA, a claim-and-QPA document, cost sharing, service code, up to four two-character modifiers, and place of service. The portal blocks any duplicate combination of claim number, date, code, and modifier unless a cooling-off closure or approved extension applies [cw4MI_h6Gns @ 00:11:17]. CMS's Matt Meidell fixes the offer math. The offer is the full amount requested, including what was already paid, expressed both as a dollar figure and as a percentage of the QPA. The QPA entered should match the initial payment or denial notice [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:20:34]. Lido's Andres flags the newest bottleneck: the centralized IDR gateway no longer offers an API. Filers either key data manually or use a vendor that automates entry without one, which Lido claims to have caught ahead of the market [NHWPEtUOyK0 @ 00:06:21].

Batching is where fee savings and rejections collide. Meidell lists the four batching tests: same provider, group, or facility; same plan or issuer; same or similar service code; and all items furnished within the same 30 business day period [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:23:43]. Staffingly confirms CMS allows grouping to save fees but says improperly grouped claims are immediately rejected [ASHyX7v13Eo @ 00:02:33]. The evidence package is the other heavy lift. Cartabiano's three core elements are a demographic case positioning the organization as one that commands premium in-network rates, a clinical complexity review pulled from the medical record, and a financial comparison. That comparison lines up the in-network commercial rate, amount paid, Medicare, and Medicaid for that service and region, with specifics rather than broad strokes [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:05:47]. Cartabiano sharpens the package by meeting IDR entities directly to learn what they score as a successful argument, then building that feedback into the process. The CoGenetix and ANS Solutions team builds the same thing as standing provider profiles covering credentials, service complexity, and regional market benchmarks. They argue fellowship training and surgical complexity are powerful arbitration tools for orthopedic groups [dTrA6r3pGyE @ 00:01:54].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Ingest documents and run the eligibility audit
   - Pull the CMS-1500, 835, EOB, and insurance card into one record, then decide eligibility and route.
   - Lido ingests from email, Google Drive, OneDrive, SharePoint, and the EMR, then flags missing 835 or EOB documents that block the eligibility call and generates document requests from the same screen [Lido — NHWPEtUOyK0 @ 00:01:52]
2. Send the open negotiation notice
   - Within 30 business days of the payment or denial, send a notice carrying dates of service, service codes, the initial payment or denial, an offer, and contact details.
   - These are the required notice elements, and they should already appear on the payer's initial payment or denial of payment [CMSHHSgov — 7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:06:15]
3. Build the arbitration package during negotiation
   - Assemble the organization demographics, the clinical complexity review, and the financial rate comparison before the negotiation period lapses.
   - Cartabiano requires a clinical complexity case plus an in-network commercial versus paid versus Medicare versus Medicaid comparison for that service and region, because the four-day window leaves no room to start from scratch [Ryan Downs — 7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:06:04]
4. File in the portal and serve the payer
   - Submit the Notice of IDR Initiation, pick a preferred certified IDR entity, attest to no conflict of interest, then send the PDF to the payer.
   - The initiating party must download the PDF and send a copy to the non-initiating party on the same day the form is submitted, and both sides then receive portal confirmation emails [CMSHHSgov — cw4MI_h6Gns @ 00:15:54]

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Win-rate math is where the vendor pitches split hardest. CoGenetix and ANS Solutions open with a win rate of over 90% for providers, later quoting a 90% to 95% track record, and cite industry data showing approximately 86% of decisions favoring providers [dTrA6r3pGyE @ 00:01:16]. Staffingly runs a lower headline of 88% wins with payouts averaging 450% of the qualifying payment amount, then immediately cuts that number down: one in five disputes was deemed ineligible due to errors and yielded zero recovery [ASHyX7v13Eo @ 00:01:25]. Adam Cartabiano, appearing on Ryan Downs' mini episodes, attacks the idea that any win rate is predictable at the claim level. He says IDREs lack an objective way to evaluate each claim on its own merit, so two identical claims for the same service, submitted with identical information, can split with one won and one lost [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:09:05]. Some IDREs judge holistically from a case-rate perspective while others go through every single CPT, HCPCS and DRG code before landing on a fair number [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:08:28]. His fix is consistency: meet with the IDREs to learn what they treat as a successful argument, then build that feedback into purpose-built technology so every submission argues the same way with high confidence.

The fee-risk story depends entirely on who is talking. CoGenetix tells providers that IDR filing fees are a necessary component but are typically reimbursed by the payer because the win rate is so high, framing the whole program as a low-risk, high-reward strategy [dTrA6r3pGyE @ 00:02:11]. Staffingly paints the opposite picture at system scale, citing Georgetown University research that IDR administrative fees reached $844 million in just the first half of 2025, and argues that practices managing the workflow internally are actively losing money once labor costs and filing-error penalties are counted [ASHyX7v13Eo @ 00:00:49]. CMS staff on the disputing-party call draw the hard line: administrative fees are non-refundable, although they should not be collected until the certified IDR entity finishes its initial screening for eligibility [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:26:12]. Vee Healthtek runs a middle playbook built on pre-filing triage. It ranks inventory by net recoverable value, weighing likely additional payment, filing expense, specialist effort and time remaining, and warns that a large balance is not automatically the best opportunity when stronger evidence and cheaper collection sit elsewhere [s3194z7bdwY @ 00:01:11]. A repeated payer methodology hitting hundreds of similar claims, in its view, calls for negotiation, payment validation or an upstream correction instead of recovering the same shortfall dispute after dispute.

Operating models diverge on who carries the workload. Staffingly prices an in-house specialist at $55,000 to $75,000 a year against 32% annual billing turnover and an average 68 days to replace, then sells an outsourced, AI-augmented team for a flat $399 per week, with most practices live in 14 days [ASHyX7v13Eo @ 00:04:27]. CoGenetix goes strictly performance-based, with no upfront costs and a tiered structure, plus a complimentary out-of-network assessment to open the relationship [dTrA6r3pGyE @ 00:03:49]. Lido splits the difference, running an end-to-end IDR resolution service internally while letting customers who prefer to do the process themselves work the same eligibility data in its portal. Even the IDRE selection mechanics get described differently. Cartabiano says there are 13 IDRE vendors, soon going to 15, and that the payer can reject the provider's pick up to three times before the fourth gets assigned automatically [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:07:36]. CMS staff describe a tighter clock: the non-initiating party has three business days to agree with the preferred entity or make an alternative selection, and if the parties cannot agree, the Departments randomly select a certified IDR entity no later than six business days after initiation and notify both sides by email [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:16:43].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive failures happen on the clock, not in front of the arbitrator. Staffingly calls the post-negotiation filing slot an absolute four business day window and says missing it forfeits the claim entirely [ASHyX7v13Eo @ 00:02:49]. Cartabiano explains why teams blow it: the open negotiation letter is one form with a small amount of information, but the arbitration submission needs hospital demographic positioning, a clinical complexity review pulled from the medical record, and a financial comparison of in-network commercial, paid, Medicare and Medicaid rates, all while new cases keep running off their own negotiation periods [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:05:47]. Skipping open negotiation is fatal too, because the arbitration organization rejects the claim without proof that the first step happened [7lqb5ev54mA @ 00:02:51]. Venue mistakes kill files before the clock even matters. In bifurcated states, fully funded plans governed by the state Department of Insurance go to the state process while self-insured ERISA populations stay federal, and Cartabiano warns that sending a claim to the wrong place gets it kicked out. Lido codifies the same split, routing fully insured Texas plans to SB 1264 arbitration and self-funded ERISA plans to federal IDR [NHWPEtUOyK0 @ 00:04:44].

The CMS web form demos surface a second layer of traps that cost operators real filings. The Notice of IDR Initiation form must be completed in a single session and times out after 60 minutes of inactivity; it requires at least one upload proving the open negotiation start date, and if that date is more than 34 business days before the current date it forces an extension justification [cw4MI_h6Gns @ 00:04:22]. Answering that the provider received patient consent to waive surprise billing protections makes the dispute ineligible, and the form blocks duplicate line items sharing the same claim number, date of service, service code and modifiers [cw4MI_h6Gns @ 00:11:17]. After submission the burden flips to the filer: CMS staff say the initiation notice will not be available in the portal, so the initiating party must send the copy to the non-initiating party directly [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:15:33]. On the resubmission form, the filer must hit Print PDF before Continue Submitting because there is no way back to that confirmation page afterwards, and the initiating party alone is responsible for confirming every regrouped line item still meets batching and bundling criteria [Vy31vkzfNBE @ 00:09:48]. The original demo adds that the PDF copy goes to the non-initiating party on the same day the form is submitted.

Economics checks fail quietly upstream. Vee Healthtek warns that a paid out-of-network claim can still leave recoverable money behind because payment status confirms that money moved, not that the amount was accurate; automatic adjustments hide variances before a specialist ever looks, and a $200 recoverable variance across 5,000 claims is $1 million before fees and collection costs [GpHExH5dquQ @ 00:01:25]. Its second video adds that recovery can fail before arbitration begins when a low payment posts as final and the opportunity closes before anyone takes responsibility [s3194z7bdwY @ 00:00:00]. Lido shows the document version of that failure: a missing 835 and EOB block a final eligibility call even when the CMS-1500 lines up with the NSA rules [NHWPEtUOyK0 @ 00:02:50]. Inside the process, CMS staff spell out a default trap: an offer is not considered received until both the certified IDR entity fee and the administrative fee are paid, and if one side's offer is not received the entity selects the other party's offer as the final payment amount [7lzQt8aAlG0 @ 00:25:17]. They also want the offer stated as the full requested amount including anything already paid, expressed as a dollar figure and a percentage of the QPA, and the QPA entered should be the one listed on the initial payment or denial notice.
