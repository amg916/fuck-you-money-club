## 1. Assign twelve play lanes to four accountable seats  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the starting pod around one full-time owner, one acquisition contractor, one boarding and residual-control contractor, and one ATM field-service contractor. The owner retains sponsor relationships, commercial exceptions, treasury authority, and final underwriting submissions; the sponsor makes its own underwriting decisions. For this operating design, organize the volume's twelve mapped plays into explicit execution lanes rather than implying that separate businesses require separate hires: wholesale Schedule A negotiation, conventional merchant placement, properly disclosed dual pricing, gateway markup attachment, approved high-risk placement, dispute deflection, residual recovery, retention and repricing, exclusive ATM venue acquisition, ATM deployment, telemetry-led service, and surcharge optimization. These are working lane labels, not additional scorecard revenue categories. Route every opportunity through IRIS CRM using merchant or terminal identifiers, sponsor ownership, signed commercial terms, and a named accountable seat. High-risk MID orchestration means matching disclosed activity to sponsor-approved processing arrangements, never cycling identities around monitoring or termination. Give contractors the minimum access their deliverables require; nobody needs unrestricted merchant exports merely to schedule a terminal installation. The owner should see one exception queue, not twelve inboxes. Maintain a written backup for each critical function so a contractor absence does not interrupt settlements, incident response, or venue service.

Use deliverable-based contractor agreements with scope, pricing, evidence, response windows, confidentiality, and termination assistance. A label alone does not establish independent-contractor status; examine actual control, economic dependence, and applicable state tests before imposing employee-style schedules or exclusivity. As an illustrative acquisition arrangement, pay $150 after an approved merchant produces its first reconciled residual, then 10% of that merchant's eligible collected contribution for twelve months. Define contribution after contractually senior processor deductions and specified direct servicing costs, and exclude taxes, merchant settlements, reserves, and vault cash. Document treatment of reversals and post-termination commissions rather than inventing clawbacks after a dispute. The boarding specialist can quote a monthly capacity retainer plus accepted-file fees; the field contractor can quote scheduled visits, emergency dispatch, and parts separately. Require a complete boarding packet within two business days of receiving complete inputs, a critical ATM alert acknowledgment within thirty minutes during purchased coverage, and geographically realistic restoration targets. These are proposed service terms, not universal industry standards. Pay accepted invoices against evidence, while rejecting only documented defects under the agreement. Contractor compensation must fit the scenario expense envelope; it cannot become an extra deduction below the stated owner profit.

At scale, keep the founder as the first full-time seat and add an operations lead as the second. Retain acquisition, boarding and residual control, and ATM field service as three contractor seats; add an independent bookkeeping and quality assurance contractor as the fourth. Six people still operate all twelve lanes, but the operations lead owns queue throughput and coverage while the independent reviewer tests collections, access changes, and payout calculations. At four people, the owner performs that review and buys only bounded specialist engagements inside the existing contractor scopes. Do not represent a contractor firm's unlimited technicians as six named people; disclose subcontractor dependencies and their costs separately. Hire against trailing workload, quality failures, and cash coverage rather than forecasted merchant volume. A useful planning trigger is eight consecutive weeks of excessive service backlog despite documented automation, paired with six months of funded incremental seat cost. Make the hiring calculation after replacement coverage and working-capital needs. Customer relationships, sponsor permissions, equipment records, and operating instructions belong in company-controlled systems. The owner should be able to take a two-week absence without becoming the emergency password vault or sole interpreter of a residual statement.

## 2. Run a weekly evidence scorecard and enforce concentration limits  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Close the weekly operating scorecard every Monday using a dated snapshot rather than a live dashboard that silently changes after the meeting. The owner reviews collected retained revenue, accrued retained revenue, active revenue-producing merchants, merchant retention, residual yield, gateway attachment, dispute exceptions, ATM availability, service backlog, and concentration. Define an active merchant as one generating an eligible residual during the measurement period; report approved but inactive MIDs separately. Calculate revenue retention on the opening merchant cohort, separating churn, repricing, volume changes, and recovered billing errors. A weekly view can flag movements, while monthly and trailing-quarter views absorb statement timing. Express merchant yield as retained ISO revenue divided by eligible processed volume, multiplied by 10,000 basis points; that denominator is activity, never recognized operator revenue. For ATMs, report retained surcharge contribution per completed withdrawal and availability during contracted venue hours. Exclude planned venue closure only when the service agreement supports that treatment, and show the exclusion. Assign every red metric an owner, a dated correction, and evidence needed for closure. Track backlog age alongside ticket count so clearing easy tickets cannot conceal an unresolved settlement discrepancy or stranded terminal.

Set a portfolio policy that no customer group and no processor or sponsor dependency contributes more than 25% of trailing-twelve-month retained revenue. Apply the same ceiling to prospective acquired portfolios before funding, and calculate it on combined books after closing. Group related merchant entities under common control; group venue contracts controlled by the same landlord or hospitality operator. For counterparties, inspect the underlying sponsor and processing chain because several reseller brands can share one failure point. If dependencies overlap, test each exposure independently rather than forcing all categories to sum to 100%. Four equal independent relationships barely satisfy a 25% ceiling and provide no growth buffer, so aim below 20% when practical. Early portfolios may mathematically fail this policy; flag that exception openly and restrict distributions or additional concentrated growth until diversification catches up. Do not claim that dividing a merchant into additional MIDs diversifies exposure. Add a contribution-based concentration view because a low-revenue venue can produce disproportionate profit. Measure vault-cash provider and field-service dependencies separately using cash exposure and covered-terminal counts, with backup arrangements rather than misleading revenue percentages. Document remediation owners and actual alternative capacity.

Make contractor quality measurable without pretending that every operating metric has a statutory threshold. Target at least 98% first-pass completeness on boarding files, reconciliation of every received monthly residual statement within five business days, and zero unexplained contractor payout differences older than thirty days. These are internal operating targets. Dispute and fraud thresholds must come from the currently applicable sponsor and card-brand program, with internal warning levels set sufficiently earlier to permit action. Use Verifi RDR and Ethoca only where enrolled and supported; measure resolved cases, refunded dollars, vendor fees, and duplicate credits rather than celebrating an unqualified deflection count. A refund can prevent escalation while still reducing contribution. For ATM service, distinguish communications outages, dispenser faults, cash exhaustion, and venue access failures because each requires a different remedy and responsible party. Sample installed pricing screens and merchant-facing receipts after configuration changes. Debit and prepaid transactions must not be swept into credit-card surcharge configurations, and state restrictions need a maintained location matrix. MATCH-related screening and escalation belong with authorized sponsor processes; the pod must never market another MID as a way around a termination. Keep evidence available for review without storing sensitive authentication data.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the weekly evidence set
   - Export IRIS CRM queues, processor statement updates, ATM alerts, and bank receipts into a dated company-controlled folder.
   - Preserve source identifiers and extraction times; separate monthly statement lag from genuine operational deterioration.
2. Reconcile and calculate
   - Bridge accruals to collections, refresh cohort retention, and recalculate customer and counterparty exposure.
   - The preparing contractor records assumptions; the owner or independent reviewer tests material differences and payout changes.
3. Assign bounded corrections
   - Hold a thirty-minute owner-led review that assigns one accountable seat and completion date to each material exception.
   - Use sponsor-specific escalation thresholds, venue response commitments, and the 25% exposure policy rather than improvised judgments.
4. Close with proof
   - Attach the corrected statement, accepted boarding packet, restored terminal log, or signed amendment before closing an issue.
   - Carry unresolved items into the next frozen scorecard and escalate repeat failures through the contracted remediation process.

## 3. Build accrual books and transferable contractual rights  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the scorecard's gross revenue label as the supplied operator-revenue total on a net retained basis, not merchant gross billings. Conservative revenue of $90,000 less $63,000 of modeled expenses produces $27,000 owner profit at 30%. Base revenue of $445,000 less $255,875 produces $189,125 at 42.5%. Scaled revenue of $800,000 less $360,000 produces $440,000 at 55%. Keep the stated teams attached to those cases: one full-time seat plus three contractors for Conservative and Base, and two full-time seats plus four contractors for Scaled. The corresponding monthly revenue averages are $7,500, approximately $37,083.33, and approximately $66,666.67. They are annual averages, not promises of smooth monthly collections. The separate $1,500–$10,000 monthly operating range is a planning reference, not a ceiling on all expenses in the larger modeled cases; base and scaled implied total expenses exceed it. Document the scope difference instead of forcing the numbers to fit. Under ASC 606, evaluate principal-versus-agent presentation for each performance obligation. The supplied retained-revenue basis must not receive another invented retained-share haircut. Merchant settlement funds and ATM vault cash remain outside recognized operator revenue and owner profit.

Create separate ledger accounts for ISO residuals, gateway markups, ATM surcharges, receivables, sponsor reserves, contractor commissions, venue payments, field service, software, and cash-custody balances where applicable. Record earned residuals using contract terms and supported estimates when statements arrive after month-end, then true up against actual statements. Distinguish a collectible timing difference from a disputed receivable that requires an allowance or write-off under the accounting policy. Preserve the bridge from gross statement components through contractual deductions to retained operator revenue. If a venue share or upstream commission already reduces the reported retained amount, do not expense it again. Conversely, commissions incurred by the operator and not already netted belong in expenses. Maintain merchant and terminal cohorts by activation month, acquisition source, sponsor, venue group, contract version, and cancellation rights. Review three-, six-, and twelve-month contribution after direct servicing costs, not just headline processing volume. Reconcile terminal transaction counts, surcharge rates, reversals, and venue splits to cash receipts. Vault cash movements need their own ownership and custody reconciliation; cash dispensed is not a service expense merely because it leaves a cassette. Support every material estimate with a preparer, reviewer, source document, and subsequent settlement check.

Transferability begins before the first contractor receives system access. Obtain signed intellectual-property assignments for original scripts, training materials, configuration documentation, and other copyrightable deliverables; 17 U.S.C. section 204(a) generally requires a signed writing for a copyright ownership transfer. Do not assume every commissioned work qualifies as work made for hire. Inventory pre-existing contractor tools and obtain licenses broad enough for continued operation and the contemplated transaction. Keep domains, telemetry accounts, CRM administration, vendor subscriptions, and recovery credentials under company control, subject to vendor transfer terms. Merchant agreements, ISO contracts, and venue leases require separate review of assignment, change of control, termination, residual survival, exclusivity, and solicitation restrictions. UCC section 9-408 can limit certain restrictions affecting security interests, but it does not generally compel a counterparty to accept a buyer's performance or provide system access. Capture required consents as named transaction dependencies, not assumed rights. Maintain ATM disclosure evidence under 12 CFR 1005.16, including the fee amount presented before commitment and the consumer's election to continue. Evaluate actual ATM activities against FinCEN's money-services-business definitions; cash dispensing alone does not establish that every operator is a money transmitter.

## 4. Price transferable cash flow, then choose sale or continued ownership  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use valuation ranges as negotiating models, not claims about current transaction comparables. An illustrative small owner-operated book might be evaluated at two to four times normalized annual seller discretionary earnings; an illustrative residual-stream proposal might quote twenty-four to thirty-six times eligible monthly residual contribution. These bases are different and must never be added together for the same cash flow. Buyers define eligible residuals differently, sometimes before servicing costs, so reconstruct the actual purchased economics from the term sheet. Applying the earnings sensitivity mechanically to Base owner profit of $189,125 gives $378,250–$756,500 before normalization. Conservative yields $54,000–$108,000, and Scaled yields $880,000–$1,760,000. These are illustrative enterprise-value sensitivities, not equity proceeds or verified market prices. Owner profit is not automatically EBITDA or seller discretionary earnings: reconcile owner compensation, recurring labor, taxes, interest, depreciation, and claimed add-backs before selecting a basis. A buyer needing an additional $90,000 annual replacement operator would reduce the Base earnings basis to $99,125 only if that replacement cost is not already captured. At the same illustrative range, that adjusted basis supports $198,250–$396,500. Operational independence is therefore an earnings issue, not cosmetic documentation.

Build a buyer data room that follows money from each surviving contract to each collected dollar. Include twenty-four months of statements where available, monthly accrual bridges, cohort retention, sponsor pricing history, dispute outcomes, concentration analysis, terminal ownership, venue rights, contractor agreements, intellectual-property assignments, and security incident records. For a younger portfolio, disclose the shorter operating history and price that uncertainty rather than manufacturing a full track record. Segment high-risk merchants, short-dated venue contracts, terminable residual arrangements, and accounts awaiting sponsor consent. A buyer can then exclude, discount, or condition those assets explicitly. Define earnouts using named cohorts, collected eligible residuals, allowed deductions, reporting access, dispute procedures, and treatment of buyer-directed repricing. Otherwise, a headline multiple can conceal an uncollectible deferred balance. Distinguish enterprise value from equity proceeds by addressing debt, transaction costs, working-capital adjustments, escrows, and separately owned cash. Do not sell a cash-loading provider's vault cash as company property. Release detailed merchant information only through authorized diligence channels with appropriate access restrictions. Transfer of a customer relationship does not automatically authorize unrestricted movement of payment credentials or vendor-controlled data. Stage access, log downloads, and revoke permissions when a bidder exits.

A premium requires reduced uncertainty that a buyer can verify: durable residual rights, consent-ready assignments, diversified relationships, stable cohorts, and a pod that works without founder intervention. Test that claim with a documented two-week owner absence before considering a sale, while preserving emergency authority and sponsor-required contacts. The same discipline supports a cash-cow strategy. Compare after-tax sale proceeds and reinvestment alternatives with realistic future distributions after replacement labor, equipment refresh, working capital, and adverse attrition. Do not simply divide enterprise value by unadjusted owner profit and call the result a payback period. Stress the loss of the largest permitted customer and largest processor dependency separately, then test their joint loss where correlated. At a 25% revenue exposure, lost profit depends on avoidable costs and contractual survival, not a flat 25% reduction in earnings. Keep the twelve lanes visible during this exercise: acquisition and pricing create revenue, approved boarding and dispute operations protect continuity, residual controls protect collections, and exclusive venues plus reliable ATM service sustain local economics. Sell when the offered risk-adjusted proceeds outperform continued ownership on your documented assumptions; retain when the pod reliably produces cash without hidden founder labor or concentrated fragility.
