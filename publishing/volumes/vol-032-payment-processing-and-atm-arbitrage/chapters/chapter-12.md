## 1. Days 1–7: Establish Sponsor Access, Twelve Play IDs, and a Cash Boundary  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start Day 1 by making the twelve plays explicit workstreams rather than twelve simultaneous launches. This chapter's operating map is P01 wholesale Schedule A negotiation; P02 interchange-plus merchant acquisition; P03 gateway markup attachment; P04 permitted credit-card surcharge deployment; P05 genuine dual-pricing deployment; P06 sponsor-approved higher-risk MID placement; P07 residual audit recovery; P08 retention and repricing; P09 Verifi RDR or Ethoca dispute deflection; P10 exclusive ATM venue acquisition; P11 ATM surcharge yield management; and P12 telemetry and vault-cash control. These are operating labels for the twelve mapped plays, not twelve independent revenue forecasts. Activate P01, P02, P03, P07, P10, and P12 first; the remaining plays require an eligible merchant, actual dispute exposure, a renewal opportunity, or measured ATM traffic. Assign the full-time operator forty weekly hours across selling, sponsor coordination, and decisions. Contractor one receives eight hours for boarding and evidence control; contractor two receives six for integrations and terminal configuration; contractor three receives six for ATM field service and cash-provider coordination. That sixty-hour weekly pod must also handle support. Reserve twelve of those hours for exceptions, and stop adding installations whenever unresolved incidents consume that reserve. No contractor receives unrestricted merchant banking access.

Use Days 2–4 to replace attractive sales language with enforceable economics. Request the sponsor or upstream ISO agreement, current Schedule A, residual ownership and vesting language, termination treatment, reserve rights, clawbacks, and the actual underwriting checklist. Confirm who registers the business with each relevant card network and what activities the agreement authorizes. An unsigned reseller arrangement is not permission to present as a registered ISO. For each merchant quote, separate interchange and network assessments from processor costs, the merchant-facing markup, and the retained operator share. For each ATM proposal, identify terminal ownership, vault-cash ownership, venue commission, switch fees, replenishment costs, and servicing responsibility. P06 does not authorize splitting one business across unrelated MIDs to escape monitoring; submit the true business model and beneficial owners through the sponsor's process. Document MATCH screening responsibility and the sponsor's current dispute thresholds without inventing a universal safe percentage. P04 and P05 require separate pricing designs: debit restrictions under 15 U.S.C. 1693o-2 do not create a general permission to surcharge debit cards. Require the processor's supported tender logic and jurisdiction-specific price presentation before either play reaches a live terminal. Keep rejected configurations as regression-test cases for every subsequent software update.

On Days 5–7, freeze the launch budget and evidence architecture. The illustrative capital package is $20,000 of unrestricted operating cash plus $5,000 of separately controlled vault cash, a $25,000 startup within the canonical $8,000–$45,000 range. Cap Days 1–30 operating cash deployment at $6,000: $2,000 equipment and installation, $1,000 sponsor and software setup, $1,800 contractor work, $600 travel and prospecting, and $600 contingency. Unused categories remain unspent; a contingency allocation is not a purchasing instruction. Limit Days 31–60 and 61–90 to another $4,000 each, preserving $6,000 operating liquidity before receipts. This cash schedule includes equipment purchases and therefore differs from recurring expense recognition. Vault principal never funds payroll, and merchant settlement balances never enter the operator's budget. Configure IRIS CRM with play ID, sponsor, consented contact source, underwriting status, quoted economics, and next evidence date. Store sensitive boarding documents only in the sponsor-approved intake system; use CRM references rather than copies of identity records. Create a separate ATM ledger linking terminal identifier, venue contract, dispenser balance, settlement receivable, and cash-service ticket. Export weekly evidence before changing any pricing or residual assumptions.

## 2. Days 8–14: Build a Qualified Pipeline and Prove Installation Readiness  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 8–10 are a narrow acquisition sprint, not a mass solicitation exercise. Select one merchant vertical the sponsor accepts and one ATM venue archetype with observable cash demand. Build thirty named merchant prospects and ten venue prospects using public business information and introductions. A qualified merchant has an authorized decision-maker, an eligible business model, a documented pricing problem, and willingness to provide a current statement through the approved channel. A qualified venue has a decision-maker, viable power and communications, an accessible placement position, and evidence of cash demand beyond an owner's estimate. Target ten completed merchant discovery conversations and four venue walkthroughs by Day 14; these are internal workload targets, not expected close rates. Use P02 and P03 as the default offer, presenting P04 or P05 only when the merchant's operating environment supports them. Capture the current effective processing cost, transaction count, average ticket, card mix, and recurring gateway charges. Do not compare an interchange-plus quote against a blended headline rate while ignoring per-item costs. For venues, record operating hours, nearby competing ATMs, existing cash-back options, seasonality, and physical access constraints. Photograph the proposed equipment position only with venue permission and exclude customers.

Days 11–12 convert interest into complete, truthful submissions. The boarding contractor checks the sponsor's required identity, ownership, bank-account, website, refund-policy, and fulfillment evidence, while the operator resolves commercial questions. Submit three complete merchant files by Day 14 if the qualified pipeline supports them; never manufacture completeness to meet the target. Record the sponsor's receipt timestamp and distinguish submitted, accepted for review, approved, installed, and first funded transaction. A merchant is not activated merely because IRIS CRM shows a signed application. Higher-risk placement under P06 stays inactive unless the sponsor expressly accepts that actual business model, descriptor, settlement structure, and reserve terms. For P09, ask whether Verifi RDR or Ethoca integration is available through the processor and determine account eligibility, event pricing, duplicate-event handling, and refund authority. Do not assume every alert prevents a dispute or that automation removes chargeback exposure. Configure refund limits and reconcile processor outcomes before expanding automation. The contractor may collect missing records but cannot edit merchant facts to satisfy underwriting. Escalate inconsistencies to the sponsor, record the response, and close the opportunity when the business cannot substantiate its activities. Protecting the upstream relationship outweighs one attractive residual.

Days 13–14 establish ATM site economics and deployment readiness without ordering equipment for an unproven location. Obtain two written venue proposals covering exclusivity, term, commission calculation, access, power, network availability, early termination, equipment removal, and responsibility for damage. Exclusivity must identify its actual scope; a vague promise does not prevent competing cash-back or another terminal elsewhere on the property. Build a downside transaction estimate from observed demand and comparable evidence, then obtain the cash provider's service frequency and minimum charge. Test the ATM fee-disclosure flow required by 12 CFR 1005.16: disclose the fee on screen or paper before commitment and obtain the consumer's election to continue. Do not substitute an exterior sticker for the required transaction-stage disclosure. Evaluate P12 alerts for low cash, offline status, dispenser errors, and tamper events, with named responders and after-hours contact coverage. FinCEN treatment depends on actual activities under 31 CFR 1010.100(ff); a conventional withdrawal ATM is not automatically an MSB solely because it dispenses cash. Expanded transfer or exchange services require a fresh activity assessment. The operator closes Week 2 with a documented readiness decision, not a ceremonial installation deadline.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Four Tactics That Preserve Residuals and Avoid Expensive Installations**
> 
> Use contract detail and transaction evidence to improve the pilot before adding prospect volume.
> - Ask for an anonymized sample residual statement before signing Schedule A; locate every fee code and confirm whether gateway revenue shares use a different calculation.
> - Ask merchants about their latest repricing notice and unresolved support incident; a documented switching reason is stronger qualification than a generic savings promise.
> - Count venue cash requests during more than one operating period; weekend-only observations can materially overstate weekday withdrawal demand.
> - Negotiate ATM removal access and commission calculation together; exclusivity has little value when a weak location traps equipment behind an uncooperative counterparty.

## 3. Days 15–21: Activate Only Approved Accounts and Reconcile Every Dollar  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Days 15–17 run controlled activation only where external approvals and contracts are complete. Candidate #32 has a canonical one-to-four-month time to first deal, so a ready pipeline without a live account can still be a valid first-month outcome. Do not force a transaction through an unapproved MID to make the calendar look successful. For each approved merchant, the integration contractor verifies descriptor, terminal identifiers, authorized tender types, refund behavior, receipt content, and funding instructions using the processor's supported test procedures. Test P04 and P05 separately; a cash-discount label does not repair an incorrectly implemented surcharge. Confirm the relevant state rule, including New York General Business Law section 518 where applicable, against the displayed total price. The operator owns the commercial explanation, while the boarding contractor retains evidence of merchant training and support contacts. For an approved ATM location, the field contractor records installation, safe access, communications, fee-screen behavior, and opening cash count with the cash custodian. Perform controlled withdrawal and reversal checks through the approved switch process. Do not store PIN data or full card credentials in tickets. Any cash discrepancy, misleading fee display, or misdirected settlement suspends that installation until corrected.

Days 18–19 separate pilot contribution from the annual scorecard. Consider an illustrative merchant processing $10,000 monthly with a retained twenty-basis-point spread: $10,000 multiplied by 0.002 produces $20, and an $8 retained gateway markup produces $28 operator revenue. With $3 incremental servicing cost, monthly contribution is $25; $150 acquisition spending therefore requires six months of stable contribution to recover. Merchant card sales are not operator revenue. For an illustrative agency-style ATM contract, 200 monthly withdrawals at $3.50 produce $700 customer surcharges. If the contract analysis supports excluding $175 of venue entitlement and $100 of processor entitlement from operator revenue, retained revenue is $425. Deduct $120 cash servicing and $30 maintenance to obtain $275 location contribution before shared overhead. Actual gross-versus-net presentation requires the ASC 606 principal-agent assessment; do not net every cost simply because cash passes through a settlement account. Label these unit examples as planning assumptions until statements validate them. P07 compares expected entitlements with processor reports, while P11 tests whether surcharge changes improve contribution without destroying withdrawals. Count completed, settled withdrawals rather than balance inquiries, reversals, or attempted transactions.

Days 20–21 reconcile the scorecard and operating dashboard without pretending that pilot receipts establish an annual run rate. Formula v2.0 uses $90,000 retained annual operator revenue at a 30% net margin for $27,000 owner profit, leaving $63,000 annual costs. The Base case is $445,000 at 42.5%, producing $189,125 profit and $255,875 costs. The Scaled case is $800,000 at 55%, producing $440,000 profit and $360,000 costs. Although the canonical scorecard labels its revenue field gross revenue, its specified basis here is retained ISO residuals, gateway markups, and ATM surcharges, not merchant settlements or vault principal. Preserve that basis in every comparison. Conservative and Base use one full-time operator plus three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time staff plus four contractors. Do not attribute the Scaled result to the launch pod. The canonical $1,500–$10,000 monthly operating range is a startup planning reference, not a ceiling on every annual scenario's total costs. Keep launch cash deployment, recurring expenses, direct costs, and capital purchases in separate columns. Track accrued residuals separately from cash received because processor payment timing can leave profitable-looking growth unable to pay next month's contractor invoices.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the entitlement snapshot
   - Export the effective Schedule A, merchant pricing, gateway terms, and ATM venue shares.
   - Attach effective dates and identifiers so later price changes cannot rewrite the original expected residual.
2. Match activity to settlement evidence
   - Join merchant and terminal identifiers to processor reports, ATM switch records, and cash receipts.
   - Separate refunds, reversals, chargebacks, reserves, and settlement timing differences before calculating revenue.
3. Recalculate retained revenue and direct contribution
   - Apply the documented presentation basis and subtract actual incremental servicing costs.
   - Investigate any unexplained difference exceeding either one percent of expected revenue or $25 for the account and period.
4. Close exceptions and authorize the next cohort
   - Assign each discrepancy an owner, processor ticket, due date, and cash exposure.
   - Reconcile physical ATM cash independently; monetary tolerances for residual reviews never excuse an unexplained cash shortage.

## 4. Days 22–30 and Day-60/90 Reviews: Kill Broken Routes, Fix Bottlenecks, Scale Evidence  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Days 22–24 turn observations into a renewal and exception system. The operator interviews every activated merchant and venue decision-maker, logging unresolved funding, pricing, receipt, and service issues. P08 captures the actual reason an account might leave; do not answer every complaint with a permanent price concession. The boarding contractor reconciles available settlement evidence, and the integration contractor reviews P09 outcomes for duplicate alerts or unintended refunds. Days 25–27 rehearse a terminal outage, a low-cash alert, and a missing residual line using test notifications rather than disrupting customers. Specify who acknowledges each alert and who may dispatch service or change configuration. Target acknowledgment within thirty minutes during contracted coverage and document after-hours arrangements separately. Days 28–29 produce the decision packet: cash spent and committed, qualified pipeline, sponsor status, live accounts, first funding evidence, direct contribution, support hours, and unresolved incidents. Report cohort denominators alongside conversion and attrition percentages. One lost account out of five is twenty percent, but that observation does not establish a mature annual attrition rate. Day 30 is a resource decision, not a demand that an external underwriting queue conform to the operator's calendar. Preserve source evidence for the next two reviews.

Apply the decision matrix in precedence order: Kill conditions override Fix, and every Scale condition must hold together. Kill means stop the affected route, account, or location, cancel avoidable future spending, and execute contractual closure; it does not mean abandon merchant support or cash reconciliation. Kill immediately for fabricated boarding information, unauthorized processing, unresolved diversion of settlement funds, or an operating model the sponsor rejects. A cash custody incident freezes the affected ATM pending investigation rather than triggering an unexplained write-off. Fix means a named defect, a measurable repair, a maximum fourteen-day window, and no expansion spending on that defect's route. At Day 30, Scale authorizes only another bounded acquisition cohort: ten qualified merchant prospects, three complete sponsor submissions, two written venue proposals, reproducible unit economics, and spending within $6,000. It does not require a live deal or authorize speculative machine inventory. At Day 60, require five funded merchants, one operating ATM, a reconciled settlement cycle for each live route, positive direct contribution, and merchant acquisition payback no longer than nine months. These are management-selected thresholds, not network standards. Sponsor-specific risk limits remain independent operating constraints.

At Day 90, authorize a larger cohort only after ten funded merchants and two live ATMs have produced at least two reconciled settlement cycles per route, including a complete residual period for merchants. Require positive contribution for every location, merchant acquisition payback within nine months, ATM installed-capital payback within eighteen months, and positive aggregate contribution in both latest completed months. Measure ATM payback against equipment, installation, and acquisition spending; report recoverable vault principal separately and still charge its actual funding cost. Require at least ninety percent merchant retention among accounts active sixty days earlier, with the cohort count displayed. Smaller or younger cohorts remain in Fix for insufficient evidence rather than receiving a fabricated retention score. Increase the next acquisition cohort by no more than twenty-five percent, rounded down, and keep total Day-90 operating cash deployment within $14,000 unless a separately documented capital decision changes the plan. Do not hire toward the six-person Scaled team until observed support demand and collected contribution can carry the added cost. If no deal has closed because a credible sponsor process remains within the stated one-to-four-month window, extend a capped validation period; do not call that Scale.
