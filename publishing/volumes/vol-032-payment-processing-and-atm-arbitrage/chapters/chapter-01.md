## 1. Define the Distribution Business Before Buying a Residual Stream  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

An independent sales organization distributes acquiring services under contracts with processors and sponsoring banks. The merchant accepts card payments; the acquirer settles them; the ISO recruits, boards, integrates, and supports the merchant in exchange for an agreed residual. Schedule A defines the ISO's buy-side economics, including processor markups, authorization charges, gateway charges, minimums, and other deductions. Interchange and network assessments are generally pass-through costs, not a secret spread the ISO can eliminate. The durable opportunity is contractual distribution arbitrage: acquire and retain merchants more efficiently than competing channels while delivering reliable service. An ATM operator applies a related model to physical access, contracting with venues, deploying machines, arranging cash replenishment, and retaining an agreed portion of withdrawal fees. Neither model is riskless arbitrage. Merchant churn, processor repricing, cash outages, equipment failures, reserves, and contract termination can erase the spread. A distributor must therefore understand who controls pricing, who owns the merchant relationship, and which residual rights survive departure. Begin with one merchant vertical or one serviceable ATM route rather than a national collection of unrelated accounts. Concentrated operating knowledge usually beats a superficially impressive volume total during the first year.

The revenue boundary is the first underwriting discipline. Merchant card settlement volume belongs to merchants and passes through the acquiring system; ATM withdrawal principal is returned vault cash, not operator sales. For this scorecard, recognized operator revenue means net retained ISO processing residuals, gateway markups, and ATM surcharges. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis to each promised service: an agent records its fee, while a principal's presentation depends on control before transfer. Do not assume that every ATM venue commission must always be netted under every contractual arrangement. Maintain a bridge from statutory accounts to this chapter's retained-revenue management basis whenever presentation differs. For example, 10,000 withdrawals carrying a $3.00 customer surcharge generate $30,000 of collected fees. If contractual venue and processing allocations consume $1.60 per withdrawal, the modeled retained fee is $14,000 before servicing and overhead. Separately, 10,000 withdrawals of $80 recycle $800,000 of cash principal without creating $800,000 of revenue. Reconcile transaction counts, fee collections, partner allocations, cash movements, and settlement timing independently. A residual report showing a large settlement total is not evidence of earned revenue or available owner distributions.

Five archetypes describe the operating choices: neighborhood merchant ISO, vertical software-integrated distributor, specialist higher-risk ISO, exclusive-venue ATM operator, and acquired residual-book steward. Each requires a different moat, despite sharing fee-based economics. A restaurant ISO wins through installation competence and outage response; a software distributor wins through useful workflow integration; a higher-risk specialist wins through accurate underwriting and stable approved activity. An ATM operator wins through venue rights and cash availability, while a book buyer wins through retention and enforceable assignment. The scorecard's $8,000–$45,000 startup range supports focused entry, not an unlimited acquisition budget or a fully funded ATM estate. Its $1,500–$10,000 monthly operating range describes an entry operating envelope, not a ceiling on the annual costs in every scenario. Time to first deal is 1–4 months, and collected residuals can arrive later. Use IRIS CRM for boarding status and residual reconciliation where supported, but retain original agreements and settlement exports outside a dashboard. Contractors can handle installation, field servicing, and specialist accounting; the owner still owns sponsor relationships, merchant retention, and cash controls. Outsourcing tasks does not transfer contractual accountability to a software vendor.

## 2. Reconcile the Three Profit Cases to Retained Fees  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Formula v2.0 supplies fixed annual outcomes, not a license to inflate the revenue denominator. Conservative revenue is $90,000 at a 30% net margin, producing $27,000 owner profit and implying $63,000 of annual costs. Base revenue is $445,000 at 42.5%, producing $189,125 owner profit and $255,875 of costs. Scaled revenue is $800,000 at 55%, producing $440,000 owner profit and $360,000 of costs. Teams are respectively 1 FT + 3 C, 1 FT + 3 C, and 2 FT + 4 C; contractor counts describe people, not full-time equivalents or equal compensation. Although the supplied scorecard labels its top line gross revenue, its specified basis is already retained operator fees. Do not subtract processor and venue allocations twice or add merchant settlement volume to that top line. These profits preserve the scorecard's cost convention; they do not independently establish owner salary treatment, tax distributions, or acquisition financing terms. Build those cash-flow schedules separately before deciding how much money can safely leave the business. A profitable statement can coexist with restricted reserves, delayed residual payments, equipment purchases, and a substantial vault-cash requirement.

Illustrative production bridges make the fixed totals testable without pretending to reproduce undisclosed engine inputs. Conservative can comprise $60 million of annual merchant volume yielding a retained 10 basis points, or $60,000, plus 30,000 ATM withdrawals retaining $1.00 each, or $30,000. Base can comprise $275 million yielding 10 retained basis points, or $275,000; 250 gateway accounts retaining $10 monthly, or $30,000; and 100,000 ATM withdrawals retaining $1.40 each, or $140,000. Together these equal exactly $445,000. Scaled can comprise $600 million yielding 10 retained basis points, or $600,000; 500 gateway accounts retaining $10 monthly, or $60,000; and the same $140,000 ATM contribution, totaling $800,000. That bridge deliberately scales the merchant channel rather than assuming every revenue stream doubles. Gateway markups must be excluded from the basis-point residual calculation if counted separately. Similarly, the ATM retained fee must already reflect the modeled partner allocations. These are annual production equivalents, not promises about merchant count, location count, or transaction demand. Validate each bridge against actual cohorts, sponsor terms, and site-level transactions before using it to support a purchase price or hiring plan.

The retention engine matters more than a seductive headline spread. A merchant processing $100,000 monthly at 10 retained basis points contributes $100 monthly before allocated operating expense. Losing 20 such merchants removes $24,000 of annualized revenue, nearly the entire Conservative profit figure before considering avoided service costs. A $500 acquisition cost has a five-month simple payback at that contribution, but only before support labor, equipment subsidies, attrition, and onboarding failures. If a processor raises the buy rate by two basis points on Base's $275 million volume, annual retained revenue falls $55,000 unless pricing or contract protection offsets it. Do not silently preserve the canonical profit after introducing that stress; present a separate sensitivity case. ATM economics need the same discipline. A machine retaining $1.40 on 400 monthly withdrawals produces $560 before maintenance, communications, travel, insurance, and cash carrying costs. Two hundred withdrawals produce only $280. Record route density and replenishment frequency alongside transaction counts. Remote telemetry can prevent missed withdrawals, but it cannot manufacture demand at a weak venue or turn borrowed vault cash into earned income for the operator.

## 3. Map Twelve Plays to Specific Merchants, Platforms, and Fallbacks  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The twelve-play map begins with three merchant-economics plays. Play 1, iso_schedule_a_dual_pricing_residual_spread, negotiates sponsor-approved buy rates and earns disclosed residuals through approved merchant pricing and venue ATMs. On $200,000 monthly card volume, a 15-basis-point retained processing spread equals $300 monthly; 500 ATM withdrawals retaining $1.20 add $600 before local servicing. These are separate revenue sources, not permission to impose a debit surcharge. If pricing is disputed, disable that configuration, restore approved transparent pricing, and remediate with the sponsor before resuming. Play 2, gateway markup retention, earns $12 monthly retained markup across 100 accounts, or $1,200; fall back to acquiring-only economics if the integration lacks an approved reseller agreement. Play 3, B2B interchange optimization, uses eligible transaction data and processor-supported qualification rules. Saving a merchant $400 monthly while contractually retaining $80 is a merchant-service proposition, not ownership of the entire savings. If qualification fails, revert to the documented standard rate and investigate missing fields. Never manufacture invoice data or guarantee a particular interchange category. For all three plays, compare actual statements before and after deployment, including monthly minimums and authorization fees that can overwhelm apparently attractive basis-point economics.

The next three plays concern subscription conversion and loss control. Play 4, web2app_quiz_subscription_billing_bypass, uses a transparent web quiz and permitted external checkout before app access. Compare current regional Apple App Store or Google Play terms rather than assuming a universal 30% saving. If 1,000 monthly subscriptions sell for $20 and an applicable store fee is 15%, the modeled store charge is $3,000. An illustrative external charge of 3% plus $0.30 costs $900, leaving $2,100 before acquisition, refunds, tax, and support; only the operator's contracted retained fee becomes operator revenue. Where required, use approved in-app billing and simplify disclosures and cancellation. Play 5, recurring billing recovery, might recover 20 legitimate $50 renewals, or $1,000 merchant revenue; a disclosed 10% service fee earns $100 if contractually payable. Stop retries after revocation or cancellation. Play 6, dispute deflection, might resolve 40 eligible cases with $15 avoided dispute fees each, yielding $600 merchant fee savings before vendor charges and refunds. Verifi RDR and Ethoca support different workflows; do not present a refund as recovered revenue. Fall back to staffed review when evidence, eligibility, or a refund threshold is ambiguous.

Three relationship plays follow. Play 7, vertical POS conversion, places a sponsor-supported Clover or comparable installation into a workflow where inventory, tipping, and settlement reconciliation actually matter. Thirty merchants retaining $75 monthly each produce $2,250 before hardware subsidies and support. Fall back to a paid service relationship or a supported terminal when software incompatibility blocks the intended deployment. Play 8, sponsor-approved higher-risk onboarding, coordinates correctly classified merchants, documented fulfillment, reserves, and explicitly approved MIDs. Ten merchants retaining $250 monthly produce $2,500, but a reserve or contractual clawback can outweigh several months of fees. MID orchestration means approved routing and transparent ownership, never transaction laundering, concealed activity, or replacement accounts used to evade restrictions. Stop boarding and repair underwriting when facts change. Play 9, residual portfolio acquisition, might purchase a verified $4,000 monthly stream for an illustrative 24-month multiple, or $96,000. That purchase exceeds this volume's stated startup range and requires a separate capital plan. A 10% revenue decline removes $400 monthly before support savings. Fall back to contingent consideration or abandon the purchase if assignment, termination survival, merchant concentration, or offset rights cannot be established from executed documents.

The final three plays operate the physical cash network. Play 10, exclusive venue ATM placement, converts a written location right into withdrawal traffic. At 450 monthly withdrawals and $1.40 retained per withdrawal, one machine contributes $630 before field costs. Fall back to relocation when verified demand misses the placement threshold and the contract permits removal. Play 11, ATM fleet acquisition, buys operating sites rather than trusting machine counts. Eight sites retaining $400 monthly each produce $3,200 before route expense; an illustrative $38,400 purchase equals twelve months of that retained revenue, not twelve months of owner profit. Reduce or defer consideration for nonassignable venues, failing hardware, or unreconciled cash. Play 12, vault-cash routing optimization, protects existing fees by reducing outages and unnecessary trips. Preventing 100 missed withdrawals at $1.40 retains $140; avoiding two $75 service trips saves $150 of expense. Keep those benefits separate and avoid counting transactions already included in the ATM forecast twice. Fall back to conservative cash buffers and manual dispatch when telemetry fails. None of these plays authorizes lending out venue cash, mixing ownership accounts, or treating replenishment deposits as sales in the operating ledger.

## 4. Build the Controls That Turn a Spread into a Small-Team Moat  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate statutes, network rules, and sponsor requirements instead of calling every restriction federal law. The Durbin provisions in 15 U.S.C. 1693o-2 and Regulation II address debit interchange and network routing; they do not create universal permission to surcharge debit cards. Visa and Mastercard generally prohibit surcharging their debit and prepaid cards even when processed without a PIN. Credit surcharge limits, notices, and eligible products require current network and sponsor verification. Cash discounting and dual pricing must reflect genuine, clearly communicated prices, not a renamed prohibited fee. New York General Business Law 518 illustrates why state-specific displayed pricing and surcharge limits matter. For an ATM imposing a covered withdrawal fee, 12 CFR 1005.16 requires the fee amount disclosed on screen or paper before the consumer is committed, followed by the consumer's election to continue. FinCEN's analysis of limited-service ATM owners does not automatically classify every cash-related business the same way; expanded services require fresh analysis under 31 CFR 1010.100. Capture the actual approved screen sequence, receipt, signage, and transaction tests rather than relying on an installer's verbal assurance.

The small-team moat is a controlled operating system, not merely software access. IRIS CRM can organize boarding and reconcile residual exports; remote ATM telemetry can flag low cash and communication failures; eligible Verifi RDR or Ethoca integrations can reduce manual dispute work. Assign an owner to every exception queue and define measurable response times. Automating a bad pricing configuration only distributes the defect faster. Maintain merchant cohorts, signed pricing exhibits, sponsor correspondence, venue agreements, machine inventories, and cash ownership records with restricted access and recoverable backups. Keep cardholder data out of informal spreadsheets and support tickets; use processor-supported tokenization and the applicable PCI DSS validation path. PCI DSS is an industry security standard, not a statute. Use separate access roles for changing refund rules, exporting residuals, and approving cash adjustments. A contractor who replenishes an ATM should not unilaterally approve a cash variance attributed to that same visit. Reconcile electronic journals, processor settlement, cassette counts, and physical cash with documented differences. Service density, clean books, and controlled access make automation useful while preserving a clear trail when a merchant, venue, or sponsor disputes a payment.

Start with a constrained pilot and demand evidence at each handoff. Obtain sponsor-confirmed merchant eligibility and complete pricing exhibits before requesting production credentials. Board a small cohort, verify actual settlement, and compare the first payable residual against the contracted waterfall. For ATMs, verify ownership, connectivity, security, applicable accessibility requirements, fee disclosures, and cash-service responsibilities before accepting public transactions. Use supported test procedures rather than creating false commercial transactions. The first monthly review should compare active merchants, retained basis points, gateway accounts, settled withdrawals, cash outages, disputes, reserve deductions, and cancellation reasons. Scale acquisition only when collections and service capacity support it; signed applications alone do not establish recurring earnings. A meaningful exit test asks whether the residual agreement survives termination, whether the sponsor permits assignment, and whether venue contracts transfer with equipment. A buyer cannot purchase a stronger right than the seller actually holds. Revisit merchant and venue concentration quarterly, maintain a practical migration plan within contractual limits, and keep cash reserves separate from owner draws. The objective is repeatable retained contribution with documented rights, not the largest possible headline processing volume on a sales presentation.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Document the economic waterfall
   - Collect signed merchant, sponsor, gateway, and venue fee exhibits.
   - Identify every fee owner, allocation, offset, reserve, payment lag, and surviving residual right before calculating retained contribution.
2. Test the approved customer experience
   - Run supported pricing, receipt, cancellation, and ATM disclosure tests.
   - Record screen captures and transaction references without exposing sensitive card data; resolve discrepancies before public deployment.
3. Close the first collection cycle
   - Reconcile processor exports and physical cash records to bank movements.
   - Separate settlement principal, retained fees, reserves, partner allocations, equipment spending, and unexplained differences.
4. Scale only the verified production unit
   - Expand the merchant cohort or ATM route with demonstrated economics.
   - Compare observed retention, service hours, cash needs, and acquisition payback against a separate sensitivity model while preserving the canonical cases.
