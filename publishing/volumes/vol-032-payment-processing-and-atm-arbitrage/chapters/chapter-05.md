## 1. Reconstruct earned residuals before trusting the deposit  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is calling merchant card volume revenue; the second is treating a processor's residual deposit as a complete profit statement. A merchant processing $100,000 monthly at a retained twenty-basis-point spread contributes $200 before any additional operator expenses, not $100,000. Separate interchange and network pass-through amounts, sponsor buy rates, gateway markups, agent splits, and operator-retained fees. Apply ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis to each contractual service instead of assuming every customer charge belongs in the operator's top line. The canonical scorecard calls its revenue field gross revenue, but this candidate's defined basis is already retained ISO residuals, gateway markups, and ATM surcharges. Do not apply a second retained-share haircut. Formula v2.0 fixes Conservative revenue at $90,000 and profit at $27,000, Base at $445,000 and $189,125, and Scaled at $800,000 and $440,000. Corresponding total annual expenses are $63,000, $255,875, and $360,000. Keep these cases separate from diagnostic examples, which explain mechanics rather than replace the scorecard. Merchant settlement funds, reserve balances owed to merchants, and ATM vault cash belong in separate reconciliations. A dollar moving through an account is not automatically earned revenue or distributable owner cash. Document the accounting policy before importing any dashboard total.

The third trap is buying a residual stream without reading Schedule A amendments, vesting language, cross-collateralization, and post-termination rights. A ten-basis-point buy-rate increase on $2 million of monthly merchant volume removes $2,000 monthly, or $24,000 annually, if selling prices remain unchanged. That sensitivity alone approaches the entire Conservative owner-profit case. Request signed schedules, effective dates, notice provisions, residual assignment restrictions, and the exact conditions permitting offsets. A processor may have contractual rights to debit future residuals for merchant losses, but those rights are not identical across sponsors. Model only the recourse actually accepted. A $600 boarding incentive subject to full recovery during the first six months becomes a $12,000 exposure when twenty incentivized accounts close inside that window. Equipment leases can survive processing termination, creating customer anger without producing continuing residuals. Merchant reserves are not the ISO's operating float; an ISO payout hold is not automatically a permanent expense. Track recognized earnings, collectible receivables, disputed deductions, and available cash independently. Before purchasing a portfolio, reconcile twelve monthly statements to bank receipts and sample the underlying merchant agreements. Refuse acquisition pricing based solely on a seller's spreadsheet or a single unusually strong month.

Build the residual audit around a stable merchant identifier, contract version, processor identifier, statement month, expected calculation, actual calculation, and deduction reason. IRIS CRM can organize boarding and residual workflows, but imported files still require source-to-bank controls. For an internal early-warning policy, investigate any unexplained merchant-level variance exceeding the greater of $25 or 2% of expected monthly residual; investigate every unfamiliar deduction code regardless of size. These are operator settings, not network limits. Reconcile portfolio totals separately so hundreds of small discrepancies cannot evade the merchant-level trigger. Track same-merchant retained basis points, paid residual per active account, involuntary closures, and ninety-day cohort survival. If retained basis points fall from twenty to sixteen on unchanged volume, unit revenue has declined 20% even when acquisition makes aggregate deposits rise. Freeze discretionary acquisition spending when two consecutive cycles contain unresolved material differences. Require a processor ticket, supporting statement, assigned owner, and resolution date for every exception. The corrective action might be a billing adjustment, an authorized merchant repricing, or a sponsor migration permitted by contract. Never recover a processor error through an undisclosed merchant fee. Keep recovered amounts tied to their original periods so a one-time reimbursement does not masquerade as recurring growth.

## 2. Contain merchant risk without creating sponsor or network exposure  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

High-risk MID orchestration means transparently arranging approved processing relationships for the merchant's actual business, not rotating identities to outrun monitoring. Fatal shortcuts include transaction laundering, inaccurate merchant category codes, fabricated beneficial ownership, and routing a terminated merchant through an unrelated company's account. Those practices can produce sponsor termination, withheld residuals, civil exposure, and difficulty obtaining future sponsorship. Mastercard MATCH is a reason-coded merchant screening system, not a universal permanent blacklist and not a problem solved by opening another entity. Obtain the sponsor's stated reason, supporting records, and available correction procedure. Before boarding, reconcile ownership documents, product pages, fulfillment promises, refund terms, descriptor text, and expected geography. A merchant promising delivery in ninety days carries different exposure from a same-day repair shop, even at identical monthly volume. Maintain a trailing unfulfilled-sales estimate: 500 undelivered orders at $120 represent $60,000 of customer exposure before refunds, recoveries, or contractual allocation. That amount is neither ISO revenue nor automatically ISO liability. Determine which agreement transfers which losses. If the sponsor declines the business, stop boarding and discuss a genuinely suitable, fully disclosed alternative. Never coach a merchant to disguise products or split transactions to conceal the underlying business model from underwriting.

Chargeback monitoring fails when operators rely on one blended percentage or assume an automated refund erases every program signal. Keep sponsor-defined counts, denominators, reporting windows, fraud indicators, and applicable Visa or Mastercard program thresholds versioned by region and effective date. For an internal policy, review a merchant at 50% of its applicable count or ratio limit, then escalate at 75%; a projected breach overrides both checkpoints. Do not substitute these percentages for the actual program methodology. A hypothetical sponsor policy measuring thirty disputes against 5,000 settled transactions yields 0.60%, but another program may measure a different population or month. Verifi RDR and Ethoca services can resolve eligible incidents earlier; coverage, fees, refund timing, and program treatment require explicit confirmation. For illustration, twenty $80 refunds plus twenty $5 alert fees cost $1,700. The benefit depends on avoided handling costs, inventory recovery, and the specific incident's downstream treatment, not simply a lower dashboard count. Use order-level idempotency to prevent an alert refund and a separate support refund from both firing. When fulfillment slips or complaint velocity doubles against a comparable baseline, contact the merchant immediately and coordinate any processing restrictions with the sponsor. An ISO should not invent powers to seize merchant funds.

Surcharge configuration is another asymmetric trap: a small extra merchant yield can trigger customer complaints, network assessments, and expensive repricing across an entire portfolio. Distinguish a credit-card surcharge from a genuine cash discount or dual-price presentation. Changing the label does not change how the terminal calculates the transaction or what customers see before paying. Visa and Mastercard prohibit surcharges on their debit and prepaid products, including debit transactions processed without a PIN; this is not a blanket surcharge prohibition created by Regulation II. Regulation II separately addresses covered debit interchange and routing requirements. Apply the most restrictive applicable network, sponsor, and state requirement, verifying current caps and required notices directly. For a $100 credit purchase, an illustrative permitted 3% charge produces $103 payable; the same configured debit transaction must not acquire that surcharge. New York General Business Law section 518 requires the highest total credit-card price to be clearly displayed under its applicable terms. Test menus, quotes, ecommerce checkout, receipts, refund behavior, and split tenders rather than inspecting a countertop sign alone. Disable the affected configuration when product classification or displayed pricing fails. Preserve test receipts and software versions so a processor update cannot silently reintroduce the defect.

>

> - Match beneficial ownership, merchant category, products, descriptor and fulfillment terms to the sponsor-approved application; obtain review for material deviations.
> - Load the current sponsor-specific dispute rules with exact denominators and effective dates; assign an owner for the 50% and 75% internal warning levels.
> - Test credit, debit, prepaid, refund and split-tender behavior; retain screenshots and receipts showing both correct classification and required price presentation.
> - Document reserve terms, loss recourse, residual vesting and clawback provisions, then confirm the operating cash forecast can absorb the accepted exposure.

## 3. Separate ATM surcharge economics from vault-cash survival  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

An ATM can appear profitable while draining cash because withdrawal principal and surcharge revenue move through different settlement paths. Consider an illustrative venue producing 800 monthly withdrawals at a $3.50 surcharge: customer surcharge collections equal $2,800. If the venue receives $0.75 per withdrawal under an agency arrangement, its $600 share leaves $2,200 of retained surcharge revenue for this example. Treat contractual routing and settlement fees consistently with the selected accounting policy; do not deduct a retained-share allocation twice. Suppose $200 of processing costs, $400 of servicing, $100 of connectivity and monitoring, and $200 of other operating expense then leave $1,300 monthly contribution before additional portfolio overhead and taxes. These are unit sensitivities, not a reconstruction of the three canonical scenarios. At a $100 average withdrawal, the machine dispenses $80,000 of monthly principal, which is not operator revenue. A $10,000 cassette requires replenishment according to actual demand and settlement timing, not a simplistic monthly average. Record the ownership of every dollar loaded, the settlement receivable created by dispensing, and each balancing adjustment. A shortage is an incident requiring evidence, not a miscellaneous fee quietly buried beneath attractive surcharge totals. Reconcile the machine journal before distributing venue payments.

The next traps are ignoring physical custody, buying unsuitable insurance, and treating venue exclusivity as permanent demand. At the example's $1,300 monthly contribution, an uninsured $10,000 cash loss consumes about 7.7 months of contribution before deductibles or repair expense. Check cash-in-machine, cash-in-transit, employee dishonesty, unattended vehicle, anchoring, alarm, and cash-limit conditions with the actual policy wording. A certificate alone does not establish coverage for the operating method. Use dual custody where practical, sealed containers, documented cassette counts, and access logs that separate cash handling from settlement adjustments. Avoid publishing replenishment routes or predictable cash balances. Contract for site access, electricity, telecommunications, relocation rights, venue sale, early termination, and removal of the machine; exclusivity without access can strand both equipment and cash. Observe applicable accessibility requirements, including the 2010 ADA Standards section 707 where applicable, rather than assuming a functioning dispenser is an acceptable installation. Before buying a location, inspect withdrawal history across ordinary weeks and seasonal troughs. A festival month is not a recurring baseline. If the venue closes or access becomes unsafe, stop dispatching cash and activate the contractual retrieval plan with the property operator.

Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.16, requires a fee-charging ATM operator to disclose the fee on screen or on paper before the consumer is committed and to obtain the consumer's election to continue. Federal law no longer requires the former exterior fee placard; other applicable requirements still need separate review. Test cancel behavior and compare the disclosed amount with journal and settlement records after every surcharge change. FinCEN's FIN-2007-G006 addresses limited-service ATM owner-operators; do not extend that analysis automatically to cash acceptance, stored-value loading, crypto exchange, or money transmission. A changed service requires fresh classification against 31 CFR 1010.100 and applicable state rules. Operationally, use remote telemetry to alert when forecast cash falls below the next service window's stressed demand, not merely when the cassette reaches an arbitrary percentage. If weekend demand could reach $6,000 before replenishment, a $4,000 balance is already inadequate. Treat any unexplained cash difference as an immediate incident; escalate dispatch according to safety and severity. Alert on two missed fifteen-minute heartbeats as an internal connectivity policy, then distinguish telecom failure from dispenser faults. Disable affected service when cash accounting or fee presentation cannot be trusted, and preserve journals before resetting equipment.

## 4. Stress-test acquisition, concentration and incident liquidity before scaling  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The mapped play google_maps_gbp_lead_gen_network, Multi-Location Google Business Profile Local-Pack Grid Arbitrage, TRACK_2, can introduce real tradespeople to merchant services, but lead-generation entities themselves are not eligible for Google Business Profiles. The eligible trade business must own its representation and authorize management. Never create fictitious shops, duplicate branches, or rented-address listings to manufacture coverage. Use the specified fallback: launch only with a real partner's physical shop or yard capable of passing live video verification, paired with organic city rank-and-rent pages. Physical existence alone is insufficient; verify actual staffing, operations, signage where required, and customer-facing eligibility. A service-area business that does not receive customers at its address should hide that address and follow Google's service-area rules. For illustrative acquisition math, $600 of monthly content and call-tracking expense producing thirty qualified calls costs $20 per call. Six completed jobs at a disclosed $150 referral fee produce $900 gross referral revenue and $300 contribution before additional costs. Those referral earnings are outside this candidate's canonical retained-payment revenue basis and must remain separate. Any resulting processing referral needs its own authorized application, acquisition cost, and residual measurement. Do not count a phone call as a boarded merchant.

Call tracking must preserve an accurate business identity and a working route to the real partner; use a partner-controlled local number and authorized forwarding configuration that follows current Google guidance. Review automation should request genuine customer feedback consistently, without fabricated reviews, incentives tied to sentiment, or filtering unhappy customers away from public review channels. Recording calls introduces separate requirements: 18 U.S.C. 2511 contains federal interception rules and exceptions, while California Penal Code section 632 can require all-party consent for confidential communications. Configure recording and notices for the actual jurisdictions, or disable recording when the necessary conditions are unresolved. Organic city pages must describe the real service relationship and coverage, not imply nonexistent branches. If a profile is suspended, stop adding listings, preserve verification evidence, use the documented appeal route, and maintain acquisition through truthful organic pages and partner referrals. Payment acquisition has its own unit test: a $900 acquisition cost against $150 monthly retained residual has six-month revenue payback, not profit payback. With $30 monthly incremental servicing expense, contribution is $120 and payback becomes 7.5 months before attrition or clawbacks. Decline expansion when observed cohort survival does not support that recovery period.

The final trap is treating profitable averages as protection against concentrated cash interruption. Track revenue by sponsor, merchant group, venue owner, acquisition channel, and common beneficial owner. If 40% of Base retained revenue depends on one sponsor, a three-month payout hold affects $44,500 of cash receipts at a uniform run rate: $445,000 multiplied by 40% and three-twelfths. That is a liquidity stress, not automatically lost revenue; permanent termination requires a separate earnings-loss scenario. The Base expense envelope averages about $21,323 monthly, although actual payment dates and variable expenses govern cash needs. Maintain a thirteen-week cash forecast that separates collectible holds, anticipated clawbacks, ordinary expenses, and restricted vault cash. Do not assume the $8,000–$45,000 startup range covers a mature portfolio's incident exposure. Run a weekly exception meeting with one accountable owner and named contractors for residual audit, merchant support, and field service. Conservative and Base staffing remain 1 FT plus 3 C; Scaled remains 2 FT plus 4 C. If exceptions consume available capacity, slow boarding rather than silently adding unbudgeted labor. Resume expansion only after reconciliations close, affected controls pass retests, and the cash forecast survives the specific incident being modeled.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Capture the exception without destroying evidence
   - Open one incident record linking the merchant, ATM, sponsor or eligible partner profile to its source evidence.
   - Preserve statements, journals, contract versions, screenshots and timestamps before equipment resets, configuration changes or appeal submissions.
2. Quantify earnings, cash and principal separately
   - Calculate lost contribution, delayed receivables, contractual recourse and restricted principal as distinct amounts.
   - Use the canonical revenue basis and keep merchant settlement, ATM vault cash and unrelated referral earnings outside retained-payment revenue.
3. Apply the narrow authorized circuit-breaker
   - Pause the affected acquisition spend, pricing configuration, onboarding activity or ATM service while assigning the correct counterparty.
   - Use sponsor-directed merchant restrictions, authorized site access and documented Google appeals rather than identity substitution or improvised control over customer funds.
4. Retest the repaired control and fund the recovery
   - Close reconciliation differences, repeat affected transaction tests and update the thirteen-week cash forecast before resuming expansion.
   - Require an accountable owner, preserved evidence and a recurrence monitor; include recovery labor within the applicable scorecard expense envelope.
