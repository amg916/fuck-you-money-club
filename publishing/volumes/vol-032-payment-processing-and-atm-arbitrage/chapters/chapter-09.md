# Chapter 09: High-Risk Accounts and Chargeback Defense

*High-risk classification and approval; high-risk gateways; supplement brands and payment routing; staying under card network thresholds; Verifi CDRN, RDR, and Ethoca alerts and enrollment setup; Verifi RESOLVE, RECOVER, and Inform (TC40/TC15 data)*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

High-risk is a bank underwriting call, not a gateway feature. Adaptiv Payments, PaymentCloud and Funnelish all say the acquiring bank decides, and the price is higher fees, reserves released in about 3 to 6 months, and a tighter chargeback leash. The thresholds cited are Visa's VAMP flag at 1.5% with 1,500 fraud reports and disputes, Mastercard at 100 chargebacks and 1.5%, and Visa tightening toward 0.5%. The street mechanics are a routed multi-processor stack (Stripe default, high-risk SKUs to Authorize.net, declines retried on NMI) plus pre-dispute alerts: Ethoca and CDRN go live in roughly 12 to 24 hours and RDR in days to weeks, priced anywhere from $5 to $40 per alert depending on vendor. Practitioners split on whether alerts are core defense or a temporary bandage that hides root causes.

## The Practitioner Consensus

The operators agree on one blunt point: high-risk is a label the acquiring bank puts on your industry, not a feature of software. Adaptiv Payments says gateways like Authorize.net and NMI are neither high-risk nor low-risk on their own; a high-risk gateway is simply one wired to a merchant account at a bank that approves your vertical, so the real question is which bank will underwrite you [D7Ea5oEHwpM @ 00:00:50]. PaymentCloud lists the triggers that get you tagged: regulated verticals such as adult, CBD and firearms, future-delivery models like tickets, travel and subscriptions, and your own creditworthiness [XwCDjPAfKtY @ 00:00:36]. Funnelish narrows it for supplement sellers to four drivers: health claims, subscriptions and free trials, a category reputation built on years of pay-shipping diet pill offers that quietly turned into $80 monthly charges, and restricted ingredients like CBD, kratom and testosterone boosters [-G2UpQAf8L0 @ 00:01:33]. Funnelish also warns that Stripe and PayPal bundle the gateway and merchant account together, which is exactly why one decision from them can shut everything down at once and leave 90 days of revenue locked inside [-G2UpQAf8L0 @ 00:01:18]. PaymentProcessorCom adds that the label does not mean you did anything wrong, and that every provider runs different rules, so the first question is whether this provider can support what you actually sell [q9nKd4oVwnw @ 00:00:47].

Every speaker prices the label the same way: higher fees, a reserve, and a tighter leash. PaymentCloud says high-risk accounts carry higher fees and longer settlement periods, plus a reserve agreement that parks a percentage of sales in a separate account, released in about 3 to 6 months if all goes well [XwCDjPAfKtY @ 00:03:04]. In exchange the merchant gets higher chargeback thresholds, fraud tools and a dedicated account manager [XwCDjPAfKtY @ 00:01:44]. The thresholds are where the consensus gets specific. Adaptiv Payments puts Visa's VAMP flag for US merchants at 1.5% with at least 1,500 fraud reports and disputes in a month, and Mastercard's program at 100 chargebacks and 1.5% of the previous month's transactions, with Mastercard announcing that limit will tighten [NdTgGwtE_N4 @ 00:01:12]. Scott E. Adams tells merchants who just received a monitoring notice that crossing 100 can trigger it but they generally also need to be over 0.9%, and to take it seriously without panicking [gROmfVmEsYs @ 00:00:55]. Pablo Torres of The Reach Network describes Visa moving the monthly ratio from 1% to 90 basis points and then toward 0.5% in 2025 [fO958wEcK8A @ 00:12:23]. Adaptiv names the end of the road: excessive chargebacks is a MATCH list reason code, and most banks step in well before the network limits, so the goal is to stay far under them [NdTgGwtE_N4 @ 00:01:29].

The third point of agreement: chargeback alerts are a pre-dispute layer, and nobody treats them as a substitute for fixing causes. Georges Rayess frames Ethoca and Verifi as two halves of one defense, Mastercard and Visa, because tools like Stripe Radar stop fraud before authorization but do nothing once a settled charge is disputed [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:00:00]. A dispute intercepted through either network never posts as a chargeback, so it never counts toward Mastercard's ECM program or Visa's VAMP [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:01:34]. Chargeflow's Dove Karp describes the same 24 to 72-hour grace period to refund before the chargeback takes root, but concedes alerts are a quick fix that does not touch underlying causes and cannot help a chargeback that gets through [sBnXe7LYFO8 @ 00:07:04]. Pablo Torres goes harder: Ethoca and Verifi should not be used as your fraud or chargeback mitigation strategy, only as a break while you find the root issue after an attack or an order mix-up [fO958wEcK8A @ 00:10:31]. Funnelish lists the root fixes that recur across speakers: compliant claims, obvious subscription terms at checkout, a clear billing descriptor, fast support replies, easy refunds and shipping confirmations, because a lot of chargebacks start as an unanswered question and a refund is almost always cheaper than a chargeback [-G2UpQAf8L0 @ 00:05:26].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Start with the stack. Adaptiv Payments splits every card payment into three pieces: the gateway captures the card, the processor carries it through the networks, and the merchant account at the acquiring bank settles it [D7Ea5oEHwpM @ 00:00:15]. Check your cart before applying: WooCommerce runs most gateways at no extra cost, while Shopify charges an outside-gateway fee of 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced and 0.2% on Plus [D7Ea5oEHwpM @ 00:01:29]. The underwriting file is website terms, refund policy and contact details, formation documents and EIN, ID for every owner at 25% or more, and 3 to 6 months of processing statements, with approval taking a few days to a few weeks [D7Ea5oEHwpM @ 00:03:58]. PaymentProcessorCom says to disclose any prior closure, because tweaking your description to hide a past issue causes bigger problems later [q9nKd4oVwnw @ 00:01:50]. Funnelish runs a route called main failover: Stripe takes every payment by default, products tagged high-risk go to Authorize.net on a high-risk merchant account, and declines retry automatically on NMI tied to a second merchant account, with rules checked top to bottom [-G2UpQAf8L0 @ 00:06:55]. Adaptiv draws the hard line: separate business lines can each carry their own account and descriptor, but splitting one business across accounts to hide chargebacks breaks network rules, and Visa can fine $25,000 per merchant, per month [D7Ea5oEHwpM @ 00:03:41].

Alert enrollment is where the numbers get concrete. Chargeback.io lists coverage: Ethoca covers 95% of Mastercard transactions, RDR covers 97% of Visa transactions, and CDRN covers US transactions only, hitting 95% of Visa among them [sH9CslJpDeA @ 00:00:18]. Ethoca and CDRN go live in up to 12 business hours versus up to 72 for RDR; Ethoca and CDRN need only a billing descriptor, while RDR also needs ARN and CAID codes; pricing is $29 per Ethoca alert and $15 for RDR or CDRN, and the shop recommends high-risk merchants enroll in all three, medium-risk in Ethoca and RDR [sH9CslJpDeA @ 00:01:33]. Scott E. Adams clocks CDRN and Ethoca at under 24 hours, while RDR can take days or weeks depending on getting BIN and CAIDs from a processor that is sometimes wrong, so submit what you have, ARNs included, and let Visa research it [gROmfVmEsYs @ 00:01:32]. Pricing swings hard by vendor. Chargeflow cites a uniform $40 per alert charged regardless of success, with Verifi mostly covering US issuers and Ethoca reaching banks in Europe, Asia and Canada [sBnXe7LYFO8 @ 00:06:00]. Georges Rayess puts the alert fee at $5 to $15 against a $15 to $25 chargeback fee, arguing the ratio protection matters more than the per-alert cost [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:01:34].

Execution detail is where operators separate. RDR runs on rules you configure once, and Visa resolves matching disputes in real time; Georges Rayess says the 24 to 72-hour alert window is too tight for manual review at any real volume, which makes RDR the high-leverage piece, though it auto-refunds disputes you might have won, so calibrate rules to your reason mix [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:01:53]. On Ethoca, Scott E. Adams sets 24 hours as the rule of thumb, close to a sure thing inside that window, and says late responses still tend to work, so respond anyway [Ghanuu5dNMk @ 00:01:34]. Adams also pushes Verifi Inform as one of the most important products in the mix: it delivers the TC40 fraud file and TC15 chargeback file in near real time, the only practical way to track fraud-flagged transactions that issuers never charge back, usually on low-ticket items [gJ_2exzq6FA @ 00:01:00]. That speed lets you stop a shipment, disentitle a digital good or block repeat purchases [gJ_2exzq6FA @ 00:02:31]. When a chargeback does land, Adaptiv Payments matches evidence to the reason code: tracking and delivery confirmation for not-received, agreed terms and cancellation records for subscriptions, and Compelling Evidence 3.0 for Visa friendly fraud, pointing to two earlier undisputed purchases on the same card with matching IP address or device [NdTgGwtE_N4 @ 00:03:50].

   - Assemble documents and clean up the website before applying to a high-risk provider.

   - Connect the high-risk merchant account through a supported gateway and set routing rules.
   - Default low-risk volume to Stripe, send high-risk tagged products to Authorize.net on a high-risk account, and auto-retry declines on NMI tied to a second merchant account so a single problem never kills the sale [Funnelish — -G2UpQAf8L0 @ 00:07:11]
3. Enroll alerts fast, then RDR
   - Turn on CDRN and Ethoca for immediate coverage, then complete RDR enrollment.

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is over how many alert networks to stack. Georges Rayess frames Ethoca and Verifi as two halves of one defense, Mastercard on one side and Visa on the other, and insists there is no overlap and no single product covers both networks, so running only one leaves the other brand's disputes uncovered [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:02:28]. Scott E. Adams draws the map less cleanly: CDRN is mostly Visa and Ethoca is mostly Mastercard, but both carry some coverage on both networks and can handle Discover and Amex depending on how the merchant is set up [gROmfVmEsYs @ 00:02:13]. Chargeflow turns that overlap into a cost leak, warning that enrolling in both can fire double dispute alerts when a cardholder's bank sits in both networks, which means double fees, so the golden rule is a cost-benefit analysis before committing [sBnXe7LYFO8 @ 00:06:18]. Sequencing splits the field too. Rayess says that if engineering time is tight, start with Verifi RDR because it is configure once and then autonomous. Adams runs the opposite order for merchants hit with a monitoring-program notice: get CDRN and Ethoca live for quick coverage, because both can be running in about 24 hours, then get into RDR and go from there [gROmfVmEsYs @ 00:03:09].

Nobody quotes the same price for an alert, and the spread changes the math. Chargeflow says both Verifi CDRN and Ethoca levy a uniform $40 charge for each alert received, regardless of its success, then pitches its own bundled layer as paying only for alerts that actually prevented a chargeback, with no duplicate charges [sBnXe7LYFO8 @ 00:06:00]. Chargeback.io quotes $29 per Ethoca alert and a shared $15 rate for RDR and CDRN, billed only for alerts received, and needs nothing but a billing descriptor to enroll Ethoca and CDRN [sH9CslJpDeA @ 00:01:33]. Georges Rayess pegs the intercept at roughly $5 to $15 per alert against a $15 to $25 chargeback fee, and argues the per-alert fee matters less than the ratio, because an intercepted dispute never posts as a chargeback and never counts toward Mastercard's ECM program or Visa's VAMP [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:01:34]. Pablo Torres remembers the older resistance: merchants Torres talked to asked why they should pay $35 for an alert when they could just focus on not getting the chargeback at all, which Torres calls the right attitude [fO958wEcK8A @ 00:10:04]. Read side by side, the same alert ranges from $5 to $40 depending on the vendor quoting it.

The deepest philosophical fight is whether to refund on autopilot or keep eyes on every dispute. Adams pitches Ethoca partly as a way to automate the chargeback process and just not worry about it, and suggests lower-ticket or higher-risk merchants simply refund the SAFE fraud-feed alerts while higher-ticket or lower-risk merchants test and possibly skip them [Ghanuu5dNMk @ 00:02:49]. Rayess flags the cost of autopilot: RDR auto-refunds disputes you might have won in representment, so the rules have to be calibrated to your reason-code mix [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:01:53]. Pablo Torres goes further, arguing Ethoca and Verifi should not be a fraud or chargeback mitigation strategy at all, only a short break while the root issue gets fixed, and that asking a vendor to stop telling you about refunds is turning a blind eye [fO958wEcK8A @ 00:10:31]. On the fight side, Adaptiv Payments says save representment for the disputes you can actually win, and refund when the customer has a point or the order was small [NdTgGwtE_N4 @ 00:04:11]. Adaptiv also says to track results by reason code, so you know which fixes are working, which is the opposite of the inbox-silencing setup Torres warns against.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The first trap is building the whole business on one processor. Funnelish opens on the nightmare case: sales start coming in and the payment account gets frozen with 90 days of revenue locked inside, a pattern common for supplement, skin care, CBD and subscription brands [-G2UpQAf8L0 @ 00:00:00]. Because Stripe and PayPal bundle the gateway and the merchant account, one decision from them can shut down everything at once, and jumping 10 times your volume overnight is a great way to get noticed [-G2UpQAf8L0 @ 00:06:03]. A high-risk account is not a free pass either; Funnelish warns you can still lose it if chargebacks climb too high. The thresholds are lower than most operators assume. Scott E. Adams says a monitoring-program notice can trigger at over 100 chargebacks combined with a ratio generally over 0.9 percent, and that merchants with no real relationship with their bank often have no idea what to do when it lands [gROmfVmEsYs @ 00:00:55]. Adaptiv Payments adds that excessive chargebacks is a reason code for the MATCH list, and that most banks step in well before the network limits, so the target is to stay far under them, not just under the line [NdTgGwtE_N4 @ 00:01:29].

The second cluster of losses comes from data the merchant never sees. Adaptiv Payments flags the detail most merchants miss: once a bank logs a fraud report, it counts toward Visa's ratio even if you refund the order later, so fraud has to be stopped before you ship [NdTgGwtE_N4 @ 00:02:37]. Adams explains why that bites low-ticket sellers hardest: issuers often flag a cheap transaction as fraud without bothering to charge it back, and that flag still goes against you in Visa's fraud monitoring, so watching chargebacks alone leaves you blind and the TC40 file becomes critical [gJ_2exzq6FA @ 00:01:17]. RDR carries its own reporting trap. The refund is pulled by the acquirer and marked with what Adams believes is an M flag, but processors, ISOs and gateways further down the chain often never get that change, see a chargeback, and treat it as one, so you have to track it really carefully; RDR also does nothing to keep you out of the fraud program [5mi35dvcRrA @ 00:04:08]. Alerts, by contrast, more often clear both the fraud flag and the chargeback, and Adams reports good luck getting clients out of monitoring programs with alerts and RDR in combination [5mi35dvcRrA @ 00:05:19].

The last set of traps is operational and shows up directly in the margin. Chargeback.io warns that without a gateway integration, Ethoca and CDRN alerts cannot be matched to the transaction, so you manually locate and refund every single alert, while only RDR refunds automatically without integration [sH9CslJpDeA @ 00:00:50]. Rayess says that at any real volume the 24 to 72-hour window is too tight for manual review, which is the whole case for RDR's automation [gH8-LQ4srZY @ 00:01:53]. Pablo Torres describes the small-team version of the failure: a one or two person dropshipping operation marking goods up 200 to 300 percent cannot keep up with customer inquiries, things fall off the side, and chargebacks follow; silence the alerts on top of that and you cannot see the disease [fO958wEcK8A @ 00:05:58]. Adaptiv Payments closes the cheapest escape route: splitting one business across several accounts to hide chargebacks breaks card network rules, and Visa can fine $25,000 per merchant, per month [D7Ea5oEHwpM @ 00:03:41]. Adaptiv draws the line clearly: separate business lines can each carry their own merchant account and descriptor, one business split to mask its ratio cannot.
