## 1. Mine twelve deal lanes for evidence of movable margin  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build the prospect universe around a change event, not a purchased list of businesses accepting cards. Start with two adjacent merchant verticals and one compact ATM service territory. Municipal business licenses, building permits, state entity registrations, liquor-license applications, and venue event calendars can reveal openings, ownership changes, expanded hours, and new cash demand. Record the source URL, publication date, business address, likely decision-maker role, and specific commercial hypothesis in IRIS CRM. An entity filing alone does not establish operating activity or buying intent; confirm the location through its own website and a direct business conversation. For each merchant, seek an observable reason to revisit processing: a POS replacement, recurring checkout errors, a second location, or an approaching contract anniversary. For each ATM venue, seek cash demand plus a service defect: queues, repeated outages, inconvenient placement, or an expiring exclusive agreement. Avoid assuming that high foot traffic produces withdrawals. A busy cashless restaurant may be weaker than a smaller entertainment venue with documented cash purchases. The twelve lanes in the first two matrices are chapter-level operating mappings, not twelve externally supplied play identifiers. Each lane needs a measurable next question and an explicit reason to stop pursuing it.

The supplied play saas_technographic_churn_pixel_scraping becomes a migration-intent detector, not a license to assert private contract dates. Fetch public commercial pages weekly with urllib.request, inspect script references with html.parser, and compare normalized observations in sqlite3. Respect site access rules, use modest request rates, and never bypass authentication. Detect visible Salesforce, HubSpot, Marketo, Segment, or NetSuite references without collecting visitor identifiers. Python's standard socket interface does not provide general TXT/CNAME lookups; use an authorized DNS-over-HTTPS JSON endpoint through urllib.request or an approved passive-DNS export. Persist first-seen and last-seen timestamps. Weekly sampling bounds a change between observations; it cannot establish the exact installation day. Script removal may reflect a tag-manager change, regional rendering, or a redesign rather than vendor churn. Confirm with passive DNS TXT/CNAME history, public job-posting mentions, and a direct operational question before routing the lead. Month 10–11 outreach is a hypothesis only after someone confirms an annual contract's start date. Prioritize migrations that actually touch commerce, subscriptions, reconciliation, or checkout. A marketing automation change without payment implications deserves a lower score, even when the technology signal is perfectly accurate. Offer a settlement-reconciliation review rather than claiming the merchant is replacing its processor.

Translate every signal into retained economics before assigning sales time. Suppose an authorized statement review establishes $80,000 monthly eligible card volume and a 25-basis-point residual remaining after processor pricing and contractual partner allocations. The operator receives $200 monthly, plus an illustrative $20 retained gateway markup, or $2,640 annually before operating expenses. The $960,000 annual card volume belongs to the merchant's activity, not the operator's revenue. A $600 acquisition cost therefore pays back in 2.73 months against $220 monthly retained revenue, or 3.16 months after a separately budgeted $30 monthly servicing expense. For a technographic test, 200 domains producing 20 confirmed changes, six relevant conversations, three statement reviews, and one activated merchant yield a 0.5% domain-to-activation rate. At $500 total acquisition cost, that merchant's $190 illustrative monthly contribution supports a 2.63-month payback. These are planning assumptions, not measured campaign results. If software signals fail to produce payment-relevant conversations, replace the lane with accountant referrals or permissioned renewal reminders. Keep rejected signals in a labeled review queue so false positives improve the rules. Never rescue a weak lane by inventing renewal dates, exaggerating incumbent costs, or treating every marketing-tag change as processor dissatisfaction.

## 2. Qualify the spread, the sponsor fit, and the venue before quoting  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a 100-point qualification scorecard that sales and operations can reproduce. Allocate 25 points to documented retained economics, 20 to contract timing and decision authority, 20 to sponsor and integration fit, 20 to payment or cash-demand evidence, and 15 to serviceability and referral potential. Require evidence for every awarded point: three statements, a contract excerpt, supported terminal specifications, a venue count, or a named owner meeting. Route scores of 75 or more to a proposal, 55–74 to evidence collection, and lower scores to dated nurture. Missing beneficial ownership information, unsupported business activity, or an unresolved exclusive ATM contract overrides a high score. These are internal routing thresholds, not industry conversion statistics. For merchant reviews, separate interchange, network assessments, processor markup, gateway charges, equipment rent, and incidental fees. Use the sponsor's actual Schedule A and residual contract to calculate retained revenue; a low advertised processor rate says little about the operator's payable share. Model referral allocations once, at the correct contractual layer. Require explicit treatment of reserves, reversals, minimums, and residual adjustments. Retain redacted working copies through an access-controlled intake channel rather than forwarding unmasked statements among contractors or storing cardholder information in lead notes.

ATM qualification begins with contribution per completed surchargeable withdrawal and the physical route, not the machine's purchase price. For illustration, 350 monthly withdrawals at a $3 surcharge produce $1,050 collected surcharges. Subtract $350 of venue participation and $105 of processor allocations to obtain $595 retained monthly operator revenue under the assumed contract. Separately deduct $180 for communications, servicing, insurance, and cash logistics; the resulting monthly site contribution is $415 before central overhead and taxes. A $4,000 installed machine therefore takes 9.64 months to recover from that contribution, excluding salvage value and separately funded vault cash. At 150 withdrawals, the same $1.70 retained amount per withdrawal yields $255 revenue and only $75 after the assumed fixed site costs. Establish the minimum withdrawal count before negotiating exclusivity. Verify power, connectivity, accessibility, secure placement, replenishment responsibility, incident response, and removal rights in the venue agreement. Vault cash is a working asset, not surcharge revenue or a machine expense. A liquor license or crowded event calendar is a prospecting signal, not withdrawal proof. When activity is unverified, negotiate a reversible pilot with a relocation clause instead of paying a large signing bonus or projecting peak-night traffic across every operating day.

Treat high-risk MID orchestration as disclosed sponsor matching and operational continuity, never transaction laundering or routing around underwriting restrictions. Obtain the actual business model, fulfillment interval, refund policy, recurring-billing terms, historic disputes, and ownership information before requesting sponsor feedback. Do not promise acceptance, conceal merchant categories, split activity to evade monitoring, or represent a second MID as permission to ignore a first sponsor's conditions. A $200,000 monthly merchant at 30 retained basis points suggests $600 monthly revenue, but one support-intensive dispute pattern can consume the apparent premium. Obtain eligibility and pricing for Verifi RDR or Ethoca services through the relevant provider; coverage, alert charges, refunds, and duplicate handling affect the merchant's economics. MATCH-related checks and decisions belong with the acquiring sponsor and applicable network processes. For surcharge or dual-pricing proposals, validate credit-versus-debit classification, network requirements, applicable state law, receipts, and displayed prices before configuring terminals. Debit does not become surcharge-eligible because the customer selects a credit-style transaction flow. New York General Business Law section 518 requires attention to total displayed credit prices and surcharge limits. If a pricing program cannot be implemented correctly, propose ordinary transparent processing rather than relabeling an added fee as a discount.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before a binding commitment**
> 
> A high prospect score cannot compensate for missing authority, unsupported economics, or an unserviceable location.
> - Verify the signer controls the merchant entity or venue placement rights, and inspect existing termination and exclusivity provisions before scheduling conversion.
> - Reconcile the proposed retained spread to the executed Schedule A, referral terms, gateway charges, and actual sponsor residual calculation.
> - Obtain sponsor confirmation for the disclosed business activity and supported integration; document unresolved ownership, dispute, and reserve questions.
> - Test the applicable pricing display or ATM fee disclosure and customer election flow, then confirm service coverage, cash responsibility, and incident ownership.

## 3. Run a permission-aware cadence that earns statements and site visits  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run a 15-business-day sequence around one verifiable observation and one useful deliverable. On day one, send a short business email identifying the observed opening, migration, or service problem and offer a statement-level comparison or ATM site assessment. Do not claim affiliation with the incumbent processor or a card network. On day three, place a manual call to the published business line and ask who owns processing or venue operations. On day six, send a one-page example explaining which charges can change and which simply pass through. On day ten, contact an existing mutual partner only with an appropriate introduction arrangement. On day fifteen, close the loop with a clear next step or an invitation to decline. Maintain a suppression list across contractors and channels. Commercial email must follow 15 U.S.C. 7704, including truthful routing and subjects, applicable advertising identification, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out honored within ten business days. Review calling rules under 47 U.S.C. 227, implementing regulations, and applicable state requirements; do not assume a business contact removes every restriction. Avoid unsolicited automated marketing texts. The objective is permission to inspect a specific economic problem, not maximum message volume or a manufactured sense of urgency.

Use a transparent cohort model rather than declaring that a particular close rate is normal. An illustrative first campaign starts with 300 raw records, removes 60 duplicates or ineligible businesses, and reaches 240 qualified contacts. Assume 72 two-way conversations, 36 permissioned discoveries, 18 complete statement reviews or ATM site assessments, ten proposals, six signed agreements, and five activations within 120 days. Those stage conversions are 80%, 30%, 50%, 50%, 55.6%, 60%, and 83.3%; overall raw-to-signed conversion is 2%, while raw-to-live conversion is 1.67%. Six signed agreements and five live accounts are different outcomes and must remain separate. At $3,000 acquisition expense, cost per signed agreement is $500 and cost per activation is $600. If each activated merchant resembles the $220 monthly retained example, five accounts create $1,100 monthly revenue and $950 after the illustrative $30-per-account servicing cost. Acquisition payback is then 3.16 months after full activation, before central overhead and attrition. A ten-win goal requires approximately twice this successful cohort or better verified conversion, not simply twice as many follow-up messages. Measure performance by source lane, owner, and cohort date so a slow sponsor queue is not mistaken for ineffective prospecting.

Build inbound assets around evidence a merchant can recognize. Publish a processor-statement field guide, a location-opening payment checklist, and an ATM venue contribution worksheet with clearly labeled assumptions. Offer a short review appointment rather than a generic quote form. Track source and appointment outcomes in IRIS CRM, while keeping uploaded statements in restricted storage with retention dates. Accountants, restaurant equipment installers, POS technicians, and commercial property managers are useful referral partners because they encounter operational change before a public directory does. Agree in writing who introduces whom, what information may be shared, how compensation is calculated, and who supports the account. A partner sending five introductions monthly, yielding three discoveries and one activation, can outperform 100 unqualified cold contacts. If that merchant generates $250 monthly before a $50 partner allocation, report $200 retained revenue where that matches the operator's contractual entitlement. Do not subtract the allocation again as though it remained inside recognized revenue. Search pages should target specific operating questions, such as reconciling deposits or planning ATM refill frequency, rather than unsupported savings percentages. Every inbound response should state the next evidence required, the review timetable, and the conditions under which the operator will decline to recommend a switch.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the first evidence-backed cohort
   - Build 300 raw records across two merchant verticals and one ATM territory.
   - During weeks one and two, attach a public source, observation date, lane, owner role, and testable margin hypothesis; remove duplicates before outreach.
2. Earn permission and collect operating evidence
   - Run the 15-business-day cadence and request secure statement uploads or site access.
   - During weeks two through six, record opt-outs, completed conversations, discovery consent, contract timing, and the specific documents still required.
3. Price only sponsor-ready opportunities
   - Score each review and reconcile proposed pricing to retained contractual economics.
   - During weeks four through ten, confirm sponsor eligibility, existing obligations, integration support, service costs, and the customer-facing pricing or ATM disclosure flow.
4. Convert signed agreements into measured production
   - Activate three to ten accounts or placements and verify their first operating cycle.
   - During weeks six through sixteen, compare signed, live, and first-paid counts; investigate settlement errors, residual differences, downtime, and acquisition payback separately.

## 4. Close the first wins without buying losses or overstating revenue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Handle objections by identifying the evidence that would change the decision. When a merchant says the incumbent will match any rate, compare the same card mix, volume, contract period, equipment obligations, and service scope; a rate match that preserves the operator's minimum retained spread can be acceptable, while an uneconomic auction should end politely. When they fear downtime, propose a supported terminal or location pilot with documented reversal and settlement tests before broader migration. When they refuse to share statements, offer an explanation of the necessary fields and a redacted upload route; do not issue a precise savings claim without a baseline. When an ATM venue requests a guaranteed payment, calculate the minimum withdrawal volume under that guarantee and offer variable participation if demand evidence is weak. A $300 monthly minimum against $1.70 retained per withdrawal requires about 177 withdrawals before servicing expenses, assuming that minimum is incremental to the modeled allocations. If it replaces an existing allocation, recalculate rather than double counting. When customers question surcharge acceptance, test properly disclosed pricing and monitor complaints instead of promising universal acceptance. Record each objection as price, trust, timing, authority, integration, or demand uncertainty, then assign one measurable next action and a deadline.

Keep the portfolio scorecard separate from deal-level illustrations. Formula v2.0 supplies $90,000 annual operator revenue at a 30% net margin for $27,000 Conservative owner profit; $445,000 at 42.5% for $189,125 Base profit; and $800,000 at 55% for $440,000 Scaled profit. The corresponding total annual expense envelopes are $63,000, $255,875, and $360,000. Although the scorecard labels its top line gross revenue, its specified basis here is net retained ISO residuals, gateway markups, and ATM surcharges, excluding merchant settlement volume and ATM vault cash. Do not apply another retained-share haircut to those canonical totals. Document the principal-versus-agent assessment under ASC 606 for each actual contract rather than assuming every collected dollar is recognized revenue. Conservative and Base use 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C. Contractor counts describe staffing structure, not full-time-equivalent workload. The stated $1,500–$10,000 monthly operating-cost range cannot substitute for the larger total expense envelopes implied by the mature scenarios. Separate recurring platform overhead from acquisition, labor, route costs, and other scenario expenses. Five illustrative merchants at $220 monthly produce only $13,200 annualized revenue, so first wins validate the acquisition engine without proving the Base portfolio exists.

Define a win as a signed, eligible account or venue that reaches production and produces reconcilable economics. For merchants, check the first authorization, reversal, batch close, settlement deposit, and residual statement. For ATMs, verify processor connectivity, withdrawal completion, electronic journal access, customer fee notice, affirmative election, and cash reconciliation. Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.16, addresses ATM operator fee disclosure before the customer is committed; do not substitute an outdated physical-sign-only checklist for the actual electronic transaction flow. Evaluate the ATM operator's activities against 31 CFR 1010.100(ff); do not assume cash dispensing automatically creates, or universally avoids, money-services-business obligations. Use remote telemetry and vault-cash alerts to prevent avoidable outages, with named human responsibility for replenishment and incidents. Reconcile IRIS CRM residual imports to sponsor statements and flag missing accounts, changed splits, clawbacks, and unexplained adjustments. Ask satisfied clients for one relevant introduction only after the promised service works. Expand the strongest lane after two cohorts show acceptable acquisition payback and support load. Stop buying machines, leads, or portfolios when retention and reconciled contribution deteriorate. The repeatable advantage is verified retained spread with reliable delivery, not the number of signatures collected before the first residual payment arrives.
