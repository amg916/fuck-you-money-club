## 1. Separate principal acquisitions from regulated intermediary activity  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Organize Candidate #13 around seven plays with named deliverables: targeted sourcing produces a seller list; mandate qualification establishes buyer capacity and seller authority; QoE establishes maintainable earnings; debt structuring establishes funded terms; contract-and-license diligence establishes transferable operating rights; closing transition establishes ownership and access; 100-day retention protects customers and employees. These are this chapter's operating map, not seven separate revenue streams. Use seven full-time roles: owner/general manager, finance lead, operations lead, sales lead, and three delivery employees. Reserve three contractor slots for transaction counsel, a QoE/tax accountant, and a systems-integration specialist. The owner owns the transaction decision; specialists produce evidence rather than silently inheriting authority. At Scaled, add one service lead and one sector specialist, reaching eight employees and four contractors. Keep one live exclusivity process within the smaller pod unless another deal can be staffed without borrowing delivery capacity. Record each workstream, decision owner, document dependency, and statutory deadline in Airtable or a controlled spreadsheet. A beautifully populated data room does not establish that a contractor's license transfers, a customer accepts assignment, or a seller actually owns the shares being sold. Those conclusions require specific evidence.

The principal-versus-intermediary distinction comes before any fee discussion. Buying an operating company for your own account differs from soliciting investors or arranging another party's securities transaction for compensation. Exchange Act Section 15(a), 15 U.S.C. 78o(a), supplies the federal broker-registration starting point; the M&A broker exemption in Section 15(b)(13) is conditional, not a blanket permission for success fees. Counsel must test the eligible privately held company definition, applicable size thresholds, buyer control and active management, required disclosures, and every excluded activity. Custody of transaction funds, public offerings, passive-buyer transactions, and certain financing or buyer-group activities can defeat reliance. Verify current threshold adjustments rather than copying an old broker presentation. State securities registration and exemption analysis remains separate, and associated real estate can introduce real-estate licensing requirements. In California, Business and Professions Code Section 10131(a) reaches compensated brokerage of business opportunities as well as real estate. Do not assume a federal securities exemption answers that question. For mandate qualification, document whether the pod acts as principal, adviser, referral source, or intermediary, and who pays whom. Calling compensation a consulting fee does not change the activities performed or resolve a registration problem.

Build a jurisdiction register using actual operating locations, employee locations, customer services, regulated assets, and deal structure. An asset purchase can require fresh permits, tax registrations, assumed-name filings, and contract assignments; an equity purchase preserves the entity but may trigger ownership-change notifications or approvals. Federal antitrust law also applies below premerger reporting thresholds: screen competitive overlap under Clayton Act Section 7 and separately test current Hart-Scott-Rodino filing rules when transaction size or aggregation warrants it. SBA eligibility is another distinct analysis, not a substitute for licensing. Have the lender identify the current SBA SOP 50 10 edition, ownership-change rules, equity injection evidence, guarantees, seller debt treatment, and required operating permits in writing. A restaurant's liquor permit, a contractor's qualifying individual, and a healthcare provider's enrollment each require their own authority-specific review. The operations lead should ask the issuing agency whether the planned structure allows uninterrupted service and obtain a written answer or recorded official instruction. If permission cannot be effective at closing, redesign timing or exclude the affected activity. Never solve a licensing gap by borrowing the seller's credentials informally while the buyer operates under a different entity.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy enforceable operating rights, not a seller's description**
> 
> Resolve the few dependencies capable of stopping revenue before expanding the diligence request list.
> - Request the top ten customer contracts before full exclusivity; rank consent requirements by gross profit contribution, not contract count.
> - Ask the issuing agency about the exact buyer entity and ownership structure; a generic license-transfer FAQ rarely resolves the deal.
> - Have counsel classify every proposed finder or adviser payment before signing a fee letter; keep principal sourcing separate from compensated introductions.
> - Reserve the three contractor slots explicitly and maintain one shared exception register so transaction work does not consume the delivery team's week.

## 2. Turn diligence findings into funded, collectible contract rights  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

For the QoE and contract-and-license plays, draft the purchase agreement from a linked exception register rather than recycling a clean template. Define purchased assets, excluded assets, assumed liabilities, excluded liabilities, transferred contracts, inventory standards, cash treatment, and debt-like items separately. Equity transactions require equivalent clarity about capitalization, subsidiaries, leakage, and liabilities remaining inside the acquired entity. State the working-capital peg, reference accounting policies, sample calculation, delivery dates, objection procedure, and independent accountant's limited role. Otherwise, the seller can win the price negotiation again through closing-account classifications. Tie representations to schedules covering taxes, employment, permits, litigation, cybersecurity, material customers, intellectual property, and related-party arrangements. Require identified customer consents and specified lien releases as closing conditions, not optimistic post-closing tasks. Specify indemnity baskets, caps, survival periods, escrow release dates, notice requirements, and who controls third-party claims. Separate known tax or wage exposures into negotiated special indemnities with realistic security. A broad promise from an empty seller entity is weaker than a narrow remedy backed by retained cash. Counsel should align fraud treatment and exclusive-remedy language with governing law rather than assuming one state's drafting conventions travel unchanged to every deal.

The adviser and sourcing agreements need their own commercial architecture. Scope should identify permitted services, deliverables, excluded authority, confidentiality duties, and the buyer's right to stop work. A fee trigger must say whether compensation arises on signing, funded closing, or receipt of specified consideration; define earnouts, rollover equity, assumed debt, installment payments, and broken transactions explicitly. For a closing-based fee, include a worked calculation and prohibit duplicate claims for the same introduction. Use a narrowly drafted anti-circumvention provision tied to named counterparties, documented introductions, preexisting-contact exclusions, and a defined tail. It should not become an indefinite tax on unrelated acquisitions. A liability cap should identify covered losses and negotiated exceptions, while acknowledging that applicable law can restrict exclusions. Specify governing law, forum or arbitration rules, emergency relief, notice addresses, and treatment of collection costs. IP/data ownership should distinguish buyer-owned work product, preexisting adviser tools, licensed deliverables, and source records supplied by the target. Limit subcontractor access and require return or controlled deletion at termination. Finally, make compensation conditional on lawful performance; a contract cannot manufacture an exemption from broker-registration requirements that the actual services fail to satisfy.

Translate hidden liabilities into a tax, employment, and secured-creditor closing schedule. For applicable asset acquisitions, Internal Revenue Code Section 1060 and Form 8594 require purchase-price allocation reporting; coordinate buyer and seller positions, contingent consideration, and later adjustments. Do not assume the parties can simply label all value goodwill to produce their preferred tax result. State successor-tax procedures require jurisdiction-specific action: California Revenue and Taxation Code Section 6811 addresses purchase-price withholding for certain business sales, while New York Tax Law Section 1141(c) imposes advance bulk-sale notification procedures. Employment diligence should reconcile payroll registers, time records, contractor classifications, accrued leave, benefit plans, and pending claims. Apply FLSA recordkeeping and wage rules, state wage-payment requirements, and structure-specific successor principles; excluded-liability language alone does not settle employee or government claims. Seven employees do not automatically trigger federal WARN, but analyze the actual employer, aggregated operations, transaction timing, and state mini-WARN rules. Search UCC records in the proper debtor jurisdiction, confirm exact legal names, and obtain payoff letters with release mechanics. Also search relevant tax liens and titled assets; a clean UCC search does not establish clean title to everything.

**Six Contract Clauses Converted into Evidence and Remedies**

| Clause requiring explicit drafting | Required substantive contract language | Evidence supporting the negotiated provision | Failure mode prevented by the provision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope separates acquisition deliverables from authority to bind parties | Name services, exclusions, deadlines, permissions, and responsible transaction owners | Signed engagement schedule matches the seven-play transaction responsibility register | Prevents advisers from promising financing or terms without authority |
| Fee trigger defines exactly when compensation becomes payable | Define closing consideration, contingent payments, exclusions, and worked calculations | Closing statement reconciles contractual fee base to actual consideration | Prevents duplicate introductions and unfunded signing events generating disputes |
| Anti-circumvention protects specific introductions without capturing unrelated opportunities | List counterparties, contact exclusions, protected transactions, and finite tail | Dated introduction records distinguish new relationships from existing discussions | Prevents an indefinite fee claim against the acquisition pipeline |
| Liability cap distinguishes ordinary breaches from negotiated exceptional exposures | Specify cap, basket, survival, security, and legally supportable exceptions | Escrow documents and seller resources support realistic recovery assumptions | Prevents paper indemnities from substituting for collectible financial remedies |
| Dispute resolution assigns appropriate forums to different contested issues | Separate accounting determinations from legal disputes and emergency remedies | Notice mechanics and selected rules match every related agreement | Prevents forum conflict from delaying working-capital adjustments and collections |
| IP and data ownership preserve usable rights after termination | Separate assigned work product from licensed tools and restricted records | Employee assignments, software licenses, and access logs support transfer | Prevents purchasing systems that cannot lawfully operate after closing |

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four verifications before authorizing closing funds**
> 
> The owner records a decision against source evidence; unresolved conditions require an explicit contractual solution.
> - Counsel confirms the purchase entity, ownership chain, intermediary permissions, and required third-party consents against signed documents.
> - Finance confirms tax-clearance or withholding procedures, authenticated payoff instructions, working-capital funding, and escrow balances.
> - Operations confirms effective licenses, responsible qualifying personnel, employment arrangements, and customer-service continuity on the transfer date.
> - The lender confirms permitted seller-note terms, current covenants, injection evidence, and the final sources-and-uses statement without conflicting side letters.

## 3. Run honest sourcing and discovery without leaking target information  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Targeted sourcing should start with a manually reviewed business list, documented source, relevant industry thesis, and one accountable sales lead. Do not assume every acquisition inquiry is a commercial advertisement or every business recipient falls outside outreach rules; classify the message and channel before launching a sequence. For commercial email covered by CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. 7704 requires accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, required sender information and advertising identification where applicable, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out. Honor covered opt-outs within ten business days and prevent contractors from reimporting suppressed contacts. Use a cautious first-touch script: 'Subject: Ownership discussion about your business. I am the principal of an acquisition company evaluating established businesses in your sector. Your service mix appears relevant to our search. Would you consider a private conversation about ownership succession? We have not reviewed your financials or obtained financing approval for your company. Reply stop and we will end acquisition outreach.' Append the sender's actual legal identity, required identification, and postal address before sending. Never describe a purchased contact list as a personal referral or imply that an SBA lender has already approved an unnamed target.

For phone outreach, separate live manual calls from prerecorded messages, artificial voices, automated dialing, and texts. TCPA restrictions under 47 U.S.C. 227 and 47 CFR 64.1200 depend on technology, number type, purpose, and consent; state telephone-solicitation and recording rules can add obligations. A published business number is not universal permission for automated campaigns, and an owner may publish a mobile number. Check applicable calling-hour and suppression requirements before contact, retain the basis for any consent, and avoid recording until the applicable authorization is established. Use this live-call opening: 'Hello, I am calling as a potential buyer, not on behalf of your lender or a government agency. Is this a reasonable time for a brief ownership-succession question? If you prefer no further calls, I will record that immediately.' For an intermediary, say: 'Before exchanging targets, please identify your client, authority, licensing or exemption basis, and proposed fee trigger. We will agree scope in writing before introductions.' Log the answer without turning it into a legal conclusion. The sales lead owns suppression records; counsel resolves channel uncertainty before the systems contractor activates HubSpot or another sequence engine.

Discovery begins with authority and information rights, not a request for the entire customer database. Use this seller script: 'Who owns the equity or assets, who can authorize this discussion, and which other approvals are required? What customer, landlord, lender, or license conditions could change on transfer? Please start with redacted monthly statements and a revenue-concentration summary; we will define deeper access under a confidentiality agreement.' Then ask: 'Which earnings adjustments represent costs a replacement operator will actually avoid, and what evidence supports each adjustment?' For employees, ask about roles and retention risks before demanding individual medical or identity documents. Stage access in SharePoint or another controlled data room with named users, expiration dates, and download logs. Where buyer and target compete, counsel should design clean-team restrictions for pricing, customer-level margins, and forward strategy. An NDA does not authorize premature operational control or unrestricted competitive information exchange. Assess whether California privacy requirements or sector rules cover the target and its disclosures; an acquisition-related transfer provision is not permission for unrelated marketing reuse. Keep live credentials, payment-card data, and unnecessary personal identifiers out of routine diligence. Contract for deletion or restricted retention if the transaction ends.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify capacity and channel
   - Identify principal or intermediary capacity and review the intended outreach method.
   - Counsel resolves broker and channel questions; the sales lead records list provenance and applicable suppression requirements.
2. Send the reviewed factual message
   - Use an accurate sender identity, restrained acquisition thesis, and workable stop mechanism.
   - Retain the actual message, required postal information, send date, and recipient record; prohibit invented funding or referral claims.
3. Qualify authority before requesting records
   - Confirm ownership, decision rights, intermediary terms, and confidentiality arrangements.
   - Start with redacted financial summaries; authorize sensitive access by role and use clean-team handling where competition risks require it.
4. Close the information loop
   - Record next steps, suppress declined contacts, and revoke unnecessary data access.
   - The sales lead maintains contact status; counsel and the systems contractor implement the agreed retention or deletion treatment when discussions end.

## 4. Reconcile lender covenants, closing cash, and the first 100 days  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Debt structuring must preserve the scorecard's accounting boundary. Conservative acquired-business sales of $750,000 at 8% produce $60,000 annual owner net profit; Base sales of $1,875,000 at 13.0% produce $243,750; Scaled sales of $3,000,000 at 18% produce $540,000. These margins are after interest and before principal repayments. Purchase price, loan proceeds, seller-note principal, and customer transaction volume are not additional recognized operating revenue. Show principal service on a separate cash bridge, alongside capital expenditure and working-capital changes, without silently changing the canonical profit cases. As an illustrative bridge only, $100,000 of annual principal payments would leave $143,750 from Base profit before additional cash uses; that principal amount is an assumption, not a scorecard input. Build lender coverage calculations from the lender's defined numerator, denominator, testing period, and permitted adjustments rather than treating net margin as debt-service coverage. Review restrictions on distributions, additional debt, liens, acquisitions, ownership changes, and seller-note payments. Keep the $30,000–$120,000 startup-capital range distinct from the $6,000–$25,000 monthly operating-expense planning range; neither range independently proves that a particular purchase price or leverage package works. Use actual target statements and funded terms to establish affordability.

The closing-transition play needs a single funds-flow document connecting the signed agreement, lender instructions, escrow ledger, payoff letters, and post-closing liquidity. Authenticate wire instructions through a known telephone number obtained independently of the payment email; require two-person review and preserve the verification record. Finance should reconcile every dollar to buyer equity, acquisition debt, seller consideration, tax withholding, transaction expenses, and retained working cash. Counsel checks that release obligations cover the assets actually purchased and that recording or filing responsibilities have named owners. The operations lead verifies insurance inception, bank-signing authority, merchant-account arrangements, payroll readiness, and customer-contract consents. Do not assume payment-processing accounts or software subscriptions transfer merely because equipment and goodwill do. For employment onboarding, determine the applicable Form I-9 treatment and document whether the buyer adopts qualifying predecessor records or completes new verification under the relevant successor rules. Review benefit continuity, COBRA obligations where applicable, and accrued-pay treatment with the accounting contractor and counsel. A small buyer can inherit complexity from a larger seller's benefit arrangements. Release funds only under the agreed escrow instructions after required evidence is complete; do not use an informal promise to repair essential operating authority after money moves.

During the 100-day retention play, maintain the contractual and statutory evidence as operating controls rather than archiving everything at closing. The finance lead owns a covenant calendar, tax deadlines, indemnity notice dates, working-capital objection periods, and seller-note restrictions. Operations owns permit renewals, qualifying-person continuity, wage controls, and employee transition commitments. Sales tracks customer contacts, required consents, churn signals, and service promises made during diligence. Use QuickBooks or the target's existing ledger for financial reporting, with controlled exports into a weekly cash forecast; automate reminders and reconciliations before automating unusual judgment calls. Hold weekly exception reviews for thirty days, then biweekly through day one hundred, while contractual deadlines remain individually tracked. Compare retained customer gross profit and actual staffing costs against QoE assumptions instead of celebrating signed revenue alone. Route an undisclosed tax claim or threatened customer termination immediately to the responsible specialist so notice periods do not expire. The seven-employee, three-contractor pod cannot absorb unlimited integration projects: prioritize payroll, collections, licenses, service continuity, and cash visibility. Defer cosmetic system migrations until those controls stabilize. Expand toward eight employees and four contractors only when the Scaled case supports the additional capacity and lenders permit the resulting commitments.
