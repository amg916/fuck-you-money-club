# Chapter 06: Seller Financing, Earnouts, and Bridging the Down Payment

*Seller notes alongside SBA loans, full-standby rule changes and the new 5% minimum, whether a seller note can cover the down payment, pairing 7(a) with seller finance, why sellers agree to carry paper, structuring and documenting earnouts vs. seller financing, all-cash vs. structured deals, SBA personal guarantees, and what to do when the bank declines after the LOI*

## Executive Summary

SBA lenders, brokers, and deal attorneys agree that the seller note is the most-used bridge between buyer cash and the purchase price. It keeps the seller's skin in the game, keeps the old owner answering the phone, and lowers the bank's exposure. The street baseline is a 90% SBA loan, 5% buyer cash, and a 5% seller note. Under the latest rule swing, that note only counts toward equity if it sits on full standby for the life of the loan and covers no more than half the required injection. Operators bridge the 10-year wait by grossing up the price by 5%, offering interest-only where the bank allows, or splitting large notes in two. Earnouts divide the room. Roland Frasier prefers them over any note, while Jason Paul Rogers says formal earnouts will not work with SBA and substitutes a consulting agreement.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every SBA operator treats the seller note as a standard layer of the capital stack, not an exotic add-on. Keira Hamilton [7RAyVC1b9Pg @ 00:00:16] lays out the canonical stack on a $1 million project: $900,000 SBA, $50,000 buyer cash, and a $50,000 seller note. She says SBA lenders love the structure because it proves the seller believes in the business and in your ability to pay them back. Josh Kim bought three businesses before turning 21 on SBA 7A money. He says almost every SBA deal he touches carries seller financing, because the bank does not want the seller throwing in the keys and walking away mid-transition [W3nhTnkKZcU @ 00:01:26]. The SBA University instructor frames it from the buyer's chair. The nightmare is a retired owner on the beach who will not return your call, and owing them money keeps their phone on [s5o1MBGuspY @ 00:01:29]. Loan To Launch banker Daniel supplies the lender math. A seller note can shrink the SBA loan amount, improve deal cash flow, and signal belief in the business, all of which reads as lower risk from the banker's desk [OSZgayF9jHw @ 00:02:26]. The shared baseline: a seller willing to carry paper is a seller vouching for the cash flow.

Practitioners agree the pitch to the seller is not charity. It is a better investment than cash sitting in a bank. Josh Kim pitches it on tax and yield. An installment sale spreads the capital gains hit across multiple years, and a seller who takes $150,000 in cash would earn far less parking it in a savings account or CD [W3nhTnkKZcU @ 00:01:50]. David C Barnett pushes sellers to swap the business-owner hat for an investor hat. Seller notes get written at four to ten percent interest, while the best US high-interest savings rate Barnett found sat a little over half a percent. Note payments can also cover a retiree's monthly expenses, so the seller never has to dip into market accounts [PO1M-_wq-m4 @ 00:11:02]. A partnership-first buyer on Beau Eckstein's channel has run deals at 60 to 100 percent seller financing. That buyer says owners who ran a company for 10, 20, or 30 years genuinely do not need a lump sum and often prefer payouts over time to save on capital gains tax [1-sviMTVjUs @ 00:02:03]. Barnett also flags the cost of refusing. Sellers who will not carry paper sit on the market longer, and their ultimate selling price typically declines as the motivations behind the sale drag on the business [PO1M-_wq-m4 @ 00:03:26].

The third point of agreement: structure gets negotiated with the lender in the room, not bolted on at the end. Escamilla Law Office breaks a $10 million headline into three pieces: $7 million cash at close, a $1 million note the buyer owes, and a $2 million earnout that may never become payable. The firm insists deferred consideration is part of the price rather than a detail to sort out later. It also wants the purchase agreement, seller note, guarantee, security documents, and senior loan built to fit together [ZGklOUqHKYg @ 00:01:36]. Josh Kim says to talk to your lender first, then put seller financing terms in the LOI instead of waiting for due diligence. He warns that most deals die from bad structure rather than bad businesses [W3nhTnkKZcU @ 00:08:10]. Evan DiLeonardi of Itero is blunt that nobody can say up front whether a bank will want a 5, 10, 15, or 20 percent note. His advice is to get a structure greenlit by the lender before you walk it to the seller [zBzv99U7MyE @ 00:04:40]. The SBA University instructor closes the loop. The lender holds ultimate approval authority over how the seller note looks and sounds, so hash out the terms at the beginning, not at the closing table [s5o1MBGuspY @ 00:04:56].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The equity-counting seller note runs on hard rules. Josh Kim separates two animals. A note counted toward the equity injection must sit on full standby for the life of the loan. A note layered on top of a full 10% buyer injection can be structured however you want, as long as three conditions hold: the bank stays in first position, the seller subordinates, and combined SBA plus seller payments still clear 1.25x debt service coverage [W3nhTnkKZcU @ 00:04:29]. Allan Peterson is the SBA lender on JackQuisitions who closed over $70 million in acquisition financing last year. Peterson adds the cap: the standby note cannot account for more than 50% of the required injection. That ends the old play of two years on standby followed by three years of principal-and-interest payments with no balloon [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:22:18]. Owner Actions spells out the floor. Buyer cash of 5% is now the minimum, reachable only with a 5% seller note on full standby across a 10-year term. Sellers who never blinked at 24 months now treat 10 years as a sticking point [lfjqb1es1E4 @ 00:00:16]. Peterson also stresses that the 10% is measured against total project cost, not just the purchase price. Total project cost includes bank-provided working capital and the line of credit Peterson always builds in [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:24:02].

The street tactics for selling a decade-long standby note are specific. Evan DiLeonardi's own first deal closed at 90/5/5, back when a 2-year standby still counted toward the down payment. DiLeonardi recommends stacking the note on top of the price. If you want to offer $1 million, offer $1.05 million and ask for the extra $50,000 on full standby. Alternatively, offer $1,025,000 and split the difference as a sweetener [zBzv99U7MyE @ 00:02:17]. DiLeonardi adds that some banks allow interest-only payments during the 10-year stint, so the seller still collects interest while principal waits, though that sits at the bank's discretion. Allan Peterson has watched buyers in the pipeline request a 5% note for the life of the loan, cite secondary market data showing it would be abnormal to hold the loan to maturity, and then bump the offer by 5% [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:23:27]. Peterson's language tip: always say 'for the life of the loan,' never '10 years.' Then instill confidence that you will pay down early or refinance into conventional business banking once you hold three years of business tax returns as owner [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:30:56]. Host Jack used the same play with a hyper-growth plan projecting a four-to-six-year payoff, and that seller got paid off significantly quicker [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:34:39].

For notes outside the injection and for contingent money, the mechanics get creative. The SBA University instructor splits large notes. A $500,000 note on a $1 million deal becomes two $250,000 notes. The first is greenlit for payments from month one, so it becomes eligible for an SBA refinance after 24 months, at which point payments start on the second [s5o1MBGuspY @ 00:06:43]. Keira Hamilton flags forgivable, performance-based note language: all or part of the note is forgiven if the buyer misses revenue, EBITDA, or customer retention targets within a set period [7RAyVC1b9Pg @ 00:03:03]. Roland Frasier sizes earnout payouts with a simple Excel model. Take a business clearing $4 million with roughly $2 million going to SBA debt service. Paying the seller $600,000 a year for 10 years eats 30% of what is left. Paying $1 million a year hits 50% and leaves little room to wiggle if things go down [onN8dTJIXAM @ 00:06:31]. Jason Paul Rogers skips the formal earnout and writes a sidebar consulting agreement instead, such as an extra $100,000 if a key client does over $1 million in sales during the first six months post close. Rogers says a formal earnout will not work inside an SBA deal, and the legal fees swamp a seven-figure transaction [7w05y7xLcNI @ 00:03:30].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Pre-qualify before you hunt
   - Send the lender a personal financial statement and a management resume to get a bank letter of support.
   - Peterson produces a buyer pre-qualification letter within 24 hours. It needs only a personal financial statement (SBA Form 413 or any template, ballpark figures accepted) plus a resume. That letter becomes your hunting license with brokers and sellers [JackQuisitions — I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:36:56].
2. Get the note size greenlit by the lender
   - Before pitching the seller, ask the lender what note percentage and standby terms they will accept on this specific deal.
   - Some banks accept a 5% note on 10-year standby. Riskier deals with customer concentration may need a 10, 15, or 20 percent note, so get a structure at least initially greenlit before the seller sees it [Evan DiLeonardi | Acquisitions — zBzv99U7MyE @ 00:04:40].
3. Write seller financing into the LOI
   - Put the seller note amount, standby terms, and repayment terms in the letter of intent rather than waiting for due diligence.
   - Kim says to tell the seller on the front end that you expect seller financing. Frame it around the seller's benefits: tax reasons, a guaranteed return, a faster close, and a bank that wants them to keep a piece of the risk [Josh Kim — W3nhTnkKZcU @ 00:08:10].
4. Document the note like a lender would
   - Draft principal, rate, accrual, schedule, balloon, default and cure terms, guarantee, collateral, setoff rights, and subordination language.
   - The note needs principal, interest start, payment schedule, balloon, default and cure provisions, guarantee, perfected collateral, and setoff limits [Escamilla Law Office — ZGklOUqHKYg @ 00:11:35]. Bank counsel will check it for subordination and standby language before closing [Josh Kim — W3nhTnkKZcU @ 00:08:23].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is how long an equity-counting seller note must sit dark, and the answer depends on which rule window a practitioner closed under. ProjectionHub celebrated the change that cut full standby from the life of the SBA loan to only 24 months, modeling a $1,000,000 purchase with $50,000 of seller note where the first installment moved from July 2033 to July 2025 [8Gok1YA-HS8 @ 00:00:42]. Keira Hamilton taught the looser version as partial standby, where at most the buyer pays interest only for the first two years, while noting a lender can still demand a 5-year full standby [7RAyVC1b9Pg @ 00:02:02]. Owner Actions reports the reversal from the deal desk: 5% down is now the floor, the 5% note goes on full standby for the full 10-year term, and in their experience sellers did not bat an eye at 24 months but treated 10 years as a big sticking point [lfjqb1es1E4 @ 00:00:52]. Allan Peterson, the SBA lender on JackQuisitions, calls it a return to normalcy driven by higher default rates and a view that the rollback helped sellers more than buyers [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:32:42]. Any partial-standby playbook is only as current as the SOP it was learned under.

Roland Frasier ranks contingent pay above fixed debt every time: between seller financing and an earnout, the earnout wins because you might not have to pay it, and on a roughly $12 million trucking deal where the owner wanted half in cash, Roland would push the other half into an earnout first and seller financing second [onN8dTJIXAM @ 00:02:44]. Escamilla Law Office refuses to lump the two together: a seller note is credit risk on money the buyer already owes, an earnout is performance risk on money that may never come due, and a vague earnout does not kill the valuation fight, it moves it from before closing to after closing [ZGklOUqHKYg @ 00:05:29]. Jason Paul Rogers says a formal earnout will not work inside an SBA deal and costs too much in legal fees on a seven-figure purchase, so the playbook is a preset price plus a sidebar consulting agreement that pays the seller for hitting retention or sales metrics, such as an extra $100,000 for keeping a key client above $1 million in sales over six months [7w05y7xLcNI @ 00:04:21]. acquiredotcom slots earnouts into high-growth businesses with uncertain short-term performance, but warns they demand trust and breed disputes over performance metrics [3Cgn92T6L1Y @ 00:02:24].

The second fault line is how to buy the seller's patience. Evan DiLeonardi prices it in: offer $1.05 million instead of $1 million and ask for the extra $50,000 on full 10-year standby, or split the difference at $1,025,000, but only where the buyer has leverage, because on competitive deals a rival will offer a structure without a decade-long wait [zBzv99U7MyE @ 00:02:17]. Allan Peterson has watched buyers request a 5% note for the life of the loan, point to secondary-market data suggesting the loan is rarely held to maturity, and raise the offer by 5% to land at 5% down [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:23:27]. The other split is whether the seller stays in the cap table at all. Beau Eckstein's channel pitched partial change of ownership with the seller dropping from 100% to 19% with no personal guarantee, or keeping 1% so an HVAC buyer can run on the seller's license [1-sviMTVjUs @ 00:05:09]. Under the newer SOP, Allan says any retained seller equity forces a stock sale and a 24-month seller guarantee, so the preferred route is a third-party licensed qualifier holding up to 19% with no guarantee, a route Jack backs as a self-described do-not-work-with-the-seller buyer [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:14:01].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive surprises come from the senior lender rewriting the seller deal after the handshake. Escamilla Law Office has seen deals where the commercial terms assumed the seller would be refinanced out at a set point, then the senior lender required the seller debt to stay in place much longer, turning a 2-year credit into a long-term loan and leaving a year-2 or year-3 balloon unpayable if the bank will not permit it [ZGklOUqHKYg @ 00:15:25]. SBA University warns that even after closing the lender has ultimate authority on when seller-note payments start and stop, and if cash flow cannot carry both loans the bank blocks the payments or the refinance, which is why full disclosure has to happen at the start rather than at the closing table or two years in [s5o1MBGuspY @ 00:07:27]. Josh Kim puts it bluntly: most deals do not fall apart because the business is bad, they fall apart because the financing or the deal was not structured correctly to begin with [W3nhTnkKZcU @ 00:07:38]. Evan DiLeonardi's fix is procedural: reach the lender ahead of time, find out how big a note they want, and walk into the seller conversation with a structure the bank has at least initially greenlit [zBzv99U7MyE @ 00:04:40].

Seller-side behavior blows up deals and notes from the other direction. David C Barnett sees sellers demand repayment in 24 or 36 months, and that quick schedule makes the note riskier because the big payments push the business into a cash squeeze where one bad stretch means missed payments [PO1M-_wq-m4 @ 00:08:25]. Barnett has also watched many sellers walk back to the table refusing a note because a financial advisor wants the money under management, countering that few places let a seller earn a 5% yield on a business they know completely [PO1M-_wq-m4 @ 00:12:45]. Escamilla Law Office flags the paper risk: a buyer often forms a fresh LLC with little history and few assets beyond the acquired company, so a seller owed $1 million has to look past the signature to guarantees, perfected collateral, priority against the bank, and any buyer right to set off indemnification claims against note payments [ZGklOUqHKYg @ 00:13:20]. The upside of underwriting like a lender shows up in Barnett's bar deal, a business banks rarely finance: the seller-financer required weekly liquor-system reports and monthly financials, and a few months after closing called the buyer to report someone was stealing [PO1M-_wq-m4 @ 00:13:34].

The buyer's own balance sheet is the last trap, and the economics checks are specific. Jon Stoddard has seen buyers sign a personal guarantee without doing their homework, watch the business tank, and then face the SBA coming for their house and savings, which is rare but catastrophic, so the push is for a limited guarantee, sunset clauses, or performance triggers [104miMfvJRg @ 00:01:35]. Roland Frasier sizes earnout and seller-note payouts against profit left after debt service: on $4 million of profit with roughly $2 million going to the SBA loan, a $600,000 annual payout is 30% of the remainder, and a $1 million payout at 50% leaves little room for wiggle if things go down [onN8dTJIXAM @ 00:07:13]. Beau Eckstein's channel warns that a buyer without seller financing is exposed to SBA rates, reporting variable-rate 7(a) borrowers who started near 7% now paying 11 to 11.5% [1-sviMTVjUs @ 00:04:19]. Allan Peterson notes the SBA minimum debt service coverage is 1.15 and calls that a deal never worth recommending, while Jack recounts a bank refusing to accept a buyer salary dropping from $170,000 to $110,000 in the coverage model, after months of calls before it came off [I6M8wAaqm5s @ 00:45:36].
