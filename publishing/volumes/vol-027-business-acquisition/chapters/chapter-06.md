## 1. Mine Demand Before Building the Seller List  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a buying thesis that identifies who pays, why they renew, and which operating constraint keeps competitors out. A useful first lane is recurring commercial service within a drivable territory: inspection, specialty repair, outsourced testing, or route-based services with documented repeat customers. Use Census County Business Patterns to size establishments, state licensing portals to identify qualified operators, and SAM.gov award records to locate recurring public buyers. An award is evidence of historical demand, not proof that its revenue transfers to a purchaser. Match each entity through its secretary-of-state record, operating website, business address, and license number before enrichment. Build 300 distinct accounts with source URLs, observation dates, ownership evidence, service territory, and a specific reason to investigate. Never infer retirement intent from a person's age or photo. A published succession statement, a broker listing, or a direct owner conversation is a usable signal. Keep the original observation separate from your interpretation in Airtable or HubSpot. This prevents a stale directory entry from becoming an invented seller motivation and makes weekly research reproducible when another analyst takes over the territory.

Map six acquisition-native plays and one adjacent tax-service play to evidence, not slogans. Renewal-Led Demand Mining tests whether repeat sales survive an ownership change: 40 customers paying $500 monthly represent $240,000 annual sales, before churn and delivery costs. License-and-Route Scarcity tests a capacity bottleneck: five additional weekly jobs at $180 yield $46,800 annual sales over 52 weeks, only if licensed labor and travel capacity exist. Retirement-Succession Outreach solves continuity rather than promising the highest bid; a 90-day handover at ten hours weekly requires about 130 owner hours, explicitly priced and scheduled. Referral-Partner Origination gives CPAs, attorneys, lenders, and trade suppliers a precise buyer brief, without demanding confidential client lists. Stalled-Listing Repricing targets documented gaps between asking price and supportable earnings. Working-Capital Recovery examines cash trapped in receivables and inventory without calling that release recurring profit. The seventh play, wotc_hourly_payroll_tax_credit_contingency_engine, can reveal service providers with employer relationships and weak documentation workflows. Treat it as a specialized diligence lane, not an automatic credit attached to every acquisition. Each play must have an observable trigger, a falsification test, and a fallback that still supports the business without speculative improvements.

Demand mining should uncover customer pain before you approach the seller with a valuation narrative. Review public procurement renewals, published inspection requirements, customer reviews, job advertisements, and branch openings. Record patterns such as repeated scheduling complaints or a new service territory, then test them through nonconfidential buyer interviews. Ask prospective customers what causes switching, how vendors qualify, and which work is deferred; do not pretend to represent the target. Ten interviews yielding six mentions of slow emergency response are a hypothesis, not six signed accounts. In the seller meeting, request service-level reports, renewal cohorts, cancellation reasons, and backlog aging to corroborate the pattern. Give each signal a freshness date and a confidence label supported by its source. A license renewal approaching expiration can indicate administrative workload, but it does not establish willingness to sell. Route density likewise requires actual stop locations and dispatch records, shared under appropriate confidentiality terms. If those records contradict the public story, revise the thesis immediately. The best sourcing advantage is knowing which demand is transferable, collectible, and profitable before spending heavily on exclusivity, lenders, and outside diligence specialists.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find Transferable Demand Before Chasing Attractive Multiples**
> 
> A proprietary list becomes useful only when the signal changes the next diligence question.
> - Match the trade name, legal entity, and license holder before outreach; asset ownership and operating permission often sit in different entities.
> - Ask a trade supplier which service categories routinely miss promised lead times, without requesting customer identities or confidential account balances.
> - Track listing edits and removed add-backs over time; a revised earnings bridge often creates a better conversation than another price inquiry.
> - Request anonymized renewal cohorts before customer names; this tests durability while reducing premature disclosure of the seller's commercial relationships.

## 2. Turn Public Signals into Permission-Based Seller Conversations  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run a measured 30-day account cadence rather than a blast campaign. On day one, send a short business email identifying the buyer, the acquisition thesis, and the specific operating feature that attracted attention. Ask whether an ownership-continuity discussion would be useful; do not imply that you already have a valuation or committed lender. On day four, make a manual business call during local business hours and leave one factual message when appropriate. On day nine, mail a concise buyer letter to the published business address. On day sixteen, follow up through email or an existing professional introduction with one relevant question about transition timing. On day thirty, close the loop and stop unless the owner requests further contact. Apply 15 U.S.C. § 7704 where commercial-email rules govern: accurate headers, nondeceptive subjects, required identification, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out. Honor covered opt-outs within ten business days and suppress them sooner operationally. Evaluate 47 U.S.C. § 227 and applicable state restrictions before calls or texts; avoid automated dialing, prerecorded messages, and unsolicited texting as default sourcing methods.

Use funnel arithmetic as a planning hypothesis, not an industry promise. From 300 researched businesses, suppose 240 have verified, lawfully usable contact routes. If 48 owners engage meaningfully, the contact-to-conversation rate is 20%; if 24 accept a discovery meeting, the conversation-to-meeting rate is 50%. Twelve signed confidentiality agreements would represent 50% of those meetings. Six complete initial data packs would represent 50% of signed agreements; three indications of interest would represent 50% of complete packs. One accepted letter of intent from three indications is approximately 33%, and one completed acquisition from that letter requires successful diligence, funding, and closing conditions. The end-to-end illustration is one closing from 300 researched accounts, or approximately 0.33%, not a dependable forecast. Define the first three to ten sourcing wins as diligence-ready opportunities: a willing seller, agreed transaction perimeter, basic records, and a credible funding path. Track completed purchases separately. A first-time operator should not reinterpret ten sourcing wins as ten simultaneous acquisitions; each closing creates integration work and capital exposure. Review conversion by source cohort monthly instead of blending referrals with cold outbound.

Create inbound credibility with a one-page buyer site describing target sectors, geography, operating involvement, capital range, and a secure contact route. Publish a sample acquisition process and explain which records are requested at each stage; do not publish fabricated portfolio logos or lender commitments. A downloadable seller-preparation checklist can attract serious owners without collecting tax returns through an open web form. Use role-based access and an invitation-only document room after confidentiality terms are signed. Allocate an illustrative $3,000 monthly sourcing budget across research, postage, CRM, and travel; over six months, $18,000 divided by six diligence-ready opportunities equals $3,000 per opportunity. At one closing, the same spend equals $18,000 acquisition sourcing cost before legal, accounting, and lender expenses. If the cohort produces no closing, report the spend and zero closings rather than manufacturing a cost per acquisition. Review partner referrals, direct mail, and inbound separately. Twelve partner meetings producing three introductions and one complete data pack reveal where to improve the brief. Do not pay transaction-based referral compensation until counsel has evaluated the transaction, intermediary activities, and applicable federal and state requirements.

**Thirty-Day Outreach Cadence and Objection-Handling Scripts**

| Contact stage and seller objection encountered | Concrete message or next operating action | Evidence required before advancing the conversation | Stop rule or respectful fallback response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day one email receives the objection: I am not selling | Ask whether a future continuity conversation would ever be useful | Record the owner's stated timing and preferred future contact permission | Stop the cadence immediately when further contact is declined |
| Day four manual call receives: Send your best price first | Explain that price requires earnings, working capital, and transaction perimeter | Obtain a seller-approved earnings summary before offering a valuation range | Offer a short record checklist instead of inventing purchase economics |
| Day nine buyer letter receives: You will cut my team | Describe the intended operating model without promising unsupported employment outcomes | Review role coverage, compensation, service capacity, and owner replacement needs | Decline when the required staffing model contradicts the seller's objectives |

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build and deduplicate the weekly target cohort
   - Research 25 businesses using public business records and direct operating evidence.
   - Record the legal entity, source URL, observation date, trigger, fit rationale, contact route, and suppression status before the account enters outreach.
2. Execute the measured thirty-day contact sequence
   - Use the approved email, manual call, buyer letter, and final follow-up sequence.
   - Log meaningful replies separately from automatic responses; stop declined contact immediately and evaluate channel-specific legal requirements before each campaign.
3. Convert interest into a complete screening packet
   - Hold discovery, sign confidentiality terms, and request a limited initial data pack.
   - Collect monthly operating statements, tax-return summaries, customer concentration, owner duties, debt schedules, and the proposed asset or equity perimeter.
4. Score, price, and review each source cohort
   - Advance supported opportunities to an indication of interest and archive failed fits.
   - Reconcile CRM stages to document-room evidence weekly; report spending, stage conversion, accepted letters, and completed acquisitions as separate measures.

## 3. Qualify Earnings, Liabilities, and the Tax-Service Edge Case  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Score opportunities before negotiating exclusivity. Allocate 25 points to earnings evidence, 20 to customer durability, 15 to management transferability, 15 to working-capital quality, 15 to funding feasibility, and ten to transaction readiness. Within each category, award zero for an unsupported assertion, half credit for partial documents, and full credit only for corroborated evidence. A score of 75 can justify deeper work, but it cannot override a disqualifier such as an essential nontransferable license or an unexplained cash discrepancy. Obtain 24–36 months of monthly statements, tax returns through authorized channels, bank records, payroll registers, aged receivables, and customer concentration. Reconcile recognized sales to collections with an explicit bridge for receivables, deposits, refunds, and timing. An add-back is acceptable only when the expense genuinely disappears without replacement cost or revenue damage. If claimed earnings are $250,000 and a necessary replacement manager costs $80,000 fully loaded, the preliminary adjusted figure is $170,000 before other corrections. Do not apply an acquisition multiple to the larger number simply because a listing labels owner labor optional. Commission independent QoE work where transaction size and complexity justify its cost.

Investigate liabilities through entity, asset, and contract trails, not solely through a seller questionnaire. Search the relevant state UCC filing office, inspect tax liens and litigation records where available, and request payoff statements for secured debt. Search results establish a research trail rather than proving that every liability has been located. Counsel should evaluate continuing security interests under the applicable state enactment of UCC § 9-315 and obtain effective releases where required. An asset purchase does not automatically eliminate employment, tax, environmental, or other successor exposure. Map contract assignment provisions, customer termination rights, license changes, and required third-party permissions. For qualifying federal contract transfers, assess FAR 42.1204 rather than assuming a SAM.gov award moves with the equipment. Investigate wage practices against applicable federal and state rules, including 29 U.S.C. §§ 206 and 207 where covered. Translate each unresolved issue into a specific response: price adjustment, retained liability, escrow, indemnity, preclosing cure, or withdrawal. A seller representation is not a substitute for an operational remedy. Ask whether losing the largest customer or key license holder would still leave enough cash for wages, suppliers, interest, and scheduled principal.

The wotc_hourly_payroll_tax_credit_contingency_engine requires separate workflows for Section 51 hiring credits and Section 41 research credits. Before modeling WOTC, verify the operative Section 51(c)(4) hire-date limitation and any enacted extension; do not assume post-2025 hires qualify. For eligible hires, employer-authorized prescreening, timely Form 8850 submission to the designated state agency, certification, hours, and qualified wages support the claim. The general submission deadline is 28 days after work begins, subject to applicable rules and relief. Ten certified employees with $6,000 eligible wages each and the standard 40% rate would generate $24,000 credits, assuming the applicable 400-hour threshold and all other requirements. A hypothetical 15% outcome-linked fee would be $3,600 service revenue only where that arrangement is permitted; the employer's $24,000 credit is not operator revenue. Evaluate professional restrictions, including 31 CFR 10.27 where applicable. Section 41 separately requires qualified research activities and substantiated expenses; routine software use does not establish eligibility. Reconcile wage use across incentives and apply Section 280C adjustments where required. Exclude unsupported claims and use fixed-fee payroll and research-documentation services under independent tax review when outcome-linked economics cannot be supported.

**Evidence-Weighted Qualification Scorecard with Non-Negotiable Failure Conditions**

| Scored diligence dimension and available points | Documents supporting a full-credit operating conclusion | Specific forensic test before pricing the business | Failure response when evidence contradicts the thesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Earnings evidence carries twenty-five points in the screening model | Monthly ledgers, bank records, tax returns, and payroll support earnings | Reconcile collections and remove add-backs requiring replacement labor or spending | Reprice documented earnings or stop when material discrepancies remain unexplained |
| Customer durability carries twenty points in the screening model | Contract terms, renewal cohorts, concentration schedules, and cancellation records agree | Stress the largest account loss using actual contribution and avoidable costs | Require retention protection or decline exposure that available liquidity cannot absorb |
| Management transferability carries fifteen points in the screening model | Role descriptions, compensation records, licenses, and transition schedules establish coverage | Price a qualified replacement for every essential seller operating responsibility | Hire credible coverage before closing or remove the opportunity from consideration |
| Working-capital quality carries fifteen points in the screening model | Receivable aging, subsequent collections, inventory records, and payable terms reconcile | Test seasonal needs and establish a consistently defined normalized delivery target | Adjust purchase terms and exclude uncollectible balances from supported asset value |
| Funding feasibility carries fifteen points in the screening model | Equity evidence, lender feedback, debt terms, and liquidity reserves align | Calculate cash after interest, principal, capital spending, and working-capital needs | Reduce price or leverage when realistic cash coverage fails lender requirements |
| Transaction readiness carries ten points in the screening model | Ownership records, seller authority, licenses, and contract permissions support transfer | Identify unresolved liens, essential permissions, and incompatible seller-note provisions | Resolve essential transfer conditions before accepting expensive binding commitments |

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before Paying for Exclusivity and Full Diligence**
> 
> A high sourcing score cannot compensate for missing authority, unsupported earnings, or an unworkable transfer.
> - Verify the seller's authority and the exact asset or equity perimeter; identify excluded assets, related-party leases, and essential operating permissions.
> - Tie preliminary earnings to bank, payroll, and tax evidence; price replacement management and document every accepted add-back.
> - Obtain lender feedback on the proposed structure, equity contribution, seller-note treatment, and postclosing liquidity before presenting funding as credible.
> - For tax-service targets, independently test program authorization dates, claim documentation, wage coordination, and fee restrictions before assigning value to the backlog.

## 4. Convert Qualified Opportunities into Fundable Offers and a Controlled First Closing  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Sequence the first 4–10 months around evidence milestones. Use months one through three to refine the thesis, establish capital capacity, build the account universe, and test outreach messages. Use months four through six to deepen partner introductions, complete seller discovery, and assemble three to ten credible opportunities as the pipeline permits. The remaining period covers indications of interest, exclusivity, QoE, lender underwriting, definitive agreements, and a controlled first closing. These are planning windows, not deadlines that justify lowering standards. An indication should state the earnings basis, valuation method, working-capital assumption, proposed consideration, and remaining evidence requirements. A letter of intent should distinguish binding provisions from the proposed transaction terms and define access, exclusivity duration, and expense responsibility with counsel. Offer sellers certainty through a visible diligence calendar rather than an inflated headline price followed by predictable retrading. For example, reducing claimed earnings from $200,000 to $170,000 changes an illustrative three-times value from $600,000 to $510,000, a $90,000 difference. Explain the documented replacement cost causing that change. If price expectations remain incompatible, preserve the relationship and revisit after new evidence rather than manufacturing an earnout neither party can administer.

Keep canonical operating cases distinct from acquisition consideration and illustrative sourcing math. Conservative sales are $750,000 at 8%, producing $60,000 annual owner net profit with seven full-time employees and three contractors. Base sales are $1,875,000 at 13.0%, producing $243,750 with the same team. Scaled sales are $3,000,000 at 18%, producing $540,000 with eight full-time employees and four contractors, twelve people total. These margins are after interest and before principal repayments. Purchase proceeds paid to the seller, lender advances, and released working capital are not acquired-business sales. As a separate cash bridge, $243,750 Base profit less $90,000 scheduled annual principal leaves $153,750 before capital spending, working-capital changes, and any other cash adjustments. Do not subtract interest a second time. Startup capital of $30,000–$120,000 and monthly operating outlays of $6,000–$25,000 are planning ranges, not a substitute for a complete target cost structure. Reconcile all payroll, delivery costs, overhead, and interest to the canonical margins rather than forcing annual costs into that monthly range. Do not add the tax-service example's hypothetical fees to these cases; they are diligence illustrations, not extra scorecard revenue.

Structure funding around verified repayment capacity and the lender's current requirements. For an SBA-backed transaction, confirm eligibility, change-of-ownership rules, equity injection, seller-note treatment, collateral, and guaranties against current SBA requirements and the lender's written interpretation. 13 CFR 120.110 identifies ineligible business categories, while 13 CFR 120.150 addresses credit criteria; neither replaces the applicable operating procedures or transaction-specific underwriting. Do not assume a seller note counts as equity or permits immediate payments. Negotiate its standby, subordination, maturity, and payment provisions explicitly. For an applicable asset acquisition, coordinate purchase-price allocation under 26 U.S.C. § 1060 and required Form 8594 reporting. Prepare the first 100 days before signing: payroll continuity, bank access, customer introductions, vendor terms, license coverage, reporting, and a weekly cash forecast. Automate bank-feed reconciliation, invoice routing, and management reporting only after control owners and exception handling are assigned. Keep the seller available for scheduled relationship transfers and document completion. Stop adding acquisitions when integration capacity is exhausted, even if sourcing remains productive. A signed letter records intent; a completed purchase with intact customers, functioning payroll, and supported liquidity is the actual operating win.
