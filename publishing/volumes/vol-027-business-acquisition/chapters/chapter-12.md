## 1. Days 1–7: Define the Buy Box, Staff the Pod, and Ring-Fence Cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start the clock when the sponsor authorizes the search, not when a seller accepts an offer. Candidate #13 typically requires 4–10 months to reach a first deal, so a successful opening month produces a qualified pipeline, documented underwriting, and a funded next stage rather than purchased revenue. Map the work into seven explicit chapter plays: P1 proprietary and broker sourcing; P2 buy-box screening; P3 Quality of Earnings; P4 SBA, conventional, and seller-note structuring; P5 diligence and transaction execution; P6 employee and customer transition; and P7 reporting automation and operating improvement. Assign each record one current play, one owner, one next action, and one evidence link in Airtable or HubSpot. On Day 1, write the acquisition thesis around service geography, recurring demand, transferable licenses, customer concentration, and manager replacement cost. On Day 2, convert the thesis into a rejection checklist and score ten historical listings. Reject a thesis that works only by treating the seller's unpaid labor as free capacity. Record the required operator salary before estimating debt capacity, and distinguish businesses worth investigating from businesses the sponsor can actually fund without consuming the operating reserve.

The canonical Conservative and Base operating configuration is seven full-time employees plus three contractors, not ten new search hires. Use seven employee seats for general management, accounting coordination, sales and account retention, service scheduling, and three delivery roles. Use the three contractor seats for a transaction attorney, a CPA with QoE capability, and an industry specialist covering systems and license transfer. These are functional assignments for a suitable target; map them to actual people before an offer and do not assume every industry supports this organization. Before closing, the sponsor performs sourcing and coordinates staged contractor work; target employees remain under the seller's authority. Request information through authorized seller channels rather than assigning work to staff the buyer does not employ. On Day 3, create a responsibility register showing which tasks become buyer responsibilities only at closing. On Day 4, obtain written contractor scopes with hour caps and exclusions. On Day 5, test a seller package handoff using a redacted sample. Require P3 findings to reach P4 underwriting and P6 staffing assumptions through the same versioned model, preventing separate teams from quietly using different earnings.

Authorize an illustrative $12,000 first-month search ceiling inside the $30,000–$120,000 startup-capital envelope, without implying that its lower end funds every target. Allocate $1,500 to data and outreach, $2,000 to initial legal review, $4,000 to limited accounting diligence, $1,000 to lender packaging, $1,000 to site visits, and $2,500 to contingency. Measure the ceiling as cash already paid plus noncancelable commitments, so an unpaid engagement letter cannot hide spending. Keep purchase equity, closing costs, and operating liquidity in separate ledger categories. The quoted $6,000–$25,000 monthly opex range is a planning input, not the complete expense bridge behind every revenue scenario; verify payroll, direct costs, debt service, and working capital separately. On Day 6, build a thirteen-week cash forecast and tag every proposed commitment to a play. On Day 7, reconcile the forecast with bank balances and contractor scopes. Stop discretionary commitments at 80% of the month ceiling, or $9,600, until remaining work is reprioritized. Never raid the target's payroll reserve to preserve a search schedule or keep an unpromising seller conversation alive.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Pre-Flight Tests Before Paid Diligence Expands**
> 
> Release only the next evidence-producing tranche. A promising listing does not justify funding the entire transaction workstream.
> - Verify sponsor liquidity with current account evidence; separate acquisition equity from search spending and the thirteen-week operating reserve.
> - Confirm a named owner for each of the seven plays and a feasible seven-employee, three-contractor target organization before assuming labor savings.
> - Obtain a seller-authorized document channel, confidentiality terms, and written limits on customer or employee contact before collecting sensitive operating records.
> - Require capped CPA and attorney scopes plus an updated commitment ledger showing first-month search exposure no greater than $12,000.

## 2. Days 8–14: Build a Qualified Funnel and Reconstruct Earnings  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Day 8, assemble 120 deduplicated prospects from broker listings, industry directories, referral partners, and targeted owner research. Distinguish a researched record from a contacted owner and a received package from a qualified opportunity. On Day 9, send twenty personalized first contacts; on Day 10, send twenty more and schedule broker calls. Describe the actual buy box, capital readiness, and management plan without implying committed financing. On Day 11, review responses and request confidentiality agreements only where the seller fits the thesis. On Day 12, seek eight usable packages containing three years of income statements, current year-to-date results, monthly sales, customer concentration, employee roles, and asking terms. Eight packages is an operating target, not a promised response rate. On Day 13, screen every available package using the same criteria; on Day 14, select up to three for deeper work. Record missing documents as specific requests with owners and dates rather than filling model cells with invented values. Keep seller-facing activity under P1, qualification under P2, and paid reconstruction under P3, so a growing contact count cannot masquerade as an expanding investable pipeline.

Reconstruct earnings from source records before negotiating on a seller's multiple. Export monthly general-ledger detail from QuickBooks Online or Xero where authorized, then tie reported sales to bank deposits, merchant settlements, accounts-receivable movements, and sales-tax liabilities. Deposits are corroboration, not a substitute for revenue recognition: loan proceeds, owner transfers, customer prepayments, and collected taxes must not become acquired-business sales. Inspect at least three recent months and the prior year-end bridge during the limited review, then expand the period when seasonality or exceptions justify full QoE. Build an add-back schedule with evidence, recurrence assessment, replacement cost, and proposed lender treatment. An owner's salary is not entirely discretionary when the seven-person employee pod still needs a general manager. Challenge related-party rent against a sustainable lease and replace missing maintenance with a realistic expense. Treat an unexplained revenue reconciliation difference above 2% of the tested period's sales as a Fix trigger, not proof of misconduct. Require a dated explanation within ten business days; falsified records or a refusal to provide material support instead justify killing that transaction.

Preserve the scorecard while testing each target against it. Conservative annual sales of $750,000 at 8% produce $60,000 owner net profit; Base sales of $1,875,000 at 13.0% produce $243,750; Scaled sales of $3,000,000 at 18% produce $540,000. These are acquired operating-business revenues, not purchase prices, loan principal, or transaction values. Monthly arithmetic equivalents are $62,500 sales and $5,000 profit; $156,250 sales and $20,312.50 profit; and $250,000 sales and $45,000 profit. Use those equivalents only as comparison lines, since actual collections may be seasonal. All three profit cases are after interest and before principal repayments. For example, subtracting an illustrative $100,000 annual principal obligation from Base profit leaves $143,750 before other cash-flow adjustments; it does not establish distributable cash. Prepare separate bridges for depreciation, capital expenditure, working capital, and applicable taxes. Do not force an expensive acquisition into the Base case by deleting principal from the cash forecast or adding financing proceeds to revenue. The purchase model must show whether the required return survives the actual price, funding mix, replacement labor, and retained liquidity.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Buy Evidence Before You Buy a Multiple**
> 
> Veteran buyers compress low-cost rejection work and preserve specialist hours for discrepancies that can change price or ownership risk.
> - Ask for monthly sales before the polished presentation; seasonality and sudden quarter-end spikes determine which bank and receivable periods deserve inspection.
> - Price the seller's replacement role before debating discretionary add-backs; a departing owner who dispatches jobs and sells accounts represents two operating dependencies.
> - Send the same short lender package to a limited, tracked lender cohort; compare reserve requirements and seller-note treatment instead of collecting vague borrowing estimates.
> - Maintain a rejected-deal ledger with reasons and evidence; recurring license-transfer or customer-concentration failures are signals to repair the thesis, not increase outreach blindly.

## 3. Days 15–21: Price Debt, Expose Transfer Risk, and Design the Handoff  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

On Day 15, build a sources-and-uses model for each surviving target including price, fees, inventory treatment, working-capital delivery, equity, senior debt, and any seller note. On Day 16, request written preliminary lender feedback on borrower eligibility, business eligibility, equity, liquidity, collateral, and seller-note terms. SBA lending requires attention to 13 CFR 120.110 and 120.150, together with current program procedures and the lender's actual credit requirements. Do not assume a seller note automatically counts as equity or may receive current payments. On Day 17, stress the proposed capital structure with sales down 15%, direct-cost ratios unchanged, fixed cash costs unchanged, and floating borrowing rates up two percentage points. Calculate debt service coverage as consistently defined cash available for debt service divided by scheduled cash interest plus principal on all payable debt. This chapter's underwriting hurdles are at least 1.35 times in the base underwriting case and 1.10 times under stress; they are internal policy choices, not universal SBA thresholds. Use the stricter applicable lender covenant when it differs, and document how standby debt, leases, owner compensation, and taxes enter the lender's calculation.

On Day 18, have the attorney map transfer risks across assets, equity, contracts, licenses, employee obligations, and tax accounts. Search applicable UCC filing offices using verified debtor names and jurisdictions, then obtain payoff and release mechanics rather than assuming an asset purchase erases liens. UCC 9-315 generally preserves a security interest after disposition unless the secured party authorizes a disposition free of it or another rule applies; the controlling state's enactment matters. On Day 19, prepare a transaction-specific diligence index with dated requests for tax filings, litigation, payroll liabilities, permits, leases, insurance claims, and material contracts. In a qualifying asset acquisition, Internal Revenue Code section 1060 requires purchase-price allocation using the applicable residual method; align the agreement and Form 8594 reporting. A letter of intent should identify the intended structure, working-capital mechanism, evidence conditions, exclusivity period, and treatment of seller debt without pretending the final allocation is already resolved. Kill a transaction if a required license cannot transfer or be replaced on an acceptable timetable. Fix a documentable release or consent gap only when a responsible party accepts a dated, verifiable cure.

On Day 20, draft the first hundred days of ownership without exercising control before closing. Identify the ten largest customers, every single-person process dependency, and the employees needed for payroll, scheduling, billing, and delivery continuity. Obtain seller permission before customer or employee contact, and use aggregated data where individual records are unnecessary. On Day 21, test the proposed handoff against the seven employee and three contractor seats. The general manager owns P6 transition, the accounting coordinator owns P7 reporting, and the industry specialist supports systems migration without becoming an invisible permanent employee. Reconcile employment authorization procedures with 8 U.S.C. 1324a and transaction-specific successor practices; employee records need restricted access and clear custody. Keep payroll, banking, and customer communications unchanged until authorized transition steps are ready. Automate a read-only weekly reporting pack before changing invoicing or bank permissions. The minimum pack shows collected cash, receivable aging, gross margin, scheduled debt service, and thirteen-week liquidity. A target requiring a permanent eleventh person at Base economics needs a revised model or a lower price, not an unexplained headcount exception hidden inside a contractor invoice.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the evidence version
   - Archive the seller package, source extracts, and adjustment register under one dated model version.
   - Record reporting periods and permissions; preserve original files so later revisions cannot overwrite the evidence behind a price decision.
2. Rebuild earnings and cash separately
   - Have the CPA reconcile sales and challenge add-backs before the sponsor sizes debt.
   - Keep recognized revenue, interest expense, principal repayments, purchase funding, and working-capital movements in distinct model lines.
3. Resolve transfer and funding conditions
   - Have the attorney and lender assign dated owners to every material release, consent, eligibility question, and financing condition.
   - Advance only conditions with verifiable completion paths; an essential license without a workable transfer or replacement path ends that transaction.
4. Authorize the next bounded tranche
   - Record the investment decision, remaining evidence gaps, contractor-hour limits, and updated thirteen-week liquidity.
   - Authorize a specific investigation or negotiation step rather than unrestricted spending; preserve the seven-play responsibility register through closing.

## 4. Days 22–30: Decide Kill, Fix, or Scale Without Mistaking Activity for Ownership  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

On Day 22, complete the three preliminary underwriting files if enough qualified packages exist; otherwise document the actual count and bottleneck. On Day 23, validate customer concentration against invoices and contract terms, escalating any customer above 25% of sales for specific retention and debt-stress analysis. On Day 24, review draft indications of interest with the CPA and attorney, including the proposed working-capital mechanism. On Day 25, update the lender package with accepted adjustments and unresolved conditions. On Day 26, obtain seller responses on material gaps and identify which can be cured within ten business days. On Day 27, rehearse the seven-play handoff and the first payroll, invoice batch, and weekly reporting cycle using samples rather than live changes. On Day 28, reconcile paid and committed search costs against $12,000. On Day 29, circulate a decision memo with evidence links and a funded next-stage plan. On Day 30, record Kill, Fix, or Scale separately for the thesis, each target, and each sourcing channel. One rejected business does not invalidate a healthy search, and one attractive listing does not validate an uneconomic acquisition thesis.

Apply the decision hierarchy in order: hard failures first, remediation second, expansion last. Kill an individual target immediately for verified falsification, an essential operating right without a viable transfer path, or economics that still miss underwriting hurdles after a documented repricing and restructuring attempt. Freeze new discretionary commitments when cumulative search exposure exceeds $12,000 at Day 30, $21,000 at Day 60, or $30,000 at Day 90. Those later limits extend this illustrative search budget; they are not additional monthly allowances. Fix a bounded evidence gap with a named owner, a ten-business-day deadline, and no more than $1,500 of remediation spending inside the existing ceiling. Scale at Day 30 only with forty delivered first contacts, eight usable packages, three completed screens, and at least one candidate meeting the preliminary debt hurdles. At Day 60, require eighty cumulative contacts, sixteen packages, six underwriting files, and two written indications of interest. At Day 90, require 120 contacts, twenty-four packages, nine underwriting files, and one lender-reviewed, diligence-ready LOI proposal. A seller signature is not required to validate the search process within a 6–18-month acquisition cycle.

Scale means authorize the next bounded search or diligence tranche, not hire ahead of revenue or acquire the first available company. Increase delivered first-contact volume by no more than 25% in the next thirty days while holding package quality and spending limits. If a milestone is missed, diagnose conversion by cohort: replies per delivered contact, usable packages per substantive reply, and qualifying targets per completed underwriting file. Rewrite one bottleneck at a time and retest it before buying more leads. Kill or materially redefine the thesis at Day 90 if twenty-four usable packages and nine completed files produce zero viable targets; insufficient evidence instead earns a bounded Fix, not a fabricated failure rate. After closing, start a separate ownership clock. Expand operating capacity only after four consecutive weeks with at least 95% scheduled service completion, no overdue payroll or debt service, and a thirteen-week cash forecast that stays above the reserve floor. Define that floor as eight weeks of unavoidable cash operating payments plus the next scheduled debt payment, without double counting. Moving from seven employees and three contractors to the Scaled eight-and-four configuration requires a separately funded labor case; the $540,000 annual profit scenario is not permission to spend it early.
