## 1. Ring-fence working capital before negotiating the purchase  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Play one, reserve-first capitalization, starts with a sources-and-uses schedule that distinguishes buyer cash from purchase price. Allocate 55% of the $200,000–$1,000,000 startup envelope to working-capital and liquidity reserves before bidding. The three illustrative budgets below sum to $200,000, $600,000, and $1,000,000; they are funding envelopes, not additional scorecard scenarios. Keep reserves in separately identified business accounts or ledger subaccounts, with weekly reporting of unrestricted balances. Security deposits, lender-controlled cash, escrowed purchase consideration, and unavailable credit lines do not count as spendable reserves. The $600,000 envelope commits $330,000 to reserves and only $180,000 to acquisition equity. If the transaction requires $250,000 of buyer equity, raise another $70,000 or change the transaction; do not silently raid payroll liquidity. Reserve coverage against the illustrative monthly overhead bands equals 7.33 months at $110,000 divided by $15,000, 8.25 months at $330,000 divided by $40,000, and 7.86 months at $550,000 divided by $70,000. Those ratios measure overhead coverage, not total survival runway. Inventory purchases, seasonal receivables, taxes, principal payments, and necessary equipment replacement belong in a separate thirteen-week cash forecast. An apparently comfortable cash balance can disappear when annual insurance premiums and the first post-closing payroll fall in the same week.

Play two, QoE-led price discipline, converts diligence from a report purchase into a funding decision. Commission a transaction-sized Quality of Earnings review that bridges tax returns, bank deposits, sales journals, payroll, and monthly management accounts. Require separate schedules for customer concentration, aged receivables, inventory obsolescence, deferred revenue, owner add-backs, and maintenance capital expenditure. A seller claiming $300,000 of adjusted earnings might include a $60,000 owner compensation add-back while the buyer must hire a $90,000 replacement manager. Substituting the actual replacement cost reduces that claimed earnings figure by $90,000, to $210,000, assuming all other adjustments survive verification. Do not add the same owner salary twice or price the business on labor that somebody still must perform. At a negotiated four-times earnings multiple, that correction changes illustrative enterprise value by $360,000. Treat this as transaction math, not a revision to the canonical annual profit cases. Define the working-capital peg using representative monthly balances and seasonality rather than the seller's best month. Document which receivables, inventory, payables, and customer deposits transfer. If source records cannot support collections or normalized earnings, suspend exclusivity spending, narrow the diligence scope, renegotiate consideration, or leave before irreversible closing costs consume the reserve.

Play three, SBA and seller-note structuring, separates acquisition consideration from operating liquidity. An illustrative $1,000,000 purchase could use $180,000 buyer equity, $720,000 senior financing, and a $100,000 seller note; fees and the $330,000 reserve remain separate uses in the $600,000 startup envelope. This capital stack is arithmetic, not a statement that any lender will accept the structure. Obtain written treatment of equity injection, seller standby, subordination, collateral, distributions, and permitted working-capital uses under the applicable program and loan documents. Under 13 CFR 120.150, repayment ability matters; an eligible business still needs credible cash generation. Model payment commencement dates explicitly, especially when a deferred seller note begins amortizing after the transition. Canonical owner profit already includes interest expense, so subtracting interest again understates cash availability. Conversely, principal repayment is excluded and must be deducted before distributions. An illustrative $72,000 annual principal requirement leaves negative $12,000, $171,750, and $468,000 from the Conservative, Base, and Scaled profits, respectively, before incremental capital expenditure, working-capital changes, and owner-level taxes. These are cash bridges, not new profit forecasts. Reject a financing structure that only works by stripping reserves or assuming immediate customer growth; reduce the price, change payment timing, or select a smaller acquisition.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Protect the reserve where acquisition budgets usually leak**
> 
> An attractive headline purchase price can conceal expensive working-capital delivery, replacement labor, and payment-timing problems.
> - Require an opening balance-sheet illustration with the letter of intent; identify customer deposits and prepaid obligations before agreeing to the working-capital peg.
> - Pay diligence providers against named deliverables and staged scopes; release the next tranche only after bank-to-ledger reconciliation supports the seller's revenue.
> - Negotiate a specific seller transition schedule with hours, introductions, and documentation; avoid paying a vague consulting retainer for availability that never materializes.
> - Request the lender's complete fee estimate and draft debt schedule early; compare reserve balances after closing, not merely the quoted interest rate.

## 2. Install twelve infrastructure layers without buying an enterprise stack  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Play five, lean infrastructure consolidation, starts by inventorying the target's working systems before purchasing replacements. Preserve a reliable point-of-sale, dispatch, inventory, or industry billing system through closing unless ownership transfer or security defects make continued use untenable. The twelve-layer matrix supplies explicit USD budgeting inputs, not live vendor quotations: verify prices, feature limits, taxes, renewal terms, and acquisition eligibility before committing. Annual-billed plans can require cash upfront even when the table expresses monthly equivalents. For ten named users and ten managed computers, the listed paid inputs total $443 monthly: Microsoft 365 $60, Bitwarden $40, QuickBooks $38, Gusto $91 for seven employees, Dropbox Sign $15, Make $9, and Backblaze $90. The remaining listed tiers carry zero subscription charges under the stated assumptions. That produces $5,316 in annual recurring subscriptions before taxes, transaction fees, implementation, and specialized operating software. Against the $12,000 infrastructure allowance, $6,684 remains for those additional items. Contractor payment charges, extra CRM seats, backup exclusions, and additional payroll jurisdictions require separate quotes. Do not confuse cheap software with low business overhead: seven employees, insurance, premises, materials, and debt service dominate this model. Each new subscription needs a named owner, export method, cancellation date, and measurable operational purpose.

Configure identity before automation. Use individually assigned Microsoft 365 accounts, multifactor authentication, separate administrative credentials, and a documented recovery contact controlled by the buyer. Store service credentials in Bitwarden collections with role-based access; never inherit the seller's shared mailbox as the permanent administrator account. Microsoft 365 Business Basic does not supply a complete endpoint-management and device-security program, so inventory device protection separately and budget any missing controls. QuickBooks Simple Start is a narrow planning baseline, not a fit for every acquired company: inventory, multiple operational users, consolidated entities, and detailed job costing can justify a different tier or retention of the incumbent ledger. Never share one login to evade seat limits. Keep payroll in Gusto only after verifying state coverage, tax setup, historical balances, benefit deductions, and the closing-date employer arrangement. HubSpot's free tier should serve a small relationship handoff, not a complex sales organization. Export customer and supplier records before changing account ownership. Run a test invoice, a test credit, and a bank reconciliation against controlled sample records. If migration fails, retain the existing system under an explicit transition arrangement and delay the cutover; the buyer needs uninterrupted collections more than a fashionable technology architecture.

Automation belongs behind reconciled source data. Use Airtable for a short transition register, Make for bounded notifications and document routing, and Looker Studio for read-only reporting. Do not let an untested integration create supplier payments or change bank instructions. At an assumed 400 workflow runs per month using twenty billable credits each, an 8,000-credit workload fits the matrix's 10,000-credit planning tier with a 2,000-credit buffer; validate actual vendor metering because connector actions can consume different amounts. Route failures to a named operator and retain a manual checklist that works without the integration. Backblaze workstation backup protects supported local files, not automatically every cloud mailbox or database; inventory each data source and test its separate export or backup path. Cloudflare DNS access must transfer with the domain registrar account, recovery methods, and billing ownership. Reduce duplicate subscriptions only after confirming retention and export requirements. For twelve users, twelve computers, and eight payroll employees, the same pricing inputs total $487 monthly, assuming unchanged tiers elsewhere. The modest $44 increase illustrates why software seat optimization cannot rescue weak acquisition economics. Review the stack after thirty days of actual transaction volumes, then upgrade the specific bottleneck rather than purchasing a bundled suite to anticipate hypothetical complexity.

## 3. Underwrite credit honestly and isolate inherited liabilities  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Play four is aged_shelf_corp_tradeline_credit_stacking, the TRACK_1 aged-entity and tradeline credit discipline play. Compare a reinstated entity with a newly formed acquisition vehicle on documented liabilities, ownership, filings, banking acceptance, and actual financing terms; entity age is not operating history. A ten-year-old dormant corporation does not have ten years of revenue, and purchased tradelines do not establish repayment capacity. Never attribute the seller's operating record to a separate borrower without accurately explaining the transaction and supplying lender-requested evidence. Verify reinstatement costs, tax arrears, judgments, prior guarantees, and liens before placing capital into an old entity. Promotional credit requires equally plain arithmetic. A hypothetical $50,000 facility with a twelve-month zero-interest promotion and a 3% upfront fee costs $1,500 before other charges. Retiring principal within twelve months requires $4,166.67 monthly, with the final payment adjusted for rounding. Confirm whether the promotion covers purchases, transfers, or cash advances and whether guarantees, utilization changes, or missed payments alter the terms. If $20,000 remains afterward at an illustrative 24% APR, simple first-month interest is about $400 before issuer-specific daily calculations. Halt applications when disclosures or repayment capacity fail; correct the record, use a transparent term facility, or reduce financed spending rather than opening more accounts.

Play six, liability isolation, begins with transaction structure rather than an entity filing receipt. An asset purchase can define assumed obligations, but statutory successor exposure, liens, tax claims, and contract restrictions still require investigation. An equity purchase generally leaves the target's obligations inside the acquired entity. Maintain separate operating accounts, books, contracts, and documented intercompany transfers where multiple entities are justified; extra companies without operational purpose add fees and reconciliation risk. Bank onboarding requires accurate entity and authorized-person information under the bank's customer identification procedures, including the framework in 31 CFR 1020.220. Confirm legal names and payment instructions through independently obtained contact channels before wiring closing funds. Search applicable state UCC records under the debtor's exact legal name, obtain payoff letters, and coordinate lien releases. UCC 9-315 explains why a security interest can continue in collateral after disposition unless the applicable exception or secured-party authorization applies. A database search alone does not establish clean title. Have transaction counsel address state enactments, tax liens, titled assets, and any required clearances. For applicable asset acquisitions, reconcile the purchase-price allocation under 26 U.S.C. 1060 and the parties' Form 8594 reporting. If evidence remains incomplete, change closing deliverables or escrow arrangements rather than treating a seller's verbal assurance as a release.

Insurance and employment hygiene protect the same reserve as credit discipline. Obtain bindable quotes before finalizing the capital budget, with the correct legal entity, operations, locations, payroll, vehicles, and revenue estimates. Compare general liability, property, workers' compensation, cyber, professional liability where relevant, and business interruption against the actual operating exposures. Claims-made policies need particular attention to retroactive dates, prior acts, and extended reporting options; a new policy may not cover earlier events. An illustrative $12,000 annual premium paid upfront consumes $12,000 of cash immediately even though management reporting recognizes $1,000 monthly. Record that timing difference in the cash forecast. Verify license transferability directly with the issuing authority, including ownership-change requirements for regulated trades and locations. Review employee classification, accrued leave, benefit obligations, wage claims, and payroll tax balances without assuming the seller's labels are correct. Under 8 CFR 274a.2, choose and document the applicable Form I-9 approach for continuing acquired employees; retaining predecessor records carries responsibility for their defects. Separate employees from genuine contractors based on the applicable legal tests, not the desired headcount. If coverage, licenses, or payroll setup cannot support uninterrupted operations, arrange a lawful documented transition or defer the affected activity instead of spending reserves to improvise after closing.

## 4. Prove the cash bridge and launch a controlled 100-day turnaround  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Play seven, a cash-led 100-day turnaround, starts before closing with a rehearsal rather than a transformation announcement. Build an opening cash forecast from bank balances, collectible receivables, supplier terms, payroll dates, taxes, debt schedules, and committed capital expenditure. Assign every input an owner and refresh the forecast weekly. The canonical business economics remain $750,000 sales less $690,000 expenses for $60,000 profit, $1,875,000 less $1,631,250 for $243,750, and $3,000,000 less $2,460,000 for $540,000. These expense totals include interest and exclude principal repayment. For planning, allocate $15,000, $40,000, and $70,000 monthly to a defined recurring overhead subset, respectively. Annualized, those amounts equal $180,000, $480,000, and $840,000, leaving $510,000, $1,151,250, and $1,620,000 of other scorecard expenses. Those residuals cover costs outside the selected overhead subset; they are not spare cash. Define the account mapping explicitly so labor, premises, software, and interest cannot be counted twice. The monthly range is therefore not a claim that the entire acquired company operates on that amount. If a target's actual cost structure cannot fit these definitions without hiding expenses, display the variance and re-underwrite the transaction rather than changing the canonical profit labels.

Stress the cash forecast using customer behavior rather than a uniform percentage reduction applied to profit. At Base sales of $1,875,000 annually, average monthly revenue is $156,250. An additional fifteen days of collections delay ties up approximately $78,125 when using a thirty-day month and assuming evenly distributed credit sales. That consumes 23.67% of the $330,000 reserve without changing recognized revenue or necessarily changing earnings. A customer representing 20% of Base sales accounts for $375,000 annually; losing that account reduces profit by the lost contribution after avoidable costs, not automatically by $375,000 or by 13% of that amount. Obtain customer-level gross contribution evidence before assigning a downside profit figure. Also test a supplier moving from thirty-day terms to payment before shipment, an equipment failure, and the first scheduled seller-note payment. Set intervention triggers in cash terms, such as forecast unrestricted cash falling below the next two payrolls plus committed tax and debt payments. Responses should have identified owners: accelerate undisputed collections, postpone discretionary projects, negotiate documented supplier terms, or draw an already authorized facility within its covenants. Do not count unapproved credit applications or hoped-for owner contributions as available liquidity. Re-run the forecast after each material customer or supplier change.

Before releasing closing funds, require evidence that the buyer can collect money, pay people, access essential systems, and satisfy transaction conditions on the first operating day. The readiness matrix below names artifacts rather than ceremonial meetings. Rehearse payment approvals with a harmless test transaction where permitted, restore a selected backup, validate invoice routing, and walk through payroll calculations without accidentally submitting duplicate wages. Retain written owner assignments for banking, accounting, customer retention, personnel, and technology. Conservative and Base staffing remain seven full-time employees plus three contractors; Scaled remains eight plus four, or twelve people. Do not present external advisers as an invisible operating workforce when recurring work belongs in the cost base. During days one through thirty, stabilize billing and customer relationships; during days thirty-one through sixty, correct reporting and purchasing leakage; during days sixty-one through one hundred, pursue measured pricing and process changes. Keep the original cash forecast alongside actual results so favorable timing cannot masquerade as operating improvement. If opening balances, licenses, insurance, or debt conditions remain unresolved, escalate to the responsible transaction professional and delay the affected commitment. The completion standard is an evidenced operating capability with funded liquidity, not a collection of signed software contracts or an ambitious integration calendar.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the funding model
   - Reconcile buyer capital, purchase consideration, transaction expenses, and reserves.
   - Use the selected startup envelope, identify each financing source, exclude restricted balances, and preserve the 55% reserve allocation.
2. Rehearse operating continuity
   - Test collections, payroll preparation, access recovery, and ledger reconciliation.
   - Use controlled samples, prevent duplicate submissions, retain test evidence, and assign a manual fallback to each essential workflow.
3. Verify transaction conditions
   - Collect written evidence for financing, liens, licenses, insurance, and employer setup.
   - Have the responsible professionals resolve discrepancies and match every closing obligation to a named owner and specific artifact.
4. Run the first hundred days from cash
   - Refresh the thirteen-week forecast weekly and compare actual results with underwriting.
   - Stabilize customers first, repair reporting second, and authorize measured improvements only after cash impacts and fallback procedures are explicit.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight verifications before irreversible commitments**
> 
> A verification passes only when another responsible operator can inspect the evidence and reproduce the underlying reconciliation.
> - Capital verification: bank evidence reconciles to every closing use, restricted cash is excluded, and the designated working-capital reserve remains funded.
> - Economics verification: source records support acquired-business sales, normalized costs, interest, principal schedules, and the thirteen-week cash forecast without double counting.
> - Continuity verification: named owners demonstrate invoicing, payroll preparation, administrative access, backup recovery, customer handoffs, and effective insurance.
> - Authority verification: transaction counsel and the lender resolve applicable entity, lien, license, employment, tax, disclosure, and financing conditions before the affected funds move.
