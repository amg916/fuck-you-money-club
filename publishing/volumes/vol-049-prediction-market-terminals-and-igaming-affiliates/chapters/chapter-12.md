## The Practitioner Consensus

The registration stack is the first thing every practitioner points to. Brokerchampion lays out the chain plainly: the exchange, CME or ICE, lists the contract, the FCM holds the margin, and the introducing broker is the retail-facing layer, with CPOs, CTAs, swap dealers and RFEDs filling out the roster. The operator warning is that the broker brand on the screen may not be the entity actually holding your funds [CIinXD9tbVo @ 00:01:06]. Simple Explain describes the IB as the futures equivalent of a stockbroker: it owns the client relationship, hands execution to an FCM, and splits fees and commissions with the executing firm by agreement, registered with the CFTC and regulated by the NFA [Osx2O3SjlCA @ 00:00:38]. The economics are specialization. Most FCMs find nationwide offices financially impossible, so local IBs, often one-person shops, supply the client reach while the FCM carries platforms, account management and exchange relationships that most IBs cannot afford to build. Earn2Trade's host Chris states that anyone entering commodity futures must register with the CFTC first [fGEHMyG3ktY @ 00:02:17], and Brokerchampion says a broker missing either CFTC or NFA registration is operating illegally [dvFmfWlkYq0 @ 00:08:04]. Their rule of thumb is blunt: if either registration is missing, walk away.

On customer money, the consensus starts with segregation and daily visibility. Brokerchampion traces daily reporting of customer funds to the 2011 MF Global collapse, with any shortfall triggering immediate action, and is blunt that the CFTC does not reimburse trading losses [CIinXD9tbVo @ 00:04:04]. At the CFTC customer-collateral roundtable, Bill Thumb of Vanguard/ICI said clients are astonished to learn they carry fellow-customer risk inside an FCM pool and have no way to diligence it; the prize everyone keeps circling is portability, margin walled off from both the FCM and the defaulting client [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 00:09:09]. Gary DeWaal of Newedge put a number on the concentration problem: about 10 firms hold 80% of customer funds, which they called an incredible concentration [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 00:52:22]. Even speakers who fought over LSOC agreed the buy side will pay for safety; the Kalpers speaker framed it as return of capital, not return on capital. Vanguard's public-disclosure wish list ran to daily segregation reports, residual interest, monthly unaudited financials and leverage, and reportable events. At the follow-on disclosure roundtable, Todd, speaking for grain hedgers, said post-MF Global due diligence and the ability to switch FCMs are what matter, and that information plus the ability to port is the final backstop when mandated clearing leaves no alternative [vqL3OS8Ot9o @ 02:26:16].

For AI trading agents, the practitioners converge on one principle: a human or a firm stays on the hook. At the CFTC Frontier Forum, Chairman Selig framed the open questions as developer registration, liability and know your agent, and said regulation should rest on function, control and economic reality [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 00:08:29]. Kush Bavaria of ORN said the firm is liable for its agents and recommended chain-of-thought review and red-teaming, while Brendan Ryan of Tempo said the operator is liable and that risk controls and audit logs belong outside the agent harness [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 01:04:27]. Andrew Ciraa of ICI compared an agent blunder to an Excel spreadsheet error: the fund manager stays accountable, and that should not fundamentally change [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 02:23:26]. The AI in Regulated Industries narrator lands in the same place from the European angle, arguing know your agent already exists under MiFID II in everything but name, and that the move is to register the principal, not the developer, and tag every order with an agent ID [bsDlbXqvPok @ 00:02:19]. Brett Harrison of Architect added the operating reality: CME already requires pre-registered operator IDs that feed commitments of traders and position limits, and venues reject orders without them. As one Frontier Forum panelist put it, the super intelligent agents are super, but they're not special.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The retail forex rails are hard numbers. Brokerchampion says RFEDs must register and join the NFA, with leverage capped at 50:1 on seven majors and 20:1 on everything else, against offshore brokers that commonly advertise 500:1 or 1,000:1 [CIinXD9tbVo @ 00:01:56]. In dollar terms, $1,000 controls up to $50,000 on a major or $20,000 on other pairs, with the EU/UK cap at 30:1 [dvFmfWlkYq0 @ 00:03:58]. The capital wall is a $20 million minimum for an RFED, which is why Pepperstone, IC Markets and Tickmill refuse US clients; on top sit fund segregation, daily reporting, FIFO execution and a ban on same-pair hedging [CIinXD9tbVo @ 00:02:46]. Rule 4.41 forces a hypothetical-results disclaimer on performance marketing. Enforcement firepower is real: FY2024 brought 58 actions and over $17 billion in relief, dominated by spoofing and crypto cases. The verification drill is three steps: run the firm through NFA BASIC by name or ID, search the CFTC enforcement database including principals and disqualification records, then pull the monthly RFED financial filings [dvFmfWlkYq0 @ 00:07:10]. Complaints escalate from NFA to CFTC to DOJ. Brokerchampion's closing filter is simple: the broker worth using is the one whose registration, funds and conduct you can verify.

Inside the collateral plumbing, the CFTC roundtable speakers put timing and cost numbers on the table. Ron Filler of New York Law School noted MF Global had about 35,000 futures accounts, DCOs had floated 50-70% margin increases, and guarantee funds of about $3 billion could balloon to $25-50 billion or more under LSOC [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 00:20:27]. A Kansas City Board of Trade speaker said customers get five business days to meet a margin call, and a DCO cannot wait that long. On the CME-SIBA guaranteed clearing participant model, Mark of SIBA said the FCM gets notified 45 minutes to an hour before 7:30 central to top up the DCO settlement account, with excess margin sitting in an individual segregated account in the customer's name for the benefit of the DCO [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 02:09:56]. Scott Paris of BMO Harris countered that individual segregation turns net payments into many separate early-morning DCO calls that must be met within 60 minutes, with no flexibility [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 02:22:09]. Joe of the KC Board of Trade priced the wire burden: 250 days of variation margin at $4 per wire comes to about $1,000 of DCO expense against a $4 fee [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 02:47:09]. Mark called custody cost a red herring at about a couple of thousand dollars for an account.

For machine traders, the numbers come from the CFTC's own meetings. At the Regulation AT supplemental meeting, the Chairman said more than 70% of futures trading is automated; the registration trigger was an average of 20,000 contracts a day over six months across all products and DCMs, own and customer accounts counted, with staff estimating about 120 AT persons, roughly 70 already registered and 50 new [ec9Mk3slriY @ 00:33:25]. Source code and log files carried five-year retention, reachable only by subpoena or a Commission-authorized special call. Chairman Selig later said that rule was never finalized and is ill suited to today [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 00:02:40]. On product mechanics, Liam Smith of Optiver walked through the onshore perpetual: funding is paid typically every 8 hours, capped near 2% per 8 hours or 6% a day; with the index at $100,000, the perp at $101,000 and a $10,000 notional long at 1%, $100 moves to the short [ZEPjpgVSavY @ 00:08:40]. Onshore liquidation cancels open orders and sends a market order, with residual losses hitting the guarantee fund or default waterfall. For agents, the AI in Regulated Industries narrator's build list is concrete: per-agent and per-strategy IDs, a named human owner, hard limits, logged mandates and a tested kill switch [bsDlbXqvPok @ 00:03:00].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The customer-collateral fight splits cleanly between clearing infrastructure and the buy side. Tim Dor of CME attacked the bankruptcy legal basis for extending legal segregation with operational commingling (LSOC) to futures. Dor argued that 24x6 trading, post-execution give-ups and accounts that come and go make end-of-day portfolio reports 'dubious', and urged regulators not to 'rush to judgment' [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 00:04:00]. Bill Thumb of Vanguard/ICI countered that clients are 'astonished' to learn they carry fellow-customer risk they cannot diligence. Thumb sold portability as the real prize, with FCMs sharing records with DCOs daily under 22.11(c) and DCOs verifying them under 22.11(e) [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 00:09:09]. Ron Filler put the cost on the table: DCOs had floated 50 to 70 percent margin increases, and guarantee funds near $3B today could balloon to $25B-$50B or more under LSOC [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 00:20:27]. Dan McGuire of LCH.Clearnet flatly disputed that math. LCH has run LSOC in Europe since December 2009, protects value net of haircuts, and McGuire said 'We do not expect to increase margins' [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 01:49:38]. The Kalpers camp said the buy side will happily pay extra margin for 'return of capital not return on capital'. State Street's Steven Winter insisted LSOC would not have stopped MF Global and pushed a custody solution instead.

Public disclosure of FCM health is the second open split. Bill Thum of Vanguard, speaking for $2T under management and 9M shareholders, demanded public daily segregation reports, daily or average residual interest, monthly unaudited financials and leverage, and reportable events [vqL3OS8Ot9o @ 01:44:08]. Alessandro Cocco of JPM pushed back that proprietary margin and customer counts should be handed over on request, not published to the world [vqL3OS8Ot9o @ 01:49:28]. Tom of NFA split the difference with three buckets: public for all FCMs, mandatory on customer request, and regulators-only so a 'Lifeline' can be extended before disruptive information hits the market. Tom also flagged that leverage figures come out 'vastly different' depending on whether seg funds are counted [vqL3OS8Ot9o @ 02:14:27]. The early-warning camp described quietly converting a weak firm into an introducing firm or transferring accounts out, and warned that public leaks kill that window because 'the rumor becomes reality' [vqL3OS8Ot9o @ 02:44:12]. Grace warned intrusive disclosure could push broker-dealers and FCMs back into separate entities. A panelist named Bill urged a holistic view that errs toward not disclosing, calling MF Global and Peregrine 'two bad apples'. Grain hedger Todd went the other way, saying post-MF Global diligence and the ability to switch FCMs justify more public disclosure.

Automated and agentic trading draws the sharpest regulatory split. On Regulation AT, Commissioner Giancarlo called special-call access to source code an 'end run' around subpoenas and 'a reckless step onto a slippery slope', citing the OPM breach of 21.5M records [ec9Mk3slriY @ 00:54:13]. The Chairman shot back that enforcement had obtained code many times without a breach, that 'this is not a slippery slope this is an uphill climb', and the vote went 2-1. At the Frontier Forum, Andrew Ciraa backed an internet-style liability shield for agent providers absent fraud or misconduct. Another panelist argued the Section 230 logic of encouraging adoption 'does not necessarily pertain' because superintelligence is already spreading fast [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 02:25:58]. A show of hands on who breaks the CEA when an agent independently places and cancels large orders split badly. Brendan said the customer, Nick said the model developer, Brad said customer and FCM, and nobody blamed the agent provider [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 02:39:21]. The AI in Regulated Industries narrator rejected developer registration entirely: register the principal and tag every order with an agent ID, as MiFID II already does with per-strategy algorithm codes. The same narrator conceded that an ID shows who an agent works for, not what it will do [bsDlbXqvPok @ 00:02:19].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The customer-fund failures that rewrote the rulebook all hit people who trusted the wrong layer. Brokerchampion warns that the broker brand on the screen may not be the entity actually holding the money. The channel adds that MF Global's 2011 collapse is why customer funds are now reported daily, with shortfalls triggering immediate action, and that the CFTC does not reimburse trading losses [CIinXD9tbVo @ 00:04:04]. Gary DeWaal of Newedge recalled Volume Investors failing in 1985, when three traders acting in concert dragged the whole firm down through fellow-customer risk. DeWaal later flagged that about 10 firms hold 80 percent of customer funds, calling it 'an incredible concentration' [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 00:12:45]. One roundtable panelist put the run in hard numbers: '75% of client funds left 5 days prior to its default' [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 01:50:04]. Michael at the customer-protection roundtable tallied MF Global and Peregrine at about $1.8 billion in lost customer funds. Michael argued that the collapse in confidence cost far more, and warned that letting an FCM put its hands on seg money is 'an open invitation to theft' [vqL3OS8Ot9o @ 02:45:29]. Before MF Global, small investors assumed an FCM segregated account was safer than a savings account, and that assumption is exactly what burned them when the firm went down.

Mechanical traps punish operators who never ran the numbers. Liam Smith of Optiver, speaking for PTG, described perpetual funding as a payment between participants, typically every 8 hours, capped around 2 percent per 8 hours or up to 6 percent per 24 hours. On a $10,000 long at a 1 percent rate, $100 moves to the short. Smith told traders to learn each venue's premium math and observation periods [ZEPjpgVSavY @ 00:09:43]. Onshore liquidation at maintenance margin cancels open orders and fires a market order rather than using last price, with residual losses falling to the guarantee fund or default waterfall. Offshore venues lean on auto-deleveraging and higher leverage instead [ZEPjpgVSavY @ 00:10:41]. Brokerchampion notes that offshore brokers commonly advertise 500:1 leverage against the US cap of 50:1 on seven majors and 20:1 elsewhere, and that Pepperstone, IC Markets and TickMill do not serve the US at all [dvFmfWlkYq0 @ 00:04:35]. In clearing plumbing, Scott Paris of BMO Harris said individually segregated calls must be met within 60 minutes with no flexibility. Joe of the Kansas City Board of Trade priced the drag: 250 days of variation margin at $4 per wire comes to about $1,000 of DCO expense against a $4 fee [yUqRhv7w9Vs @ 02:47:09].

Paperwork and automation failures are the quiet killers. Pete, a finance lawyer with 22 years at the CFTC reviewing chief compliance officer reports, watched a London bank's chief legal officer spend what Pete estimates at 'six figures every year' on New York lawyers. The foreign swap dealer could have filed its unredacted home-regulator report for free [BO0iMB23sU4 @ 00:01:36]. The trap is getting cute. Hybrid 'Frankenstein' reports and filings redacted down to swaps or futures sections get rejected because the CFTC cannot verify what was omitted. The commonly dropped 3.3(e) items are exactly what the operating divisions and enforcement review, and Pete's verdict is 'It's a yes or no' [BO0iMB23sU4 @ 00:07:27]. On agents, Ronet Jane of Liquid Compute warned that correlated agents can trigger mass liquidity withdrawal and that a single model-provider outage can leave stale quotes. Jane said each agent needs its own limits plus collective limits across a customer's agents [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 00:53:11]. Kush Bavaria of ORN ranked prompt injection as the top risk and demanded hard limits and kill switches. Brendan Ryan warned that agents exploit vulnerabilities better than they patch them. Brett Harrison pointed to the roughly 50x Signal OTC spike as the risk 'times a thousand' [Ckw1w5mSMIQ @ 02:13:11].
