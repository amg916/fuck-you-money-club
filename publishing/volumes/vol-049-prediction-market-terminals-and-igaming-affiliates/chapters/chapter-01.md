## The Practitioner Consensus

Start with venue choice, because every practitioner frames the build around the same two rails. Robot Traders calls Kalshi the only CFTC-regulated prediction market platform in the US, notes it raised $1 billion at roughly a $22 billion valuation with 5 million monthly users, and stresses that its API is free and well documented [yYjo1lzNoGI @ 00:00:17]. Part Time Larry runs a funded Kalshi account after passing KYC with their ID, wires it to a normal bank account in fiat, and contrasts that with Polymarket's crypto element and unclear country-by-country legality, while conceding Polymarket carries at least ten times the volume and dominates the election odds quoted on TV and Twitter [E2mgWN4ReqQ @ 00:02:22]. Defined adds the settlement trade-off: Kalshi resolves on authoritative official sources, which helps compliance and user trust, but settlement can take from about an hour to more than 12 hours after close, while Polymarket geoblocks and runs a separate US-regulated product so global and US users see different catalogs [LefzH8sYzRI @ 00:07:39]. ShaunsTake states the motive plainly: after being limited to $5 on PrizePicks, banned on Dabble, and capped at $25 on Underdog, they moved to prediction markets as platforms that will not limit you for winning [A3KSnSZOPtM @ 00:09:42].

The second consensus point is that raw venue APIs do not speak the same language, so normalization is the real work. OngPredicts opens by saying Polymarket and Kalshi agree on almost nothing: Polymarket returns prices as a list packed inside a string beside two token IDs more than 70 digits long, while Kalshi returns a ticker with bid and ask written as dollar strings, and Kalshi's native book only sends bids, one list for yes and one for no, leaving you to derive asks [OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:00:32]. Part Time Larry hit the same wall from the research side, building a betting model that uses semantic matching because the wording of Fed rate-cut, payroll, Beyonce album, and China-Taiwan bets differs across venues [E2mgWN4ReqQ @ 00:04:04]. Defined ranks Codex first precisely because it eliminates stitching together venue-native APIs, normalizing symbols, IDs, and resolution statuses into one GraphQL-style schema with websockets across Polymarket and Kalshi [LefzH8sYzRI @ 00:03:24]. OngPredicts' own countdown lands on Predictify for the same reason: 16 venues through one normalized API, plus grouping of equivalent markets so a builder can ask where a question is trading rather than designing around whichever venue they integrated first [HtGMBJ7ynjs @ 00:04:13].

Every serious builder also agrees that a price gap is a pointer, not a trade, and that resolution rules decide everything. OngPredicts pulled the Dan Sullivan Alaska Senate market at 26 and 27 cents on Polymarket versus 23 and 24 on Kalshi, a 3-cent gap that looks like buying yes at 24 and no at 74 for 98 cents against a $1 payout, but Predictify labeled it an indicative comparison and judged the markets not equivalent because they differ on who decides the result and how the deadline is handled, before fees and real share depth even enter [OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:04:29]. Part Time Larry learned it on a 41-cent test bet: they bet three Fed cuts in 2024, lost, because the 25, 25, and 50 basis-point moves counted as four cuts under Kalshi's rules, and now insists you read the rules or have the LLM read them [Ib0BEFKAvn0 @ 00:13:30]. Three Markets caught Grok trading a Google most-searched market without ever checking the resolution criteria or the data methodology that made the title misleading [iL5j83SC_fg @ 00:07:20]. BetSmart repeats Better Odds' warning that heavy resting money on one side does not mean sharp money until you identify the wallet behind it [jauhFM_n5d4 @ 00:02:30].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The raw Kalshi pipeline is short and both Python teachers build it the same way. Part Time Larry hits the trade API v2 events endpoint with plain HTTP requests, passes with nested markets equal to true, filters status to open, and sets limit to 200, the ceiling per page, then walks the cursor until it runs dry; requests-cache stores every response in a local SQLite database so overnight scrapes restart where they failed, tqdm shows progress, and each day's markets are dumped to a timestamped JSON file and read into a pandas DataFrame [E2mgWN4ReqQ @ 00:09:05]. For live data they post username and password to get a token, send it as a Bearer header on the websocket, and subscribe to orderbook delta, ticker, trade, or fill channels [E2mgWN4ReqQ @ 00:15:11]. Robot Traders runs the official SDK installed with pip install kalshi python sync on Python 3.13 and finds recurring markets by walking series to events to markets: filtering series by category crypto returned 225 series, and the series ticker locks onto each new BTC 15-minute market without code changes [yYjo1lzNoGI @ 00:07:22]. From the market ticker they pull the order book's first 10 levels, best yes bid and ask, spread, midpoint, and recent trades [yYjo1lzNoGI @ 00:09:49].

Execution is where the tricks live. Robot Traders generates the key under account security, picks read and write because read-only only fetches balance-style data, downloads the RSA private key as a .pem file, and loads both through a .env so the code can be shared with an AI agent without leaking secrets; a balance call returning 25.13 confirms the handshake [yYjo1lzNoGI @ 00:12:10]. Kalshi has no true market orders, so they mimic one with a fill-or-kill limit order and a deliberately high yes price, converting a dollar stake into contract count by dividing it by the current ask; resting good-till-canceled limits take the price in cents divided by 100 and are cancelled by order ID pulled from get orders [yYjo1lzNoGI @ 00:13:41]. Part Time Larry's assistant shows the AI layer in 100 lines: Streamlit caches the 30,000-event load, a keyword and category filter builds a markdown context, and Perplexity's Sonar reasoning model returns a Pydantic schema of ticker, side, bid price, reason, and a 0-to-100 confidence score that could feed an order call directly [Ib0BEFKAvn0 @ 00:21:32]. OngPredicts' JEV bot skips unless data is fresh, edge survives fees, liquidity is sufficient, and the trade fits a $10 cap, then sizes positions on confirmed fills only [kDOrvOpgzjE @ 00:03:40].

The packaged stacks come with hard numbers. Quantitative Predictions prices Predictify's free tier at 25,000 credits a month and 60 requests per minute, with history replays for backtesting reserved for the pro plan [AoLKXHhSRtM @ 00:06:28], against Sports Game Odds' $149 entry plan and a $500 pro tier still capped at 300 requests per minute with only sub-minute updates [AoLKXHhSRtM @ 00:04:27]. OngPredicts itemizes the Predictify credit meter: search costs 1 credit, an order book 5, a cross-venue comparison 10, and history 5 per venue, so the full tutorial burned 55 credits; the free key stops at 7 days of history and returns 403 plan required past that, while Builder at $48.88 a month buys 500,000 credits, 12 months of history, and a replay route where 47,000 Kalshi quotes cost 16 credits [OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:07:59]. OddsFantasy's no-code home-favorite preset buys moneyline yes on the home team between 1.25 and 1.8 odds with spread under 0.05, stakes $5 per market, sets a 50% drawdown control, runs from one hour before kickoff on a 2-minute refresh and stops right after kickoff, then previews which matches pass the entry rules before choosing live or limit-quote execution [s_VGmKtzL-0 @ 00:02:31].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Provision keys and secrets
   - Create a read-and-write Kalshi API key, save the RSA private key as a .pem, and load both from a .env file.
   - Read-only keys only fetch account data; placing bets needs read and write, and keeping keys outside the code lets you hand scripts to an AI agent safely. Verify with a balance call before trading [Robot Traders — yYjo1lzNoGI @ 00:12:10]
2. Build the cached market catalog
   - Page the events endpoint at 200 per request with nested markets, cache to SQLite, and save a dated JSON snapshot.
   - Use requests-cache so long scrapes restart where they failed, then load the daily file into pandas for filtering by title or ticker instead of re-hitting the API [Part Time Larry — E2mgWN4ReqQ @ 00:10:09]
3. Normalize and qualify cross-venue gaps
   - Run matched markets through a comparison that checks resolution equivalence, fees and depth before treating a gap as tradable.
   - A 3-cent Alaska Senate gap was flagged not equivalent because the venues differ on who decides the result and how the deadline is handled [OngPredicts — OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:04:11]
4. Dry-run the bot with hard caps
   - Set a $10 trading cap, run in dry-run mode, and read the logs before enabling live trades.
   - Confirm the logs show the data snapshot, the model's estimate, and the reason for every trade or skip, then keep stakes small once live [OngPredicts — kDOrvOpgzjE @ 00:03:22]

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive trap is treating a price gap as a trade. OngPredicts lined up the Dan Sullivan Alaska Senate race at 26 to 27 cents on Polymarket and 23 to 24 cents on Kalshi, then read the verdict underneath: an indicative comparison only, with the two markets judged not equivalent because they differ on who decides the result and how the deadline is handled [OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:04:11]. On paper, yes on Kalshi at 24 and no on Polymarket at 74 costs 98 cents for something paying a dollar, but it only pays if both venues settle on the same result, and that is before fees on both sides and before checking how many shares really sit at those prices [OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:04:29]. Part Time Larry got burned on rules at test size: a 41-cent bet on three Fed cuts in 2024 lost because the 25, 25 and 50 basis point sequence counted as four cuts under the contract rules, so you or the LLM must read the rules before trading [Ib0BEFKAvn0 @ 00:13:30]. Three Markets caught the same failure inside the AI arena, where Grok bet no on a Google most-searched-people market without ever going to the resolution criteria or the data methodology behind a misleading title [iL5j83SC_fg @ 00:07:20].

Backtests and pricing tiers are where unit economics bite. OngPredicts ran a dip-buying rule on the free plan's seven days and got zero finished trades, and asking for 30 days on a free key returns 403 plan required rather than quietly shortening the window [OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:06:17]. On a paid key the same rule read 73 candles per venue, closed three trades and lost $4 because the price drifted down while it kept buying the dip; the stricter replay on Kalshi quotes, filling only a quarter of shown size and paying Kalshi's fees, took 12 to 18 fills per 100-share order and came out at minus $6.77, with $4.27 of that going to fees [OEhjqCpa1Uw @ 00:07:35]. Quantitative Predictions warns that Monit's per-call pricing is cheap to start but adds up very quickly once you are copy trading or running an actual bot [AoLKXHhSRtM @ 00:01:06], and slams the Sports Game Odds pro plan, where $500 still buys only sub-minute updates when you would hope for sub-second on prediction market data, with $149 as the cheapest paid tier [AoLKXHhSRtM @ 00:04:27]. Predictify's builder plan, the tier that unlocks 12 months of history and the replay route, runs $48.88 a month with 500,000 credits.

Live bots fail on plumbing before they fail on picks. Part Time Larry found Perplexity's structured outputs work about 90% of the time, sometimes nesting results under a contracts key, sometimes returning a bare list, sometimes wrapping the JSON in backticks, which forced string juggling before any order could be built [Ib0BEFKAvn0 @ 00:22:52]. A rerun of the same Democratic nominee payload then flipped Mark Cuban to undervalued and added Buttigieg at 6 cents, which is why the build stayed an assistant instead of an agent told to bet $20 [Ib0BEFKAvn0 @ 00:25:05]. OngPredicts designs around partial fills, noting that requested size and filled size can differ, so the Jeff bot books positions only on confirmed fills and skips any round where data freshness, post-fee edge, liquidity or the $10 cap fails [kDOrvOpgzjE @ 00:04:02]. ShaunsTake's day-one tape bot returned insufficient recent activity even on a busier Bitcoin strike, either a bug or a thin market still waiting on a fix [A3KSnSZOPtM @ 00:08:24]. Three Markets watched the leading LLMs, each handed $10,000, burning it, with Deep Seek down 10% as the best performer and Gemini holding yes on DraftKings launching a prediction market in 2025.
