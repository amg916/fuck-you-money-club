## Executive Summary

Practitioners across the CFTC bench, the defense bar, and the exchange side converge on one operating rule: event contracts are swaps or futures, so anything offered to US retail must list on a registered DCM that polices insider trading, manipulation, and contract design itself. Street mechanics are concrete: binary contracts priced 1 to 99 cents, fully collateralized $100 notionals that won Railbird reporting relief, self-certifications that now need heightened manipulation showings, rulebooks around 100 pages, and enforcement that priced a former White House teleprompter operator's roughly 50 mention-market trades at a $65,000 penalty under the up-to-25% cooperation tier. The fault line is jurisdiction: the prediction-market side rides the broad swap definition and federal preemption, while Nevada, Massachusetts, and tribal-gaming advocates in Congress argue sports wagering never left state control, and states now sue directly in state court.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The jurisdictional plumbing is not contested among the people building or policing these venues. Profeissor walks through the base layer: event contracts are derivatives that settle on whether a future event occurs, they are mostly structured as binary payouts, and any prediction market offering them to the public generally registers as a designated contract market, the CFTC-regulated exchange layer [4Vqws7tSo8E @ 00:01:37]. Andrew Kim, an appellate and gambling litigator at Goodwin, keeps hammering the same lever from the courtroom side: the statutory definition of a swap turns on the words 'associated with' and 'potential,' which are written so broadly that sports contracts start every case with a structural advantage, which is why he calls the swaps fight a trap for the states [7na4n6_OBK0 @ 00:09:54]. Chair Michael Selig closes the escape hatch for crypto builders, stating flatly that derivatives offered to retail must sit on a registered exchange, so there is no option to skip registration and offer product 'through the ether'; you either obtain your own exchange or partner with one [0Yj5x9uwKAE @ 00:00:32]. Money Blueprint frames the payoff in business terms: Kalshi's slow, expensive years of federal authorization and litigation built a regulatory moat that geographically restricted rivals cannot copy overnight [IrZSO1sh3ME @ 00:05:32].

Every enforcement voice agrees that insider-trading law follows the money onto event contracts. CFTC enforcement director David Miller says there was real confusion before his appointment over whether insider trading and manipulation rules applied to prediction markets; his short answer is that they do, and the division brought its first prediction-market insider case in April alongside SDNY, followed weeks later by a Google-linked case, plus further insider and manipulation actions [uh3GgMs9ha8 @ 00:02:09]. His mechanism is the misappropriation theory: anyone who owes a duty to the source of material nonpublic information, whether an employer, a campaign, or the US government, commits insider trading by trading or tipping on it, and even a trader using their own information can still be charged with manipulation if they move prices with false public statements [uh3GgMs9ha8 @ 00:07:42]. Selig told the House Agriculture Committee the agency runs a zero-tolerance policy on fraud, manipulation, and insider trading against whoever engages in it [e6Xc_HIIRbQ @ 00:07:52]. The former enforcement director now at Sidley Austin adds that the CFTC has regulated and policed event contracts and DCMs for years, with 'will gold hit 5,000' yes/no contracts as the decades-old template, where sellers of gold hedged their downside by taking the no side [oEiVp7yDA6s @ 00:01:49].

On contract design, regulators, ex-regulators, and analysts converge on a single risk factor: settlement that a small group of people can steer. Profeissor reads the staff advisory as flagging sports contracts because a handful of athletes hold concentrated control over outcomes, so DCMs must keep settlement objectively determinable, monitor actively, and work with leagues on their integrity frameworks and information asymmetries [G-sfCPFFZ5o @ 00:11:09]. The same channel breaks down the mention-market advisory: because settlement turns on one named person's discrete speech or conduct, staff may treat these contracts as presumptively readily susceptible to manipulation under core principle three, which bars any DCM from listing contracts readily susceptible to manipulation, forcing a heightened showing to list [YIkTrxNRVlc @ 00:02:59]. Dorothy DeWitt, who led the team that designated Kalshi, says she always thought mention markets may be inconsistent with that core principle, and she points to the pullback already underway: Kalshi agreed to pause sports mention markets, Polymarket US does not list them, and Robinhood blocks its customers from trading them [9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:15:06]. Miller adds that the advisory was pretty specific about the risks, that the agency has been concerned about mention markets, and that it is watching [uh3GgMs9ha8 @ 00:29:33].

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The ticket math is plain. Money Blueprint describes Kalshi's core instrument as a yes-or-no contract on a real-world event, from Fed rate cuts to elections, priced anywhere between 1 cent and 99 cents [IrZSO1sh3ME @ 00:00:50]. R. Capital Intelligence translates that price directly into odds: a contract trading at 70 cents implies a 70% probability, turning opinions into tradable assets [BP1ZGlo9HqM @ 00:01:40]. The former CFTC lawyer behind A Lawyer In Finance Explains walks through the collateral structure on a $100 contract: at 60/40 odds one side posts $60, the other posts $40, and the winner collects the full $100, with everything paid up front instead of initial margin and later top-ups [gpIcoaEPb7Q @ 00:02:20]. That full-collateral design, defined under regulation 39.2 as holding enough to cover the maximum possible loss at liquidation or expiration, is what won Railbird its no-action relief. Railbird plans standard binaries plus variable-payout contracts that might split 10/90 or 20/80, clears through QC Clearing, bars trading through third-party clearing members, publishes timestamp, quantity, and price on its own website instead of reporting to a swap data repository, and keeps records open to the CFTC, SEC, and DOJ; in exchange it skips the unique alphanumeric swap identifiers and the Part 43 and 45 reporting stack [gpIcoaEPb7Q @ 00:05:39].

Getting a contract live runs through a fixed pipeline. Profeissor lays out the baseline: DCMs answer to 23 statutory core principles covering manipulation prevention, position limits, governance, conflicts, and system safeguards, and every new event contract goes through either self-certification or CFTC approval, with the staff advisory urging DCMs to engage staff early on novel contract types [G-sfCPFFZ5o @ 00:05:38]. DeWitt says the self-certification package is where the work is: exchanges must run a full manipulation analysis, the CFTC just signaled it is not getting enough information to evaluate filings, and staff has also told DCMs that rulebook changes around incentive programs were insufficient or non-compliant, in rulebooks that typically run about 100 pages [9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:15:41]. For vertically integrated stacks, meaning an affiliated DCM, FCM, DCO, and sometimes a market maker, her firm builds a conflicts matrix, walks each conflict one by one, layers information barriers and procedures, and documents equal treatment, because complaints from rival FCMs or customers land at the CFTC [9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:20:13]. Selig points to Backpack as the full-stack template: the first on-chain DCM and DCO, paired with an FCM registered through the NFA, while other protocols are instead partnering with existing registrants [0Yj5x9uwKAE @ 00:00:16].

The contract-design screen comes straight from the special rule. Profeissor's breakdown of the 267-page proposal shows the CFTC's tests for sports: aggregate outcomes of professional or collegiate sports are less concerning, settlement on publicly reported or league-verified data helps, and contracts on player injuries, physical altercations, referee judgment calls, or a single player's discrete actions draw public-interest concern because one player or official could swing settlement [4Vqws7tSo8E @ 00:13:28]. Facially neutral contracts get a drafting trap: a contract on whether a foreign government facility stays functional involves war unless its terms expressly exclude the war pathway [4Vqws7tSo8E @ 00:08:04]. For mention markets, the staff's four non-exhaustive factors are whether the speaker faces legal, contractual, or fiduciary duties that deter steering the outcome, exposure to external pressure campaigns, independent verification under public scrutiny, and the DCM's own surveillance, insider identification, and position limits [YIkTrxNRVlc @ 00:03:36]. When settlement goes wrong, DeWitt maps the dispute chain: Kalshi gives a narrow complaint window to an outcome resolution committee that may make a trader whole or settle for half, arbitration follows under member agreements, and she flags the American Arbitration Association's AI product as a cost-efficient fit for $10 to $200 tickets [9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:28:12].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Pick the registration route
   - Decide between a full-stack license and partnering with an existing registrant.
   - Backpack came in with its own exchange and clearing house as the first on-chain DCM and DCO, plus an FCM registered with the NFA; others partner with registrants instead [Backpack Exchange Official — 0Yj5x9uwKAE @ 00:00:16].
2. Run the three-step special-rule screen
   - Test every contract: is it an event contract, does its reference event involve an enumerated activity, and is it contrary to the public interest.
   - The CFTC asks whether the reference event itself is unlawful activity, terrorism, assassination, war, or gaming, not whether trading the contract is gaming; elections and economic indicators fall out of scope [🎓Profeissor.com🚀Digital Assets, AI & FinTech — 4Vqws7tSo8E @ 00:03:25].
3. Build a heavy self-certification file
   - Submit a full manipulation analysis under core principle three with every self-certified contract.
   - DeWitt says the CFTC just signaled it is not getting enough information in self-certifications, and mention markets in particular face a presumption of manipulability that demands a heightened showing [PredictionNews — 9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:15:41].
4. Stand up surveillance and conflicts controls
   - Identify known insiders and controllers, calibrate position limits and surveillance, and document a conflicts matrix.
   - Staff expects DCMs to identify potential controllers and known insiders and calibrate position limits, accountability, reporting, record keeping, and surveillance to the risks those persons present [🎓Profeissor.com🚀Digital Assets, AI & FinTech — YIkTrxNRVlc @ 00:05:20].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The deepest fault line is whether a sports event contract is a derivative or a bet wearing a federal wrapper. The CFTC position, laid out by Profeissor.com, is that event contracts serve price discovery and risk management on centralized exchanges, gambling is entertainment with no real economic purpose, and only the CFTC regulates the space [G-sfCPFFZ5o @ 00:04:25]. At a House Agriculture hearing a lawmaker put two betting lines in front of Chair Michael Selig, argued consumers cannot tell the sportsbook from the prediction market, and branded a contract on whether Jose Altuve homers tonight a bet rather than a hedge; Selig answered that these markets must be well-functioning and comprehensively regulated by the CFTC [e6Xc_HIIRbQ @ 00:02:41]. The former CFTC lawyer behind A Lawyer In Finance Explains goes after the commodity hook itself: the commodity definition is broad, but whether it reaches the Giants beating the Cowboys is an even bigger stretch than the motion-picture receipt futures Commissioner Summers dissented on in 2010 [MHeX-gRNpCE @ 00:07:33]. Goodwin's Andrew Kim reads the same statute the opposite way: the swap definition, built on 'associated with' and 'potential', is so broad that attacking it is a trap for the states, and the only question that matters is whether Congress left the states any room [7na4n6_OBK0 @ 00:06:54].

Practitioners also split on where the jurisdiction war gets won. A Lawyer In Finance Explains reports the states rewrote their playbook: cease-and-desist letters let Kalshi run to federal court and win injunctions, so Nevada sued Polymarket and Massachusetts sued Kalshi straight in state court, forcing any removal fight onto the states' terms [MHeX-gRNpCE @ 00:00:36]. Andrew Kim calls the scoreboard even: the states hold Maryland and the Crypto.com Nevada ruling, while Kalshi beat the California tribes at the preliminary-injunction stage and looks headed for a win in the Third Circuit appeal against New Jersey [7na4n6_OBK0 @ 00:00:50]. The second split is over how the public-interest screen should run. Dorothy DeWitt, who led the CFTC team that designated Kalshi, says the agency's 2011-2012 rule bans enumerated categories like war, terrorism and gaming wholesale, while the statute contemplates contract-by-contract review, and that mismatch has never been fixed; a later Biden-era proposal to patch it was withdrawn under the new administration [9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:06:41]. The 267-page proposal unpacked by Profeissor.com answers with a three-step test: is it an event contract, does the reference event itself involve an enumerated activity, and only then a case-by-case, multi-factor public-interest analysis [4Vqws7tSo8E @ 00:04:18].

The go-to-market split is onshore registration versus offshore speed. Money Blueprint frames Kalshi's founders as taking the painfully slow, high-risk route of years knocking on CFTC doors, DCM status in late 2020, then suing their own regulator, while Polymarket set up offshore and leaned on crypto to stay outside American borders [IrZSO1sh3ME @ 00:02:14]. Selig argues the onshore path is the only one that holds: without a regulated U.S. route these markets will develop offshore anyway, and U.S. persons with true demand will find ways to reach them [SYcmPpHGHIo @ 00:07:40]. On Backpack, Selig lays out two onshore plays, either building a full-stack exchange and clearinghouse plus an NFA-registered FCM or partnering with existing registrants, and warns there is no lighter on-chain rulebook, because that would be unfair to incumbents who already comply [0Yj5x9uwKAE @ 00:00:32]. Mention markets split the camp a third way: the staff advisory explained by Profeissor.com treats them as presumptively readily susceptible to manipulation, yet stops short of a ban, letting a well-designed contract with DCM trading rules, surveillance and controls rebut the presumption, judged on four factors running from the controller's legal and confidentiality duties to independent verification and public scrutiny [YIkTrxNRVlc @ 00:03:18].

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive trap is assuming insider-trading law stops at the stock market. CFTC Enforcement Director David Miller says the confusion is over: insider trading and manipulation rules apply to prediction markets, the Maduro raid case brought in April with SDNY was the first prediction-market insider case, and a Google-linked case followed weeks later [uh3GgMs9ha8 @ 00:01:47]. The theory is misappropriation, a duty owed to the source of the information, whether employer or government. White House teleprompter operator Gabriel Perez breached that duty with roughly 50 trades on mention markets tied to the president's speeches, gained about $100,000 to $107,000, disgorged it, and paid a $65,000 penalty pegged to a one-time gain and cut under the new cooperation tiers [uh3GgMs9ha8 @ 00:22:00]. Going offshore does not cover you: in the Van Dyke and Google-linked cases the alleged trades ran on Polymarket's international platform, yet the CFTC still established venue in the Southern District of New York [uh3GgMs9ha8 @ 00:26:14]. Political heat compounds the exposure: a lawmaker cited suspicious, well-timed trading ahead of the US-Iran ceasefire and sent Polymarket a letter demanding it preserve and share data on suspicious national-security bets [e6Xc_HIIRbQ @ 00:08:28].

Litigation is where balance sheets bleed. R. Capital Intelligence lays out the sequence: in 2022 the CFTC charged Polymarket for running an unregistered derivatives platform open to Americans and Polymarket paid a civil penalty, then in 2023 the CFTC blocked Kalshi's congressional-control contracts [BP1ZGlo9HqM @ 00:05:40]. Money Blueprint calls Kalshi's November 2023 suit against its own regulator a bet-the-company move; for months the company bled cash on legal fees until a September 2024 ruling and the D.C. Circuit's October 2, 2024 refusal of an emergency stay let the contracts list [IrZSO1sh3ME @ 00:03:44]. The election win did not travel to sports contracts or into state court. A Lawyer In Finance Explains notes that Nevada, the only case to reach trial, went against Kalshi, which guts the likelihood-of-success prong needed for injunctions elsewhere, and Massachusetts left open whether existing positions with residents get frozen or cancelled [MHeX-gRNpCE @ 00:01:46]. Andrew Kim warns judicial reversals are a live risk on both sides: Judge Gordon granted Kalshi's first injunction, then signaled a 'what have I done' turn, and an opinion built on thin outcome-versus-occurrence reasoning sets itself up for Ninth Circuit reversal [7na4n6_OBK0 @ 00:02:45].

Inside the exchange, the traps are operational. Dorothy DeWitt says the CFTC just told DCMs their self-certifications lack enough information to judge compliance with the core principles, and that rulebook filings on incentive programs and affiliated market makers have been insufficient or non-compliant [9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:15:58]. DeWitt also flags a sleeper liability: KPI contracts on whether a company beats its numbers may be unregistered security-based swaps, and a civil judge could hand any losing trader a rescission right against the exchange, which is class-action territory [9aHX4chc8YY @ 00:32:33]. Andrew Kim adds that the CFTC's core principles were never written for app-based retail trading or sports integrity, the PredictionNews host recalls the 2019 Robinhood tragedy as the kind of scandal that sticks, and Kim points to the 18-versus-21 age conflict as a classic impossibility-preemption paper cut [7na4n6_OBK0 @ 00:24:24]. The economics check runs the other way for fully collateralized products: the Railbird no-action letter, decoded by A Lawyer In Finance Explains, drops swap-reporting duties because both sides post the full $100 up front, removing counterparty-default risk, provided trades are published on the website with timestamp, quantity and price and no third-party clearing members are allowed [gpIcoaEPb7Q @ 00:04:23].
