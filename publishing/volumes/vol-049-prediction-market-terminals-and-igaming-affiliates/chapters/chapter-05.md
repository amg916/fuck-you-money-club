## 1. Stop registration drift before a productive feature becomes a different business  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The first fatal trap is treating non-custodial as a universal exemption. A dashboard can avoid possessing customer money yet still cross a registration boundary through solicitation, order handling, individualized advice, or compensation arrangements. Under 7 U.S.C. § 1a and 17 CFR § 1.3, activity matters more than the label analytics. An introducing broker can solicit or accept covered orders without accepting the money supporting them; custody is therefore an incomplete test. Evaluate commodity trading adviser status and any applicable publisher exclusion or exemption separately, including 17 CFR § 4.14 where relevant. Keep the initial product read-only: authorized market observations, documented probability calculations, and links whose destination and commercial terms have been checked. Do not accept trading credentials, transmit customer orders, aggregate stakes, or operate pooled accounts. A permitted builder rebate requires a written program and an activity-specific registration assessment, not a marketing representative's reassurance. Record every proposed feature against account access, order influence, compensation, and user interaction. Stop release when any answer changes. This catches the apparently harmless trade button before it converts a subscription product into a materially different operating model.

The second trap is copying one state's affiliate workflow into every territory. NJ DGE, PA PGCB, and MI MGCB administer distinct supplier and affiliate requirements; classification can depend on services, compensation, and operator relationships. WV and Ontario require their own analysis, and Ontario is a Canadian jurisdiction rather than another American state. The chapter's 21+ access policy is an intentionally restrictive product choice, not a statement that every listed jurisdiction uses the same statutory age. Confirm the audience, operator license, affiliate status, approved advertising terms, responsible-gaming disclosures, and permitted tracking fields for each destination. A visitor's self-declared location is not sufficient evidence for a state-specific acquisition offer. Use lawfully obtained location signals to restrict the portal, while the licensed operator performs its own account, identity, location, and eligibility checks. Suppress offers when signals conflict; never suggest VPN workarounds. Under 16 CFR § 255.5, disclose material affiliate relationships clearly beside recommendations. The third trap is burying that disclosure while calling paid placement an objective ranking. Preserve ranking inputs and creative versions so a complaint can be reconstructed without collecting unnecessary sensitive identity documents.

Use twelve mapped operating plays as this chapter's control index, not as twelve independently forecast revenue businesses. They are publisher-boundary isolation, jurisdiction-specific offer eligibility, settlement-equivalent probability comparison, stale-feed suppression, free_tier_api_waterfall_multiplexing, signed deposit attribution, cohort-qualified CPA purchasing, deduction-level NGR reconciliation, negative-carryover containment, seasonal acquisition throttling, dependency concentration limits, and evidence-led incident recovery. These descriptive labels organize the chapter; only the waterfall identifier is supplied as an external mapped identifier. The waterfall is the TRACK_1 Multi-Provider Free-Tier API Router & B2B Data-Enrichment Reselling play, applied to authorized business research rather than bettor profiling. Assign an owner and a fallback to every play. For example, if a $300 CPA is conditional on a qualified first deposit, a click and even an account opening are worth zero earned CPA until the contract's conditions hold. If a builder agreement is suspended, disable the compensated link and retain independently licensed subscription analytics. If a jurisdiction becomes uncertain, remove its acquisition offers while preserving permitted read-only material. None of these fallbacks includes offshore crypto routing, unlawful sweepstakes destinations, borrowed accounts, or relabeling compensation to conceal what the operator actually does.

## 2. Make market comparisons and upstream economics survive contact with reality  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The fourth trap is selling apparent cross-venue arbitrage between contracts that do not settle identically. A Kalshi event contract, a ForecastEx contract available through an authorized channel, and a sportsbook proposition can disagree on settlement source, deadline, cancellation, overtime, or exceptional events. An apparent four-cent probability gap is meaningless when one contract counts overtime and another excludes it. Normalize event identifiers, outcome wording, settlement authority, fees, depth, and executable size before presenting a comparison. An illustrative $1 contract bought for $0.48 against an independently estimated 0.51 probability has three cents of modeled gross edge; two cents of transaction cost and one cent of slippage remove it completely. Estimation error can make the result worse. Sportsbook decimal odds of 2.10 imply about 47.62% before adjusting for the full market's overround; that is not automatically comparable to a contract's displayed midpoint. Label the observation time and calculation method. Suppress positive-EV labels when matching is incomplete or available depth cannot support the displayed size. The safe fallback is an informational comparison with explicit differences, not an asserted executable hedge or a promised return.

The fifth trap is mistaking a fast WebSocket connection for a reliable market feed. Transport latency, upstream publication age, sequence gaps, and local processing delay are different measurements. For an illustrative liquid-market service target, flag quote age above one second and suppress actionable comparisons above two seconds; tune both limits to documented venue cadence rather than presenting them as universal rules. A quiet market requires heartbeat evidence, not fabricated price updates. Record monotonic receive time, source timestamps where supplied, sequence identifiers, and snapshot version. On reconnect, rebuild state from the provider's supported snapshot and replay procedure; do not stitch an old order book to new deltas blindly. Separate a genuine empty book from a missing update. The sixth trap is treating accessible feeds as commercially redistributable. Obtain rights for storage, derived displays, attribution, and downstream use from each provider; a public endpoint alone supplies none of those permissions. Use token buckets, bounded retries, and provider-specific budgets. A rate-limit response must reduce traffic rather than trigger account rotation. If authorized live access fails, show clearly timestamped historical analytics or disable the affected instrument instead of silently substituting scraped sportsbook prices.

The seventh trap sits inside free_tier_api_waterfall_multiplexing: free requests are not free resale rights. Its specified design aggregates 30–60+ B2B data, email-verification, firmographic, DNS, and SERP providers offering roughly 50–1,000 monthly developer lookups each. Forty providers averaging 250 permitted lookups yield 10,000 raw monthly calls, not 10,000 complete enriched records. At two calls per cache miss and an 80% fresh-cache hit rate, 20,000 requested records consume 8,000 calls, leaving 2,000 for retries and exceptional fields. Ten customers at $199 monthly produce $1,990 subscription billings; $150 hosting and $400 support leave $1,440 before acquisition, other labor, and overhead. This is a standalone sensitivity example, not additional scorecard revenue. Maintain 40+ interchangeable adapters, Postgres field provenance, expiry rules, and contractual resale flags. Prioritize the local graph cache when quotas tighten, then use permitted direct public-web, DNS, and MX extraction for business records only. MX existence does not prove mailbox deliverability. Exclude providers prohibiting aggregation or resale, honor shared account quotas, and degrade record completeness visibly. Never multiply free accounts, evade blocks, or enrich individual bettors with unrelated personal attributes to rescue unit economics.

## 3. Reconcile qualified deposits to collectible commissions, not dashboard celebrations  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The eighth trap is optimizing to raw first-time-depositor events instead of contract-qualified, collectible CPA. An illustrative campaign buys 120 candidate depositors at $180 acquisition cost each, or $21,600. At $300 headline CPA, gross expected commission is $36,000. A 15% invalidation rate removes $5,400, leaving $30,600 before $3,000 servicing costs and $21,600 acquisition costs: only $6,000 contribution. The effective commission per candidate is $255, not $300. Duplicate accounts, existing customers, excluded locations, chargebacks, minimum-deposit failures, and prohibited traffic sources can all defeat qualification, but only the actual contract determines the applicable reasons and timing. Record click, registration, deposit, qualification, reversal, invoice, and cash dates as separate states. Use operator-supported signed S2S callbacks, idempotent event keys, and server-held secrets; never let browser parameters declare an earned commission. Compare mature cohorts rather than yesterday's incomplete traffic. Investigate a five-percentage-point increase in reversals against the mature baseline, and pause a placement if expected contribution per candidate becomes nonpositive. These are initial business triggers, not regulatory limits. Keep reversal reason codes and appeal evidence without retaining raw payment credentials or excess identity records.

The ninth trap is reading NGR as a universal accounting definition. It is a negotiated commission base whose deductions can include bonuses, taxes, payment charges, administration, jackpot contributions, and adjustments. Start with an illustrative $50,000 gaming gross revenue figure, already distinct from stakes and deposits. Subtract $10,000 bonuses, $5,000 administration, and $5,000 taxes where the agreement permits those deductions: eligible NGR becomes $30,000. A 25% share produces $7,500 before carryover. If the contract carries an $8,000 negative balance at the commission-share level, current cash commission is zero and $500 remains negative. A balance carried at the underlying NGR level would produce a different result; store its level explicitly. This is the tenth trap: assuming each month resets or that losses cannot cross brands, countries, or players. Negotiate ring-fenced balances and a clear expiry or reset where commercially available. Never subtract the same bonus once inside NGR and again from the affiliate invoice. Alert when total deductions rise more than five percentage points against the mature contractual baseline, then reconcile each line before buying further traffic into that cohort.

Keep the Formula v2.0 scorecard intact while exposing its economic plumbing. Conservative revenue of $75,000 less $55,500 expenses equals $19,500 owner profit, or 26%. Base revenue of $362,500 less $224,750 expenses equals $137,750, or 38.0%. Scaled revenue of $650,000 less $325,000 expenses equals $325,000, or 50%. An illustrative Base allocation is $180,000 subscriptions, $12,500 permitted builder rebates, and $170,000 affiliate commissions after deductions. The affiliate bridge starts at $240,000 provisional commissions and subtracts $40,000 contractual bonus and administration deductions, $18,000 invalidations, and $12,000 share-level carryover absorption. Its $170,000 revenue less $72,000 CAC and $28,000 other affiliate costs contributes $70,000. Subscription and rebate revenue of $192,500 less $124,750 remaining expenses contributes $67,750, reconciling exactly to Base owner profit. These allocations illustrate the supplied totals rather than replace them. Assess principal-versus-agent presentation and variable consideration under ASC 606; neither deposits nor user trading volume become operator revenue. No separate retained_share factor was supplied, so do not invent one or apply another haircut to already net commissions. A cash reserve changes liquidity, not profit again unless a separate expense is actually warranted.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the cohort evidence
   - Export authorized event states, contract version, creative identifier, jurisdiction, and operator statement for the same mature cohort.
   - Preserve event timestamps and stable pseudonymous identifiers; separate reporting delay from actual qualification failure.
2. Rebuild qualification and deduction bridges
   - Match every provisional CPA and NGR line to qualification, reversal, permitted deduction, and carryover records.

## 4. Cut spend before seasonality, concentration, and bans turn a leak into insolvency  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The eleventh trap is annualizing NFL-season CPA rates into a twelve-month forecast. Repeat the earlier campaign after budgets tighten: 80 candidate depositors at $220 headline CPA yield $17,600 provisional commission. The same 15% invalidation rate leaves $14,960. Acquisition at $180 per candidate costs $14,400, and $3,000 servicing produces a $2,440 contribution loss. More volume accelerates that loss unless acquisition economics or fixed servicing change. Maintain sport-calendar cohorts, operator budget confirmations, and campaign-level stop dates. Track collectible commission per candidate against fully allocated acquisition and servicing cost, not a blended dashboard return inflated by older profitable cohorts. As an initial policy, stop a placement when its mature expected contribution is nonpositive; investigate sooner when commission per candidate falls 15% against its matched seasonal baseline. Never describe this stress case as a revised canonical forecast. It is a sensitivity test around the supplied Base case. The fallback is to reduce paid affiliate acquisition, retain useful subscription analytics, and renegotiate placement terms. Email reactivation also needs audience permission and suppression handling; repeated messaging to excluded or vulnerable users is not a substitute for a broken CPA spread.

The twelfth trap is using shortcuts that destroy the underlying distribution account. Fake depositors, self-referral rings, purchased identities, prohibited brand bidding, cloaked landing pages, fabricated testimonials, and unapproved incentivized traffic can trigger commission reversal, termination, and wider liability. Do not experiment with them to discover an operator's tolerance. Store approved traffic sources and creative versions, then reject campaign launches that do not match. Monitor unusual repeated device or payment-linked signals only through operator-authorized, privacy-conscious reporting; shared household patterns alone do not prove fraud. A suspected attribution defect calls for reconciliation, not accusations or concealment. Platform dependency is another asymmetric exposure: a single search account, payment processor, feed license, or affiliate operator can interrupt collections while payroll continues. Treat more than 35% of trailing ninety-day collectible revenue from one commercial counterparty as a concentration alert, not an automatic legal threshold. Maintain exportable customer entitlements, documented recovery procedures, and alternative authorized providers. An alternate account must never be used to evade a suspension. Preserve subscription access where permitted, remove the disputed dependency, and use the provider's documented appeal channel with a complete evidence package.

Build a daily exception sheet that connects product health to cash survival. Include quote suppression minutes, callback signature failures, unmatched qualified events, mature reversal percentage, deduction ratio, negative balances, receivable aging, and available cash after committed obligations. For an illustrative policy, investigate unmatched events above 1% after the contracted reporting window, quarantine every invalidly signed callback, and escalate invoices fifteen days beyond their contractual due date. Compare unrestricted cash with the next ninety days of payroll, hosting, acquisition commitments, and plausible contractual repayment exposure. The published $1,500–$10,000 monthly opex range is a planning input, not permission to omit CAC or contractor costs from the canonical expense bridge. Stress the largest counterparty's delayed payment and one season of reduced CPA together. If that combined stress consumes the operating buffer, stop discretionary acquisition before cutting monitoring or reconciliation. During an incident, preserve signed event evidence, revoke compromised credentials, disable only the affected surface, and notify contractual counterparts as required. Restore service after replay tests and cash-ledger reconciliation demonstrate that the original defect is closed. A profitable screen is insufficient evidence; collectibility and recoverability are the operating standard.
