## Executive Summary

Pro prediction-market desks agree the edge is structural: retail buys emotional YES longshots, makers harvest them, and exchanges do not limit winners. Becker's 72M-trade Kalshi dataset shows 5-cent contracts winning 4.18% of the time and makers earning about +2.5% per trade, while only 22% of markets resolve YES. ## The Practitioner Consensus

The loudest point of agreement across the desk is that retail order flow carries a structural YES and longshot bias that pros harvest. Jonathan Becker's dataset of 72 million Kalshi trades, walked through by Part Time Larry, shows 5-cent contracts actually winning only 4.18% of the time, while NO bought at 94 cents wins 95% of the time [vT0qMNgOkxo @ 00:04:34]. Larry's own Rockets YES bought at 7% sank to 4%, turning $43 into $24, and the summary is blunt: usually the boring outcome wins, nothing ever happens [vT0qMNgOkxo @ 00:16:37]. Larry calls late cheap YES buying pouring money into a dead bet. Three Markets lands in the same place: fabiano.soul's count shows only 22% of markets resolve YES, and the play is buying NO early in a 90/10 market because it tends to drift to 95/5 [DvHXWcq2iWc @ 00:02:17]. Semi, profiled with P&L over $1M from a $286 deposit in June 2024, calls the approach reverse stupidity: fading sensationalism bias. InGenius by Veritasium's three-year Polymarket study adds that the skilled winners, roughly 3% of accounts, avoid overpriced longshots and favour favourites [CTUqmUHvd2Y @ 00:20:52]. The shared rule: treat emotional YES buying as the flow you trade against.

Second consensus point: live on the maker side of the book. Becker's data shows makers earning about +2.5% excess return per trade while takers lose about the same, and Reichenbach and Walther (2025) found the identical split on Polymarket [vT0qMNgOkxo @ 00:05:36]. Becker's own custom NBA script found roughly $7M in taker losses. InGenius by Veritasium measured skilled directional winners averaging $3,243 of profit over two years against $11,831 for market makers, with the top 0.1% of accounts acting as makers about half the time [CTUqmUHvd2Y @ 00:22:13]. The working pros on Inside Prediction Markets back it with their own books. Clay, the fully manual trader out of Princeton, New Jersey, runs 80-85% maker and calls the process completely manual [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:12:30]. Steven, the part-time market maker who crossed $1M in P&L, flipped from 95% taking in January to roughly 20% taking and 80% making, mostly RFQs on combos plus niche markets such as NASCAR. The nuance the pros add is that making is the volume engine, not automatically the fattest margin: Steven still books about 7-8% ROI taking against 2-3% making, and the taking P&L still leads the account [ieRL4BXtMGA @ 00:06:55]. Making buys scale; selective taking still pays the bills.

The third pillar is venue logic: sharps migrated because prediction markets take their action instead of cutting them off. Troy Cooks of OddsJam, a pro for two to three years who made over $250K in 2025, puts it simply: sportsbooks hate winners, while peer-to-peer exchanges like Novig charge fees and could care less how much you profit [zMU9NWt0gdE @ 00:02:38]. Cooks also pitches these as the newest and least efficient markets, with insider flow visible because every portfolio is public, and reports about $20K in 30 days from arbitrage, +EV and the tool. Steven adds that offshore books are in a slow death of limits, slow pay and 15-second timers [ieRL4BXtMGA @ 00:12:00]. NBC News frames the structural driver: CFTC federal oversight lets users in even where sports gambling is illegal, while sportsbooks carry large capital reserves and tight regulation, and the segment's critic warns the regulatory gate is completely down [APN3zwcwPdQ @ 00:01:52]. Where to hunt is also agreed: Becker's data flags entertainment, crypto and sports as inefficient and finance as efficient [vT0qMNgOkxo @ 00:12:34]. The baseline prerequisite is cultural as much as technical: think like the casino, diversify across a large number of bets, and accept that longshots sometimes hit.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Clay's first-touchdown playbook is the most granular manual workflow on the desk. Quote the top 10 players, price the team without the ball at the pregame line minus about 4 cents for roughly 5% ROI per player, refill in 900-contract lots, and before each snap drop the team with the ball to 1 cent before repricing after the play [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:14:55]. The take runs about $3K per primetime game, net negative in only two or three games a year, but Clay got courtsided at 4,000 contracts and 95 cents and now says that edge is dead [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:18:24]. The live focus has shifted to end-of-game football, the highest-ROI window because people have no idea what they're doing, plus hockey, tennis, soccer and Chinese basketball. Clay listens to referee audio rather than trusting sportsbook data feeds. Liquidity has transformed underneath all of it: early Kalshi college football spreads were 3-4 cents wide, and now roughly $70K sits at a 1-cent spread [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:10:42]. Novig goes further, with Henry and Chris pointing to 300-500K-contract walls quoted a penny wide with no fees [ZmMVHubZpns @ 00:06:04]. Clay pegs fair margins at 2-4% live and 1-2% pregame, and says landing in a 5% range is quite profitable.

Steven's RFQ operation shows how fast quoting edges compress. RFQ started at the end of May, the World Cup made June and July easy, and from August competitors began offering 0.1% hold, pushing the focus to latency and combo quoting [ieRL4BXtMGA @ 00:08:22]. Pricing is about 80% top down, and some NFL markets carry 40-plus makers, so being five milliseconds behind means getting nothing [ieRL4BXtMGA @ 00:22:05]. Steven expects proprietary pricing to matter within three to four months, because low-hold SGP quotes suggest rivals originate their own numbers. The build is one developer working with Steven for nine months, because Steven's own builds were garbage, with developers originally sourced through coaching calls. Michael Hoffman, head of engineering on the Inside Prediction Markets trading team, came from StoneX FIX connectivity into about 100 exchanges, hires for distributed systems and messaging-bus skills, and discounts big-tech candidates [4UVkZUKEmPI @ 00:35:53]. Clay runs the opposite stack: no models, intuition only, and no appetite for troubleshooting, which makes tooling cost a real line item. Multi-venue order-book software runs about $0.02 per contract, tiered, which on Clay's roughly $5M a month of volume would cost more than $10K [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:25:32]. Hoffman stays black boxed from pricing entirely, walling engineering off from the trading book.

The contract math is simple. Event contracts pay $1 or $0, so YES at 67 cents plus NO at 33 cents equals a dollar, and InGenius by Veritasium's spread-capture template is to model 67% fair value, bid YES at 65 cents and NO at 31 cents, pay 96 cents and lock 4 cents, with the risk that only one side fills [CTUqmUHvd2Y @ 00:23:49]. The same team flags multi-outcome arbitrage, buying all YES for under $1 or all NO for under $2 in a three-outcome market, and notes the top 0.1% average more than 2,300 trades a day against a median of 78. On the taker side, Troy Cooks prices a 60-cent contract as returning 40 cents of profit and says OddsJam's Prediction Traders Tool, running the Sniper 2.0 model at a $250 average bet and about 10 bets a day, is pitched at +$5,000 over 30 days via copy trading [zMU9NWt0gdE @ 00:04:45]. For vetting traders, the Inside Prediction Markets team uses a distribution-based CLV band, checks sizing against optimal Kelly, and demands live results inside their own system rather than backtests [xM4qzY2FKFw @ 00:29:10]. Tax mechanics matter too: SharpMoney's Gary Conler notes Kalshi's terms call its contracts 1256 contracts, which would make profits capital gains outside the 90% loss rule [O3v0_n4fZLQ @ 00:22:29].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The loudest split on the desk is whether 'just bet NO' is still an edge or already a crowded trade. Part Time Larry runs the 'nothing ever happens' playbook straight off 72 million Kalshi fills: pick emotional categories, target 5 to 10 cent longshots, buy the NO side as a resting maker limit, and spread the book across many bets [vT0qMNgOkxo @ 00:15:33]. Three Markets backs the bias with Fabiano.soul's stat that only 22% of markets resolve YES, and Andrew Lonardi wants to buy the entire index of NOs, yet the same hosts float the counterpoint that degens hammering the 10 cent side may already offset the YES lean [DvHXWcq2iWc @ 00:04:39]. Clay, who has pushed roughly $60M in manual volume, goes further and says competition has flipped the edge toward YES on the sports ladders they trade [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:29:30]. InGenius splits the difference: its skilled-winner cohort avoids overpriced longshots and leans toward favourites rather than blanket-shorting every headline [CTUqmUHvd2Y @ 00:20:52]. The operator read is that the NO tilt is real in emotional, entertainment, crypto and sports categories but needs to be re-measured per venue and per season, because the practitioners who live in liquid sports ladders already see the premium being competed away by other makers.

Making versus taking is the second fault line, and the numbers do not line up neatly. Part Time Larry's Kalshi data shows makers earning roughly 2.5% excess return per trade while takers bleed about the same, a pattern Reichenbach and Walther also found on Polymarket [vT0qMNgOkxo @ 00:05:36]. InGenius lands on the same side: market makers in its sample averaged $11,831 versus $3,243 for other skilled winners, and the top 0.1% are makers about half the time [CTUqmUHvd2Y @ 00:22:13]. Clay runs 80 to 85% maker flow and does it completely by hand, quoting ladders on intuition rather than a model [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:12:30]. Steven complicates the story: their book has swung from 95% taking in January to about 80% making, mostly RFQs on combos and niche markets like NASCAR, yet taking still prints the bigger PnL at a 7 to 8% ROI against only 2 to 3% on the making side [ieRL4BXtMGA @ 00:06:55]. The practical split: making supplies steady, thinner margin while live taking is where Steven still books the fat edges, and they run both rather than picking one. Anyone copying maker returns from aggregate studies should expect thinner per-trade margins once forty competing quoters show up.

The remaining arguments are about desk structure and where the big money sits. On the Novig episode, Chris argues the true end bosses stay offshore out of tax and habit [ZmMVHubZpns @ 00:23:41], while Henry counters that it is really stranded offshore and crypto balances keeping them there, and adds that prediction markets let multiple millions go down quietly [ZmMVHubZpns @ 00:27:08]. On building the team, Larry wants a named head for each sport for accountability, topped up with generalists and part-time specialists for Grand Slams and F1, whereas Joe prefers a quant-firm stack of generalist traders, developers and researchers [xM4qzY2FKFw @ 00:19:36]. Politics produces the sharpest live trade dispute: Gavin says pundits underplay tails like a 55 to 56 seat Democratic Senate and wants to buy tail Democrats such as Turek in Iowa rather than Talarico at 76, while a host pushes back that with Democrats at 75% for the Senate, 55 to 56 seats cannot be the likely outcome [4UVkZUKEmPI @ 00:16:43]. Gavin narrows the claim to the underdog Democrats being slightly underpriced, not the favourite. Gavin also warns that market makers should fear correlation in political parlays, and few political RFQ trades get done at all.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive mistakes on this desk come from contract wording, not pricing. Steven misread the weather resolution rule on a NASCAR pole-position market and lost about $35K, calling it the single most expensive mistake of their career [ieRL4BXtMGA @ 00:38:21]. Clay took a similar hit on cricket after misreading a 19.5 versus 18.5 line on a player who went on to score about 100 [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:36:20]. Venue rules diverge even on the same event: on the Bermuda Open, Novig voided bets while Kalshi let them stand, which turns an identical position into a live bet on one screen and a void on another [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:06:02]. The Inside Prediction Markets team flags this as a structural staffing cost, because trading Novig, Kalshi and Polymarket at once means handling different rules on each, and every new league added, from F1 to NASCAR, needs more people watching it [xM4qzY2FKFw @ 00:15:52]. The check before any size goes on: read the resolution source, the void conditions and the exact line on every venue in the hedge, and do not trade a market whose rules you cannot explain in one sentence.

Edges on this desk decay fast, and the practitioners name the dates. Clay's first-touchdown ladder, quoting the top ten players at the pregame line minus about 4 cents for roughly 5% ROI each, earned about $3K per primetime game until they got courtsided at 4,000 contracts at 95 cents; they now say that edge is dead [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:18:24]. Clay also dropped 61K in a single week betting against the Knicks, a reminder that a 10% lifetime ROI on about $60M of volume still carries ugly weeks [tM5MKtoBoZ0 @ 00:20:35]. Steven's RFQ book coasted through the World Cup in June and July, then from August competitors started quoting a 0.1% hold, compressing edges and forcing the shift into latency and combo quoting [ieRL4BXtMGA @ 00:08:22]. Chris ran the defensive answer, quoting only at a 10%-plus margin early and absorbing late flow, and still lost the McGregor versus Holloway example running it [ZmMVHubZpns @ 00:35:23]. The economics check is to log ROI per strategy per month, because each of these traders found out the edge had closed only after a costly fill, and the next liquidity crunch on island and playoff games is already expected.

The back office is where profitable traders quietly bleed. Gary Conler stresses that the 90% rule cuts losses, not winnings, so $1M won against $1.1M lost still leaves taxable income, and they watched a client report $1.2M of W2Gs while IRS records showed $3.7M [O3v0_n4fZLQ @ 00:13:56]. Andrew McMillan of REH CPA adds the recreational trap: losses only count if you itemize, capped at winnings, and you cannot net wins against losses at all [rN19s1QAAYo @ 00:01:24]. Custody is the second leak: after the Polymarket international front-end hack and the Binance compromise rumor, the Inside Prediction Markets hosts concluded never to keep money on an exchange, while admitting hot wallets are needed to trade [4UVkZUKEmPI @ 00:09:20]. Automation is no shortcut either: InGenius built a weather market-making bot with $103 and finished six days down $21.50, warning that professional firms have better infrastructure and network connections [CTUqmUHvd2Y @ 00:29:19]. The reconciliation check is to tie every return to the IRS wage and income totals, keep session records both ways until attorneys sign off, and hedge each book toward zero at year end.
