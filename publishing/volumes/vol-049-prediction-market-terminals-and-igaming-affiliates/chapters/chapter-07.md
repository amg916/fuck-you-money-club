## Executive Summary

Practitioners from Trackdesk, Christian Strutt, CASINOAFFMASTERY, 15M iGaming and Anton Konvertov converge on one rule: the headline commission rate means nothing until the contract's NGR definition, admin fees, negative carryover and qualification terms are run through the math. Benchmarks on the table: tier-1 CPA of $150-$400 with a $250 median, revshare of 25-45% of NGR with a 30% median, and hybrids making up about 45% of new tier-1 deals. A $1,000 GGR month walks down to $700 NGR and a $245 payout at 35%, an effective 24.5% of gross. Strutt shows a $400 CPA collapsing to $20 when only 5% of players qualify. Splits remain: Crestani and Strutt chase lifetime revshare upside, CPA buys faster liquidity, and Konvertov and Puolakka insist retention, RTP and withdrawal policy at the operator decide whether any model pays.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

Every speaker works the same three levers: CPA, revenue share and hybrid, and every serious one starts the math at the casino's own ledger. iGaming Express defines GGR as total bets minus total wins, $1M wagered against $800K won for $200K top line, then strips out bonuses, processing, affiliate commissions, licensing and taxes to reach NGR, the money the operator actually keeps [zEJgRn6SLjs @ 00:01:23]. Lou 1win runs a thinner example, $1,000,000 wagered and $950,000 paid back for only $50,000 GGR, and hammers the point that NGR has no universal formula: each operator and each contract defines which deductions apply, so the NGR clause in your agreement directly sets your revshare check [d_2br4amklI @ 00:01:54]. Affnook adds the trap that matters for affiliates: heavy bonusing can lift GGR while cutting NGR, so a brand that looks hot on wagering can still pay you less [Wzz58pqo-ag @ 00:01:49]. Trackdesk, speaking from the program side, says pay on NGR and never on gross, because a strong deposit month can be erased by withdrawals, and a platform that cannot calculate NGR natively has an infrastructure problem that gets more expensive the bigger the program grows [zjkFJJ0rAJ0 @ 00:02:07]. Consensus baseline: read the revenue definition before the percentage.

Trackdesk frames model choice as fraud exposure: 'It's actually a risk management decision' [zjkFJJ0rAJ0 @ 00:01:09]. CPA paid on FTDs in bonus-heavy markets rewards the player who deposits once, grabs the bonus and vanishes; revshare on NGR ties the affiliate to real outcomes; and CPA only holds up when tight terms filter behavior before the payout fires, because loose CPA terms attract the wrong affiliates. Christian Strutt states the trade from the affiliate chair: CPA means less money, paid faster, with deals running $50 to $700, while revshare in a 20-60% band has paid them over 1,000 per player [6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:03:06]. In a separate breakdown Strutt calls revshare passive income that compounds but decays without fresh players, noting some programs paid 100 months straight while others paid nothing roughly one month in six [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:02:40]. Paul Puolakka, working mainly hybrid deals, says LTV has always been the number one metric even though some affiliates still chase FTD counts, and warns that when operators fail to look after players, affiliates waste their own traffic [TzU_M6ldezQ @ 00:03:19]. The shared baseline: the player's lifetime behavior after the first deposit, not the deposit itself, is what makes any model profitable, and that behavior lives inside the operator, not the affiliate.

The second point of agreement: the operator is the real product, and it gets vetted like an investment before any traffic moves. Christian Strutt's third rule is to work only with licensed operators, and their Australian portfolio built on unlicensed, grey-market brands became 'worth nothing overnight' when those operators lost the market [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:12:39]. In the program-selection walkthrough Strutt calls unlicensed brands 'like gangsters,' names GPWA and Affiliate Guard Dog as reputation checks, and recommends depositing £5-10 of your own money to test the site before sending a player [6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:05:43]. Adam at CASINOAFFMASTERY is blunt: you only make money once it is received, because casinos can go bankrupt or simply not pay [JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:06:04]. Anton Konvertov rejects percentage-only shopping: the KPIs that decide revshare income are player return rate and retention rate, and both are set by the project's own policies on RTP, bonuses, withdrawal limits and reputation [GRpptuPlok4 @ 00:00:39]. Across all four, the prerequisite list converges: a brand licensed in your region, a reputation check on the forums, a small live deposit to see the cashier from the player side, and every term read line by line before a single tracking link goes live.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Start with the CPA tape, because every hybrid and revshare comparison is priced against it. The 15M iGaming breakdown, using Track360's 2026 benchmarks, puts tier-1 CPA at $150-$400 with a $250 median, tier-2 at $80-$200 and tier-3 at $25-$90, flags all three as indicative rather than universal, and runs the funnel at 6.4% click-to-registration and 41% registration-to-deposit, so 1,000 clicks yield 64 registrations and 26 FTDs [WaWQNIAchz0 @ 00:01:35]. Twenty FTDs at $250 is $5,000 gross, and that commission is not profit, because content, staff, technology, SEO and ads all come out of it. Christian Strutt shows how terms gut the headline: if a CPA requires a $400 deposit and wager and only 5% of players qualify, 'your $400 CPA is actually a $20 CPA,' and caps bite too: Strutt was capped at 10, delivered 25 and was paid for 10 [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:06:35]. Adam at CASINOAFFMASTERY models a small comparison site at 50 visits a day, 5 signups and 20% depositing, or about one FTD daily and 30 a month, then assumes 8 of every 10 FTDs qualify [JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:10:03]. Strutt's benchmark for a healthy funnel: 1 to 5% conversion from click to depositing player is good [6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:07:53].

Revshare is where the contract math hides. The 15M iGaming walk takes $1,000 GGR, subtracts $150 in bonuses, $100 in tax and $50 in processing to land at $700 NGR, then pays 35% for $245, which equals 24.5% of the original GGR; negative carryover and admin charges can push it further, so a 30% deal can beat a 35% deal and comparing revshare offers by percentage alone misleads [WaWQNIAchz0 @ 00:04:55]. Adam at CASINOAFFMASTERY prices the same leak from the affiliate side: admin fees run 0-35% and turn a 50% deal into something 'more like... 35' effective [JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:05:48]. Negative carryover is the bigger killer. Strutt's example is a 10k loss against a 100k win that leaves a 90k deficit, so they insist on terms where it does not roll forward [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:04:19]. In the program-selection walkthrough Strutt calls monthly resets a must and tells the story of a player who won about 20,000, the balance reset, the player then lost about 10,000, and Strutt was paid 4-5,000; the punchline is that a 25-30% deal with clean terms can beat a 60% deal [6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:14:13]. Read every clause that touches the revenue figure before you look at the percentage.

Hybrids and the deal sheet itself close the loop on unit economics. The 15M iGaming data puts hybrids at about 45% of new tier-1 deals, typically $75-$150 CPA plus 15% revshare, with a worked example of 10 players at $100 plus 20% of $2,000 for $1,400 [WaWQNIAchz0 @ 00:03:37]. Adam's template deal is 50% revshare, €50 CPA, no negative carryover, separate funds, a cumulative €20 baseline and a €500 payout threshold, with bank-wire thresholds running higher than crypto; ladder deals like Video Slots paying €5 per player at 1-5 players and €25 at 48+ with 5% revshare get a flat no [JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:06:56]. On the tracking side, Strutt's workflow explainer says cookies last 30-90 days and anti-fraud filters block click spam and black-hat traffic [FnNlK_XZ270 @ 00:02:03], while Trackdesk recommends putting conversions on hold and only releasing them after checking whether the player kept playing past the bonus and produced positive NGR [zjkFJJ0rAJ0 @ 00:05:40]. Trackdesk also puts a hard number on payouts: manual processing works at 20 affiliates and fails well before 200, so automation is a scaling prerequisite. Program-side and affiliate-side operators are reading the same sheet: hold periods, thresholds and carryover terms move more money than the headline percentage.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Normalize every offer to NGR
   - Rebuild each offer's payout from GGR down through the contract's own deductions and fees.
   - Run the walk: $1,000 GGR minus $150 bonuses, $100 tax and $50 processing equals $700 NGR, so 35% pays $245, or 24.5% of GGR [15M iGaming Media & Dealflow Platform — WaWQNIAchz0 @ 00:04:55]; then apply any admin fee, which can turn a 50% deal into about 35% effective [CASINOAFFMASTERY — JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:05:48].
2. Stress-test CPA qualification and caps
   - Find out what deposit and wager trigger the CPA, what share of your players hit it, and whether volume is capped.
   - At 5% qualifying, a $400 CPA becomes a $20 CPA, and caps must be confirmed in writing after Strutt delivered 25 players on a cap of 10 and was paid for 10 [iGaming Marketing Agency Tips by Christian Strutt — _puyhQJIomQ @ 00:06:35].
3. Lock the deal sheet
   - Negotiate carryover, baseline, threshold and payment method before any traffic goes live.
   - Push for the template of 50% revshare, €50 CPA, no negative carryover, separate funds, a cumulative €20 baseline and a €500 threshold, and refuse ladder deals [CASINOAFFMASTERY — JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:06:56]; insist on monthly resets of negative balances [iGaming Marketing Agency Tips by Christian Strutt — 6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:14:13].
4. Audit the operator before sending players
   - Check RTP, bonus wager structure, withdrawal limits and payment reputation, then test the cashier yourself.
   - Some projects cap withdrawals around 50,000 rubles per day, which drives off whales, and Konvertov found only 1 of 12 casinos met all requirements [Traffic Cardinal EN — Affiliate Press — GRpptuPlok4 @ 00:12:51]; deposit £5-10 of your own money to test the site [iGaming Marketing Agency Tips by Christian Strutt — 6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:13:58].

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is over which commission model to take in the first place. Christian Strutt maps the choice to traffic type: an SEO site goes revshare, social or influencer volume goes CPA, paid ads stacked on a content site go hybrid, and a new affiliate still testing programs should sit on CPA or hybrid until they own real traffic data [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:08:57]. Trackdesk argues from the program side and calls the model choice a risk management decision. CPA on FTDs in bonus-heavy markets rewards players who deposit once, take the bonus and leave. Revshare on NGR ties the affiliate to real outcomes, and CPA only survives with tight terms that filter behavior before the payout fires [zjkFJJ0rAJ0 @ 00:01:09]. The 15M benchmark shows the market drifting to the middle, with hybrids at roughly 45% of new tier-1 deals, typically $75-$150 CPA plus 15% revshare [WaWQNIAchz0 @ 00:03:37]. Paul Puolakka works mainly on hybrid deals and will not call the end state. Puolakka says LTV has always been the number one thing, while some affiliates still fixate on FTD counts [TzU_M6ldezQ @ 00:03:19]. Strutt's own caution on hybrids is that both sides get paid less and the terms can shift mid-deal.

Practitioners disagree on what a fair revshare number even is, and they agree that the headline number lies. Strutt quotes a 20-60% band [6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:01:18]. CASINOAFFMASTERY quotes 20-50% and calls 20 very low, then shows admin fees of 0-35% turning a 50% deal into something more like 35 effective [JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:05:48]. Anton Konvertov treats 40-50% as standard. Konvertov reports a large program cutting from about 50% to 35%, sometimes 15%, which left webmasters unable to recoup their investment [GRpptuPlok4 @ 00:15:21]. The 15M walk shows why the percentage alone misleads. $1,000 GGR minus $150 bonuses, $100 tax and $50 processing leaves $700 NGR, so a 35% deal pays $245, an effective 24.5% of GGR. Once negative carryover and admin charges enter the contract, a 30% deal can beat 35% [WaWQNIAchz0 @ 00:05:47]. Strutt lands in the same place from the other end: monthly negative-carryover resets are a must, and a 25-30% deal with clean terms can outpay a 60% deal that drags losses forward. The working rule across all four is to price the deduction stack and the carryover clause before looking at the percentage, because the contract's definition of NGR sets the check, not the rate.

Traffic playbooks split hard. John Crestani pushes free traffic for revshare because paid ads on revshare take 3-6 months to recoup and need a bankroll to survive the gap. Crestani also prefers programs that have been around for a longer period of time [rEP2Pyna6UA @ 00:15:35]. Adam at CASINOAFFMASTERY runs SEO comparison sites as the main channel and launches for under $100 in hosting, template and domain. Adam tells beginners not to start with PPC, because margins are thin, calibration spend is high, and many who try it quit [_cAwM5AXvUE @ 00:08:12]. The deepest split is on who to monetize. Crestani openly steers toward whales and what Crestani calls the one percent of problem gamblers, maximizing player losses. Konvertov argues the opposite economics from the product side. Low RTP only wins in the short term, players drift away, and the return rate that feeds revshare erodes over millions of spins [GRpptuPlok4 @ 00:08:00]. Puolakka's line is that when the operator does not take care of the players, the affiliate wastes traffic too [TzU_M6ldezQ @ 00:03:36]. That puts long-term player value, not one-month extraction, at the center of the revshare bet for both of them.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most expensive losses came from contract fine print, not traffic. Strutt describes negative carryover as a 10k player loss sitting against a 100k win, leaving the affiliate a 90k deficit to burn through before earning again, and insists on terms where that deficit does not roll [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:04:19]. On CPA, Strutt shows how qualification rules gut the headline: if the CPA requires a $400 deposit and wager and only 5% of referred players qualify, the $400 CPA is actually a $20 CPA [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:06:53]. Caps bite the same way. Strutt had a deal capped at 10 players, delivered 25, and was paid for 10 [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:07:14]. CASINOAFFMASTERY flags ladder deals as a trap. One Video Slots structure paid €5 per player at 1-5 players, €25 only at 48+, plus 5% revshare, and the rule is never to agree to ladders [JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:08:04]. Add listing fees of €200-2,000 and loss clauses like a $300 CPA that only fires after the player loses $300, and the gap between quoted and banked commission becomes the real unit economics. The counter-template CASINOAFFMASTERY uses is 50% revshare, €50 CPA, no negative carryover, separate funds, a cumulative €20 baseline and a €500 threshold.

The second failure class is the operator itself going bad after the traffic is already sent. Strutt had a program terminate payments on hundreds or thousands of players. Strutt also warns that programs may hide or refuse a large loss, using a $20,000 whale as the example [6jlPOcl5CnI @ 00:13:28]. Strutt watched an Australian portfolio built on unlicensed operators become worth nothing overnight when those operators lost the market, and reports programs that close the account once traffic stops [_puyhQJIomQ @ 00:12:57]. Konvertov saw play a talk, an outfit running 4-6 casinos, delay payments and then shut down within 2 days. Of 12 casinos Konvertov checked, only one met 100% of the requirements, and Konvertov admits affiliates simply trust the partner by default [GRpptuPlok4 @ 00:16:06]. CASINOAFFMASTERY's blunt check is that commission only counts once it is received, since casinos can go bankrupt or simply not pay [JUjTzv8yGw4 @ 00:06:04]. The product side kills revshare quietly too. Withdrawal caps around 50,000 rubles per day drive off whales, and one project blocks withdrawals until 3 days after deposit. Strutt's retention tell is only 1-2 of 5-10 players redepositing, which signals poor retention or possible de-tagging.

Dashboard health is the trap that hides everything else. Trackdesk describes fraud that looks exactly like performance: high clicks, solid FTDs, then players who deposit once, trigger a bonus, produce negative NGR and leave. On the same program side, manual payouts hold at 20 affiliates and fail well before 200 [zjkFJJ0rAJ0 @ 00:04:11]. The 15M funnel math is the sanity check. 6.4% of clicks register and 41% of registrations deposit, so 1,000 clicks yield about 26 FTDs, and commission is not profit because content, staff, technology, SEO and ads all come out of it [WaWQNIAchz0 @ 00:01:51]. Crestani's bankroll warning lands here as well: paid ads on revshare take 3-6 months to recoup, so the affiliate needs bankroll to carry the gap [rEP2Pyna6UA @ 00:15:35]. The iGaming Real Talk host cites data that roughly 60-70% of new-acquisition traffic drops after 1 month, likely from poor operator retention, so a cohort's first month is a weak read on its revshare value [TzU_M6ldezQ @ 00:02:20]. CASINOAFFMASTERY adds concentration risk: a friend who runs a casino says 95% of revenue comes from 5% of players. The 15M briefing also flags that the UK Gambling Commission holds operators responsible for third-party marketing breaches.
