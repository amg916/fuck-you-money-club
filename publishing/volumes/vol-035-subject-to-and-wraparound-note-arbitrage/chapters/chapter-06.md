## 1. Mine Debt and Property Events Before Buying Another Lead List  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one county, two adjacent postal areas, and a property type your title company, insurance broker, and servicer already understand. Build a parcel-centered ledger in Airtable or PostgreSQL with parcel identifier, recorded owner, mailing address, deed date, mortgage recording date, lien events, permit status, and source timestamp. Pull county recorder indexes, assessor ownership exports, municipal inspection results, and court foreclosure dockets through permitted downloads. A recorded mortgage amount is historical principal, not today's payoff; a recording year suggests a rate cohort but does not establish the contract rate. Require a seller-authorized current statement before scoring debt economics. Cross-reference expired listings through an authorized broker relationship rather than scraping restricted MLS screens. Deduplicate by parcel and owner before spending on enrichment. Exclude active bankruptcy cases pending specialist review, properties beyond your repair capacity, and situations where statutory foreclosure-rescue restrictions defeat the proposed approach. The useful signal is a solvable mismatch: affordable existing debt, a seller needing a timely exit, manageable property defects, and buyers whose documented income supports the eventual payment. Cheap debt without transferable operating economics is simply an attractive line in a spreadsheet. Record source permissions alongside each import so a vendor change does not erase provenance.

Implement municipal_code_violation_trigger_arbitrage as a narrow daily exception queue, not a citywide scraping campaign. Poll municipal building department portals and county recorder dockets for failed structural or fire inspections, expired permits, new code citations, and newly filed mechanic's liens. Join events to parcels, suppress duplicate notices, and distinguish an open violation from a cured case. A lien filing is an allegation requiring document review, not proof that every claimed dollar is collectible. An illustrative weekly batch of 100 events might yield 25 unique properties, eight reachable owners, three authorized assessments, and one viable acquisition conversation. At $250 for permitted data and research, that is $10 per unique property and $250 per acquisition conversation before labor. With owner permission, route the remediation track exclusively to one qualified contractor or construction attorney while separately documenting any acquisition interest. Do not receive a percentage of legal fees or disguise settlement-service referrals as data sales. Treat any separate remediation revenue as outside this volume's scorecard. When portal access is restricted or rate-limited, use low-frequency scheduled API or bulk exports. Identify every message as private commercial contractor or remediation assistance; never imitate an inspector, municipality, or court.

Use eleven mapped plays as operating queues, with the required municipal play and ten chapter-specific sourcing workflows shown below. These workflow labels describe execution rather than additional canonical identifiers. Rate each queue by verified conversations per research hour, not the dramatic appearance of its distress signal. Low-equity owners with current payments can be better counterparties than owners facing tomorrow's auction because there is time for title, disclosures, buyer underwriting, and lender review. Mechanic's liens and failed inspections usually belong in a remediation-first queue until release costs and repair scope are established. Interview three local agents and two property managers about homes that attract payment-sensitive buyers but stall under ordinary financing. Ask for property characteristics and transaction bottlenecks, not protected-class profiles. Build a small inbound page explaining existing-debt purchases, potential lender acceleration, seller credit exposure, and the documents needed for evaluation. Give sellers a choice between a conventional sale referral, a documented creative-finance review, and no further contact. Public-record distress never establishes permission for unrestricted calling or texting. Keep acquisition outreach separate from mortgage advertising, and have the RMLO review any advertised credit terms before publication. Review queue performance every Friday and stop funding channels that produce inaccessible documents rather than usable seller decisions.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Win the Document Race Rather Than the Distress Race**
> 
> Prioritize verified debt and repair facts that competing cash-offer campaigns rarely obtain.
> - Save both the original municipal event and its latest disposition; a cured violation should not keep generating distress messages.
> - Ask for the mortgage statement before discussing a purchase premium; a recent refinance can erase the presumed low-rate advantage.
> - Have a contractor price the inspection failure separately from cosmetic improvements so a repair budget does not bury a life-safety issue.
> - Offer the seller a secure, revocable document-sharing invitation rather than requesting mortgage statements through ordinary text messages.

## 2. Qualify Both Sides Before Calling a Spread Profitable  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Apply a 100-point seller-and-property score only after basic ownership and contact permissions are established. Allocate 25 points to verified debt economics, 20 to title and transfer feasibility, 20 to buyer-supported affordability, 15 to property condition, 10 to seller objectives, and 10 to reserves and exit liquidity. Advance scores of 80 or more, request specific missing evidence at 65 through 79, and archive lower scores unless a documented fact changes. These thresholds are internal priorities, not statistical predictions. A hard stop overrides every score: unresolved authority, an unmanageable lien, unsafe occupancy, unsupported repayment capacity, or inadequate acceleration reserves cannot be offset by a low interest rate. Have title counsel inspect the mortgage, deed restrictions, junior liens, tax balances, association claims, and any foreclosure timetable. The Garn-St Germain framework generally permits enforcement of due-on-sale clauses subject to enumerated exceptions; an ordinary investor acquisition does not become protected merely because payments remain current. Do not use a trust as a concealment device. Identify whether lender consent, a permitted assumption, payoff, or another documented structure can solve the transfer issue. If it cannot, price the exposure explicitly or decline. Record who reviewed the proposed structure and which documents support their conclusion.

Build buyer demand before locking scarce reserves into inventory, but do not confuse a large down payment with repayment capacity. Route every owner-occupant wrap buyer through a licensed RMLO under the applicable SAFE Act, state licensing, and Dodd-Frank Ability-to-Repay framework. Counsel and the RMLO should identify which creditor duties and seller-financing provisions apply; the chapter's operating standard uses licensed underwriting even when a narrow exclusion might otherwise be available. Collect application data through the licensed workflow, obtain appropriate authorization for consumer reports, and avoid storing Social Security numbers in the marketing CRM. Evaluate verified income, employment where relevant, obligations, credit history, mortgage-related expenses, and the applicable payment calculation. Apply consistent criteria and handle adverse-action duties through the responsible creditor. Build demand maps from payment ranges, property needs, and timing, not ethnicity, disability, familial status, or neighborhood proxies for protected characteristics. Ask the RMLO for de-identified counts of completed, supportable files rather than raw applicant records. A pipeline of five documented households at an affordable total housing payment is more useful than fifty people clicking seller-finance advertisements. Refresh capacity before contracting because debt, employment, insurance costs, and property taxes can change between inquiry and closing.

Test the spread using amortization rather than subtracting two advertised interest rates. Illustratively, an underlying $240,000 balance at 3% with 360 monthly payments has principal-and-interest payments of about $1,012. A $270,000 wrap at 7.5% over the same term produces about $1,888. The roughly $876 monthly payment difference is not retained interest revenue: first-month interest is $1,687.50 on the wrap and $600 on the underlying loan, a $1,087.50 interest difference, while principal amortization explains the remaining distinction. Servicing, losses, carrying costs, and operating expenses further affect cash and profit. Add taxes, insurance, association charges, and lawful escrow treatment to the buyer's affordability analysis. Obtain actual insurance quotes for the ownership and occupancy structure; preserving an unsuitable old policy is not a savings strategy. For a $30,000 buyer down payment, $12,000 seller equity, $4,000 arrears, and $4,000 transaction costs leave a $10,000 provisional upfront economic spread before accounting classification. Allocating another $6,000 to reserves leaves $4,000 deployable cash, not a second expense or additional revenue. Have the accountant distinguish sale economics, financing principal, restricted cash, and recognized operator spreads. Replace every illustrative input with closing documents before approving an offer. Stress the exit against both buyer default and lender acceleration.

## 3. Run a Permission-Aware Cadence That Converts Documents into Offers  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use a six-touch, twenty-one-day cadence only where each channel is lawful and the recipient has not opted out. Day one is a plain mailed letter naming your company, property, private commercial purpose, and document-review offer. Day three is a manually placed call after applicable do-not-call screening and state-law review. Day seven is a short email where an address was properly obtained, with accurate sender information and an effective unsubscribe process. Day ten is a useful follow-up explaining what a current mortgage statement reveals. Day fourteen offers a fifteen-minute comparison of conventional sale, assumption if available, and existing-debt acquisition. Day twenty-one closes the sequence and offers a clear stop-contact choice. Do not treat a public telephone number as permission for automated dialing, prerecorded messages, or marketing texts. Use Twilio only after a documented channel-specific review and required permissions; use the CRM to enforce suppression across vendors. For inbound demand, publish one factual local case walkthrough and a secure intake form rather than promising universal qualification. Keep credit advertisements under RMLO review, including Regulation Z triggering-term requirements. A channel that creates complaints faster than qualified files is expensive even when its nominal response rate looks impressive.

Model conversion with an explicit denominator. An illustrative cohort of 300 unique researched owners might produce 240 deliverable, eligible contacts, 36 two-way conversations, 18 document-complete seller files, nine fully screened opportunities, six written offers, four signed purchase agreements, and three closings. That equals 80% contact eligibility, 15% conversation conversion from eligible contacts, 50% document completion from conversations, and 1% closing conversion from raw records. A signed purchase agreement is an intermediate milestone, not booked revenue; a referral mandate also does not transfer property. At $1,500 of data and outreach spend, acquisition marketing cost is $500 per closing before labor, legal work, inspections, and failed-file costs. Label these figures as planning hypotheses until your own cohorts mature. Track cohorts by first-contact month and source so a long closing cycle does not make this week's channel look ineffective. Three closings from 300 records does not mean ten closings require exactly 1,000 records; seller mix, title defects, buyer capacity, and reserves can change conversion materially. Review document-completion and offer-acceptance rates separately. If conversations are healthy but statements never arrive, fix trust and secure intake before purchasing more records. Never scale marketing faster than the underwriting team's document review capacity.

Handle objections by clarifying the actual exposure, not by winning a debate. When a seller asks whether the loan leaves their name, answer that a subject-to transfer ordinarily leaves their existing borrower obligation in place unless the lender releases it. Offer payment visibility and independent review, not a promise of release. When asked whether the lender can call the loan, explain the due-on-sale clause, applicable law, and the transaction's documented response plan. If a seller demands all retail equity immediately, compare conventional sale proceeds rather than forcing an unsuitable structure. If a buyer asks to qualify solely through a deposit, explain that licensed repayment-capacity review still applies. If a contractor wants an exclusive lead, establish owner permission, service scope, response time, and any lawful fixed-price work separately from mortgage settlement referrals. Record the objection category and the next evidence-producing action in the CRM. A respectful decline with a specific reason is a useful outcome because it preserves time and prevents unsuitable commitments. Follow every substantive call with a written summary of discussed alternatives, requested records, and unresolved risks. Avoid scripts claiming government affiliation, guaranteed credit repair, hidden lender protection, or inevitable foreclosure savings. Those claims undermine the very trust required to obtain reliable documents.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build and clean the weekly cohort
   - Import permitted records, deduplicate parcels, and verify owner contact eligibility.
   - Assign a source timestamp, source cost, suppression status, and responsible operator to every record before the first message.
2. Convert conversation into an evidence packet
   - Request authorized mortgage statements, ownership records, repair facts, and seller objectives.
   - Use a secure intake service and measure document completion within seven days of the first substantive conversation.
3. Match seller economics with licensed buyer review
   - Complete title screening, affordability assessment, servicing design, and reserve stress tests.
   - Issue a written offer only when unresolved matters are identified, priced where appropriate, and assigned to a qualified reviewer.
4. Close and reconcile the cohort
   - Track agreement, closing, boarding, and verified first-payment milestones separately.
   - Calculate cost per closing and document the reasons for every withdrawal before increasing the next cohort's marketing budget.

## 4. Land Three Controlled Closings, Then Earn the Right to Pursue Ten  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Sequence the first two to six months around evidence, not an arbitrary closing quota. During weeks one and two, engage local real-estate counsel, title or escrow, a licensed RMLO, an insurance broker, and a third-party servicer licensed where required. Confirm who can actually support wrap transactions; a familiar company name does not establish jurisdictional capability. During weeks three through six, work the first researched cohort and build parallel buyer intake. During weeks seven through twelve, advance the strongest files through inspections, underwriting, disclosures, and closing preparation. Months four through six should concentrate on verified servicing performance and a second cohort only when reserves remain adequate. Three completed transactions are a practical initial target; reaching ten within this window requires unusually strong conversion, capacity, and capital, rather than a different definition of a win. Distinguish signed agreements, completed acquisitions, completed wrap sales, and performing boarded notes on the dashboard. Some transactions will not close in the window. A file that exits to an ordinary sale can validate your sourcing relationship without generating wrap revenue. Do not count it in this volume's retained-interest production. Assign each live file a next action, accountable person, and dated dependency so outside professionals are not mistaken for available full-time staff.

Keep the Formula v2.0 scenarios fixed while treating sourcing volumes as illustrative support. Conservative annual operator revenue is $100,000, expenses are $72,000, and owner net profit is $28,000 at 28% with 1 FT + 3 C. Base revenue is $475,000, expenses are $285,000, and owner profit is $190,000 at 40.0% with 1 FT + 3 C. Scaled revenue is $850,000, expenses are $408,000, and owner profit is $442,000 at 52% with 2 FT + 4 C. One arithmetic bridge is ten recognized upfront spreads averaging $4,000 plus twenty performing note-year equivalents averaging $3,000 of retained interest for Conservative; twenty-five times $7,000 plus fifty times $6,000 for Base; and forty times $10,000 plus seventy-five times $6,000 for Scaled. These are illustrative revenue mixes, not additional scorecard inputs or first-year promises. A note-year equivalent represents twelve months of recognized contribution, so midyear closings contribute fractionally. Despite the scorecard's gross-revenue label, its basis already excludes property face value, loan principal, and pass-through debt service. Preserve that retained-share basis without taking the spread twice. Have the accountant apply ASC 606 where applicable to fee or sale components and the relevant lending guidance to interest; cash collection alone does not determine recognized revenue.

Board each closed wrap with a documented payment waterfall and monthly reconciliation of buyer receipts, underlying debt service, taxes, insurance, servicing charges, reserves, and operator distributions. Autopay is a collection mechanism, not evidence that the underlying lender was paid. Obtain servicer reports and independently verify the underlying account through authorized access or statements; investigate differences before distributing cash. Keep buyer defaults and underlying-payment failures in separate exception queues because one can occur without the other. Reconfirm escrow sufficiency after tax reassessments and insurance renewals. For Texas transactions, review Property Code Section 5.016 alongside Finance Code Chapter 159, including applicable licensing, disclosures, borrower rights, and payment obligations; one notice provision does not cover the entire structure. Review federal servicing requirements and state collection procedures with the servicer before drafting default messages. Reserve planning should use actual local foreclosure timelines, repair exposure, and an acceleration-response model, not merely six scheduled installments. The $12,000–$65,000 startup range and $2,000–$12,000 monthly operating range do not authorize thin property reserves or replace the annual scenario expense totals. Increase sourcing only after the first three transactions demonstrate clean document retention, timely underlying payments, and accurate revenue classification. A performing note with transparent servicing is the repeatable product; an impressive closing photograph is not.
