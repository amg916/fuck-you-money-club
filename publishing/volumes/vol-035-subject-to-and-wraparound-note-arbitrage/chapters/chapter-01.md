## 1. Acquire a financing position, not an exemption from mortgage risk  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Subject-to describes a title acquisition in which an existing mortgage remains attached to the property and the original borrower ordinarily remains liable on the underlying note. It is not a lender-approved assumption, and making payments does not release that borrower. A wraparound resale creates a new buyer obligation that includes the economic burden of the existing debt. The operator collects the wrap payment through a licensed third-party servicer, funds the underlying obligation, and retains the permitted spread after expenses. The specified play, subject_to_wraparound_rmlo_note_spread, targets underlying coupons of 2.75%–4.25% and buyer coupons of 8.5%–9.9%, subject to underwriting, pricing restrictions, and state execution rules. Those bands describe a screening thesis, not an entitlement to charge any particular borrower a particular rate. Start with the actual note, security instrument, payment history, payoff statement, title commitment, insurance declarations, and escrow analysis. A seller's dashboard screenshot cannot establish lien priority, reinstatement cost, or permission to transfer. Record who owns the property, who owes each debt, who services each payment, and who can enforce each remedy. These relationships determine whether the apparent spread survives a missed payment, casualty loss, lender demand, or disputed closing.

The simplest unit illustration uses a $200,000 underlying balance at 3.5% and a $220,000 wrap balance at 9.0%. Opening-balance annualized interest is $7,000 underneath and $19,800 on the wrap, producing $12,800 before servicing, losses, carrying costs, and overhead. This is an interest snapshot, not a twelve-month amortization forecast. Calculate actual monthly interest on declining balances and split every payment between principal, interest, escrow, and charges. If the resale price is $240,000 and the buyer contributes $20,000, the resulting $220,000 note fits that illustration; neither the $240,000 price nor the entire down payment becomes retained operating revenue. Compare the scheduled underlying payment with the buyer's fully underwritten housing payment, including taxes, insurance, association charges, and applicable assessments. Reject a structure that works only because escrow shortages or repairs are ignored. A $45 monthly servicing budget consumes $540 annually; an additional $2,400 operating reserve allocation reduces distributable cash further, although transferring cash to a reserve is not itself an accounting expense. If acceleration occurs, engage counsel and the servicer, seek lender-approved assumption or refinancing, and execute a documented lawful sale or payoff when necessary.

Five operator archetypes share the same payment plumbing but require different acquisition discipline. A low-equity seller specialist solves a narrow exit problem; an equity-rich seller specialist must separately fund the seller's equity rather than pretend a cheap first mortgage finances everything. An owner-occupant wrap originator needs a qualified buyer and documented repayment capacity. A performing-note operator earns through collected cash flows rather than repeated closings. An institutional process specialist supplies servicing oversight and evidence management without representing that administration transfers creditor obligations. Use PropStream or ATTOM to identify candidate properties, then verify ownership and recorded liens through authoritative county records and a title professional. Low-equity and pre-foreclosure signals are research inputs, not invitations to pressure distressed households. Map state foreclosure-rescue restrictions before discussing a transaction with an owner facing an active sale date. The practical moat is a network of title personnel, RMLOs, servicers, insurance agents, and local counsel who can reject defective transactions quickly. The beginner mistake is building marketing volume before establishing this rejection capability. One disputed deed or uninsurable transfer can consume several years of modeled interest margin and strand the original borrower.

## 2. Separate asset value, cash collections, retained revenue, and owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The scorecard's gross revenue field uses a deliberately narrow economic boundary: net upfront option or down-payment spreads plus retained annual wraparound interest margin. It excludes home face value, mortgage principal collections, escrow receipts, and pass-through debt service. Treat that boundary as a management reporting convention, not a substitute for transaction-specific accounting. A property sale, loan origination, purchased note, and separately contracted administrative service can follow different recognition rules. Interest-bearing instruments are generally analyzed under applicable financial-instrument guidance rather than automatically applying ASC 606 to every receipt. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis matters where a service arrangement actually falls within its scope. A buyer's down payment can represent purchase consideration, principal recovery, deferred amounts, or refundable funds; it is not automatically earned fee income. Build a closing statement bridge that identifies seller proceeds, lien cures, third-party charges, refundable balances, basis recovery, and any amount actually eligible for recognition. Have the accountant reconcile the scorecard presentation to the financial statements. Do not apply another retained-share haircut to scorecard dollars already expressed on a retained basis. Equally, never inflate those dollars by adding the mortgage payments handled for somebody else.

The canonical Conservative case is $100,000 revenue, $72,000 expenses, and $28,000 annual owner profit at 28%. Base is $475,000 revenue, $285,000 expenses, and $190,000 profit at 40.0%. Scaled is $850,000 revenue, $408,000 expenses, and $442,000 profit at 52%. Staffing remains one full-time operator plus three contractors in the first two cases and two full-time operators plus four contractors in the third. These are the fixed Formula v2.0 outputs, not projections derived from the single-property illustration. A transparent illustrative bridge uses $25,000 retained upfront economics plus $75,000 retained interest for Conservative; $175,000 plus $300,000 for Base; and $300,000 plus $550,000 for Scaled. The bridge reconciles revenue but does not assert an undocumented portfolio count or closing cadence. Recurring overhead of $2,000–$12,000 monthly is only one component of total expenses. Direct origination work, acquisition expenses, servicing, collection costs, realized losses, and other properly recognized charges explain why annual total expenses can exceed that overhead range. Keep owner distributions, debt principal, and cash reserve transfers outside the expense bridge unless their underlying accounting treatment requires otherwise. Avoid subtracting underlying interest twice after reporting interest margin net of that same cost.

Startup capital of $12,000–$65,000 and a two-to-six-month first-deal window describe access conditions, not sufficient capitalization for every acquisition. A property requiring $18,000 of reinstatement, $7,000 of closing and repair cash, and $10,000 of liquidity already absorbs $35,000 before any seller equity payment. An $800,000 portfolio balance does not become operating revenue merely because the operator controls its payment instructions. Build separate ledgers for property basis, underlying debt, wrap receivables, escrow liabilities, retained income, and unrestricted cash. Use QuickBooks Online for the general ledger and a licensed servicer's loan-level statements for contractual balances; neither should silently overwrite the other. Reconcile bank deposits to collection batches, batches to borrower statements, and underlying remittances to lender confirmations. A principal collection improves cash availability while reducing a receivable; interest recognition and loss provisioning require their own analysis. Stress liquidity for borrower delinquency, tax reassessment, insurance renewal, legal expense, and an accelerated first mortgage. The largest downside is often a payoff demand rather than one missed installment. Profit quality therefore depends on funded survival time and exit feasibility, not only the percentage printed beside the annual revenue figure.

## 4. Build a small-team control system that survives acceleration and default  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Federal due-on-sale law is the first structural constraint. Under 12 U.S.C. §1701j-3, lenders generally may enforce contractual due-on-sale provisions subject to specified protections and exceptions. An ordinary investor acquisition does not automatically qualify for those protections. A trust label, payment history, or servicing arrangement does not independently neutralize acceleration. Explain continuing seller liability and record the agreed response to a lender demand. For Texas property, Tex. Prop. Code §5.016 addresses notice requirements for certain conveyances encumbered by recorded liens, with statutory exceptions; Texas Finance Code Chapter 159 separately addresses wrap mortgage loan licensing, disclosures, and related obligations. Treat these as distinct analyses rather than one universal form. For owner-occupant buyers, require licensed RMLO involvement as the operating standard, then determine applicable federal and state creditor, originator, disclosure, and ability-to-repay duties. The SAFE Act framework, 12 U.S.C. §§5101–5116, does not replace Regulation Z analysis. Seller-financer exclusions under 12 CFR §1026.36 have conditions and limited scope; they do not automatically eliminate obligations under §1026.43. Resolve coverage before advertising terms, collecting application fees, or promising a closing date to the buyer.

A one-person operator should coordinate specialists, not impersonate all of them. In the Conservative and Base team design, the three contractor roles cover transaction and legal coordination, licensed origination, and licensed servicing or bookkeeping coordination. Required title, insurance, and other vendors remain external service providers; the staffing shorthand is not a claim that three people hold every necessary credential. At Scaled, a second full-time operator owns portfolio exceptions while four contractors support repeatable execution. Build a monthly evidence packet containing buyer collections, underlying lender confirmations, escrow movements, tax and insurance status, delinquencies, and current reserve availability. Use the servicer's controlled portal for sensitive records and restricted accounting access for reconciliation. Avoid emailing complete identity documents across a casual deal group. Separate payment authorization from reconciliation wherever practical, even if that separation is an external bookkeeper checking the owner's instructions. Route exceptions by severity: insurance cancellation and acceleration notices outrank routine document cleanup. A shared calendar tracks notice deadlines, renewal dates, escrow reviews, and note maturities. Automation should collect evidence and expose exceptions; it should not independently issue legal notices or change borrower terms without authorized professional review.

The final purchase decision combines profitability with survivability. Require a positive retained spread after realistic servicing and loss assumptions, a buyer payment supported by verified capacity, accurate insurance, a lawful closing path, and an executable response to acceleration. A six-month reserve based on $1,600 of monthly underlying payment and $400 of property carrying costs equals $12,000, but it does not fund a $200,000 payoff demand. Track those risks separately. If a buyer defaults, contact the servicer and counsel, preserve records, evaluate permitted workouts, and follow applicable notice and foreclosure procedures. Do not use lockouts, utility interruption, or informal repossession as collection tools. If the underlying lender accelerates, confirm the demand and deadlines, update payoff figures, evaluate lender-approved assumption or refinancing, and prepare a lawful sale if necessary. The seller should receive the communications promised in the transaction documents rather than discover problems through damaged credit. Review portfolio concentration by underlying lender, geography, insurance exposure, maturity date, and servicer. The durable small-team business earns a financing spread while continuously verifying performance; the fragile version spends upfront cash and hopes nobody exercises a contractual right.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build the verified debt and title file
   - Collect authorized lender records, recorded instruments, seller documents, and insurance information.
   - Reconcile balances and arrears to current statements; identify due-on-sale language, lien priority, and state-specific transfer requirements.
2. Underwrite the buyer and retained economics
   - Coordinate licensed RMLO review and model both amortization schedules with complete housing costs.
   - Separate principal, interest, escrow, refundable receipts, and eligible retained economics; reject structures dependent on unsupported refinancing assumptions.
3. Close through documented professional execution
   - Use counsel and title personnel to finalize instruments, notices, disbursements, and servicing authority.
   - Confirm accurate insurance, licensed servicing capacity, underlying payment access, and funded reserves before releasing transaction funds.
4. Reconcile performance and refresh the exit
   - Review collections, lender confirmations, escrow disbursements, and exception reports every month.
   - Escalate delinquency or acceleration immediately; update liquidity and pursue documented lawful workout, assumption, refinancing, sale, or payoff paths.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four pre-flight checks before committing acquisition capital**
> 
> Proceed only when the evidence supports the transaction as structured, not a more convenient version of it.
> - Verify title, current balances, seller authority, transfer restrictions, required notices, and the original borrower's continuing liability in the closing file.
> - Obtain licensed RMLO underwriting and a documented determination of applicable origination, disclosure, pricing, and ability-to-repay requirements.
> - Execute the licensed servicing arrangement, test the payment waterfall, and confirm that insurance and tax monitoring reflect actual ownership and occupancy.
> - Fund carrying reserves and document a separate acceleration response supported by realistic assumption, refinancing, sale, or payoff capacity.
