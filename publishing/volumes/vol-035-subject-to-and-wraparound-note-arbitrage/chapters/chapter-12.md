## 1. Days 1–7: Fund the Pod and Define Eleven Executable Plays  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with an operating map, not a property advertisement. For this chapter, the eleven mapped plays are P01 debt-trigger sourcing, P02 permission-based seller outreach, P03 underlying-debt verification, P04 title and lien clearance, P05 due-on-sale exposure analysis, P06 subject-to acquisition structuring, P07 licensed RMLO buyer underwriting, P08 wrap pricing and retained-spread analysis, P09 disclosures and documented closing, P10 licensed third-party servicing, and P11 payment audit, reserves, and workouts. These identifiers organize the volume's mechanics rather than create eleven independent businesses. The single full-time operator owns P01, P02, P03, and P08. Contractor one is local real-estate counsel coordinating title and settlement for P04, P05, P06, and P09; contractor two is the licensed RMLO responsible for P07; contractor three is the appropriately licensed servicer responsible for P10 and supporting P11. The operator retains final cash-control accountability. Title companies, insurers, and inspectors remain transaction vendors, not additional dedicated pod seats. Record each contractor's scope, jurisdiction, backup contact, fees, and weekly capacity before assigning files. Limit active acquisition files to five and buyer underwriting files to three during the first month. A file exceeding those limits enters a dated queue rather than silently expanding the four-person operating model.

Fund the sprint separately from the first acquisition. The startup capital range is $12,000–$65,000, while the operating-expense range is $2,000–$12,000 monthly; neither range establishes adequate transaction liquidity. Set a first-month nonrecoverable burn ceiling of $6,000, allocated as $1,800 for counsel and title scoping, $900 for RMLO process design and lawful file charges, $600 for servicing setup and testing, $900 for data and measured outreach, $600 for inspection and insurance diligence, and $1,200 for administration and contingency. These are procurement caps, not quoted vendor prices. Record paid invoices plus committed cancellation charges against the ceiling, so deferred invoices cannot disguise consumption. A founder starting with $12,000 therefore preserves at least $6,000 before transaction funding, but that remainder does not automatically support a closing. Each proposed acquisition needs a separately funded ledger for reinstatement, settlement, repairs, and reserves. Require liquid reserves covering six months of stressed property carry plus a counsel-quoted enforcement allowance, and document a credible acceleration response. Six months of payments cannot repay an accelerated mortgage. Do not spend buyer funds before settlement or count refundable deposits as available launch capital. Stop new commitments when the ceiling is reached without an explicit funded revision.

Build the evidence system before sourcing volume. Use Airtable or a protected spreadsheet for stage tracking, a restricted document repository for statements and identity records, and QuickBooks for cash classifications. Keep Social Security numbers and credit reports out of the general pipeline. Require multifactor authentication, role-limited access, and a document retention schedule approved for the actual lending and servicing relationships. Verify RMLO status through NMLS Consumer Access and applicable state records; verify the servicer's required licenses separately because an origination credential does not establish servicing authority. Counsel maps 12 U.S.C. §1701j-3, applicable state foreclosure law, and the actual loan's transfer provisions before any offer language is approved. A subject-to transfer does not release the original borrower, and a low interest rate does not neutralize acceleration rights. Treat seller distress as a verification trigger, never as permission to imply lender affiliation or promise foreclosure relief. Days one through seven should finish with signed scopes, spending controls, a state-specific document checklist, and ten manually screened properties. The week's success metric is a usable operating system and lawful contact basis, not the number of automated messages a platform can transmit.

## 2. Days 8–14: Convert Seller Signals into Verified, Rejectable Files  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

During week two, source enough evidence to reject weak deals quickly without overwhelming the pod. Build a cohort of forty unique, manually reviewed property records from county recorder filings, authorized data services such as PropStream, and referral partners. A recorded mortgage amount is not a current payoff, and a foreclosure filing does not establish the seller's remaining cure period. Deduplicate by parcel and ownership entity, then log source date, debt vintage, apparent occupancy, and contact basis. Target twenty individualized first contacts, eight substantive seller conversations, and four written document authorizations by Day 14; these are internal planning thresholds, not expected market conversion rates. Count a conversation only when an owner or authorized representative discusses debt, timing, and objectives. Do not count voicemail, duplicate callbacks, or broker forwards as additional conversations. Counsel reviews telephone and text practices against 47 U.S.C. §227 and relevant state restrictions; manual activity still needs applicable suppression and solicitation controls. Honor opt-outs immediately. Keep acquisition dialogue separate from any foreclosure-rescue representation. If twenty properly targeted contacts produce fewer than four conversations, change one variable—list quality, contact timing, or message specificity—before purchasing a larger list. Preserve the original cohort so later comparisons remain meaningful.

P03 and P04 turn a seller narrative into a payment and title record. Obtain seller-authorized statements, note and security-instrument copies, current servicer contact details, payment history, escrow analysis, and any reinstatement or payoff quotation. Match borrower names, property address, account identifiers, and statement dates across documents. A screenshot showing a low monthly payment is insufficient when an adjustable rate resets next year or escrow excludes insurance. Title work must identify junior liens, judgments, tax delinquency, association claims, bankruptcy issues, and recorded interests affecting transfer. Counsel determines priority and required releases rather than assuming every lien disappears at closing. Request an insurance quote reflecting actual occupancy and ownership; continuing the seller's old policy without carrier review can leave an uninsured interest. Build a twelve-month property cash calendar showing principal and interest, tax installments, insurance renewals, association charges, and known repairs. Record whether each item is escrowed or paid directly to avoid double counting. Set a two-business-day internal turnaround target for reviewing a complete seller packet, not for producing third-party records. An incomplete packet stays pending with a named missing document and due date. It never becomes verified merely because the seller needs an immediate answer.

P05 and P06 determine whether the transaction should exist. Under 12 U.S.C. §1701j-3, lenders generally may enforce contractual due-on-sale provisions subject to statutory limitations and protected transfers. An ordinary investor acquisition should not be assumed protected, and a trust wrapper is not a universal exception. Counsel documents the exact transfer, lender rights, required communications, and practical refinancing or disposition alternatives. In Texas, review Property Code §5.016 for applicable notices and timing, including its exceptions, and Finance Code Chapter 159 for wrap-specific requirements. Notice is not equivalent to lender permission. Price the deal only after modeling reinstatement, seller consideration, settlement costs, reserves, and probable buyer terms. For pipeline screening, require at least $300 monthly retained cash contribution after underlying debt service, servicing fees, and explicitly modeled property obligations, excluding principal recoveries from revenue. Stress six months without buyer receipts, a 20% increase in property taxes and insurance, and the counsel-estimated enforcement period. Those stresses test liquidity rather than redefine accounting profit. Reject a file with fabricated documents, an uncurable title defect, or no feasible acceleration response. Hold a potentially repairable file for seven calendar days with one specific cure request; do not negotiate indefinitely around missing evidence.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the Payment Failure Before the Seller Finds a Buyer**
> 
> Veteran operators protect the spread by reconciling documents and cash timing before negotiating headline rates.
> - Compare the latest escrow analysis with the mortgage statement; an escrow shortage can consume the apparent spread before a wrap is originated.
> - Request the reinstatement quotation's expiration date and daily accrual terms; a stale arrears number can break the settlement funding schedule.
> - Test the proposed ownership and occupancy structure with the insurance carrier before binding acquisition terms, rather than inheriting an unsuitable seller policy.
> - Schedule underlying-payment verification ahead of the contractual due date, with a funded backup remittance procedure that does not depend on buyer autopay succeeding.

## 3. Days 15–21: Underwrite the Buyer and Rehearse the Money Movement  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Week three connects a verified acquisition to a genuinely underwritten buyer, not merely someone holding a down payment. Route every owner-occupant wrap buyer through the licensed RMLO under this operating policy. Counsel and the RMLO determine the transaction's actual coverage under the SAFE Act, 12 U.S.C. §5103, Regulation Z's loan-originator provisions, and the ability-to-repay requirements of 12 CFR §1026.43. Seller-financer exclusions and exemptions have conditions; one exclusion does not erase other federal or state duties. Collect income, employment, debts, credit history, proposed housing obligations, and assets using the RMLO's secure process. Obtain consumer reports only with a permissible purpose and appropriate authorization under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. The RMLO documents the applicable repayment analysis and required disclosures; a large down payment is not a substitute for repayment capacity. Establish pricing and qualification criteria before evaluating applicants, apply them consistently, and route required adverse-action communications to the responsible creditor. Do not advertise a promised approval or steer a buyer toward an unaffordable balloon. If no suitable buyer exists by Day 21, keep the acquisition conditional where legally available or stop it. The two-to-six-month first-deal window makes patience an operating requirement, not evidence that the sprint failed.

P08 must separate principal movement, cash spread, and recognized operator revenue. Consider an illustrative $180,000 underlying balance at 3.5% with 300 months remaining and a $200,000 wrap balance at 7.5% amortized over 360 months. Scheduled principal-and-interest payments are approximately $901 and $1,398 monthly, respectively. The roughly $497 payment difference is not all interest income: first-month wrap interest is $1,250 and underlying interest is $525, while the two loans amortize principal at different speeds. Maintain separate amortization schedules and classify principal collections correctly. A $35 servicing fee reduces payment cash spread to roughly $462 before other obligations, reserves, and taxes; actual timing and contractual allocations can change that result. The canonical annual revenue basis includes net upfront option or down-payment spreads and retained wrap interest margin, excluding home face value and pass-through debt service. A buyer down payment is not automatically current-period income. Have the accountant assess sale, financing, refundable-deposit, and principal components, including principal-agent presentation where ASC 606 applies and the relevant receivable accounting. Never multiply a servicing retained-share assumption into an already net spread without reconciling the underlying ledger. Cash available for distributions must also respect reserves and contractual restrictions.

P09 and P10 require a settlement-to-servicing rehearsal before an actual closing. Counsel and the settlement agent assemble the acquisition instruments, wrap note, security instrument, disclosures, applicable notices, escrow instructions, insurance evidence, and recording sequence. The servicer maps incoming buyer funds to underlying payments, escrow obligations, fees, principal, retained interest, and any suspense balance. Verify underlying lender acceptance and payment posting through authorized channels; a servicer's outgoing transaction reference alone does not prove the mortgage was credited. Test a normal payment, a returned ACH debit, a partial payment, and a late underlying remittance using synthetic data rather than live consumer funds. Specify who advances a shortfall, where the advance originates, and how it is replenished. Set a one-business-day internal exception escalation target and a five-business-day month-end reconciliation target, without replacing statutory deadlines with these internal goals. Autopay does not transfer accountability away from the operator. P11 starts before closing by defining the reserve ledger, delinquency contacts, and counsel-led enforcement path. Never use lock changes, utility interruption, or informal repossession as substitutes for the applicable foreclosure or possession process. End the week with a reproducible payment waterfall and explicit ownership of every failure condition.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Freeze the reviewed transaction inputs
   - Version the debt statement, buyer terms, title findings, and settlement funding worksheet.
   - Any change to balance, rate, occupancy, ownership, or closing date triggers review of the affected underwriting and disclosure calculations.
2. Reconcile separate cash and revenue schedules
   - Match both amortization schedules to the settlement ledger and the proposed servicing waterfall.
   - Show principal, interest, escrow, servicing charges, refundable funds, and operator revenue separately; resolve every difference before funds move.
3. Prove the underlying payment control
   - Run synthetic normal, returned, partial, and delayed-payment cases through the servicer's process.
   - Record the alert owner, backup funding source, posting-verification method, and replenishment instruction for each case.
4. Release only an evidenced closing packet
   - Obtain documented counsel, RMLO, servicer, and operator signoffs within their assigned responsibilities.
   - The operator confirms available liquidity and completed conditions; unresolved statutory timing or material file defects stop settlement.

## 4. Days 22–30: Reconcile the Scorecard and Apply Day 30/60/90 Decisions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the final nine days to audit the machine, not manufacture month-one earnings. Reconcile every invoice and commitment against the $6,000 launch ceiling, review each active file with its specialist, and preserve written reasons for rejection. The annual Formula v2.0 cases are fixed reference points: Conservative revenue of $100,000 less $72,000 of total expenses produces $28,000 owner net profit at 28%; Base revenue of $475,000 less $285,000 produces $190,000 at 40.0%; Scaled revenue of $850,000 less $408,000 produces $442,000 at 52%. Conservative and Base use one full-time operator plus three contractors; Scaled uses two full-time staff plus four contractors. These reconciliations do not imply that all expenses are monthly overhead or that this chapter has recalculated the scorecard engine. Acquisition-related costs, specialist fees, and other modeled operating costs must remain consistent with the canonical annual totals. For dashboard comparison only, annual revenue divided by twelve equals approximately $8,333, $39,583, and $70,833; upfront spreads make actual monthly receipts uneven. Do not extrapolate one down payment into recurring revenue. Track recognized upfront spreads and earned retained interest separately, then reconcile their combined annual amount to the selected case. An unclosed pipeline contributes zero booked revenue.

Define the decision vocabulary before viewing results. Kill means stop the affected deal, tactic, or launch commitments; it does not abandon existing payment and consumer obligations. Fix means maintain or reduce spending while assigning one measurable correction with a seven-day owner and deadline. Scale means authorize a bounded capacity increase after evidence passes, not promise the Scaled annual outcome. At Day 30, require forty screened properties, twenty lawful first contacts, eight substantive conversations, four authorized seller packets, two fully verified acquisition files, and one file meeting pricing and liquidity requirements. With those counts and zero material unresolved defects, increase next month's contact budget by at most 25%, while preserving the five-file and three-buyer capacity limits. A closing is not required. At Day 60, require cumulative counts of eighty screened properties, forty first contacts, sixteen conversations, eight authorized packets, four verified files, and two economically qualified files. At Day 90, require 120 screened properties, sixty first contacts, twenty-four conversations, twelve authorized packets, six verified files, and three economically qualified files. Measure conversion within dated cohorts; overlapping files cannot be counted twice. These thresholds are management assumptions to test locally, not published industry averages or substitutes for individual deal underwriting.

Apply safety and liquidity failures before funnel counts. Any fabricated record, unresolved material origination defect, or unsupported acceleration plan kills the affected transaction immediately. Hitting the spending ceiling pauses additional commitments until existing obligations and a funded revision are reconciled. Fewer than four conversations from twenty contacts calls for a sourcing fix; zero economically qualified files after ten fully verified files kills that sourcing thesis unless a documented pricing change restores feasibility. At Day 60, capacity may increase only when all numeric milestones pass, the required reserves remain funded, and four servicing tests still pass. At Day 90, portfolio expansion additionally requires two consecutive fully reconciled monthly cycles on each existing serviced account, zero unresolved underlying-payment exceptions, and median operator handling time below three hours per verified acquisition file, excluding specialist labor. Without seasoned accounts, expand research only and keep acquisition limits unchanged. No closing by Day 90 prompts a bottleneck review, not automatic shutdown, because first-deal timing remains two to six months. Approve the six-person Scaled pod only after sustained workload exceeds 80% of measured capacity for four weeks and forward retained revenue supports its incremental cost. Growth must follow verified servicing performance, never compensate for absent payment evidence.
