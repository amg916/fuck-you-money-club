## 1. Normalize buyer collections into earned, retained cohort economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

For a transparent reconciliation, model four, fifteen, and twenty-five new closings with earned upfront spreads of $15,000, $20,000, and $22,000 each. Those closings contribute $60,000, $300,000, and $550,000. Separately, eight, thirty-five, and sixty performing note-year equivalents contribute $5,000 each, producing $40,000, $175,000, and $300,000. A note-year equivalent means twelve months of performing exposure; two notes performing for six months equal one equivalent. These are mixed-vintage portfolio illustrations, not claims that this year's closings instantly create an entire seasoned book. Document beginning balances, acquisition dates, payoffs, and delinquency periods before assigning exposure. The resulting totals reconcile exactly to the three scorecard cases without counting home values, borrowed principal, or pass-through payments as revenue. Maintain one property identifier across title records, the wrap note, underlying loan statements, and servicing exports. Without that join, a payoff or duplicate receipt can make an apparently excellent cohort profitable twice while its actual cash reserve remains empty. Track seasoning explicitly in every monthly performance review.

The cash bridge assumes buyer closing collections of $25,000, $30,000, and $32,000 per new transaction, with $10,000 per closing assigned to principal, restricted amounts, or documented reimbursements rather than operator income. The remaining illustrative spreads require actual earned economics and supporting agreements; a down payment alone does not establish revenue. Across the portfolios, gross closing collections therefore equal $100,000, $450,000, and $800,000. Illustrative annual wrap interest receipts are $10,500 per performing note-year, against $5,500 of underlying interest, leaving the modeled $5,000 margin. Combined collections in this limited bridge are $184,000, $817,500, and $1,430,000. Remove $40,000, $150,000, and $250,000 of excluded closing cash, then $44,000, $192,500, and $330,000 of underlying interest to recover the canonical totals. Principal amortization and escrow collections sit outside this bridge and still require separate cash schedules. The interest amounts are annual exposure assumptions, not a recommended interest-only consumer product or an amortization quote. For actual notes, calculate interest from outstanding balances and payment dates, then replace the simplifying assumptions without changing the revenue definition or disguising principal as yield.

Keep the management scorecard separate from financial-statement recognition. Calling retained spread gross revenue in the supplied model does not make all customer receipts revenue under generally accepted accounting principles. ASC 606 principal-agent analysis can govern qualifying service arrangements, while note interest, loan accounting, and property dispositions require their applicable accounting guidance. Do not automatically apply a single retained_share percentage to a property sale or offset legally distinct interest accounts in the statutory books. An accountant should map each transaction to its actual contract, asset, obligation, and recognition timing, with a reconciliation back to this operator dashboard. Likewise, a refundable option deposit remains a liability until its contractual treatment supports recognition. Show restricted escrow, refundable deposits, principal collections, earned fees, and interest margin as separate ledger categories in QuickBooks or an equivalent accounting system. Export the licensed servicer's payment allocation report and reconcile it against bank settlement, not merely the borrower's payment confirmation. A collection that later reverses must reverse the relevant receivable or revenue treatment without deleting the event history. This discipline makes the annual margin waterfall auditable and prevents cash temporarily held for another party from financing owner distributions.

## 3. Fund collection lag, locked payouts, and underlying-payment continuity separately  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Profit is a period measure; liquidity is a dated obligation schedule. For recurring charges, calculate days sales outstanding from earned receivables and their actual collection dates. Do not call the entire property principal balance overdue revenue. For acquisitions, separately measure days from the first unreimbursed expenditure to unrestricted closing proceeds. This illustration uses effective funding lags of forty-five, sixty, and seventy-five days, with committed monthly operating burn of $2,000, $6,000, and $12,000. Those assumptions require $3,000, $12,000, and $30,000 of operating-lag liquidity using thirty-day months. The burn amounts are subsets of the annual expense waterfall, not additional deductions, and distinguish fixed commitments from variable deal spending. They sit within the supplied monthly operating range without claiming that total annual expenses are limited to twelve times that range. Contractor invoices, closing costs, and workout events remain incremental cash events on the schedule. Use a thirteen-week forecast with exact tax, insurance, underlying mortgage, contractor, and servicer dates. A positive month-end balance can conceal a midmonth shortfall when the underlying payment falls due before buyer funds settle. Test daily minimum cash, not just monthly totals.

Add payout-lock exposure explicitly rather than hiding it inside average collection timing. Assume ten percent of earned upfront spread remains unavailable on one, two, and three concurrent closings. That creates $1,500, $4,000, and $6,600 of additional locked cash. This is a stress assumption, not a universal servicer policy; replace it with the settlement agreement, return exposure, and documented release dates. Next reserve three months of the simplified underlying interest obligation: $11,000, $48,125, and $82,500 across the modeled portfolios. Actual amortizing loan payments require additional principal liquidity. Add three months of a $300 monthly tax-and-insurance obligation per performing exposure unit, yielding $7,200, $31,500, and $54,000. This is operator backup capital for collection failure, separate from borrower escrow held for its designated purpose. Actual reserve sizing uses current active loans rather than annual equivalents; the illustration assumes those counts coincide at the measurement date. Neither tax reserves nor escrow accounts can fund acquisition marketing. Maintain account-level cash restrictions, and confirm insurance coverage fits the actual ownership and occupancy arrangement. A paid premium does not prove the policy correctly covers the transferred risk.

Finally, cash-fund the annual reversal allowance of $6,000, $25,000, and $35,000 and stage incremental deal expenditure outside committed operating burn. Assume $9,500, $10,000, and $8,600 of acquisition, variable delivery, and closing outlay per simultaneous file, with one, two, and three files prefunded. These timing assumptions create $9,500, $20,000, and $25,800 of deal float; validate that invoices included here are excluded from the fixed-burn calculation. Combined illustrative liquidity is $38,200, $140,625, and $233,900. Funding reserves moves cash into protected availability; it does not create a second expense after the provision already reduced profit. The supplied $12,000-$65,000 startup range can support a narrowly staged launch, but it does not finance the entire Base or Scaled reserve stack. Bridge that difference with accumulated retained earnings, fewer concurrent files, or committed financing whose covenants permit the use. Do not rely on the next buyer's refundable deposit. None of these totals funds full due-on-sale acceleration, acquisition equity, or a major rehabilitation. Model those exposures separately using actual payoff balances and executable financing or sale alternatives before accepting a transaction.

## 4. Stress retained margin before committing capital to a transfer  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run sensitivities against the canonical cases without silently rebuilding their expense assumptions. A twenty-percent reduction in retained revenue, with all modeled expenses held constant, leaves $8,000, $95,000, and $272,000 of owner profit. A fifty-percent acquisition-cost increase alone leaves $22,000, $167,500, and $409,500. A twenty-percent reduction in retained annual interest margin alone removes $8,000, $35,000, and $60,000, leaving $20,000, $155,000, and $382,000. Doubling the reversal provision alone leaves $22,000, $165,000, and $407,000. Combine the revenue reduction, acquisition inflation, and doubled reversal provision, but do not also subtract the interest-only sensitivity because that would overlap the revenue shock. Combined profit becomes a $4,000 loss, $47,500 profit, and $204,500 profit. These are deterministic tests, not probability-weighted forecasts. Their purpose is to expose how the Conservative case fails before larger portfolios exhaust their apparent margin. Record which expenses truly flex with volume and which remain contractual commitments. A contractor cancellation right may reduce future spending, but it does not cancel work already performed or restore a refund already paid. Reforecast cash and earnings together after each observed variance.

A profitable stress case can still fail through acceleration or an unworkable foreclosure timeline. The Garn-St Germain framework at 12 U.S.C. 1701j-3 permits enforcement of due-on-sale clauses subject to specified limits and exceptions; an ordinary investor subject-to purchase is not automatically protected. Neither a wrap note nor timely payments remove the underlying lender's contractual rights. Obtain written lender consent where required for the chosen structure, or price the acceleration exposure with an executable alternative rather than a hoped-for refinance. Underwrite each owner-occupant wrap buyer through a licensed RMLO, document the applicable ability-to-repay analysis under 12 CFR 1026.43, and determine licensing and seller-financer treatment under the actual facts. The SAFE Act and Regulation Z contain distinct requirements; using an RMLO does not automatically satisfy every creditor duty. For Texas transactions, evaluate Property Code Section 5.016 notice requirements and Finance Code Chapter 159 wrap-mortgage requirements, including their applicability and exceptions. Budget required notices, disclosures, delivery evidence, and timing before scheduling settlement. If the structure cannot satisfy applicable requirements economically, decline the acquisition or route it through a properly structured conventional sale.

Make the final investment decision with three separate outputs: recognized operator economics, unrestricted liquidity, and maximum event exposure. For the Base case, the starting outputs are $190,000 annual owner profit, $140,625 illustrative operating liquidity, and an additional acceleration exposure determined from actual underlying payoff statements. The first two numbers cannot substitute for the third. Require the licensed servicer to report delinquency aging, underlying-payment confirmation, escrow sufficiency, and unresolved exceptions each month. Validate licensing for the relevant state and activity rather than assuming a national software brand supplies the required authorization. Set distribution policy around verified unrestricted cash after reserve restoration, not a percentage of buyer receipts. When a buyer defaults, preserve records, obtain the jurisdiction-specific enforcement timeline, and forecast taxes, insurance, property protection, and debt payments through resolution. Do not assume immediate possession or immediate resale. Feed actual recoveries and timing back into cohort assumptions quarterly. Keep the eleven-play portfolio narrative subordinate to the verified file-level economics: an early probate record is useful only when authority, title, buyer capacity, servicing, and cash continuity all support a transaction that the operator can fund through a bad outcome.
