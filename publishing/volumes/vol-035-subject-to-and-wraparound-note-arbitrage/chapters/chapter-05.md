# Chapter 05: Structuring and Underwriting Creative Deals: Hybrids and the Morby Method

*Sub-to + seller-finance hybrids, no-money-down structures, the Morby Method, multifamily creative finance, judging whether terms are good, underwriting wraps and sub-to, wrap drafting for consumer buyers, deed of trust vs. contract for deed, and a first-deal cost lesson*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Pace Morby, James Heller, Kevin Cho and the Flipping Mastery crew run the hybrid the same way: take the existing low-rate loan subject-to, then carry the seller's equity on a separate 0% or near-0% seller note, with private money in third position to fund the entry fee. Street numbers anchor it: Heller's Colorado Springs townhouse at 146,000 on 1.5% with $400/month and a 10-year balloon; Kevin's 381,000 duplex at 4.25% sub-to plus 0% equity; Pace's $100k note at $375 for 240 months netting $925/month. The Morby Method swaps seller carryback for seller ownership in the buyer's LLC when the seller's cash-down demand cannot be negotiated away. Disagreement centers on wraps and balloons: Pace says acquire on hybrid and dispo on wrap, Fusion Notes demands Dodd-Frank underwriting and avoids balloons, and Landlord Dropout warns the underlying payment never stops.

## The Practitioner Consensus

The hybrid is a two-layer buy: the existing loan stays in place subject-to, and the seller's equity gets carried back on a separate seller-finance note. Pace Morby lays out a Georgia example with roughly 150 taken subject-to plus roughly 120 seller-financed, $0 down, 0% interest, at $300 a month [-IIDN2AQQsg @ 00:00:13], and on Flipping Mastery TV he puts hybrids at about 15% of his creative deals [6Bdxz-HKeFI @ 00:02:31]. The decision rule is equity-driven. Pace says flatly that if the seller has no equity, the deal is a sub-to [pvgEqb4r4gw @ 00:06:01]. The hybrid lives in the middle, where a cheap existing loan is worth preserving but the seller still holds real equity they want paid. James Heller's Colorado Springs townhouse fits that pocket exactly: a 360,000 ask against a 194 mortgage at 2.99%, leaving 166,000 of equity that he refused to hand over in cash because it made no sense for the deal [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:03:31]. His answer was to keep the 2.99% loan in place and finance the gap with the sellers on a slow, low-rate note. The townhouse had sat about 60 days on the MLS and cash offers came in under the sellers' number, which is the exact friction a hybrid is built to dissolve.

Every operator here opens the seller conversation the same way: find out what the money is actually for, then trade price for terms. Pace teaches to always start with cash, never with creative finance, and to ask every seller why they haven't sold yet [pvgEqb4r4gw @ 00:02:35]; his Janie Munson lesson, find the bunnies, means locating the seller's real problem before structuring anything. Heller's key question was what the sellers planned to do with the proceeds, and the answer was savings or a CD, which made a 0%-to-low-rate note an easy swap for money that would have sat idle [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:01:13]. Pace runs the same trade on a straight seller-finance rental: he paid Susan full retail at $100k when wholesalers were sitting at $60-70k, and told her that if she gave him terms he would give her the 100,000 she wanted [Ke0nzzYXEfk @ 00:01:23]. Transparency closes it. Heller told his sellers he was making the property a rental and would lose money every month if he paid more, then showed them the math [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:08:01]. The pattern holds across all of them: full or near-full price satisfies the seller's number, while the payment, rate, and timing are engineered around the rent.

The paper underneath the hybrid follows a consensus too. Scott Horne's Owner Finance Network lists the acquisition routes side by side: a seller-financed note and deed of trust, a wrap of the seller's first lien, subject-to with a second lien back to the seller, or third-party second and third liens, and he insists the note and deed of trust both describe the underlying debt so any future title or mortgage company knows its lien position [it6vSh38OhY @ 00:00:54]. The Real Estate Lawyer frames the deed of trust as a three-party security instrument, borrower, lender, and a lender-designated trustee, summed up as if you don't pay you don't stay [2PWuaqjQVgc @ 00:01:07]. Pace's lien education on Flipping Mastery TV adds the ordering rule: priority follows the recorded date, taxes supersede all liens, and you don't want to sit in second unless something is in it for you and you understand the higher risk [pvgEqb4r4gw @ 00:25:10]. On the Kevin Cho duplex, the private lender sits in third behind the sub-to and the seller carry, and the pitch is the same safety net used with sellers: if the buyer defaults, they can take over the whole asset, equity and cash flow included [Yw9I--p2Ef8 @ 00:05:24].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Heller's negotiation is the cleanest worked example of the hybrid math. He opened at 0%, principal-only, and offered $400 a month; the sellers wanted more, floated annual payments of 4,800, and then asked for at least 1.5% so they could sell the note later [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:04:48]. The final terms: the sellers knocked 20k off, the seller note landed at 146,000 at 1.5%, $400 a month on roughly a 40-year amortization, with a 10-year balloon [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:05:26]. Against the 1,138 PITI on the 2.99% loan and about 1,900 in rent, he reports roughly $150 a month of cash flow, likely eaten by operating expenses, but on a zero-down, no-repair, rent-ready townhouse [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:06:23]. Kevin Cho's duplex runs the same skeleton across two units: a 381,000 price, the existing loan taken sub-to at 4.25%, and the seller's equity financed at 0% with no balloon [Yw9I--p2Ef8 @ 00:02:06]. PITI of about 1,733 plus roughly 250 of seller carry puts the debt stack near $2,000, and the plan is midterm rentals for travel nurses on three-month stays, with the 3/1 front unit at 2,400 to 2,500 covering the full foundational cost so the back unit becomes the profit.

Funding the entry fee is where the third lien comes in. Pace defines the entry fee as all the cash a deal needs and says about 70% of his deals require private money, with the other 30% closing without it [pvgEqb4r4gw @ 00:32:52]. On Kevin's duplex that meant about $30k of private money and none of his own: Maricella lent $40k at 12% for 24 months in third position at $400 a month, leaving roughly $10k for the first payment and cleanup, and pushing the foundational monthly cost to $2,400 [Yw9I--p2Ef8 @ 00:06:04]. On the Flipping Mastery hybrid masterclass, the scripted offer is take over 100k at 3%, add a 100k second note with no payments for 24 months, and source the down payment from a private money lender [6Bdxz-HKeFI @ 00:06:47]. When the seller needs more money down than can be negotiated away, Pace switches to the Morby Method: get the seller's yes before the inspection period ends, sign a purchase agreement with a 30- or 60-day close, pull the lender's term sheet for down payment, rate, and payment, then go back and finalize, with the seller retaining part ownership of the buyer's LLC under a separate partnership agreement instead of a carryback [11lUs1njQMw @ 00:02:27].

Pace's straight seller-finance rental shows how to defer the down payment entirely. He took over tenants paying $1,650 a month and structured a $100k note at 0%, $375 a month for 240 months, explicitly not sub-to [Ke0nzzYXEfk @ 00:00:27]. Susan's original ask was $20k down, 8% carry, and a 10-year balloon; he pushed back, closed escrow March 31, 2020, and set the down payment as $5k at month six and another $5k at month twelve [Ke0nzzYXEfk @ 00:01:58]. The cash-flow table carries it: $1,650 rent minus $375 seller payment, $150 taxes and insurance, and a $200 vacancy and repair reserve nets $925 a month, so six months of net covers each $5k installment from tenant cash. He also sets a hard line on the Morby Method: if there's not active income coming in on the deal, he will not do one [rQCg4GFRLAI @ 00:05:44]. His Maricopa VA underwriting shows the same discipline on cash flow. Payments of 1313 plus 165 solar plus 325 on the seller note total 1803 against 2,200 rent, and his recalculated cost of $1,818 against $2,200 leaves $382 before the private money lender is paid, which he labels a non-cash flowing deal [t7aFESM6V_k @ 00:11:06].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Sort the lead by equity
   - Classify every lead as free-and-clear, high equity, or low equity before writing any offer.
   - Free-and-clear and high-equity owners get seller finance; low-equity owners get sub-to or lease option; a cheap existing loan sitting under real equity is hybrid territory [Pace Morby — IHTKcj3dVN0 @ 00:45:01].
2. Open with cash and find the bunnies
   - Lead with a cash number, ask why the house hasn't sold, and ask what the seller plans to do with the proceeds.
   - Always start with cash, never creative finance, then locate the seller's real problem; Heller's sellers only planned savings or a CD, which opened the door to a low-rate note [Flipping Mastery TV — pvgEqb4r4gw @ 00:02:35].
3. Underwrite backwards from the rent
   - Start from the existing mortgage payment and market rent, find the margin, then set the maximum seller payment.
   - Open the seller note at 0% principal-only, factor repairs and down payment, and leave margin for PM, utilities, and repairs before agreeing to any rate [James Heller -- Hellerman Industries — nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:08:34].
4. Fund the entry fee and paper the stack
   - Layer private money behind the sub-to and seller note, then document every underlying lien.
   - Kevin's lender put $40k at 12% for 24 months in third position, pitched on taking the whole asset on default [Pace Morby — Yw9I--p2Ef8 @ 00:05:24]; the note and deed of trust must describe each underlying debt [Scott Horne's Owner Finance Network — it6vSh38OhY @ 00:00:54].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether to buy on a wrap at all. Scott Horne's Owner Finance Network treats the wrap as a standard acquisition lane next to seller notes and subject-to with a second lien back to the seller. It comes with one hard floor: the wrap balance must be at least equal to all underlying debt, and you can budget for PMI or MIP but cannot charge the buyer for insurance you are not providing [it6vSh38OhY @ 00:04:30]. Pace Morby, teaching on Flipping Mastery TV, says don't buy on a wrap. Keep the subject-to note clean, treat a wrap at 6% over a 3% loan as arbitrage, and run the rule acquire on hybrid, dispo on wrap [6Bdxz-HKeFI @ 00:17:20]. The Landlord Dropout ran that disposition play on a former rental, selling for $140k with $4k down and $136k carried at $1,350 a month over 30 years on top of roughly a $90k underlying balance. That turned about $250 of net rental income into a $650 spread, with the buyer handling maintenance [hIflx_8aWVI @ 00:02:24]. Fusion Notes splits the difference: wraps can be sold, but they need stronger underwriting than straight notes, and most traditional note buyers pay off the underlying loan when they buy one [JAyoDBTIEIE @ 00:55:11].

Seller-note pricing is the second fault line. Pace Morby says 'the majority of the deals we do are 0% seller finance' [t7aFESM6V_k @ 00:12:31]. On the Poyer rental, the seller opened at $20k down, 8% carry and a 10-year balloon. Pace paid full retail at $100k, traded price for terms, and landed $375 a month for 240 months at 0%, with the down payment deferred into two $5k installments [Ke0nzzYXEfk @ 00:01:39]. James Heller -- Hellerman Industries also opens every negotiation at 0% principal-only. On a Colorado Springs townhouse, though, the sellers wanted at least 1.5% so they could sell the note later, and Heller conceded: $20k knocked off, $146,000 at 1.5%, $400 a month on roughly a 40-year amortization, and a 10-year balloon [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:05:26]. Fusion Notes pushes against both styles from the note-buyer side. Avoid balloons, rising rates and rising payments because they also confuse servicers, and on owner-occupied loans any balloon has to sit at least 60 months out. The speaker is unsure whether rate adjustments follow the same rule and advises against both [JAyoDBTIEIE @ 00:53:22]. Heller's 1.5% coupon was driven by the sellers wanting a note they could sell, which is the same convertability Fusion Notes underwrites every loan toward.

The third split is how to paper a deal when the seller's side pushes back. A seller's attorney shut down a straight subject-to on a $1M house with $750K owed plus $50K in HOA dues. Pace Morby's fix was an agreement for sale to appease the attorney: the seller keeps the deed and the mortgage, seller-finances for about five years, and the buyer refinances once there is enough equity [11lUs1njQMw @ 00:01:22]. When the seller needs more money down and it cannot be negotiated away, the Morby Method gets a yes before the inspection period ends and signs a purchase agreement with probably a 60-day close. It then pulls the lender's term sheet and gives the seller part ownership of the buyer's LLC under a separate partnership agreement instead of a carryback [11lUs1njQMw @ 00:02:27]. Pace caps that same tool hard: no Morby Method without active income on the deal, because people take $400k and then bail on the payments, and a novation is the alternative offered [rQCg4GFRLAI @ 00:05:44]. TerraFunded shows why instrument choice matters in Texas. An unconverted contract for deed lets the seller keep everything, while a deed of trust forces a trustee sale that hands roughly $27,500 back to the buyer at a $130k sale after 36 payments [Q9c1Be5WnWI @ 00:06:20].

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The recurring firsthand loss is that the underlying debt never stops. The Landlord Dropout moved into creative finance after a roof, a driveway and HVAC across three rentals cost over $25k in 90 days, more than eight years of one property's $250 monthly cash flow [hIflx_8aWVI @ 00:00:23]. On the resulting wrap sale, the same operator lists the exposures that remain. The underlying payment stays due if the buyer stops paying, maintenance is not guaranteed, servicing and default costs still apply, and 30 years of payments have not actually been collected yet [hIflx_8aWVI @ 00:04:26]. Fusion Notes reports the note-side version: when a wrap defaults you still pay the underlying, taxes, insurance and legal fees. The speaker has four to five foreclosures active, and borrowers often file bankruptcy the day before the sheriff's sale, most recently about six weeks earlier [JAyoDBTIEIE @ 00:41:03]. Scott Horne's Owner Finance Network flags the slow-motion version on double wraps. One stack seen in the field had three modification liens on top of down-payment assistance and a first lien. HUD liens carrying 0% interest and no payments make it easy to lose track of balances and end up upside down unless every balance is tracked [it6vSh38OhY @ 00:05:25].

Pace Morby's own live underwriting shows how quickly a creative deal stops cash flowing. On a Maricopa VA loan, the stack ran $1,313 on the first, $165 for solar and $325 on a $90k seller second with a five-year balloon, totaling $1,803 against $2,200 rent. The $45k raise for the deal added about $350 a month, pushing it to roughly $400 negative [t7aFESM6V_k @ 00:01:01]. To save it, the seller payout was ground down to $26k plus $9,500 in arrears for a $35k entry, and the assignment fee was cut from $10k to $5k and deferred until a second deal [t7aFESM6V_k @ 00:04:18]. Even then, the final $1,818 monthly cost against $2,200 left $382 before the private money lender got paid, and Pace labeled it a non-cash flowing deal [t7aFESM6V_k @ 00:11:06]. James Heller -- Hellerman Industries closed a zero-down, no-repair townhouse at about $150 a month and admits that probably gets eaten up by operating expenses. Heller is counting on equity paydown and depreciation of 20 to 30 thousand or more instead. The margin capped the seller at $400 a month, and Heller told them that paying more meant losing money every month [nO_A1GtSlaA @ 00:06:23].

Closing and exit risk kills deals after the numbers already work. Pace Morby lists child-support and IRS liens as deal killers and has a Kermit, Texas RV park stuck under contract for two years over a title error [IHTKcj3dVN0 @ 02:42:07]. On another acquisition, Claude AI surfaced 15 of 45 homeowners suing the builder over uncompacted dirt during diligence, a defect that would never show up in the payment math [IHTKcj3dVN0 @ 02:43:10]. On the back end, Fusion Notes warns that cashing out paper is slow: several weeks, and sometimes over a month even at banks with established processes. Institutional buyers pay more than retail buyers because of lower cost of capital, and retail buyers are flaky. Without the underwriting package and TRID disclosures, some buyers, such as First National Acceptance in Michigan, will not even make an offer [JAyoDBTIEIE @ 00:32:05]. A big check at closing does not rescue the paper either. A 25 to 30% down payment with no documented income cannot be made compliant, cannot be sold to a bank, and may be viewed as predatory, which leaves the note holder carrying 30 years of payments with no exit [JAyoDBTIEIE @ 00:35:22].
