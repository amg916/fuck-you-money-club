## 1. Identify the payer, the pressure, and the person authorized to release money  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The wrap buyer writes the recurring check, while the property seller usually contributes an opportunity rather than a recurring fee. A low-equity seller may face a mortgage cure deadline, relocation costs, vacancy expenses, or a sale price insufficient to cover conventional brokerage and settlement charges. Their decision is whether a documented transfer improves their position relative to a negotiated lender workout or ordinary sale. Buying subject to an existing mortgage does not automatically release that seller from personal liability. Put that continuing exposure, the payment-monitoring arrangement, and the lender's acceleration rights into the transaction discussion before requesting signatures. The buyer's pressure is different: they want an affordable home and a financing path their documented income can support. A thin credit file or unconventional employment history is a reason to investigate repayment capacity, not permission to skip it. Ask what monthly housing payment remains affordable after taxes, insurance, association dues, and ordinary living obligations. Neither impending foreclosure nor bank rejection creates payment capacity. The operator earns a spread only when these two situations can be matched without selling either party a hidden obligation.

Authority follows the asset and the account, not the loudest participant on a sales call. Confirm every record owner, required spouse signature, entity manager, trustee, and estate representative before treating a seller's acceptance as executable. Obtain the underlying borrower's written authorization for lender communications and statement access; that authorization is not lender consent to the transfer. The settlement agent controls closing disbursements under signed instructions, while the licensed servicer controls recurring collections under its servicing agreement. The operator cannot casually redirect either account. On the buyer side, identify who owns the down-payment funds, whether another person is providing a gift, and whether repayment obligations affect underwriting. Verify bank records through approved secure channels and document large recent deposits. A purchase contract with two buyers but funds from an unrelated business requires explanation before closing, not a convenient third-party payment link. For a $20,000 buyer deposit, a signed allocation might direct $12,000 toward seller equity and arrears, $3,000 toward documented acquisition and settlement costs, and $5,000 toward the operator's modeled upfront spread. That allocation describes deal economics; it does not by itself determine the accounting treatment of a down payment.

Counterparty screening must test willingness, capacity, and authority separately. For the seller, examine title, judgment liens, bankruptcy filings, delinquent taxes, association balances, and actual reinstatement figures. A bankruptcy filing can invoke the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. §362; do not treat a distressed owner's signature as sufficient authority to continue collection or foreclosure activity. For the buyer, use a licensed residential mortgage loan originator, or RMLO, to document income, employment, assets, debts, credit history, and repayment capacity under applicable rules. Apply consistent written criteria rather than neighborhood assumptions or protected characteristics. Consumer reports require a permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. §1681b, and credit decisions can trigger adverse-action duties under Regulation B and the Fair Credit Reporting Act. For the servicer, inspect applicable licenses, trust-account controls, complaint history, continuity plans, and sample reconciliations. For the lender, read the actual security instrument and identify acceleration exposure. The Garn-St Germain exceptions in 12 U.S.C. §1701j-3 are specific; an ordinary investor purchase is not automatically protected. Decline the deal when an affordable buyer payment, a defensible transfer structure, and a funded downside plan cannot coexist.

## 2. Match each payment to a rail, a release condition, and a reversal risk  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A payment rail moves funds; it does not establish entitlement to them. For acquisition and resale closings, use a settlement professional permitted to handle the transaction in the property's jurisdiction. Confirm wire instructions through a previously verified telephone number, never through contact details supplied in an unexpected instruction-change email. Record the callback, beneficiary identity, bank routing information, and the settlement file reference. Release funds only against the signed disbursement statement and the applicable recording and title conditions. A domestic wire generally offers stronger settlement finality than a consumer debit, but a bank's receipt confirmation still does not prove that every contractual condition has been satisfied. Escrow is an account-control and instruction framework, not a separate electronic network. It can receive wires or other permitted funds while restricting who may release them. Likewise, a milestone hold is a contractual release mechanism layered over an account. Keeping these categories distinct prevents a common sales error: describing money as protected merely because a payment application displays a successful transfer. The relevant questions are where the funds sit, who owns them, and which verified event permits disbursement.

Recurring buyer payments should flow to the licensed servicer rather than the operator's general operating account. ACH debits require appropriate authorization and remain exposed to returns and disputes; a settlement date is not universal finality. Regulation E, including 12 CFR §1005.10, governs relevant consumer preauthorized transfers and restricts conditioning credit on repayment by recurring electronic transfers. Offer a workable alternative rather than making autopay the sole route to financing. Require the servicer to define its cutoff times, posting rules, returned-payment treatment, partial-payment handling, and underlying remittance schedule. Build an operating buffer so a late buyer payment does not instantly become a late underlying mortgage payment. For example, three months of a $900 underlying installment requires $2,700 per property before adding taxes, insurance, repairs, or foreclosure expenses. Ten similarly exposed properties require $27,000 just for that installment buffer. Keep this liquidity separate from customer escrow balances. The published $12,000–$65,000 startup range therefore cannot support every portfolio configuration; reserve needs, acquisition cures, and transaction expenses determine the feasible opening size. Automation reduces missed tasks but does not supply missing cash.

The remaining rails are useful mainly because they reveal what should not fund a wrap. A professional retainer pays for a defined engagement and follows the applicable trust-account and earned-fee rules; it is not a substitute for buyer escrow. Statutory disbursement sends proceeds through a court, county, or state process and follows that process's claimant verification, waiting periods, and fee restrictions. A platform checkout may collect an eligible service charge, but card chargebacks, processor restrictions, and account freezes make it a poor default for mortgage installments or property down payments. Check Stripe or PayPal terms for the precise activity instead of assuming ordinary merchant onboarding permits real-estate funds. Factoring monetizes eligible receivables by selling them at a discount; selling a mortgage note instead requires separate transfer, lien, servicing, and investor analysis. Neither route cures a weak borrower. A milestone hold can reserve $2,000 pending an agreed repair inspection when the contract, account structure, and local rules permit it. Specify the inspector, completion standard, dispute procedure, and release deadline. Never withhold an underlying mortgage installment as leverage over an unrelated fee dispute or repair disagreement.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Verify payers and instructions
   - Match each incoming payment to an authorized party and signed obligation.
   - Independently confirm settlement bank details and document third-party funding explanations before accepting closing money.
2. Board both payment schedules
   - Load the wrap obligation and underlying mortgage into the servicer's reconciled account structure.
   - Check first-payment dates, escrow responsibilities, amortization schedules, authorized payment methods, and remittance lead times.
3. Reconcile collections and remittances
   - Compare bank settlements, borrower postings, underlying lender receipts, and escrow movements.
   - Investigate every returned transfer, suspense balance, short payment, and missing lender receipt before releasing distributable cash.
4. Release only earned distributions
   - Approve operator distributions against the ledger and current reserve requirement.
   - Retain the monthly evidence packet and escalate exceptions through named staff without using customer escrow for operating costs.

## 3. Protect the earned spread without confusing collections, principal, and revenue  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Start the ledger with the difference between collection volume and operator revenue. Consider an illustrative opening month with a $200,000 wrap balance at 5.4% and a $180,000 underlying balance at 4.0%. Opening-month interest is $900 on the wrap and $600 on the underlying debt. If the respective contractual principal and interest installments are $1,200 and $900, each reduces its balance by $300 that month. Add a separate $250 buyer escrow collection for taxes and insurance, assuming those amounts are not already included in the underlying installment. The servicer receives $1,450, sends $900 to the underlying lender, allocates $250 to escrow, and charges a $50 servicing fee. The remaining $250 is cash before other expenses and reserves. Retained interest revenue is $300, with the $50 servicing charge treated here as an operating expense. Neither the $300 principal collection nor the $250 escrow receipt becomes revenue. Recalculate later months from actual amortization schedules; equal principal reductions in this opening example do not establish a permanent relationship. This waterfall illustrates mechanics rather than a prescribed consumer loan design or a substitute for originator underwriting.

The canonical Formula v2.0 cases use annual operator revenue of $100,000, $475,000, and $850,000, not home sales volume or aggregate borrower payments. One illustrative reconciliation uses $5,000 of earned net upfront spread per completed transaction and $1,000 of retained annual interest revenue per performing note-year. Conservative activity is ten completions plus fifty note-years: $50,000 plus $50,000 equals $100,000. Base activity is fifty-five completions plus two hundred note-years: $275,000 plus $200,000 equals $475,000. Scaled activity is one hundred completions plus three hundred fifty note-years: $500,000 plus $350,000 equals $850,000. These are alternative portfolio assumptions, not annualizations of the preceding monthly example. A note-year measures time-weighted performing exposure; two notes performing for six months contribute one combined note-year. Existing inventory can therefore exceed current-year closings. Annual operating costs of $72,000, $285,000, and $408,000 leave exact owner profits of $28,000, $190,000, and $442,000. The corresponding margins are 28%, 40.0%, and 52%. The teams remain 1 FT + 3 C, 1 FT + 3 C, and 2 FT + 4 C. These cost totals include more than the published monthly overhead range; reconcile deal costs, contractors, servicing, and credit losses separately.

Protect fees by documenting what is earned, when it is earned, and which authorized account pays it. A buyer's down payment can be consideration toward property acquisition or principal, rather than a standalone fee. An option payment can carry refund, credit, or performance conditions. Have the accountant map each component to the applicable accounting guidance and avoid treating every dollar arriving at closing as current income. ASC 606 principal-versus-agent analysis can matter for qualifying service arrangements; mortgage interest and financial assets require their own accounting treatment. For this scorecard, the retained operator basis excludes underlying property value, pass-through debt service, and customer escrow. Do not deduct underlying interest twice after already calculating retained interest revenue. Contracts should state the consideration, funding source, permitted setoffs, cancellation treatment, and disbursement instructions. Record security instruments through the settlement process, retain executed note custody records, and document any later assignment. Those protections preserve enforceable rights; they do not authorize self-help eviction, unilateral account withdrawals, or fee extraction from protected funds. If a buyer disputes an ancillary charge, isolate the dispute while continuing accurate allocation of undisputed mortgage payments under the governing documents and law.

## 4. Screen statutory exposure and build a funded fallback before collecting  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Make licensed origination the operating standard for every owner-occupant wrap buyer in this playbook. The SAFE Act framework, including 12 U.S.C. §5103, governs licensing or registration for covered loan originators. Dodd-Frank's residential mortgage provisions and Regulation Z impose additional obligations when their coverage tests apply. The ability-to-repay rule in 12 CFR §1026.43 requires a reasonable, good-faith repayment determination for covered transactions. Limited seller-financer exclusions within 12 CFR §1026.36 do not create a universal exemption from every lending, disclosure, or state requirement. Give the RMLO the actual note terms, balloon provisions, fees, property charges, and borrower documents rather than asking them to approve a predetermined payment. In Texas, review Finance Code Chapter 159 for residential wrap mortgage origination requirements and Property Code §5.016 for applicable encumbered-property conveyance notices. These provisions address different duties, and exceptions must be checked against the actual facts. Do not collapse them into a generic signed acknowledgment. Establish the required notice recipients, delivery methods, timing, licensing status, and evidence retention before scheduling closing. A settlement date chosen for marketing convenience cannot shorten a statutory notice period.

The mapped play distressed_asset_recovery, Distressed Asset Recovery — TRACK_1, offers an adjacent sourcing and revenue discipline: locate mispriced, orphaned, or administratively stranded assets and earn a lawful recovery fee or acquisition spread. Its universe includes tax-sale overages, state unclaimed funds, dormant judgments, government surplus lots, and distressed micro-businesses; each asset type has different ownership and collection rules. A homeowner's surplus claim is not automatically collateral for your wrap transaction. Suppose a separate verified claim produces $30,000, and the applicable jurisdiction permits a properly documented 10% contingent recovery fee. The fee is $3,000, the claimant receives $27,000 before any separately authorized charges, and $800 of direct research and permitted filing support leaves $2,200 before overhead. If a statutory cap or waiting period prevents that contract, revise the structure or decline; do not relabel the fee. Screen assignments, heirship, competing liens, foreclosure distribution priorities, and representation restrictions before forecasting collection. The prescribed fallback is diversification across multi-state dockets with attorney-backed filing partnerships where required. That means jurisdiction-specific procedures and qualified local professionals, not shopping for weaker consumer protections. Keep this separate example outside the canonical wrap revenue bridge unless explicitly budgeted.

Design the failure response while everyone is still cooperative. If the underlying lender accelerates, immediately obtain the written demand and a verified payoff figure, involve transaction counsel, and evaluate funded refinancing, negotiated resolution, or an orderly sale. A $180,000 principal balance can require more than $180,000 to resolve after accrued interest and permitted costs; a $2,700 installment reserve is plainly not an acceleration reserve. Never conceal ownership changes or make false occupancy statements to preserve a rate. If the buyer defaults, continue underlying payments from operator liquidity while the servicer applies notices, loss-mitigation procedures, and foreclosure requirements applicable to that account. Regulation X provisions such as 12 CFR §1024.41 have defined coverage and exemptions; have the servicer document applicability instead of assuming every portfolio follows identical procedures. Budget a six-month $900 installment interruption as $5,400 before property protection, counsel, taxes, and insurance. Review reserve adequacy monthly against actual delinquencies and renewal costs. Payment monitoring must prove receipt by the underlying lender, not merely a debit from your servicer. Withhold owner distributions when reconciliation breaks, then restore them only after the shortage and its cause are resolved.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four verifications before funds become distributable**
> 
> A signed contract and a visible bank balance are insufficient. Assign an accountable reviewer to each verification and retain the evidence in the transaction record.
> - Confirm title, execution authority, bankruptcy status, underlying loan terms, and a funded response to acceleration before acquiring the property.
> - Confirm licensed RMLO underwriting, applicable disclosures, state wrap requirements, and an affordable fully loaded payment before financing the buyer.
> - Confirm licensed servicing, segregated escrow responsibilities, independently verified settlement instructions, and a successful boarding reconciliation before collecting scheduled installments.
> - Confirm underlying lender receipt, current tax and insurance funding, resolved payment exceptions, and adequate operator reserves before releasing owner distributions.
