## Executive Summary

Surplus recovery earns money from permitted services on money actually distributed, not from impressive county balances. Three structures require separate underwriting: administrative contingency services, attorney-led judicial recovery, and authorized purchases of interests. Each has different fee restrictions, claimant protections, and cash requirements. This chapter follows a $48,000 recovery through an illustrative $8,200 contribution, then connects file economics to acquisition cohorts and annual owner profit. Candidate #2 carries an editorial score of 87/100; that score is not a collection probability. Local procedure and documented entitlement determine which files deserve capital.

## Choose the revenue structure before quoting a percentage  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Model one is a direct administrative county tax-overage contingency agreement, using a negotiated 10% to 30% only where that structure and percentage are permitted. The percentage describes a possible commercial range, not a nationwide entitlement. First identify the sale type, fund custodian, claimant category, relevant statute, and county claim packet. Obtain the sale ledger, recorded deed history, distribution statement, and current surplus balance before proposing compensation. A $48,000 posted balance may already face superior claims or prior distributions. Price against the claimant's supported recoverable interest rather than the headline fund. Give the claimant the official self-claim route and distinguish your document collection service from representation before a court. Do not assume notarization makes an otherwise restricted agreement enforceable.

Model two is attorney-led judicial recovery through a motion or interpleader proceeding. Counsel represents the claimant, checks priority and standing, prepares pleadings, and handles contested entitlement. The operator may originate the relationship and perform separately permitted factual support, but co-origination is not permission to split legal fees with a nonlawyer. Counsel must establish whether the proposed compensation and referral arrangement are allowed before work begins. A written disbursement schedule can direct escrow or a trust account to pay lawful, separately authorized charges after receipt; the account itself does not legalize a prohibited charge. If the operator cannot earn compensation under a permissible arrangement, refer the claimant without a recovery-linked referral payment and remove that projected fee from the pipeline.

Model three purchases an interest through an assignment where the governing law recognizes the transfer and its disclosure requirements are satisfied. This is not the same cash business as a service contingency: the buyer may advance purchase money, absorb a failed claim, and recover only the assigned interest. Track purchase consideration separately from operating expense and never disguise a capped service fee as a sale discount. Florida section 45.033 addresses foreclosure surplus entitlement and voluntary transfers; it is not a blanket authorization for tax-deed finder contracts. Texas Tax Code section 34.04 governs excess proceeds following tax sales. Have local counsel identify applicable compensation restrictions, assignment validity, filing deadlines, and claimant priority rather than transplanting a form between these two systems.

**Three structures, three underwriting questions**

| Structure | Revenue basis | Pre-contract evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administrative contingency | Permitted percentage of received recovery | County process and applicable fee limit |
| Attorney-led recovery | Separately permissible service compensation | Engagement and lawful disbursement schedule |
| Assignment | Recovery less purchase basis and expenses | Valid transfer, disclosures, and funded consideration |

## Underwrite the file waterfall without hiding claimant deductions  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use the following waterfall only in a jurisdiction and transaction where a 25% charge is permitted. A completed $48,000 surplus recovery generates a $12,000 gross fee and leaves $36,000 for the claimant when all listed operating costs are borne from that fee. The firm then absorbs $900 for ownership and encumbrance title work, $1,800 for partner foreclosure attorney filing work, and $1,100 of allocated skip tracing, notary, and dead-lead acquisition expense. Contribution is $8,200, or 68.33% of recognized fee revenue. It is not $8,200 of final owner profit. Office overhead, insurance, software, management time, additional payroll, and subsequent collection adjustments still have to be funded. The model also assumes no separate priority deduction reduces the stated $48,000 recovery.

Define the attorney line precisely in the engagement and internal estimate. In this illustration, $1,800 is the firm's budgeted cost for a permitted counsel-supported file, not a promise that counsel will handle any contested proceeding for that amount. Court charges, publication, additional service, hearings, or an appeal can consume more. If counsel instead contracts directly with the claimant and the claimant pays counsel separately, redraw both the waterfall and claimant net before signing; do not quietly retain the original $36,000 representation. Where rules prohibit the assumed arrangement, the model does not apply. Use a lower permissible operator fee or a different service structure and recalculate contribution before authorizing title work, travel, or paid acquisition against an attractive but unusable headline percentage.

Put actual invoices beside every estimate and record which party owes each charge if the recovery fails. A contingency promise can shift collection uncertainty without making third-party costs disappear. For example, a 10% fee on the same $48,000 produces $4,800; keeping the same $3,800 cost bundle leaves only $1,000 before fixed overhead. A further $1,200 contested-hearing expense turns that contribution negative. This sensitivity explains why a fee cap can change minimum viable file size without justifying pressure on the claimant. Calculate the break-even recovery as expected file costs divided by the permitted fee rate, then add a contribution target and a probability-weighted allowance for failure. Reject files that require undisclosed deductions or an optimistic priority assumption to appear profitable.

## Make dead leads and payment delay visible in the cohort  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

A useful acquisition cohort begins with a dated county list rather than a collection of successful testimonials. Consider 100 listed records yielding 40 verified reachable claimants, 20 signed engagements, 12 supported submissions, and eight paid recoveries. These are planning assumptions, not measured industry conversion rates. If the cohort consumes $8,800 in tracing, notary, and acquisition spending, eight paid files must carry $1,100 each. Dividing by 20 signed clients would report only $440 per engagement and conceal the cost of nonpayment. Keep failure reasons distinct: duplicate record, depleted balance, superior lien, deceased owner without an authorized representative, refused contact, expired claim window, and disputed entitlement. Each reason points to a different operational fix and a different amount of avoidable spend on the next list.

The $1,100 allocation above covers only its named acquisition and execution pool. Failed-file title reports or counsel work require their own allocation unless explicitly included in the original estimates. If the same cohort also spends $2,400 on title work for abandoned files, allocate another $300 to each of eight paid files; contribution falls from $8,200 to $7,900 before fixed overhead. Do not move those invoices into an invisible miscellaneous account. Maintain record counts through discovery, contact, engagement, evidence completion, submission, order, and cleared payment. A signed contract does not erase lien priority, and an order does not necessarily mean available cash. Reconcile every closed record to the custodian's distribution evidence and a cleared bank receipt before reporting a collected-fee conversion rate.

Timing makes a profitable cohort capable of exhausting cash. Use a 90 to 180 day payout assumption for planning, while recognizing contested matters can last longer and uncomplicated matters can finish sooner. Enter expected receipts by month and confidence class, but fund committed costs from existing cash rather than unsigned future contracts. Recalculate cost per paid file only after stating the cohort's observation window; a 60-day-old group cannot be compared fairly with a fully matured annual group. Record pending claims separately instead of labeling them permanent failures to improve later metrics. Conversely, do not count pending balances as recognized fees. Review the oldest unpaid files weekly for missing documents, unresolved priority, defective service, and scheduled hearings, assigning a named person and dated next action to each blockage.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Tag the cohort
   - Assign county and extraction month.
   - Deduplicate parcel, case, claimant, and fund records.
2. Attach expenditure
   - Link each invoice to a file or cohort.
   - Allocate abandoned-file costs without double counting.
3. Close on evidence
   - Reconcile cleared receipts and distributions.
   - Report paid, pending, and failed denominators separately.

## 20-state targeting: price the sale route before the spread  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use the three tiers below as workload categories, not rankings of legal permissiveness or promised margins. Tier 1 identifies administrative entry points and potentially attractive spreads, but Georgia disputes, Virginia judicial sales, and Missouri distribution proceedings can still require counsel and a court order. For each county, open a route card recording the sale instrument, governing statute, present custodian, sale date, confirmation or ratification date, posted balance, outstanding claims, and current official instructions. A tax collector's spreadsheet and a trustee's foreclosure notice are not interchangeable lead sources. California illustrates the distinction: Revenue and Taxation Code section 4675 addresses tax-sale excess proceeds, whereas Civil Code section 2924j addresses trustee-sale surplus. The tax-sale assignment pathway requires attention to the post-90-day execution requirement and statutory disclosures; waiting ninety days does not establish priority or create a universal finder percentage. Obtain the actual assignment requirements before quoting. For an initial experiment, select one sale route in two counties, complete five factual screenings, and compare evidence costs before purchasing a statewide list. Record a zero fee assumption whenever the permissible compensation remains unresolved; that keeps attractive gross balances from becoming fictitious revenue in the acquisition forecast.

Tier 2 budgets for legal procedure before contact volume. Florida foreclosure entitlement under section 45.033 must be separated from tax-deed distribution under section 197.582, which has its own notice, claim, and priority rules. In North Carolina, G.S. 45-21.31 governs surplus disposition within a foreclosure framework that also includes the upset-bid process under G.S. 45-21.27; do not forecast collection from an initial bid before the sale becomes final. Pennsylvania Rule 3136 requires attention to the sheriff's distribution schedule and the exceptions period, rather than a generic county refund application. Ask local counsel to price uncontested work separately from priority disputes, estate administration, service failures, and hearings. Use this verbatim intake question: 'Which sale produced this balance, who holds it today, what event starts the claim deadline, and which document authorizes payment?' Put the answer and its supporting record into the file, not merely into staff recollection. Then model expected fee less research, counsel, service, and failed-file allocation. A judicial file with a large surplus can yield less contribution than an administrative file with a modest balance once delay and contested priority are included.

Tier 3 flags jurisdictions where constitutional changes, strict procedural notices, or narrow compensation rules demand especially precise underwriting. Tyler v. Hennepin County, 598 U.S. 631 (2023), addresses governmental retention of value beyond a tax debt; it does not supply a nationwide claim form, reopen every expired deadline, or authorize any particular recovery fee. Michigan's MCL 211.78t includes a July 1 notice-of-intention cutoff in the foreclosure year for the applicable remaining-proceeds process, followed by additional procedural steps. Texas Tax Code section 34.04 limits attorney compensation for obtaining excess proceeds to the lesser of twenty-five percent or $1,000 and separately imposes requirements on assignments, including execution on or after day 36 after deposit with the clerk. Do not treat that attorney limit as permission for a nonlawyer to charge an equivalent percentage or use an assignment to evade restrictions. Minnesota and Massachusetts require attention to the sale or foreclosure vintage and available post-Tyler remedy. Across every tier, preserve dated statutory text and current custodian instructions before spending materially. Calendar notices separately from motions and final claim deadlines, and replace an unverified deadline with an immediate counsel inquiry rather than an assumed extension.
