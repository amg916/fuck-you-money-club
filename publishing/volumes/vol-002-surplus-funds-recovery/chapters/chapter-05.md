## Executive Summary

A posted surplus is a custody balance, not the former owner's payday. Experienced operators reconstruct the foreclosed interest, rank competing claims, and identify who can authorize a recovery before quoting a fee. This chapter separates mortgage, tax-deed, and association sales; distinguishes federal notice requirements from redemption periods; and explains why bankruptcy can move control to a trustee. The practical objective is a documented residual supported by payoff evidence and a valid claimant, rather than an attractive auction number. Every unresolved priority question changes the forecast before outreach begins.

## The $90,000 celebration that becomes a $5,000 file  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The classic rookie mistake begins with a clerk ledger showing $90,000 remaining after a first mortgage foreclosure. The operator multiplies that number by a proposed contingency percentage and starts celebrating. But the ledger describes money held, not money adjudicated to the former owner. Assume a second mortgage has a verified $55,000 claim, a HELOC has $20,000 outstanding, and a judgment creditor has a valid $10,000 claim against the relevant ownership interest. If all three claims qualify and rank ahead of the owner, only $5,000 remains. A hypothetical twenty percent fee on that residual is $1,000, not $18,000. Additional allowed costs or interest could reduce the residual further, while a satisfied or defective claim could increase it.

Build the distribution waterfall from the foreclosure complaint, final judgment, sale report, certificate of disbursements, recorded mortgages, assignments, modifications, satisfactions, and judgment records. Identify the mortgage actually foreclosed and its priority date; do not infer first position from a lender's familiar name. A junior lien extinguished against the real estate can still support a claim against excess proceeds. That distinction explains why a clean post-sale property interest does not necessarily produce a clean owner recovery. Conversely, a mortgage senior to the foreclosed lien may remain attached to the property rather than consume the same surplus in the assumed sequence. Record the specific disposition of each interest instead of treating every recorded dollar as an automatic deduction.

The priority worksheet needs claimant identity, recording reference, affected parcel or debtor interest, apparent rank, current payoff, participation in the action, and unresolved objections. A recorded face amount is not a current payoff: revolving balances, advances, accrued interest, releases, and settlements change the number. Request a dated payoff or claim statement and reconcile it with the court docket. Do not count one obligation twice because both an original mortgage and its assignment appear in the index. Mark disputed amounts as reserved exposure rather than quietly deleting them. Send the owner a range tied to the evidence, and suspend any firm recovery quotation while a competing claim could exhaust the fund. The owner's willingness to sign does not cure missing priority work.

| Second mortgage | $55,000 | $35,000 |

## Tax deeds and federal interests require separate clocks  `🏷️ STATUTORY_FACT`

A tax deed is not a mortgage foreclosure with a different auctioneer. State law determines which private interests are extinguished, which governmental interests survive, and how proceeds move through the distribution process. In Florida, read section 197.582 for tax-deed disbursement and surplus procedures together with section 197.552 for surviving interests. Governmental liens can receive treatment different from private mortgages, including survival rules for qualifying unpaid governmental liens. A municipal code enforcement balance therefore cannot be dismissed merely because the tax deed issued. Obtain the recorded lien, the municipality's current balance, and the custodian's distribution record. Distinguish amounts already paid from sale proceeds from obligations still attached to the parcel; subtracting both without reconciliation produces a false owner residual.

Federal tax liens add a second legal system. Under 26 U.S.C. section 7425, a qualifying nonjudicial sale may leave a federal tax lien undisturbed when required notice was not supplied. The statutory framework includes the lien filing timing relative to sale, including the thirty day condition, and generally requires notice at least twenty five days before sale in the prescribed manner. These are not the redemption period. Where section 7425 permits discharge, the United States generally has a redemption period of 120 days after sale or the longer period available under local law. Keep the sale date, federal lien filing date, notice delivery evidence, and redemption endpoint as separate fields. A county mailing list alone is not proof that federal notice requirements were satisfied.

Judicial proceedings involving federal liens require their own analysis under 28 U.S.C. section 2410, including proper joinder, service, lien description, and the sale's legal effect. Section 2410 generally provides a one year federal redemption period, but for an internal revenue tax lien the period is 120 days or the longer state period. Do not apply the tax lien exception to every federal lien or import nonjudicial notice rules into a judicial case. Redemption affects the purchaser's title exposure and transaction timing; it is not a universal prohibition on submitting every surplus claim. Route questionable notice, missing federal participation, or conflicting sale orders for case specific legal review. Forecast cash only after separating title uncertainty from the court's actual distribution requirements and competing federal claim.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Three clocks, three records**
> 
> Never label every federal date a 120-day deadline.
> - Record lien filing and applicable notice deadline.
> - Record sale date and applicable redemption period.
> - Record the independent surplus claim deadline.

## Bankruptcy changes who controls the claim  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Search bankruptcy before asking a former owner to authorize collection, and refresh that search before filing or disbursement. Match the debtor using multiple identifiers, not a common name alone. Obtain the petition date, chapter, schedules, trustee appointment, exemption claims, conversion history, and orders affecting the property. Under 11 U.S.C. section 541, a debtor's legal or equitable interest in surplus can become estate property. A prepetition sale with funds still held is not automatically outside the estate merely because the deed has changed hands. Likewise, a postpetition sale requires examination of the automatic stay and any relief order. A county's continued display of the former owner's name does not establish that the owner can assign or receive the money personally.

In an active Chapter 7 case, a nonexempt, unadministered estate surplus claim ordinarily belongs under the trustee's administration rather than a private recovery agreement with the debtor. Send the trustee the sale evidence and proposed calculation, identifying all competing liens and any disputed balance. Do not describe an estate asset as abandoned because the case looks old or the debtor received a discharge. Section 554 distinguishes actual abandonment and scheduled property abandoned at closing from property that remains in the estate. An omitted asset may remain estate property after closure and require further administration. Confirm the operative docket entry and its effect on this particular claim. A signature from the former owner cannot substitute for the trustee's authority over an estate asset.

Chapter 13 requires more nuance than the slogan that every dollar goes to a trustee. Sections 1306 and 1327, the confirmed plan, local law, and specific orders affect estate property, vesting, and distribution. Identify whether the debtor retains possession, whether proceeds must fund the plan, and which actor can prosecute or settle the claim. Never assume that a confirmed plan automatically frees every later surplus payment for direct collection. A recovery company's compensation arrangement also needs to fit the estate's actual authority and any required court treatment; a debtor's preexisting contract does not bind the trustee by magic. Keep the claim deadline visible while authority is resolved so diligence does not destroy a valid recovery through delay. Record the authorized payee before forecasting collectible revenue.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Match the debtor
   - Retrieve the bankruptcy docket.
   - Reconcile identity, petition date, chapter, and sale date.
2. Trace the asset
   - Review schedules and operative orders.
   - Document exemptions, stay relief, vesting, and abandonment.
3. Confirm authority
   - Identify the party controlling the claim.
   - Preserve claim deadlines while trustee or debtor authority is established.

## HOA priority mispricing versus title-curative work  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Consider an association foreclosing a $6,000 lien while a $300,000 first mortgage remains recorded. A buyer who assumes every foreclosure erases every older mortgage may purchase an interest still burdened by that entire first mortgage. The small association debt is not evidence of a small total encumbrance. In many jurisdictions the first mortgage retains priority; limited association superpriority rules elsewhere are highly specific about covered assessments, notice, tender, and foreclosure effect. Even a statute describing a superpriority component does not eliminate the need to examine controlling decisions and the actual proceeding. For surplus operators, this mistake also corrupts outreach: the former owner's residual, the buyer's equity, and debts remaining on title are three different calculations, not one interchangeable opportunity number.

Track 01 play hoa_super_lien_tax_deed_redemption_arbitrage pairs HOA Super-Lien & Sub-Threshold Tax-Deed Statutory Redemption research with a forensic test of apparent mispricing. Treat the play as an investigation framework, not a claim that every state offers a cheap redemption option. A tax debt below some dollar threshold does not create a universal redemption entitlement. Identify the exact statute, eligible redeemer, triggering event, permitted assignment if any, payment formula, and terminal date. Separate a genuine statutory redemption from purchasing a claim, negotiating a release, or seeking a judicial title remedy. For a recovery firm, no acquisition position or redemption promise belongs in the client proposal until the actor's authority and the transaction's priority consequences are supported by the actual records.

When the priority thesis fails, use the Title-Curative Fallback only if a viable service remains: reconstruct missing releases, reconcile duplicate lien entries, locate a recorded satisfaction, or help assemble evidence for counsel handling a disputed interest. Do not present title curative work as a power to erase valid senior debt. The work product is an indexed evidence packet and a clearly bounded unresolved issue, not a fabricated clean title certificate. Compare expected collectible compensation against search expense, specialist fees, and time tied up in the dispute. If the owner residual disappears, archive the surplus prospect even when the property itself attracts speculators. Experienced operators preserve the distinction between a provable claim to held money and a speculative bet on what a buyer acquired.

**Priority-Mispricing Trap vs Title-Curative Fallback**

| Forensic test | Priority-Mispricing Trap | Title-Curative Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HOA sale | Assume $6,000 wipes $300,000 mortgage | Trace priority, service, tender, and surviving debt |
| Small tax balance | Invent a universal redemption threshold | Verify statute, standing, amount, and deadline |
| Missing release | Treat an absent payoff as zero debt | Obtain satisfaction or documented resolution |
| No owner residual | Sell speculative recovery | Close prospect; separate any viable title work |

## Post-Tyler Pre-Sale Notice Traps & Upset-Bid Resets  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Tyler v. Hennepin County, 598 U.S. 631 (2023), recognized a property interest in value exceeding a tax debt; it did not establish one nationwide surplus application or erase every state procedural deadline. A sourcing calendar therefore needs more than a column labeled days after auction. Separate foreclosure, preservation notice, sale finality, custody, and distribution into distinct events, with an evidence reference for each. A familiar post-sale bar date can mislead an operator entering a jurisdiction where the claimant must act before proceeds even exist. Michigan's MCL 211.78t illustrates the difference: under the statutory remaining-proceeds process, a claimant must submit the prescribed notarized Notice of Intention to Claim Interest in Foreclosure Sales Proceeds, Treasury Form 5743, to the foreclosing governmental unit by July 1 immediately following the effective date of foreclosure. This can precede the auction by weeks or months. A workflow that waits for an excess-proceeds spreadsheet can therefore discover an attractive balance only after the statutory preservation deadline has passed. Record the foreclosure judgment's effective date and the correct governmental unit rather than substituting the auction year or sending the form to an unrelated court office.

For that Michigan statutory route, failure to file the timely notarized notice forfeits the ability to claim remaining proceeds through the prescribed process even though the auction may not yet have occurred. Treat this as a standing-preservation task, not the final request for payment. Obtain the current form and instructions, identify the interest held at foreclosure, complete the required notarization, and retain evidence of timely submission using an accepted delivery method. A draft saved in the customer folder, an unsigned attachment, or a promise to send it later is not evidence of filing. Preserve the received copy or acknowledgment with the parcel and foreclosure identifiers. The subsequent notice and court-motion stages under MCL 211.78t require their own calendar; filing Form 5743 does not automatically release money. Older foreclosure cohorts and constitutional litigation may present different remedies, so do not declare every late claimant permanently without recourse solely from this operating rule. Escalate those dated facts for a specific remedy analysis while keeping the ordinary statutory route accurately classified. Tyler is a reason to investigate retained equity, not a substitute for documenting which procedure remains available.

North Carolina presents a different timing trap: the auction price may still be provisional. Under N.C.G.S. 45-21.27, a qualifying upset bid filed with the clerk during the applicable ten-day period starts another ten-day opportunity measured from the latest qualifying upset-bid filing. The statute also addresses the deadline when the final day falls on a weekend, legal holiday, or other day the clerk's office is closed for transactions. Do not calculate finality by adding ten ordinary calendar days to the auction and ignoring the filed sale report, later bids, or office closures. Section 45-21.31 addresses when the parties' rights become fixed if no further upset bid is filed within the applicable period; that milestone still does not prove payment or a deposited owner surplus. Premature outreach based on a $180,000 auction bid can understate or misstate the eventual proceeds when another bidder increases the price, while a failed completion can defeat the assumed deposit altogether. Store every report and bid with its filing date, recompute the next deadline, and label the current amount provisional. Before quoting an available recovery, refresh the docket, establish the closing of the upset-bid period, and reconcile actual receipts and deductions with the custodian. Keep this finality clock separate from the claimant's distribution deadlines.

**Preservation, sale finality, and distribution are different clocks**

| Procedure | Trigger and action | Evidence before quoting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Post-sale claim bar | Apply the governing notice or sale trigger; do not borrow another state's period | Operative notice, rule, and computed filing date |
| Michigan MCL 211.78t | Notarized Form 5743 by July 1 immediately following effective foreclosure; auction may follow | Timely submission to foreclosing governmental unit; later motion calendar |
| North Carolina 45-21.27 | Track sale report and each qualifying upset bid; reset the ten-day period | Latest clerk filing and applicable closure adjustment |
| North Carolina 45-21.31 | Establish expiration without another upset bid, then verify completion | Finality record, actual receipts, and current surplus ledger |
