# Chapter 04: Realistic Startup Requirements

*Working capital for title abstracts, mobile notaries, counsel retainers, and 90–180 day payout cycles.*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

A surplus recovery pilot needs enough capital to establish a valid service, prove claimant entitlement, and wait for distributions without spending claimant money. The $6,500 two-county budget is a tightly bounded experiment, not a funded multi-state company. Established operations may need $10,000 to $60,000 in working capital against $2,500 to $14,000 in monthly operating expense. This chapter separates startup purchases from recurring burn, specifies attorney vetting before client signatures, and defines practical protections for identity documents. Expansion follows documented paid-file economics and adequate runway, not a growing spreadsheet of uncollected balances.

## Buy a bounded two-county experiment, not an imaginary full firm  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The lean pilot allocates exactly $6,500: $1,500 for the LLC, state registration or bonding where applicable, and contract review; $850 for skip-tracing and title ownership-and-encumbrance credits; $1,650 for mobile notaries, certified mail, and court copies; and $2,500 as operating reserve. These are spending ceilings for an illustrative two-county test, not vendor quotes or a claim that every state requires a bond. Obtain current written prices before committing any category. If required bonding or counsel review alone exceeds its allowance, increase committed capital or select a smaller operating scope. Do not solve the mismatch by omitting a required service and pretending the original budget still describes an executable pilot. Record deposits and unused credits separately from expenses actually consumed.

Choose two counties with accessible surplus records and sufficiently similar procedures to make the first test interpretable. Similar geography does not establish identical rules: confirm whether each list covers tax sales, judicial foreclosure, or another proceeding. Request the official claim form, supporting-document list, distribution ledger, and contact for procedural questions. A clerk can explain submission mechanics but should not be treated as counsel for fee contracts or disputed entitlement. Start with a small batch of documented, uncontested ownership situations rather than deceased owners with unresolved estates or properties with several competing lienholders. That scope limits early legal spend and reveals whether the basic acquisition, identity verification, document assembly, and follow-up process can work before complexity obscures the results.

The $850 credit pool cannot fund unlimited title abstracts. If a file needs the $900 title-and-lien package used in the prior chapter's example, that single purchase already exceeds this category. The pilot therefore needs cheaper preliminary screening, fewer full reports, or an explicitly revised budget; preliminary screening is not a substitute for the evidence required to distribute funds. Similarly, the $1,650 execution allowance is not spare acquisition money merely because notarization looks inexpensive on a website. Travel, failed appointments, certified-copy requests, and repeated mailings consume it quickly. Track committed costs before invoices arrive. Spend against a named record with a documented next step, and keep the reserve intact until its release is justified by a current cash forecast.

## Match the multi-state stack to the collection calendar  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

An established multi-state operation may carry $10,000 to $60,000 in working capital with monthly operating expense of $2,500 to $14,000. These ranges describe different operating configurations, not interchangeable endpoints. The stack must cover recurring software and communications, title orders, approved counsel retainers, case execution, insurance, contractors, and any payroll commitments. Distinguish refundable attorney trust deposits from earned legal expense and distinguish prepaid data credits from cash available to pay wages. An assignment business also needs purchase consideration beyond this service-operation budget. List each jurisdiction's incremental setup and maintenance costs separately so adding a state cannot quietly consume the funds assigned to existing claimants. Count only cash the business owns as available runway; claimant proceeds and disputed fee balances belong outside that calculation.

The runway calculation exposes mismatched budgets immediately. At $2,500 monthly burn, $10,000 buys four months before new receipts, while $60,000 buys 24 months if spending stays flat. At $14,000 monthly burn, the same balances buy approximately 0.71 months and 4.29 months. Six months at the high burn requires $84,000 before exceptional case expenses, already above the stated capital range. A 90 to 180 day planning collection cycle therefore does not fit every combination of these numbers. Either lower committed burn, secure additional actual funding, or stagger intake so earlier supported collections can replenish later cohorts. Do not present a large pipeline as financing: an anticipated fee cannot pay a notary invoice due tomorrow unless cash is already available.

Build a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast plus a monthly view extending beyond six months. Show opening unrestricted cash, invoices due, retainer replenishments, insurance payments, contractor commitments, and scenario-based cleared receipts. Keep a zero-new-collections case alongside the expected case; this reveals whether a single delayed order would prevent existing files from being serviced. Include the owner's living-cost requirement explicitly, either as funded personal runway outside the business or as a modeled withdrawal. A low expense estimate that assumes unpaid owner labor can be useful for a pilot, but it cannot also claim to fund market-rate full-time employees. Update the forecast when a hearing is rescheduled, a claim becomes contested, or a new county requires expensive authenticated copies rather than accepting ordinary electronic records.

## Vet local surplus counsel before signing the first client  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Before signing a single client, speak with attorneys who actually handle the relevant local surplus procedure. Verify active licensure and ask which sale types, counties, and claimant situations they cover. Request a walkthrough using a redacted sample docket: identify the fund holder, required entitlement evidence, likely competing claimants, service requirements, hearing practice, and expected cost escalation. Ask who the attorney represents, who can give instructions, and how conflicts between the finder, purchaser, and claimant are handled. A generic real-estate practice description is not evidence of experience recovering this type of surplus. Keep at least one alternate qualified contact for capacity or conflict problems, but do not send claimant identity documents to several firms merely to solicit an estimate.

Ask counsel to review the proposed compensation agreement and any assignment against the actual statute before the first signature. Florida section 45.033 concerns foreclosure surplus entitlement and voluntary transfers, including conditions affecting transfer-based recovery; it should not be substituted for the rules governing a Florida tax-deed surplus. California Civil Code section 2924j addresses distribution of surplus proceeds after a nonjudicial trustee sale, including notice and claim procedures and court involvement when distribution cannot be resolved. A California trustee-sale file should not be sold as a routine county administrative tax-overage claim. These distinctions determine document requests, fee assumptions, and whether a client needs immediate legal representation. Preserve the reviewed form version and the attorney's written scope so staff do not reuse it for an unrelated process.

Get a written engagement or service schedule that explains retainers, replenishment thresholds, court costs, contested work, and termination. Separate the claimant's legal engagement from the firm's permitted research services, and have counsel address any proposed referral compensation or escrow disbursement; simply calling a payment a marketing expense does not resolve professional fee-sharing restrictions. Set a response expectation for urgent deadlines and identify who monitors the docket when an assigned employee is absent. Before accepting intake, test the handoff with an anonymized sample file containing sale date, case number, available funds, deed history, liens, and missing evidence. If counsel cannot identify the required next step or the firm cannot afford the minimum retainer, postpone that file category rather than signing clients whose submissions cannot be completed.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Classify jurisdiction
   - Identify sale type and fund holder.
   - Collect the official claim packet and relevant statute.
2. Vet counsel
   - Verify license, scope, conflicts, and local experience.
   - Use redacted records and obtain written pricing.
3. Test the handoff
   - Run an anonymized sample before client intake.
   - Confirm document ownership, deadlines, and permissible payment arrangements.
4. Fund execution
   - Reserve the quoted file costs.
   - Do not count expected claimant proceeds as available operating cash.

## Protect claimant records and stop the pilot before cash disappears  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Claimant SSNs, identity documents, probate records, signatures, and bank instructions create practical security obligations from the first file. Collect only information required for the next documented step; use truncated identifiers in the lead tracker and keep full identity evidence in a separate access-controlled repository. Require multifactor authentication, encrypted devices, individual accounts, and secure document transfer rather than ordinary email attachments. Contractors should see only assigned files, with access removed when the engagement ends. Independently verify any changed payment instructions through a previously established contact channel, not the phone number supplied in the change request. Preserve a record of who accessed sensitive documents, avoid uploading them into public lookup tools, and test recovery from an encrypted backup before relying on it during a deadline.

Obtain errors-and-omissions and cyber insurance quotes describing the actual business activities, including any assignment purchases, funds handling, contractor use, and identity-document storage. Ask about exclusions for title work, legal services, social engineering, fraudulent transfers, and prior acts; a policy name alone does not establish coverage. Compare deductibles and response costs with the $2,500 pilot reserve, and add the selected premium to the forecast rather than silently assuming it fits an unrelated category. Establish a short incident procedure: restrict compromised access, preserve logs, contact the insurer and qualified responders, and determine notification duties with counsel. Define record retention by claim requirements and applicable law, retain evidence needed for disputes, and securely remove unnecessary copies instead of allowing every contractor to keep a permanent claimant archive.

Set pilot stop conditions before buying a second list. Pause new acquisition when required counsel is unavailable, the lawful fee cannot cover expected file costs, identity or entitlement remains unsupported, or remaining unrestricted cash cannot cover committed execution and the next eight weeks of forecast burn. These are specific operating limits, not reasons to abandon existing clients: preserve deadlines, communicate status, and arrange a documented handoff where needed. Review the experiment after the first bounded cohort and again when its payment window matures. Report dollars spent, supported submissions, cleared receipts, pending claims, and actual reasons for failure. Expand only when observed collections support the next batch's budget, while retaining separate funding for existing obligations. If no receipts arrive by day 180, reassess timing and capital before committing another acquisition dollar.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Pause intake, preserve existing obligations**
> 
> A stopped pilot still owes careful handling of active files and sensitive records.
> - No qualified counsel for the required proceeding: stop that file type.
> - Unfunded committed costs or eight-week cash shortfall: pause new acquisition.
> - Unverified identity or unsupported entitlement: do not submit.
> - Day 180 without receipts: rebuild the forecast before spending more.
