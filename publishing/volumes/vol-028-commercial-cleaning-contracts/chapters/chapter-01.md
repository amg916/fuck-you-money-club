## 1. Define the service obligation before counting the revenue  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Commercial cleaning contracts exchange scheduled labor, supplies, equipment, and supervision for a recurring business payment. The customer buys an agreed condition of designated spaces, not an unlimited promise that everything will always look new. A workable scope identifies cleanable square footage, room types, frequencies, access windows, consumable responsibility, inspection criteria, and excluded restoration work. Nightly trash removal, restroom sanitation, and traffic-lane vacuuming belong in the recurring specification; stripping floors, cleaning construction residue, and emergency spill response require separate pricing unless expressly included. ISSA cleaning-time resources provide a starting vocabulary for estimating tasks, but the operator must validate production inside the actual building. Badge delays, occupied rooms, elevator queues, and furniture density can defeat an attractive square-foot estimate. The five archetypes below share this contract discipline while differing in access friction, training, equipment, and purchasing cycles. Candidate #10 earns its 82/100 score because repeat demand is real but delivery remains labor intensive. Scheduling software can remove dispatch work; it cannot empty another wastebasket. A defensible offer therefore specifies who cleans, what they clean, how completion is checked, and which changes create additional charges before the first crew enters.

The revenue boundary follows the operator's actual promise. A cleaning company that controls the service before delivery generally records the customer consideration as gross service revenue and records wages, supplies, and subcontract delivery as costs. Hiring another business does not automatically convert a principal into an agent. Conversely, a coordinator that only arranges another provider's service generally records its earned coordination fee, not the entire provider invoice. Apply the principal-versus-agent analysis to the contract and facts under ASC 606 rather than choosing whichever presentation looks larger. A twelve-month, $36,000 stand-ready cleaning arrangement might produce $3,000 monthly revenue when performance is even and that pattern faithfully represents delivery. Advance collections remain a contract liability until earned; an annual signature does not create immediate annual revenue. Variable service credits reduce expected consideration when the recognition requirements apply. Customer rent rolls, building valuations, medical collections, and procurement ceilings never become cleaner revenue merely because the cleaner serves that customer. Separate revenue, invoices, receivables, and bank receipts in QuickBooks Online. Reconcile each to completed service periods and approved extras so cash timing cannot conceal poor contract economics.

The first chapter operating play is dense office routes: cluster repeatable sites so paid travel does not swallow productive hours. Consider 20,000 cleanable square feet at an illustrative 4,000 square feet per productive hour. Five production hours plus one paid hour for setup, transfers, and closeout produces six hours per visit. At three visits weekly for 52 weeks, the account consumes 936 paid hours. A $23 loaded hourly cost produces $21,528 of labor against $36,000 annual revenue. Allocate another $10,152 to supplies, equipment, supervision, insurance, selling, and administration, leaving $4,320, or 12%, under these stated assumptions. This account example illustrates bidding mechanics rather than replacing the canonical company scenarios. At $26 loaded hourly cost, unchanged scope adds $2,808 of cost and reduces the remainder to $1,512. The fallback is a documented scope revision, contractual repricing, or orderly nonrenewal, not unpaid work. Verify whether the production benchmark already includes restrooms and detail tasks before adding allowances. The moat is a measured route with backup access and reliable coverage, not a spreadsheet that assumes every building is empty, unfurnished, and next door.

## 2. Choose sub-verticals by delivery friction, not headline contract size  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The second chapter operating play is medical zoning contracts. Separate reception and administrative cleaning from treatment-room environmental cleaning, blood-spill response, instrument processing, and regulated waste handling. Do not sell those categories as interchangeable. A facility-approved room map should identify surfaces, products, frequencies, restricted areas, and escalation contacts. Where employees have occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials, 29 CFR 1910.1030 creates concrete duties including an exposure control plan, training, appropriate protective equipment, hepatitis B vaccination provisions, and post-exposure procedures. An illustrative 2,000-square-foot treatment-area scope at 1,000 square feet per hour takes two productive hours per visit. Five weekly visits over 52 weeks require 520 hours before additional allowances. At $26 loaded hourly cost, that component costs $13,520. A $24,000 contract allocating another $7,600 to remaining delivery and overhead costs leaves $2,880, or 12%. Validate those allowances through observed shifts rather than presenting them as medical industry averages. If required dwell times, exposure precautions, or occupied-room delays break the estimate, narrow the service boundary or reprice before launch. Stop an untrained crew from improvising a response to sharps or uncontrolled biological contamination.

The comparison matrix pairs real products with purchasing and delivery mechanics; it does not imply that a software subscription creates customers or certifies clinical competence. Swept can support janitorial communication and inspections; Aspire can support estimating and job management; ServiceChannel can route work orders for participating facilities. Obtain current vendor quotes instead of hard-coding subscription prices that depend on package, seats, or customer agreements. Customer fees should reflect the service: monthly recurring charges for stable specifications, per-visit charges for variable schedules, and separately authorized project prices for restoration. Distinguish a service credit from a clawback. A contractual credit reduces the amount earned for a missed standard; a refund returns cash already collected; a disputed invoice may remain unpaid while evidence is reviewed. Specify notice, inspection, cure periods, evidence, and deduction limits instead of permitting unlimited unilateral offsets. A photograph should show the relevant surface and timestamp without exposing patient records or confidential customer material. Get customer permission for inspection capture, restrict access, and retain only what the service dispute process requires. Better evidence protects both parties; indiscriminate photography creates another operating problem rather than stronger proof of delivery.

The underlying cleaning contract is not that coordinator's sales. This example is separate from the canonical cleaning-company cases. Eligibility alone does not satisfy performance limitations. Review FAR 52.203-5 where applicable before using award-contingent compensation. Municipal programs require their own ordinance and solicitation analysis. The fallback is to withdraw noncompliant bids and operate as a disclosed subcontractor on unrestricted work.

## 3. Reconcile the scorecard to paid hours and supervisor capacity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

The canonical Conservative scenario is $250,000 annual gross revenue at an 8% net margin, producing $20,000 annual owner net profit with 5 FT plus 4 C. Base is $725,000 at 12.0%, producing $87,000 with the same stated roster. Scaled is $1,200,000 at 16%, producing $192,000 with 6 FT plus 5 C, or eleven people. The implied annual expense envelopes are $230,000, $638,000, and $1,008,000 respectively. These are scorecard targets, not evidence that the listed staff can be funded at every wage rate or workload. FT denotes full-time people and C denotes contractors; neither label supplies actual hours, pay, geography, or owner compensation treatment. Five full-time employees at 2,080 paid hours each and $23 loaded hourly cost already cost $239,200 before contractors or other expenses. That exceeds the Conservative total expense envelope. Preserve the canonical numbers while explicitly flagging this unresolved staffing feasibility problem; do not quietly relabel full-time workers as part-time or omit wages. Build a local wage-and-hours schedule before treating any scenario as executable. The source figures alone cannot demonstrate that the Conservative staffing configuration supports its stated profit.

The third chapter operating play is supervisor pods: assign a working lead to a compact group of accounts, cross-train relief coverage, and reserve paid inspection time. Suppose three cleaners each work an eight-hour shift containing seven productive hours and one hour of paid transfers, setup, and closeout. That supplies 21 productive hours from 24 paid hours. A route demanding 24 productive hours therefore does not fit; add paid capacity, improve the physical process, or remove scope rather than pressuring people to work off the clock. At an illustrative $23 loaded hourly cost, those 24 paid hours cost $552 before any separately scheduled lead time. The fallback for an absence is a trained relief assignment with measured travel and lawful overtime, followed by customer notice when the promised window cannot be met. A pod becomes a moat when the lead catches recurring defects, coaches technique, and maintains access knowledge across workers. It becomes overhead when supervision means driving between scattered buildings to photograph already obvious failures. Track productive hours, total paid hours, rework hours, complaints, and supervisor load by route. Never count automation savings as removed cleaning labor without observing the changed task.

Working capital determines whether a profitable contract survives its first billing cycle. The supplied startup capital range is $10,000 to $50,000, monthly operating outlay range is $3,000 to $14,000, and time to first deal is one to four months. Those outlay figures cannot represent total steady-state company expenses for every canonical case: the implied average monthly expenses are about $19,167 Conservative, $53,167 Base, and $84,000 Scaled. Treat the supplied range as requiring clarification about launch stage and included cost categories, not as a substitute for payroll budgeting. At a hypothetical $40,000 monthly cash operating cost, a 45-day collection cycle exposes roughly $60,000 before considering billing delays, deposits, taxes, or seasonal timing. Use a thirteen-week cash forecast with payroll dates, customer invoice acceptance dates, equipment payments, and overdue balances. QuickBooks Online can track receivables; Deputy or a comparable scheduling system can support shift planning, subject to configuration and privacy controls. Neither replaces a supervisor's confirmation that a site is staffed. Customer concentration deserves its own stress test: remove the largest account's contribution, retain unavoidable costs, and identify the cash runway before committing to another vehicle or permanent supervisory hire.

## 4. Build the operating system that survives turnover and disputes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Labor discipline starts with the actual employment relationship. A worker does not become an independent business because the operator requests an invoice or signs a contractor form. Analyze federal wage-law status, payroll-tax treatment, and applicable state tests separately; they need not use identical standards. California Labor Code section 2775 establishes an ABC framework subject to statutory exceptions and other conditions, making casual substitution of contractors for core cleaning employees especially risky. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, covered nonexempt employees generally receive overtime after forty hours in a workweek; state rules can require more. Capture required setup, travel between jobs during the workday, closing duties, and required training rather than recording only time inside the customer's suite. Use a correction process that lets workers report missed time without retaliation. For genuine subcontract businesses, verify their service responsibility, staffing control, insurance, invoicing, and classification facts without assuming those documents settle every legal test. On covered federal service contracts, the Service Contract Labor Standards statute and incorporated wage determination can impose prevailing wages and fringe benefits. A cheap subcontract quote does not erase the prime's applicable duties or make an underpriced award profitable.

Chemical safety is an operating specification, not a binder purchased after an incident. Under 29 CFR 1910.1200, implement the applicable hazard communication program, maintain accessible safety data sheets, label workplace containers as required, and train employees on relevant hazards and protective measures. Keep products in compatible storage and prohibit improvised mixing, especially bleach with acids or ammonia-containing products. Select gloves and other protection through the actual exposure assessment rather than buying one generic kit for every task. For EPA-registered disinfectants, match the product label to the intended surface, use site, dilution, and contact time; FIFRA makes inconsistent use unlawful subject to its statutory exceptions. Follow required precleaning instructions and keep the surface wet for the specified duration. Ordinary janitorial insurance may exclude some pollution, biological exposure, or specialized restoration activities, so check the actual policy and endorsements before adding services. Certificates summarize coverage but do not rewrite policy terms. Workers' compensation obligations depend on jurisdiction and employment facts. Record an incident's time, location, products, actions, and notifications without speculating about fault. Safe task boundaries protect workers while preventing a routine account from becoming an uninsured specialty operation.

A durable small-team moat is the accumulated ability to deliver the same agreed outcome when the usual cleaner is absent, the buyer changes, or a complaint arrives. Store site instructions, access contacts, product assignments, and approved scope changes in a controlled operating record rather than one person's phone. Inspect a rotating sample of rooms against observable standards and record both defects and successful corrections. Tie recurring defects to training, tools, time allowances, or scope ambiguity instead of repeatedly telling crews to be more careful. Invoice from the contract schedule and authorized extras, attach only the evidence the buyer needs, and investigate deductions while records are fresh. The pre-flight checks below convert that discipline into a repeatable mobilization process. Scheduling, inspection logs, and billing are the principal automation levers; use them to shorten administrative loops while preserving accountable human supervision. Reject beginner hype that promises passive income from selling work to the cheapest available crew. Renewals depend on service continuity, accurate wages, safe chemicals, and credible correction of failures. Grow only when the next signed account has a priced labor plan, funded payroll, and a named person responsible for delivery.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Survey and establish the task inventory
   - Walk the actual service window with the customer's authorized site contact.
   - Measure cleanable areas, count fixtures, identify restricted tasks, test access, and record inherited surface damage with permission.
2. Build the paid-hour bid
   - Translate tasks into validated production time and all additional paid time.
   - Apply local loaded wages, applicable public-contract requirements, supervision, supplies, equipment, insurance, and overhead before setting price.
3. Mobilize the crew and relief plan
   - Assign trained workers, a responsible lead, and an operational backup.
   - Test keys, review site instructions, stage labeled products, and observe initial shifts against the estimated task durations.
4. Close the service and billing loop
   - Reconcile completed work, inspection defects, paid time, and earned invoices.
   - Correct defects promptly, document authorized extras, investigate deductions, and update the next payroll and cash forecast.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify the account before promising its start date**
> 
> Each check needs an accountable owner and retained evidence. A signed sales proposal alone does not establish delivery readiness.
> - Confirm measured cleanable area, room-level tasks, service windows, exclusions, and authorized change-order contacts against the signed scope.
> - Reconcile paid hours, applicable wages, overtime, relief coverage, and contractor facts to the account's actual cost envelope.
> - Verify site-specific chemical instructions, exposure procedures, access permissions, and applicable insurance terms before assigning workers.
> - Confirm billing acceptance requirements and funded payroll; for public work, verify eligibility, wage determinations, and applicable subcontract limitations.
