## 1. Build a Source Register Around Four Revenue Decisions  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Organize the desk around four mapped plays, not four disconnected lead lists. Play one builds recurring commercial route density by finding offices, managed buildings, and nearby replacement accounts. Play two qualifies medical janitorial sites whose room mix, exposure controls, and service windows justify differentiated pricing. Play three captures public-sector rebids before the walkthrough deadline, using actual solicitations and award history rather than purchased intent scores. Play four protects supervisor-pod retention and cash collection by connecting inspections, corrective work, approved scope changes, and invoices. Give every record one primary play and one next decision: verify occupancy, identify the buyer, inspect the site, or resolve a delivery exception. For the 1 FT + 3 C planning envelope, assign a designated employee an intake block and a supervisor an evidence-review block; these are duties within existing capacity, not two added hires. Contractor participation depends on actual agreements and classification, not the database label. Limit prospecting to service windows and geographic clusters the pod can cover. A thousand distant addresses cannot compensate for missing evening coverage, and a rich contact record cannot rescue a contract priced below its paid labor requirement.

Treat the following directory as a source-selection register rather than a promise that every publisher offers a stable API. Official records are usually free to inspect, but retrieval engineering, member resources, exports, and commercial lookups have separate costs. Preserve the source URL, retrieval time, publisher's effective date, query parameters, and evidence category with every result. Publisher latency and your polling interval are different: checking an award daily does not make a delayed award submission current. For each connector, record the live account quota and service terms before activation; where no universal numeric limit is published, use serialized requests, caching, and bounded retries rather than inventing one. Respect Retry-After responses and stop retrying authentication failures. The New York sources illustrate exact municipal access routes; substitute the corresponding local assessor, permitting authority, and procurement office when operating elsewhere. Parcel ownership, permitted construction, and provider enrollment are discovery evidence, not confirmation that a particular buyer controls cleaning procurement. Save the specific record that motivated outreach, then verify occupancy and responsibility directly. This prevents the desk from contacting a landlord about a tenant-managed suite or treating a provider's mailing address as a medical facility.

For public work, start with SAM.gov opportunity notices filtered by place of performance and NAICS 561720, then inspect the scope instead of assuming the classification is complete. Capture notice identifiers, amendments, site-visit instructions, response deadlines, contract periods, and incorporated wage determinations. USAspending award amounts help describe purchasing history, but obligations, options, modifications, and recognized contractor revenue are different measures. Calculate a possible rebid window from the actual period of performance and option structure; confirm it with current procurement documents. State and municipal contracts often appear only on local portals. Medical discovery follows a different evidence chain: CMS facility records establish a regulated provider identity, NPPES helps reconcile organization names and addresses, and the facility confirms which locations and rooms need janitorial service. Neither registry proves that a listed clinician buys cleaning. For private commercial routes, parcel and building records establish location context while property management websites identify likely operators. Keep historical ownership separate from current management. Across all three acquisition plays, a walkthrough remains the decisive source for cleanable area, access constraints, consumables responsibility, and attainable production rates; public data earns the visit rather than replacing it.

## 2. Run an Evidence-Preserving Enrichment Waterfall  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Create a small relational model with separate organization, site, contact, opportunity, contract, and evidence tables. A company can operate several sites, a building can house several companies, and one property manager can represent several owners. Collapsing these relationships into a single spreadsheet row causes duplicate outreach and scope errors. Use an internal site identifier as the durable key, with parcel identifiers, NPI organization identifiers, agency notice numbers, and accounting customer identifiers stored as source references. Normalize street suffixes and suite formatting, but preserve the original address for review. Match exact source identifiers first, verified address and domain combinations second, and fuzzy names only into a review queue. Never merge two medical practices solely because they share a street address. Store contact roles explicitly: owner, property manager, practice administrator, procurement officer, or facility supervisor. A source assertion should include its evidence URL, observed value, retrieval time, and verification state. Prefer field-level confidence over one opaque lead score, because a verified building address can coexist with an unverified buyer. The supervisor needs to know which assumption still requires a call before committing scarce walkthrough time.

Run enrichment in a fixed economic order. First obtain free official identity and location evidence. Second inspect the organization's own website for current occupancy, a business domain, service hours, and a published purchasing contact. Third use paid location or domain discovery only when a required field remains unresolved. Fourth verify the chosen business contact and suppress duplicates, previous objections, and invalid addresses before outreach. A paid verification result describes delivery risk; it does not prove that the recipient manages cleaning or wants a solicitation. Commercial email must satisfy 15 U.S.C. 7704, including accurate routing information, required identification and address information, and a functioning opt-out process. Record suppression centrally so another employee cannot restart the sequence from a fresh export. For calls and texts, review the applicable requirements of 47 U.S.C. 227 and the actual dialing method rather than treating a public number as blanket permission. Keep outreach centered on the facility's operating problem: missed inspections, inconsistent coverage, or a coming procurement cycle. Do not collect patient information or individual health details to personalize medical janitorial prospecting; facility-level operational evidence is sufficient for this business.

Implement the first pipeline with Python's sqlite3, csv, json, urllib.request, and logging modules, plus scheduled execution appropriate to the operator's workstation or approved server. A small desk does not need a distributed event platform to manage several hundred qualified buildings. Keep source adapters separate from matching rules so a changed municipal column does not corrupt the contract ledger. Validate required columns and data types before promotion, quarantine malformed rows, and preserve a checksum of each permitted raw download. Keep credentials outside source files and exclude them from logs. Set explicit connection timeouts, bounded exponential backoff with jitter, and a daily spend ceiling for paid services. Log the request category and source identifier without retaining unnecessary personal fields. Review a sample of accepted matches and every suspected cross-organization merge. Measure qualified walkthroughs per enrichment dollar, not records processed per minute. Within the four-person planning envelope, batch unresolved records into one daily review queue and stop collection when the pod's available walkthrough slots are filled. That capacity rule keeps automation from creating a second administrative workload while field employees are already covering absence, travel, inspection, and corrective service.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Select one serviceable cluster
   - Filter one territory by travel time, service windows, and available crew capacity.
   - Tag every candidate with one of the four plays and retain only records that could produce a feasible next action.
2. Resolve site and buyer separately
   - Match official identifiers and service addresses before researching purchasing contacts.
   - Preserve source evidence, review ambiguous suites, and confirm whether landlord, tenant, agency, or administrator controls the scope.
3. Spend only on unresolved decisions
   - Use paid enrichment after free official records and the organization's own website.
   - Apply current account quotas, field restrictions, suppression rules, and a daily budget before sending any lookup or outreach batch.
4. Hand off a bounded walkthrough queue
   - Give the supervisor verified sites, buyer roles, evidence links, and remaining scope questions.
   - Limit scheduled visits to available capacity and feed measured labor assumptions back into qualification and pricing.

## 3. Turn Records Into Paid-Hour Bids and Verifiable Operating Requirements  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Convert site intelligence into a room-and-task workload before entering a monthly price. Public gross building area is not cleanable contract area, and an ISSA reference rate is not a universal promise of field productivity. Separate offices, corridors, restrooms, exam rooms, procedure spaces, floor types, fixtures, and service frequencies. Add setup, security access, equipment movement, waste handling, travel that must be paid, inspection, and expected corrective work. As an illustrative site calculation, 20,000 cleanable square feet divided by a locally tested blended rate of 4,000 square feet per productive hour produces five productive hours per visit. Adding one paid hour for access, setup, and inspection yields six paid hours. At 260 annual visits, that is 1,560 paid hours. A hypothetical fully loaded labor cost of $24 per hour produces $37,440 in annual labor cost; adding $7,560 for supplies, supervision allocation, equipment, and other assigned costs produces $45,000 total cost. At a $54,000 annual price, contribution after those assigned costs is $9,000, or 16.67%. This is a site-level illustration, not the portfolio's owner profit; unallocated overhead and actual performance still determine the final result.

Keep the canonical portfolio cases separate from the illustrative site calculation. Conservative recognizes $250,000 in cleaning contract revenue and $20,000 owner net profit at 8%, leaving $230,000 for all costs. Base recognizes $725,000 and $87,000 at 12.0%, leaving $638,000 for all costs. Scaled recognizes $1,200,000 and $192,000 at 16%, leaving $1,008,000 for all costs. Customer property values, hospital billings, procurement ceilings, and underlying agency spending never enter recognized operator revenue. Conservative and Base retain the specified 1 FT + 3 C roster labels; Scaled uses 2 FT + 4 C, or eleven people. These labels do not supply wage rates, contractor utilization, or scheduled hours. In particular, the Conservative cost envelope requires a direct feasibility check before anyone assumes five employees can be fully funded for a year at local loaded wages alongside four contractors and operating overhead. Do not silently convert the listed full-time roles into part-time labor to make arithmetic fit. Report any unsupported capacity assumption, then retain the canonical figures as the comparison benchmark. Scheduling, inspection logs, and billing reduce leakage; they do not make paid cleaning hours disappear.

Attach operating requirements to the bid evidence rather than burying them in generic policy documents. The Fair Labor Standards Act provisions at 29 U.S.C. 206 and 207 establish federal minimum-wage and overtime requirements, while state and local rules may impose higher obligations. Employee records under 29 CFR 516.2 must support actual pay calculations. For covered federal service contracts, examine 41 U.S.C. 6702 and 6703, the solicitation's wage determination, fringe benefits, and incorporated clauses; do not assume ordinary commercial wage estimates satisfy that scope. A contractor agreement alone does not settle worker classification. Record the applicable federal and state analysis, actual supervision arrangements, and insurance evidence for genuine subcontractors. For chemicals, 29 CFR 1910.1200 requires applicable hazard communication controls, including labels, safety data sheets, and training. Where occupational exposure exists, assess 29 CFR 1910.1030 requirements before medical work begins. EPA List N addresses a particular pathogen context; the registered product label and facility protocol determine whether the product fits the required task. Capture dilution, contact time, surface compatibility, PPE, and training in inspection design. Those details change achievable production rates and therefore belong in the price model.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four Checks Before a Prospect Becomes a Delivery Commitment**
> 
> The supervisor and pricing owner must resolve these four checks before promising price, scope, or a service start date.
> - Verify cleanable area, room mix, keys, alarms, service windows, consumables ownership, and exclusions through a documented walkthrough.
> - Verify required paid hours, local loaded wages, applicable overtime, covered-contract benefits, and roster capacity against the full cost envelope.
> - Verify worker arrangements, insurance evidence, chemical labels, safety data sheets, and exposure-specific training for the actual assigned work.
> - Verify signed scope, authorized buyer, inspection acceptance method, billing identifiers, payment terms, and the route for documenting changed work.

## 4. Close the Loop From Inspection Evidence to Collected Cash  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use one contract identifier to join the schedule, inspection record, corrective ticket, approved change, invoice, and receipt. Swept or another verified janitorial platform can organize field execution, while QuickBooks Online remains the accounting ledger when that is the chosen system. Confirm export rights and integration capabilities in the purchased plan instead of designing around an assumed API. A completed shift is not automatically an accepted inspection, and an accepted inspection is not automatically recognized revenue or collected cash. Preserve those states separately. Capture scheduled and actual paid hours, site identifier, checklist version, defect category, correction owner, client acceptance where required, and the linked billing period. Keep medical inspection records focused on work surfaces and service outcomes; prohibit patient identifiers in notes and avoid photographs containing charts, screens, or people. Restrict employee contact and location information to operational need. When offline submissions arrive late, record both service time and synchronization time. Otherwise the supervisor may misread a transmission delay as a missed visit, dispatch duplicate labor, and damage the very margin the tooling was purchased to protect. Route every exception to a named duty within the existing pod.

Build diagnostics that answer operational questions, not just whether a scheduled job returned a successful exit code. A source freshness monitor should compare retrieval age with the publisher's release cadence. A schema monitor should detect missing identifiers and changed field types. A matching monitor should report duplicate sites and unresolved suite conflicts. A labor monitor should compare estimated paid hours with actual paid hours by task and account. A billing monitor should identify completed eligible service that has no invoice, invoices without supporting scope, and receipts not matched to the ledger. Test the pipeline with saved, permitted fixtures, including a repeated webhook, an expired credential, a partial export, a changed address, and a delayed inspection upload. For write operations, record an internal operation identifier and reconcile destination state before retrying an ambiguous timeout; blind retries can create duplicate invoices. Use unittest for matching and arithmetic checks, sqlite3 integrity checks for local storage, and structured logs for connector failures. Back up the database and configuration separately from credentials, then perform a restore drill. A backup that nobody has restored is weak evidence that Monday's routes and receivables can survive a failure.

Review the four plays through one weekly exception meeting. For commercial route density, inspect qualified walkthroughs, paid travel, and the number of viable replacement accounts inside each service cluster. For medical specialization, review room-specific labor variance, inspection defects, and required training completion. For public-sector rebids, review approaching deadlines, unresolved amendments, and wage-sensitive estimate changes. For supervisor-pod retention and billing control, review repeat defects, unbilled eligible work, invoice disputes, aging receivables, and customer concentration. Keep software and enrichment spending inside the canonical total cost envelopes rather than subtracting it after announcing owner profit. Record the hours consumed by administration and supervision alongside field hours; moving a supervisor into data cleanup creates a coverage cost even when no additional subscription appears. Expand collection only after the current queue produces executable visits and profitable service decisions. Expand delivery only after verified crew capacity, not after a dashboard fills with leads. The Scaled case explicitly adds a sixth full-time role and a fifth contractor, so its larger revenue should never be described as the same four-person pod doing unlimited extra work. The information edge is earlier, better decisions about paid labor and retained accounts.
