## 1. Mine four demand signals inside a serviceable route  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with a route, not a purchased national contact file. Draw a service polygon around the existing supervisor's evening travel pattern, then enumerate offices, outpatient practices, and multi-tenant properties inside it. The four mapped plays are P1 new-occupancy offices, P2 outpatient medical suites, P3 property-manager clusters, and P4 incumbent-service recovery. Each needs a different evidence trail and the same final test: can this address support profitable, repeatable work during an available crew window? For P1, inspect municipal permit portals, planning agendas, certificates of occupancy where publicly available, and broker move-in announcements. A tenant-improvement permit is an investigation trigger, not proof that the tenant has possession or controls janitorial purchasing. Match the address to the assessor's parcel record, property website, and business website; distinguish the owner, manager, tenant, and facilities buyer. Record the source URL, observation date, likely opening window, usable contact route, and reason the prospect fits the route. Exclude demolition sites, hazardous remediation, and construction cleanup unless those services are separately equipped and priced. The asset being sourced is contracted service demand with manageable travel, not the building's market value.

For P2, combine CMS Care Compare, state facility-license searches, and practice expansion announcements to identify operating medical locations. These sources have different coverage; a directory entry does not establish a cleaning need, and an NPI mailing address may not be the service site. Call the practice's public office number to verify location, purchasing responsibility, cleaning frequency, and whether the landlord handles common areas only. Ask about room turnover boundaries, sharps handling exclusions, disinfectant contact times, and access after the final appointment. For P3, mine manager websites, leasing brochures, and public ownership records for adjacent properties sharing a purchasing contact. Offer one accountable supervisor and property-specific inspection records rather than an indiscriminate portfolio discount. For P4, watch public bid notices, buyer-issued requests, and recurring service complaints that can be confirmed directly. Reviews are prompts for questions, not evidence to repeat as accusations. Ask whether the actual failure is missed visits, restroom replenishment, inconsistent staffing, or an unworkable scope. A replacement contract is attractive only when the buyer will repair the operating cause instead of transferring an impossible price to the next vendor.

Create one account record per purchasing unit in HubSpot or Pipedrive and separate the account from its service addresses. Required fields are play code, source, buyer role, square footage by cleanable zone, frequency, renewal date if confirmed, current problem, travel time, and next action. A building appearing in two public feeds remains one prospect. Use Google Maps for routing estimates, then validate travel during the actual shift; a fifteen-minute daytime hop can become a forty-minute evening transfer. Give the five-full-time, four-contractor pod a single demand board so separate sellers cannot promise the same start slot. Contractors belong on the capacity board only after availability, lawful classification, and scope are verified. Do not equate nine names with nine interchangeable cleaners: supervision, absence cover, training, and specialty work consume capacity differently. Each week, review new signals with the field supervisor and reject distant opportunities before spending time on proposals. Keep public-sector notices in a separate lane when procurement timing, wage determinations, or bonding differ from ordinary commercial work. Research only public business information and buyer-provided records; tenant rosters do not justify collecting patient information or private access credentials.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Find the operational opening before discussing price**
> 
> Experienced cleaning sellers win by resolving the buyer's specific service failure while protecting the supervisor's route and the crew's paid time.
> - Ask whether restroom supplies are owner-furnished; empty dispensers can create a service complaint even when cleaning is complete.
> - Walk the loading entrance and elevator route during the proposed shift; locked docks and shared elevators change paid production time.
> - Ask property managers for the next nearby renewal after proving the first site; geographic adjacency is more valuable than scattered logo count.
> - Separate initial floor recovery from maintenance pricing; years of buildup should not become an unpaid first-month obligation.

## 2. Qualify authority, task hours, and pod economics before bidding  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Use a one-hundred-point qualification card before scheduling a full walkthrough. Award twenty points for verified purchasing authority and a decision window within one hundred twenty days; twenty for route and shift fit; twenty-five for task-level economics; fifteen for a documented recurring service problem; ten for workable payment terms and payer identity; and ten for manageable access and safety requirements. Score each category against written evidence, not seller enthusiasm. A score of seventy-five earns a walkthrough, sixty through seventy-four enters timed nurture, and lower scores leave the active pipeline. Missing authority, unfunded payroll, or uncontrolled exposure overrides the numerical result. Confirm whether the signer controls the entire contract or needs an owner, regional manager, or procurement team. Ask when incumbent notice must be delivered without encouraging breach of an existing agreement. Document invoices, purchase-order requirements, and who resolves disputes. For medical sites, distinguish ordinary environmental cleaning from instrument reprocessing, regulated waste transport, and tasks involving occupational exposure. The latter can require equipment and training outside the initial pod's capability. Declining those tasks preserves a viable recurring contract rather than creating an unsupported service promise.

During the walkthrough, measure cleanable zones and count fixtures, waste points, stair flights, and high-touch surfaces. Use ISSA task-time references as starting estimates, then adjust using timed observations for occupancy, soil load, equipment, security procedures, and clutter. Do not apply one square-feet-per-hour assumption to both carpet vacuuming and treatment-room disinfection. An illustrative office requires three productive hours per visit, five visits weekly, and fifty-two weeks: 780 productive hours annually. Add 156 paid hours for travel, setup, and inspection, producing 936 paid hours. At an illustrative $24 loaded hourly cost, direct labor is $22,464. Add $3,600 for supplies and equipment allocation; a $36,000 annual contract then leaves $9,936, or 27.6%, before remaining company overhead and owner profit. This is a bid example, not an additional scorecard scenario. A ten-percent increase in paid hours adds $2,246.40 of labor expense and reduces that contribution to $7,689.60. Validate the loaded rate against local wages, payroll taxes, workers' compensation, paid leave, overtime, and recruiting costs. Quote periodic floor work and initial restoration separately, with quantities and service dates.

Keep the canonical annual cases intact: Conservative revenue of $250,000 at eight percent produces $20,000 owner net profit; Base revenue of $725,000 at twelve percent produces $87,000; Scaled revenue of $1,200,000 at sixteen percent produces $192,000. Their implied total annual cost envelopes are $230,000, $638,000, and $1,008,000 respectively. Conservative and Base each specify five full-time employees plus four contractors; Scaled specifies six plus five. Those headcounts do not establish affordable payroll. Five employees at forty hours for fifty-two weeks represent 10,400 paid hours; even an illustrative $20 loaded rate consumes $208,000 before contractor costs and other overhead. That leaves only $22,000 in the Conservative cost envelope, making local wage and coverage validation decisive. If actual costs exceed the envelope, flag the scenario as operationally infeasible rather than quietly reducing wages or relabeling employees. Maintain a weekly capacity ledger separating sold productive hours from travel, supervision, training, and reserve coverage. Add contracts only when the relevant shift has funded capacity. Scheduling, inspection logs, and billing improve coordination, but growth still requires more paid crew hours and cannot be modeled as software-like incremental revenue.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before promising a start date**
> 
> A high sourcing score cannot compensate for missing authority, negative unit economics, unsupported staffing, or uncontrolled site hazards.
> - Verify the signing entity, authorized signer, service addresses, incumbent notice timing, and invoice recipient in writing.
> - Recalculate paid hours, overtime exposure, supplies, equipment, travel, and company overhead against the proposed recurring price.
> - Have the supervisor confirm named coverage, backup availability, training time, and funded payroll through the expected collection date.
> - Confirm chemical labels, accessible safety data sheets, required training, insurance evidence, and exposure controls for the actual contracted tasks.

## 3. Run a measured outbound cadence with a local inbound loop  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Work a compact, evidence-led cadence rather than blasting every business in the county. On day one, send a short email naming the verified trigger, the relevant service problem, and a fifteen-minute scope call. For P1, reference the announced move and ask who owns recurring cleaning after handover. For P2, ask whether treatment-room cleaning and landlord common-area service have separate owners. On day three, call the published business line manually and confirm the responsible role. On day six, send a one-page task-frequency example matched to that property type. On day ten, offer two walkthrough windows aligned with the supervisor's route. On day fifteen, close the loop and ask whether a future renewal month is more appropriate. Stop immediately after an opt-out and suppress the address across campaigns. Commercial email must follow 15 U.S.C. 7704, including truthful routing information, nondeceptive subjects, the required advertising identification, a valid physical postal address, and a functioning opt-out honored within ten business days. Do not assume a published mobile number authorizes automated calls or texts. Use manual business outreach and evaluate applicable federal and state calling restrictions before adding automation.

Treat conversion numbers as planning hypotheses until this territory produces measured cohorts. One illustrative batch contains 240 unique accounts, ninety-six verified buyer contacts, thirty-six two-way conversations, eighteen qualified opportunities, twelve completed walkthroughs, eight economically acceptable proposals, and four signed wins. These imply forty-percent contact coverage, 37.5-percent contact-to-conversation conversion, fifty-percent conversation-to-qualified conversion, 66.7-percent qualified-to-walkthrough conversion, 66.7-percent walkthrough-to-proposal conversion, and fifty-percent proposal-to-win conversion. Overall account-to-win conversion is 1.67 percent. They are test targets, not measured industry benchmarks. Track timestamps so a deal that signs in month four stays attributed to its original cohort. Count wins only after executed agreements establish funded, schedulable service obligations; a verbal yes is not a win. At the same illustrative yield, three wins require roughly 180 accounts and ten require roughly 600, subject to capacity and lead quality. Process sixty researched accounts weekly for four weeks to test the first batch, then revise from observed results. A surge in conversations without walkthroughs usually points to weak authority or timing qualification; walkthroughs without proposals can indicate bad targeting, inadequate budget, or uncontrolled estimating effort.

Build inbound around proof that answers a local facilities buyer's next question. Maintain an eligible Google Business Profile with accurate service areas and business details, then publish separate office, outpatient, and property-management pages describing actual supported tasks. Use Search Console and tagged website forms to distinguish organic inquiries from outbound replies. Show an anonymized inspection example, a task-frequency schedule, and a clear walkthrough request; avoid security layouts, patient records, badge images, or customer logos without permission. Ask satisfied buyers for honest reviews without conditioning rewards on positive sentiment. Pursue reciprocal introductions with commercial brokers, floor-care specialists, and facility maintenance vendors, documenting referral arrangements and buyer restrictions. Keep one seller accountable for follow-up and batch supervisor walkthroughs into two route-compatible blocks weekly. Do not pull productive cleaners off contracted shifts to create appearances of sales activity. Review channel results every Friday by qualified opportunities, quoted contribution, signed recurring revenue, and hours sold, not impressions or raw form counts. Schedule early starts sequentially so the pod can establish inspection baselines before taking the next site. A first-three-to-ten-win objective remains subordinate to service quality and funded capacity during the one-to-four-month acquisition window.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Build and deduplicate the weekly account batch
   - Research sixty route-compatible accounts across the four mapped plays.
   - Attach dated source evidence, purchasing-unit identity, service address, play code, and the next verification question to every account.
2. Verify the buyer and apply the qualification card
   - Confirm authority, timing, route fit, and supported task scope.
   - Advance scores of seventy-five or higher only when authority, funded capacity, and safety conditions also pass the pre-flight checks.
3. Run the cadence and measure the walkthrough
   - Complete the fifteen-day sequence while respecting stop requests.
   - Use actual task quantities, access constraints, and timed production observations to construct an auditable labor estimate.
4. Review conversion and release supported start slots
   - Audit cohort conversions, proposal contribution, and weekly crew capacity.
   - Book only executed, funded agreements; sequence mobilizations and send failed bids back to targeting or pricing review with recorded reasons.

## 4. Convert objections into scoped agreements and retained routes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Handle objections by exposing the service variable beneath the requested concession. When a buyer says the incumbent is cheaper, compare visit frequency, cleanable zones, periodic work, consumables, inspection time, and relief coverage before comparing totals. Offer a reduced task frequency only where the buyer's actual operating needs permit it; do not silently remove required disinfectant contact time or worker protections. When asked for a free trial, offer a short paid pilot with defined rooms, visits, acceptance criteria, and a decision date. When the buyer says staff changes caused prior failures, show the supervisor escalation path, training records, and backup assignment rather than promising zero turnover. For price pressure from a property manager, exchange route density or a longer committed schedule for documented savings only after recalculating hours. A medical practice requesting complete infection prevention needs precise task boundaries, not a sweeping claim that janitorial work eliminates clinical risk. Respond to slow procurement by providing insurance evidence, a completed vendor packet, and a written scope; do not start unpaid service to bypass purchasing controls. Record each objection and resolution so future targeting reflects actual buyer economics rather than the seller's recollection.

Close with a service schedule that names each area, task, frequency, access window, inspection method, and excluded activity. State responsibility for keys, alarm codes, equipment storage, consumables, and waste streams. Keep access credentials out of the sales CRM and issue them through a controlled operational process. Define the initial restoration charge, recurring price, separately authorized extras, invoice timing, dispute process, renewal terms, and termination notice. For wages, apply the Fair Labor Standards Act's minimum-wage and overtime requirements under 29 U.S.C. 206 and 207 plus applicable state and local rules. A subcontract agreement alone does not settle worker classification; evaluate the actual relationship under applicable wage, tax, and state standards. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200 requires a hazard communication program where applicable, including labels, safety data sheets, and employee information and training. Where reasonably anticipated occupational exposure exists, assess 29 CFR 1910.1030 requirements before assignment. Ordinary incidental access during janitorial service does not automatically make the vendor a HIPAA business associate; evaluate whether the contracted function involves protected health information under 45 CFR 160.103. Keep patient information out of cleaning logs and follow the customer's authorized privacy procedures.

The first signed agreement becomes a sourcing asset only after the operation works. Before the first shift, walk the route with the assigned supervisor, confirm supply staging, test permitted access, and document baseline conditions without capturing sensitive material. Inspect after the first service, review exceptions with the buyer during the first week, and compare estimated versus paid hours after two weeks. If excess time comes from added scope, document it and agree a change order; if it comes from poor training or routing, correct the internal process without shifting blame. At thirty days, review task completion, complaint closure, labor variance, collections, and contribution. Request an introduction to one adjacent property only after this review supports the claim of reliable service. Track concentration by recognized contract revenue and by supervisor dependence: three buildings under one manager may represent one cancellation decision. Set an internal concentration review threshold, such as twenty-five percent of revenue, and test the payroll impact of losing that buyer. Continue sourcing replacements and adjacent opportunities, but never count unsigned proposals as revenue. Reliable first wins come from measured work, paid capacity, and explicit accountability rather than a growing list of addresses.

**Buyer Objections, Contract Repairs, and First-Month Retention Controls**

| Buyer objection or operating concern | Question that reveals the underlying constraint | Specific response or contract mechanism | Evidence required before declaring the issue resolved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The incumbent's monthly price is materially below your proposal | Which tasks and frequencies are included in that comparison | Compare measured scope and offer explicit priced frequency alternatives | Buyer accepts the revised task schedule and associated recurring price |
| The buyer wants free service before making any commitment | Which observable service outcomes would establish acceptable vendor performance | Offer a paid pilot with fixed visits and acceptance criteria | Signed pilot terms identify payment timing and final decision date |
| Previous cleaners changed constantly and repeatedly missed assigned tasks | Which shifts and tasks generated the most repeated service failures | Present supervisor accountability and verified backup coverage by shift | Inspection logs demonstrate completed tasks and documented exception closure |
| The property manager requests discounts across several distant buildings | Do these buildings actually share efficient routes and service windows | Price each site before crediting demonstrated shared operating savings | Route model supports the discount without reducing funded labor coverage |
| The medical buyer expects broad clinical infection prevention coverage | Which environmental tasks are requested versus clinical treatment responsibilities | Define supported cleaning tasks and exclude unsupported clinical functions | Written scope and exposure assessment match actual worker training |
| The new account consumes more paid hours than the estimate | Did task quantities change or did the execution process fail | Use measured variances to separate change orders from internal corrections | Buyer-approved scope and subsequent paid-hour records reconcile the difference |
