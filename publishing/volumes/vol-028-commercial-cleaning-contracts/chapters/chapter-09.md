## 1. Establish the labor, licensing, and sector perimeter before quoting  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Map this chapter's four operating plays to separate execution files: recurring office contracts, outpatient medical janitorial service, properly classified W-2 and subcontractor delivery, and supervisor-pod retention. These are working labels for the four plays, not four additional revenue streams. Candidate #10 starts with five full-time employees and four contractors; assign one employee as the working supervisor and four as cleaners, while defining contractor packages by genuine independent business deliverables. A contractor label does not establish independence. Apply the Fair Labor Standards Act economic reality analysis, federal employment-tax rules, and each applicable state test separately. California Labor Code section 2775 generally uses an ABC framework, subject to statutory exceptions whose conditions must actually be satisfied. A cleaner performing the company's ordinary cleaning service creates a substantial usual-course issue under that framework. Do not present an invoice, insurance certificate, or entity registration as a cure. Require a dated classification assessment before assigning any contractor, and move controlled employee-like work onto payroll. Supervisor dispatch, required personal attendance, company equipment, and indefinite hourly assignments are evidence to evaluate, not details to hide from the file.

Build a jurisdiction register from actual worksite addresses, not the customer's headquarters. Record state and municipal wage floors, overtime rules, paid sick leave, meal and rest requirements, payroll timing, workers' compensation coverage, and any local business license. Under 29 U.S.C. sections 206 and 207, covered nonexempt employees receive applicable minimum wages and overtime; more protective state rules still apply. Count required setup, training, and travel between sites during the workday as compensable when the governing rules require it. Do not deduct a meal period during which a cleaner keeps answering dispatch calls. California janitorial employers also need an analysis of Labor Code sections 1420 through 1434, including registration and applicable training and recordkeeping duties. If a contract directly serves a federal agency, examine Service Contract Labor Standards coverage, the incorporated wage determination, and required fringe benefits before pricing. Ordinary private offices do not inherit federal contract wage terms merely because a tenant sells to government. Use Gusto or ADP for payroll configuration, but reconcile settings against the location register and actual time records. Software defaults cannot decide statutory coverage or excuse an incorrect overtime calculation.

Separate ordinary janitorial work from regulated services before accepting a medical account. OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200, requires the applicable written program, labels, safety data sheets, and employee information and training. Evaluate reasonably anticipated occupational exposure under 29 CFR 1910.1030; covered bloodborne-pathogen work requires exposure controls, training, appropriate protection, and other specified measures. A clinic label alone neither establishes nor eliminates exposure. Define sharps, visible blood, pharmaceutical waste, and regulated medical waste handling as distinct tasks with explicit responsibility and state-specific authorization. Routine janitorial access with merely incidental exposure to protected health information generally does not make the cleaner a HIPAA business associate; document why the actual service fits that boundary. Record destruction or deliberate handling of patient information requires a different assessment. Use EPA-registered disinfectants for the intended application and follow label dilution, surface compatibility, and contact-time directions. Never sell sterilization when the process only supports disinfection. Require the clinic to secure records and identify its infection-prevention contact. Supervisor-pod inspections should verify these boundaries without photographing patient names, charts, screens, or treatment records. Stop excluded work and notify the designated contact rather than improvising a higher-risk service.

## 2. Turn the walkthrough into enforceable scope and collectible fees  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The contract package should contain a master services agreement, a site-specific statement of work, a task-frequency schedule, an access protocol, and a pricing exhibit that resolve conflicts through an explicit order of precedence. Measure cleanable area rather than leasing area, identify floor types, and count restrooms, fixtures, occupied stations, and waste points. Use ISSA production-rate references as estimating inputs, then validate them through a timed walkthrough and pilot shift; neither a published rate nor a square-foot price proves adequate staffing. Specify office tasks by daily, weekly, monthly, and periodic frequency. For medical accounts, identify approved products, required contact times, isolation-room boundaries, and excluded waste. State who buys liners, paper products, soap, dispensers, and consumables. Define a completed service visit through assigned tasks and inspection evidence rather than a vague promise that premises will always look pristine. Give the customer a defined deficiency reporting window and a practical cure process without eliminating remedies for latent damage. Attach the initial condition record so inherited floor damage does not become a disputed cleaning failure. Obtain customer acknowledgment before the first routine invoice and preserve the signed version.

Make the fee trigger objectively auditable: a monthly recurring fee for the scheduled service obligation, invoiced on the agreed date, with separate authorized charges for added visits and periodic work. Define how holidays, customer lockouts, closures, and emergency requests affect service and charges. State payment terms, lawful late charges, the invoice-dispute process, and when undisputed amounts remain due. Require written change authorization identifying the task, quantity, price, and start date; a receptionist's informal request should not silently expand nightly labor. Include a transparent repricing mechanism for documented wage mandates, changed occupancy, added cleanable area, and customer-directed frequencies, with notice and an agreed termination option if revised pricing is rejected. Anti-circumvention should protect defined introductions or confidential pricing only within a narrow, supportable scope. Do not convert it into a broad employee no-hire arrangement, an industry-wide customer restriction, or a penalty for ordinary competition. Prefer confidentiality, clear ownership of proposal materials, and payment for services actually authorized. Employment restraints, liquidated damages, and restrictive covenants need jurisdiction-specific review before inclusion. Do not add a restrictive clause merely because a template vendor supplied it.

Allocate liability to the party controlling the risk and connect each promise to available insurance. Obtain commercial general liability, required workers' compensation, and appropriate automobile coverage; evaluate employee dishonesty, key loss, and chemical or pollution exclusions against actual site exposures. A certificate of insurance is evidence of coverage information, not the additional insured endorsement itself. Request required endorsements and examine policy conditions rather than promising that every spill or theft will be covered. Negotiate a liability cap with a clear measurement period, treatment of consequential damages, and expressly negotiated exceptions; enforceability and mandatory liabilities vary by state. Use fault-based indemnity that addresses defense handling and settlement authority rather than accepting liability for the customer's sole negligence. Specify governing law, forum, escalation contacts, and whether disputes proceed through courts or arbitration. Preserve access to urgent protective relief where appropriate. State that customers own their site records and delivered reports, while the operator retains preexisting checklists and methods subject to the customer's agreed usage rights. Define photo restrictions, access-log retention, credential revocation, return of keys, and deletion of customer data after termination.

**Eight Contract Clauses That Protect Paid Hours and Collections**

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope and frequency establish the purchased service obligation | List cleanable zones, recurring tasks, exclusions, and frequencies | Attach measured walkthrough notes and acknowledged condition photographs | Reject unlimited extra tasks disguised as routine cleanliness |
| Fee trigger separates recurring obligations from additional purchases | State invoice dates, earned charges, closures, and payment terms | Match invoices to signed schedules and authorized additional visits | Prevent unsupported billing for services outside the agreed trigger |
| Change control prevents quiet expansion of paid labor | Require authorized task quantities, pricing, and effective service dates | Retain buyer authorization linked to the revised dispatch schedule | Do not absorb recurring additions through casual frontline requests |

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Contract habits that veteran cleaning operators use**
> 
> Small contractual details protect more margin than broad promises about quality when the same crew must cover several sites.
> - Photograph existing floor damage without people or sensitive records, label each image by zone, and attach the acknowledged condition record to the statement of work.
> - Time restroom replenishment separately from floor cleaning; hidden dispenser and supply work routinely defeats square-foot-only office bids.
> - Price medical contact-time requirements into the route before selling the visit frequency; chemicals cannot compensate for a shift too short to follow the label.
> - Use a numbered key register with signed transfers and immediate loss escalation; compare key-loss coverage with the building's actual rekey exposure.

## 3. Reconcile legal promises to the scorecard and supervisor capacity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Keep the scorecard arithmetic separate from the staffing feasibility test. Conservative revenue of $250,000 at an eight percent net margin produces $20,000 annual owner net profit and permits $230,000 of total annual costs. Base revenue of $725,000 at twelve percent produces $87,000 profit and permits $638,000 of costs. Scaled revenue of $1,200,000 at sixteen percent produces $192,000 profit and permits $1,008,000 of costs. These are cleaning contract revenues, not client sales, facility property values, or procurement budgets. The first two cases retain five full-time employees plus four contractors; Scaled uses six full-time employees plus five contractors. Do not rewrite full-time employees as occasional workers to force a result. As an illustrative stress test, five employees paid for 2,080 hours each at $20 per hour require $208,000 in straight-time wages. A twenty percent illustrative employment burden raises that amount to $249,600, already exceeding the Conservative total cost allowance before contractor invoices, supplies, insurance, or administration. That case fails under those assumptions. Preserve the canonical numbers as benchmarks, flag the local infeasibility, and decline contracts that depend on unpaid work.

For every bid, build a service-hour ledger containing productive cleaning time, setup, supply handling, travel, required training, supervisor inspections, expected rework, and paid absence coverage. Separate paid hours from customer-visible cleaning hours. An illustrative office contract at $6,000 monthly revenue yields $72,000 annually. If delivery requires 150 paid hours monthly at $25 loaded hourly cost, labor costs $3,750 monthly; adding $600 for supplies, travel, and site-specific insurance allocation leaves $1,650 monthly contribution before central overhead and owner net profit. It does not establish a 27.5 percent company net margin. Test the full portfolio against the applicable total cost allowance after allocating payroll administration, recruiting, equipment, supervision, and customer acquisition. Treat contractor invoices as delivery costs, not revenue offsets that hide actual scale. Maintain enough cash to bridge payroll and customer payment dates; a signed annual contract does not fund next Friday's wages. The $10,000 to $50,000 startup capital range and $3,000 to $14,000 monthly operating-expense planning range cannot substitute for a complete scaled payroll model. Do not use that expense range as an all-in ceiling for every scenario.

The four-person pod needs a named coverage plan, not simply nine names in scheduling software. Use Swept or Jobber to assign sites and inspection reminders, QuickBooks Online to connect invoices to the contract register, and a controlled document store for signed agreements and training evidence. One working supervisor cannot clean a full route, inspect every building, recruit replacements, and resolve every access incident without reserved hours. Allocate supervision explicitly and test simultaneous absences before promising service windows. Contractors should receive legitimate independent service packages where classification permits, not serve as an off-payroll reserve for employee shifts. Review concentration monthly using each customer's percentage of recognized revenue, contribution, receivables, and scheduled hours. A large account can create four different dependencies. Establish internal limits based on cash reserves and replacement capacity rather than presenting an arbitrary percentage as a statute. Move to the eleven-person Scaled team only when signed demand and verified hourly economics support the additional employee and contractor. Scheduling, inspection logs, and billing automation reduce administrative friction; they cannot replace required disinfectant contact time, lawful breaks, or physical cleaning labor. Audit those constraints before adding sites.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Measure the service
   - Complete a timed walkthrough and classify every requested task.
   - Record cleanable area, fixtures, access delays, chemical contact times, exclusions, and paid travel before calculating the fee.
2. Price the lawful hours
   - Apply site wage rules and build the complete delivery budget.
   - Include overtime, required benefits, employment burden, absence coverage, contractor invoices, supplies, and supervisor inspection time.
3. Bind the operating agreement
   - Execute the scope, pricing, access, insurance, and data provisions.
   - Verify buyer authority, registration where required, effective coverage, task training, and the first invoice's contractual trigger.
4. Reconcile after launch
   - Compare the first four weeks of paid hours with the bid.
   - Investigate every material variance, document customer changes, and update the forecast without concealing shortfalls through unpaid labor.

## 4. Use permission-aware outreach and discovery scripts that survive delivery  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start outreach with a narrow account list: office managers, property managers, and outpatient practice administrators within the pod's service radius. Record the business source, role relevance, contact channel, and suppression status in HubSpot or a controlled spreadsheet. Commercial B2B email remains subject to CAN-SPAM, including accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, the applicable advertising identification requirement, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out mechanism. Honor opt-outs within ten business days and keep suppression records when changing vendors. Use this email body verbatim after replacing bracketed fields with verified facts: 'Hello [name], this is a commercial offer from [company]. We provide recurring office cleaning in [service area]. Would a fifteen-minute walkthrough help you compare your task schedule, access requirements, and inspection process? We quote only after measuring the work. If another colleague handles cleaning, please point me to their business contact. To stop these emails, reply stop or use [working opt-out link]. [Company], [valid postal address].' Use the subject 'Cleaning walkthrough for [site name]' only when the site identification is accurate. Do not imply an existing account relationship or attach fabricated inspection findings.

For telephone prospecting, distinguish a live manual business call from autodialed calls, artificial or prerecorded voice calls, and texts. The TCPA and FCC rules depend on technology, number type, purpose, and consent; a business listing does not create permission for every channel. State calling and recording laws can add requirements. Keep the default campaign to reviewed manual calls to verified business lines, use accurate caller identification, observe applicable calling restrictions, and honor stop requests. Do not record discovery calls until the recording-law assessment and required permissions are complete. Use this opening: 'Hello, this is [name] with [company]. We provide commercial cleaning in [area]. Is this a reasonable moment for a brief question about your cleaning contract?' If invited, continue: 'Who manages your task schedule and inspections, and when do you next review service performance?' For medical prospects, say: 'We are discussing environmental cleaning, not clinical services. Which spaces and waste tasks would remain with your staff or specialist vendor?' If declined, close: 'Understood. I will mark this contact so we stop outreach.' Record and honor that instruction rather than rotating to another employee or number.

Discovery should establish authority, scope, and delivery economics before the salesperson promises a start date. Ask: 'Who can authorize this agreement and approve additional work? What is the cleanable area, required visit schedule, occupancy pattern, and current service failure? Which doors, alarms, and restricted rooms affect access? Who supplies consumables? Are there blood, sharps, isolation, or medical-waste tasks? Which insurance endorsements and vendor registrations do you require? What are the invoice submission and payment steps?' Follow with: 'May we perform a measured walkthrough and return a task-level scope with explicit exclusions?' For scope creep, use: 'That task is outside the signed frequency schedule. We can price it as a separate visit or recurring addition; who can authorize the change?' Close proposals with the actual start conditions: 'We can confirm mobilization after the agreement, access test, staffing assignment, and required site training are complete.' Do not promise a hospital-grade outcome, insured loss recovery, or immediate crew availability without supporting facts. Log the buyer's answers against all four plays and hand the supervisor a single approved scope. The salesperson remains responsible for transferring commitments, not merely collecting a signature.
