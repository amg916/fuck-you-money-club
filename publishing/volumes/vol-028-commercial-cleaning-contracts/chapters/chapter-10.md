## 1. Instrument Four Operator Plays Before Automating the Pod  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Treat the automation target as a denominator problem before buying software. For Candidate #10, the four mapped plays in this chapter are territory research and qualification, labor-aware scheduling, inspection evidence and corrective action, and billing with renewal and referral distribution. Each play owns explicit administrative events, not an imaginary percentage of all cleaning work. Research events include deduplicating properties and retrieving permitted business contact information; qualification events include checking service geography and recording building type. Fulfillment events include preparing rosters, organizing inspection evidence, and matching completed service to contract schedules. Acquisition events include maintaining approved service pages, routing inquiries, and preparing account-specific outreach drafts. Define an event as one completed business outcome with a stable identifier; splitting a single invoice into twenty technical operations must not inflate the automation rate. Record whether each outcome finished unattended, required human judgment, or failed and entered recovery. A 70%+ objective applies only to this eligible repetitive workload. It excludes mopping, restroom servicing, clinical-area cleaning, travel, training, and supervisory walkthroughs. Establish a two-week baseline by event family, including handling minutes and rework, before setting targets. Report end-to-end completion beside automation coverage so a fast pipeline cannot conceal unanswered customers or missing visits.

Assign ownership inside the 5 FT + 4 C constraint rather than inventing a separate technology department. One illustrative allocation is a working owner handling sales and account decisions, one working supervisor handling dispatch and inspections, and three full-time cleaners supporting recurring routes. Four contractors provide genuinely independent contracted capacity where the actual relationship supports that status; the scorecard's C label does not determine classification. Count the owner's and supervisor's field hours explicitly when testing service capacity. The owner maintains contract versions, price floors, and customer concentration limits. The supervisor maintains task standards, access restrictions, training records, and exceptions. A contractor business receives an agreed scope and evidence requirements without being represented as a convenient substitute for employee payroll. Use a job identifier shared by HubSpot, a scheduling system such as Jobber, and QuickBooks Online, subject to the selected products' supported interfaces. Keep the authoritative crosswalk in an access-controlled register. Store property identifiers separately from personal contact details and restrict building access instructions to assigned personnel. Weekly ownership review should examine open exceptions, stale credentials, expiring vendor documents, and route overload. This makes the automation accountable to named people without pretending nine people can absorb unlimited new buildings.

Build the first integration as a small Python service with a durable event ledger, not a chain of fragile email rules. Receive provider webhooks over HTTPS, validate signatures according to each provider's documented scheme, check replay controls where supported, and persist the event before returning success. A single-host pilot can use sqlite3 transactions, json serialization, hashlib digests, and hmac verification; protect the database, back it up, and keep external API requests outside write transactions. For greater concurrent volume, move the ledger to a managed transactional database without changing the event contract. Store source, event identifier, contract revision, object identifier, received time, processing state, attempts, and correlation identifier. A unique constraint on source and event identifier prevents repeated delivery from creating repeated work. Use an outbox for outbound changes and reconcile remote results after timeouts instead of blindly repeating invoice creation. An LLM may extract proposed fields or draft an explanation, but deterministic code validates required fields, arithmetic, allowed status transitions, and authorization. Treat web pages, uploaded documents, and customer notes as data, never executable instructions. Redact sensitive content before model submission and retain the extraction evidence needed for a reviewer to resolve ambiguity.

## 2. Schedule Paid Capacity and Inspect Evidence Without Delegating Judgment  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start scheduling from a measured task budget, not a contract's attractive monthly price. ISSA production-rate references provide estimating inputs; actual equipment, soil load, furniture density, security procedures, and medical-area protocols determine the usable rate. For illustration, a 20,000 cleanable-square-foot office at an assumed blended 4,000 square feet per productive hour requires five productive hours per visit. Twenty visits produce 100 monthly productive hours. Adding twenty hours for setup, supervision, and paid route activity yields 120 budgeted hours. At an illustrative loaded labor cost of $24 per hour, labor costs $2,880. A $4,500 monthly contract then leaves $1,620 before supplies, equipment, insurance, administration, and other overhead; that remainder is not owner profit. Store these assumptions beside the bid and replace them with observations after mobilization. The scheduling service can propose workers against access windows, task qualifications, equipment availability, and travel buffers. It must reject overlapping assignments and surface insufficient capacity rather than silently shortening visits. Confirm acceptance before publishing a changed roster, and preserve the prior version so the supervisor can restore it during an outage or dispute. Higher software throughput does not create another paid cleaning hour.

Use payroll rules as explicit scheduling constraints while keeping actual time capture independent of the planned budget. Under 29 U.S.C. § 207, covered nonexempt employees generally receive overtime after forty hours in a workweek; apply relevant state requirements as well. Travel between job sites during the workday is work time under 29 CFR § 785.38. Track training, required meetings, and other compensable activities under the applicable rules rather than hiding them inside production assumptions. A projected overtime alert routes to the supervisor, but software must never delete worked time to preserve a bid margin. Maintain wage and hour records consistent with 29 CFR § 516.2, and preserve corrections with the original entry and the reason. Worker classification requires a documented assessment under applicable federal and state tests; a vendor invoice or signed label alone does not settle it. For California work, evaluate Labor Code § 2775 and any genuinely applicable exceptions before treating cleaning labor as independent contracting. Separately verify insurance requirements against customer contracts and local requirements. Where the current staffing model fails those tests, reprice or restructure the work instead of asking automation to conceal the mismatch. That is an operating constraint, not an integration defect.

Inspection automation should accelerate evidence handling while leaving cleanliness acceptance with a qualified person. Configure mobile forms by site, zone, task frequency, and risk: restrooms, break rooms, entrances, clinical rooms, and periodic floor work need different observations. Require job identity, task completion, exceptions, and timestamps, with offline capture for basements and secured facilities. Photos are optional only where the contract permits them; prohibit patient details, visible records, and unnecessary personal images in medical settings. Assess whether the actual service arrangement involves handling protected health information rather than assuming every janitorial contract creates the same obligations. An LLM can group recurring complaints and draft a corrective-action summary, but a photograph cannot establish microbial safety or proper disinfectant contact time. OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR § 1910.1200, makes chemical labeling, safety data sheets, and training concrete operating requirements. Where occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials exists, evaluate 29 CFR § 1910.1030 requirements before assigning work. Route spills, exposure incidents, chemical substitutions, and disputed completion to the supervisor immediately. During connectivity loss, crews follow the approved local procedure and synchronize records later; they do not improvise chemical mixtures or wait for an LLM to authorize safe work.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify Four Conditions Before Expanding Automated Dispatch**
> 
> Consequential decisions remain assigned to the owner or qualified supervisor. An automated recommendation cannot substitute for these checks.
> - Verify that route capacity includes setup, required training, between-site travel, and realistic absence coverage before accepting another recurring building.
> - Verify actual worker relationships, timekeeping rules, and insurance documents before enabling assignments to employees or contractor businesses.
> - Verify current chemical instructions, required safety training, site-specific exposure procedures, and authorized evidence capture before publishing mobile forms.
> - Verify offline rosters, escalation contacts, restored backups, and a successful duplicate-event test before relying on unattended dispatch preparation.

## 3. Close the Contract-to-Cash Loop and Price Every Automated Outcome  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Make billing a three-way comparison among signed scope, service evidence, and invoice terms. A recurring fixed-fee contract normally generates its scheduled charge under the agreement; missing inspection evidence should trigger review, not an invented switch to hourly billing. Separately identify authorized extras, credits, periodic floor work, applicable taxes, and customer purchase-order requirements. A Python worker builds a draft invoice payload from validated fields and attaches a contract revision reference. Human review is mandatory for first invoices, rate changes, credits, disputed service, and out-of-scope additions. Stable recurring invoices may follow a previously authorized release policy with exception thresholds and reconciliation. Use the accounting platform's supported idempotency mechanism where available; otherwise retain a local business key and query the remote system before retrying an uncertain creation. Never interpret an HTTP timeout as proof that an invoice was absent. After release, reconcile invoice identifiers, amounts, delivery status, payments, and credits back to the ledger. Keep payment credentials in the payment provider's controlled workflow rather than in model prompts or webhook logs. Collection reminders must pause on an open dispute and route to the account owner. Accurate contract-to-cash automation protects customer trust by explaining charges, not merely sending them faster.

Price the automation against completed outcomes rather than token volume alone. Consider an illustrative month containing 1,000 eligible administrative events across the four plays. Suppose 730 finish unattended and 270 require human review before completion: the straight-through completion rate is 73%, provided retries and failures remain attached to their original events. Assume allocated monthly costs of $36 for model requests, $24 for execution and queue infrastructure, and $48 for integration subscriptions. Those assumptions yield $108 in machine costs, or $0.108 per completed event. At two review minutes per referred event, reviewers spend nine hours. At a loaded $30 hourly cost, review adds $270. Four additional hours of random audits and recovery drills add $120, producing a fully counted administrative pipeline cost of $498, or $0.498 per event. These are budgeting assumptions, not vendor quotations. If manual handling previously averaged four minutes per event, the baseline was 66.67 hours; thirteen review and audit hours leave about 53.67 hours available for other work. Track actual maintenance separately and include it before claiming realized savings. Freed time becomes incremental profit only when costs fall or usable capacity supports profitable contracts without equivalent additional overhead.

Keep every operating experiment reconciled to the canonical scorecard. Conservative annual cleaning contract revenue is $250,000 with an 8% net margin and $20,000 owner net profit, implying $230,000 of total costs. Base revenue is $725,000 with a 12.0% net margin and $87,000 owner net profit, implying $638,000 of costs. Scaled revenue is $1,200,000 with a 16% net margin and $192,000 owner net profit, implying $1,008,000 of costs. The first two cases use 5 FT + 4 C; Scaled uses 6 FT + 5 C, eleven people. These are recognized cleaning contract revenues, not customer property values, rent rolls, or payment volume. The scorecard does not supply wage schedules, utilization, or contractor hours, so validate each case through a bottom-up staffing model before treating its headcount as feasible capacity. Place software, review time, and implementation amortization inside the relevant total cost envelope rather than adding hypothetical savings to published profit. The illustrative $498 monthly pipeline equals $5,976 annually; it must replace or fit existing costs if canonical profit remains unchanged. Reconcile actual results monthly by building, separating labor overruns, scope growth, rework, customer credits, and overhead allocation so automation receives credit only for measured improvement.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the billable source packet
   - Join the signed contract revision, service period, and authorized change records.
   - Reject missing references and route conflicting purchase-order terms to the account owner before drafting charges.
2. Validate and review the invoice draft
   - Recalculate amounts deterministically and identify every exception requiring a person.
   - Review initial invoices, rate changes, credits, disputes, and extras against their supporting records; the LLM cannot calculate the authoritative total.
3. Release once and reconcile remote state
   - Submit through the authorized accounting integration using a durable business key.
   - After a timeout, query remote records before retrying; match the returned invoice identifier, customer, currency, and amount.
4. Measure collection and automation outcomes
   - Reconcile payments and log completed events, review minutes, failures, and recovery work.
   - Pause disputed reminders, investigate unmatched payments, and include audit and maintenance time in the monthly cost report.

## 4. Compound Route Density Through Evidence-Led Distribution and Recovery  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Build distribution around buildings the pod can actually serve. In the territory research play, a scheduled Python job normalizes permitted property records, existing CRM accounts, inbound forms, and manually verified business contacts into a territory register. Score location, building use, estimated service window, approximate cleanable area, and fit with existing routes; do not infer private tenant information or pretend public floor area equals contracted cleaning area. A reviewer checks high-priority opportunities and authorizes outreach claims before an approved sequence runs. For commercial email, implement truthful sender information, accurate subject lines, required identification and address information, and an effective opt-out process under 15 U.S.C. § 7704. Synchronize suppression across tools and honor requests within the statutory period; make internal suppression immediate where practicable. Telephone and text outreach require separate channel-specific review rather than borrowing email rules. Measure cost per qualified walkthrough, proposal, and signed recurring contract, then track retained gross contribution after mobilization. A hundred distant leads can be worse than one adjacent office because travel and supervision consume scarce paid capacity. Feed lost-bid reasons and actual first-month labor back into qualification so the next campaign selects better buildings instead of simply creating more sales activity.

The programmatic growth loop begins with verified service capability, not bulk page production. Maintain a structured inventory of real service territories, building types, equipment capabilities, documented task standards, and approved customer evidence. Generate draft pages for meaningful combinations, such as recurring office cleaning within an existing route or clinic cleaning supported by trained personnel. Human editors remove duplicate pages, unsupported infection-control claims, fabricated testimonials, and locations the crew cannot cover. Publish only useful pages containing concrete scope boundaries, walkthrough requirements, and an inquiry path that enters the same qualification queue. The referral loop begins after resolved inspections and stable service, when the billing and distribution play prepares a concise account recap. Ask the authorized customer contact for an introduction to another property manager or neighboring tenant, and obtain permission before publishing customer names, quotations, or site images. Track the referring account and resulting contract without exposing one customer's inspection history to another. A property manager with several buildings can accelerate distribution but also increase concentration risk. Review revenue and receivables by controlling customer group, not merely street address, and balance expansion against collections, service capacity, and the cost of replacing that relationship if it ends.

Make recovery behavior part of the growth system before increasing volume. Webhook receivers should acknowledge only durably recorded events; workers retry transient failures with bounded exponential backoff and jitter, respect provider rate limits, and move exhausted attempts into a visible exception queue. Reject invalid signatures and malformed payloads without processing them. A daily reconciliation compares source-system changes with ledger outcomes because a webhook may never arrive. Contract revisions invalidate stale drafts, and a per-account pause stops outreach, dispatch changes, or invoice release while a dispute is investigated. Maintain local copies of approved rosters and emergency contacts, with controlled access and a clear synchronization procedure after outages. Test duplicate events, delayed events, reordered updates, expired credentials, network timeouts, and restoration from backup against representative records. Review the four-play dashboard weekly: unattended completion, review burden, recovered failures, route labor variance, repeat inspection defects, collection aging, and acquisition contribution. Expand only the workflows whose error rates and recovery times the current pod can support. Candidate #10 remains labor intensive at 56/100: the durable flywheel is better evidence producing better targeting, denser routes, reliable service, stronger retention, and credible referrals, with more paid crew hours added when contracted cleaning demand genuinely increases.
