## Executive Summary

Practitioners agree public janitorial work is won on registration discipline, mandatory site visits, airtight compliance, and cash reserves deep enough to float payroll through net 30 to 60 terms plus about 30 extra days for new vendors. The street math is specific: divide cost by 0.8 for a true 20% margin, stack 15% payroll tax, 30% overhead, and 20% profit on a production-rate labor build, and bill federal work off 1,920 hours, not 2,080, or risk Department of Labor back pay. Winning technical volumes lean on quality-control plans, staffing plans, and green chemistry. The live split is over price: Sheenapreneur calls janitorial a bottom-feeder market where agencies usually pick the lowest bid, while Commercial Cleaning CEO and GovKidMethod report weighted, best-value scoring where past performance and technical approach can beat the cheapest number.

## The Practitioner Consensus

Every operator who has actually chased public janitorial work starts in the same place: registration and keyword discipline. Commercial Cleaning CEO tells owners to phone city or town hall and ask for vendor relations, the buyer, the purchaser, or the asset manager, then register in each Vendor Portal with email notifications routed through dedicated tags for state, county, and city, and to search 'custodial' or 'janitorial' instead of 'commercial cleaning' [j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:01:35]. Owlsfield Financial Corp runs the same play across levels: Delaware's 'my marketplace' notices, entity registration at sam.gov for federal work, and separate systems for cities and school districts, followed by reading every document for locations, hours, insurance, security rules, and deadline [LARqKW_L-QI @ 00:00:46]. GovKidMethod sizes the federal prize before anyone registers, pulling five fiscal years of NAICS 561720 spend on USASpending and landing on roughly $10B, led by DoD at $5.3B and GSA near $1.6B [ua_m84N0cwA @ 00:02:54]. CleanTender reports the identical fragmentation problem in the UK, where about 415 local bodies use different tendering websites and England alone runs over 60 portals, so consolidated feeds like Contracts Finder, Public Contracts Scotland, Sell 2 Wales, and eSourcing NI become the starting point before any council site [M3p8970sZyg @ 00:00:20].

The second point of agreement among operators who have actually submitted is that compliance kills more bids than price does. Commercial Cleaning CEO reports that site visits are often mandatory, that non-attendees are 'immediately rejected,' that some walkthroughs demand pre-approval or security clearance, that the lowest number does not automatically win because agencies score against a weighting formula, and that agencies prefer local firms while out-of-state companies rarely win [j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:05:39]. GovKidMethod breaks every federal proposal into three components, pricing, past performance, and technical, and walks through a VA cemetery solicitation awarded to the 'most advantageous' offer rather than the cheapest, with prices checked against competing bids, historical prices, the government estimate, and market research [dOj6rxt15uE @ 00:06:21]. Owlsfield Financial Corp states it flatly: 'A missing requirement or late bid can eliminate an otherwise strong proposal,' so acknowledge every amendment, submit before the deadline, and save the confirmation [LARqKW_L-QI @ 00:01:45]. Stronger Built reaches the same verdict on the $6.51M San Francisco RFP, where late submissions 'are literally not even considered,' missing one piece of minimum-qualification documentation is an 'automatic fail,' and failing to show good faith LBE outreach gets a bid rejected as non-responsive [EoNW1HDL3PI @ 00:06:01].

The third consensus point is that winning and operating are separate tests, and cash flow decides which one you pass. Karen Denton KSC frames the $100K, $250K, and $1M contracts that beginners chase against staffing, payroll, insurance, equipment, supplies, supervision, quality control, and cash flow, and asks one blunt question before any bid goes out: could you fund payroll for 10 employees before the first payment arrives, because 'You might have to pay your employees before your customer even pays you' [xeZ8_Jlv0AI @ 00:01:44]. GovKidMethod quantifies the gap on federal work as net 30 or net 60 plus roughly 30 extra days for new vendors, which means floating payroll is simply 'how government contracting is' [dr59GJ2T5v4 @ 00:18:03]. Owlsfield Financial Corp tells beginners to start with a manageable local job or subcontract to an established cleaner, to invoice exactly as the contract requires after award, and to 'Learn the process before committing to a contract your cash flow cannot support' [LARqKW_L-QI @ 00:02:17]. Sheenapreneur adds the labor side of capacity: build the crew before projects land so they have synergy, because when supervisors or crew quit, quality 'rolls downhill,' and residential-only cleaners get exposed by empty-building work that shows everything [hyUO_-BE-qc @ 00:02:17].

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Small Business Bid School runs the cleanest unit-economics build: 5 nights at 4 hours is 20 hours a week, times 4.33 weeks is about 86.67 hours a month, and a $22 wage loaded 25% for payroll taxes and workers' comp becomes $27.50 an hour, or roughly $2,383 a month in labor [uARedcLLS5c @ 00:00:38]. Add $180 supplies, $120 transportation, and $300 overhead for about $2,983 in cost, then divide by 0.8, not multiply by 1.2, to hit a true 20% margin at about $3,729 a month, because 'A 20% profit margin is not the same as adding 20% to your costs' [uARedcLLS5c @ 00:02:12]. victoria menifee builds from production rate instead: 25,000 sq ft divided by 2,300 sq ft per hour is about 11 hours, times 4 days is 44 hours a week, times 4.2 weeks is 185 hours, times $15 is $2,772, plus 15% payroll tax is $3,187.80, plus 30% overhead is $4,144, plus 20% profit lands at $4,972 [5htVyehKCRo @ 01:07:06]. The rule of thumb behind it is 50% labor, 30% overhead, 20% profit, and never below 10% profit [5htVyehKCRo @ 01:14:41]. Both builds load labor first and stack every other cost on top, and Small Business Bid School warns that holidays, absences, and supervision belong in the number before any margin is applied.

On federal work the wage floor is set for you. GovKidMethod prices a VA cemetery bid off a wage determination of $14.25 plus $4.54 health and welfare, about $18.79 an hour, then layers FICA, FUTA, and SUTA on top, and builds incremental increases into each option year [dOj6rxt15uE @ 00:31:22]. On Florentina's live Questa Ranger District bid the same operator hammers the hours trap: the SCA determination carries two weeks of vacation and two weeks of holidays, so there are 2,080 hours in a year 'but you can only bill the government off of 1920 hours,' and Department of Labor back-pay findings have hit 'hundreds of thousands' [dr59GJ2T5v4 @ 00:43:54]. Line items are priced per CLIN as unit times quantity: the monthly service CLIN times 12, strip and wax times 1, windows twice a year, and liners and paper flagged as government-furnished are left out of the price entirely [dr59GJ2T5v4 @ 00:15:44]. Kizzy Parks works a different determination of $16.20 an hour plus $4.98 health and welfare up to 40 hours, enters pricing both on the schedule page and on the SF1449, and divides the annual total by 12 because the agency pays monthly [UVPaRFj3LDE @ 00:09:03]. Following the notice on SAM daily is part of the mechanic, because one student's set-aside flipped to full and open midstream.

The technical volume is where operators separate from price-only bidders. Commercial Cleaning CEO's local proposal ran past 100 pages, two printed copies meant over 200 pages, and it packed a staffing plan, org chart, personnel handbook, quality control and biohazard procedures, a training roster, a green chemical chart, key procedures, equipment and consumables lists, a building-by-building schedule, and a commitment to APPA Level 1 out of five levels [j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:16:48]. Format rules are enforced literally; another client of the same operator mis-submitted on a flash drive and binder requirement. Sheenapreneur has won contracts on the quality-control plan alone, built around contingencies such as a 2-hour supervisor response, with green products named in both the capability statement and the proposal [hyUO_-BE-qc @ 00:22:45]. Kizzy Parks, who claims over $70M in contract wins, uses ChatGPT to draft the 'about us' section and a 200-word management plan, then rewrites it in the agency's own language and lists staff by name without resumes [UVPaRFj3LDE @ 00:13:51]. GovKidMethod attaches one merged PDF to a short email, PDFs the pricing sheet first, and fills forms with smallpdf.com at about $108 a year [dOj6rxt15uE @ 01:03:10]. Kizzy Parks instead combines the cover sheet and pricing into one PDF, attaches the DFARS and SF1449 separately, requests acknowledgment of receipt, and follows up after three weeks.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Register and wire the alerts
   - Register in every relevant vendor portal and in SAM, then set keyword alerts on custodial and janitorial.
   - Call city or town hall for vendor relations, register in the Vendor Portal with separate email tags for state, county, and city, and store each vendor ID and login securely [Commercial Cleaning CEO — j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:02:08]. For federal work, register the entity at sam.gov, expedite the CAGE code, and verify the bank account in SAM [Kizzy Parks — UVPaRFj3LDE @ 00:17:44].
2. Walk the site and interrogate the incumbent
   - Attend every site visit, count the work, and pull the incumbent's pricing.
   - Count spaces, restrooms, and floor types at the site visit [Owlsfield Financial Corp — LARqKW_L-QI @ 00:01:29]. Ask the CO who the incumbent is, the contract number, and the total value [Kizzy Parks — UVPaRFj3LDE @ 00:07:11], then divide the incumbent's monthly price by 4.3333 weeks to back out the real hourly wage [Bea's Business — 3vr4bkvaByo @ 00:01:12].
3. Build the number from labor up
   - Load wages, add supplies, transportation, and overhead, then apply margin by division.
   - Load the wage 25% for payroll taxes and workers' comp, add supplies, transportation, and overhead, and divide by 0.8 for a true 20% margin [Small Business Bid School — uARedcLLS5c @ 00:02:12]. On wage-determination work, price against 1,920 billable hours, not 2,080 [GovKidMethod — dr59GJ2T5v4 @ 00:43:54].
4. Assemble, acknowledge, submit, confirm
   - Acknowledge every addendum, submit in the exact required format, and confirm receipt.
   - Acknowledge addenda by title and date or enter 'none,' name files with the company name plus 'complete,' and download the submitted files to confirm everything is correct [Bea's Business — 3vr4bkvaByo @ 00:07:07]. Request acknowledgment of receipt and follow up after three weeks [Kizzy Parks — UVPaRFj3LDE @ 00:19:32].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest split is whether price alone decides janitorial awards. Sheenapreneur calls janitorial a "bottom feeder" market where agencies usually pick the lowest price, but adds that agencies burned by the lowest bidder will pay more, and that agencies have raised the operator's price for great work [hyUO_-BE-qc @ 00:08:07]. Commercial Cleaning CEO argues the opposite on local and state bids: read the whole document, because lowest price does not automatically win and a weighting formula decides the score [j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:06:25]. GovKidMethod backs that reading on a VA cemetery solicitation, where award goes to the "most advantageous" bid based on past experience and price, with prices compared against other bids, historical prices, the government estimate and market research [dOj6rxt15uE @ 00:10:33]. Kizzy Parks lands on both sides inside one walkthrough, first telling bidders price is the first criterion, then saying "experience in past performance are more important than price" [UVPaRFj3LDE @ 00:11:02]. For an operator, the split means the evaluation clause, not instinct, sets the bid posture. Where the notice scores on weighting or best advantage, the technical volume and staffing plan carry real points; where agencies have been burned by a cheap incumbent, Sheenapreneur's experience says a higher, well-documented number can still be the winning number.

The second fight is over the math itself. Small Business Bid School warns that "a 20% profit margin is not the same as adding 20% to your costs," and builds its worked example by dividing total cost of about $2,983 by 0.8 to land near $3,729 a month [uARedcLLS5c @ 00:02:12]. victoria menifee stacks percentages instead: labor of $2,772, plus 15% payroll tax to $3,187.80, plus 30% overhead to $4,144, plus 20% profit to $4,972, with a rule of thumb of 50% labor, 30% overhead and 20% profit and never below 10% profit [5htVyehKCRo @ 01:14:23]. The burden assumptions also differ: Small Business Bid School loads 25% for payroll taxes and workers' comp, while victoria menifee uses 15% for payroll tax. GovKidMethod's book model starts lower still, bidding around 15% and then adding roughly 10% through "earning multipliers" to reach 25% across base-plus-four contracts [ua_m84N0cwA @ 00:22:07]. Sheenapreneur anchors on the street rate instead: 10 to 30 cents per square foot in Georgia in 2024, with 4 to 5 cents seen in the wild, and warns that an extra $50 a month in profit may not be worth it [hyUO_-BE-qc @ 00:10:33]. Run the same job through both the division method and the stacked method before submitting.

Practitioners also split on how a newcomer should enter the market. Sheenapreneur says the preferred route is subcontracting to an experienced company [hyUO_-BE-qc @ 00:04:20]. GovKidMethod shows the limits of that route: one live ranger-district bid allowed no subcontracting at all, above the simplified acquisition threshold the prime must do at least half the work, and on the sub seat the verdict is blunt: "I think subcontractors got it really hard" [dr59GJ2T5v4 @ 01:09:42]. victoria menifee runs a different playbook, chasing no-bid routes such as micro purchases up to $10,000, sources sought and expiring contracts, claiming the average first contract takes 18 months against a 3 to 6 month target, and suggesting work in another state when the local market is thin [5htVyehKCRo @ 00:03:16]. Commercial Cleaning CEO pulls the opposite way on geography for local and state bids: agencies prefer local firms, out-of-state companies rarely win, and it helps when the owner personally attends the site visit [j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:08:13]. The same speaker treats federal work as "a whole different ball game," while victoria menifee builds the entire shortcut around federal micro purchases that require SAM registration and a bank merchant account accepting credit cards, with no PayPal or third parties.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most repeated firsthand trap is winning work the bank account cannot carry. Karen Denton KSC separates winning a contract from being able to operate it, and sets a blunt test before bidding: could you fund payroll for 10 employees before the first payment arrives, because "you might have to pay your employees before your customer even pays you" [xeZ8_Jlv0AI @ 00:01:44]. GovKidMethod puts numbers on the gap: federal payment runs net 30 or net 60, plus roughly 30 extra days for new vendors, so the contractor floats payroll, and the government never fronts money for resale, which leaves asking suppliers for credit against the purchase order [dr59GJ2T5v4 @ 00:18:03]. Owlsfield Financial Corp closes the loop after award: invoice exactly as the contract requires and keep cash for the next payroll, and learn the process before committing to a contract your cash flow cannot support [LARqKW_L-QI @ 00:02:00]. Commercial Cleaning CEO adds the timing squeeze on the front end, with about one week between walkthrough and due date on a proposal that ran past 100 pages, printed twice for more than 200 pages [j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:22:40]. Staffing up inside that window, with payroll leaving before the first check arrives, is where undercapitalized bidders break.

Labor-cost errors are the second failure mode, and they surface late. GovKidMethod warns that an SCA wage determination carries $4.54 an hour in health and welfare plus two weeks of vacation and two weeks of holidays, so the bid bills against 1,920 hours, not 2,080, and Department of Labor back-pay findings of "hundreds of thousands" have been seen; the verdict is "It's not funny at all" [dr59GJ2T5v4 @ 00:43:54]. Runjob Software flags workers' comp coded one rate per worker class as a common mistake, since the same laborer rates differently sweeping than working on the 30th floor or 16 feet underground, and the annual audit trues up whatever was estimated [0pkOO_oVhN8 @ 00:01:23]. Bonds compound it: premiums are stepped from around $7 per $1,000 of contract value, weaker firms pay more, and terms beyond 24 months add about 1% per month. victoria menifee admits misclassifying employees as subcontractors early on and warns it invites IRS complaints [5htVyehKCRo @ 01:11:21]. Small Business Bid School lists the quieter leaks: required wages, benefits, insurance and equipment in the solicitation, plus holidays, absences and supervision, because "a contract that looks profitable on paper can become unprofitable if you underestimate the work" [uARedcLLS5c @ 00:02:57].

The third cluster is losing on paperwork before price is even read. Commercial Cleaning CEO reports mandatory site visits where non-attendees are "immediately rejected," sometimes behind pre-approval or security clearance, and another client who mis-submitted against an explicit flash drive and binder format requirement [j4duUCt9Dlw @ 00:05:39]. Stronger Built walks through San Francisco's $6.51 million janitorial RFP, where failing to show LBE good faith efforts means rejection as non-responsive, skipped aggregates must carry exactly 1 cent in the price cells, late submissions "are literally not even considered," and missing one minimum-qualification document is an "automatic fail" [EoNW1HDL3PI @ 00:06:31]. Kizzy Parks tells bidders to follow the notice on SAM daily for amendments, after one student's set-aside switched to full and open, and to keep attachments small and request acknowledgment of receipt [UVPaRFj3LDE @ 00:09:39]. GovKidMethod shows the upside of pushing: an email to contracting forced an amendment adding a pricing schedule the agency had omitted, so the lesson is to email contracting often, then submit one merged PDF on a short email [dOj6rxt15uE @ 00:59:08]. The pattern across all four speakers is the same: compliance misses are binary, and no amount of sharp pricing survives a rejected envelope.
