## 1. Protect working float before buying capacity  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Begin with three bank-ledger buckets: operating cash, lender-owned deployable principal, and a restricted contingency balance. Never commingle customer escrow money with any of them; the settlement agent holds transaction funds under written instructions. The allocation below commits 60% of each startup budget to working capital, comfortably above the 50% minimum, but only two-thirds of that bucket is initially deployable. At $25,000, reserve $15,000 for float, including $5,000 held back, leaving $10,000 available for a transaction. At $100,000, the corresponding figures are $60,000, $20,000, and $40,000. A $75,000 purchase therefore exceeds even the larger launch's internal funding limit. Secure a documented outside capital commitment or decline the file; a polished proof-of-funds letter cannot create liquidity. Issue every proof-of-funds statement with an amount, expiration, conditions, and a source that actually exists. Keep contingency cash outside the ordinary wire queue, because a Friday exception can occupy capital through a holiday. This allocation is a planning model, not a claim that every state, insurer, or bank will accept the same startup expense. If required setup costs rise, reduce launch scope or increase capital rather than silently consuming the protected float.

Treat runway as a separate problem from principal availability. The 20% runway allocation supplies $5,000, $10,000, or $20,000 at the three capital levels. At the lean $2,000 monthly operating baseline, that buys 2.5, five, or ten months before revenue. The $25,000 launch therefore cannot safely assume the full four-month end of the stated one-to-four-month first-deal range: four months requires $8,000 of runway, creating a $3,000 gap. Resolve that gap through additional owner equity or a smaller validated operating plan before accepting a funding obligation. An illustrative $2,000 monthly envelope allocates $150 to software and communications, $250 to insurance, $400 to bookkeeping and legal retainers, $500 to contractor availability, $200 to bank and wire charges, $300 to sourcing, and $200 to contingencies. These are spending ceilings requiring quotes, not vendor invoices. Contractor availability buys defined tasks rather than unlimited labor from two people. Deal-specific counsel, capital charges, losses, and origination labor require additional transaction budgets. At $12,000 monthly overhead, even $20,000 of runway lasts only 1.67 months. Do not adopt the scaled expense structure before recurring collected fees can support it.

Size each funding lane against a cash calendar rather than an annual revenue target. A $10,000 advance earning a $300 transactional fee produces $300 of revenue when earned, not $10,300; principal repayment restores the float account. If the advance remains outstanding for three days, that is one completed capital turn, not evidence that ten identical turns will occur every month. Track requested release, actual release, expected payoff, actual payoff, and bank availability separately. A 24-to-72-hour double close requires verified unconditional end-buyer cash already on deposit and settlement instructions explaining why separate interim funding is still necessary. Conditional loan approval, pending ACH, and an investor's screenshot do not satisfy that condition. Earnest-money lending usually has a longer and more contingent recovery path, so cap it separately and never present it as equivalent liquidity. The 0%-APR corporate-credit lane receives zero assumed capacity until issuer terms expressly permit the proposed transfer. A $20,000 draw carrying a 3% transfer fee costs $600 before interest; a $500 earned fee already loses $100 before operations. If settlement cash cannot arrive through permitted rails within the cutoff, use documented equity or decline instead of manufacturing a card purchase.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make idle capacity cheaper than a bad deployment**
> 
> A small liquidity desk survives by preserving available cash and collecting clean fees, not by maximizing every possible turn.
> - Ask the settlement desk for its actual outgoing-wire cutoff and holiday calendar before quoting a funding window; price the occupied days rather than the advertised closing date.
> - Give every proof-of-funds letter a unique identifier and short expiration; prohibit reuse after the underlying capital is committed elsewhere.
> - Negotiate outside-capital availability before sourcing larger files, but count only binding commitments with documented draw conditions as usable capacity.
> - Set corporate-card capacity to zero when cash-equivalent treatment, transfer fees, issuer restrictions, or promotional expiration dates cannot be established from written terms.

## 2. Install twelve lean layers without automating away control  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Buy software only when it replaces a named task or preserves evidence that a bank, insurer, accountant, or settlement desk will need. The twelve-layer matrix uses exact tier names and budgeted unit prices, with $0 tiers preferred wherever commercial use is permitted. Paid prices are procurement assumptions, not a live price verification; confirm current terms, billing commitments, taxes, user minimums, and feature availability before purchase. Google Workspace Business Starter at $7 per user monthly on an annual commitment and Bitwarden Teams at $4 per user monthly on annual billing are the two paid baseline assumptions. Two individual users cost $22 monthly under those assumptions, leaving room inside the $150 software envelope for connectivity, storage, and service charges. Free software still creates configuration and supervision work. Self-hosted accounting should remain local or on a deliberately administered machine, never an improvised public server. A free mailbox is not the preferred fallback for settlement instructions. If cash is tight, remove a nonessential marketing subscription before removing individual business identities or hardware-backed authentication. The system of record is the transaction file and reconciled ledger, not the customer relationship manager or an automation tool's success notification.

Give every file an immutable deal identifier before intake, then carry that identifier through underwriting, proof-of-funds issuance, wire instructions, accounting entries, and payoff evidence. Store borrower identity, entity authority, business purpose, property address, purchase agreements, settlement agent details, and expected cash movements in a controlled folder. A form submission may create a checklist, but it must never authorize payment or generate an unconditional proof-of-funds statement. Keep complete bank account numbers out of CRM notes and ordinary chat. Restrict the wire instruction document to named personnel and retain an independent verification record. For a one-person launch, the second reviewer can be a contracted closing coordinator with a distinct identity and a narrowly defined review obligation; they do not need unrestricted banking access. Require a fresh callback when beneficiary details change, even if the email thread and attached letterhead appear familiar. Use a telephone number established through an independent source, not a replacement number supplied in the change request. Persist who performed the check, when it occurred, which details matched, and which exceptions remain. Automation should assemble this evidence and flag missing fields, while humans retain the release decision.

Build the payoff ledger before building a borrower portal. At minimum, record opening principal, additional advances, earned funding fees, earned origination points, contractual JV allocations, bank charges, returned principal, and closing principal by deal. Separate expected fees from earned fees and collected cash, because those dates can differ. Reconcile the bank daily during active funding and have the bookkeeper independently reconcile month-end totals. A $10,300 receipt against a $10,000 advance should clear principal and recognize the appropriate $300 fee, rather than inflate sales. Attach the settlement statement and bank confirmation to the same identifier. Back up the ledger and transaction documents daily to an encrypted destination, with a separate copy outside the primary account's administrative reach. Test restoration before first funding; a backup job's green icon does not prove the files can be recovered. Set access reviews whenever a contractor leaves, and periodically reconfirm that shared links have expired. When the stack hits a free-tier limit, compare incremental cost against saved labor and evidence quality. Upgrade the bottleneck rather than replacing the entire stack with an expensive loan-management suite whose unused servicing features add configuration risk without improving escrow certainty.

## 3. Separate the lending entity from adjacent fee engines  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Form the operating entity, obtain its EIN, document beneficial owners and authorized signers, and provide banks an accurate description of transactional funding and expected wire activity. Banks may reject a lending model despite easy online account opening; obtain acceptance before advertising available capital. Use counsel to classify each state and product rather than assuming that an LLC borrower establishes an exemption. Regulation Z excludes qualifying business-purpose credit under 12 CFR 1026.3(a), but that federal exclusion does not eliminate state licensing, usury, commercial-financing disclosure, or broker requirements. California Financial Code section 22100 illustrates why a commercial lender must examine licensing and specific exemptions instead of relying on marketing labels. A fixed repayment obligation can remain debt even when the document says JV. For insurance, distinguish your professional liability and cyber coverage from the settlement agent's fidelity, crime, and escrow-related coverage. Verify the agent's license where required, bond, insurer, limits, insured entity, and relevant exclusions directly through reliable channels. A title policy is not blanket wire-fraud coverage. Ask your broker specifically about social engineering, voluntary transfers, lender activities, deductibles, and claims conditions. Budget premiums and deductibles separately so an insured event does not exhaust operating cash immediately.

The mapped play b2b_commercial_vcc_interchange_rebate_arbitrage embeds automated AP and vendor settlement in freight brokerage, construction, medical supply, property management, or media agencies. Its 140–195 basis-point commercial-card rebate range is a negotiated program hypothesis, not a universal Visa or Mastercard entitlement. On $1,000,000 of eligible settled invoice volume, that range yields $14,000–$19,500 before sponsor allocations, platform costs, refunds, and contractual clawbacks. If the operator contractually retains 70%, the retained amount is $9,800–$13,650; principal-agent analysis under ASC 606 determines presentation, and invoice GMV never becomes operator revenue. At $100 million, the same assumptions produce $980,000–$1,365,000 retained before operating expenses, entirely outside this volume's canonical funding-fee cases. Obtain written rebate caps, reserves, eligibility, and settlement timing rather than promising uncapped economics. Inject accurate Level-3 invoice metadata only where supported; it does not compel card acceptance or guarantee the advertised rebate. For card-averse vendors, a genuine 1.5%–2% early-payment discount on a $100,000 invoice creates $1,500–$2,000 gross discount value before funding cost and contractual sharing. Focus on fragmented long-tail vendors, preserve an ACH fallback, and never route escrow principal through fictitious supplier invoices.

The second specified mapped play, litigation_finance_mass_tort_docket_securitization, requires separate capital, governance, and professional responsibility analysis. An Arizona ABS must satisfy Arizona Supreme Court Rule 31.1 and applicable licensing requirements; an ordinary marketing company cannot become co-counsel by contract. At $1,200–$2,500 CAC, 1,000 retained plaintiffs require $1.2–$2.5 million before overhead; 25,000 at the upper bound requires $62.5 million. That is institutional-facility territory, not this chapter's startup float. A 100-case illustration at $1,500 CAC costs $150,000; a hypothetical 2.5-times transfer price produces $375,000 proceeds and $225,000 spread before financing, verification, rejects, and legal expenses, only if the transaction is permissible and actually closes. Do not book hypothetical 35%–40% settlement-fee participation as current revenue or promise a buyer for client claims. The advertised $6.50 HITECH record figure is an optional flat-fee method for certain individual electronic access requests, not a universal third-party record-acquisition cap. Apply 45 CFR 164.524 and valid authorizations where required. Fund only genuinely EMR-verified retainers without buying prohibited referrals; diversify six to ten independently assessed dockets, recognizing their shared legal risks. An institutional transfer recovering 50% of the $150,000 cost returns $75,000 only when funded; until then, it provides no liquidity.

## 4. Prove readiness, then reconcile every scenario to earned fees  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Run a complete rehearsal before sending the first funding wire. Use a synthetic transaction with no real borrower secrets, a nominal bank-approved test where appropriate, and deliberately inconsistent beneficiary details to confirm that the workflow stops. Verify the settlement firm's identity, required license, bond, insurance, and bank instructions independently; then document unconditional end-buyer cash, the double-close sequence, disbursement authority, and the repayment waterfall. Obtain written instructions addressing an aborted closing, missed cutoff, and any inability to return funds. Never assume a settlement firm can reverse a completed disbursement because your agreement uses reassuring language. Require two distinct human checks on amount and beneficiary before bank release, with dual bank authorization where the account supports it. Where only the owner can release, retain the contractor's independent check and bank confirmation as compensating evidence. The control must survive vacation, illness, and a contractor departure. If the end buyer withdraws or the escrow cash is conditional, do not substitute an optimistic resale forecast for the funding condition. Decline or restructure the exposure as separately underwritten credit with its own documented capital source; do not force it through the short-duration transactional funding workflow.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the capital and authority packet
   - Approve the launch allocation and document the permitted operating scope.
   - Attach bank balances, capital-source agreements, entity authority, state analysis, insurance evidence, and a runway forecast covering the expected first-deal window.
2. Configure identities, records, and limits
   - Install the twelve layers with named access and restricted settlement records.
   - Assign deal identifiers, configure principal and fee accounts, require individual authentication, set wire limits, and document contractor review duties.
3. Rehearse an exception and a successful payoff
   - Test an altered beneficiary, a missed cutoff, and a principal-plus-fee receipt.
   - Record the rejected instruction, prove escalation reaches the owner, restore the transaction file, and reconcile the simulated payoff without treating principal as revenue.
4. Fund narrowly and review actual outcomes
   - Release the first eligible file within documented capital and settlement limits.
   - Perform both human checks, save bank confirmation, track actual cash availability, and review the first three live closings before increasing commitment limits.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Four conditions that must hold before the first release**
> 
> A complete file is necessary but insufficient: each condition must remain true when the bank instruction is submitted.
> - Capital check: reconcile available cash to the bank, reserve existing commitments, and demonstrate that the proposed wire leaves protected contingency untouched.
> - Authority check: confirm entity signing authority, bank acceptance of the business model, and documented state-specific treatment of the actual funding product.
> - Settlement check: independently verify the bonded settlement agent, beneficiary instructions, unconditional end-buyer cash on deposit, and written disbursement and repayment conditions.
> - Recovery check: complete a two-person wire rehearsal, restore the evidence packet from backup, and reconcile simulated payoff into principal and earned-fee accounts.
