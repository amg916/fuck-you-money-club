## 1. Convert every introduction into a documented, fundable closing packet  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A completed intake is not evidence of fundability. Obtain the title commitment, proposed settlement statements, payoff instructions, buyer funding evidence, and written escrow instructions before reserving capital. Use DocuSign envelope identifiers and document hashes to bind signatures to the specific packet; a signature badge alone does not authenticate its economic contents. Call the settlement office through an independently sourced number, confirm the agent's authority and applicable bond coverage, and record who confirmed the beneficiary account. Require unconditional, collected end-buyer cash on deposit, not a lender commitment, pending ACH, screenshot, or promised wire. Corporate cards enter the liquidity model only when the issuer expressly permits the transaction and the escrow agent accepts the payment rail. ## 2. Build a six-module ledger that serializes reservations and freezes uncertain wires  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The reference below is a compact, executable SQLite core rather than a payment provider integration. Concatenate its six code cells in numbered order into one Python file; they share a namespace and use only the standard library. Run one connection per worker on a local durable filesystem, never a shared network drive. SQLite WAL, foreign keys, a busy timeout, and BEGIN IMMEDIATE serialize every read-modify-write decision. Unique intake keys bind retries to identical payloads; conflicting reuse fails instead of silently replacing a borrower or amount. A second unique operation key protects each accounting event, while a globally unique bank reference prevents one confirmed movement from paying two deals. Integer cents eliminate floating-point drift. The schema also seals posted journal entries: no editing a closed batch to make a reconciliation look correct. Corrections require separately evidenced reversal entries. Module two applies a deliberately narrow production policy: approved lanes, positive fees, and a $40,000 single-ticket ceiling. It does not substitute an algorithm for state-law classification or settlement judgment. Those determinations enter module three as signed, attributable evidence, with their hashes preserved inside the frozen payment payload.

The capital reservation and payment request are committed together. With $100,000 contributed cash and a $20,000 protected buffer, active reservations cannot exceed $80,000; queued, claimed, ambiguous, and funded deals all consume capacity until settlement. Concurrent workers therefore cannot each observe the same unreserved dollars and spend them twice. The verification packet expires after ten minutes for dispatch purposes, and its digest locks the beneficiary reference to the amount. A changed account requires a new reviewed workflow, not an update to an existing outbox row. Module four claims a request once and calls an injected bank adapter with the persistent idempotency key. The adapter must return only a final, verified debit reference; a request-accepted response is insufficient. If the process crashes or times out, the reservation stays locked. There is intentionally no lease expiry that automatically resends a claimed payment. An investigator retrieves bank status using the original key and then confirms the original movement. Local race prevention is enforceable here; cross-system exactly-once movement requires the bank's idempotency contract or supervised resolution. Software must not claim that a database transaction can undo a wire already accepted by an external institution.

Module five records principal as a receivable and cash movement, then records earned fees only after the return is matched and the contracted service is complete. Its narrow settlement function accepts full principal recovery; partial returns and impairment require a separate reviewed exception journal instead of falsely closing the deal. Brokerage receipts have their own operation keys and supporting service references. The reconciliation view shows outstanding principal, net cash movement, and earned fees by deal; the trial balance also includes brokerage and contributed capital. Module six checks balancing, orphaned payment acknowledgments, terminal-state principal, and the reservation ceiling. Supply the independently obtained closing bank balance to its final comparison, including all opening capital and operating disbursements in the ledger first. These assertions test recorded consistency, not document authenticity or bank connectivity. Keep database ownership restricted to the service, encrypt backups, and retain signed daily exports outside the working machine. Before live use, exercise two-process reservation contention, duplicate callbacks, a crash immediately after bank acceptance, and a restored-backup reconciliation. The design resolves concurrent transitions atomically and treats uncertain external side effects as operational holds rather than opportunities for blind retry.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create and classify the immutable intake
   - Assign the business idempotency key and validate the proposed lane and cents.
   - Preserve source contracts and economic terms; conflicting retries must fail without overwriting evidence.
2. Verify the closing packet and reserve principal
   - Complete independent callback, buyer-cash verification, legal classification, and second-person review.
   - Commit the frozen evidence, payment request, and capital reservation in the same database transaction.
3. Dispatch once and establish the actual bank outcome
   - Use the original idempotency key and accept only a verified final debit reference.
   - Keep crashes, timeouts, expired evidence, and uncertain responses on hold without automatic retransmission.
4. Match the return and close the accounting evidence
   - Separate returned principal from earned fees and reconcile the bank cutoff.
   - Post balanced entries, investigate exceptions, run QA, and retain the signed daily reconciliation package.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Make the cash calendar stricter than the sales calendar**
> 
> Experienced operators protect liquidity through small operational habits that dashboards cannot replace.
> - Ask the settlement agent for the actual collected-funds cutoff and outgoing-wire cutoff separately; one does not establish the other.
> - Reserve expected peak principal by date and hour, including held transfers; monthly turnover can conceal a fatal afternoon cash shortfall.
> - Keep the independently verified callback number outside the incoming document packet so a forged packet cannot authenticate itself.
> - Export the outbox, bank references, and journal hashes before staff handoff; unresolved transfers remain assigned to a named investigator.

## 4. Close exceptions, prove the economics, and earn permission to increase throughput  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Run an exception meeting before releasing the next day's wires. The owner reviews every expired verification, changed beneficiary, unmatched debit, late payoff, disputed fee, and state-classification exception with the responsible contractor. A buyer withdrawal before release closes the queue through a reviewed cancellation procedure; the compact reference deliberately does not expose a convenience cancellation endpoint. After a possible submission, cancellation requires bank evidence establishing the actual outcome before freeing the reservation. A wrong-account concern triggers immediate contact with the originating bank's fraud desk and the independently verified settlement office, preservation of communications, and assessment of recovery or reporting obligations. Do not promise that a recall will recover a settled wire. Late principal stays in receivables and consumes capital; an impairment adjustment must carry evidence and cannot masquerade as an ordinary settlement. Refunds reverse the original earned-fee event through a new journal, preserving the original trail. For merchant services, a disputed obligation halts placement until independently resolved. If creditor activity brings a transaction within Regulation B, apply the business-credit notification provisions of 12 CFR section 1002.9 to the actual applicant and transaction rather than assuming commercial purpose removes those duties.

Assign work by control boundary, not by whoever is available. At Base, the full-time owner owns pricing, capital allocation, exceptions, and final cash accountability. One contractor assembles and independently verifies title and settlement evidence; another reconciles bank activity, service completion, fee rights, and daily journal exports. Separate verification from release wherever staffing permits, and use the second contractor or an authorized external reviewer when the owner assembled the packet. Shared credentials defeat that separation. Give each person an individual account, restrict beneficiary changes, require strong authentication, and store banking secrets outside intake documents. At Scaled, the second full-time role owns operations while three contractors cover verification, accounting, and specialist underwriting; the canonical five-person team does not justify unattended exception handling. Measure minutes per reviewed file, funded principal-days, rework by settlement office, actual callback completion, unresolved payment age, and fee realization after refunds. These indicators explain why revenue can rise without proportional headcount, while also exposing a brittle operation whose apparent efficiency depends on skipped checks. Review permissions after role changes and rehearse absence coverage before the principal decision-maker takes leave. An unstaffed control is a scheduling constraint, not an automation opportunity.

Close each month with a three-way proof: bank cash matches posted cash, escrow statements match principal receivables, and completed service agreements support recognized operator fees. Confirm the principal-versus-agent assessment under ASC 606 for each service arrangement; the scorecard's revenue basis already excludes principal and treats participation income as the operator's retained amount. Underlying property sale proceeds, merchant remittances, and loan principal never become revenue merely because they pass through an account.
