# Chapter 07: The Gator Method

*Gator lending vs. transactional lending, EMD funding and lender criteria, contracts for earnest money deposits, the seven Gator strategies, live-call deals and 200% returns, practitioner case studies, starting with little money, and critical reviews of the course and upsell concerns*

## Executive Summary

Gator lending, as Pace Morby teaches it, means funding the small, fast checks big lenders ignore, wholesaler earnest money deposits first, and charging a flat chunk instead of interest. The consensus economics: $1,000 to $3,000 out, $1,500 to $3,000 back, with headline deals such as $1,500 returning $5,000 and $5,000 returning $3,000 in ten days. Safety rests on getting onto the HUD, a contract clause routing the deposit back to the lender, signed seller releases and title confirmation by email and phone. Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez publishes hard terms: 5% or $350 upfront, EMD loans up to 60 days, transactional money at 2% plus 0.2% per day. Crushing REI counters that EMD is overrated and posted about 10% of the time in Michigan, while Tom McKay's guest spent close to $10,000 across the stack and saw EMD offers draw no engagement.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus - Gators Eat the Dead Carcass Big Lenders Ignore

Every Pace Morby session lands on the same definition: a gator is a transactional lender who prices money in chunks rather than annual interest and runs a high count of fast in-and-out deals [x24Yz464jLE @ 00:01:27]. The contrast is the whale who parks capital at 7-10% just to preserve it. Pace argues ten percent a year on a $1,500 earnest money check is not worth a big lender's time, which leaves small EMD deals as the dead carcass that gators eat. He runs the math on stage: ten percent on $5,000 is only $500 a year, while his probate deal pays $10,000 on that same $5,000, a 200% hit [7yVZPbb03bc @ 00:13:41]. The street version is a $1,500 EMD advance that returns $5,000 at close while the wholesaler banks $25,000, with one non-negotiable condition: 'as long as I'm on the HUD' [-9RSC5hpr9o @ 00:04:29]. Even Crushing REI, which openly questions how much money the model makes, defines it the same way: lend wholesalers their EMD and get repaid at close 'plus 12 15 20%,' possibly higher, with the speaker unsure what rate the course recommends [jDAbeOWsBX4 @ 00:00:54]. The chunk, not the rate, is the product everyone agrees on.

Demand is the second point of agreement. Pace says 85% of wholesalers lack the cash for earnest money, and his average gator check runs $1,000 to $3,000 with $1,500 to $3,000 coming back [iVg9EsSij2c @ 00:00:46]. The squeeze comes from listing agents, who will not let an investor get under contract without EMD; on a $200,000 house they want 1-3%, meaning $2,000 down today and $198,000 at close on a 30-day contract, and wholesalers juggling five, six, or ten contracts run dry [qbBAO0ozP-0 @ 00:00:59]. Market averages climb fast: Pace pegs Phoenix at $5,000, Nevada at $4,000 and LA at $15,000 [aIHVhFGaNAw @ 00:04:20]. Crushing REI confirms the borrower profile from the other side, noting that large wholesalers likely already hold $1,000, so the realistic targets are broke newbies [jDAbeOWsBX4 @ 00:05:16]. Pace frames the imbalance as millions of wholesalers against a few thousand gators, and tells people sitting on cash that the market is literally paying for access: 'people are paying 25 Grand to find your 25 Grand.' The consensus prerequisite is not a big bankroll; Pace puts the cash needed to start lending at roughly $1,000 to $1,500, with a no-money path through credit lines and wrestling other gators.

The third consensus point is that the deposit only stays safe if title knows who owns it. Pace's contract carries an irrevocable instruction in Section seven returning the deposit 'to the buyer or any person or entity designated by the buyer' during inspection, and he warns that once the agent submits commission instructions, title must already know the EMD is lent and expects a return [qbBAO0ozP-0 @ 00:07:40]. His best-practice script is to email title confirming the due-diligence end date, the seller release, and that the EMD returns to the lender rather than the buyer or assignor, then follow up by phone [T5jtdYhM6tQ @ 00:07:01]. Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez builds the same guardrails into a written criteria sheet: funding only inside the inspection period with at least 48 hours left, a written understanding from title or escrow, and the fee paid directly to the gator [min0RpX32uc @ 00:05:30]. Even the critic lines up here; Crushing REI calls EMD lending an unsecured loan and says the mitigation is seeing the contract, knowing exactly when due diligence makes the deposit non-refundable, and keeping communication tight [jDAbeOWsBX4 @ 00:04:02]. Pace and Abdiel both run proper lending paperwork and a JV agreement, with a title officer in the loop, as the baseline before a single dollar moves.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

The core Gator 1.0 waterfall is laid out with a $200,000 contract carrying a $2,000 EMD, a 30-day close and a 10-day inspection period [aIHVhFGaNAw @ 00:02:34]. The wholesaler assigns at $225,000 for a $25,000 fee; at closing the seller takes $198,000, the gator gets the $2,000 reimbursed plus a $5,000 chunk, and the wholesaler walks with $20,000 [aIHVhFGaNAw @ 00:10:00]. Pricing on a live call is relationship-based: repeat clients pay a flat fee around $2,000, first-timers pay roughly 30% of the wholesale fee. Justin, who stays under $500,000 purchase prices at 1% EMD so the deposit never tops $5,000, agreed to $3,000 flat going forward, while on the first deal Pace put in $2,000 and took $4,000 on top [A3l3Dg0cJLk @ 00:02:01]. Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez prices more formally: EMD loans capped at 60 days, a non-refundable upfront deposit of 5% or $350 minimum credited toward the fee, a fee calculated on calendar days, and typical fees of $3,000 to $4,000 on deposits in the $3,000 to $10,000 range, rarely over $15,000, with capacity up to $100,000 [min0RpX32uc @ 00:04:24]. On the house split Pace reported, a $20,000 profit left the house $6,500 and the gator $13,500.

Beyond EMD, the ladder moves into transactional money. Pace pegs the average double close at $400,000 priced at 1-3 points, or $4,000 to $12,000 for a single day [iVg9EsSij2c @ 00:02:40]. Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez prices transactional funding at 2% for the first 24 hours with a $2,000 minimum plus 0.2% per day, always funded to escrow, capped at seven days and wired in under 48 hours across all 50 states [min0RpX32uc @ 00:07:09]. Gator 2.0 as Pace teaches it on one stage is a referral play: send borrowers to myinvestorloan.com for 1 point, for example $2,250 on a $225,000 loan, with zero risk and a lifetime point on repeat loans; he says he referred $75 million last year and was paid $750,000 [aIHVhFGaNAw @ 00:15:20]. The advanced version raises money at 10% and lends at 16-18% plus 2 points, but only after an operator hits $15,000 to $30,000 a month and 15 to 30 deals. The bigger tickets come through escrow: Luis needed $300,000 parked in escrow as proof for a conventional refinance, the cash sat two days without moving, and the deal paid 2 points, 1 to the capital partner and 1 split between Dan and Aaron Kim [z2rCv8gn1cE @ 00:07:07].

Sourcing is a phone game. Pace's line is 'call a title company,' backed by Facebook wholesaler groups, YouTube comments and double closes, plus a three-part pitch that offers to underwrite deals, revive dead leads and provide transactional lending [7yVZPbb03bc @ 00:35:09]. Dan's version targets investor-friendly title shops after generic ones rejected the pitch; from a 250-name list roughly every tenth officer said they 'could have used you last week,' and one later emailed asking for $400,000 for two days [z2rCv8gn1cE @ 00:24:54]. Pace backs those relationships with about $5,000 a year in escrow-officer gifts such as Nordstrom cards and flowers, and claims one escrow officer can feed 50 lifetime deals. Capital for the chunks comes from credit: a student with poor credit got a $2,000 line in two weeks through Prime Corporate Services, and Pace funded his Justin deal off a PCS line, calling it a '200 return' in nine days [A3l3Dg0cJLk @ 00:04:58]. The tool stack splits by tier, with paceandabraham.com handling EMD and Alpha Gator handling double closes, DSCR, hard money and commercial loans, which Pace calls 'the shovel' rather than the training [_WfyIRKBE8o @ 00:01:29]. No-cash operators are told to JV, 'Gator wrestle' other lenders, and comment 'I have money to lend here's my Ig' on raise-capital YouTube videos.

1. Source Wholesalers Through Title and Escrow

2. Screen and Price the Request
   - Confirm the inspection window, collect an upfront fee and set a flat or percentage chunk.
   - Fund only inside the inspection period with at least 48 hours remaining and take 5% or $350 minimum upfront, credited toward the fee [Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez — min0RpX32uc @ 00:05:30]. Pace charges $200 to $1,000 upfront to filter bad deals [Pace Morby — T5jtdYhM6tQ @ 00:05:26].
3. Paper the Deal and Route the Deposit
   - Execute the JV and EMD loan documents and instruct title where the EMD returns.
   - Use a JV agreement, EMD loan agreement, assignment of EMD and signed mutual release [Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez — min0RpX32uc @ 00:02:59]. Pace's TC tells the attorney where the EMD returns under attorney-drafted gator docs [Pace Morby — A3l3Dg0cJLk @ 00:02:30].
4. Confirm With Title, Wire, Collect on the HUD
   - Email and call title, wire to escrow, and collect reimbursement plus the chunk at close.
   - Email title confirming due-diligence end date, seller release and lender routing, then follow up by phone [Pace Morby — T5jtdYhM6tQ @ 00:07:01]. Release funds only once the gator is on the HUD [Pace Morby — 7yVZPbb03bc @ 00:12:40].

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Lock the Release Before You Wire**
> 
> The money is made at contracting and at title, not at closing. These are the moves practitioners use to make sure the deposit comes back to the gator.
> - Have the seller pre-sign the title company's EMD release form at contracting and leave it in the file with a sticky note, because title policy can demand signed authorization even when the contract already allows the return [Pace Morby — qbBAO0ozP-0 @ 00:14:54].
> - In mutual release states such as Texas and Utah the seller must consent to release EMD, so Pace's contract has the seller waive that consent up front [Pace Morby — A3l3Dg0cJLk @ 00:03:40].
> - Before lending, ask the borrower how many deals they have completed; 25 completed deals is the signal Pace flags as worth funding [Pace Morby — OBl7C-hFLY4 @ 00:07:13].
> - Write a daily late fee into longer loans by taking the monthly fee, dividing by 30 and pushing it up a little; Pace's $65,000 first-position loan carried $500 a day [Pace Morby — S9WonQBbHG8 @ 00:43:59].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first fault line is whether earnest money is even the choke point. Pace Morby builds the entire Gator 1.0 thesis on it: agents will not let an investor get under contract without EMD, and on a $200,000 house that means $2,000 to $6,000 in cash on day one [qbBAO0ozP-0 @ 00:01:33]. Pace puts the share of wholesalers without EMD at 85%, with a typical outlay of $1,000 to $3,000 returning $1,500 to $3,000 [iVg9EsSij2c @ 00:00:46]. Crushing REI pushes back hard from a Michigan shop closing over 100 deals a year: they post earnest money maybe 10% of the time and never lead with it, because most sellers do not ask for EMD unless a realtor represents them [jDAbeOWsBX4 @ 00:02:35]. Their read is that EMD is overrated, an inspection contingency makes it almost always refundable, and a Gator has to hunt down wholesalers who consistently post deposits, which may be more common in other states or bigger cities. Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez splits the difference by widening the lane beyond wholesale EMD into fix-and-flip deposits, double closes and seller carryback, so a Gator is not dependent on one deal type to stay deployed [min0RpX32uc @ 00:02:38]. The operator takeaway is market-specific: test local contract habits before assuming EMD demand exists.

Pricing is the second split, and nobody quotes the same rate card. Pace Morby charges a chunk rather than interest: a flat fee around $2,000 for repeat clients and roughly 30% of the wholesale fee for first-timers, which on Justin's deal became $3,000 flat going forward and $4,000 on top of a $2,000 deposit for the first close [A3l3Dg0cJLk @ 00:01:05]. Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez runs a calendar-day model instead, collecting an upfront deposit of 5% or a $350 minimum that is non-refundable but credited toward the fee, with the fee paid at close of escrow from the assignment fee or a private money lender [min0RpX32uc @ 00:02:59]. On transactional money, Abdiel prices 2% for the first 24 hours with a $2,000 minimum, plus 0.2% per day, always funded to escrow, on terms capped at seven days [min0RpX32uc @ 00:07:09]. Crushing REI describes the play as repayment at close plus 12, 15 or 20%, and admits being unsure what rate Pace actually recommends [jDAbeOWsBX4 @ 00:00:54]. Whichever card you run, decide before the first call whether you sell a percentage, a flat chunk, or a daily meter. The spread between these models is wide enough that two Gators funding the identical $2,000 deposit can walk away with very different checks.

The third split is whose money goes into the deal and what it costs to get in the door. Pace Morby calls lending your own cash being lazy, and offers to fund a new Gator's first 10 transactions on a 70/30 split with 70% to the Gator [aIHVhFGaNAw @ 00:17:59]. When students complained that building credit takes too long, Pace told them to JV and Gator wrestle with capital holders instead, pointing at $180,000, $250,000 and $500,000 offers dropped into a single live chat [OBl7C-hFLY4 @ 00:01:44]. Tom McKay counters from the other side of the ledger: money is cheapest directly from a bank and private money lending is expensive, and the guest Mike, a Massachusetts police officer, stacked close to $10,000 across the sub-to course, the Gator program with lead-gen, two LLCs, tax help and corporate-credit coaching [cvQX0jcvIFc @ 00:17:05]. Pace's pricing ladder has the Gator price climbing to $5,900 with 3.0 and $7,900 with 4.0, with existing members paying nothing extra [_WfyIRKBE8o @ 00:03:22]. Tom's filter is blunt: do not buy anything that is not worth considerably more than you pay, and check Reddit and the BBB first. Mike also reports that the community is flooded with private-money requests driven by connector fees, which cuts against the idea that capital is the easy part.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

The most repeated failure point is getting the deposit back out of escrow. Pace Morby warns that when an agent submits commission instructions, the Gator has to make sure title knows the EMD is lent and expects a return, and on the Texas deal Stewart Title's escrow officer said policy requires signed authorization even if it is within the contract [qbBAO0ozP-0 @ 00:14:14]. The pivot was having the seller pre-sign Stewart's EMD release form, held with a sticky note, because Texas and Utah are mutual release states and the Section seven clause only works 90% of the time [qbBAO0ozP-0 @ 00:19:17]. On a $250,000 deposit, the attorney required notice to the seller before cancellation, so the team sent UPS overnight letters and posted timestamped notices on the door to recover the money, and the lesson Pace drew is that cancellation processes differ by title company [T5jtdYhM6tQ @ 00:09:18]. Abdiel Garcia Rodriguez builds the guardrail into intake: funding only inside the inspection period with at least 48 hours remaining, plus a written understanding from title or escrow, and a signed mutual release in the file [min0RpX32uc @ 00:05:30]. Skip any one of those steps and the money can end up paid to the assignor instead of the lender.

Borrower risk scales with the tier. Crushing REI calls EMD lending an unsecured loan aimed mostly at broke newbies, since large wholesalers likely keep $1,000 on hand, and reports that a buyer on one of their own assigned deals borrowed EMD and the deal still fell through because the buyer had no funding [jDAbeOWsBX4 @ 00:09:06]. Pace Morby labels second-lien renovation money, Gator 3.0, the most dangerous tier, using the example of a contractor named Juan taking a $60,000 deposit [aIHVhFGaNAw @ 00:24:36]. Roger and Diane Oquist lived it: an Oklahoma loan split three ways ended with about $1,000 lost after a non-judicial foreclosure that cost roughly $6,000 and took a couple of months, because with a deed-in-lieu all the liens follow the property while foreclosure clears them [S9WonQBbHG8 @ 00:32:39]. Their fix afterward was a maximum of one or two deals per borrower, lower ARVs, and weekly reports plus a timeline instead of monthly payments, after admitting they were not getting weekly or bi-weekly progress reports at the time [S9WonQBbHG8 @ 00:40:17]. A second $25,000 deal with the same borrower was recovered, but another borrower ran and turned out to be carrying a separate lien.

The economics checks cut both ways. Pace Morby's case for the chunk model is that 10% on $5,000 is only $500 a year, against $10,000 earned on a $5,000 probate deal, but building to that level takes about a year [7yVZPbb03bc @ 00:13:41]. Late fees are a real line item: Pace had $65,000 in first position for 11 days returning $30,000 with a $500-per-day late fee that had accrued about $7,000, and a Mississippi loan of $60,000 ran 35 days late at $150 a day for $5,250 in late fees and $15,300 total [S9WonQBbHG8 @ 00:44:44]. Fraud is the other check: on deals over $1 million, Pace says to physically visit the office or send a FaceTime proxy, with the California and Utah Attorneys General slated to present on fraud including fake title companies, and to charge $200 to $1,000 upfront because that fee also filters out bad deals [T5jtdYhM6tQ @ 00:10:08]. Tom McKay's audit is that roughly 90% of the success stories show no completed deals, and Mike says 'can fund EMD' posts draw no engagement [cvQX0jcvIFc @ 00:12:20]. Run the numbers against deal flow, too: the rule of thumb on the Oquist call is underwrite 10, you get one.
