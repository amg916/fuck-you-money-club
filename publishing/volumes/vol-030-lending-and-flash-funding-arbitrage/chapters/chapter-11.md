# Chapter 11: Bridge Loans & Lines of Credit

*Buy-before-you-sell bridge loans and their alternatives, investor and commercial bridge loans with exit plans, hard money vs. bridge, how bridge loans go wrong, business lines of credit, velocity banking, high-limit business cards, and closing the small-business funding gap*

> THE BIG MONEY PLAYBOOKS · THE FUCK YOU MONEY CLUB

## Executive Summary

Bridge lenders, brokers, and loan officers agree that a bridge loan is short-term, equity-sized debt underwritten on the exit, sale or refinance, which lenders ask about before almost anything else. Residential buy-before-you-sell bridges cap at roughly 70% to 80% combined LTV over 6 to 12 months, and one program advances 70% of value at 0% interest for a fee of 2.4% or $9,000, whichever is higher. Investor bridges run 12 to 18 months at 70% to 75% leverage, tiered by deal count and FICO. They are priced at 11% to 12%, or 1 to 3 points plus higher rates. Line-of-credit operators preach building capacity before a crisis and paying interest only on draws. The fault lines are three: whether a HELOC, recast, or sale contingency beats bridge debt nine times out of 10; the 70% versus 80% cap; and whether revolving business credit should ever fund real estate.

## Section 1: The Practitioner Consensus

The operators who originate and broker bridge debt agree on one idea that carries the whole product: a bridge loan is a short-term instrument sized on equity and underwritten on the exit. Easy Lending USA frames it as one subtype inside the hard money family. It is sized on the as-is appraisal with no rehab budget, runs 12 to 18 months, and is driven by the exit, whether a sale or a DSCR refinance [GcefOZxHIAg @ 00:01:06]. Michael Daniel Investments says every bridge loan has an exit and the lender asks about it before almost anything else. The same channel warns that both exits, sale and refinance, depend on other people performing [0EcBhBq5U08 @ 00:01:28]. MKK Capital calls the exit plan non-negotiable and ranks it after collateral, asset-type experience, and liquidity in its commercial review order [k0m3lOvAU7U @ 00:01:06]. Zach Smith, the COO interviewed on Rene Nelson's channel, puts it bluntly: "We look at exit first." The shop will even lend on as-stabilized value when the exit math holds [VIt86OFRY3Y @ 00:06:13]. The shared read is that collateral and the takeout path carry the file, while borrower income matters less than on a conventional mortgage. Speed is the product being bought, and every speaker prices that speed above long-term debt.

On the residential side, the consumer-facing lenders converge on one use case: buying the next home with a non-contingent offer before the departing residence sells. Jeb Smith, a Coldwell Banker agent in Southern California, says the whole point is to avoid contingent offers, which sellers pass over when multiple bids land. The same agent calls equity "the key component" behind roughly 80% combined LTV [xOBdb3zZB9Y @ 00:01:33]. Ryan Marks of SEI Mortgage caps combined LTV across both homes at 75%. The three triggers on that list are non-contingent offers, relocation, and speed, and the loan comes interest-only with deferred payments over a 6 to 12 month term [d8gbJrywXFE @ 00:01:40]. Steve Ferguson's standard bridge sits as a lien on the departing residence, in second position or first if the home is free and clear, at up to about 70% of value. Fees run higher because the bank has a short window to earn [AGFnuSOGL68 @ 00:01:10]. Tim Talks Real Estate agrees on the 6 to 12 month term and the hot-market, non-contingent fit, while calling bridge debt one of the most expensive short-term loans available [AkHsQo6uSuU @ 00:01:24]. Across all four, the cost buys the removal of the sale contingency, and equity in the departing home decides who gets in.

The line-of-credit operators share a parallel rule: build the capacity before the crisis. Fast Business Lending says you apply when cash flow is strong, not when you're desperate, and lock in a bank line ahead of trouble [-EPbq7NKBZY @ 00:04:00]. Korean Atlanta Mentorship claims 26 lines of credit totaling $494K, including a $280K HELOC, and gives the same advice: get credit before you need it [NgkU4dL1_sU @ 00:04:36]. The business-credit camp adds that lenders judge how the entity is set up, not just the owner. Velocity Channel's presenter CJ says lenders research the company online and that a gmail.com address gets the applicant written off. CJ adds that lenders date the business from the day its bank account opens, regardless of incorporation date [5IAt1l4ev5w @ 00:04:31]. Noelle Randall says approval needs a registered LLC, a business address, a dedicated phone line, and clean personal credit. Randall's summary is that when the business is set up the right way, banks will lend [UkLPuHL4krM @ 00:07:21]. The core mechanic all of them rely on is revolving access: interest runs only on what is drawn, repaid principal refills the line, and unused capacity sits in reserve at no cost. Bridge lenders underwrite the exit; line lenders underwrite credit score, time in business, and monthly revenue.

## Section 2: Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Steve Ferguson walks through the numbers on his preferred program, which is not a lien at all. It advances up to about 70% of value at 0% interest with no restriction on use. It also keeps the departing residence out of DTI, provided the home is listed within a set period after closing [AGFnuSOGL68 @ 00:02:07]. In the worked example, a $500K home at 70% equals $350K. Subtract $200K owed and $150K remains, which goes down on a $500K purchase, so the new loan is $350K. Any surplus at sale arrives as a separate check [AGFnuSOGL68 @ 00:04:22]. If the home sits unsold for four months, the program takes ownership and covers payments, taxes, insurance, and repairs. The fee is 2.4% or $9,000, whichever is higher. Eligibility is tight: conventional, FHA, VA, or USDA financing; five acres maximum with no exceptions; no subdivisions heavy with new construction; single-family only; good condition; and pretty good credit. Ryan Marks runs the combined-LTV version. A $1M free-and-clear home plus a $2M purchase caps borrowing at $2.25M under the 75% ceiling, with one loan on two properties and 1 to 3 points [d8gbJrywXFE @ 00:01:06]. RJ Baxter's 80% math differs: $500K times 80% is $400K, and subtracting $300K owed nets $100K [XH6IkQFi_8M @ 00:00:31].

Investor bridge pricing runs on experience tiers and seasoning. Easy Lending USA lends 75% to borrowers with two or more deals at FICO 620+, 72.5% with one deal, and 70% to new investors at FICO 650+. Loan sizes run from $115K to $8M, with cash-out around 65% [GcefOZxHIAg @ 00:03:53]. In the worked deal, a $400K duplex closes in 10 days, bridged at 75% for $300K, which leaves roughly $100K plus costs to bring, with a DSCR refinance as the exit. Seasoning decides the basis. Owned more than 12 months, the loan sizes off appraised value; owned less, it sizes off cost basis, so keep every receipt [GcefOZxHIAg @ 00:04:35]. Zach Smith quotes private-money economics from the lender seat. Investors expect 8 to 10%, so rates land at 11 to 12% with no index tie. Interest-only payments run roughly 1% of balance per month, and max LTV is 70 to 75%, constrained by cash flow. Minimum FICO is 540 [VIt86OFRY3Y @ 00:43:19]. Terms run 12 months for residential deals and 24 months plus a 24-month extension for commercial ones. Aravision Funding pitches the speed end of the market: closings in 3 to 5 days, interest-only, no prepayment penalties, no credit checks, no upfront fees, and a reply within one business day [4jdvXuchjXo @ 00:01:01].

On lines of credit, Fast Business Lending runs the cleanest math. On a $50K line with a $20K draw, interest applies only to the $20K. A bank line at prime plus margin costs about $583 over six months, while online lenders run $1,800 to $3,488. A "1.55% monthly fee" works out to roughly 31% APR [-EPbq7NKBZY @ 00:02:35]. Online lenders want 6 to 12 months in business and a score around 625; banks want two-plus years and 680+. The harder the underwriting barrier is to pass, the less the money costs. Velocity Channel's setup sequence is website first, then company-domain email at about $40 a month, then an EIN-backed bank account. The claim is that an owned domain moves approvals into five-digit lines of $10K to $50K+ instead of $3K to $5K [5IAt1l4ev5w @ 00:03:22]. Korean Atlanta Mentorship's velocity-banking play moves $137K of card debt onto a 7.5% line and ranks debts by cash-flow index, balance divided by minimum payment, lowest first. The full paycheck then goes into the line [NgkU4dL1_sU @ 00:06:40]. Noelle Randall reports approvals of $25,000, $35,000, or higher right out of the gate on cards like Chase Ink Unlimited, with 0% for 12 months and stacking across multiple Chase cards [UkLPuHL4krM @ 00:06:12].

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Lock the exit before you call a lender
   - Write down the primary exit (sale or refinance after stabilization) and a second option that fits current market speed.
   - MKK Capital treats the exit plan as non-negotiable [MKK Capital Commercial Hard Money Lenders — k0m3lOvAU7U @ 00:01:06], and Zach Smith's shop looks at exit first [Rene Nelson — VIt86OFRY3Y @ 00:06:13].
2. Size the advance against equity
   - Run combined value times the lender cap (70%, 75%, or 80%), subtract existing liens, and confirm the net covers the down payment.
   - Ferguson's math: $500K at 70% is $350K, minus $200K owed leaves $150K [The Mortgage Man - Steve Ferguson — AGFnuSOGL68 @ 00:04:22]; Marks caps combined LTV at 75% [SEI Mortgage - Ryan Marks — d8gbJrywXFE @ 00:00:46].
3. Price the money in APR terms
   - Add points, rate, origination, and any flat monthly fees, then convert to an annualized figure before comparing offers.
   - Bridges carry 1 to 3 points plus higher rates [SEI Mortgage - Ryan Marks — d8gbJrywXFE @ 00:04:46]; a 1.55% monthly fee works out to roughly 31% APR [Fast Business Lending — -EPbq7NKBZY @ 00:02:35].
4. Assemble the lender package
   - Bundle track record, team, a one-page financial statement, proof of liquidity, DSCR, and a pro forma.
   - Private lenders underwrite experience, track record, and team first, then financials and liquidity [Rene Nelson — VIt86OFRY3Y @ 00:55:37]; liquidity and reserves come right before exit in commercial review [MKK Capital Commercial Hard Money Lenders — k0m3lOvAU7U @ 00:00:49].

## Section 3: Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

Leverage is the first fault line. Steve Ferguson sizes the departing-residence advance at roughly 70% of value and keeps the old house out of the debt-to-income math entirely, which is why his favorite non-lien product lets a buyer pull $150K out of a $500K home owing $200K [AGFnuSOGL68 @ 00:02:52]. Ryan Marks runs combined loan-to-value across both properties and caps it at 75%, so a $1M free-and-clear house plus a $2M purchase tops out at $2.25M, and Marks sells the product to wage earners and self-employed borrowers alike [d8gbJrywXFE @ 00:00:46]. Jeb Smith puts the ceiling near 80% combined and calls equity the key component [xOBdb3zZB9Y @ 00:01:33]. RJ Baxter splits from all of them on qualification: Baxter quotes an 80% cap, runs $500K times 80% minus $300K owed to land at $100K, and says the borrower must qualify on all three payments, which Baxter calls almost impossible [XH6IkQFi_8M @ 00:01:02]. Ferguson and Marks pitch bridge money precisely because it dodges the debt-to-income wall; Baxter treats that wall as the reason to walk away. Operators shopping this product should expect the answer on leverage and qualification to change depending on which lender's desk the file lands on, and price the deal on the most conservative number.

The second split is whether to pay for a bridge at all. Tim Talks Real Estate calls it one of the most expensive short-term loans, says it is not a scam but whoever recommends it earns from it, and pushes two cheaper routes: write a sale contingency, or open a HELOC before listing and pay interest only on what gets drawn [AkHsQo6uSuU @ 00:03:06]. RJ Baxter stacks a longer menu: a HELOC near 80% with a question to the bank about prepayment penalties, a 401(k) loan or withdrawal, putting 5-10% down and recasting after the sale, a family loan or gift, or a buy-before-you-sell product that liens both homes and gets paid down at sale [XH6IkQFi_8M @ 00:02:53]. The counterargument comes from the offer side. Jeb Smith says the whole point is avoiding contingent offers, which sellers disfavor when multiple offers are on the table [xOBdb3zZB9Y @ 00:00:31]. Ryan Marks argues the HELOC route breaks down because a HELOC requires debt-to-income on every payment, while the bridge carries no DTI, defers payments, and runs interest-only as one loan on two properties [d8gbJrywXFE @ 00:02:38]. The working rule from Tim: ask what this would cost, because nine times out of ten a cheaper option surfaces.

The loudest disagreement is over credit lines as real estate capital. Fast Business Lending, a loan officer ranking seven products, draws a hard line: a line of credit is a short-term operating tool for gaps like Friday payroll against a 30-day invoice, and it is the wrong tool for purchasing real estate or building out a headquarters [-EPbq7NKBZY @ 00:03:43]. Velocity Channel pitches the opposite, saying it is easier to borrow a million than earn it, that a business with an existing $50K to $1M+ line can be bought for under $10K, and that the line can be used to buy real estate, with the claim that defaults on no-personal-guarantee lines stay off personal credit [J25bp-FJXOA @ 00:04:05]. Noelle Randall funds fix-and-flip rehabs on 0% business cards, ranking Chase Ink Unlimited first with $25K-$35K+ starting limits and stackable Chase cards [UkLPuHL4krM @ 00:04:32]. Korean Atlanta Mentorship runs velocity banking: move $137K of card debt onto a 7.5% line, rank debts by balance divided by minimum payment, and push every paycheck through the line, while openly admitting the projections are not built for realism [NgkU4dL1_sU @ 00:05:25]. Short-term working capital versus leveraged asset buying is the fork, and the speakers do not reconcile it.

## Section 4: Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

Exit failure is the trap every bridge speaker circles back to. Michael Daniel Investments says both exits, a sale or a refinance, depend on other people, and when the timeline slips the borrower is forced to pay for more time or sell in a hurry; the fix is building slack into the schedule and calling the lender early, before the squeeze arrives [0EcBhBq5U08 @ 00:02:24]. Easy Lending USA lists the four mistakes it sees: using a bridge for a big rehab, closing without a clear exit, ignoring seasoning, and treating bridge debt as long-term money. Seasoning bites hard because property owned under 12 months gets valued at cost basis, not appraisal, so missing receipts shrink the loan [GcefOZxHIAg @ 00:05:18]. MKK Capital calls the exit plan non-negotiable, ranking it behind collateral, matched experience, and liquidity, and expects a refinance after stabilization or a sale [k0m3lOvAU7U @ 00:01:06]. Jeb Smith names the residential version of the same failure: carrying a bridge creates pressure to sell below target, and the fees run higher than a HELOC with a mandatory refinance stacked on top [xOBdb3zZB9Y @ 00:04:23]. Speed costs extra, and Daniel warns a thin deal lets that premium eat the profit outright.

Rene Nelson's guest Zach Smith, COO of a private bridge lender, walks through the ways borrowers wreck their own deals. Rehab budgets are the first miss: unit turns run $25K to $30K, not the $5K to $10K that borrowers budget, and a plan built on the low number blows up [VIt86OFRY3Y @ 00:21:33]. Reserves are the second. In one deal a borrower diverted reserve money into another flip, and the lender had to cross-collateralize to save the deal [VIt86OFRY3Y @ 00:37:31]. The third trap sits in the loan terms themselves: Smith flags Dutch interest as a red flag, along with junk fees such as $250-500 draw charges and surprise inspections [VIt86OFRY3Y @ 00:53:05]. Pricing compounds the damage: Smith puts interest-only carry at about 1% of the balance per month, at rates of 11-12% set by what investors need to earn rather than any index. Smith also calls no-money-down structures a house of cards, and Nelson adds that lenders want roughly 40% down. Michael Daniel Investments supplies the interrogation that catches these traps before signing: ask the extension cost, whether there is a prepayment penalty, how draws are staged, and what exit the lender expects [0EcBhBq5U08 @ 00:03:02]. Wrong uses, per Smith, are thin margins, no exit, and borrowers already overleveraged.

On the credit-line side, the failures are pricing and profile mistakes. Fast Business Lending prices a $20K draw on a $50K line: a bank line at prime plus margin costs about $583 over six months, while online lenders run $1,800 to $3,488, and a 1.55% monthly flat fee works out to roughly 31% APR, which the loan officer calls a mathematical trap [-EPbq7NKBZY @ 00:02:35]. Velocity Channel warns that maxing personal loans and cards drags the score and makes consolidation loans expensive, and that personal credit should be guarded and used only to build wealth [J25bp-FJXOA @ 00:01:04]. The same channel's part two flags profile mistakes that cap limits: a gmail.com address gets a file written off, and business age counts from the day the business bank account opened, so a company incorporated ten years ago with an account opened yesterday started yesterday [5IAt1l4ev5w @ 00:04:31]. The Conversation Capital adds the funder's view: 72% of SMEs are not compliant, and funders reject applications because records are not well detailed or well pooled [nU-tvSUTP8g @ 00:51:07]. The check is simple: compute the real annualized cost of every draw, and fix the paper trail before applying.
