## 1. Define the earned-fee cohort before measuring yield  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Build the ledger around a funded transaction identifier, not the wire amount. For each transaction, record committed principal, principal actually released, contractual fee, earned date, fee settlement date, principal return date, capital provider, and beneficiary verification evidence. A $100,000 escrow advance that returns $103,500 contains $100,000 of recovered principal and $3,500 of potential fee income; it does not create $103,500 of revenue. Separate refundable deposits and unearned charges from earned fees. An origination point on a $100,000 facility produces $1,000 only when the contract and applicable accounting policy support recognition. Track principal as an asset and recovered principal as an asset conversion. Do not recognize uncollected interest merely because the bank statement shows a larger payoff request. Use QuickBooks Online or a comparable ledger with a transaction subledger exported from the settlement platform. ALTA settlement statements, executed funding agreements, and bank confirmations must reconcile to the same identifier. A proof-of-funds letter documents stated capacity, not cash already available for repayment. Revenue quality depends on enforceable entitlement, completed performance, and collection evidence rather than the apparent velocity of repeated wires. Report funded counts alongside canceled applications so the denominator cannot conceal underwriting waste.

The illustrative cohorts reproduce the canonical fee totals without inventing principal revenue. Conservative comprises 40 transactional closings at $2,000, 20 EMD participations at $1,500, and ten originations at $1,000: $120,000 across 70 fee events. Base comprises 100 closings at $3,500, 60 EMD participations at $2,500, and 35 originations at $1,000: $535,000 across 195 events. Scaled comprises 150 closings at $4,500, 80 EMD participations at $3,000, and 35 originations at $1,000: $950,000 across 265 events. These are reconciliation assumptions, not observed production or quoted market pricing. Count a fee event separately from a unique customer; a repeat sponsor can generate several events, while one closing can contain multiple separately documented services. Avoid billing the same service twice under different labels. The EMD assumptions require enforceable participation economics and eligible escrow conditions. Ordinary early-stage earnest-money advances often lack unconditional end-buyer cash, so they fail this platform's release standard. Exclude those opportunities rather than weakening verification to preserve the modeled count. If the eligible pipeline cannot supply 60 Base participations, reduce revenue explicitly and rebuild the cost waterfall; do not substitute unsigned JV expectations.

Resolve gross-versus-net presentation at the contract level. The scorecard uses the operator's earned transactional fees, EMD JV splits, and origination points before the modeled deductions. It does not authorize recognizing a partner's entire settlement entitlement. If a JV collects $5,000 but this operator owns a 50% participation, the modeled EMD fee is $2,500. Store retained_share as 0.50 for that contract and reconcile the other $2,500 to its owner. Where ASC 606 applies, evaluate principal-versus-agent presentation; lending interest and certain origination fees instead require analysis under the relevant lending guidance, including ASC 310-20. Do not force every funding charge into service revenue. Here, the fee cohorts already reflect the operator's entitlement, so applying a second participation haircut would understate income. The reversal allowance below reduces that earned-fee planning basis for expected concessions and failed settlements. Accordingly, the canonical margins use gross fee revenue as their denominator, not financial-statement revenue after contra-revenue adjustments. Preserve both views in the workbook with explicit labels. Review state licensing and interest characterization before quoting a $3,500 charge for three days: commercial purpose, a corporate borrower, or a fee label alone does not establish an exemption.

## 2. Reconcile every dollar from fee billings to owner profit  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Treat the canonical owner-profit figures as fixed reconciliation targets and the expense allocation as an explicit illustrative budget. Make contractor delivery labor measurable rather than hiding it inside a target margin. The Base $72,000 budget can represent 1,200 settlement-review hours at $40 plus 600 underwriting-support hours at $40, allocated between two contractors. Maintain two loss mechanisms because a canceled fee and missing principal have different economics. A cash reserve is a liquidity designation, not an additional expense when the same expected loss has already entered the waterfall. When an anticipated loss becomes a write-off, charge the established allowance and recognize only the necessary incremental adjustment. Likewise, paying a previously accrued refund settles a liability rather than creating a second deduction. At Base, $535,000 less $16,050 of reversals yields $518,950 of net fees before the remaining expenses. Dividing $227,375 by that net figure produces approximately 43.81%, which must not replace the scorecard's 42.5% gross-basis margin. Allocate the $75,000 Base capital budget transparently: 100 transactional events at $500 cost $50,000, 60 EMD events at $400 cost $24,000, and facility administration costs $1,000. These are negotiated-budget assumptions, not lending quotes. Avoid counting a capital partner's share twice when the contract already defines revenue net of that entitlement. Reconcile contractual payment priority before assigning any amount to revenue, financing cost, or partner payable.

## 3. Size principal locks separately from fee DSO and promotional credit  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

A profitable cohort can fail because principal returns after the next commitment becomes payable. Measure three clocks: principal lock from funding release to reusable cleared cash, fee DSO from earned date to collection, and facility repayment from borrowing to its contractual due date. The Base illustration assumes $100,000 average transactional principal for 100 events and three calendar days of lock. Average occupied capital is $100,000 times 100 times three divided by 365, or $82,192. Add 60 EMD events at $25,000 with ten-day locks: $41,096 average occupied capital. Their combined $123,288 average does not establish adequate capacity. Two simultaneous transactional closings require $200,000, and three simultaneous EMD positions require another $75,000. These overlap assumptions are planning limits, not measured percentiles. Weekend cutoffs, bank holidays, recording delays, and returned wires can expand calendar-day locks even when documents describe same-day funding. End-buyer cash must be unconditional, independently verified on deposit, and available under the settlement instructions before release strictly to a bonded title or settlement agent. Bonding does not replace verification or prove that a loss is covered. Confirm coverage limits, exclusions, and the identity of the protected party rather than treating a certificate as repayment.

Build the liquidity envelope from cash obligations rather than adding every balance-sheet metric together. Base cash operating deductions, excluding reversal and credit-loss allowances, total $270,175 annually. A 21-day expense runway therefore requires $15,544 rounded to whole dollars. Add the $275,000 simultaneous principal commitment and a separately designated $37,450 cash buffer matching the modeled reversal and credit-loss allowances. The resulting planning envelope is $327,994. This buffer is cash earmarked against already modeled expenses, not another profit deduction. With $100,000 of genuinely available equity, the remaining capacity requirement is $227,994; with $25,000, it is $302,994. Neither figure establishes that a lender will provide the facility. At five-day fee DSO, Base fee receivables average approximately $7,329 using $535,000 divided by 365 times five. Report that receivable as a collection indicator rather than automatically adding it to the expense runway, which already bridges operating payments. A daily cash schedule must capture exact overlaps, collateral restrictions, facility availability, and minimum balances. Never count money in a client's escrow account as unrestricted corporate cash. UCC account control concerns collateral rights; it does not itself authorize withdrawals or displace settlement instructions.

The aged_shelf_corp_tradeline_credit_stacking play must compare a reinstated entity with genuine, disclosed business borrowing, not treat entity age as operating history. Verify beneficial ownership, tax filings, actual revenue, existing liens, and the lender's permitted use of proceeds. A promotional $50,000 facility with a 3% transfer charge costs $1,500 even at zero stated APR. Repaying it over ten months requires $5,000 of monthly principal reduction plus the charge and any other contractual costs. If $20,000 remains when a 24% APR begins, one month costs approximately $400 of interest before daily-balance effects. Cash advances may carry immediate interest and a separate fee; a purchase promotion does not establish a usable wire facility. Do not simulate purchases, cycle merchant transactions, or overstate business history to create escrow liquidity. Compare the fully loaded cost with a transparent term facility: $50,000 held for 90 days at 18% simple annual interest costs approximately $2,219 before origination charges. Include only the selected funding route in the capital-cost ledger. If underwriting disclosures are inaccurate or repayment depends on fresh applications, halt applications, correct disclosures, and use a transparent term facility or reduce financed spending. Unused promotional limits are not committed settlement cash.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Create the dated funding packet
   - Join the signed agreement, settlement statement, and beneficiary verification to one transaction identifier.
   - Record earned fees separately from principal, partner entitlements, refundable deposits, and contingent charges.
2. Reserve daily funding capacity
   - Enter principal release, expected reuse, fee collection, and facility repayment on the same daily cash schedule.
   - Deduct existing commitments and restricted balances before calculating available capacity; apply the four-day delay stress.
3. Verify the settlement cash independently
   - Confirm unconditional buyer funds and authenticated wire instructions with the authorized settlement agent.
   - Use an independently sourced callback channel and retain the confirmation record; changed instructions restart verification.
4. Close the principal and fee ledgers
   - Match returned principal, collected fees, capital charges, contractor invoices, and any reversals after settlement.
   - Release unused capacity only when cash is reusable; update cohort DSO, lock duration, CAC, and allowance experience.

## 4. Stress the margin and quarantine slow bankruptcy recoveries  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Stress costs against the fixed fee cohorts before allowing growth assumptions to rescue the answer. Apply three simultaneous changes: fee reversals rise by two percentage points of gross fees, acquisition spending rises 20%, and capital charges rise 25%. Base incurs $10,700, $11,000, and $18,750, reducing profit by $40,450 to $186,925, or approximately 34.94%. Scaled incurs $19,000, $15,000, and $23,750, reducing profit by $57,750 to $464,750, or approximately 48.92%. These stressed outcomes are overlays, not replacements for the canonical scenarios. A separate uninsured $100,000 incremental principal loss beyond existing allowances would reduce unstressed Base profit to $127,375. It would exceed Conservative annual profit by $64,000. That concentration matters more than a small improvement in fee conversion. Separately, expanding Base transactional locks from three to seven days increases average transactional capital from $82,192 to $191,781, approximately $109,589 more. This is an average funding increase, not a sufficient peak facility limit. Recalculate simultaneous commitments and repricing before accepting delayed transactions; the capital charge stress does not automatically fund the extra principal.

The chapter11_trade_claim_convenience_class_rollup play belongs in a separate duration bucket because bankruptcy distributions do not replenish a 72-hour escrow facility. Consider a documented $10,000 trade claim purchased for $2,500. Assume an 80% probability of allowance and an applicable plan distribution of 60% of allowed face value. Expected receipts are $4,800, before timing adjustments. Subtract $2,500 purchase price, $400 verification and servicing, and $450 carrying cost, representing two years at 9% simple interest on the purchase price. Expected contribution is $1,450 over two years, not annual flash-funding income. The simplified allowance assumption treats disallowance as zero recovery; replace it with supported claim-specific outcomes when available. If the applicable recovery becomes 30%, expected receipts fall to $2,400 and contribution becomes negative $950. Obtain invoices, delivery evidence, debtor payment history, transfer documentation, and the operative plan rather than buying from Schedule E/F alone. A scheduled liability does not establish final allowance or a distribution entitlement. Under 11 U.S.C. 502, objections can change the allowed amount. Use PACER and the court-authorized claims agent to reconcile claim numbers, amendments, objections, voting treatment, and distribution status before committing acquisition cash.

Convenience-class treatment arises from the actual plan and 11 U.S.C. 1122(b), not from mechanically dividing a large claim into smaller invoices. Read thresholds, election deadlines, aggregation provisions, transferee treatment, and restrictions on claim splitting before modeling any enhanced recovery. Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 3001(e) distinguishes transfer procedures; for a qualifying transfer after proof of claim has been filed, file evidence of transfer and account for the transferor's 21-day objection period after the clerk's notice. Other transfer circumstances require their own procedure. Do not equate completion of that process with allowance or payment. If an objection or plan provision defeats the thesis, stop affected purchases, enforce documented seller recourse where available, and price only supported general-unsecured recoveries. Seller recourse is worth the seller's actual ability and obligation to pay. Keep these claims outside the three canonical fee cohorts; their inclusion would otherwise fabricate current revenue and conceal long-duration capital consumption. Monthly management review should reconcile published scenario profit, current cash availability, and downside exposure as separate outputs. Grow only after realized collection timing and delivery capacity support the next commitment. The durable edge is verified repayment plus disciplined capital allocation, not a spectacular annualized yield on money that cannot yet be reused.
