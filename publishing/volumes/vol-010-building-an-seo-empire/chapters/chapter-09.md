## 1. Attach an authority and evidence owner to every publishing surface  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start the portfolio register with domains, legal entities, audience locations, content sources, monetization partners, and the person who can stop publication. A general search publisher ordinarily needs no federal publishing license, but entity registration, assumed-name filings, local business permits, and tax registrations depend on its actual operations. Sector activity changes the boundary: selling insurance, arranging mortgages, practicing medicine, or providing individualized investment recommendations can trigger separate licensing and compensation restrictions. An affiliate label does not turn a regulated referral into ordinary advertising. For mortgage-related referrals, review RESPA section 8, 12 U.S.C. § 2607, before accepting payment tied to settlement-service business. For health content, distinguish editorial discussion from collecting symptom histories or transmitting leads to clinics. Washington's My Health My Data Act can reach consumer health data outside HIPAA-covered relationships. Maintain separate routing rules for sensitive topics instead of sending every visit into the same advertising stack. A multi-site portfolio should use one central rule register with domain-specific exceptions, named reviewers, effective dates, and supporting contracts. Do not represent Google Search eligibility, affiliate acceptance, or app-store distribution as a government license; each is a separate contractual permission that can disappear without changing the underlying statutory duties.

Build the content-rights ledger around 17 U.S.C. §§ 106, 107, and 204. Record the author, source material, signed assignment or license, permitted formats, territory, term, sublicensing rights, and evidence location. Paying a freelancer does not automatically transfer copyright, and commissioned articles do not automatically qualify as works made for hire. Use a present written assignment as the fallback, with identified preexisting materials separately licensed. Fair use requires a contextual four-factor analysis; a short excerpt or source link is not automatic permission. Public-domain cinema also requires title-by-title verification because restoration elements, music, translations, and artwork may carry separate rights. Programmatic pages should contain original analysis or genuinely licensed datasets rather than repackaged competitor text. For GEO citation capture, publish stable URLs, attributable research, revision dates, and reproducible measurements instead of manufacturing quotations or identities. Apply 15 U.S.C. § 45 and the FTC Endorsement Guides to affiliate recommendations and paid reviews: material connections belong where readers encounter the recommendation. FTC rules at 16 CFR Part 465 also address specified fake-review and testimonial practices. Retain screenshots showing the actual disclosure placement on mobile, not merely a policy page that a reader would have to hunt down.

Treat privacy as a data-flow decision, not a footer project. Under California Civil Code § 1798.100 and related CCPA provisions, determine coverage using the current thresholds, affiliated-entity rules, and actual processing activities. Covered businesses must distinguish service-provider processing from sale or sharing, provide required notices, and implement applicable opt-out rights, including recognized opt-out preference signals. Colorado and other states add their own applicability tests, sensitive-data requirements, and universal opt-out mechanisms. Wiretapping and session-replay exposure depends on the implementation, information captured, and governing state law; review California Penal Code § 631 before deploying third-party interception technology. A first-party-looking hostname does not settle that question. Children's services require separate COPPA analysis under 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506 and 16 CFR Part 312. Avoid combining children's audiences with general-purpose advertising profiles. Google Search spam policies, including scaled-content abuse and site-reputation abuse restrictions, are platform rules rather than statutes, but they can still destroy distribution. Use Search Console, crawl logs, and a versioned deployment register to connect traffic changes to releases. Disable disputed collection first, preserve minimal incident evidence securely, and investigate whether correction, deletion, partner notification, or another response is required before restoring the affected integration.

## 2. Make the contract allocate attribution, ownership, payment, and exit risk  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a master agreement plus a domain-specific order form for each sponsor, affiliate arrangement, dataset license, and production vendor. Scope should identify URLs or inventory classes, delivery dates, review rounds, allowed claims, placement duration, and what happens when a merchant changes its offer. State that editorial ranking remains independent unless a placement is explicitly purchased and labeled. Separate deliverable acceptance from search ranking: a contract can promise a published comparison and measured click reporting, not control over Google's indexing decisions. Define a fee trigger with the network event, attribution window, validation period, reversal reasons, reporting source, and payment deadline. For sponsorships, specify whether payment buys time, impressions, clicks, or a package, then write the corresponding makegood method. Do not let an ambiguous conversion standard shift every merchant operational problem onto the publisher. A narrow anti-circumvention clause can protect specifically introduced commercial relationships for an agreed period, but it should exclude documented preexisting relationships and ordinary consumer navigation. Reject clauses that pretend to own every future customer interaction. For affiliate programs, capture the accepted program terms and negotiated exceptions because a sales representative's enthusiastic email may not amend the controlling network agreement or authorize a restricted traffic source.

Write ownership provisions for both normal operations and a sale. The publisher should control its domain registrations, CMS accounts, source repositories, analytics configuration, original content, and assignable commercial agreements. A contractor may retain general tools, but the publisher needs durable rights to project-specific output and enough documentation to replace that contractor. Separate licensed datasets from owned research; many licenses prohibit redistribution to an acquirer or training use even when website display is permitted. Personal information is not unrestricted inventory merely because a contract calls it an asset. Limit processing purposes, recipients, retention, onward transfers, and subcontractors, with assistance for applicable access, deletion, and opt-out requests. Specify incident notification timing and the minimum facts the vendor must supply. For content generated with automated tools, require source review, factual verification, and disclosure of third-party restrictions without representing every output as exclusively protectable. Keep a rights manifest that travels with the asset purchase agreement. At diligence, buyers will test whether affiliate accounts, ad agreements, stock-image licenses, and audience records can actually transfer. Where assignment requires permission, identify that dependency early and model temporary revenue interruption instead of promising an uninterrupted switch on the closing date.

Allocate liability by risk rather than copying a vendor's standard terms. Define covered claims, defense control, settlement consent, insurance requirements, and liability caps with clear treatment of confidentiality, IP claims, data misuse, fraud, and unpaid fees. A twelve-month-fees cap can be commercially negotiable, but a tiny contract may leave major exposure uncovered; evaluate insurance exclusions and each party's ability to pay. Specify governing law, forum, escalation contacts, notice mechanics, and whether arbitration applies, including fees and emergency remedies. Reconcile contract reporting to the canonical scorecard without treating merchant checkout totals as publisher sales. Conservative revenue of $80,000 less $64,000 in modeled total costs leaves $16,000 at 20%. Base revenue of $490,000 less $330,750 leaves $159,250 at 32.5%. Scaled revenue of $900,000 less $495,000 leaves $405,000 at 45%. These are annual whole-business cases, not incremental promises from any single play. Conservative and Base use 1 FT + 3 C; Scaled uses 1 FT + 3 C. Allocate contract administration, rights review, reversals, and monitoring within those modeled costs. If actual vendor commitments break the envelope, revise the operating plan rather than quietly changing the canonical profit figures.

## 3. Rebuild the three sensitive plays around permission and verifiable value  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

For identity_graph_pixel_arbitrage, treat a vendor's claimed 35–55% resolution rate as an unverified proposition, not permission to identify visitors. Across 10,000 anonymous bouncing visits, that claim implies 3,500–5,500 matches; it says nothing about accuracy, lawful collection, permitted sharing, or incremental profit. Reject covert matching of IP or device signals against email-hash lookup collections to expose names, LinkedIn profiles, or home addresses. A CNAME endpoint does not convert an outside recipient into the publisher, and SHA-256 hashing does not make linkable personal information anonymous. Use aggregate search-intent reporting or affirmatively enrolled first-party audiences instead. Illustratively, 10,000 visits producing 200 enrolled subscribers, with 10% purchasing and a $12 validated commission, yield $240 publisher revenue before acquisition and service costs. Do not buy or exploit bidstream data to reconstruct individual browsing histories. The proposed fallback also needs correction: physical mail falls outside CAN-SPAM's email rules, but remains subject to applicable privacy, consumer-protection, and sector restrictions. Hashed advertising-audience uploads still require appropriate rights and platform permission. US-only edge filtering reduces geographic scope but proves neither residence nor authorization. Suppress consumer cold-email campaigns from this workflow, honor applicable opt-outs, and prohibit vendor reuse through enforceable processing restrictions.

For coupon_checkout_tab_under_injection, build legitimate checkout-search interception around merchant-approved offers and explicit user action. The legacy identifier does not authorize tab-unders, forced redirects, hidden frames, or attribution cookies placed without a genuine referral. A useful deal page identifies the merchant, eligibility, expiration, exclusions, verification date, affiliate relationship, and what a click will do. Negotiate an exclusive code that provides a real benefit, then confirm whether code attribution, click attribution, or another rule controls commission. Example: 1,000 user-initiated outbound clicks at 20% validated conversion produce 200 orders. At $80 average merchant order value, underlying sales equal $16,000; an 8% commission produces $1,280 publisher revenue. If the publisher owes shoppers 30% of that commission, the $384 cash-back expense leaves $896 before content, support, reversals, and tax treatment. Document the assumed accounting presentation and adjust it if the contractual principal-agent analysis differs. Neither $16,000 nor $896 replaces the commission revenue line in this illustration. Explain cash-back eligibility, validation delays, and cancellation handling before enrollment. When a code expires or merchant permission ends, remove the claim promptly and switch to a clearly described public offer or an unpaid link. Never substitute fake reveal buttons or claim that merchant permission alone eliminates attribution disputes.

For ctv_mfa_programmatic_supply_arbitrage, treat the proposed fleet of 50–200 utility, ambient, public-domain cinema, weather, or casual-gaming channels as a hypothesis requiring staged validation. Roku, FireTV, Samsung Tizen, and mobile distribution each have separate developer, advertising, content, and privacy terms. Buyer CPMs of $18–$35 and audience acquisition quotes of $0–$2 describe different denominators unless the contract explicitly aligns them. Illustratively, one million eligible ad opportunities at 70% paid fill and a $24 gross cleared CPM produce $16,800 in buyer spend. A 25% supply fee leaves $12,600 publisher receipts; subtracting $2,000 acquisition expense leaves $10,600 before content rights, streaming, support, measurement, and other costs. Use gross or net recognized revenue only after examining principal-agent terms; do not automatically book buyer spend. Prohibit hidden playback, manufactured impressions, device spoofing, and simulated remote activity. Use genuine viewer check-ins and stop requesting ads when sessions become inactive under the agreed measurement standard. Maintain accurate app-ads.txt entries, seller identities, and supply-chain declarations; these documents do not establish human attention by themselves. Diversification toward six or more independent SSPs and direct agency PMPs is a concentration target, not permission to resell through undisclosed paths. Launch fewer channels until rights, audience quality, and net collections survive reconciliation.

## 4. Use truthful scripts and a repeatable launch-to-exit evidence routine  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use commercial email only after documenting the source of the contact, relevance, applicable jurisdiction, and suppression checks. CAN-SPAM, 15 U.S.C. § 7704, applies to commercial B2B email as well as consumer messages. It requires accurate routing information, nondeceptive subjects, advertising identification where required, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out honored within ten business days. Do not charge for opting out or require more than the permitted identifying information. Keep the mechanism available for the required period and monitor vendors acting on the publisher's behalf. This federal framework does not override other applicable privacy or sector rules, and other countries may require prior permission. A permission-first merchant opener is: 'Commercial partnership inquiry: I publish independent buying guides for readers comparing your product category. Would you like a proposal for an accurately disclosed placement or a genuine exclusive discount? We do not use hidden referrals or promise search positions. Reply stop to end commercial messages.' Before sending, append the actual sender identity and valid postal address from the approved company signature. Suppress requests across the sending stack, not just one salesperson's inbox. Avoid scraped consumer lists and do not switch to automated calls or texts without separately assessing the TCPA and applicable state rules.

Run discovery as an attribution and truthfulness interview, not a pitch for guaranteed traffic. Read this verbatim: 'Which entity signs the agreement, which products and jurisdictions are included, and who can authorize the offer? What event earns our fee, how long does attribution last, and which cancellations reverse payment? May we use your brand assets on the named pages, and which claims have supporting evidence? What happens to accepted orders if the program ends?' Then ask: 'Will any data leave our sites beyond ordinary referral measurement? Identify each recipient, purpose, retention period, and deletion process before we consider integration.' For a sponsor, use: 'We sell the described placement and reporting package, not editorial conclusions. Readers will see the sponsorship relationship alongside the content. We can correct factual errors without giving a sponsor control over independent comparisons.' For CTV demand, ask: 'Which inventory types, attention rules, geography, seller paths, and invalid-traffic adjustments will you accept in writing?' Record answers in the order form rather than relying on the call recording alone. If a prospect requests fabricated reviews, undisclosed endorsements, or silent tracking, offer a contextual sponsorship or decline the incompatible component. Keep the remaining legitimate transaction available instead of treating every disagreement as an all-or-nothing negotiation.

Run a four-step evidence routine before launch and after material changes. First, inventory the proposed pages, claims, rights, data flows, and counterparties. Second, sign the controlling agreements and resolve unsupported permissions. Third, test the rendered experience and event chain using controlled accounts and minimal test data. Fourth, reconcile reporting, suppression, rights expiry, and cash collections on a scheduled basis. Use a Playwright mobile screenshot test for disclosure placement, Search Console for indexing signals, and a controlled referral test against Impact or Awin reporting when the program permits testing. Do not generate fake purchases or billable ad impressions to prove the system works. Record exceptions, owners, remediation dates, and the evidence needed to reopen suspended activity. For an exit, create a rights and contract index before sharing commercially sensitive documents, restrict access by diligence role, and avoid exposing raw audience records merely to demonstrate traffic. The buyer's first useful question is whether reported earnings can continue under transferable permissions. Preserve aggregate cohort reports, settlement reconciliations, signed assignments, and incident summaries that answer it. Review retention requirements before deleting records; retain necessary tax, dispute, and contractual evidence while removing personal information that no longer has an appropriate purpose.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Map the proposed release
   - List affected domains, claims, content inputs, vendors, recipients, and payment events.
   - Assign an owner to every rights, privacy, disclosure, and attribution decision; flag sector-specific activity before making commercial promises.
2. Secure documented permissions
   - Execute rights assignments, commercial terms, and necessary processing restrictions.
   - Record controlling versions, signed exceptions, assignment dependencies, termination rights, and the exact event that creates publisher revenue.
3. Test the actual experience
   - Verify mobile disclosures, genuine referral actions, opt-out handling, and approved inventory behavior.
   - Use controlled nonbillable tests where available; preserve screenshots and event records without manufacturing sales or advertising demand.
4. Reconcile and maintain the evidence
   - Review settlements, reversals, permissions, vendor changes, and retention on a recurring schedule.
   - Suspend defective integrations, document remediation, and update the buyer-ready rights index without exposing unnecessary personal records.
