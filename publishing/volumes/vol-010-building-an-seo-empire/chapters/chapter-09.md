## Build the hit-versus-helped comparison at page level

BSM - A Denver SEO Company ([2jejTy2fWTg @ 00:02:13]) begins its post-update examination in Search Console with total impressions rather than clicks alone. The explanation is practical: improved rankings do not necessarily put a page immediately into the leading position, so visibility can change before clicks show a comparable movement. In the demonstration, the speaker then examines individual pages, finding a recently updated SEO cost guide among the winners and an older SEO packages guide among the losers. Julian Goldie SEO ([HWSpTVX7qMI @ 00:03:46]) describes an agent prompt that saves clicks and average position by page for the pre-update week, then compares the same pages after completion plus seven days. True SEO ([y6yAJ9Ocl3U @ 00:03:59]) adds a restrained monitoring routine: check Search Console daily, watch clicks and impressions, and open the pages tab to identify what moved. These are complementary demonstrations, not identical date selections: BSM's shown comparison uses a period before the update concluded, whereas Goldie's workflow explicitly anchors its baseline before the update started. Retaining that difference prevents the chapter from silently changing what BSM actually demonstrated. The common operational emphasis is to identify the affected pages and the measurements that changed before choosing which content deserves attention.

Kate Smoothy | SEO Specialist ([zS5xebRUE5Y @ 00:09:49]) moves the investigation beyond the site's own chart by asking what an AI overview actually says when traffic or sales fall. The example concerns an overview highlighting a competitor's free delivery: if the business also offers it, the suggested response is to compare how prominently that information appears on each page. Reviews provide another example of information that might be present for a competitor but missing from the business's product page. Edward Sturm ([kroDk8LuKtY @ 00:11:07]) addresses a different interpretation problem: a site with very few clicks can show large percentage movements because each click represents a substantial share of its total. Authority Hacker Podcast ([ePxZx_vQNFo @ 00:37:04]) adds that third-party traffic estimates can fluctuate because of the tool's calculations or updates, rather than an equivalent change in actual site traffic. The speakers therefore describe distinct checks for distinct readings: inspect the overview's product comparison, consider the size of the click total, and examine what a traffic-estimation graph represents. Smoothy's proposed page changes address missing purchasing information, not an assumed penalty. Sturm's advice addresses volatility at low volume, while Authority Hacker's warning concerns the measurement source rather than the site's content.

Julian Goldie SEO ([s_GIhK4WE18 @ 00:06:20]) demonstrates turning Google's helpful-content questions into five grading criteria with ChatGPT, then applying them to a page to obtain improvement suggestions. The later instruction is to prioritize pages associated with the largest pre-versus-post losses, rather than automatically rewriting the entire collection. In the newer workflow, Julian Goldie SEO ([HWSpTVX7qMI @ 00:04:37]) asks an agent to answer each self-assessment question with yes, partly, or no, quote the supporting line, and provide one specific fix. Tim The SEO Guru ([cOL8sezUyEQ @ 00:04:32]) supplies the timing discipline around that assessment: confirm completion through the search status dashboard, record the dates, and wait a full week before analyzing the comparison. Authority Hacker Podcast ([ePxZx_vQNFo @ 01:07:28]) brings the audit back to what competing pages now do better and whether search intent has changed since the old content was written. Together, these instructions support a page-specific explanation of the proposed edit: which answer is missing, what supporting evidence is absent, or which part of the page no longer serves the query. Goldie's grading output is a content-assessment workflow; the speakers do not present its score as a measurement of Google's own ranking calculation.

## Compare recovery proposals without flattening the disagreement

Edward Sturm ([kroDk8LuKtY @ 00:08:18]) plays Craig Campbell's account of a site populated with 1,200 AI articles that gained traffic, was hit roughly six months later, and did not recover after the team replaced the content. That testimony concerns an attempted recovery that failed, not merely a recommendation about what to change next. WordsAtScale ([mpV06kMELhI @ 00:02:55]) instead shows automated review pages containing real screenshots, internal links, comparisons, pricing screenshots, and other review components, and attributes the project's continuing performance partly to that workflow. Julian Goldie SEO ([s_GIhK4WE18 @ 00:03:57]) highlights the wording that content may be written or reviewed by an expert, while warning against changing a publication date without improving the content. Tim The SEO Guru ([cOL8sezUyEQ @ 00:01:28]) likewise distinguishes AI assistance from large quantities of lightly reviewed pages, rewritten ideas, and unsupported claims. These accounts give the operator concrete differences to examine in the production process: what was reviewed, what original material was added, and what the page helps a reader decide. They also retain different outcomes. Campbell's unsuccessful replacement exercise is not converted into a rule that every domain is permanently damaged, and WordsAtScale's surviving workflow is not substituted for evidence of recovery on Campbell's site.

Kasra Dash ([I26NAT6T6Gs @ 00:02:50]) says to prioritize services that make money and have search demand before expanding into supporting articles. The criticism is directed at businesses publishing many peripheral posts while leaving their actual services underdeveloped. Dash's source-context method starts with what the business sells, whom it serves, and the attributes it wants associated with its name; buyer questions then become supporting pages linked back to the service. Koray Tuğberk GÜBÜR ([DoFTx8z5X2g @ 00:05:16]) examines a different level of coverage: important concepts, their connections across the site, and how competitors use definitions, evidence, and research around them. The demonstration asks not just whether a term appears, but how it is connected within the information graph. WordsAtScale ([mpV06kMELhI @ 00:00:50]) similarly says breadth without depth is insufficient and calls for comprehensive topic coverage plus thorough treatment of subtopics. These speakers are not all prescribing a larger publishing count. Dash narrows the starting map to the business; GÜBÜR investigates meaningful conceptual coverage; WordsAtScale emphasizes depth. Their proposals can therefore be reported as different questions about the existing content network, without treating Dash's video title as a rejection of every form of topical organization or treating concept coverage as permission to publish unrelated pages.

Surfside PPC ([0_SAQbGnl3Y @ 00:02:47]) explains its affiliate-site losses partly through the composition of the results: retailers and niche stores supplied pricing, delivery, and returns while the speaker's site remained purely affiliate. Later, Surfside proposes starting an e-commerce site, even using dropshipped products, as a possible response to continued non-recovery. Authority Hacker Podcast ([ePxZx_vQNFo @ 01:00:02]) offers an important contrasting examination of another affected site: author credentials and additional expertise displays had not produced recovery, while competing results did not necessarily display stronger credentials. Bill Mcintosh ([UGKO4HDAk8w @ 00:04:10]) explicitly describes E-E-A-T as a quality framework rather than one ranking score or a switch. True SEO ([y6yAJ9Ocl3U @ 00:03:00]) turns experience into visible work: add actual jobs, photographs, numbers, mistakes, fact-checking, and a real name to AI-assisted drafts. The result is a set of attributed proposals rather than a single recovery formula. Surfside's suggested business-model change responds to its own long-running losses and observed competitors; the other speakers ask what evidence and usefulness appear on the page. Authority Hacker's case also prevents an authorship box from being presented as sufficient merely because several other speakers recommend genuine authorship as part of their content checks.

## Repair demonstrated barriers and keep recovery claims specific

Kasra Dash ([yzNo7jJ8WBE @ 00:05:37]) demonstrates a page-level indexing investigation with Detailed SEO, checking indexability and whether the canonical points to the intended URL. The same audit expands into Screaming Frog for bulk inspection and later examines architecture, internal links, and pages that are several clicks from the homepage. True SEO ([y6yAJ9Ocl3U @ 00:03:37]) includes fixing anything the crawler cannot read among its eight proposed improvements, alongside merging thin pages and linking strong pages to revenue pages. Authority Hacker Podcast ([ePxZx_vQNFo @ 01:09:41]) places technical audits, internal linking, and site structure among the ordinary work that site owners often postpone until an update forces attention. These recommendations make technical inspection a concrete part of diagnosis without requiring a claim that the update specifically targeted canonicals or site architecture. Dash shows a website undergoing a rebuild, with deleted pages and incoming articles that explain some visible errors. That context matters: the audit demonstrates how to locate barriers on the site being examined, not a completed core-update recovery. The operational question is whether important pages can be reached, read, and indexed as intended before attributing their lost visibility solely to a change in how content is ranked against competitors.

Kasra Dash ([yzNo7jJ8WBE @ 00:22:54]) reruns a speed audit after caching-plugin adjustments and shows its score moving from 57 to 64. Earlier in the demonstration, image resizing and optimization are discussed; afterward, mobile rendering and Search Console's crawl information receive attention. The reported improvement is an audit-score movement, not a demonstrated return of lost search traffic. Bill Mcintosh ([UGKO4HDAk8w @ 00:09:25]) warns that useful improvements may take months to be fully reflected and describes deletion as a last resort rather than the automatic response to a large decline. Koray Tuğberk GÜBÜR ([DoFTx8z5X2g @ 00:01:27]) describes repeatedly updating content, including a six-month routine for the project shown, with revisions responding to query, topical-distance, and user-behavior changes. These are different kinds of progress in the speakers' accounts: a measured technical adjustment, a warning about interpreting recovery timing, and an ongoing content-maintenance practice. Keeping them separate avoids presenting the improved speed score as proof that a ranking loss has been reversed. It also preserves GÜBÜR's emphasis on continuing the work through both gains and losses, rather than changing the entire strategy whenever Search Console shows an emotionally encouraging or discouraging movement.

Kasra Dash ([yzNo7jJ8WBE @ 00:31:00]) prioritizes critical access and visibility barriers first, then errors on important pages, with slow assets and less urgent linking work following those problems. The demonstration exports errors into a Google Sheet and adds completion checkboxes so the work can be followed. Bill Mcintosh ([UGKO4HDAk8w @ 00:09:40]) gives an equally precise interpretation of the manual-actions report: an empty report means no human-issued penalty is listed, not that rankings cannot change for another reason. Authority Hacker Podcast ([ePxZx_vQNFo @ 01:10:10]) warns against stopping all productive work while trying to identify the perfect explanation, recounting a site that returned during a later update after its owner had spent months inactive. Edward Sturm ([kroDk8LuKtY @ 00:12:14]) asks owners facing major losses to inspect the hardest-hit pages honestly, especially whether they satisfy intent quickly and clearly above the fold. The combined closing discipline is specific: distinguish the error actually found, the repair actually completed, the content improvement proposed, and the subsequent search movement. That preserves the practitioners' practical recommendations without claiming that a completed worksheet, an empty manual-actions report, or a better page automatically explains the original drop or establishes that recovery has already occurred.
