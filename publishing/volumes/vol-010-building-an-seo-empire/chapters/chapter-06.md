## 1. Build an evidence queue before building another site  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one commercially coherent audience, such as operations managers migrating field-service software, rather than a collection of expensive keywords. Build a weekly evidence queue from Google Search Console exports for properties you control, Ahrefs or Semrush competitor research, vendor changelogs, public pricing pages, and support forums that permit observation. Capture the triggering URL, observation date, buyer task, existing result quality, prospective merchant, and available monetization contract. Search volume is a screening input; it does not establish demand that your site can capture. Inspect actual results for dominant vendor documentation, video carousels, discussion threads, shopping features, and answer summaries that reduce outbound clicks. A promising gap combines recurring pain, a weak answer, and a merchant willing to pay for qualified outcomes. Separate advertiser prospects from acquisition targets and editorial topics in the CRM, while linking all three to the same evidence record. This prevents a software vendor from being counted as both a signed sponsor and an acquired asset. Use Airtable or a spreadsheet initially; retain dated screenshots of change signals and direct source links rather than copying whole competitor articles into your production system.

Public records reveal ownership events and budget triggers that keyword tools miss. SEC EDGAR filings can identify product discontinuations or acquisitions affecting a software category; SAM.gov award notices can identify public-sector purchasing activity where an integration guide serves an observable workflow. State business registries help verify the entity behind a publisher, but registration alone does not establish website ownership. Require authorized analytics access, domain-control evidence, and matching payment records before treating a seller as qualified. Read public release notes for forced upgrades, API deprecations, seat-price changes, and export restrictions. Those events create specific editorial assignments and timely sponsor conversations. For example, a dated announcement retiring a connector supports a migration checklist and a targeted introduction to three replacement vendors; it does not support claiming that every affected customer intends to switch. For GEO citation capture, publish reproducible tables, dated methodology, and original measurements that another system can attribute. Record observed citations separately from referral sessions and commercial conversions. Neither a generated answer mentioning your site nor an estimated keyword position belongs in booked revenue. Retain the underlying evidence even when the original announcement changes, subject to copyright and permitted archival use.

Score each opportunity out of 100 before spending production money: demonstrated buyer urgency earns 25 points, feasible distribution 20, verified monetization 20, original evidence advantage 15, counterparty reliability 10, and manageable rights and maintenance obligations 10. Assign points using written anchors, not enthusiasm. Full monetization credit requires a current commission schedule or a named sponsor decision-maker with a budget conversation; an affiliate directory listing earns only partial credit. Advance scores of 75 or more into a bounded pilot, investigate scores from 60 through 74, and archive lower scores with a reopening trigger. Treat missing ownership proof, prohibited traffic sources, fabricated review requests, and unavailable publication rights as hard stops regardless of the total. Limit the first sprint to two topical clusters and one monetization partner per cluster so failures remain diagnosable. A $1,200 research-and-production pilot producing three verified guides costs $400 per guide before promotion and maintenance. Require a credible path to recovering that cost from actual fees, not merchant transaction value. Review scores monthly as rankings, partner terms, and buyer workflows change; a stale high score is not a reusable investment thesis.

> **INSIDER PRO-TIPS — Exploit evidence gaps rather than keyword-tool optimism**
> 
> Experienced publishers preserve proof, distinguish channels, and price editorial maintenance before promising commercial inventory.
> - Diff public vendor pricing pages weekly; a changed minimum seat count often creates a stronger comparison angle than a headline price change.
> - Use Search Console page-query pairs to find an existing page deserving an update before commissioning a second page that competes with it.
> - Keep an original benchmark workbook and recording date beside every software demonstration so a sponsor can inspect the method without controlling the conclusion.
> - Interview the seller's largest revenue partner before valuing an acquisition; contract transferability can matter more than the site's reported traffic multiple.

## 3. Run an evidence-led cadence from raw lead to signed pilot  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Use a cohort funnel rather than an undated contact count. For an illustrative first campaign, research 200 raw organizations, qualify 80, reach 60 through verified permitted business channels, receive 18 substantive replies, hold ten discovery conversations, issue six scoped proposals, and close three paid pilots. The resulting rates are 40% raw-to-qualified, 75% qualified-to-reached, 30% reached-to-reply, 55.6% reply-to-discovery, 60% discovery-to-proposal, and 50% proposal-to-signed. Overall raw-to-signed conversion is 1.5%. These are planning benchmarks for a tightly researched cohort, not measured industry averages. At that same hypothetical yield, roughly 667 raw records would support ten signatures in expectation; actual sourcing should proceed in small cohorts rather than assume linear performance. Count a win when a commercial agreement is executed and its required initial payment clears. Track affiliate-program acceptance separately until approved commission revenue arrives. An acquisition letter of intent remains a pipeline event, not a completed purchase. Keep advertiser signatures, publisher purchases, and commission-producing partnerships in separate funnels. Combining them obscures cycle times and lets low-value account registrations masquerade as commercial traction. Review rejection reasons weekly before increasing outbound volume.

Run a restrained four-touch sequence over roughly three weeks, stopping immediately after an opt-out or a clear refusal. On day one, send a concise business email naming one observed trigger, one audience problem, and one proposed paid test. On day five, share a useful evidence excerpt through the same channel or an appropriate professional network, without attaching a fabricated performance claim. On day twelve, offer a short call with a specific agenda: audience fit, eligible conversion, attribution, budget owner, and payment terms. On day twenty-one, close the loop and explain what changed in the underlying research. Use accurate sender information and subject lines, a valid postal address, and a clear opt-out for covered commercial email under 15 U.S.C. § 7704. Honor opt-outs within the statutory period and suppress them across campaigns. Do not scrape private communities or automate prohibited platform messaging. For inbound, publish a methodology page, a sample audience report, and a sponsorship brief stating available inventory and measurement limits. Ask only for contact information needed to evaluate the request. Route replies to a named operator within two business days, and retain only records supported by your stated retention schedule.

Make the first proposal small enough to evaluate and specific enough to reject. Offer a $1,500 sponsorship covering one independently produced guide, one original demonstration, four weeks of identified placement, and a source-attributed report, provided your production budget supports that scope. At $900 direct delivery cost, the pilot contributes $600 before shared overhead; three identical pilots produce $4,500 revenue and $1,800 contribution, not annual owner profit. When a buyer says 'your traffic is small,' show relevant query coverage and propose a bounded audience test rather than inventing reach. When they demand 'guaranteed rankings,' offer contracted deliverables and observable reporting instead. When they request editorial control, distinguish factual corrections from control over verdicts and disclose paid placements. When attribution is disputed, reconcile link parameters, eligible events, attribution windows, reversals, and reporting timestamps before discussing expansion. Specify cancellation, ownership of commissioned materials, reuse permissions, invoice timing, and remedies for missed deliverables. Reject any requirement for deceptive endorsements. A signed pilot becomes renewable inventory only after delivery evidence, commercial reporting, and payment reconciliation agree. Use that documented result to request a relevant introduction, not an undisclosed positive public review.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Collect and score a dated cohort
   - Research 200 organizations within one defined software buying workflow.
   - Attach evidence URLs, separate prospect types, apply the 100-point rubric, and retain the reasons for every exclusion.
2. Prepare proof before sending outreach
   - Build one sourced comparison excerpt and one original workflow demonstration.
   - Confirm rights, commercial disclosures, accurate calculations, merchant eligibility, and the precise pilot deliverables.
3. Execute the measured four-touch sequence
   - Contact qualified prospects on days one, five, twelve, and twenty-one when appropriate.
   - Stop after refusal, maintain suppression records, and report replies and meetings against the reached-contact denominator.
4. Close, deliver, and reconcile
   - Sign a bounded pilot, collect the required payment, and deliver its evidence report.
   - Reconcile placement delivery, approved merchant actions, reversals, direct costs, and renewal interest before expanding.

For undervalued publishing assets, source from direct owner conversations, specialist brokers, public sale listings, and neglected sites already ranking for your chosen workflow. Value verified transferable cash generation, not screenshots or seller-adjusted excitement. Inspect authorized Search Console and analytics data, reconcile payment records to reported revenue, and segment traffic by page, geography, source, and device. Check whether three pages, one merchant, or one seasonal event explains most earnings. Review content ownership, contractor assignments, licensed media, domain control, backlinks, manual actions where visible to the owner, and merchant contract transfer restrictions. That calculation excludes taxes, reinvestment, and resale proceeds. Stress the largest traffic source and partner separately before combining shocks. Use escrow, staged transfer verification, and explicit asset schedules rather than sharing personal credentials. Preserve clean source records for a future media exit: buyers should be able to reproduce earnings and rights diligence without depending on the founder's memory or continued account access.

Sequence the 6–24-month window around evidence milestones rather than a promised traffic curve. In months one through three, validate one audience, instrument reporting, and publish the first original research cluster. Through month six, refresh pages from observed queries and build relevant merchant relationships. During months six through twelve, pursue the first paid pilots where audience evidence supports them; through month twenty-four, expand toward ten commercial wins only when delivery and contribution remain measurable. Use the $15,000–$120,000 startup capital range to set a spending limit, not to justify exhausting cash before a signal appears. At $5,000 monthly cash burn, $30,000 funds six months before reserves, taxes, or acquisitions. Automate technical site audits, content refresh queues, and revenue reporting, but require human review of factual changes and commercial representations. Review copyright permissions, endorsement disclosures, and applicable privacy duties before each new data collection workflow. Publish clear correction channels and maintain a partner concentration dashboard. When rankings fall, pause expansion, diagnose affected templates and intent, and retain cash for useful updates rather than manufacturing links or bulk content. The portfolio earns its next investment through reconciled outcomes, not the size of its publishing queue.

> **OPERATOR GATE — Verify four conditions before committing money or commercial promises**
> 
> A strong opportunity score cannot compensate for missing rights, unreconciled earnings, prohibited participation, or an unworkable pilot.
> - Verify publication rights, original test evidence, dated pricing sources, and accurate affiliation language before launching comparison pages or software recordings.
> - Verify current affiliate eligibility, reversal rules, attribution windows, placement permissions, and payment terms before including projected commissions in a pilot budget.
> - Verify platform participation rules and relationship disclosures; never sell identities, favorable reviews, editor permissions, or claimed algorithmic influence.
> - Verify signed scope, affordable delivery cost, required initial payment, and a reproducible reporting method before counting a commercial win or expanding production.
