## 1. Define the expensive job before choosing the tool  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Start with one buyer, one recurring task, and one costly failure. A useful wedge is not AI for small businesses. It is a weekly exception queue that tells a store operator which checkout changes broke a documented customer journey and assigns the repair. Write down who owns the queue, who pays, how often the problem returns, and what happens when nobody fixes it. Interview ten buyers in the same segment rather than ten friends with different businesses. Ask them to show the last incident, the time spent, and the current workaround. Record observed labor separately from guessed lost revenue. A problem with a named owner and a repeated budget consequence deserves another test; a vague wish for better software does not. Keep that distinction visible in every prospect record.

Gate one passes only when five of those ten buyers independently describe the same recurring job, identify its owner, and provide a recent example. This is a chosen operating threshold, not an industry conversion benchmark. Save a short evidence record for each account: incident date, workflow, time consumed, current tool, and purchasing role. Do not count a survey response as an interview or count two colleagues as two independent accounts. Ask what they have already tried and why it failed. If the pain is seasonal, collect evidence from the last active period instead of pretending it occurs weekly. If fewer than five accounts meet the rule, narrow the segment or change the job. Do not relax the threshold because a founder likes the proposed feature or has already purchased infrastructure.

Translate pain into a modest price hypothesis before presenting a demo. Suppose an operations lead documents four hours of weekly rework at a loaded cost of forty dollars per hour. That is roughly six hundred forty dollars across four weeks, not proof of thousands in recoverable sales. A proposed two hundred dollar monthly subscription must remove enough of that work to justify switching and ongoing attention. Ask what portion can actually disappear, because review and escalation may remain. Keep promised savings below measured addressable effort until a pilot proves otherwise. This framing also rejects weak wedges: a task worth twenty dollars monthly cannot support expensive onboarding and a high support burden. The best early buyer can explain the purchase to a manager without repeating the founder's enthusiasm or mentioning a model brand.

## 2. Join installation changes with verified complaint themes  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The saas_technographic_churn_pixel_scraping play builds a weekly account change ledger from public commercial DNS TXT and CNAME records plus visible DOM script tags. Capture the hostname, observed vendor signature, timestamp, and prior observation. A new tag may indicate a trial; a missing tag may reflect a tag manager change rather than cancellation. Require two observations and a second independent clue before labeling a change meaningful. Never infer private contract terms from DNS alone. Use buyer confirmed annual start dates to prioritize Month 10 and Month 11 conversations, then check the actual notice deadline because some contracts require earlier action. Without a confirmed term, describe the installation change as a conversation trigger rather than a renewal fact. Keep collection light, honor access restrictions, and avoid login walls or hidden account data.

The review_gating_reputation_extortion_funnels identifier names a pain discovery play here, not permission to suppress reviews or pressure a merchant. Mine verified one star G2, Capterra, Shopify App Store, and Google Business Profile reviews through permitted access or licensed exports. Preserve the source, date, verification signal, product version when known, and a short factual complaint label. If a platform does not verify purchase or identity, mark that uncertainty rather than manufacturing certainty. Cluster complaints about missing alerts, broken exports, slow handoffs, and opaque billing. Exclude insults and demands unrelated to a fixable workflow. The FTC Consumer Review Rule addresses fake reviews and specified review suppression practices; do not fabricate praise, condition incentives on sentiment, or threaten publication to force payment. Sell compliant monitoring and workflow replacements, never reputation hostage taking or selective review routing.

Gate two passes when ten accounts in the chosen segment have a current, documented signal and five relevant owners confirm that the signal maps to the job from gate one. Join account evidence, not personal dossiers. A public complaint from an agency serving large retailers may not describe a small merchant's budget or workflow. A tool removal can mean the company shut down, consolidated vendors, or changed its website. Ask the buyer which explanation fits before pitching a replacement. Rank accounts by evidence freshness, fit, and a reachable owner rather than raw scraping volume. Keep suppressed outreach addresses out of new campaigns and delete stale research that no longer serves the test. The output is a small qualified queue with explicit unknowns, not a giant list dressed up as validated demand.

## 3. Turn a reproducible diagnostic into a demonstrated workflow  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

The ssl_ada_wcag_compliance_scanner_outbound play starts with a narrow technical observation, not a legal accusation. On public pages where scanning is permitted, run a low rate headless browser with Axe-core rules tagged for WCAG 2.1 AA and its underlying A requirements. Record the tested URL, viewport, browser version, rule identifier, affected element, and reproduction steps. Inspect public TLS certificate validity and hostname matching, then validate structured data syntax and required properties against the intended schema. Treat these as three separate diagnostic families. A valid certificate does not establish secure application design, and valid schema does not promise search visibility. Do not probe authenticated routes, submit forms, or run intrusive vulnerability tests without explicit authorization. Redact personal information from screenshots and retain only the evidence needed to explain the observed defect to its owner.

Manually reproduce every defect used in outreach, then explain the limits in ordinary language. Automated accessibility checks cover only part of accessibility; keyboard operation, focus behavior, labels in context, and assistive technology journeys require additional evaluation. ADA Title III, including 42 U.S.C. section 12182, addresses discrimination by public accommodations. Whether a particular website falls within an applicable duty depends on facts and jurisdiction; an Axe result is not a court finding. Offer a verified technical report rather than an ADA certification or a litigation forecast. Show one broken journey and one proposed repair workflow. If the product only detects and routes issues, say so. Never imply it repairs the underlying site automatically unless the pilot actually performs that repair and the customer can inspect and reverse the change safely afterward.

Gate three passes when five target owners use a live diagnostic or clickable workflow prototype and independently complete the core task without the founder narrating every click. A prototype may be static, and delivery may be manual; neither requires a multi-tenant backend. Ask each participant to locate the defect, assign an owner, and recognize a verified resolution. Measure elapsed time, mistakes, and the remaining manual work against the buyer's current process. Agree on one pilot success measure before discussing payment, such as cutting weekly exception triage from ninety minutes to thirty while preserving review quality. Record failures as product evidence rather than filtering them out of the demo. If buyers admire the report but cannot identify a recurring owner or a repeat use case, stop treating the scanner as a subscription business.

> 
> A verified defect earns a factual conversation, not leverage through fear.
> - Name the tested page and scope.
> - Separate technical findings from legal conclusions.
> - Demonstrate the recurring workflow before proposing recurring fees.

## 4. Enforce four demand gates before shared backend development  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Gate four requires five to ten distinct target buyers to sign a paid pilot agreement or a substantive letter of intent after seeing the live diagnostic or clickable workflow. Five is the minimum; ten improves evidence diversity without turning discovery into indefinite research. Define a substantive letter as a named buyer, named budget owner, proposed price, scoped workflow, intended start date, success measure, and explicit purchasing conditions. A friendly email saying interesting does not count. A letter may be nonbinding and may never convert, so list it separately from signed paid pilots, collected cash, and active subscriptions. Do not write multi-tenant backend code before this threshold. Even after passing it, release engineering time in small increments against the shared workflow rather than using five signatures to justify a sprawling feature roadmap.

Offer a thirty day paid pilot with one workflow, a fixed account scope, a delivery schedule, and a clear end decision. For example, charge five hundred dollars for setup and supervised weekly exception reporting, with a proposed two hundred dollar monthly subscription after acceptance. These are test prices, not market averages. Specify whether the pilot fee is refundable or credited, what access is needed, who performs repairs, and how customer data is returned or deleted. Collect payment according to the signed terms; do not report the invoice as cash before it clears. Track pilot delivery hours, inference consumption, hosting, and support tickets by account. If one customer's bespoke integration consumes the entire fee, narrow the scope or raise the price instead of subsidizing an unrepeatable service with imagined future subscriptions.

Make the four gates deterministic by recording counts and outcomes in one ledger. Each account has a stable identifier so multiple meetings never inflate the denominator. Gate one needs five matching incidents among ten interviews; gate two needs ten evidenced accounts and five owner confirmations; gate three needs five successful workflow demonstrations; gate four needs five to ten signed qualifying commitments. The same account may progress through all stages, but it counts once within each stage. Review the ledger weekly and record the exact reason an account stalls. If scope changes materially, recheck the affected evidence rather than carrying old signatures into a different product. Demand validation is a budget decision under uncertainty, not proof of product market fit. Renewals, retention, support load, and actual margins still need measurement after the first delivery cycle.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Repeated pain
   - Interview ten target accounts.
   - Pass at five matching recent incidents with owners.
2. Verified signal
   - Document ten current account signals.
   - Pass when five owners confirm the same job.
3. Demonstrated outcome
   - Test a diagnostic or clickable workflow.
   - Pass at five independent task completions.
4. Commercial commitment
   - Secure five to ten signed pilots or substantive LOIs.
   - Only then build shared backend capability; track cash separately.

## 5. Close a bounded pilot and protect the owner economics  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Send a short commercial email containing one reproduced observation, a useful next step, and a bounded offer. Fill every bracketed field in the script with verified facts before sending; never invent a customer relationship or pretend a cold message is a reply. Under CAN-SPAM, use accurate headers and subject lines, identify the commercial nature of the message, include a valid physical postal address, and provide a functioning opt-out. Honor opt-outs within ten business days and maintain the required working opt-out mechanism for at least thirty days after sending. Suppress recipients immediately operationally rather than waiting for the legal deadline. Other jurisdictions can impose different outreach requirements. Do not attach alarming legal claims to an accessibility report. The buyer should understand the observed problem, the scope of your service, and the price without opening an attachment.

Model annual gross subscription billings separately from pilot receipts and every cost layer. The Conservative case is $50,000 gross at 15% net, leaving $7,500 net and a $42,500 total expense envelope. Base is $375,000 gross at 30%, leaving $112,500 net and $262,500 of expenses. Scaled is $700,000 gross at 45%, leaving $315,000 net and $385,000 of expenses. These are illustrative targets, not demonstrated operating outcomes. The staffing plans are respectively 1 FT + 2 C, 1 FT + 2 C, and 2 FT + 3 C, where FT means full-time and C means contractor. Contractor counts do not specify hours. The Conservative envelope cannot casually fund one market salary plus two substantial retainers. If founder labor is unpaid, report that subsidy explicitly and show a separate normalized profit after pricing that labor.

For a worked Base allocation, reserve $37,500 for inference and hosting COGS, $15,000 for payment costs and service credits, $150,000 for labor, and $60,000 for acquisition and overhead. Those expenses total $262,500, leaving the modeled $112,500 owner operating profit before personal taxes and financing effects. This assumes billings are earned and collectible within the modeled year; annual prepayments require separate cash and revenue schedules. At $250 monthly per account, $375,000 implies 125 average paying accounts across twelve months, not 125 signups at year end. Measure gross additions, churn, expansion, and average active accounts separately. Cap expensive model calls, cache stable diagnostic results, and route uncertain outputs to human review. If measured inference or support costs exceed the allocation, revise pricing or scope before extrapolating the Scaled margin from a small pilot cohort.
