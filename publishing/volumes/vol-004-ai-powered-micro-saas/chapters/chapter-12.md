## The Practitioner Consensus

Separate the customer who chooses to leave from the customer whose renewal simply fails. RonBuilds starts with a cohort of cancellations and payment failures, then divides it into voluntary cancellation by the customer and involuntary loss through declining cards, insufficient funds, or expiry. That distinction changes the work: the first group requires an explanation for leaving; the second may need a working payment method rather than another product pitch. Churnkey describes the silent version as friction rather than dissatisfaction: the customer did not cancel or complain, but the card did not go through. Freemius gives the same operational sequence for that problem: retry, remind, and provide an easy way to update payment details. Keep the revenue view alongside the customer count. Ryan Floyd of Ask a VC emphasizes dollar churn in enterprise B2B because customers can contribute very different amounts of revenue; logo churn and revenue churn align only when customers pay the same amount. Floyd also separates gross cancellations from net churn, which incorporates expansion and contraction. RonBuilds warns against treating the aggregate churn rate as a diagnosis and immediately launching win-back campaigns. The shared starting point is therefore specific: identify what happened, which customers experienced it, and what revenue those customers represent. At-risk monitoring moves the intervention ahead of the cancellation decision. Dan Martell asks operators to define what healthy usage looks like, what an account about to churn looks like, which features customers have deployed, and when they log in. The resulting signals should feed customer support or customer success rather than remain an unused measurement exercise. CSM Practice broadens that view beyond activity and pleasant interactions: a useful health score must detect both desired customer outcomes and an appropriate experience. The speakers say many models emphasize experience because outcomes can be harder to collect and may require information from customers themselves. They also describe a practical advantage of a strong relationship: customers missing value may give advance notice about a required capability instead of disappearing without warning. Gamify Data makes the prediction target explicit. Cancellation, contraction, renewal after usage collapses, and failed-card loss are different events; choose one per model and specify the prediction window. Its example scores renewal 120 days ahead, then produces a weekly list ranked by revenue at risk, with a reason and a named representative. Together, these approaches connect usage, outcomes, relationships, and an assigned response while keeping the predicted event specific. The score is useful to the team because it identifies an account to work, not simply an account to label. ## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Hormozi's onboarding prescription is deliberately hands-on: run two group Zoom onboardings each day, one in the morning and one at night, and book new customers into onboarding when they sign up. Walk them through the product, watch the friction, and get the video and editing actions completed in roughly 20 minutes. The booking push is not limited to a passive dashboard button: Hormozi names text, calls, email, and making the booking the next step inside the signup flow. He then segments by acquisition channel and customer avatar so the strongest customer groups can shape front-end messaging. Billing is another lever in the same discussion. The founder sells a $60 monthly package and a roughly $360 annual option, reporting that 40–50% now choose annual. For customers selling only a few houses each year, Hormozi discusses quarterly or annual billing to align the billing cycle with the value cycle. Vasco Aires uses human contact at the other end of the relationship: a client-success call during cancellation can reveal confusing interfaces or inadequate documentation. RonBuilds adds the data plumbing needed to connect these interactions: set the user identifier in the application's database and share it with external tools so emails, support tickets, conversions, and value can be related to one person. For payment recovery, configure the retry engine and the customer-facing path as distinct pieces of work. In Churnkey's roundtable, Precision Retries are enabled through a toggle in advanced payment-recovery settings, while Stripe remains the source of truth for subscription status. The recommendation is four Stripe retries within one month, alongside four or five Churnkey Precision Retries during that month. Dodo Payments describes automatic retries for fixable failures such as insufficient funds, while skipping a stolen card; a successful attempt returns the subscription to active. Its manual retry is separate from automatic attempts and is intended for moments such as a customer saying funds have just been added. Freemius presents a built-in alternative: failed card or PayPal renewals retry one day later, then three days, then five, with update-link emails along the way. Ecom Mastery shows GoHighLevel's payment-received trigger filtered to failed transactions, followed by an invoice or payment-link message, a wait, and an if/else payment-status check. Success sends thanks and exits; an unresolved payment proceeds to the next reminder and retry. After the final retry, the operator chooses the demonstrated cancellation or pause path and communicates that result. These are specific platform workflows, each with its own settings and sequence, rather than one identical retry schedule applied everywhere. ## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The sharpest disagreement is how far automation can carry payment recovery. Churnkey promotes an automated combination of retries, payment walls, and campaigns; its involuntary-churn presentation reports recovery of up to 70% of failed payments, while its multichannel presentation claims up to 89% across the channels it describes. Gravy argues that dunning software and automated emails alone are not enough, saying 85% of people are not responsive to those efforts. Its alternative has three ingredients: a person focused on recovery, a proven one-to-one communication process, and visibility into the recovery metric. Gravy attributes over 70% of saved payments to a person sending a customized email and reports average recovery of 51% of failed revenue within 30 days. Freemius puts a different question ahead of buying another service: what is already included in the payment platform? It identifies built-in dunning, standalone software, and building internally as three routes, then says standalone tools offer more control but run from about $130 to $269 monthly. Freemius includes its own dunning without an extra tool fee. The choices therefore differ in staffing, platform packaging, and claimed results: Churnkey sells automation breadth, Gravy sells dedicated human attention, and Freemius tells sellers to inspect existing recovery numbers before purchasing another tool. Cancellation handling also has competing operating styles. Vasco Aires deliberately adds steps: a video and invitation to book a call, a cancellation-reason form requiring a substantive comment, an option to send feedback and wait for the team, and a final confirmation emphasizing what the customer loses. Vasco explicitly rejects the extreme of making customers call during a narrow window, but still says the business tries to delay cancellation and gather as much feedback as possible. Dan Martell's early-stage suggestion is a conversation before automatic cancellation, potentially through email, when the team does not yet understand why people leave; later, those responses can become self-cancellation choices and roadmap input. Raaft centers the reason-matched response instead: show the next charge and exact end date for billing confusion, repair a broken cancellation path, offer human contact for support problems, and offer a cheaper plan or pause for price objections. RonBuilds narrows where intensive save work belongs. Activated, engaged, valuable customers with a clear reason may justify personalized win-back treatment; low-value, price-sensitive users who are unlikely to stay may not. These approaches put different weight on conversation, deliberate cancellation friction, and matching the remedy to the reason. Vasco's sequence also distinguishes a final cancellation from sending feedback and waiting, which leaves the subscription uncanceled. ## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

A completed activation event can be a false signal. RonBuilds describes teams copying actions performed by successful users, choosing an arbitrary repetition count, and declaring a habit without verifying that the action distinguishes customers who stay from those who leave. If departing users perform the same event, that event alone does not prove activation. He calls for checking the data and correlation rather than stopping at a retention curve. The founder in Hormozi's discussion exposes the same gap by saying they believe first-video creation correlates with retention but would need to double-check. Hormozi's response is to verify it, observe onboarding directly, and avoid scaling beyond the current position until the churn problem is fixed. Vasco Aires supplies a concrete discovery from customer calls: some people want to cancel because they do not understand the product, possibly because the documentation is inadequate or the interface is confusing. A ten-to-fifteen-minute conversation can uncover that issue and keep a customer. Vasco also says the business still has churn and that improvements take time, describing one or two months before seeing an actual reduction. The operational trap is confusing a checked box, a new feature, or a newly installed sequence with a demonstrated change in customer behavior. A recovery workflow can look complete while still needing configuration. Ecom Mastery demonstrates GoHighLevel's AI-generated automation, then points out that part of the if/else condition may need to be configured and describes the result as a useful skeleton. The demonstration also calls for tweaking the unsuccessful branch to continue the retry workflow. Churnkey's roundtable identifies a different operational miss: payment auto-retries are often not toggled on in customer campaigns, so the opportunity to collect immediately before sending a reminder is unused. The same speaker calls the failed-payment wall the most underutilized recovery product encountered on customer calls, despite seeing teams use it effectively. Retry intensity has its own trap. The roundtable warns that eight attempts in the first two weeks might draw pushback from card companies or look suspicious, and instead recommends its stated month-long arrangement with Stripe. Dodo draws the failure-reason boundary explicitly: retry insufficient funds, but skip a stolen card; when the card needs replacing, move to update-link reminders rather than expecting retries to fix it. The operational distinctions are specific: an automation skeleton still needs its conditions configured, an available recovery option can remain switched off, and a dead payment method needs customer action rather than another ordinary attempt. Keep the economics and evidence checks attached to the recovery work. Ryan Floyd warns that expansion in some accounts can hide gross churn, so both gross and net figures matter. He also cautions that monthly churn views can be misleading when customers pay annually and only some contracts come up for renewal during the period. Gamify Data names a prediction failure that can make the dashboard look much better than the deployed model: letting a cancellation ticket enter inputs that are supposed to represent an earlier scoring date. Build inputs as of that date, test on later renewals, and compare the model with an existing rule such as usage falling more than 30%. If the model cannot beat the rule, keep the rule. Raaft separates evidence of reasons from evidence of successful saves: its review analysis found 53 leaving accounts among 1,024 reviews, with overlapping explanations, but those reviews cannot establish which intervention would have worked. RonBuilds adds a direct reminder that failed-payment loss can be accidental: after receiving a new card, they forgot to update subscriptions and experienced involuntary cancellations. Their wider recovery approach still checks customer value before investing heavily in discounts or retargeting. Recovered revenue, expansion, and prediction scores each need the particular checks their practitioners describe.
