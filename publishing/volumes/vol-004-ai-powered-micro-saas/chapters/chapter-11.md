## The Practitioner Consensus

Start with buyers who are choosing, not an audience that merely recognizes the category. Josh Gallant places comparison searches at the evaluation stage immediately before purchase: the prospect already wants a solution and is deciding which one belongs on the shortlist. Their opening argument is that competitors will occupy that conversation if you leave it unanswered [gDJkMfIyJJg @ 00:00:13]. Rock The Rankings gives the commercial mechanism a second job: explain the feature set and product fit clearly enough to attract suitable customers while directing unsuitable ones toward a better-fitting competitor. The channel connects that filtering to reducing churn rather than maximizing every possible signup [DXrqf5Xqpd4 @ 00:02:28]. Minuttia likewise treats these pages as reusable sales resources, not just search landing pages. A representative can send a structured comparison instead of repeatedly explaining the same differences on calls [SBSEbXntw7c @ 00:01:54]. Across those approaches, the work is positioning delivered at a buying moment: describe what the product does, make the differences usable, and help the prospect choose. Gallant's shortcut is entering an existing evaluation rather than creating every earlier awareness touchpoint. Rock The Rankings adds that winning the wrong customer is not the point. Minuttia adds leverage for the team already handling those buying questions.

Get the language from the people making the decision. On UserActive, Federico Jorge offers two interview questions: what was happening when the customer started looking for a solution, and how did their life or job change after using it? The answers supply the problems, challenges, and impact that belong on the page, rather than copy built around the writer's creativity [JVpDfAIFgYk @ 00:05:46]. Rob Hoffman applies the same listening discipline to competitor customers. They would rather target people frustrated by something the product actually solves, whether that is customer service, missing AI functionality, or price, than automatically pursue the largest brand in the market [mPeQxVCunPU @ 00:11:40]. At MicroConf, the speaker describes copywriting as listening even when those customers belong to someone else: take the complaints on competitor review pages and explain how your product solves the problem differently, more cheaply, or more easily [oYRzvehnC64 @ 00:30:34]. This is a shared research habit with different entry points. Jorge starts with customers' before-and-after stories; Hoffman starts with switching pain; MicroConf starts with published complaints. Each approach gives the comparison a reason to exist beyond putting two logos beside a table. The copy answers an actual buying problem instead of merely declaring that the smaller vendor deserves attention.

Measure discovery through business outcomes, and build pages that describe the product's actual use. B2BSEOio says TrackDesk's keyword research selected use cases that made business sense, not merely terms with search volume. The work prioritized features, solutions, and use-case pages, with those pages built within a year [P2hKSGQZLEw @ 00:02:34]. Gonçalo Builds reports five recurring page types across the software businesses they examined: comparisons, alternatives, solutions, case studies, and integrations. Their inventory extends discovery beyond a single versus article and into the contexts in which software gets selected [J8vBw3XaW6A @ 00:00:44]. Fomin Unscripted makes the outcome test blunt: website visits, signups, and payments matter more than random views. Their preference is a smaller audience that needs the product, followed by more variations of the content already bringing customers [xWcG7R7Xm2Y @ 00:03:44]. Loopex Digital closes its agency ranking with the same purchasing criterion: ask for pipeline, not just traffic [shXLenFsH2E @ 00:04:09]. The common ground is not that every business should publish the same number of pages or hire the same agency. It is that search targets, page types, and reporting should remain attached to buyers and the software's commercial use. Traffic is part of that work, not the final score.

## Exact Street Mechanics, Tools & Numbers

Build the competitor target list before writing the page. Hoffman's demonstrated research starts in Claude: request a large competitor list, make it easy to paste into Ahrefs, and append the word alternatives to each name. Paste the resulting list into Keywords Explorer, then inspect matching terms for search volume and keyword difficulty [mPeQxVCunPU @ 00:07:13]. Gallant supplies a second route to the same list: review category listings on G2, Capterra, and Software Advice; search best-category-software queries; then add companies that have appeared in won or lost deals. Prioritize using search demand and the competitors you repeatedly meet in actual evaluations [gDJkMfIyJJg @ 00:35:27]. Vasco's automation demonstration starts with the business URL and asks Claude Code to analyze the site, ideal customer profile, competition, and niche. The described output is a table with type, keyword, buying intent, article title, and notes [qAl321gTCg4 @ 00:18:44]. These are different production routes to a concrete editorial queue. Hoffman demonstrates keyword expansion and research tooling; Gallant grounds competitor selection in what prospects actually encounter; Vasco hands the research output toward automated page generation. The deliverable in each case is a specific target and page idea, rather than a general instruction to publish more content.

Give the buying question an explicit page structure. Gallant keeps comparison titles plain: brand versus brand, followed by a reason to click. Their URL recommendation is a compare subfolder containing individual you-versus-them pages, with creativity reserved for the description rather than obscuring the comparison in the title [gDJkMfIyJJg @ 00:16:54]. Gonçalo Builds separates that one-to-one page from an alternatives list. In the latter, place your product strategically near the top and identify what each competing option is best for; their ClickUp example captures people searching for Jira alternatives rather than people already comparing ClickUp against a named rival [J8vBw3XaW6A @ 00:03:49]. Solopreneurs' nuBeginning lays out an alternatives article as a problem-focused introduction, a quick-scan list, detailed entries, a comparison table, a needs-based decision guide, and a low-friction call to action [FDzwKvUF_xw @ 00:06:44]. Hoffman adds firsthand product evidence: buy the competitor's cheapest plan, or use its free plan, and capture screenshots and videos showing the tools rather than relying on vague superiority claims [mPeQxVCunPU @ 00:20:18]. Together, those mechanics specify the search entry, the comparison format, the decision support, and the evidence a visitor can inspect before taking the next step.

For Reddit visibility, Sabyr Nurgaliyev starts with the questions prospects ask before choosing an HTML5 advertising tool, then finds existing discussions in the subreddits those prospects use. Viewst appears as a recommendation answering the particular problem, rather than as a request to search for an unfamiliar brand [YVRP3QoP6bs @ 00:02:19]. Paul Ogonowski builds the measurement loop at signup: require an answer to how the buyer first heard about the company, separate AI sources into named options, and ask what the buyer asked the assistant [smAcRrXdcgw @ 00:01:38]. Their monitoring routine uses fifteen to twenty-five buyer prompts, run at least twice weekly, tracking how often the product appears and which competitors appear instead [smAcRrXdcgw @ 00:10:24]. Vasco takes a separate publishing route through an automated campaign: connect the site, choose article frequency, select the CMS integration, and let the system generate and publish. The demonstration gives thirty new articles per week as an example and lists WordPress, Ghost, Shopify, Wix, Webflow, Zapier, API, Squarespace, and webhook options [qAl321gTCg4 @ 00:19:52]. The working pieces are explicit: questions, discussion placement, buyer attribution, repeated visibility checks, and a chosen publishing cadence. Nurgaliyev's Reddit campaign does not depend on adopting Vasco's owned-site production workflow.

## Where Practitioners Disagree & Alternative Playbooks

The first split is when to start and what the comparison should look like. Minuttia recommends waiting until product-market fit and some brand awareness: an early product may be immature, and the offer or strategy may change enough to make today's comparisons obsolete [SBSEbXntw7c @ 00:14:18]. Rock The Rankings takes the opposite entry position, saying these pages can make sense with minimal authority, a minimal link profile, and little brand awareness; review aggregators in the results are not a reason to abandon the opportunity [DXrqf5Xqpd4 @ 00:04:30]. The page itself produces another disagreement. Solopreneurs' nuBeginning calls for a feature table with the publisher's product in the top row as the clear winner, emphasizing its rating, pricing, and features the rival lacks [FDzwKvUF_xw @ 00:05:59]. The MicroConf speaker says to remove the feature-for-feature approach and instead develop a differentiated view of the right product for the right person. Their criticism is aimed at checking a marketing box rather than presenting a genuine, deep comparison [oYRzvehnC64 @ 00:27:18]. These are distinct playbooks, not one unanimous formula: wait for maturity versus enter early, and visually establish a winner versus lead with the buyer's identity and situation. Each speaker ties the choice to a different concern.

Automation and AI optimization produce a second split. Vasco explicitly proposes generating these page types in volume, with the research prompt feeding automated production rather than leaving the operator to write every article [qAl321gTCg4 @ 00:18:01]. Coutinho rejects hundreds of new AI-generated pages as a beginner's starting point, arguing that a new website should master creating pages, attempting indexing, and reworking pages that do not index [KYpd0fJp3Qw @ 00:05:38]. Their differences are operational: Vasco presents volume generation as the production engine; Coutinho concentrates scarce attention on getting individual pages accepted and improving them. Paul Ogonowski rejects another cluster of popular optimizations, saying they would skip llms.txt and schema markup based on the studies and tests they cite [smAcRrXdcgw @ 00:09:40]. Agency Review Insider, by contrast, describes schema on every page as part of 95 Projects' package, alongside product facts and outside mentions on software lists, review sites, and industry publications [7smrgVmHACQ @ 00:01:39]. The disagreement therefore covers both publishing throughput and which technical work deserves attention. Ogonowski's position is not the same as the agency ranking's packaging, and Coutinho's weekly indexing discipline is not Vasco's mass-production promise. Those alternatives should remain visible when assigning the work.

The agency rankings offer competing buying maps rather than a shared winner. Loopex Digital places itself first for measured AI search and quotes pricing from $1,500 monthly on a month-to-month basis, while acknowledging the absence of Fortune 500 references [shXLenFsH2E @ 00:00:18]. Agency Review Insider instead puts 95 Projects first and quotes a $5,000 monthly starting price without multi-year contracts; its described opening work tests real buyer questions across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews [7smrgVmHACQ @ 00:03:11]. Even a shared agency has different reported entry terms. Loopex lists Rock The Rankings from $4,500 monthly with a 120-day minimum [shXLenFsH2E @ 00:02:53]. Agency Review Insider describes a typical ninety-day starting program, strategy from $5,000 monthly, and a full program from $7,500 to $15,000 or more. It also highlights matching CRM records with buyers' discovery answers to attribute AI-driven demos [7smrgVmHACQ @ 00:04:23]. The offers are not interchangeable: one ranking emphasizes its measured-search leader and lower starting price; the other emphasizes its preferred combined SEO and GEO team. Rock The Rankings appears in both, but the quoted budgets and initial engagement lengths remain different. Those specific descriptions matter more than the shared word agency.

## Firsthand Failure Modes, Traps & Economics Checks

Coutinho's experience puts a cost on treating SEO as the easy channel. They report approximately one thousand search-engine clicks across almost ten months, roughly one hundred monthly, and question whether a visual product could have done better through another distribution strategy [KYpd0fJp3Qw @ 00:06:37]. Bing supplied the sharper lesson: almost ten months passed before the first click, and Coutinho accepts responsibility for not paying enough attention. Clicks began arriving as pages became indexed, reinforcing their conclusion that SEO was not hands-off [KYpd0fJp3Qw @ 00:06:52]. Fomin Unscripted describes the parallel conversion trap: visitors and even signups can arrive without payments. Their response is not simply more publishing, but examining the landing page, whether visitors understand the product, pricing, and traffic fit [xWcG7R7Xm2Y @ 00:05:30]. At MicroConf, the speaker recounts founders announcing batches of fifty to one hundred AI articles or new ad experiments, then reporting little to show months later. They call this random marketing performed to tick a box [oYRzvehnC64 @ 00:06:14]. The bottlenecks differ, but the firsthand pattern is recognizable: unattended indexing, activity without a buying response, and initiatives started without an engineered connection to the rest of the business. Coutinho's reversal is to retain SEO while no longer treating it as the main initial distribution channel.

Visibility reports can conceal both discovery and measurement mistakes. At a US company where Paul Ogonowski ran growth, analytics credited AI search with two percent of visits, while twenty-three percent of new signups said they heard about the company through AI. Their proposed correction starts with asking buyers rather than trusting referral attribution alone [smAcRrXdcgw @ 00:00:41]. B2BSEOio makes a different measurement correction in the TrackDesk case: an Ahrefs report showed average traffic of eight thousand, but the speaker says Search Console showed five thousand. They explicitly choose the lower figure when explaining the result [P2hKSGQZLEw @ 00:00:45]. Federico Jorge identifies a less technical reporting failure in their own work. Clients had reported stronger conversions or better leads, but Jorge admits failing to follow up and document the results consistently [JVpDfAIFgYk @ 00:08:43]. These are separate problems: an attribution blind spot, disagreement between a third-party estimate and direct search reporting, and missing follow-through after a client reports improvement. None is repaired by collecting another ranking screenshot. Ogonowski asks for discovery answers, B2BSEOio identifies the measurement source, and Jorge acknowledges the documentation gap. The commercial result and the evidence used to describe it need to stay connected in the operator's reporting.

Comparison content also creates maintenance and competitor-response work. Gallant reports a B2B SaaS project producing roughly forty to fifty comparison pages: one competitor sent a demand that led to removing a page, while another requested a correction that the team made. Their account is a removal and a revision, not a campaign that required no follow-up [gDJkMfIyJJg @ 00:07:10]. Minuttia's recurring operational instruction is to monitor changing competitor pricing and update comparison pages often, because the competitive landscape moves [SBSEbXntw7c @ 00:13:02]. Hoffman identifies the economic check after publication: a ranking alone can coexist with hundreds of readers and no signups, while a small audience may produce a valuable customer. They illustrate that contrast with a branding-agency client that won a single $100,000 contract [mPeQxVCunPU @ 00:30:23]. Gallant's forecasting approach also includes ongoing upkeep and optimization rather than only the initial page-production bill; their example stretches the opportunity and maintenance investment across thirty-six months [gDJkMfIyJJg @ 00:11:28]. The operating account therefore includes the page that gets pulled, the claim that needs correcting, the pricing that changes, and the continuing cost of keeping the asset useful. The payoff is evaluated through customers and their value, not simply whether the article has reached a search position.
