## 1. Sell a bounded evidence packet in 48 hours  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Make 48 hours the response target, not a promised procurement outcome. A three-person team means one full-time owner and two contractors with scoped hours, not three salaried departments. The owner handles commercial terms; one contractor maintains infrastructure evidence; the other checks contracts and customer documentation within their competence. Start the clock when the buyer supplies its questionnaire, data categories, intended users, and purchase authority. Within four hours, acknowledge scope and identify unsupported requests. Within one business day, send a dated packet containing the architecture map, security answers, subprocessor register, retention schedule, incident contact, and proposed agreements. By hour 48, resolve standard questions and present specific exceptions. Record unanswered buyer dependencies separately so a stalled customer review never becomes a fictional internal service failure or a fabricated closing statistic.

Build evidence around a synthetic customer record moving through the product. Show collection, tenant authorization, application storage, retrieval, inference, output storage, support access, and deletion. Identify which paths contain personal data and which carry only account identifiers or aggregate metrics. Keep screenshots free of customer content, credentials, and internal secrets. Include the date, environment, configuration owner, and next review date for each control. A buyer should distinguish an implemented safeguard from a scheduled improvement without decoding marketing language. Publish stable public summaries and share sensitive supporting documents through restricted access. Never paste another customer's questionnaire answers into a new buyer's packet without checking scope. Reuse the underlying evidence, not unsupported assurances. Measure first-response time, exceptions per deal, and lost gross contribution attributable to missing controls each month.

Qualify the buying obstacle before commissioning an audit. Ask whether the buyer requires a completed SOC 2 report, accepts a security questionnaire, or will sign against a dated remediation commitment. SOC 2 Type I addresses control design at a point in time; Type II evaluates operating effectiveness over a period. Neither is a government certification or a substitute for the buyer's own requirements. Request the requirement in writing and connect it to a named opportunity, expected contribution, and decision date. A hypothetical $20,000 readiness project cannot be justified by an unqualified $200,000 pipeline. Start with access reviews, restoration tests, incident exercises, and change records that improve operations even if the deal disappears. Purchase external assurance when signed or strongly qualified demand supports its full recurring cost, including internal labor.

## 2. Contract for the data you actually process  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Publish a clean Subprocessor List naming each legal entity, service purpose, processing location, data categories, and transfer mechanism where relevant. Include hosting, model inference, support, analytics, and email providers when they process customer personal data on your behalf. Pair it with a GDPR Art. 28 Data Processing Addendum, or DPA, describing subject matter, duration, nature, purpose, data types, and data subjects. Address documented instructions, confidentiality, security, assistance with rights requests and incidents, deletion or return, and information needed for audits. General written subprocessor authorization requires notice of intended changes and an opportunity to object; do not hide changes behind an undated webpage. Article 28 does not itself solve international transfers. Map those separately and keep the executed agreement consistent with the technical routes actually enabled for that buyer.

Start the master services agreement with aggregate liability limited to fees paid during the twelve months preceding the event giving rise to the claim. Define the measurement period, covered claims, and interaction with indemnities rather than leaving a headline cap disconnected from the rest of the document. This is a negotiating position, not a universal legal ceiling. Some liabilities cannot be limited under applicable law; buyers may request separate treatment for confidentiality, data protection, fraud, or intellectual property claims. Price accepted exceptions against insurance, cash reserves, and contract value. A customer paying $12,000 annually should not quietly create an uncapped balance-sheet exposure. Have qualified counsel review the actual jurisdiction and exceptions. Track signed deviations centrally so sales cannot promise one risk allocation while delivery assumes another on the same account.

Treat health information as a distinct product boundary, not a checkbox in an enterprise questionnaire. Determine whether the intended arrangement makes the company a HIPAA business associate and whether a BAA is required with the customer and relevant downstream providers. A cloud provider's general healthcare offering does not make every model, region, feature, or logging route eligible. Confirm the exact covered services and execute required agreements before accepting protected health information. If the current product cannot meet those obligations, offer a demonstrably deidentified workflow or decline that data category while preserving ordinary business use. Compare the incremental engineering, insurance, support, and contracting expense against qualified contribution from healthcare deals. A signed BAA allocates responsibilities; it does not replace access controls, incident response, workforce training, or evidence that the chosen architecture satisfies them.

## 3. Prove zero retention across the whole inference route  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

Separate zero training, zero provider retention, and zero application retention in every answer. They are different commitments. For OpenAI, verify whether the organization has approved Zero Data Retention controls and whether the chosen endpoint and features qualify. For Anthropic, verify contractual retention arrangements and feature coverage rather than treating ordinary commercial API access as automatically ZDR. For AWS Bedrock and GCP Vertex, inspect the exact managed service, model publisher, region, abuse-monitoring terms, and customer-controlled logging settings. Marketplace models and optional tools may introduce different conditions. Do not transplant a direct provider agreement onto a cloud reseller route. Store dated evidence of the account settings and governing terms. Where a setting cannot be inspected directly, obtain written confirmation identifying the account and service rather than relying on a generic sales statement.

Configure the product around an allowlist of verified routes, with separate credentials and project boundaries where practical. Reject unsupported attachments, persistent assistants, caching modes, or tool integrations on workloads sold as ZDR unless their retention behavior is documented and compatible. Disable optional payload logging and tracing; redact before records enter monitoring systems rather than scheduling deletion after ingestion. Examine queues, retry stores, dead-letter queues, embeddings, uploaded files, and support exports. Provider ZDR does not erase your database or an engineer's debug capture. Keep operational metadata only where justified, with defined fields and expiry. On provider failure, return a clear retryable error or use another verified route; never silently send a protected workload to an ordinary fallback. Track route identifiers with requests so an incident can identify affected accounts without retaining their prompts.

Test the promise using synthetic canary strings, never real customer secrets. Submit a canary through success, timeout, retry, support, and deletion paths, then inspect every store your team controls. Absence from local logs is evidence about your system, not proof of the provider's undisclosed implementation. Pair those tests with contractual evidence and provider documentation. Define what happens to backups and when deleted data ages out; if immediate erasure is impossible, say so precisely and restrict restoration access. Revalidate whenever a model, feature, region, logging library, or subprocessor changes. Maintain a short exception register with owner and expiry. If an exception contradicts a customer's retention promise, stop that route for that customer until corrected. Protect the promise through executable configuration, not through a security-page sentence that engineering cannot substantiate.

**SOP — Standard Operating Procedure**

1. Map
   - Inventory every data destination.
   - Include retries, tools, support, and backups.
2. Verify
   - Capture account-specific retention evidence.
   - Record provider, region, model, feature, and date.
3. Constrain
   - Allow only verified routes.
   - Remove payload logging and unverified fallbacks.
4. Exercise
   - Run synthetic retention tests.
   - Record findings and retest after configuration changes.

## 4. Use the SaaS Cancellation & Save-Flow Protocol verbatim  `🏷️ OPERATING_FRAMEWORK`

ROSCA, 15 U.S.C. section 8403, requires clear disclosure of material terms before obtaining billing information, express informed consent before charging, and simple mechanisms to stop recurring charges for covered online negative-option transactions. It does not prescribe the exact interface below. Do not present the FTC's 2024 amendments to 16 CFR Part 425 as an operative nationwide click-to-cancel mandate: the Eighth Circuit vacated those amendments in July 2025. The earlier rule concerned prenotification negative-option plans, not every SaaS subscription. Check subsequent developments and applicable state automatic-renewal requirements before publication or deployment. A B2B label also does not settle every applicability question. Use cancellation parity as a product standard while counsel maps actual obligations. Keep disclosures, consent evidence, renewal terms, and cancellation implementation consistent rather than assuming a convenient button alone resolves subscription law.

SaaS Cancellation & Save-Flow Protocol: Display price, billing interval, trial conversion, renewal terms, and cancellation timing before collecting billing details; capture express informed agreement before charging. In authenticated Billing, show equally visible Pause, Downgrade, and Cancel subscription buttons with the applicable price and effective date beside each. Each button executes its labeled action in one click without a second confirmation. Cancel subscription stops renewal immediately and preserves paid access through the displayed date unless an immediate termination or refund right applies. Display: 'Renewal stopped. Your paid access ends on [date]. No further subscription renewals will be charged.' Send the same receipt by email. Pause states its duration, access limits, and any resumption date before activation; downgrade states its new price and effective date. Neither option substitutes for the customer's chosen cancellation.

Protocol continued: Never require a survey, telephone call, retention conversation, extra password entry, or offer dismissal after an already authenticated customer's cancellation click. Offer optional exit reasons after execution, with Skip as a normal choice. Automatically tag cancellation as customer_cancelled and reason as not_provided unless the customer supplies a reason; never infer dissatisfaction from protected characteristics or private prompt content. Keep save offers outside the cancellation path and require a separate affirmative action to reactivate. Use an idempotency key, durable cancellation record, processor reconciliation, and duplicate-webhook handling. Distinguish request receipt from confirmed billing state; if the processor fails, suppress renewal attempts under your control and escalate reconciliation without falsely claiming success. Test near-renewal races, multiple subscriptions, delayed events, invoices already issued, and receipt failures. Explain legitimate outstanding charges without disguising another renewal as arrears.

## 5. Own the work and price the trust burden  `🏷️ ILLUSTRATIVE_MODEL`

Obtain written contractor code and prompt assignments before work begins, then link deliverables to repository history and invoices. Under 17 U.S.C. section 204(a), a transfer of copyright ownership generally requires a writing signed by the owner of the rights conveyed or an authorized agent. Payment alone is not an assignment, and calling every contractor deliverable work made for hire does not make it qualify. Use present assignment language for rights the contractor owns, identify covered deliverables, and require disclosure of preexisting components with an adequate license to use them. Include confidentiality, provenance, further assurances, and restrictions on importing third-party confidential material. Have counsel adapt the document to the jurisdiction and engagement. Record assignments for code, documentation, evaluation datasets, and protectable prompt material without asserting that every short prompt is independently copyrightable.

Audit ownership through the actual build rather than a signature folder alone. Inventory open-source dependencies, licenses, copied snippets, generated assets, datasets, and contractor background tools. Preserve required notices and evaluate distribution or network-use obligations before choosing a dependency. Provider terms allocating output rights do not establish that an output is copyrightable, unique, or free of third-party claims. Require contractors to disclose material generated contributions and review them for license, security, and provenance concerns. Keep customer inputs outside reusable training or evaluation collections unless the contract and lawful basis support that use. At offboarding, revoke credentials, transfer operational documentation, and obtain delivery of source and build instructions. Ownership is commercially useful only when the company can operate and modify the product without a departing contractor's personal account, undocumented secret, or continuing discretionary permission.

Use the annual scenarios below as planning arithmetic, not observed benchmarks. Gross subscription billings exclude sales taxes and assume no refunds, credits, or deferred-revenue timing differences for this simplified model; reconcile those separately in actual accounts. Inference and hosting COGS cover model usage, compute, storage, and network service. Other operating costs include payment processing, support, acquisition, legal work, insurance, assurance, and budgeted labor. Net owner profit here is the residual after those costs and before owner personal taxes, not gross margin or cash collected. FT means full-time operator; C means scoped contractor capacity, not a full-time equivalent. The conservative case cannot support a market-rate full-time salary plus broad contractor hours: it assumes a lightly compensated founder and narrow engagements. Reprice or reject enterprise exceptions that consume the residual, and rerun the model using actual labor commitments before hiring.
